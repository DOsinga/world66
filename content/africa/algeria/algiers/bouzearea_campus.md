The central campus of the University of Algiers occupies a large colonial-era complex in the upper city. Founded in 1879 as part of the French colonial academic expansion, it was one of the first universities in North Africa and remains one of the largest in the Arab world, with over 100,000 students across multiple campuses.

The main building — a grand French academic palazzo with arcaded galleries, carved stonework, and a central courtyard — is architecturally impressive. The campus is open and you can walk through it. The surrounding streets are full of student cafes, bookshops, and photocopy shops. For a sense of how Algiers functions as a young, educated, politically conscious city, spending an hour here is more revealing than most museums.
