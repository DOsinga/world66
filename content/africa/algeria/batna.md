Batna is a gateway town in eastern Algeria, sitting at over 1,000 metres on the northern edge of the [Aurès Mountains](/africa/algeria/batna/aures_mountains). The town itself is functional rather than charming — the French built it as a military camp in 1844 to control the roads running south into the Sahara — but the surroundings are the reason to come.

The Roman ruins of [Timgad](/africa/algeria/timgad), one of the best-preserved Roman cities in North Africa, are just 35 kilometres east. The [Aurès Mountains](/africa/algeria/batna/aures_mountains) themselves offer dramatic gorges and Berber villages rarely visited by outsiders. Also within easy reach is [Lambèse](/africa/algeria/batna/lambese), the Roman legionary fortress at Tazoult, which sees a fraction of the visitors that Timgad does. The [Musée de Batna](/africa/algeria/batna/musee_de_batna) provides useful context for both sites before you head out. [Constantine](/africa/algeria/constantine) and the Tunisian border are both within easy reach. At this altitude, the climate stays pleasant through the hot summer months when the lowlands become stifling.
