The Musée de Batna is the principal museum in the Aurès region, housing an archaeological collection that draws heavily from Roman and pre-Roman sites in the province. The centrepiece is the Roman material: inscriptions, sculpture, oil lamps, coins, and ceramics from Timgad, Lambèse, and the dozens of smaller sites scattered across the highlands. There is also a solid collection of prehistoric objects — flint tools, Neolithic pottery, and rock engravings copied from the Aurès massif.

The museum occupies a building from the French colonial period in the town centre, and the displays, while not slick, are well-labelled and thoughtfully organised. For anyone planning to visit Timgad, an hour here first adds useful context: you will recognise object types on site and read the inscriptions with more understanding. The Berber ethnographic section, covering traditional costume, jewellery, and domestic objects from Chaoui communities in the mountains, is a decent complement to what you will see in the villages if you venture south.
