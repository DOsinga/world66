Constantine is one of the major cities of eastern [Algeria](/africa/algeria), not far from the Tunisian border. The setting is extraordinary: the [Rhumel Gorge](/africa/algeria/constantine/rhumel_gorge), a deep canyon carved through a limestone plateau, protects the old city on almost all sides, giving it a dramatic, nearly impregnable position that no amount of urban development has managed to diminish.

The gorge can be crossed by seven bridges and viaducts. The most famous is the [Sidi M'Cid Bridge](/africa/algeria/constantine/sidi_mcid_bridge), a suspension bridge 175 metres above the river; the most imposing is the [Sidi Rached Viaduct](/africa/algeria/constantine/sidi_rached_viaduct), a 107-metre stone arch structure that was the longest of its kind in the world when built in 1912. The gorge is unfortunately polluted, which is a shame given the spectacular natural setting, but the views from the bridges remain vertiginous and unforgettable.

The [Ahmed Bey Palace](/africa/algeria/constantine/ahmed_bey_palace) is the finest Ottoman building in Algeria; the [Cirta Museum](/africa/algeria/constantine/cirta_museum) covers the Roman city of Cirta that preceded Constantine; and the [old medina](/africa/algeria/constantine/old_medina) rewards hours of wandering on its steep lanes along the gorge edge. The [Emir Abdelkader Mosque](/africa/algeria/constantine/emir_abdelkader_mosque) in the new city is among the largest in the world. Constantine has a long tradition as a centre of learning and culture in the Maghreb — this is a city where people sit in cafés for hours talking philosophy and politics, and where conversation is one of the main attractions.
