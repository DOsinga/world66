Djanet is one of the most extraordinary places in Algeria — a Saharan oasis town sitting at the foot of the [Tassili n'Ajjer](/africa/algeria/djanet/tassili_najjer) plateau, surrounded by some of the most dramatic desert landscapes on the continent. If you have any interest in the deep Sahara, this is where you come.

The town itself is small and quiet, a collection of low buildings and palm gardens fed by springs at the edge of the sand sea. The population is mostly Tuareg, and the pace of life here is unhurried in a way that feels genuine rather than posed. Djanet serves as the gateway to Tassili n'Ajjer National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site that preserves both an otherworldly sandstone landscape and one of the most important collections of prehistoric rock art anywhere in the world. The paintings and engravings, some dating back 8,000 years, depict a time when this desert was green savanna teeming with cattle, giraffes, and hippos. The hidden guelta of [El Ihrir](/africa/algeria/djanet/el_ihrir) — a permanent rock pool deep in the canyon system — holds some of the most accessible rock art on the plateau. To the southeast, the [Tadrart Rouge](/africa/algeria/djanet/tadrart_rouge) plateau offers a different desert experience: deep red sandstone pinnacles and golden dunes that have nothing to do with tourism circuits.

The [old town](/africa/algeria/djanet/old_town) quarter at the base of the cliffs gives a sense of what Tuareg oasis life looked like before concrete, with palm gardens irrigated by ancient spring channels and a small market selling silver jewellery and indigo-dyed cloth. Getting to Djanet requires commitment — flights from Algiers are infrequent and sometimes cancelled, and the overland route from the north is a genuine desert expedition. Once here, exploring the Tassili plateau requires a local guide and permits. Multi-day treks are the main draw, sleeping under the stars among rock formations that look like they belong on another planet.
