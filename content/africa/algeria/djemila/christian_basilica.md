  The large Christian basilica at Djemila was built in the fourth century, after
  Constantine's Edict of Milan legitimised Christianity in 313 AD. The baptistery
  beside it — a stunning octagonal structure with a central pool — is one of the
  best-preserved early Christian baptisteries in the world. Early Christianity
  required full-body immersion for baptism, and the pool here is large enough for an
  adult to stand in. The mosaics that once covered the floor depicted scenes meant
  to prepare the newly baptised for their new life.

The Christian quarter of Djemila occupies the southern expansion of the city, developed in the third century and adapted for Christian use in the fourth. The basilica is a large three-aisled church, and while the roof is long gone, the scale of the building is impressive — this was a substantial community with the resources to build on a major scale.

The most extraordinary element is the adjacent baptistery: an octagonal structure with a central sunken pool surrounded by a beautifully preserved mosaic floor. The geometry is elegant and the construction quality is high. Interpretive panels (in French) explain the baptismal ritual and the theological significance of the octagonal form.

Together, the basilica complex represents one of the most complete early Christian sites in North Africa. It's a reminder that Djemila's story did not end with Rome's pagan monuments — the city continued to develop and change well into the Byzantine period.
