Ghardaïa is the main city of the [M'zab Valley](/africa/algeria/ghardaia/mzab_valley_overview), a UNESCO World Heritage site in the northern Sahara about 600 km south of Algiers. The valley contains five fortified cities (ksour) built by the Ibadi Mozabites over a thousand years ago, and they are among the most extraordinary urban landscapes in Africa — tiered clusters of pastel-coloured houses rising to a minaret at the summit of each hill, all designed with a strict religious and social logic that Le Corbusier studied and admired.

The [old town of Ghardaia](/africa/algeria/ghardaia/ghardaia_ksar) itself, centred on its marketplace and mosque, is the largest and most accessible of the five. [Beni Isguen](/africa/algeria/ghardaia/beni_isguen), the holiest of the ksour, is a walled city that remains conservative and closed to outsiders without a guide. [El Atteuf](/africa/algeria/ghardaia/el_atteuf), the oldest settlement dating from 1012 AD, has Saharan architecture at its most austere and beautiful. [Melika](/africa/algeria/ghardaia/melika) sits on a ridge with one of the most distinctive skylines in the region.

Visiting requires some planning. The Mozabite communities are private and conservative — modest dress is essential, photography restrictions are strict, and a local guide is mandatory for entering the old towns. The valley is best reached by domestic flight from Algiers or by shared taxi from the north.
