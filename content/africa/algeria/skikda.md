Skikda (formerly Philippeville under French colonial rule) is a coastal city in northeastern Algeria, built on a hillside overlooking a natural bay on the Mediterranean. The city has a lively port and a relatively relaxed atmosphere by Algerian standards.

The [French colonial town centre](/africa/algeria/skikda/colonial_architecture) is compact and largely intact — a grid of arcaded streets, civic buildings in Beaux-Arts and neo-Moorish style, and a central square that still anchors daily life. At the heart of it stands the [Roman theatre of Rusicade](/africa/algeria/skikda/roman_theatre), the largest Roman theatre in Algeria, built under Hadrian and a remnant of the ancient port city that preceded Philippeville by more than a thousand years.

A few kilometres west, the village and fishing port of [Stora](/africa/algeria/skikda/port_of_stora) occupies the original anchorage of the ancient city on a calm inner bay. Skikda is also an important industrial centre — one of Algeria's main liquefied natural gas export facilities is located here — though this doesn't intrude on the old town or the waterfront at Stora. The [beaches](/africa/algeria/skikda/beaches) along the Gulf of Stora are good in summer, when they fill with local families.
