Timimoun is an oasis town in central Algeria, deep in the Sahara, built on the edge of a great salt flat — the *sebkha* — that runs along the eastern fringe of the [Grand Erg Occidental](/africa/algeria/timimoun/grand_erg_occidental) dune sea. It is one of the most visually striking towns in the Algerian south: the buildings are painted in a deep, rust-red colour — a style called Soudano-Saharien architecture — set against the pale dunes and a vast [palm grove](/africa/algeria/timimoun/palm_grove) below. The [old ksar](/africa/algeria/timimoun/old_ksar) is a maze of arched alleyways and crumbling mud-brick towers.

The area around Timimoun is dotted with [smaller oasis villages](/africa/algeria/timimoun/gourara_ksour) and [sebkha viewpoints](/africa/algeria/timimoun/sebkha_viewpoint) that reward a few days of exploration. The town comes alive during the regional festival celebrating the date harvest in autumn. Timimoun is not easy to reach — it lies far from the main Saharan routes — but that distance keeps it relatively free of the tourist infrastructure that shapes visits to better-known oases like [Ghardaïa](/africa/algeria/ghardaia) or [Tamanrasset](/africa/algeria/tamanrasset). If you make the effort to get here, you are unlikely to regret it.
