---
title: When to Go
type: section
---

Tizi Ouzou has a Mediterranean mountain climate — summers are hot and dry in the valley but pleasantly cool at altitude; winters are cold and often snowy above 1000m. The best times to visit are spring (April–June) and autumn (September–October), when temperatures in the city are mild, the mountain trails are accessible, and the landscape is at its most dramatic.

**Spring (April–June)** is the prime season. The Djurdjura slopes are green after winter rains, wildflowers cover the mountain meadows, and the days are long and clear. The Berber New Year (Yennayer) is celebrated in mid-January with family gatherings and traditional foods — not a tourist event, but if you are in the region you may be invited to share in it.

**Summer (July–August)** can be uncomfortably hot in the city (38–40°C is not unusual), though the mountains provide cool relief. The annual Kabyle cultural festivals and music events tend to cluster in summer, including concerts of the traditional izlan musical tradition.

**Autumn (September–October)** brings harvest time — olive picking, fig drying — and a mellow light on the mountains. Temperatures are ideal for hiking and village exploration.

**Winter (November–March)** is wet and cold. The Djurdjura gets significant snowfall; Tikjda has basic ski facilities, but the road conditions above 1500m can be difficult. The city itself remains lively year-round.

Ramadan is observed seriously across the region; visiting during Ramadan means fewer restaurants open at lunch and a different social rhythm, but the evening breaking of the fast (iftar) is a compelling experience if you can share it with local families.
