Tlemcen sits in the hills of northwestern Algeria, close to the Moroccan border. Historically it had closer cultural ties to [Fez](/africa/morocco/fez) than to other Algerian cities, and that Moorish influence is still visible in its architecture and traditions. The city was the capital of a medieval Berber kingdom and a major centre of Islamic learning.

The [Mosque and Tomb of Sidi Bou Medien](/africa/algeria/tlemcen/sidi_bou_medien), on the outskirts of the city, is the most important sight — a beautifully decorated complex dating from the 14th century, built around the tomb of the Sufi saint who is the city's patron. The [Great Mosque](/africa/algeria/tlemcen/great_mosque) in the old town dates from 1136 and is one of the finest Almoravid buildings in the Maghreb. A short walk west, the great ruined minaret of [Mansourah](/africa/algeria/tlemcen/mansourah) marks the site of the abandoned Marinid siege city; the restored Zayyanid [Mechouar Palace](/africa/algeria/tlemcen/mechouar_palace) inside the medina holds the small archaeological museum.

The Ville Nouvelle, the French colonial quarter centred on [Place Emir Abdelkader](/africa/algeria/tlemcen/place_emir_abdelkader), is among the most handsome in North Africa — wide boulevards, arcaded buildings, and a pleasant central square. Tlemcen's altitude (about 800 metres) keeps it noticeably cooler than [Oran](/africa/algeria/oran) on the coast, making it a pleasant place to spend a few days. For an afternoon's escape from the centre, the cable car up to the [Lalla Setti plateau](/africa/algeria/tlemcen/lalla_setti_plateau) gives the classic panorama of the city.
