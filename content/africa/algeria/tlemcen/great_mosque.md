The Great Mosque (al-Jami' al-Kabir) was begun by Almoravid Yusuf ibn Tashfin around 1082 and expanded in the early twelfth century. The slim minaret was added by the Zayyanid rulers in 1236 and is decorated with the geometric brickwork and green ceramic tiles characteristic of Tlemcen. Inside, the prayer hall is a forest of horseshoe arches; the mihrab and its delicate pierced dome are the climax.

Non-Muslims are generally allowed in outside prayer times — quiet behaviour and modest dress are essential. The exterior, with the slim brick minaret rising over the medina alleys, is one of the iconic views of Tlemcen. Combine with the Mechouar Mosque and Palace, just a few minutes away.
