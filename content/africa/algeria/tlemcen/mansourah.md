story: "Mansourah was a city built outside the walls of Tlemcen by the Marinid sultans of Morocco as a permanent siege camp during their eight-year blockade of the Zayyanids in 1299–1307. The sultan Abu Yaqub Yusuf raised a complete city of his own, with mosque, palaces, and souks — and when the siege failed and the Marinids withdrew, they abandoned it. The bones survive: the great hollow square of the prayer hall, and one of the most stunning minarets in the western Islamic world."
---

Two kilometres south-west of Tlemcen, the ruins of Mansourah occupy a low rise outside the city walls. The most striking remnant is the minaret of the Great Mosque: a square brick tower forty metres high, carved with horseshoe-arched blind arcades and panels of geometric brickwork, and now split open by an earthquake at the top to reveal the staircase inside. The roofless prayer hall behind it, with two surviving rows of columns and the mihrab still in place, gives a vivid sense of the scale of the project.

The site is open without ticketing and can be reached by a short taxi ride or thirty-minute walk from the city centre. There is no shade. Late afternoon is best for the light on the minaret. Combine with a stop at the nearby tomb of Lalla Setti for a fine view across to the medina.
