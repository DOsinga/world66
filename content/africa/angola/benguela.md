Benguela is a port city on Angola's Atlantic coast, about 700 kilometres south of [Luanda](/africa/angola/luanda). Founded by the Portuguese in 1617, it was one of the main departure points for the transatlantic slave trade and later a hub for the Benguela Railway, which ran inland to the copper mines of the Congo.

The old colonial centre has some handsome Portuguese architecture in various states of repair — the [Igreja de Nossa Senhora do Pópulo](/africa/angola/benguela/nossa_senhora_do_populo) and the fortress above the harbour are worth seeing. The [waterfront promenade](/africa/angola/benguela/waterfront_promenade) has been renovated and is pleasant for walking. Benguela is quieter and more manageable than Luanda, and the beaches to the south — particularly [Baía Azul](/africa/angola/benguela/baia_azul), about 30 kilometres away — have clean sand and warm water.

The Benguela Railway, one of Africa's great rail lines, was rebuilt with Chinese investment and reopened in 2015, running east to [Lobito](/africa/angola/lobito) and beyond toward the border. Lobito, just 30 kilometres north, has the better natural harbour and a long sandbar beach. The two cities are close enough to visit together. Angola remains an expensive and bureaucratically challenging destination, but Benguela is one of its more approachable cities.
