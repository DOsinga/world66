story: >
  The Benguela Railway was built by British engineer Robert Williams between 1903 and 1929 to carry copper from the Katanga mines of the Belgian Congo to the Angolan coast. At its peak it was one of Africa's most important rail routes, connecting the Atlantic coast to the copper belt more than 1,300 kilometres inland. The line was rebuilt by Chinese contractors and reopened in 2015 after decades of closure during Angola's civil war.
---

The Benguela Railway terminus is a fine example of early 20th-century colonial railway architecture — a whitewashed building with terracotta-tiled roofs that gives the railway a more Mediterranean than African feel. The station itself was designed to impress, reflecting the commercial ambitions of the line's British backers. Today it serves as the western terminus of what is now a functioning cargo and passenger service running east through [Lobito](/africa/angola/lobito) toward the Democratic Republic of Congo border.

Even if you're not taking the train, the station area is worth a visit. The old locomotive sheds and railway infrastructure around the station give a sense of the scale of the original operation. A train journey east from here, even for a short distance, is one of the more interesting things you can do in Angola.
