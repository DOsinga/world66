---

The Fort of São Filipe de Benguela stands on a promontory above the harbour and is the oldest surviving Portuguese military structure in the city. Originally built in the early 1600s and rebuilt in stone in the 18th century, it gives a commanding view over the bay and the modern port below. The fort's history is inseparable from the slave trade — Benguela was one of the main embarkation points for the forced transport of Africans to Brazil, and the fort was the site that controlled the harbour through which they passed.

Today the fort is a national monument. The walls and ramparts are intact, and the view from the top over the bay is worth the climb. There's limited interpretive material inside, but the setting is powerful enough to speak for itself.
