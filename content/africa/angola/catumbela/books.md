**Pepetela — *Mayombe* (1980).** A novel set during the Angolan independence war, following a MPLA guerrilla unit operating in the Mayombe forest. Pepetela, who fought in that war himself, writes with unusual honesty about ideology, solidarity, and its fractures. The book is set far from Catumbela, but it opens the door to understanding the conflict that shaped modern Angola.

**Pepetela — *The Return of the Water Spirit* (1995).** Set in Luanda and the central coast region after independence, this novel traces the decline of the idealism that drove the liberation movement through the eyes of a wealthy Angolan family. Pepetela is Angola's greatest living novelist, and this is among his most accessible works in translation.

**Ryszard Kapuściński — *Another Day of Life* (1976).** A first-hand account of Angola's 1975 civil war by the Polish journalist who stayed in Luanda as the Portuguese departed and the fighting intensified. Short, vivid, and without sentimentality — one of the best books written about Africa in conflict. The road south from Luanda through Benguela Province runs through the book's geography.

**Sousa Jamba — *Patriots* (1990).** A novel about an Angolan boy caught between the MPLA and UNITA factions during the civil war, tracing his journey across the country. Jamba grew up in Angola and writes from inside the experience; the book is a useful counterpoint to Western journalism about the war.
