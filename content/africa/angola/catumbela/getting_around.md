---
title: "Getting Around"
type: section
---

Catumbela is a small town and most of its points of interest are within comfortable walking distance of each other. The riverfront, colonial-era streets, and estuary are all reachable on foot in under 20 minutes from the centre.

**Taxis.** Private taxis are available throughout town and are the easiest option for trips to the airport or further afield. Agree on a price before setting off — there are no meters. Drivers at the market area or near the river bridge are usually easy to find during daylight hours.

**Candongueiros.** Shared minibuses run frequently along the main road connecting Catumbela to Benguela and Lobito, picking up and dropping off passengers along the route. The fare is low; ask locals for the current rate. These are the main form of public transport between the three towns and run from early morning until early evening.

**On foot.** The town centre is compact enough that walking is practical for most visits. The colonial riverfront, the railway bridge, and the market are all within a short walk of each other. Wear comfortable shoes — some streets are unpaved — and carry water in the dry season when the Benguela Current mist has burned off and the sun becomes strong.
