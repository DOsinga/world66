Huambo sits high on Angola's central plateau, a cooler, greener city once planned by the Portuguese as Nova Lisboa. That ambition is still visible in the broad civic spaces around [Praça de Agostinho Neto](/africa/angola/huambo/praca_de_agostinho_neto), though the civil war left the city battered and recovery is still uneven.

This is not an easy sightseeing city, but it has a strong identity. The [Jardim da Cultura](/africa/angola/huambo/jardim_da_cultura) is the best place to watch everyday life, the [Sé Catedral do Huambo](/africa/angola/huambo/se_catedral_do_huambo) anchors the religious centre, and [Huambo Railway Station](/africa/angola/huambo/huambo_railway_station) keeps the Benguela Railway story alive.

Huambo makes most sense for travellers already moving between [Benguela](/africa/angola/benguela), [Lobito](/africa/angola/lobito), and the interior. Give it a day if you want to understand Angola beyond the coast: planned colonial order, Ovimbundu highland culture, war damage, and rebuilding all sit close together here.
