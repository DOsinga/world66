The Port of Lobito is one of the finest natural deep-water harbours on the African Atlantic coast, sheltered by the Restinga spit and offering depths of 15 to 36 metres. It was the founding reason for the city — built as the ocean terminus of the Benguela Railway and designed to ship the copper and mineral wealth of central Africa to world markets.

The port area has a colonial-era character: warehouses, rail infrastructure, and the general bustle of a working harbour. The first phase of harbour construction completed in January 1928, and successive expansions extended the quay to over a kilometre. A large shipyard — Estaleiros Navais de Angola, once the largest in Angola — occupies part of the waterfront.

The port is not open to casual visitors, but the surrounding area and the view from the Restinga give a clear sense of its scale and activity. For anyone interested in African industrial and transport history, the story of the Benguela Railway and Lobito harbour is one of the continent's great infrastructural narratives.
