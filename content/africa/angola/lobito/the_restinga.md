  The Restinga is one of the most unusual urban geographies in Africa: a 5-kilometre
  sandbar barely wide enough for two rows of buildings, with the Atlantic Ocean on
  one side and the sheltered harbour on the other. It was on this sliver of land that
  Lobito first grew, and the original city's character — fishing families, markets,
  the smell of salt and smoke — survives here in a way it does not on the mainland.

The Restinga is the defining feature of Lobito — a long, thin sandbar spit that extends into the bay and shelters the harbour from the open Atlantic. It is one of the finest natural harbours on the West African coast precisely because of this spit, and the city grew up on and around it.

Walking the Restinga is the essential Lobito experience. The ocean is to one side, the calm harbour water to the other, and the strip between them is lined with informal restaurants, fish stalls, and the small houses of families who have lived here for generations. The catch comes in from boats beached on the ocean side, and is cleaned, grilled, and sold within metres of where it was landed.

Bring a sense of adventure and basic Portuguese — this is not a tourist district and facilities are minimal. But the atmosphere, the seafood, and the extraordinary geometry of the place make it genuinely memorable. Go in the afternoon when the light is best and the fish market is at its most active.
