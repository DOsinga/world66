Kissama (also spelled Quiçama) National Park is Angola's most accessible wildlife reserve, lying about 70 km south of Luanda on the coast. It covers around 9,600 square kilometres of savannah, wetland, and coastal habitat — a large area that was devastated during the civil war but has been gradually recovering since 2002. The park is home to elephant, giant sable antelope (Angola's national symbol), buffalo, hippo, and several antelope species. A significant project called Operation Noah's Ark in 2000–01 translocated animals from Botswana and South Africa to help restock the park after wartime poaching.

The park is most easily visited on a day trip from Luanda by private vehicle or organised tour — roads inside the park are rough and a 4WD is necessary. The dry season (May–October) is the best time for wildlife sightings; much of the park becomes inaccessible during the rains. There is a small lodge near the main entrance for those who want to stay overnight.
