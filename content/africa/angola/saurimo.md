Saurimo is the provincial capital of Lunda Sul, deep in eastern Angola's diamond country. The city sits on the Lunda Plateau at around 1,100 metres, straddling the main road from Luanda and the north-south route linking Lunda Norte — and the DRC border beyond — with Moxico province. That crossroads position gives it a restless energy: long-distance truckers and traders stop here, diamond industry workers come and go, and the market hums even in the dry-season heat.

The city was known as Henrique de Carvalho during the colonial period, named for the 19th-century Portuguese explorer who reached the Lunda royal court. Today it is the gateway to the Catoca Diamond Mine — the fourth-largest kimberlite pipe in the world — located some 35 km to the north. That mine now drives most of the local economy, and a dedicated Diamond Hub is under construction near the site to house cutting factories, a training school, and an exchange.

Away from the mining story, Saurimo is an excellent base for encountering the Chokwe people, whose homeland stretches across Lunda Sul and into the DRC and Zambia. The Chokwe are celebrated for their carved wooden masks and sculptures — the female Mwana Pwo mask and the royal Chihongo mask are the most renowned — and examples can be found in the market and from craftspeople in the surrounding villages. Prices are lower here than in Luanda.

Accommodation is limited and prices are high this far inland, as transport costs add significantly to everything. The surrounding bush is beautiful, and the flights from Luanda make the journey manageable — the airport is served by TAAG Angola Airlines several times a week on a roughly 90-minute hop.
