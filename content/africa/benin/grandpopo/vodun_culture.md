Benin is the acknowledged birthplace of Vodun — what the Western world calls voodoo — and Grand-Popo is in the heart of it. The town and its surrounding villages have active Vodun temples, shrines, and practitioners whose traditions predate the colonial era by centuries. Vodun is not a performance or a tourist attraction here; it is the religion of most of the local population.

Visits to ceremonies or meetings with practitioners are possible but must be arranged through a trusted local guide and approached with genuine respect. The annual Vodun Festival in January draws practitioners from across West Africa and the diaspora, and is the best time to visit if you want to understand the tradition's scope and seriousness. The Villa Karo, a Finnish-Beninese cultural centre in town, runs exhibitions and workshops that provide context and introduce visitors to local arts and traditions, and is a good first stop for anyone wanting to learn more.
