The southern edge of Pendjari is hemmed in by the Atakora mountains — really an escarpment of weathered sandstone and quartzite that runs across northwestern Benin and into Togo. From the park side, you see them as a wall of green hills rising abruptly out of the savanna; from the southern side, around Natitingou and the Somba villages, they are a landscape of rocky ridges, gallery forest, and small farming valleys.

The Atakora is not part of the park itself but it shapes the climate and the wildlife corridors of the wider region. Rain runs off the escarpment to feed the seasonal streams that flow north into the Pendjari River system. The road from Natitingou to Tanguiéta climbs and twists through the foothills, with the Tanougou Falls cut into the escarpment as a side trip. For travellers willing to stop and walk, the lower slopes hold villages, baobab groves, and quiet bush that is some of the most beautiful country in Benin.
