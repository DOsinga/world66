The lion you might encounter in Pendjari is not the same animal you would meet in the Serengeti. The West African lion (often grouped with the Asiatic lion in the northern Panthera leo leo clade) is smaller, with a thinner mane on the males, and lives in smaller groups in tougher country. It has been wiped out across most of its historic range over the past half century, and the WAP complex now holds the largest remaining population — perhaps 100–150 animals on the Pendjari side alone, give or take in any given year.

You are not guaranteed a sighting. Lion density is lower than in East or Southern Africa, the bush is thicker, and the animals are shy of vehicles. But the chances are real: African Parks staff and most local guides can give you a fairly current read on which prides are active where, and the waterhole circuit in March and April is where most encounters happen. A good guide and a couple of unhurried days dramatically increase your odds.
