Porto-Novo is the official capital of Benin, a compact city on the shores of a lagoon that traces its origins to the 16th century, when the Portuguese used it as a staging point for the Atlantic slave trade. That layered history gives it an architectural character unlike anywhere else in West Africa: colonial administrative buildings, a Brazilian-influenced mosque built in the shell of a church, and Yoruba palace architecture all coexist within a short walk of each other.

The heart of the old town is the [Palais Royal](/africa/benin/portonovo/palais_royal) of King Toffa, now the Musée Honmè, which gives a vivid picture of court life before colonial rule — throne rooms, royal possessions, and genealogical paintings of the Yoruba dynasty. A short distance away, the [Musée Ethnographique](/africa/benin/portonovo/musee_ethnographique) holds one of the better collections of Yoruba masks and ceremonial objects in the region. The [Brazilian Mosque](/africa/benin/portonovo/brazilian_mosque), with its ochre Afro-Brazilian facade, stands a few minutes walk away — a church built by repatriated freed slaves from Brazil, later converted to a mosque. The old city grid is best explored on foot or by zémidjan (motorcycle taxi).

Just east of town, the [Grand Marché d'Adjara](/africa/benin/portonovo/grand_marche_adjara) convenes every four days in a rotating cycle — a large open-air market drawing vendors and buyers from the surrounding villages. [Cotonou](/africa/benin/cotonou), the economic capital and transport hub, is only 30 km to the west, and [Lake Nokoué](/africa/benin/portonovo/lake_nokoe_pirogue) to the south offers pirogue trips to Ganvié, a village built entirely on stilts above the water.
