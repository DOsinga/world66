The Chobe River waterfront between Kasane and the park is the most accessible game-viewing area in northern Botswana and one of the finest in Africa. A dirt road runs along the river bank for about 15km, with multiple official viewpoints. In the morning and evening, the road is alive with animals: elephant families in the shallows, buffalo herds drinking at the bank, crocodiles on the sand, and the enormous bird life of a permanent African river.

The density of elephants here is simply extraordinary. At the dry-season peak (August–October), when animals are concentrated around the permanent water, hundreds of elephants can be visible simultaneously from a single viewpoint. The spectacle of a 200-strong herd entering the river to swim and drink — trunks raised, calves held above the water, the whole group moving with surprising coordination — is genuinely hard to absorb.

The waterfront can be driven independently in any 4WD from Kasane, but many visitors combine it with a boat safari for the river-level perspective. The combination — morning boat, afternoon drive — gives the most complete experience of the riverfront ecosystem.
