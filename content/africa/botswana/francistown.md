Francistown is Botswana's second-largest city and its oldest urban settlement, sitting over 400 km north of [Gaborone](/africa/botswana/gaborone) in the country's northeast corner. The city owes its existence to gold: in the 1860s the Tati Concession attracted prospectors in what was one of the first gold rushes in southern Africa, predating Johannesburg by two decades. It was named after Daniel Francis, who held the original mining concession, and the bones of that mining-town past still show in the older parts of the city centre.

Francistown today is mainly a transit hub, and it wears that role comfortably. The population hovers around 100,000, making it a genuinely functioning city rather than a village with a big map marker — there are decent supermarkets, fuel stations, banks, and a manageable range of accommodation. Travellers pass through heading to the Zimbabwean border at Plumtree, or north towards the [Okavango Delta](/africa/botswana/okavangodelta), [Moremi Game Reserve](/africa/botswana/moremigamereserve), and [Chobe National Park](/africa/botswana/chobenationalpark). The borders with [Zimbabwe](/africa/zimbabwe) and [Zambia](/africa/zambia) are both within a few hours' drive.

It would be dishonest to dress Francistown up as a major attraction. But if you're passing through, it merits a half-day: the Supa Ngwao Museum tells the story of the gold rush and the local Kalanga people with more care than you'd expect, and the main market has the lived-in energy of a working African town rather than anything tourist-facing. The pace is slow, the locals are friendly, and the cold Botswana beer in any bar in town is perfectly adequate compensation for the long road ahead.
