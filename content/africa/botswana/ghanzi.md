Ghanzi is a small ranching town in the central Kalahari of Botswana, the kind of place where cattle outnumber people and the horizon is flat in every direction. The town itself is basic — a few shops, a fuel station, somewhere to eat — but it is the gateway to San (Bushman) cultural experiences that offer a window into one of the oldest continuous cultures on earth.

Several community-run lodges and campsites around Ghanzi offer guided walks with San trackers. [Ghanzi Trail Blazers](/africa/botswana/ghanzi/ghanzi_trail_blazers), about eight kilometres from town, is the most established: San guides demonstrate tracking skills, fire-making, and plant knowledge acquired over generations. The D'Kar settlement 35 kilometres to the north is a centre for San art; the [Kuru D'Kar Museum and Cultural Centre](/africa/botswana/ghanzi/kuru_dkar_museum) has a working studio, gallery, and museum documenting San life, and sells original paintings and crafts at fair prices.

East of Ghanzi, the [Central Kalahari Game Reserve](/africa/botswana/ghanzi/central_kalahari_game_reserve) — one of the largest wildlife reserves in the world — begins its vast, semi-arid sweep. Lions, cheetahs, and wild dogs roam the fossil river valleys. North of town, the remote [Gcwihaba Caves](/africa/botswana/ghanzi/gcwihaba_caves) (Drotsky's Caverns) contain spectacular stalactite formations and are on Botswana's UNESCO tentative list — reaching them requires a serious 4WD expedition.

Ghanzi sits on the A3 highway between Windhoek and Maun, making it a natural overnight stop on the long drive into the Okavango Delta region. The sunsets over the Kalahari bush are extraordinary.
