The Orapa diamond mine is one of the largest diamond mines in the world by area — the open pit measures roughly 2 km by 1.5 km and descends more than 200 metres below the surface. It was the first major diamond mine to be developed in Botswana after independence, opening in 1971 on the basis of a kimberlite pipe discovery made four years earlier. The mine is operated by Debswana, the joint venture between De Beers and the Botswana government.

Access to the mine and its immediate surroundings requires a permit from Debswana, and the town of Orapa is itself gated and permit-controlled — one of the few genuinely closed company towns still operating in southern Africa. Occasional organised tours are available through the Orapa House museum, which covers the geology of diamond formation, the history of the discovery, and the development of the mine. Contact Debswana in Gaborone well in advance to arrange access.
