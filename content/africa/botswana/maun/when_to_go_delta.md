The annual delta flood is the ecological engine that makes the Okavango extraordinary. Water from the Angolan highlands flows south-east through Namibia's Caprivi Strip and into Botswana, spreading across the delta floodplain between May and August. At peak flood in July, the delta swells to roughly 15,000 square kilometres — filling channels, lifting papyrus, and driving animals onto the dry islands.

Game concentration on these islands during flood is what makes the high season (July to October) so exceptional for wildlife viewing. Herbivores converge on elevated ground; predators follow. Mokoro trips through flooded channels bring you level with the waterline in a way that no road safari can match.

For bird enthusiasts, the flood season is transformative: wattled cranes, openbill storks, jacanas, and herons exploit the shallow waters, while Pel's fishing owl hunts the deeper channels at night. The carmine bee-eater colony at the river cliffs near Maun is active from August to October, when thousands of these vivid birds excavate nest burrows simultaneously.
