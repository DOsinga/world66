Koudougou lies west of [Ouagadougou](/africa/burkinafaso/ouagadougou) on the Mossi Plateau, close enough to the capital for a long day but different enough to justify the trip. It is one of Burkina Faso's largest cities, with Moore heard everywhere, Lyèlè in the background, and a centre that still revolves around markets, courtyards, and open-air cultural spaces.

The main reason to come is not a single monument but a cluster of good anchors. The [Central Market](/africa/burkinafaso/koudougou/central_market) is an Aga Khan Award-winning piece of practical architecture, built from compressed earth blocks and still used as a commercial heart. [Rayimi Museum](/africa/burkinafaso/koudougou/rayimi_museum) gives a local view of Issouka and colonial-era memory, while the [Théâtre Populaire](/africa/burkinafaso/koudougou/theatre_populaire) and the Nuits Atypiques festival show why Koudougou has a stronger cultural identity than many towns of its size.

Architecture-minded travellers should also look south to [Lycée Schorge](/africa/burkinafaso/koudougou/lycee_schorge), one of Francis Kéré's most distinctive school projects. Koudougou is still a working Burkinabè city rather than a polished tourist stop, so the reward is texture: markets, street food, contemporary earthen architecture, and a glimpse of provincial life beyond Ouaga.
