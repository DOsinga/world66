Tiébélé is a small Kassena village of around 450 people in southern Burkina Faso, roughly 150km south of Ouagadougou near the border with Ghana. It is one of the most remarkable places in West Africa: the traditional courtyard compounds here are built from mud and laterite and painted by local women with geometric and figurative designs in white, red ochre, and black. The patterns carry symbolic meanings — fertility, protection, identity — and the whole village is repainted after each rainy season, so the houses are at their most vivid in October and November. UNESCO World Heritage recognition is in progress.

Visits are organised through the Association pour le développement de Tiébélé, whose guides are local residents with deep knowledge of Kassena culture, the building techniques, and the meaning behind the paintings. The guided tour takes in the royal court — the most elaborately decorated compound in the village — as well as residential areas. It is an intimate, unhurried experience. Note that Burkina Faso has faced serious security instability since a 2022 military coup and ongoing jihadist conflict across the Sahel; check your government's latest travel advisory before planning a trip and travel with a reputable local operator who can advise on current conditions.
