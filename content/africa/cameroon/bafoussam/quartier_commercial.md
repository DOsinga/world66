The Quartier Commercial is the commercial heart of central Bafoussam — the dense grid of streets between Marché A and the main post office where banks, phone shops, import traders, and the city's better restaurants and patisseries are concentrated. It is the most walkable part of a city that otherwise spreads across the hills in a dispersed, low-density pattern, and the busiest hour of the day here — between 8am and 10am when traders are arriving and the market is filling up — gives the best sense of how Bafoussam functions as western Cameroon's commercial capital.

The neighbourhood carries the particular energy of a highland trading city: not the coastal bustle of Douala, but something more organised and purposeful. The Hausa Quarter (Quartier Haussa), running north from Rond Point Biao toward the Grande Mosquée, occupies its own distinct corner of this area with kola nut wholesalers, Arabic-language tailoring shops, and the smell of grilled meat from the open-air restaurants that line its narrow streets.
