---
title: When to Go
type: section
---

Bafoussam sits at 1,400 metres in the western highlands, which moderates what would otherwise be a purely equatorial climate. Temperatures stay broadly between 15°C and 28°C year-round, with highland nights feeling cool enough for a light jacket even in the warmest months.

The dry season runs roughly from November to March and is the most comfortable time to visit. Roads — many unpaved outside the city — are passable, the highland landscape glows golden in the haze, and the Saturday market at Marché A is at its most vibrant. The harmattan wind arriving from the Sahara in December and January brings a dusty, drying quality that can obscure visibility across the hills but makes the air feel crisp.

The wet season from April to October brings heavy afternoon downpours, turning some roads to mud. The Bamboutos highlands turn intensely green, which is dramatic to see, but reaching the surrounding chiefdom villages and Lac Baleng becomes more challenging. Travel to Foumban and along the Ring Road north is slower in the rains, though not impossible.

The most culturally interesting periods coincide with traditional ceremonies at the chefferies — the main royal dances and ceremonies generally happen at dry season festivals and after major harvests. Asking at the Chefferie Supérieure whether any ceremony is scheduled during your visit is always worthwhile.
