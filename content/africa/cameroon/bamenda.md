Bamenda is the main city of the anglophone Northwest Region of [Cameroon](/africa/cameroon), set among the hills of the Bamenda Highlands at around 1,600 metres elevation. The altitude gives it one of the most pleasant climates in West Africa — cool, green and often misty.

The countryside around the city is spectacular — rolling hills covered in tea plantations and grasslands, dotted with traditional Tikar and Bamiléké chiefdoms. The surrounding villages have ornately decorated palaces: the [Mankon Fon's Palace](/africa/cameroon/bamenda/mankon_palace) in the southern part of the city, the [Bafut Palace](/africa/cameroon/bamenda/bafut_palace) made famous by Gerald Durrell, and the [Bali chiefdom](/africa/cameroon/bamenda/bali_chiefdom) to the west. The crater lakes of [Awing](/africa/cameroon/bamenda/lake_awing) and [Bambili](/africa/cameroon/bamenda/lake_bambili) make pleasant day trips.

Bamenda is reached by road from [Yaoundé](/africa/cameroon/yaound) (a long, rough drive). The city has an airport, but flights are rare and irregular. The main attraction is as a base for exploring the highlands and the [Ring Road circuit](/africa/cameroon/bamenda/ring_road) — a loop through the surrounding mountains, villages and crater lakes that is one of Cameroon's great drives. Note that the anglophone crisis has disrupted travel in the region since 2017; check the security situation before going.
