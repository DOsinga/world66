---
title: "Things to Do"
type: section
---

Douala is not a city that rewards conventional sightseeing. There are no great monuments, no famous skyline, no must-see museum. What it has instead is texture: the noise and energy of West Africa's busiest port city, excellent food and nightlife by Central African standards, and the unexpectedly interesting [doual'art public art trail](/africa/cameroon/douala/doual_art_public_art) scattered across its neighborhoods.

The [Musée de Douala](/africa/cameroon/douala/musee_de_douala) in the Hôtel de Ville holds the best collection of Bamoun and Bamileke traditional art outside Yaoundé. The [Marché Central](/africa/cameroon/douala/marche_central) is the sensory experience of the city distilled into one chaotic space. The [Akwa District](/africa/cameroon/douala/akwa_district) is the easiest neighborhood for walking — colonial streets, hotel terraces, and the main commercial strip along Boulevard de la Liberté. The [Cathedral of Saints Peter and Paul](/africa/cameroon/douala/cathedral_saints_peter_paul) in Bonanjo is worth a look for the contrast between European ecclesiastical architecture and the African city around it.

Outside the city, [Limbe](/africa/cameroon/limbe) (90 minutes northwest, black volcanic beaches and the wildlife center) and [Kribi](/africa/cameroon/kribi) (3–4 hours south, white sand and waterfalls) are the main day trips. Most visitors heading into the Cameroon interior use Douala's excellent rail and road connections — the train to Yaoundé takes about 4 hours and is scenic.
