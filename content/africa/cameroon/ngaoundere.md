Ngaoundéré is the northern terminus of the railway from Yaoundé, some 800 kilometres southwest, and a natural stop on the way to [Bénoué National Park](/africa/cameroon/benouepark) or Maroua in the far north of [Cameroon](/africa/cameroon). The arrival of the overnight Camrail train at [Ngaoundéré Central Station](/africa/cameroon/ngaoundere/ngaoundere_central_station) is the main event in town most mornings.

The town has few sights, but the [Palais du Lamido](/africa/cameroon/ngaoundere/palais_du_lamido) is the main attraction — especially interesting on Fridays when locals come to pay their respects to the Lamido (traditional ruler) after midday prayers at the [Grand Mosque](/africa/cameroon/ngaoundere/lamido_grand_mosque) opposite. The compact centre, which also contains a large [market](/africa/cameroon/ngaoundere/central_market), is easily explored on foot. Along the main street you will find cheap places to eat and stay, such as [L'Épi d'Or](/africa/cameroon/ngaoundere/lepi_dor), between the Grand Mosque and the Cameroon Airlines office.

Set at an elevation of 1,100 metres, Ngaoundéré has a pleasant climate compared to the lowlands. The flat-topped [Mont Ngaoundéré](/africa/cameroon/ngaoundere/mount_ngaoundere) above the town gives the city its name. Meiganga, about 30 kilometres away and 200 metres higher, is something of a hill resort. The [Ranch de Ngaoundaba](/africa/cameroon/ngaoundere/ranch_de_ngaoundaba), set around a crater lake outside town, is a popular place to stay.
