---
title: "Bars and Nightlife"
type: poi
---

Yaoundé has a lively after-dark scene, quieter and less frenetic than Douala but with its own character. The Bastos neighbourhood is where many of the city's better bars and restaurants are found — it is the diplomatic quarter, and its establishments cater to an international crowd alongside Cameroonian professionals and students. Draught Castel and 33 Export are the default beers, though locally brewed Mutzig and Brasseries du Cameroun labels are equally common.

For a more local experience, the neighbourhood bars (often simply called cabarets) scattered through Biyem-Assi, Omnisports, and other residential quartiers are where most Yaoundéans spend their evenings. Music — a mix of makossa (Cameroon's dominant popular genre), bikutsi (a more frenetic rhythm from the Beti people around Yaoundé), Congolese rumba, and Afrobeats — plays most nights. The city's arts scene has a small but active live music circuit, and venues around the Quartier du Lac occasionally host concerts and cultural events.
