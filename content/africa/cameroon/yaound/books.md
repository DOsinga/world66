---
title: "Books"
type: section
---

Cameroon has produced some of Africa's most significant literature. **Mongo Beti** (born Alexandre Biyidi-Awala in Yaoundé) is the country's most celebrated novelist; his satirical novels *Mission to Kala* and *The Poor Christ of Bomba* dissect colonialism and its aftermath with sharp wit. His later work, written after years of exile in France, remains essential reading for anyone wanting to understand Cameroonian society.

**Ferdinand Oyono's** *Houseboy* (Une vie de boy) is another classic of Francophone African literature — a brief, devastating account of colonial life told through the diary of a Cameroonian servant. For background on Cameroon's complex history, **Mark DeLancey and Rebecca Mbuh's** *Historical Dictionary of the Republic of Cameroon* is a useful reference. Travellers heading into the forests or the north may also find **Dervla Murphy's** *Cameroon with Egbert* entertaining, though her 1980s journey covers more of the country than the capital alone.
