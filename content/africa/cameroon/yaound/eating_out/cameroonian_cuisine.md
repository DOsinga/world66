---
title: "Cameroonian Cuisine"
type: poi
---

Yaoundé's restaurants and street-food stalls offer a good introduction to Central African cooking. The signature dish is ndolé — a rich, slightly bitter stew made from ndolé leaves (related to bitterleaf), ground peanuts, and fish or meat, typically served with plantain or rice. It is the national dish in all but name and appears on menus across the city. Other staples include grilled fish with pepper sauce, poulet DG (chicken braised with plantain and vegetables), and various preparations of cassava, yam, and cocoyam.

For everyday meals, the best value is often found in simple neighbourhood restaurants known as bars-restaurants, where a plate of the day comes with a starch, a protein, and a sauce for a few hundred CFA francs. The Bastos neighbourhood, where many diplomats and expats live, has a cluster of French-influenced restaurants and some Lebanese and Asian options. The central market areas are the place to graze on street food — skewered grilled meat (brochettes), fried plantain, and freshly squeezed juices.
