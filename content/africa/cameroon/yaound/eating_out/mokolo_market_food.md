---
title: "Mokolo Market"
type: poi
---

Mokolo Market is one of the largest open-air markets in Central Africa and an essential stop for anyone wanting to understand how Yaoundé actually functions. Stalls sprawl across the hillside in apparent disorder but operate with their own internal logic — textiles in one section, spices and dried goods in another, fresh produce, household items, and meat deeper in. The food section is particularly rich: mountains of dried fish, smoked crayfish, groundnuts, plantain, cassava flour, and dozens of chillies and peppers.

Around the market perimeter and through the lanes, vendors sell cooked food to the market workers and shoppers. This is some of the most honest street food in the city — not aimed at visitors, but simply good and cheap. Grilled fish, rice and beans, and fried plantain are common. Go in the morning when the market is at its liveliest and the produce is freshest. Take the usual sensible precautions with bags and valuables in a crowded space.
