---
title: "Getting Around"
type: section
---

Yaoundé's hilly geography and dense traffic make getting around more of an effort than it looks on a map. Shared taxis (taxi brousse) are the most common way to move between neighbourhoods — you flag one down, state your destination, and if the driver is heading that way you pay a fixed fare and squeeze in. For longer or more direct journeys, private taxi hire is straightforward and reasonably affordable by negotiation.

Motorcycle taxis (moto-taxis or benskins) are everywhere and fast for short hops, though the steep hills and unpredictable traffic make them a risk worth considering. Urban minibuses also run on fixed routes but can be extremely crowded. Walking is feasible in the city centre and around Bastos and the Quartier du Lac, though the hills add up quickly. Fuel costs and urban sprawl make having a driver or using ride-hailing apps (where available) a good option for half- or full-day sightseeing.
