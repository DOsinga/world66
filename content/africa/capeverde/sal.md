Sal is the flattest and most arid island in Cape Verde — a disc of volcanic rock and sand in the middle of the Atlantic with barely a tree in sight. It is also the most visited, thanks almost entirely to its international airport, which deposits European package tourists directly into the resort village of [Santa Maria](/africa/capeverde/sal/santa_maria_beach) on the south coast.

That sounds unpromising, but Sal has a genuine draw beyond the resort infrastructure. The beaches are excellent, the water is warm and clear year-round, and the consistent trade winds make the island one of the world's better spots for kitesurfing and windsurfing. And scattered around the flat interior are a few things worth seeing: the volcanic [salt crater at Pedra de Lume](/africa/capeverde/sal/pedra_de_lume) — where you float in water so salty it holds you up without effort — and the eerie rock formation at [Buracona](/africa/capeverde/sal/blue_eye) on the northwest coast, where light filters through a sea cave to create the famous "Blue Eye."

[Espargos](/africa/capeverde/sal/espargos), the island's actual capital, sits in the middle near the airport. It's a working town rather than a resort, and a better base than Santa Maria for travellers who want local prices and local food — in particular, the cachupa stew that is Cape Verde's national dish. There are daily inter-island flights from Sal to [Praia](/africa/capeverde/saotiago/praia) on Santiago and to [Mindelo](/africa/capeverde/saovicente) on São Vicente.
