---
title: "Things To Do"
type: section
---

Sao Filipe rewards the slow traveller. The town's colonial core is one of the most intact in Cape Verde, built from dark volcanic basalt and painted in faded ochres and blues. The *sobrados* — the two-story mansions of the old landowning class — line the streets near the church and the main square, their wrought-iron balconies overhanging the narrow lanes. Walk in any direction from the central praça and you'll find architecture that feels genuinely old, not restored or prettified. The pace is unhurried, the scale is human, and the volcanic cone of Pico do Fogo dominates the skyline to the north as a permanent reminder of where you are.

The town sits on a bluff above the Atlantic, and on clear days you can see Brava island floating on the horizon to the south-west. There's a covered market worth a morning, a municipal museum in a restored sobrado, a handsome parish church, and a cliff walk with the best views in town. But the real draw for most visitors is what lies beyond: the caldera of Fogo, an hour away by shared taxi, where a village rebuilt itself in the lava field left by the 2012 eruption. Sao Filipe is the place to sleep, eat, and get your bearings before heading up.
