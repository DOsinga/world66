Bangui is the capital of the Central African Republic, set on the western bank of the Oubangui River with the Democratic Republic of the Congo visible on the opposite shore. The city developed around a French military post established in 1889 and served as the administrative centre of the territory of Ubangi-Chari before independence in 1960.

With its broad avenues, surrounding hills, and riverside setting, Bangui has a certain charm that belies the country's difficult recent history. The centre of town is the [Place de la République](/africa/centralafricanrepublic/bangui/place_de_la_republique), where a Roman-style triumphal arch — a legacy of former Emperor Bokassa — faces the central market. Avenue Boganda, the main commercial street, is always busy.

The [Oubangui riverfront](/africa/centralafricanrepublic/bangui/oubangui_riverfront) is the city's best public space — pirogues crossing to the DRC, fishermen hauling nets, riverside cafés filling up in the afternoon. The [Musée de Boganda](/africa/centralafricanrepublic/bangui/musee_de_boganda), near the centre, holds a small but worthwhile ethnographic collection covering the country's many peoples. The [Marché Central](/africa/centralafricanrepublic/bangui/marche_central) is noisy, colourful, and the best place to buy fabric. The [Cathédrale Notre-Dame](/africa/centralafricanrepublic/bangui/cathedrale_notre_dame) on the riverside is a landmark, and the venue for Pope Francis's 2015 visit. [KM5](/africa/centralafricanrepublic/bangui/km5_district), five kilometres out along Avenue Boganda, is the liveliest neighbourhood for cheap food and music, though it requires local advice and caution.

Bangui is not an easy city — infrastructure is limited, security can be unpredictable, and getting around requires patience. But for travellers who make it here, it is an authentic and memorable African capital.
