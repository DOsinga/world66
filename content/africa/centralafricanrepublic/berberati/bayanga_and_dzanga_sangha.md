Bayanga is a small town about 200 kilometres south of Berberati on a rough forest road, and it is the main entry point for the Dzanga-Sangha Special Reserve — one of the most important protected forest ecosystems in Central Africa. The reserve covers 3,359 square kilometres of dense equatorial rainforest at the triple border where the CAR meets Cameroon and the Republic of Congo. Forest elephants gather at the Dzanga bai (a forest clearing and salt lick), western lowland gorillas live in the deep forest, and bongo, forest buffalo, and a vast variety of primates and birds are present throughout.

The Bayaka (Ba'Aka) people, indigenous forest hunter-gatherers, live in and around the reserve and are known internationally for their extraordinary polyphonic vocal music — a living tradition of extraordinary depth. The WWF has had a long-term presence in Bayanga, and there is a small ecolodge that can host visitors.

Getting to Bayanga from Berberati requires a 4WD vehicle and most of a day in good conditions; in the rainy season it can take much longer or become impossible. Arrange transport and accommodation in advance if possible, and check current security conditions carefully — the southern forest road has been affected by armed groups in recent years.
