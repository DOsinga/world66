The forests around M'Baïki are home to Aka (also written BaAka or Ba'Aka) communities — semi-nomadic hunter-gatherers who have lived in the Congo Basin rainforest for thousands of years. They are among the most extensively documented forest-dwelling peoples in Africa, having been the subject of anthropological and musical research since the 1970s.

Encounters with Aka communities are possible in the area around M'Baïki, but visitors should approach carefully. These communities have had decades of contact with researchers, filmmakers, and tourists, and have complex and sometimes uneasy relationships with outsiders. Going through local guides or the regional prefecture office is advisable; arriving unannounced is not. The forest visits are genuinely extraordinary — the ecological knowledge, the net-hunting practices, and the musical traditions are unlike anything you will encounter elsewhere — but they should be experienced on the community's terms.
