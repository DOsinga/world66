---
title: Eating Out
type: section
---

Eating in M'Baïki is simple and local. There are no restaurants in a formal sense — what you will find are small food stalls, market vendors, and basic eating houses (referred to locally as maquis or gargotes) serving Centralafricaine staples. Expect to eat what is available rather than what is on a menu.

The local diet is built around cassava — pounded into a stiff paste (gozo) or left as flour — served with stewed greens, fish, smoked meat, or groundnut sauce. Plantain is common, fried or boiled. River fish from the Lobaye are a regular feature. Bushmeat (game) appears frequently, though the species vary and some are from protected forest areas.

Meals are inexpensive by any standard, and guesthouse owners will usually arrange food for guests who ask in advance. Fresh fruit — pineapple, papaya, mango in season — is sold at the market.
