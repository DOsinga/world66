---
title: When to Go
type: section
---

The Lobaye region has a classic equatorial climate with two wet seasons and two dry seasons, though the pattern is not as sharply defined as in more temperate climates. The main dry season runs from November to March and is generally the best time to visit: roads are more passable, the heat is still intense but somewhat moderated, and wildlife is easier to spot at waterholes and forest clearings as vegetation opens up slightly.

The main rainy season (July to October) brings heavy daily downpours, turning unpaved roads to mud and making travel to more remote areas difficult or impossible. The road from Bangui can be particularly problematic. A shorter wet period falls around April to June, with rains less sustained than the main season.

Temperatures stay high year-round — typically 25–33°C — and humidity is consistently elevated in this equatorial zone. The forest provides shade but little relief from the humidity. Bring light, breathable clothing, strong insect repellent, and malaria prophylactics regardless of when you visit.

For the Dzanga-Sangha Reserve further south, the dry season is also preferred for wildlife-watching, particularly for seeing forest elephants at Dzanga Bai. Gorilla trekking is possible year-round but paths are easier in drier conditions.
