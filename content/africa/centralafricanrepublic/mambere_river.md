The **Mambéré River** is the defining natural feature of the Mambéré-Kadéï prefecture in the southwestern [Central African Republic](/africa/centralafricanrepublic), the watercourse that gives the region half its name. It rises in the highlands to the north and flows south and west through forested terrain near [Berbérati](/africa/centralafricanrepublic/berberati) before joining the Kadéï River close to the Cameroon border, the combined waters eventually feeding the Sangha and, far downstream, the Congo.

The river runs through the transition zone where the savanna of the central CAR gives way to dense equatorial rainforest, and its banks support a ribbon of riparian forest distinct from the drier country around it. For the communities along it, the Mambéré is a working river — used for fishing, washing, and moving people and goods by pirogue — and watching daily life on the water gives a different perspective on the region than the diamond-trading bustle of Berbérati.

For a visitor, the appeal is in the quieter stretches reachable near town. In the dry season the river level drops and sandy banks emerge, making the bank accessible for a short walk or a picnic; in the rains the water rises and the current quickens. Access is by moto-taxi or hired vehicle to the nearest road point, followed by a walk down to the water. There are no formal facilities, so bring what you need and go with someone who knows the way.

Conditions on the ground change, so ask locally or through the Catholic mission in Berbérati for current guidance on which sections are easiest to reach and safe to visit before setting out.
