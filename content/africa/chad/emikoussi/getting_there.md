---
title: "Getting There"
type: section
---

Getting to Emi Koussi is one of the hardest logistical challenges in African travel. There are no roads, no fuel depots, and no accommodation of any kind in the Tibesti Mountains. The only viable approach is by organised expedition with experienced guides.

**From N'Djamena:** The most practical starting point is the Chadian capital, 1,000+ km to the south. An expedition drive takes a minimum of 4–5 days each way across unmarked desert tracks. All fuel must be carried in jerry cans for the entire journey — the nearest fuel station is hundreds of kilometres from the mountain.

**Tour operators:** A handful of specialised tour operators run expeditions to the Tibesti, typically departing from N'Djamena or occasionally from northern Niger. Research thoroughly; local knowledge of current security conditions is essential.

**Security:** Check your government's travel advisory before planning any trip. The Tibesti has experienced armed conflict and kidnapping incidents. The security situation changes; what was safe two years ago may not be safe now. Work only with operators who have current, reliable local intelligence.

**Permits:** Access to the Tibesti requires permits from the Chadian government. A reputable tour operator will handle this.
