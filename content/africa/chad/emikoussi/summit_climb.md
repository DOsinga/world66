Emi Koussi is a shield volcano — a broad, gently sloping mountain built up by successive lava flows rather than a single explosive eruption. The climb to the rim is not technically difficult but the altitude, heat, and complete absence of infrastructure make it serious. Reaching the summit requires a multi-day expedition from the nearest settlement. The final approach crosses black volcanic scree and ancient lava fields.

From the summit, on clear days, the curvature of the Earth is perceptible across the flat Saharan plains stretching in every direction. The caldera itself is 15 kilometres wide — one of the largest in Africa — and contains a smaller, deeper depression called the Trou au Natron, stained white and orange by evaporite minerals. The silence at the summit is absolute.
