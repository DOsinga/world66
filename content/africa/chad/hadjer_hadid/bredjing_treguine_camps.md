Bredjing and Treguine are two large refugee camps near Hadjer Hadid, established in 2003 to house Sudanese refugees fleeing the Darfur conflict. Together they have housed tens of thousands of people over the course of two decades, making them among the longer-established refugee settlements in the Sahel. International organisations including the UNHCR, MSF, Oxfam, and the Red Cross maintain a presence in and around the camps.

The camps are not places for tourism or casual visits. Access is restricted and visitors generally need accreditation through an international organisation or specific permission from Chadian authorities. Aid workers based here spend extended periods in the area; journalists who have reported on the camps describe a community that has developed its own internal economy, social structures, and even schools, within the constraints of displacement. If you are traveling through eastern Chad in a humanitarian capacity, this is the primary point of reference for the area.
