The larger of the two lake groups, Ounianga Kébir sits adjacent to the main settlement and contains the most visually dramatic water of the entire system. The group includes around a dozen lakes in close proximity, with water chemistry ranging from mildly brackish to intensely hypersaline.

**Lake Yoa** is the most studied and most famous of the group — a deep, stratified lake whose sediment layers preserve a continuous 10,000-year climate record of the Sahara. Swiss scientists have cored the lakebed and extracted a chronology of wet and dry periods reaching back to the African Humid Period. The surface water is highly saline; below a density boundary, anoxic water sits undisturbed and has accumulated organic sediment without disturbance for millennia.

**Lake Katam** is smaller and more accessible from the shore, with striking reddish water caused by blooms of the salt-tolerant alga *Spirulina*. The contrast between the orange-tinged water and the surrounding dunes is one of the most photographed images from this part of Chad.

Ounianga Kébir town itself has a small market, a fuel supply that is sometimes available, and a military post where permits must be registered. There is no hotel — travelers camp.
