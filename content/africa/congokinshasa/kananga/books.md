**The Shadow of the Sun** by Ryszard Kapuściński — The Polish journalist's dispatches from across Africa, including the Congo during the tumultuous post-independence years. Vivid and personal, it conveys better than most history books what daily life during that period felt and looked like.

**Congo: The Epic History of a People** by David Van Reybrouck — The definitive single-volume history of the DRC, from pre-colonial times to the present. Van Reybrouck draws on hundreds of interviews with Congolese people of all backgrounds. Thorough, readable, and essential for anyone who wants to understand the country beyond the headlines.

**A Bend in the River** by V.S. Naipaul — A novel set in a fictional city in Central Africa, widely understood to draw on the Congo of the Mobutu era. It captures the atmosphere of post-independence uncertainty and the strange, isolated lives of those who stayed — merchants, officials, expatriates — as the world around them shifted. Controversial but powerful.

**Dancing in the Glory of Monsters** by Jason Stearns — A deeply reported account of the Congo wars of the 1990s and 2000s, focusing on the human cost borne by ordinary Congolese people. The Kasai region is among the areas affected. It explains how interconnected regional politics, ethnic grievances, and resource extraction produced some of the worst violence of the late twentieth century.
