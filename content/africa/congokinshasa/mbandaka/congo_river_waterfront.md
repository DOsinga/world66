  The pirogues (dugout canoes) that ply the Congo River here can be enormous — up to 20 metres 
  long, carved from a single tree trunk, capable of carrying a tonne of cargo or dozens of 
  passengers. Some have been fitted with outboard motors; others are still paddled by hand, 
  exactly as they have been for centuries. At the Mbandaka port, you can watch them arrive 
  from downriver villages loaded with fish, plantains, and forest products, and see the trade 
  economy of the Congo Basin in immediate, physical form.

The waterfront at Mbandaka is the city's most compelling public space. The Congo River here is enormous — several kilometres wide in places — and at dawn, before the heat rises, the quality of light on the water is extraordinary. Pirogues and river ferries arrive from upstream and downstream; traders unload fish, smoked meat, and cassava; children swim in the shallows while men repair nets. It is the kind of scene that you can sit and watch for an hour without moving.

The larger ONATRA ferry (if it is running — schedules are unreliable) docks at the main quay on its journey between Kinshasa and Kisangani, one of the great river journeys in Africa. The boat can take up to two weeks to travel the 1,700 kilometres of river between the two cities, stopping at remote villages along the way. If you have the time and the constitution for it, boarding the ferry here for even a few days is an experience with few equals. The quay area also has a small market where river fish are sold fresh and smoked.
