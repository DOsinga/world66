Abydos is one of the great ancient sites of Upper Egypt, but it has a very different rhythm from Luxor. The buses are fewer, the village is quieter, and the monuments sit in desert light beside cultivated fields. For ancient Egyptians this was sacred ground: a royal cemetery, a pilgrimage centre of Osiris, and a place where kings linked themselves to the oldest dynasties.

The reason to come is the [Temple of Seti I](/africa/egypt/abydos/temple_of_seti_i), a superbly preserved New Kingdom temple with some of the finest relief carving in Egypt. Behind it is the strange, partly sunken [Osireion](/africa/egypt/abydos/osireion), while the wider landscape includes the early royal cemetery of [Umm el-Qa'ab](/africa/egypt/abydos/umm_el_qaab) and the massive mudbrick enclosure of [Shunet El Zebib](/africa/egypt/abydos/shunet_el_zebib).

Abydos is usually visited as a long day trip from Luxor, often paired with [Dendara](/africa/egypt/dendara). That makes for a full day, but it is one of the best temple excursions in Egypt if you care about history and want a break from the crowds around Karnak and the Valley of the Kings.
