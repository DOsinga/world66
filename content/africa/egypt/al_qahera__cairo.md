Cairo is the largest city in Africa and the Arab world, a metropolis of 20 million people built around the point where the Nile emerges from its narrow valley and fans into the Delta. It is simultaneously one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world and one of the most densely crowded. The [Pyramids of Giza](/africa/egypt/al_qahera__cairo/pyramids_of_giza) rise on the western edge of the city, visible from rooftops across the metropolis — ancient Egypt's dead coexisting with one of the world's most alive modern cities.

The name al-Qāhira means "The Vanquisher" — named after the planet Mars, which was rising when the Fatimid dynasty founded the city in 969 CE. Before the Fatimids, the site had already been inhabited for millennia: Heliopolis, ancient Memphis, and the Roman fortress of Babylon all preceded Cairo on this same stretch of river. The accumulated history visible in the city's monuments — from the Pharaonic, through the Coptic Christian, Islamic, Ottoman, and colonial periods — is without parallel anywhere in the world.

Islamic Cairo, contained within a rough grid of medieval streets between the [Citadel of Saladin](/africa/egypt/al_qahera__cairo/citadel_of_salah_e) and the Fatimid northern gates, is the heart of the historical city. [Al-Muizz Street](/africa/egypt/al_qahera__cairo/al_muizz_street) runs through its centre, lined with a thousand years of mosques, mausoleums, and markets. [Khan el-Khalili](/africa/egypt/al_qahera__cairo/khan_el_khalili) has been a bazaar since the 14th century. [Old Cairo](/africa/egypt/al_qahera__cairo/coptic_cairo), a short metro ride south, contains some of the earliest Christian churches in the world. The [Grand Egyptian Museum](/africa/egypt/al_qahera__cairo/grand_egyptian_museum) at Giza, opened in 2023, is the largest archaeological museum on earth.

Cairo is not an easy city. Traffic is extraordinary, the heat from May to September is fierce, and navigating without some Arabic is genuinely difficult in parts of the city. But for the traveller willing to engage with its complexity, it rewards lavishly.
