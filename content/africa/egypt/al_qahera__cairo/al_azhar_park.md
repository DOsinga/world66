  Al-Azhar Park was built on a site that served as Cairo's rubbish dump for 500 years — a mountain of refuse 15 metres high accumulated during the medieval period. The Aga Khan Trust for Culture, which funded the park's development in the early 2000s, excavated the site and found, buried in the rubbish, a substantial section of the 12th-century Ayyubid city wall. The wall was restored and is now one of the park's historical features. The rubbish became a garden; the garden revealed history.

Al-Azhar Park is a 30-hectare green space on the Muqattam hillside east of Islamic Cairo, created on the site of a former rubbish dump by the Aga Khan Trust for Culture. It opened in 2005 and has become the most pleasant public park in Cairo — an unusual statement in a city that has very little green space.

The park's elevated position gives outstanding views over the minarets and rooftops of Islamic Cairo, with Al-Azhar Mosque and the Citadel framing the panorama. A section of the 12th-century Ayyubid city wall, discovered during the excavation of the rubbish dump, has been restored and runs through the park. The park also contains a lakeside restaurant and several cafes. Go in the late afternoon for the light on the minarets.
