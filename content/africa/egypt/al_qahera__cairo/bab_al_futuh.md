Bab al-Futuh (Gate of Conquests) and Bab al-Nasr (Gate of Victory) are the two surviving northern gates of Fatimid Cairo, built in 1087 by the Armenian general Badr al-Jamali from stones quarried at Heliopolis. They are among the finest examples of medieval military architecture in the Arab world.

Both gates can be entered and climbed: the interior chambers, walkways along the top of the old city wall, and the views out over the city are excellent. The towers of Bab al-Futuh were used as prisons, and the walls bear graffiti carved by Crusader prisoners in the 12th century — crosses, names, and dates scratched into the stone. The gates mark the northern end of Al-Muizz Street and are best visited as part of a walk down that historic thoroughfare.
