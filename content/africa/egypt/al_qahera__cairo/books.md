**Palace Walk** by Naguib Mahfouz (1956, translated 1990) — The first volume of the Cairo Trilogy, set in a traditional merchant household in Islamic Cairo in the years around the First World War. Mahfouz, who won the Nobel Prize for Literature in 1988, captures the texture of the old city with extraordinary specificity. Reading it before walking Al-Muizz Street transforms the experience.

**The Cairo Trilogy** collectively (Palace Walk, Palace of Desire, Sugar Street) — Mahfouz's masterwork, tracking three generations of a Cairo family through the first half of the 20th century. Indispensable for anyone who wants to understand the city's recent history from the inside.

**Out of Egypt** by André Aciman (1994) — A memoir of an Alexandrian Jewish family's expulsion from Egypt in the 1960s, written with aching precision about loss and belonging. More Alexandria than Cairo, but essential for understanding Egypt's cosmopolitan past.

**The Yacoubian Building** by Alaa Al Aswany (2002) — A novel set in a once-grand Downtown Cairo apartment building, whose residents represent every stratum of contemporary Egyptian society. Caustic, compassionate, and very readable — a portrait of Cairo on the cusp of revolution.
