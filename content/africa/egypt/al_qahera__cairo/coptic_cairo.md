Coptic Cairo — also called Old Cairo or Misr al-Qadima — is the oldest inhabited part of the city, built within the walls of a Roman fortress and home to some of the earliest Christian churches in the world. The Hanging Church (Al-Mu'allaqa), built over the Roman gatehouse, has been in continuous use since the 3rd century. The church of Abu Serga dates to the 5th century and is said to stand above the cave where the Holy Family sheltered.

The Coptic Museum within the compound holds the world's finest collection of Coptic Christian art — textiles, icons, manuscripts, and architectural elements spanning the 1st to 13th centuries. The compact area also contains the Ben Ezra Synagogue, converted from a church and a centre of Jewish life from the 9th century, where the Cairo Genizah — a vast archive of medieval documents — was discovered in 1896. Reached by metro (Mar Girgis station).
