  The Coptic Museum was founded in 1908 by Marcus Simaika Pasha, a Coptic Christian Egyptian statesman who recognized that Coptic art was being stripped from churches across the country and sold to foreign collectors. He spent decades lobbying the government for a dedicated museum and purchasing objects to preserve them. The collection he assembled is now the world's finest repository of Coptic Christian art — a legacy that would not exist without one man's determination.

The Coptic Museum in Old Cairo houses the world's finest collection of Coptic Christian art — textiles, ivory carvings, icons, manuscripts, and architectural elements spanning from the 1st century CE to the medieval period. It occupies a complex of historic buildings within the walls of the Roman fortress of Babylon, adjacent to the ancient churches it was built to serve.

The collection's depth is extraordinary: early Gnostic manuscripts, including texts from the Nag Hammadi Library; elaborately carved wooden screens from medieval churches; textiles showing the transition from pharaonic to Christian imagery; and a magnificent collection of Coptic icons. The building itself, with its mashrabiyya screens and courtyard gardens, is architecturally lovely. Allow 90 minutes.
