  The centrepiece of the Egyptian Museum's Tutankhamun collection is the gold death mask — 11 kilograms of solid gold, inlaid with lapis lazuli, quartz, and obsidian, crafted around 1323 BCE. Howard Carter, who opened Tutankhamun's tomb in 1922, described seeing the mask for the first time and finding his hand was shaking too badly to hold his notebook. The mask remained in the museum for nearly a century before being transferred to the new Grand Egyptian Museum at Giza in 2023.
---

The Egyptian Museum on Tahrir Square has been the custodian of ancient Egypt's treasures since 1902. Its 120 rooms house over 170,000 artefacts spanning 5,000 years, from pre-dynastic figurines to Greco-Roman mummy portraits. The collection is overwhelming in its richness, and the building's slightly chaotic, old-fashioned arrangement adds to the sense of being surrounded by accumulated centuries.

The highlights are concentrated in the Tutankhamun galleries on the upper floor: the gold death mask, the gilded throne, the alabaster canopic chest, the ceremonial daggers — each item extraordinary on its own, together forming the most complete royal burial assemblage ever found. Allow a full morning at minimum. Many major pieces, including the Tutankhamun collection, have been or will be moved to the new Grand Egyptian Museum near the Pyramids; check current status before visiting.
