  The minaret of Ibn Tulun Mosque is unique in Egypt: it has an external spiral staircase winding around the outside of the tower, a design that echoes the Great Mosque of Samarra in Iraq. The connection is not coincidental — Ahmad ibn Tulun was born in Samarra, where his father served the Abbasid court, and brought his architectural memories of Iraq when he came to govern Egypt. The mosque he built in 879 CE has outlasted almost everything else from his era.
---

The Mosque of Ibn Tulun is the oldest mosque in Cairo still in its original form — built in 879 CE and surviving largely intact for over a thousand years. It is also the largest mosque in Cairo by area, with an enormous courtyard surrounded by arcades of brick arches decorated with stucco carvings.

The design is distinctive: the mosque is built on a raised platform, entered through an outer courtyard that once separated it from the surrounding city. The spiral external staircase of the minaret is unique in Egypt. The Gayer-Anderson Museum, housed in two 16th-century houses attached to the mosque, contains a remarkable private collection of Islamic art and furnishings assembled by a British officer in the early 20th century. Both can be visited together.
