  Khan el-Khalili was established in 1382 by the Mamluk amir Jarkas el-Khalili on the site of the Fatimid royal cemetery — a politically charged act of redevelopment that erased the graves of the previous dynasty. The caravanserai he built became the commercial heart of medieval Cairo, and the market has been in continuous operation for over 600 years. Under the maze of covered alleys and copper trays, the bones of Fatimid royalty allegedly still lie beneath the foundations.

Khan el-Khalili is Cairo's great medieval bazaar, a labyrinth of covered alleys and cramped shops in the heart of Islamic Cairo. It has been a commercial centre since the 14th century, and walking its lanes — past perfume sellers, spice merchants, goldsmiths, papyrus shops, and coffee houses — feels genuinely historical even amid the tourist trade.

The market sells everything: gold and silver jewellery, inlaid wooden boxes, alabaster figurines, spices by the gram or the kilogram, shisha pipes, cotton clothing, and every kind of souvenir. Bargaining is expected. The El-Fishawy coffee house at the bazaar's centre has been open continuously since 1773 and is the place to stop for tea, coffee, and shisha in authentic surroundings. Best explored on foot, in the morning before it gets crowded.
