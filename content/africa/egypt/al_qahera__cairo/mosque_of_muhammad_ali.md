The Mosque of Muhammad Ali dominates the skyline of the Citadel of Saladin. Built between 1830 and 1848 for Muhammad Ali Pasha, the Ottoman governor who founded the dynasty that ruled Egypt until 1952, it is a deliberate statement of imperial ambition — its Ottoman-style domes and pencil minarets visible from across the city.

The interior is vast and theatrical, with alabaster cladding throughout — hence its other name, the Alabaster Mosque — and multiple hanging chandeliers. The ablutions fountain in the courtyard holds a French clock that has barely worked since it was installed. The mosque stands at the highest point of the Citadel, and the terrace beside it offers sweeping views over Cairo to the Pyramids in the west.
