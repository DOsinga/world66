  The Great Pyramid of Khufu was the tallest structure in the world for 3,800 years — from its completion around 2560 BCE until the construction of Lincoln Cathedral in England in 1311. It contains an estimated 2.3 million stone blocks, each averaging 2.5 tonnes. The precision of its construction — the base is level to within 2.1 centimetres and aligned to true north within 0.05 degrees — was not surpassed by any building in the world until modern times.

The Pyramids of Giza are the only one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World still standing, and they remain the most extraordinary man-made structures on earth. Three pyramids — Khufu (the Great Pyramid), Khafre, and Menkaure — occupy the plateau above Cairo's western edge, with the Sphinx guarding the valley below.

Whatever photographs have prepared you for, the reality is larger. The Great Pyramid is 139 metres tall and covers 13 acres at its base. You can enter the pyramids (though the interior is minimal — a long descending corridor to an empty granite chamber), hire a camel for a circuit of the plateau, or pay for the Sound and Light show after dark. The best views of all three pyramids together are from the panoramic viewpoint on the plateau's western edge. Visit at dawn for the light and to beat the crowds.
