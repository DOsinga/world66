The Step Pyramid complex at Saqqara, about 30 kilometres south of Cairo, is older than the Giza pyramids and architecturally their ancestor. Built around 2630 BCE for the pharaoh Djoser by the architect Imhotep, it is the first large-scale stone structure in human history — a series of six mastabas stacked in decreasing size to create a stepped pyramid 62 metres high.

The surrounding complex is enormous: a kilometre-long enclosure with reconstructed colonnaded halls, dummy structures, and courts for the sed festival. Many of the underground chambers are richly decorated. The recently restored pyramid is now open to visitors for the first time in decades. Saqqara can be combined with Memphis (the ancient capital's ruins, including a colossal statue of Ramses II) and the Dahshur pyramids for a full day of sites predating Giza.
