The Mausoleum of Aga Khan III stands on a hilltop on the west bank of the Nile, visible from the Aswan corniche across the water. Sir Sultan Muhammad Shah, Aga Khan III, was the 48th Imam of the Shia Ismaili Muslims and spent his winters in Aswan for decades. His tomb, completed in 1960, is built of Aswan rose granite in a style that blends Fatimid and Mughal influences.

The building is modest in scale but spectacular in situation, perched high on the west bank with views across the Nile to the city and south toward the first cataract. The interior holds a single white marble sarcophagus. Access is by boat across the Nile and then a steep climb. The surrounding area is rugged desert and the walk to the hilltop is best attempted in the early morning or late afternoon.
