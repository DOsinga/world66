The Fatimid Cemetery is a stark, beautiful spread of mud-brick domes and tombs on the edge of Aswan. The cemetery dates back to the early Islamic centuries and was used for a long period, leaving a landscape of small mausoleums rather than a single grand monument.

It is not as immediately spectacular as Philae or the Unfinished Obelisk, but it gives Aswan a deeper historical range. The dry light, rough brick, and quiet rows of graves make a strong impression, especially if you come with an interest in Islamic architecture or the city's role beyond the pharaonic story.
