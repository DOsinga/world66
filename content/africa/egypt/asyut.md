Asyut is Egypt's largest city in Upper Egypt outside of Luxor, sitting on the west bank of the Nile about 380 km south of Cairo. It's primarily a transport and commercial hub rather than a tourist destination, but it does have some points of interest for those passing through.

The city is home to [Assiut University](/africa/egypt/asyut/assiut_university), one of Egypt's oldest, which gives it a young, relatively lively atmosphere. The nearby [Deir el-Muharraq Monastery](/africa/egypt/asyut/deir_el_muharraq), located about 50 km northwest, is a significant Coptic Christian pilgrimage site believed to mark a resting place of the Holy Family during their flight into Egypt. The cliff-side [Monastery of the Virgin at Durunka](/africa/egypt/asyut/durunka_monastery) draws over a million pilgrims for its August moulid.

The area around Asyut has ancient roots — the nearby [Meir tombs](/africa/egypt/asyut/meir_tombs) contain rock-cut burials from the Old and Middle Kingdom periods, and the ancient city of Lycopolis once stood here. In town, the [Alexan Pasha Palace](/africa/egypt/asyut/alexan_pasha_palace) and the [Assiut Barrage](/africa/egypt/asyut/assiut_barrage) on the Nile are worth a look. Most travellers stop only briefly, but Asyut is a reasonable overnight break on the long journey along the Nile valley.
