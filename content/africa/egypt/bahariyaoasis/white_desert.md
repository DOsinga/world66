The White Desert National Park (Sahara el-Beyda) lies about 120 km south of Bahariya Oasis, just north of the town of Farafra. It is the most visually striking desert landscape in Egypt — a plain of pale yellow sand dotted with thousands of chalk formations sculpted by wind erosion into shapes that seem impossible in nature: mushrooms, arches, sitting rabbits, recumbent camels, and towers of brilliant white chalk rising against a bare sky.

Most visitors experience the White Desert as an overnight camping trip from Bahariya. 4WD vehicles from Bawiti drive south through the Black Desert, stop for sunset at the Crystal Mountain (a natural quartz crystal arch on the desert road), and then camp in the White Desert for the night. The chalk formations are most atmospheric at sunrise and sunset when the slanting light picks out the textures, and by moonlight when the white rock glows. The park covers 3,010 km² and the interior is entirely uninhabited; designated camping areas are scattered through the accessible sections.
