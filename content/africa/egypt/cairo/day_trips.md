Cairo makes an excellent base for day trips, and the greatest of them all is barely an hour away. **[Giza](/africa/egypt/giza)**, the western half of greater Cairo across the Nile, holds the last surviving Wonder of the ancient world — the three Great Pyramids and the Sphinx on their desert plateau — along with the new [Grand Egyptian Museum](/africa/egypt/cairo/grand_egyptian_museum), the largest archaeological museum on earth. Go early, before the tour buses and the camel touts, and give it at least half a day.

For something older and quieter, head 30 km south to **[Saqqara](/africa/egypt/saqqara)**, the necropolis of ancient Memphis. The Step Pyramid of Djoser here predates Giza by a century, and the painted tombs of the nobles are among the finest in Egypt. It combines easily with the open-air ruins of Memphis and the pyramid fields of Dahshur into a single half-day outing.

Further afield, Alexandria is three hours by train and worth an overnight for its Mediterranean light and Greco-Roman past. The desert oases — Siwa, Bahariya, Farafra — require more time but are reachable with a rental car or organized tour from the city.
