Downtown Cairo (Wust el-Balad — literally "heart of the city") is the European-influenced commercial centre built by Khedive Ismail in the 1860s–1870s as he reimagined Cairo as a Parisian-style capital. The street grid is regular, the buildings are limestone facades in Italianate and neo-classical styles, and the main boulevards — Talaat Harb, Qasr el-Nil, and 26th July — are lined with cafés, bookshops, and the kind of unglamorous commerce that makes a city function.

Tahrir Square anchors the district to the south, with the Egyptian Museum on its northern edge. Downtown is the city's densest concentration of mid-range restaurants, ahwas (traditional coffee houses), street food vendors, and the kind of everyday Cairo life that tourist itineraries centred on the pyramids or Islamic Cairo tend to skip. The Nile corniche is a ten-minute walk west.
