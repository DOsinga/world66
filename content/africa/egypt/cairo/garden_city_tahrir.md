Tahrir Square (Liberation Square) is Cairo's central public space and the symbolic heart of modern Egypt. Built in 1865 as Ismailia Square and renamed after the 1952 revolution, it gained worldwide attention as the gathering point for the 2011 uprising that ended Hosni Mubarak's 30-year rule. On the square's northern edge stands the Egyptian Museum; to the south is the Garden City diplomatic quarter; to the west the Nile and Zamalek; to the east the dense commercial streets of downtown.

The square was restructured in 2021, with a new central obelisk and landscaping. An ancient Rameses II statue was relocated here from central Cairo. The square is not the pedestrian public space that many cities' central squares are — it remains a traffic roundabout — but its historical resonance and central location make it an obligatory landmark on any Cairo visit.
