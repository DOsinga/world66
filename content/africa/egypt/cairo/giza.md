Giza sits on the desert plateau just west of Cairo, separated from the city by the Nile but reachable in 20 minutes by taxi. It holds the only surviving Wonder of the Ancient World — three great pyramids built for the pharaohs Khufu, Khafre, and Menkaure — along with the Great Sphinx and, since 2024, the Grand Egyptian Museum.

The pyramids are best seen on foot, from the plateau itself. The standard route takes you past all three and gives you the classic panoramic view with all four monuments in a single frame. You can enter the Great Pyramid of Khufu for an extra fee — the narrow, steeply raked passage to the burial chamber is claustrophobic but worth the experience once. The Solar Boat Museum, adjacent to the Great Pyramid, houses one of the world's oldest intact wooden vessels, discovered in pieces in a sealed pit beside the pyramid in 1954.

The Grand Egyptian Museum, which opened its full galleries in 2024, sits at the plateau's northern edge within sight of the pyramids. It is one of the largest archaeological museums in the world, built specifically to house the complete contents of Tutankhamun's tomb and the transferred collections of the Egyptian Museum in Tahrir Square. The building's sloping glass-and-stone facade was designed to frame the pyramids from inside.

Go early — the site opens at dawn, and the morning light on the pyramids before the tour buses arrive is worth the effort. The hawkers and camel touts around the entrance are persistent; a firm no and a short walk are enough. The sunset from the plateau is also exceptional, but expect crowds.

The Saqqara necropolis, 15 km south, holds the Step Pyramid of Djoser — the world's oldest large stone structure — and is a worthwhile half-day side trip from here.
