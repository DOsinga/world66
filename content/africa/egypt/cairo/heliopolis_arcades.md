The commercial arcades along Ibrahim Laqqany Street and the surrounding streets of old Heliopolis are the best-preserved part of Édouard Empain's 1906 garden city — covered walkways in Moorish revival style, supported by horseshoe arches and decorated with tile and carved stucco. The arcades were designed to provide shade for pedestrians and to give the district a continuous Mediterranean-Islamic character. Many have been adapted into shops and cafés while retaining their original form.

Walking the arcaded streets in the early evening — when the cafés are open and the light hits the arches at a good angle — is the best way to understand what Empain was attempting to create. The mix of architectural influences (Moroccan, Mamluk, Venetian Romanesque) is genuinely eccentric and unlike anything elsewhere in Egypt. The area around Al-Ahram Street has the most intact surviving examples.
