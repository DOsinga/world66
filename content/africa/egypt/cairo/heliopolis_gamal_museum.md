The Gamal Abdel Nasser Museum in Heliopolis is housed in the villa where Egypt's president and pan-Arab leader lived from 1952 until his death in 1970. The museum preserves his personal effects, gifts from world leaders, official correspondence, and documents relating to his presidency — including the Suez Crisis, the formation of the United Arab Republic, and the aftermath of the 1967 war. Personal rooms, including his study and the room where he died, are maintained as they were during his lifetime.

For anyone interested in Egyptian or Arab political history, this is one of Cairo's more evocative museums — the scale of Nasser's personal collection of gifts from foreign leaders reflects the extent of his international influence. The villa is modest compared to similar presidential shrines, which adds to rather than detracts from its authenticity.
