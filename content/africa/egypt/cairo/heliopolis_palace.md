The Heliopolis Palace Hotel is the grandest of Empain's architectural gestures — a vast Moorish-baroque complex of 750 rooms, domes, and arcaded galleries, opened in 1910 as a luxury hotel intended to attract Europe's aristocracy to his new desert city. It worked: the hotel was one of the grandest in the world for its first decade. It has since been converted to the Egyptian presidential palace and is no longer accessible to visitors, but the exterior — visible from the surrounding streets — remains one of Cairo's most remarkable buildings.

The facade along Ibrahim Laqqany Street reveals the full scale of Empain's ambitions: the ornate Moorish arcades, the multiple domes, and the sheer length of the building create an impression somewhere between a palace and a mirage. The surviving commercial arcades around the hotel are the most intact remnant of Empain's planned garden city.
