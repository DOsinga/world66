Mohandiseen (Mohandessin, meaning "Engineers" — it was developed as housing for Egypt's engineering class in the 1960s) is a dense residential district on the Giza side of the Nile, west of Zamalek. The grid of apartment blocks is unremarkable architecturally, but the neighbourhood punches above its weight for food and nightlife: Shehab Street and the surrounding lanes have a concentration of restaurants, juice bars, and cafés that serve a mostly Egyptian middle-class crowd rather than tourists.

The bar scene here — centred on Gameat el-Dowal el-Arabeyya Street — is accessible and less polarised than the expat-focused bars of Zamalek or Maadi. Several of Cairo's better Lebanese restaurants are in Mohandiseen, along with Egyptian fast food, shawarma stalls, and ahwas. It's a 15-minute drive from downtown or a short ride across the Nile bridge.
