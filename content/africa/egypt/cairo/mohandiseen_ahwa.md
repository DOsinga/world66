The ahwas (traditional coffee houses) of Mohandiseen are the best introduction to this Egyptian institution for visitors — informal outdoor or semi-covered spaces where men (and increasingly women) gather to drink tea or coffee, smoke argileh (shisha), play backgammon or cards, and watch Egyptian TV or football. Unlike the Western-style cafés of Zamalek, ahwas are a genuinely Egyptian social institution with their own customs and rhythms.

The tea is typically mint tea or black tea with sugar — often very sweet. Coffee comes as Turkish-style (strong, sediment-settled in the cup) or Nescafé (instant, mixed with hot milk). Argileh is available in apple, mint, and many other flavours. The ahwa experience is best after 9 pm, when the whole neighbourhood moves outdoors and the footpaths become extensions of the café seating. Prices are minimal — LE 10-20 for tea and argileh combined.
