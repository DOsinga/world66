The National Museum of Egyptian Civilization is one of Cairo’s essential museums, especially for travellers who want the long sweep of Egyptian history rather than only pharaohs and pyramids. Its galleries move from prehistoric and ancient Egypt through Coptic, Islamic, and modern periods, giving the city’s layered identity a clearer frame.

The headline draw is the Royal Mummies Hall, where New Kingdom rulers are displayed with unusually sober interpretation. It is best visited separately from the Egyptian Museum or the Giza plateau; the museum sits in Fustat, near Coptic Cairo, and pairs well with the Coptic Museum, Abu Serga, and the older churches around Babylon Fortress.
