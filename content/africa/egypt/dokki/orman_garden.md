The Orman Garden is one of the most pleasant green spaces in Cairo — a proper botanical garden covering about 15 acres near Cairo University, with labelled trees, shaded walks, and an atmosphere that makes the city noise feel distant. The collection includes trees from across the tropical world, brought to Egypt during the late 19th century when the country had the resources and the botanical connections to build a serious collection.

On weekdays it is relatively quiet, with university students reading under the trees and families on benches. On Fridays it gets busy with local families. The garden is closed on Thursdays. Admission is cheap and the combination of shade, birdsong, and the smell of flowering trees is a genuine respite from Cairo's intensity.
