El Alamein is a small city on Egypt's Mediterranean coast, famous for the 1942 battles that stopped the Axis advance toward Alexandria and the Nile. The modern town is plain, and the new resort developments along the coast can feel detached from the history, but the war cemeteries and memorials make this one of Egypt's most serious and worthwhile stops for twentieth-century history.

The sites are spread along the Alexandria-Marsa Matruh road. The [Commonwealth War Cemetery and Alamein Memorial](/africa/egypt/el_alamein/commonwealth_war_cemetery_and_alamein_memorial) is the emotional centre, with thousands of graves and a memorial to soldiers whose remains were never found. Nearby, the [El Alamein Military Museum](/africa/egypt/el_alamein/el_alamein_military_museum) explains the North African campaign with vehicles, weapons, maps, and outdoor displays.

West of town, the [German War Cemetery](/africa/egypt/el_alamein/german_war_cemetery) and [Italian War Cemetery](/africa/egypt/el_alamein/italian_war_cemetery) give the visit a wider frame. Add the Greek-Roman ruins of [Leukaspis](/africa/egypt/el_alamein/leukaspis), and El Alamein becomes a strong day trip from [Alexandria](/africa/egypt/alexandria) rather than just a name from a history book.
