Giza is the western half of greater Cairo — a teeming city in its own right of over eight million people, sprawling up to the desert edge where the **[Great Pyramids](/africa/egypt/giza/great_pyramid)** stand on their plateau. The pyramids are what everyone comes for, and they deserve it: these are the last surviving Wonder of the ancient world, the **Pyramid of Khufu** (Cheops) alone containing more than two million limestone blocks and older than almost anything else still standing on earth. The [Great Sphinx](/africa/egypt/giza/sphinx) crouches just below, weather-eaten and half the height of its pyramid, staring east toward the Nile.

The [Giza Plateau](/africa/egypt/giza/giza_plateau) is both overwhelming and frustrating. The monuments are genuinely awe-inspiring, but the edge of the city now laps right up to them and the area is a circus of touts, camel handlers, and persistent "guides". Arrive early — ideally at opening, around 8 am — buy your ticket, hire a guide or firmly decline all offers, and walk the complex in the cool of the morning. Beyond the three famous pyramids, the [Solar Boat Museum](/africa/egypt/giza/solar_boat_museum) and the [Valley Temple of Khafre](/africa/egypt/giza/valley_temple_khafre) are essential additions. The new **[Grand Egyptian Museum](/africa/egypt/giza/grand_egyptian_museum)**, opened nearby, collects the best of the pharaonic artefacts previously scattered across Cairo, and makes a natural half-day companion to the pyramids.

Beyond the plateau, Giza is also a city with its own character: the university district around [Cairo University](/africa/egypt/giza/cairo_university) is where genuine Egyptian daily life happens at its most vivid, and the Nile corniche offers felucca rides and fish restaurants far from the tourist circuit. The [Taha Hussein Museum](/africa/egypt/giza/taha_hussein_museum) and the extraordinary [Mohamed Mahmoud Khalil Museum](/africa/egypt/giza/mahmoud_khalil_museum) — Impressionist paintings in a Nile-side villa — are two of the city's less-visited but genuinely worthwhile stops.

The older pyramid fields at **Saqqara** (the stepped pyramid of Djoser, oldest of them all) and **Dahshur** (the Red and Bent pyramids) make for a quieter and in some ways more rewarding day trip than the main site. They are 30 and 40 km south of Cairo respectively and best combined into one trip.
