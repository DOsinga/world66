The pyramid fields of **Saqqara** and **Dahshur** lie 30 and 40 km south and are the essential day trip from Giza. [Saqqara](/africa/egypt/cairo) holds the **Step Pyramid of Djoser**, the oldest monumental stone structure on earth (c. 2670 BC), and the astonishing painted reliefs of the **Mastaba of Mereruka**. Dahshur has the **Bent Pyramid** — built with a corrected angle partway up, as the engineers realised the original slope was too steep — and the **Red Pyramid**, the first true smooth-sided pyramid ever built. A taxi for the day from Cairo or Giza, with time for both sites, is the sensible way to see them; the two sites are only 10 km apart.

**[Alexandria](/africa/egypt/alexandria)** is 220 km north and doable as a long day trip by train — the Library of Alexandria, the Roman catacombs of Kom el-Shoqafa, and a seafood lunch on the corniche — though an overnight is better. Trains run frequently from Cairo Ramses station and the journey takes around 2–2.5 hours.

**Abu Rawash**, 8 km north, holds the ruined [Pyramid of Djedefre](/africa/egypt/giza/pyramid_of_djedefre) — Egypt's most northerly pyramid, largely destroyed but dramatically sited on a high ridge. It gets almost no visitors and requires a taxi; combine it with the Giza visit rather than making it a dedicated trip.
