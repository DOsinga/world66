The most visible building in central Hurghada is the El Mina (Port) Mosque, a large white-walled mosque with two slender minarets and a turquoise main dome standing on the seafront between Sakkala and the marina. Finished in the late 1990s and substantially expanded since, it is built in a contemporary Mamluk style — pointed arches, banded stonework, plaster filigree on the minaret balconies — and serves as the city's main Friday mosque. The interior carpet is striking, with the qibla wall picked out in green marble, and the exterior plaza is a good place to come at sunset when the call to prayer rolls along the coast. Non-Muslim visitors are welcome outside prayer times; remove shoes and dress modestly.
