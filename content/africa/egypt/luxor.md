Luxor is built on top of ancient Thebes, and the weight of that history is everywhere. This was the capital of Egypt during the New Kingdom — arguably the most powerful civilization in the ancient world — and what remains is staggering: two enormous temple complexes on the East Bank, and across the Nile, an entire necropolis carved into the limestone cliffs of the West Bank. Few places on earth have this density of monumental architecture.

The Nile divides everything here. The [East Bank](/africa/egypt/luxor/east_bank) is the living city, where [Luxor Temple](/africa/egypt/luxor/temple_of_luxor) sits right on the Corniche and the vast complex at [Karnak](/africa/egypt/luxor/temple_of_karnak) is a 20-minute walk away along a partly-excavated [Avenue of Sphinxes](/africa/egypt/luxor/avenue_of_sphinxes). The [West Bank](/africa/egypt/luxor/west_bank) is quieter, more agricultural, and still overwhelmingly ancient: the [Valley of the Kings](/africa/egypt/luxor/valley_of_kings), the [Valley of the Queens](/africa/egypt/luxor/valley_of_queens), the [Temple of Hatshepsut](/africa/egypt/luxor/temple_of_hatshepsut), and the [Colossi of Memnon](/africa/egypt/luxor/colossi_of_memnon) are all within a few kilometers of each other.

The modern city is functional and not particularly charming, but it has everything you need. The tourist infrastructure is well-developed, which also means persistent hassle from vendors and touts near the main sites — it's worth accepting early on and not letting it ruin the experience. Prices for almost everything are negotiable.

Plan for at least two full days: one for each bank. Early starts are rewarded — the light is better, the heat is manageable, and the sites are quieter before the tour groups arrive. A [hot-air balloon ride](/africa/egypt/luxor/hot_air_balloon) at sunrise over the West Bank is genuinely worth doing if conditions allow. The [Luxor Museum](/africa/egypt/luxor/luxor_museum) and the [Mummification Museum](/africa/egypt/luxor/mummification_museum) on the East Bank Corniche fill the gaps between outdoor sights.
