The Temple of Edfu, 115 kilometres south of Luxor, is the best-preserved ancient temple in Egypt and one of the most impressive. Built by the Ptolemies between 237 and 57 BC, it is a complete complex — the outer walls, the great pylon, two courtyards, hypostyle halls, and the innermost sanctuary all standing to their full height. Walking through the sequential spaces from the great pylon to the naos gives the clearest possible sense of how an Egyptian temple actually functioned.

The temple is dedicated to Horus and is decorated with a complete mythological text describing the victory of Horus over Seth — effectively the ancient Egyptian epic of good defeating evil. The reliefs cover every surface, from scenes of royal rituals in the outer court to the most intimate texts of the inner sanctuary. Allow two to three hours. The site is easily reached by taxi from Luxor, or as a stop on a Nile cruise. The horse-drawn carriage rides to and from the site are an unavoidable tourist feature; agree on a price firmly.
