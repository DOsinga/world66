Hot air balloon flights over Luxor's West Bank at sunrise are one of Egypt's great tourist experiences, and among the few that genuinely live up to their reputation. Flying at low altitude over the temples and desert as the sun rises behind the cliffs gives a perspective on the scale and distribution of ancient Thebes that is impossible from the ground. On a clear morning, with the Valley of the Kings, the Temple of Hatshepsut, and Medinet Habu all visible below, the effect is extraordinary.

Dozens of operators offer flights, typically departing before sunrise and landing after an hour in the air. Prices vary; book through a reputable company rather than through touts at the temple gates. Flights are weather-dependent and can be cancelled at short notice — early October through April is the most reliable window. The safety record for Luxor balloon operations has been mixed; use a company that has been operating for several years and check recent reviews. Go if conditions allow: this is one of the things that people remember from Egypt.
