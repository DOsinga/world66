Malkata is the sprawling remains of the palace complex of Amenhotep III, built around 1390 BC on the West Bank of the Nile south of Medinet Habu. In its time it was one of the largest buildings in the ancient world, covering roughly 80 hectares and comprising multiple palaces, a royal harbour, and an entire support city. What remains today is mainly mud-brick foundations and scattered fragments — not a conventional tourist sight, but a powerful reminder of ancient scale.

The site is unexcavated in most areas, with ongoing archaeological work by Egyptian and international teams. Visiting requires a walk across rough terrain, and there is nothing spectacular to see without an archaeologist's eye. The reason to come is the scale: standing in the ruins of a building complex that covered the equivalent of several city blocks, knowing that it housed the most powerful monarch on earth for decades, gives a different kind of understanding than the restored temples offer. Access is free but there are no facilities and no shade.
