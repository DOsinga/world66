The Mortuary Temple of Seti I stands near the edge of the cultivation on the West Bank, backed by the cliffs that separate the agricultural land from the necropolis. It is less famous and less visited than Hatshepsut's temple or Medinet Habu, which makes it one of the more rewarding places on the West Bank for travellers who want something quieter and more contemplative.

The reliefs inside are among the finest of the New Kingdom period — delicate and precise in a way that differs from the bombastic battle scenes that Ramses II preferred. The astronomical ceiling in the hypostyle hall, painted with figures representing constellations, is particularly worth finding. The temple garden is pleasant in the early morning, with the cliffs behind and the Nile plain in front. Combine this with the nearby Colosseum of Memnon, which is only ten minutes' walk away.
