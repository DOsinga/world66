Minya is a mid-sized city on the Nile roughly halfway between Cairo and Asyut, and most travelers pass straight through — which is a mistake. The surrounding area contains some of the most vivid ancient Egyptian remains in the country, away from the crowds of Luxor.

The rock-cut tombs at [Beni Hassan](/africa/egypt/minya/beni_hassan), across the river, are decorated with extraordinarily well-preserved painted scenes of daily life from the Middle Kingdom period — wrestlers, dancers, hunting scenes, agricultural work, all in astonishing colour. Further south, [Tell el-Amarna](/africa/egypt/minya/tell_el_amarna) is the site of the short-lived capital built by the heretic pharaoh Akhenaten in his attempt to impose monotheism on Egypt; the remains of his city lie in the desert plain and are eerie in their emptiness. [Tuna el-Gebel](/africa/egypt/minya/tuna_el_gebel), the necropolis of ancient Hermopolis, contains a strange mixture of ibis and baboon burials alongside a well-preserved Graeco-Roman tomb complex. The ruins of [el-Ashmunein](/africa/egypt/minya/el_ashmunein) (ancient Hermopolis itself) have surviving columns from a temple of Thoth and a Christian basilica built from pharaonic blocks. The unusual step [Pyramid of Zawyet el-Maiyitin](/africa/egypt/minya/pyramid_of_zawyet_el_maiyitin), just south of the city, is one of the oldest provincial pyramids in Egypt.

Minya has a substantial Coptic Christian population and a history of intercommunal tension, but it is a functioning city with reasonable facilities for travelers using it as a base for the archaeological sites.
