Safaga sits on the Egyptian Red Sea coast about 53 km south of Hurghada, and it's one of those places where the working port and the tourist town have grown up side by side without quite resolving the tension between them. Container ships and ferries bound for Saudi Arabia share the waterfront with dive boats heading out to some of the Red Sea's most reliably excellent reefs. That combination gives Safaga a gritty, unpretentious character that many travellers find refreshing after the resort-bubble polish of its neighbours.

The real draw is the water. The offshore reefs around Safaga — Seven Pillars, Panorama Reef, Abu Kifan, and Gamul Kebir among them — are consistently rated among the best in the Egyptian Red Sea, with strong currents that bring in pelagic fish and keep the walls colourful. Walls drop hundreds of metres here; reef sharks, Napoleon wrasse, barracuda, and turtles are routine sightings rather than lucky ones.

The same relentless northerly wind that makes the diving slightly trickier also makes Safaga one of the top windsurfing and kitesurfing destinations in Egypt. The wide lagoon at Soma Bay, just north of town, gives beginners flat water while advanced riders chase the chop outside. Peak wind season runs roughly May through October, with the best consistent conditions in summer when afternoon gusts regularly hit 25–30 knots.

On land there's little in the way of sightseeing — Safaga is not a city of monuments. It's a place you come to be in the water, and the laid-back pace suits that agenda well. The ferry terminal adds an unexpected dimension: if you're heading to or from Saudi Arabia by sea, this is where you cross.
