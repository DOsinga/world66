title: "Books"
type: section
---

The southern Sinai has produced a small but interesting literature. Three books between them give a good sense of the place beyond the resort fence:

**Sinai: The Great and Terrible Wilderness** by Burton Bernstein. A 1979 New Yorker piece expanded into a book, tracing the journey of the Israelites — and of the author — across the peninsula. Dated in its politics but extremely readable on the landscape and the people.

**The Bedouin of Mount Sinai** by Emanuel Marx. An anthropologist's study of the Jebeliya tribe who have lived in the high mountains around St. Catherine's monastery for fifteen hundred years. Clear-eyed about how Bedouin life has changed under successive occupations and the arrival of tourism.

**Sinai: A Physical Geography** by Ben-Avraham et al. Drier than the others, but indispensable for understanding why the landscape looks the way it does — the volcanism, the granite, the rifting that opened the Red Sea. Worth dipping into before a trip into the interior.

**Beyond the Reef: The Underwater World of the Red Sea** by Bernhard Edmaier. A coffee-table volume of aerial and underwater photography that captures the strange, vivid quality of the Red Sea biome better than most books on the subject. Good preparation for the snorkelling.
