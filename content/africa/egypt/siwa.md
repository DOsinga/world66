Siwa is an oasis town deep in the Western Desert of [Egypt](/africa/egypt), close to the Libyan border and about 560 km from [Cairo](/africa/egypt/cairo). It is one of Egypt's most remote and distinctive destinations — a place where Berber culture persists, locals speak Siwi rather than Arabic, and the pace of life has little in common with the Nile Valley.

The oasis sits in a depression surrounded by sand, fed by hundreds of freshwater springs. The old town, [Shali](/africa/egypt/siwa/shali_fortress), is a crumbling mud-brick fortress perched on a hill, slowly dissolving after a rare rainstorm in 1926. Below it, palm groves and olive orchards stretch across the oasis floor. The [Temple of the Oracle](/africa/egypt/siwa/temple_of_the_oracle), where Alexander the Great is said to have been proclaimed a god, stands at the edge of town.

[Cleopatra's Spring](/africa/egypt/siwa/cleopatras_spring) is a popular swimming spot — a clear pool surrounded by palms. Beyond the town, the [Great Sand Sea](/africa/egypt/siwa/great_sand_sea) offers some of Egypt's best desert scenery, and local guides run trips by 4WD into the dunes.
