Equatorial Guinea is the only Spanish-speaking country in Africa, a small nation of two parts: a mainland territory (Río Muni) between Cameroon and Gabon, and the island of Bioko in the Gulf of Guinea, where the capital [Malabo](/africa/equatorialguinea/malabo) sits. Oil wealth since the 1990s has transformed the country — GDP per capita is among Africa's highest — but that wealth is very unevenly distributed and the country remains largely closed to tourism.

Bioko island is volcanic and mountainous, with some of the highest primate diversity in Africa: the Bioko Biodiversity Protection Program has documented several endemic subspecies of drills and colobus monkeys. The southern part of the island is difficult to access and rarely visited. [Luba](/africa/equatorialguinea/luba) on the western coast is the main town outside Malabo.
