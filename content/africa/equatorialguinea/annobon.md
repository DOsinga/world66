Annobon is one of the most isolated inhabited islands in Africa — a volcanic speck in the Gulf of Guinea, about 500 km southwest of the Equatorial Guinean mainland. Known locally as Pagalu, the island is just 8 km long and 3 km wide, yet it packs in real drama: three distinct peaks rise from dense cloud forest, including [Quioveo](/africa/equatorialguinea/annobon/quioveo), the highest point, an extinct volcano with a sacred crater lake. Getting here takes serious effort, which means almost no tourists ever do.

The island's only significant settlement is [San Antonio de Palé](/africa/equatorialguinea/annobon/san_antonio_de_pale), a small fishing village of a few hundred people on the northwestern coast. Life moves slowly here. The population speaks Annobonese, a Portuguese-based creole with roots in the island's colonial past, and fishing remains the main livelihood. The surrounding waters are exceptionally clear and rich in marine life. There is little infrastructure and no conventional tourist facilities, but for the determined traveller, Annobon offers something genuinely rare: an inhabited island that the modern world has largely passed by.
