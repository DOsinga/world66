story: >
  Bioko Island is biologically extraordinary. It separated from the African mainland about 10,000 years ago, when rising sea levels filled in the land bridge. Long enough ago to produce endemic species, recent enough that the forests still share much of their fauna with the Congo Basin. The island has more primate species per square kilometer than anywhere else in Africa, and several of them — including the drills and red-eared guenons — exist here in their last significant populations.
---

The Luba Crater Scientific Reserve covers the southern tip of Bioko Island, protecting a substantial tract of montane and lowland tropical forest that rises to the peaks of the Gran Caldera. The reserve is managed by the government with support from international conservation organizations and requires a permit to enter, obtainable in Malabo.

The wildlife is exceptional. Forest elephants still move through the interior forests, chimpanzees are present in the highland areas, and the beaches at Ureca (accessible via the southern road from Luba, when passable) host nesting leatherback sea turtles from October to April — some of the highest densities of nesting leatherbacks on the Atlantic coast. Several primate species found nowhere else in the world are present in the reserve. Independent access is difficult; arranging a guide through conservation organizations operating in Malabo is strongly recommended.
