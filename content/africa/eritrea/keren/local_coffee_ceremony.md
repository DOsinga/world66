The Eritrean coffee ceremony is worth making time for in Keren, where the cafe culture has its own unhurried character. The ceremony starts with green coffee beans roasted over charcoal in a long-handled pan — the roasting is done at the table, and the smell alone is reason enough to sit down. The beans are then ground and brewed in a clay pot called a jebena, and the coffee is served three times in small cups: the first brew strong, the second lighter, the third lighter still. Each round has a name, and finishing all three is considered polite.

Keren's cafes carry traces of the Italian colonial period — the espresso tradition runs alongside the local ceremony, and you can usually get a macchiato if that's what you're after. But the ceremony is the thing to go looking for, and the cafes near the market are the most likely places to find it done properly. Bring patience: the whole process takes thirty to forty minutes, which is entirely the point. It is one of the more enjoyable ways to spend a midmorning in a city that has no particular interest in rushing.
