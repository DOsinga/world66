Senafe is a highland market town in southern Eritrea, close to the Ethiopian border and surrounded by some of the country's most important ancient sites. It is not polished, and it is not busy with tourists. That is exactly why it feels so compelling: a quiet town of stone, fields, churches, and upland air, with the ruins of older civilizations scattered just beyond it.

The main reason to come is archaeology. [Qohaito](/africa/eritrea/senafe/qohaito) sits on a plateau a short drive away, with traces of a pre-Aksumite and Aksumite city spread across open country. [Matara](/africa/eritrea/senafe/matara), also known as Metera or Balaw Kalaw, lies south of town and has obelisks, inscriptions, and the remains of a settlement that linked the Red Sea trade routes with the highlands.

Senafe is best visited from Asmara with the right permits and a driver who understands the southern route. Come for atmosphere and deep history rather than easy logistics. The sites are under-excavated, lightly interpreted, and often empty, which makes them frustrating if you need labels and wonderful if you like places where the past still feels half-buried.
