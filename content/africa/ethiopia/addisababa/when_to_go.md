---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

Addis Ababa's elevation of 2,500 metres keeps the climate mild year-round — rarely hot, rarely cold by the standards of a city this close to the equator. The distinction that matters is between the dry and rainy seasons.

The best time to visit is October to January, immediately after the long rains. The air is clear, the city's parks are green, and the days are warm and sunny. The major religious festivals also fall in this period: Meskel (late September/October), Timkat (January), and Christmas in the Ethiopian calendar (January 7). These festivals are worth timing a trip around, but expect crowds.

The main rainy season runs from June to September, when afternoon downpours can be heavy and the roads and footpaths flood. Travel within Ethiopia is harder during this period, though Addis itself remains fully functional.

March to May brings a shorter dry period before the rains. Temperatures are pleasant, and the city is less crowded with tourists than in the October–January peak.
