A sheltered rock face in the hills about 40 kilometres southwest of Dire Dawa, covered with several hundred prehistoric paintings of humped cattle, antelope, and human figures. The paintings are attributed to early pastoralists of the Ethiopian Pastoralist tradition, possibly between 4,000 and 1,500 years old.

The site is remote and unguarded; reaching it requires a 4WD and ideally a local guide. The figures are surprisingly fresh, painted in ochre and white, with stylised cattle that demonstrate how thoroughly the Horn of Africa was already a pastoralist landscape thousands of years ago.
