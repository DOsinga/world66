story: "Construction of the Djibouti–Addis Ababa railway started in 1897 and reached Dire Dawa in 1902. The French built the line under a 99-year concession from Emperor Menelik II; it was the first railway in the Horn of Africa and for decades the only way to move goods from the coast to the highlands. The original metre-gauge line was retired in 2014, replaced by a Chinese-built standard-gauge railway running parallel."
---

The original 1902 French station, a long single-storey building with a clock tower, pastel-painted walls, and a sleepy frontage that has seen better days. The station was the reason Dire Dawa exists — the railway needed flat ground 40 kilometres north of [Harar](/africa/ethiopia/harrar), which is up in the mountains, and the town grew around the depot.

The old line has been retired but the buildings are preserved and partly used as a museum. The yard contains rusting French locomotives and carriages from the 1920s through the 1970s. Knock at the office for the caretaker, who will let you wander. The new Chinese-built standard-gauge railway runs from a separate station to the north.
