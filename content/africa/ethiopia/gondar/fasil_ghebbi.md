The Royal Enclosure is Gondar's defining monument — a walled compound of about 70,000 square metres enclosing six castles, palaces, and ceremonial buildings constructed by successive emperors between 1636 and the early 18th century. The complex is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the most striking sights in sub-Saharan Africa.

The oldest and grandest structure is the **Castle of Fasilidas**, built by Gondar's founder around 1640. It rises four storeys, with towers at each corner and a rooftop terrace that once offered views across the city and toward the Simien Mountains. The later **Palace of Iyasu I** is almost as impressive and shows a more refined architectural hand. Other buildings include the palace of Empress Mentewab, a library, a chancellery, and the remains of stables and a lion house where the emperors kept lions as symbols of their lineage from Solomon.

The architecture is a genuine curiosity — distinctly Ethiopian in its massing and stonework, but with Baroque-influenced arches and Moorish battlements that reflect the influence of Indian and Portuguese craftsmen, Jesuit missionaries, and Arab traders who passed through the court.
