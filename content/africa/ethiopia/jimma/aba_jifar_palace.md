The Palace of Abba Jifar sits on a hill north of Jimma city, looking out over the surrounding coffee-growing highlands in a way that makes the choice of location feel deliberate. The compound was the seat of the Kingdom of Jimma, a prosperous Oromo Muslim state that controlled the coffee trade across the Gibe region from the early 19th century until its absorption into the Ethiopian Empire in 1932.

The most prominent building is the main palace hall, a large rectangular structure with a corrugated iron roof that was advanced construction for the region when it was built. The entrance gate, the royal mosque within the compound, and several outbuildings survive in varying states of repair. The compound as a whole conveys the scale and organisation of what was a sophisticated court — the king hosted foreign merchants and envoys here, received tribute from subordinate chiefs, and managed a commercial network built largely on coffee exports. A guide is available at the gate and well worth taking; the oral history they carry is more interesting than the architecture alone.

Getting here from central Jimma takes around 20 minutes by bajaj. The road climbs out of town through small farms and coffee plots before reaching the compound gate.
