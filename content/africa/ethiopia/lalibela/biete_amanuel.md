Biete Amanuel — the House of Emmanuel — is widely considered the finest example of craftsmanship in the Lalibela complex. The exterior is carved with horizontal bands of recessed and projecting stone in alternating courses, creating a pattern that echoes the wooden-beam construction of ancient Axumite buildings — a deliberate architectural quotation rendered in stone. The effect is remarkably sophisticated, giving the church a layered, textile-like surface that catches the light differently at different times of day.

The church is part of the southeastern group and sits within a deep rock-hewn courtyard. Its interior is smaller and darker than Biete Medhane Alem but more atmospheric: the carved stone columns and capitals are particularly well preserved, and the sanctuary curtains are embroidered with traditional Ethiopian cross motifs. The approach to Biete Amanuel involves navigating a narrow tunnel through the rock, which adds to the sense of arrival. After the crowds at Biete Giorgis and Biete Medhane Alem, the southeastern group — including Amanuel — tends to be quieter and more contemplative.
