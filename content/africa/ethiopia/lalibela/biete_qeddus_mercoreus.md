Biete Qeddus Mercoreus — the House of Saint Mercurius — is part of the southeastern group of churches and carries a dedication to an Egyptian Christian martyr, one of several Egyptian saints venerated in the Ethiopian Orthodox tradition. The church is carved as an underground monolith, meaning the roof is level with or below the surrounding rock surface, giving the structure a secretive, subterranean quality that sets the southeastern group apart from the more visible northern churches.

The church is larger than its modest entrance suggests, with a nave flanked by columns and an apse at the east end. The walls carry faded traces of early fresco work, and the floor is worn smooth by centuries of barefoot pilgrims. Visiting the southeastern churches — Amanuel, Gabriel-Rufael, Lehem, and Mercoreus — in sequence rewards patience; each has a distinct character and the warren of connecting tunnels between them takes time to explore properly. Allow at least two hours for the full southeastern group.
