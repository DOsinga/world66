Yemrehanna Kristos is a 12th-century church built inside a cave about 35 kilometres northwest of Lalibela. The cave itself, a high natural chamber in the limestone escarpment, would be remarkable even without the church — the structure inside it, constructed in Axumite style with horizontal bands of olive wood and white plaster, is extraordinary. It predates King Lalibela's rock-hewn churches and represents an earlier phase of Zagwe dynasty building.

The interior is dark and has a distinctly archaic atmosphere: carved wooden ceilings painted in geometric patterns, ancient icons in the apse, and the smell of old incense embedded in the walls. The rear of the cave — beyond the church — holds the remains of thousands of pilgrims, stacked and mummified by the cave's dry air over many centuries. This is not a macabre attraction but a place of continuing veneration. The site requires a guide (easily arranged in Lalibela) and is a full-day excursion; combine it with the Na'akuto Lab'ab monastery if you have a vehicle.
