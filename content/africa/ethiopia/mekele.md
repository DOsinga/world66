Mekele is the capital of the Tigray region in northern Ethiopia, sitting at about 2,000 metres above sea level on a plateau east of the Ethiopian Highlands. It's a city shaped by imperial history — Emperor Yohannes IV made it his capital in the 1870s — and more recently by conflict; the city was at the centre of the Tigray war that devastated the region from 2020 to 2022.

For travellers, Mekele is primarily a base for ventures into some of Ethiopia's most extreme landscapes. The [Danakil Depression](/africa/ethiopia/mekele/danakil_depression_tours) — one of the lowest, hottest, and most geologically active places on earth — lies to the northeast, and organised tours depart from here to the sulphurous Dallol hot springs and the permanently active Erta Ale volcano. The [rock-hewn churches of Tigray](/africa/ethiopia/mekele/tigray_rock_churches), scattered through the surrounding mountains, are also accessible from Mekele — some of the oldest functioning Christian churches in the world, their Byzantine frescoes still vivid against the carved stone.

The city itself has the [Atse Yohannes Palace Museum](/africa/ethiopia/mekele/atse_yohannes_palace), Emperor Yohannes IV's 19th-century stone residence now converted into a museum of imperial and ecclesiastical artifacts, and a lively [central market](/africa/ethiopia/mekele/mekele_market) that serves the surrounding farming communities. Check current security conditions before travelling to the region; Tigray has been recovering from the 2020-2022 war and the situation continues to evolve.
