The Omo River runs from the Ethiopian Highlands south to Lake Turkana on the Kenyan border, carving its way through some of the most remote and culturally rich territory in East Africa. The [lower Omo valley](/africa/ethiopia/omoriver/lower_omo_valley_peoples) is home to several indigenous peoples — the Mursi, Hamer, Karo, Daasanach, and others — who maintain traditions largely unchanged by the outside world. UNESCO recognised the Omo valley as a World Heritage Site in 1980; the region remains one of the most ethnically diverse places on earth.

The river offers one of Africa's great [rafting journeys](/africa/ethiopia/omoriver/omo_river_rafting): deep sandstone gorges, powerful rapids, and prolific wildlife including some of the largest crocodiles on the continent. The [Mago National Park](/africa/ethiopia/omoriver/mago_national_park) along the Mago tributary protects elephant, lion, and buffalo country alongside the cultural landscape of the Mursi people. It is not easy to reach and not easy to run, but for those who make the effort, the Omo is an immersion in a landscape that feels genuinely prehistoric.
