Ethiopian wolves are one of the three endemic species that justify the Simien Mountains' UNESCO status, and seeing one in the wild is a legitimate wildlife highlight. The animals live in the high-altitude Afro-alpine moorland above 3,000 metres, where they hunt rodents — particularly giant mole rats — in the open grasslands. Sightings are most likely in the high areas around Chennek camp and the moorland approaching Ras Dashen.

The wolves are noticeably dog-like in appearance — a russet-red coat with white underparts, long legs, and an elongated muzzle evolved for probing rodent burrows. Unlike most endangered predators, they are relatively approachable because they evolved without large human populations in their range; a wolf hunting in the moorland may come within 20–30 metres without concern. Dawn and dusk are the best times. Sightings are not guaranteed — given the small total population, Bale Mountains in southern Ethiopia offers more reliable encounters — but the Simien does produce wolf sightings on most extended treks.
