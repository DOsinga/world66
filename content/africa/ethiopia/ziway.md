Ziway, also called Batu, sits on the western shore of Lake Ziway, one of Ethiopia's freshwater Rift Valley lakes. The town is a working roadside stop on the Addis Ababa-Hawassa road, but the lake gives it a reason to pause: papyrus boats, fishing skiffs, waterbirds around the jetty, and occasional hippos further out on the water.

This is not a polished resort town. The appeal is simple and outdoorsy, and it works best if you come with a driver or arrange a boat locally. The standard outing starts at the [Lake Ziway jetty](/africa/ethiopia/ziway/lake_ziway_jetty), where fishermen land their catch and boatmen offer birding and hippo trips.

The lake also has a cultural layer. Its islands are home to Zay communities and old Ethiopian Orthodox traditions, especially [Tulu Gudo Island](/africa/ethiopia/ziway/tulu_gudo_island), whose Maryam Tsion monastery is tied to local stories about the Ark of the Covenant. Ziway is close enough to [Addis Ababa](/africa/ethiopia/addisababa) for a long day trip, but it feels much more like the Rift Valley than the capital.
