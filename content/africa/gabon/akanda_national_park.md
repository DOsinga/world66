Akanda National Park covers the mangroves and tidal mudflats just north of Libreville, wedged between the Moka and Mondah estuaries. It is the smallest of Gabon's national parks, but it punches well above its size biologically: this is a major staging post on the East Atlantic flyway, where large flocks of pelicans, flamingos, sandpipers and other waders pause to feed on the rich mudflats. For a birder, it is one of the most rewarding half-days in the country.

The appeal here is quiet rather than dramatic. There are no big-mammal safaris, no roaring waterfalls. Instead you drift by pirogue at low tide past sandbars crowded with birds, with the city skyline still faintly visible behind you. The light on the water at dawn and the slow tidal rhythm of the place are the draw, and the proximity to Libreville makes it an easy escape.

You reach the park by boat, either from the city or from the fishing village of Cap Estérias on the coast just to the north. Birding guides and boat operators can be arranged through specialist tour companies in Libreville, and it is worth doing so — knowing where the flocks gather with the tide makes the difference between a pleasant outing and a memorable one. A morning is enough to see the highlights.

Go with realistic expectations and tidal timing in mind. The mangroves are best at low water when the mudflats are exposed and the birds concentrate to feed; at high tide much of the spectacle disappears under the estuary. Bring binoculars, sun protection, and patience for the slow pace of a place that rewards stillness.
