**On the Edge of the Primeval Forest** by Albert Schweitzer is the obvious starting point — Schweitzer's own account of his first years at Lambarene, written in plain prose by a man who saw himself as a doctor rather than a writer. It is short, vivid, and remarkably free of self-importance, considering its author later won the Nobel Peace Prize.

**Reverence for Life: The Words of Albert Schweitzer** collects the philosophical writing that made him famous in Europe before he came to Africa. Read it after the memoir and the place starts to make a different kind of sense.

**The Africa House** by Christina Lamb, while set in Northern Rhodesia, captures the same eccentric impulse — a European building an outpost of European life in the equatorial forest — and helps explain why Lambarene still feels the way it does.
