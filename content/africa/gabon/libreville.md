Libreville is Gabon's capital and its only real city. Perched on the Gabon Estuary facing the Atlantic, it is a surprisingly cosmopolitan place with French restaurants, air-conditioned shopping centers, and a level of polish that most of Central Africa cannot match. If you have been traveling rough through the region, Libreville will feel like a different world.

That polish comes at a price. Libreville is one of the most expensive cities in Africa, and the cost of everything from taxis to meals will remind you that this is an oil-rich capital. Budget travelers will need to be resourceful.

The [Boulevard de l'Independance](/africa/gabon/libreville/boulevard_de_l_independance) runs along the waterfront and is your main point of orientation. The heart of the city lies between the [Presidential Palace](/africa/gabon/libreville/presidential_palace) and the commercial district. The palace, built by Omar Bongo in the 1970s at a rumored cost of $800 million, is not open to the public but is impressive even from outside. Photography of the palace is forbidden.

The [Eglise Saint-Michel de Nkembo](/africa/gabon/libreville/eglise_saint_michel_de_nkembo) is worth a visit, especially on Sunday morning when the congregation fills the church with song. The carved wooden columns inside are remarkable. The [Musee des Arts et Traditions](/africa/gabon/libreville/musee_des_arts_et_traditions) houses a fine collection of Gabonese masks, carvings, and musical instruments, and gives useful context for the cultural traditions you may encounter elsewhere in the country.

The [Marche du Mont-Bouet](/africa/gabon/libreville/marche_du_mont_bouet) is one of the great markets of Central Africa. It sprawls across several blocks and sells everything: fish, produce, bushmeat, fabric, traditional medicines, household goods. Come with a sense of adventure and keep your valuables secure, as pickpockets are active.

Libreville is also the jumping-off point for most of [Gabon's national parks](/africa/gabon/libreville/lope_national_park) and for the short boat trip to [Pointe Denis](/africa/gabon/libreville/pointe_denis), a sandy peninsula across the estuary with beaches and calm water.
