Loango National Park is Gabon's most cinematic wilderness: a place where forest elephants step onto pale Atlantic beaches, hippos wallow near the surf, and blackwater lagoons cut deep into the rainforest. It sits on the wild coast between the Nkomi and Ndogo lagoon systems, a long way from the easy circuits of East and Southern Africa.

The park is not cheap or casual. You usually arrive via Port-Gentil, Omboue, or a lodge transfer from Libreville, and once inside you move by boat, 4WD, and guided walks. That effort is the point. Loango still feels like a real edge-of-the-map park, with fewer vehicles, fewer visitors, and a stronger sense that the animals have not adjusted their lives around you.

The headline is wildlife in unexpected settings. The [Loango coast](/africa/gabon/loango/loango_coast) is famous for elephants and buffalo on the sand, while [Iguela Lagoon](/africa/gabon/loango/iguela_lagoon) opens into mangroves, fishing channels, and bird-rich shallows. Gorilla tracking near [Yatouga](/africa/gabon/loango/yatouga_gorilla_area) is one of the rare chances to see western lowland gorillas in a coastal forest rather than a mountain setting.

Come with realistic expectations. Sightings are never as guaranteed as in fenced or heavily managed safari country, and rain can slow everything down. But when Loango works, it offers one of Africa's great travel moments: rainforest, ocean, and large mammals sharing the same frame.
