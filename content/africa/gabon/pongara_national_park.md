Pongara National Park spreads across the southern shore of the Gabon Estuary, directly opposite Libreville, taking in mangroves, coastal forest, and a long, wild Atlantic beach that runs south from Pointe Denis. Despite sitting just across the water from the capital, it feels remote: empty sand stretches for kilometres, broken only by the tracks of nesting turtles and the occasional forest elephant that wanders down from the trees.

The park is one of the most important leatherback turtle nesting sites in the world. Between November and February, these enormous reptiles haul themselves ashore at night to dig nests and lay their eggs, and guided beach walks during the season are the main reason most travellers come. The rangers are strict about red-filtered headlamps and no flash photography, and rightly so — the rules exist to keep the turtles from being disturbed during one of the most demanding moments of their lives.

Most visits combine a stay at one of the small ecolodges in or near the park with night-time guided walks during nesting season. Access is by boat across the estuary from Libreville, often as part of a lodge package. Outside the turtle window the park is still worth a visit: forest elephants frequent the beach and forest edge, and the empty Atlantic shore is a destination in its own right.

If you come for the turtles, be patient and dress for a long, slow night on the sand — sightings depend on timing and a measure of luck. Bring a red-filtered headlamp if you have one, follow the rangers' instructions closely, and treat the encounter as the privilege it is.
