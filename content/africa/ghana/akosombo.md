Akosombo is a small, orderly town on the Volta River, built in the 1960s to house workers constructing the [Akosombo Dam](/africa/ghana/akosombo/akosombo_dam). The dam itself is impressive — it created [Lake Volta](/africa/ghana/lakevolta), the world's largest man-made lake by surface area, and still generates a large share of Ghana's electricity. The surrounding hills and the sheer scale of the reservoir give the place an unexpectedly dramatic feel for such a quiet town. The hilltop [St Barbara Catholic Church](/africa/ghana/akosombo/st_barbara_catholic_church), built for the Italian dam engineers in the 1960s, has the best easy view of the gorge.

The town is the main gateway for boat travel on Lake Volta — see [Lake Volta Cruises](/africa/ghana/akosombo/lake_volta_cruises). The slow passenger ferry to Yeji (a two-day journey north through the lake) departs from [Akosombo Port](/africa/ghana/akosombo/akosombo_port), and shorter day cruises run out to Dodi Island, which is especially lively during the annual Dodi Festival. A short drive east is [Akwamufie](/africa/ghana/akosombo/akwamufie_palace), the ceremonial capital of the pre-colonial Akwamu kingdom. Akosombo is about two hours east of [Accra](/africa/ghana/accra) by road — easy as a day trip, though an overnight stay lets you get out on the water more comfortably.
