Fort Metal Cross stands on a rocky promontory at the village of Dixcove, about two kilometres west of Busua, commanding sweeping views over the bay and the Atlantic. Built in 1692, it is one of the best-preserved colonial-era forts on Ghana's coast, less visited and less commercialised than the more famous Elmina or Cape Coast castles further east. The fort's position on its rocky headland above the fishing community of Infuma gives it a dramatic setting.

The fort was built for the slave trade that defined this coastline for two centuries, and like all the coastal forts it is both an architectural monument and a place of profound historical darkness. The interior has been partially restored and can be explored; local guides from the village offer tours that provide context on both the fort's history and its role in the Atlantic slave trade. The walk from Busua to Dixcove along the coast takes about 30–45 minutes on a path that passes fishing villages and rocky coves. You can also take a boat across the bay, which local fishermen are usually willing to do for a negotiated price.
