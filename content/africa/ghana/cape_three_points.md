Cape Three Points is the end-of-the-road corner of Ghana's Western Region, a small peninsula where forest, fishing villages, and rough Atlantic surf meet at the country's southernmost tip. It is often described as the land closest to Null Island, the imaginary 0 degrees latitude and 0 degrees longitude point far out in the Gulf of Guinea.

The cape is not a polished resort. Roads are slow, transport is irregular, and most visitors come as a long day trip from Butre, Busua, Dixcove, or Akwidaa. That is part of the charm. The mood is quieter and more remote than the better-known beaches around Cape Coast and Elmina.

The main reason to come is the [Cape Three Points Lighthouse](/africa/ghana/cape_three_points/cape_three_points_lighthouse), where the ruined 1875 light and the working 1925 tower mark a high lookout over the coast. Around it are beaches, patches of protected forest, and the fishing village of [Cape Three Points](/africa/ghana/cape_three_points/cape_three_points_village). If you have your own transport, combine the cape with [Akwidaa](/africa/ghana/cape_three_points/akwidaa) or the fort at [Princes Town](/africa/ghana/cape_three_points/fort_gross_fredericksburg).
