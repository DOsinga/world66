Dixcove is a small fishing village on Ghana's southwest coast, about 30 km west of Takoradi. It sits on a rocky headland around a natural harbour, with fishing canoes pulled up on the beach and the smell of the sea everywhere. It's a quiet, unpretentious place — not a resort, just a working village that happens to have one of the most impressive forts on the coast and easy access to one of Ghana's best beaches.

[Fort Metal Cross](/africa/ghana/dixcove/fort_metal_cross) dominates the headland above the harbour. Built by the English in 1691, it served as a trading post and later as a transit point in the slave trade. You can walk up to it, look out over the bay, and buy fresh fish from the market below — the contrast is uncomfortable and worth sitting with. The fort is in reasonable condition and gives a good sense of how these coastal fortifications worked.

[Busua Beach](/africa/ghana/dixcove/busua_beach) is a 10-minute walk or short taxi ride from Dixcove, through a shaded bush path. The beach itself is wide, white-sanded, and backed by palms — genuinely beautiful. The sea is calmer here than on many stretches of the Gulf of Guinea coast, making it one of the better spots for swimming in the region.

The [harbour market](/africa/ghana/dixcove/dixcove_harbour) is worth a wander. Fresh shark, swordfish, and lobster come off the boats, and prices are low if you bargain directly with the fishermen. The neighbouring fishing village of [Butre](/africa/ghana/dixcove/butre_day_trip), with its lagoon and Dutch fort ruin, is an easy half-day walk along the coast.
