Nzulezu is one of the most extraordinary inhabited places in West Africa — a village of over 500 people built on bamboo platforms and wooden stilts over Lake Tadane, a shallow freshwater lake in Ghana's Western Region. There is no solid ground, no road, no vehicle. Every path is a boardwalk; every journey between households is over open water or through connected platform spaces above it.

The village functions as a complete community. The church, primary school, chief's palace, and several small bars and shops are all on the water. Residents fish from the platforms and navigate by dugout canoe. The community has lived this way for centuries, and despite the physical isolation and the tourist attention it receives, it retains a strongly functional, lived-in character rather than a preserved or performative one. The site is on the UNESCO World Heritage Tentative List.
