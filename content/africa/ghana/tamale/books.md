title: "Books"

---

**Ghana Must Go** by Taiye Selasi (2013) follows a Ghanaian-Nigerian family dispersed across continents — a surgeon father in Accra, children studying in America and Britain, a family held together and torn apart by migration, memory, and the particular silence that forms around things left unsaid. It is a novel about the Ghanaian diaspora written from inside it, and it captures something real about what Ghana means to people who grew up partly elsewhere: the pull of a country that is home in theory and foreign in practice. Selasi grew up between the US and London but identifies deeply as Ghanaian, and that double perspective gives the novel its particular quality: loving and clear-eyed at the same time.

**The Beautyful Ones Are Not Yet Born** by Ayi Kwei Armah (1968) is the essential Ghanaian novel — the one that shaped how a generation of writers and readers understood what independence actually looked like once the flags went up and the speeches faded. Set in Accra in the years after independence, it follows an unnamed railway worker who refuses to take bribes in a society where corruption has become the only rational strategy. It is a bleak book and an honest one. You will read it and recognise things you have seen in Ghana — the hierarchy of favours, the exhaustion of integrity, the particular kind of shame that comes from watching your country fail its own ideals. The north barely features, but the book explains something about the national mood that is as true in Tamale as in Accra.
