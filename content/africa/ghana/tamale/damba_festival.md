The Damba Festival is the centrepiece of Tamale's cultural calendar and one of the most impressive traditional celebrations in West Africa. It marks the birth of the Prophet Muhammad and falls in the Islamic month of Maulid — roughly July or August in the Gregorian calendar, though the exact date varies each year with the lunar calendar. The festival lasts several days, and timing a visit around it transforms Tamale from a transit hub into something you will not forget quickly.

The highlight is the appearance of the Ya-Na, the paramount chief of the Dagomba, and the assembly of regional sub-chiefs in full ceremonial dress. Chiefs arrive on horseback, accompanied by drummers and praise-singers. The regalia is extraordinary — layered robes, elaborate headgear, and the horns and drums that have accompanied the Dagomba court for centuries. Drumming and dancing continue throughout the festival, and the city fills with people who have travelled from across the region. The festivals are largely open to visitors; ask locally about the schedule and protocol, since certain ceremonies are more public than others.
