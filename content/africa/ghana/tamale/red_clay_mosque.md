Tamale is a predominantly Muslim city and the Central Mosque reflects that identity clearly. The building follows the Sudanese-influenced architectural tradition common across the Sahel — thick mud-brick walls, projecting wooden beams, and a minarets that taper upward against the sky. It is not as old or as celebrated as the mosque at Larabanga, but it is a working place of worship at the living centre of the city.

The area around the mosque is particularly worth visiting on a Friday, when the afternoon prayer draws large crowds and the surrounding streets fill with market stalls selling prayer mats, fabric, and food. Non-Muslims should be respectful about visiting the grounds, particularly during prayer times, but the architecture and street life around it are freely observable from outside. The mosque sits in a part of the city where Islamic culture is most visible — a contrast to the more mixed character of the market area, and a reminder that Tamale has a distinct identity separate from the tourist south.
