---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

Guinea-Bissau has a classic West African tropical climate: a dry season from November to May and a wet season from June to October. Bafatá, in the interior, follows this pattern with less coastal humidity but sometimes more extreme heat.

**November to February** is the most comfortable time to visit. Temperatures are in the mid-20s Celsius, skies are clear, roads are passable, and the Geba River bridge stays open. The Harmattan — a dry northeasterly wind carrying Saharan dust — arrives in December or January and can reduce visibility and make the air hazy for days at a time.

**March to May** is hotter and increasingly dusty before the rains break. Daytime temperatures can reach 38°C in April. Roads are still good.

**June to October** is the rainy season. Rain comes in heavy afternoon downpours rather than all-day drizzle. The countryside turns intensely green. Road conditions deteriorate markedly on unpaved tracks outside the main highway. The Geba bridge can be closed for days at a time when the river is in flood. Travel is possible but requires more patience and flexibility.

There are no major festivals or events in Bafatá that would draw visitors at a particular time of year. The town's annual rhythms follow the agricultural calendar — harvest season (October–November) is the most active market period.
