story: On 3 August 1959, Portuguese colonial police opened fire on striking dock workers at Pidjiguiti, killing at least 50 people. The massacre became the founding moment of the independence movement — Amílcar Cabral cited it as the event that proved peaceful resistance was impossible, and within months the planning for armed struggle had begun. The date is now a national holiday in Guinea-Bissau: Martyrs of Colonialism Day.

The port of Bissau, known as Porto Pidjiguiti, is both a working harbour and a place of historical weight. The docks where dock workers were massacred by Portuguese colonial police in 1959 have been memorialised at the waterfront, and the date of the massacre — 3 August — is observed as a national holiday. The Maão de Timba monument in nearby Praça dos Mártires (Martyrs' Square) commemorates the independence struggle more broadly.

The port is still active — ferries to the Bijagos Islands depart from here, and fishing boats work the estuary — and the waterfront area is one of the more animated parts of the city. Walking along the esplanade in the late afternoon is one of Bissau's more pleasant activities.
