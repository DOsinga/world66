---
title: "Music"
type: section
---

Conakry has been a great African music city for more than half a century. In the 1960s and 70s, under Sékou Touré's cultural-revival policy, the state funded a network of orchestras — Bembeya Jazz, Balla et ses Balladins, Keletigui et ses Tambourinis — that married jazz, Cuban son and traditional Mandé music in a sound exported across the continent. Many of those records, recently reissued, are now hard to better.

On the ground today, the live scene works in fits and starts. The main venues are bars and cultural centres in Kaloum and Camayenne; nights start late (after 11 pm) and run until dawn. Acoustic guitar, kora and balafon are common in the older clubs; the younger generation plays a mix of mbalax, coupé-décalé and Afrobeats. Ask at your hotel or guesthouse for what is on this week — listings are largely word of mouth.

For records to take home, look for second-hand vinyl in the small shops on Avenue de la République; cassettes and burned CDs of local musicians are sold at the markets. Outside the country, the Sterns Africa label and the Syllart reissue catalogue cover most of the classic Guinean material in good remastered form.
