---
title: "Eating Out in Abidjan"
type: section
---

Abidjan has the most varied restaurant scene in West Africa — a legacy of the French colonial period, the Lebanese merchant community, and the city's own substantial wealth during the boom years.

**Ivorian food** centres on attiéké (fermented cassava couscous, served with grilled fish), aloko (fried plantain), and kedjenou — a slow-cooked chicken or guinea fowl stew from Treichville. Street food along Boulevard Delafosse and around the Adjamé market is excellent and cheap.

**Lebanese restaurants** in Cocody and Les Deux Plateaux cover the middle market: reliable mezze, grilled meats, and fresh bread. For French cuisine, the restaurant at the Hotel Ivoire is a standard choice. The Deux Plateaux area has an increasing number of international options including Vietnamese, Chinese, and modern bistro cooking serving the expatriate community.
