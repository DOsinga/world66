The Goua is the acrobatic dance tradition of the Dan people of western Ivory Coast. Performances involve young male dancers executing high-speed physical routines — spinning, jumping, balancing acts — accompanied by drums and horns. The Dan understand the Goua not as entertainment but as a ritual communication with spirits, and performances are tied to the social calendar of the village: ceremonies, harvests, and significant occasions rather than scheduled tourist shows.

Biankouma is one of the villages where Goua performances are most likely to be seen, particularly on Sundays when cultural activities concentrate. Visitors should understand that seeing the dance depends on timing, local circumstances, and the willingness of the village to welcome outsiders. It cannot be reliably scheduled or booked. Come with patience, approach the village chief first to ask permission, and treat any performance you see as a privilege rather than an attraction. Photography should be approached with care and may not be appropriate during parts of the ceremony.
