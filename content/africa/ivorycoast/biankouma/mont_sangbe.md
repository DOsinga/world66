Mont Sângbé National Park lies north of Biankouma on the road toward Touba, covering 95,000 hectares of the Monts du Toura — a range of hills and forest on the western Sassandra watershed. The park was created in 1976 and encompasses the most significant remaining forest block in this part of the Man region. Wildlife includes chimpanzees, forest elephants, buffaloes, and numerous bird species.

Visiting Mont Sângbé is not simple: there is no formal visitor infrastructure, the roads into the park are poor tracks, and the park administration is minimal. Independent visits require a guide, a good vehicle, and advance arrangement with local authorities or a Man-based tour operator. The best approach is to arrange a visit from Man rather than from Biankouma, as the guide network and logistics are more developed there. Those who make the effort are rewarded with genuinely wild forest and the chance to encounter chimpanzees — the Monts du Toura area supports one of the larger chimpanzee populations remaining in Ivory Coast.
