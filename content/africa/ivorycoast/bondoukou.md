Bondoukou is one of the oldest trading towns in West Africa, sitting close to the Ghana border in the northeast of Côte d'Ivoire. It was a major crossroads on the trans-Saharan caravan routes and grew rich on kola nuts, gold, and cloth. That mercantile past is still visible in the town's architecture and in the makeup of its population — Dyula Muslim traders have been here for centuries, living alongside the Kulango, the original inhabitants of the region.

The town is best known for two things: its [Grand Mosque](/africa/ivorycoast/bondoukou/grand_mosque), one of the oldest in Côte d'Ivoire, and its spectacular masquerade traditions. The [Bedu masquerades](/africa/ivorycoast/bondoukou/bedu_masquerade) of the Kulango minority and the [Sakaraboutou carnival](/africa/ivorycoast/bondoukou/sakaraboutou_carnival) of the Dyula majority are performed around the same time each year, creating an unusual juxtaposition of Christian and Muslim West African performance traditions in a single town. If you can time your visit around one of these festivals, do it.

Bondoukou is not on most tourist itineraries, which is part of its appeal. The [old quarter](/africa/ivorycoast/bondoukou/old_quarter) around the mosque rewards a slow wander. The [weekly market](/africa/ivorycoast/bondoukou/central_market) draws traders from across the region. It makes a reasonable base for exploring the [Zanzan region](/africa/ivorycoast/zanzan), and the border crossing into Ghana at Sampa is only a few hours away.
