Grand-Bassam was Côte d'Ivoire's first colonial capital, briefly the seat of French power from 1893 to 1896 before a yellow fever epidemic forced the administration to move to [Bingerville](/africa/ivorycoast/bingerville). The [old colonial quarter](/africa/ivorycoast/grandbassam/historic_town) — a peninsula between the ocean and the [Comoé lagoon](/africa/ivorycoast/grandbassam/comoe_lagoon) — was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2012. It's one of the best-preserved examples of a late 19th-century colonial trading town in West Africa.

It makes an easy and rewarding day trip from [Abidjan](/africa/ivorycoast/abidjan), about 40 km to the west. The [Governor's Palace](/africa/ivorycoast/grandbassam/governors_palace) and the old Mairie are in reasonable shape; the Palais de Justice and custom houses are more ruined but atmospheric. The town also has a solid [National Costume Museum](/africa/ivorycoast/grandbassam/national_costume_museum) housed in one of the old buildings. The [artisan market](/africa/ivorycoast/grandbassam/artisan_market) that has grown up in the vacant colonial houses is one of the better places in the country to buy traditional crafts. Outside the heritage zone, the long [Atlantic beach](/africa/ivorycoast/grandbassam/comoe_beach) draws Abidjanais on weekends.

For even better beaches, continue another 40 km east to Assini, widely considered the finest stretch of coast in the country.
