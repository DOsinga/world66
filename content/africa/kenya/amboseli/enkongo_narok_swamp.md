Enkongo Narok — "the black swamp" in Maasai — is the largest and most productive of Amboseli's permanent wetlands. Fed by underground water from Kilimanjaro, it stays green and wet even during severe droughts when the surrounding landscape is bone-dry, making it a refuge for wildlife from far beyond the park's boundaries. Hippos spend their days in the deeper channels; buffalo wade at the margins; and the reed beds hold a remarkable density of birds including herons, ibises, and the striking saddle-billed stork.

Game drives through and around the swamp are among the best in the park. The roads loop through the papyrus and yellow-barked fever trees, bringing you close to animals that have little reason to flee. Early morning is especially good — hippos move back toward water from night grazing, and the air is clear and cool. The swamp is also a reliable spot for crocodiles, though they are less numerous here than in the Mara or Tsavo.
