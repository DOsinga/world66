Meru is a substantial market town on the northeastern slopes of Mount Kenya, home to the Meru people — close relatives of the Kikuyu and Embu, with their own distinct traditions and language.

Most travelers pass through rather than linger, but Meru rewards a stop. The town is famous throughout East Africa for *miraa* — the twigs of the *Catha edulis* shrub, chewed for their stimulant properties. Grown legally here in large quantities, the miraa trade is the economic heartbeat of the region; the [central market](/africa/kenya/centralhighlands/meru/meru_market) is the place to see it being bundled for the road. If you want to try it, you won't find it fresher anywhere in the world: chew the bark of the young twigs for an hour and you'll feel the effect of a couple of strong coffees.

The [Meru Museum](/africa/kenya/centralhighlands/meru/meru_museum) is worth an hour if you have time — it covers the natural and cultural history of the region and gives useful context for the broader area, including [Meru National Park](/africa/kenya/centralhighlands/meru/meru_national_park) to the east and the [Chogoria route](/africa/kenya/centralhighlands/meru/chogoria_route) up Mount Kenya to the south-west.
