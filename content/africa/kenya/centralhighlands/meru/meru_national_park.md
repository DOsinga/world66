Meru National Park is one of Kenya's less-visited but more rewarding parks, about 80 kilometres east of Meru town in a lower, hotter, drier landscape than the highlands. The Tana River runs through it and supports a different ecology from the more famous savanna parks — riverine forest, doum palm groves, papyrus swamp, and open plains.

The Big Five are all present (a fenced rhino sanctuary at the heart of the park holds both black and white rhino). Elephant herds along the rivers are particularly easy to see. The park's relative obscurity means you can sometimes spend a full game drive without seeing another vehicle — an unusual experience in Kenya. Several modest lodges and campsites operate within and on the borders of the park.
