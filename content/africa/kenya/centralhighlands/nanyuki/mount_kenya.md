Africa's second-highest peak (5,199m at Batian summit) rises directly from the equatorial plains, its glaciated upper flanks visible from Nanyuki on clear mornings. The mountain is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and national park. Most trekkers aim for Point Lenana (4,985m), the highest non-technical summit, reached via the Sirimon route from Nanyuki in three to four days. The scenery — Afroalpine moorland, giant lobelias, and glacier lakes — is unlike anywhere else on the continent. Guides and porters are hired in Nanyuki; the Naro Moru River Lodge is the classic base.
