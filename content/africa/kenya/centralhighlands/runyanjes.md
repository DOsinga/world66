Runyenjes is a market town in Embu County, in Kenya's central highlands, sitting in the fertile territory between [Embu](/africa/kenya/centralhighlands/embu) town to the south and the foothills of Mount Kenya to the north. The town takes its name from Chief Runyenje, a colonial-era leader who held authority across the territory between the Thuchi River and Sagana in the early 20th century.

The town functions primarily as a trading hub for the surrounding agricultural area — the farms here grow coffee, tea, maize, and bananas, and Runyenjes hosts one of the larger weekly open-air markets in the district. Buyers from across Embu County converge on market day, which is the most animated and interesting time to visit.

For travellers, Runyenjes is a stopping point rather than a destination in itself, though the [Nthenge Njeru Falls](/africa/kenya/centralhighlands/runyanjes/nthenge_njeru_falls) — a double waterfall in the Kirimiri Forest — are a worthwhile short detour. The [Mwene Ndega grove](/africa/kenya/centralhighlands/runyanjes/mwene_ndega_grove), 3 km from town, is an important cultural site for the Embu people, a remnant of the indigenous forest where oral tradition locates the origins of the community. Embu town, 20 km south, has more accommodation options and a wider range of services.
