Luo Nyanza is the western strip of Kenya along the eastern shore of Lake Victoria, homeland of the Luo people — Kenya's third-largest ethnic group and one of the country's most politically influential communities. The region is flat, agricultural, and hot, with the lake providing both livelihood and identity: fishing, boat-building, and the rhythms of the water define life here in ways that are entirely different from the highland Kenya most visitors see.

The main town is [Kisumu](/africa/kenya/kisumu), the region's commercial hub and Kenya's third city, where Victorian-era architecture and a working lakeshore port give it more character than its reputation suggests. Smaller towns include [Siaya](/africa/kenya/luo_nyanza/siaya_town), the district headquarters about 40 km north of Kisumu, notable mainly as the ancestral home of the Obama family — the village of Kogelo, 30 km beyond Siaya, draws visitors for that connection.

The region is off the main tourist circuit and infrastructure is modest, but that is part of the appeal for travelers who want to understand Kenya beyond the national parks. The Luo are warm hosts, the lake is genuinely beautiful at sunset, and the food — fresh tilapia grilled on the shore — is some of the best in East Africa.
