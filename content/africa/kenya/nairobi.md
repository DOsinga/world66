Nairobi is Kenya's capital and the unofficial capital of East Africa — a mile-high city 145 km south of the equator that has grown from an empty patch of swamp at the end of the Uganda Railway in 1899 to a metropolis of nearly five million people. Most travellers pass through on their way to a safari; those who stay a day or two find a livelier, more multicultural city than the reputation suggests, with excellent restaurants, a serious arts and music scene, and something genuinely unusual — a city where lions and rhinos live inside the municipal boundaries.

The [Nairobi National Park](/africa/kenya/nairobi/nairobi_national_park) is the only major wildlife reserve inside a capital city anywhere in the world. Lions, giraffes, rhinos, and several hundred bird species live on the plains with the city skyline as backdrop. A half-day safari before your flight out is very feasible. The park shares its grounds with the [David Sheldrick Wildlife Trust](/africa/kenya/nairobi/david_sheldrick_wildlife_trust), which rescues and rehabilitates orphaned elephants and rhinos. The feeding sessions are open to the public for one hour each morning and are worth timing a trip around.

Nearby in the [Karen](/africa/kenya/nairobi/karen) suburb, the [Giraffe Centre](/africa/kenya/nairobi/giraffe_centre) lets you feed the endangered Rothschild's giraffe from a platform at eye level; the [Karen Blixen Museum](/africa/kenya/nairobi/karen_blixen_museum) is the farmhouse where the Danish author (and narrator of *Out of Africa*) lived from 1917 to 1931. The adjacent [Giraffe Manor](/africa/kenya/nairobi/giraffe_manor) — a 1930s manor hotel where giraffes join guests for breakfast — is one of the most photographed spots in Kenya. [Langata](/africa/kenya/nairobi/langata)'s [Bomas of Kenya](/africa/kenya/nairobi/bomas_of_kenya) cultural centre stages traditional dances from Kenya's 42 ethnic communities — a useful crash course in the country's diversity.

In town, the [Nairobi National Museum](/africa/kenya/nairobi/nairobi_national_museum) is genuinely worth a morning — the Hominid collection (fossils of Homo habilis, Homo erectus, and the Turkana Boy discovered by the Leakeys) is one of the great scientific collections in Africa. The [Railway Museum](/africa/kenya/nairobi/railwaymuseum) has atmospheric original rolling stock from the Uganda Railway, including the carriage from which a superintendent was seized by one of the man-eating lions of Tsavo. For contemporary culture, the [Kuona Trust Arts Centre](/africa/kenya/nairobi/kuona_trust) and [Kitengela Glass](/africa/kenya/nairobi/kitengela_glass) — a glass sculpture estate on the southern plains — are both worth seeking out.

[Westlands](/africa/kenya/nairobi/westlands) is where Nairobi's bar and restaurant scene is concentrated; the [Carnivore Restaurant](/africa/kenya/nairobi/carnivore_restaurant) in Langata is an institution worth the taxi ride for its sword-skewered meats cooked over open charcoal.

**Safety**: Nairobi's reputation for crime — the old nickname "Nairobbery" — is not undeserved. Avoid walking alone after dark, especially in the downtown and the River Road area, and use trusted taxis or ride-hailing apps rather than roadside taxis. Daytime in well-trafficked areas (Westlands, Kilimani, Karen, and around the major hotels) is generally safe.

The climate is among the most pleasant of any capital — highland elevation means daytime temperatures are consistently 20–25°C, with long dry seasons from January to February and again from July to October. The rainy seasons (March–May and November–December) still produce sunny mornings.
