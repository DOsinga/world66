The Gede Ruins (also spelled Gedi) are the remains of a medieval Swahili town, abandoned some time in the 17th or early 18th century. The site is about 4 km inland from Watamu, surrounded by coastal forest, and it is one of the most atmospheric historical sites on the Kenyan coast. Stone mosque foundations, a palace, houses, and a city wall are all visible, partially engulfed by fig trees and tangled vegetation.

The forest around the ruins is home to colobus monkeys and a variety of birds. A small museum at the entrance displays the porcelain, coins, and glass beads excavated from the site. The combination of archaeology and wildlife makes it one of the most rewarding day trips from Watamu.
