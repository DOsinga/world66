Naivasha is a moderately sized town about 90 km northwest of [Nairobi](/africa/kenya/nairobi), at the foot of the Great Rift Valley escarpment. It is the gateway to several of [Kenya's](/africa/kenya) most accessible natural attractions — and one of the few parts of the country where you can spend a rewarding few days without a safari vehicle.

[Lake Naivasha](/africa/kenya/theriftvalley/naivasha/lake_naivasha) is a large freshwater lake fringed with papyrus and fever trees, home to hippos, fish eagles, and remarkable concentrations of waterbirds. Boat trips on the lake are the main activity; [Crescent Island](/africa/kenya/theriftvalley/naivasha/crescent_island), a private sanctuary on the eastern shore, is one of the few places in Kenya where you can walk freely among giraffe, zebra, and wildebeest. [Hell's Gate National Park](/africa/kenya/theriftvalley/naivasha/hells_gate_national_park), south of the lake, is one of the very few Kenyan parks where you can walk and cycle — its volcanic cliffs and geothermal gorges make for a dramatic day out. [Mount Longonot](/africa/kenya/theriftvalley/naivasha/mount_longonot), a dormant stratovolcano visible from much of the valley, rewards the hike to its crater rim with panoramic Rift Valley views. For something older still, [Kariandusi](/africa/kenya/theriftvalley/naivasha/kariandusi), a Palaeolithic site north of town, has hand axes made by Homo erectus up to 1.4 million years ago.

Naivasha sits on the main road between Nairobi and [Nakuru](/africa/kenya/theriftvalley/nakuru) and is easily reached by matatu or bus.
