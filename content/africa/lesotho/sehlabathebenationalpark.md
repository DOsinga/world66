Sehlabathebe National Park is Lesotho at its wildest: a high, lonely plateau in the far southeast, where sandstone outcrops rise from grassland, clear streams run through rock pools, and weather moves fast across the Maloti Mountains. It is part of the Maloti-Drakensberg World Heritage Site, shared with South Africa's uKhahlamba Drakensberg Park.

This is not an easy park to reach. The roads are long and rough, public transport is of little use, and bad weather can turn a straightforward plan into an ordeal. But travelers who like remote mountain country will understand why Sehlabathebe has such a pull. It feels far from everything, including the more familiar Lesotho pony-trekking routes around Malealea and Semonkong.

The rewards are walking, birding, sandstone shapes, [rock pools](/africa/lesotho/sehlabathebenationalpark/rock_pools), waterfalls, and rock shelters with San paintings. [Bushman's Nek Pass](/africa/lesotho/sehlabathebenationalpark/bushmans_nek_pass) links the area conceptually to the South African Drakensberg, though crossing logistics should always be checked locally.

Come prepared for altitude and isolation. Nights can be cold even when the lowlands are mild, and the park is best treated as a self-sufficient mountain trip rather than a casual day out from Maseru.
