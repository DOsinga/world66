Germa, ancient Garama, is one of the great names of the central Sahara. This was the capital of the Garamantes, the desert kingdom that controlled oases, caravan routes and underground irrigation systems across the Fezzan long before modern Libya existed.

The place can look modest at first: low ruins, a small museum, desert light and the palms of the Wadi al-Ajal. But the story is huge. The Garamantes built towns in a landscape outsiders often imagined as empty, farmed with foggara irrigation, traded across the Sahara and were powerful enough for Roman writers to take notice.

A visit should include the [Garama ruins](/africa/libya/germa/garama_ruins), the [Germa Museum](/africa/libya/germa/germa_museum), the [Germa necropolis](/africa/libya/germa/germa_necropolis) and the rock-art and settlement area at [Zinchecra](/africa/libya/germa/zinchecra). The surrounding [Wadi al-Ajal](/africa/libya/germa/wadi_al_ajal) gives the ruins their setting: a long oasis corridor between sand and escarpment.

Germa is reached from [Sebha](/africa/libya/sebha) along the Wadi al-Hayat/Wadi al-Ajal route and is often combined with the Fezzan desert sites to the south. Travel here requires specialist logistics, current security advice and respect for fragile archaeology.
