---
title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

Eating options in Ghadames are limited, reflecting its small and remote character. The local cuisine draws on the Tuareg and Berber traditions of the western Sahara — hearty dishes built around lamb, dates, semolina, and camel milk. Couscous is the staple, often served with a lamb stew fragrant with cumin and coriander. Dates from the surrounding palm gardens are eaten at every meal and offered to guests as a matter of hospitality.

A handful of small restaurants and guesthouses serve food to visitors, typically set meals rather than à la carte menus. The best meals are often eaten in guesthouses, where hosts prepare home-style cooking. If you are staying with a local family or at a family-run guesthouse, take the opportunity — the food is usually excellent and the hospitality generous. Tea — heavily sweetened green tea poured from a height in the Tuareg tradition — is served throughout the day and accepting a glass is a social courtesy.

During the annual Ghadames Festival (usually held in autumn when the town is most accessible), food stalls appear in the public squares serving traditional Libyan Saharan food to festival crowds. Outside of festival season and the main tourist months, options may be extremely limited, so bring provisions if you plan an extended stay.
