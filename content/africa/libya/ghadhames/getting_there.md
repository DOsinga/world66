---
title: "Getting There"
type: section
---

Ghadames is about 600 km southwest of Tripoli, across the Nafusa Mountains and the vast Hamada al-Hamra plateau. Before Libya's political crisis, the standard approach was to drive from Tripoli — the journey took around seven to eight hours by car on the main highway. The road crosses dramatic scenery, including the spectacular escarpment of the Nafusa Mountains and the barren red gravel plain of the Hamada, before arriving at the oasis. The drive itself is considered one of the great desert road trips of North Africa.

There was a small domestic airport at Ghadames (LTD), with occasional flights from Tripoli, though service has always been irregular and has been further disrupted by the political situation. When flights are operating, the journey is around one hour. Check current status before planning to rely on this.

Some visitors in the past approached from Tunisia via the border crossing at Dehiba–Wazen/Ras Jedir, making Ghadames part of a Trans-Saharan crossing or a circuit including southern Tunisia. This border crossing has also been affected by Libya's instability. All entry routes into Libya require a visa and, typically, arrangement through an accredited local guide or tour operator — independent travel without a guide was not straightforward even in the best years, and the current situation makes it essential.
