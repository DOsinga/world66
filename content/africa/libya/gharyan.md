Gharyan (also Gharian) is the main town of the Nafusa Mountains, about 90 km south of [Tripoli](/africa/libya/tripoli) on the road toward Ghadames. It sits at around 900 metres elevation, which makes it noticeably cooler than the coast in summer.

The town was one of the centres of Libyan resistance during the Italian invasion of 1911–32. Several important figures in that resistance came from Gharyan. It later became a centre of opposition to Gaddafi, and in 2019 it was briefly seized by forces loyal to General Haftar during the campaign for Tripoli before being retaken by government forces.

Gharyan's main draws are its [troglodyte dwellings](/africa/libya/gharyan/troglodyte_dwellings) — underground houses carved into the limestone hillside that kept families cool in punishing summers — and its [ceramics workshops](/africa/libya/gharyan/ceramics_workshops), which have produced the region's distinctive glazed pottery for centuries. Grain, olives, and figs are grown in the surrounding hills, and the [Nafusa Mountain landscape](/africa/libya/gharyan/nafusa_mountain_landscape) is worth the drive from the coast in its own right. It makes a natural first stop if you are heading south toward [Ghadames](/africa/libya/ghadhames).
