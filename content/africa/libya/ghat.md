Ghat is an oasis town on Libya's far south-western edge, close to Algeria and deep in Tuareg country. It sits where old Sahara caravan routes once threaded between the dunes, the Acacus Mountains, and the Tassili n'Ajjer beyond the border.

The town itself is modest, dusty, and remote, but its setting is extraordinary. The old quarter and ruined fort look out over palms and sand, while the [Tadrart Acacus](/africa/libya/ghat/tadrart_acacus) to the east holds some of the Sahara's greatest rock art. Paintings and engravings show cattle, hunters, giraffes, and other animals from a greener Sahara, a reminder that this desert has not always been desert.

Ghat is not a casual detour. Travel requires local permits, a guide, a capable vehicle, and a realistic reading of Libya's security situation. For serious desert travellers, though, this is one of the great names of the central Sahara: a frontier oasis with history in its walls and prehistory in the surrounding stone.
