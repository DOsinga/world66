Kufra is as remote as it gets in Libya — an oasis cluster in the far southeast, near the Egyptian and Sudanese borders, closer to Khartoum than to Tripoli. There are no tourist facilities here, and reaching it requires serious preparation. This is a destination for serious desert travellers, not casual visitors.

The [Kufra oasis](/africa/libya/kufra/kufra_oasis) sits above enormous underground aquifers, part of the Nubian Sandstone Aquifer System, which made it an important stop on trans-Saharan caravan routes for centuries. The Senussi religious order established a major zawiya (lodge) here in the 19th century, making Kufra a political and spiritual centre for much of the central Sahara. Italian forces didn't capture it until 1931 — among the last places in Libya to fall to colonial conquest. The Great Man-Made River project was designed to pipe the fossil water north to the coast; construction began under Gaddafi and partially functions, though conflict-related disruption has slowed it significantly.

Beyond the oasis itself, the [Rebiana Sand Sea](/africa/libya/kufra/rebiana_sand_sea) stretches north and west — one of the largest ergs in the Sahara, and the reason the oasis exists as an island of green in an otherwise impassable landscape. Kufra was historically a starting point for trans-Saharan crossings south to Chad; that route remains extremely difficult and demands specialist logistics and current security knowledge.
