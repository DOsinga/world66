Tobruk is a port city on Libya's far eastern Mediterranean coast, set around one of the best natural harbours in North Africa. Its name is inseparable from the Second World War, when the town and surrounding desert became a hard-fought hinge in the North African campaign.

The harbour still explains the place. Tobruk's deep, sheltered anchorage made it valuable long before modern Libya, and made it almost impossible for either side to ignore during the war. Today the main reasons to stop are the [war cemeteries](/africa/libya/tobruk/tobruk_war_cemetery), the [German cemetery](/africa/libya/tobruk/german_war_cemetery), and the austere battlefield landscape west of town.

This is not a soft-edged seaside destination. It is a working port with sombre history and long distances in every direction. Travellers interested in desert war history will find it compelling; others may find it more meaningful as a stop between Cyrenaica and Egypt than as a destination in itself.
