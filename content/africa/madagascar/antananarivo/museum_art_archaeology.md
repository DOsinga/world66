The University of Madagascar's Museum of Art and Archaeology, located in the Isoraka neighbourhood, is the most academically serious museum in Antananarivo. The collection spans Malagasy history from prehistoric times through the nineteenth-century kingdom: ceramics, iron tools, woven textiles, ritual objects, royal regalia, and artefacts documenting the island's distinctive blend of African, Southeast Asian, and Arab cultural influences.

The museum benefits from its university setting — interpretive panels are thoughtful and the collection is well-organised. Temporary exhibitions on archaeological excavations and ethnographic research give it a sense of active inquiry rather than static display. It is less visited than the Andafiavaratra Palace but arguably offers more depth. The Isoraka neighbourhood around the museum is pleasant for a walk, with French-era buildings and small cafes.
