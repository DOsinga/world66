The Rova — meaning "fortified royal compound" in Malagasy — stands on the highest point of Antananarivo's ridge at 1,468 metres, and has dominated the city's skyline for four centuries. Originally a royal residence of the Merina kings and queens, the complex enclosed several palace buildings, royal tombs, and a Lutheran church within its stone ramparts. Queen Ranavalona I greatly expanded it in the nineteenth century, and for a time it was the symbolic heart of the Malagasy kingdom.

The 1995 fire left only the stone walls and foundations standing, but visiting the site remains worthwhile. The panorama from the hilltop is exceptional — all of Tana spreads below, with its stacked houses clinging to terraced ridges and the Hauts Plateaux stretching to the horizon. Guided tours explain the layout of the former palaces and the slow work of restoration. Even as a ruin, the Rova communicates the scale of Merina ambition and the depth of what was lost.
