Antsirabe is a highland town in Madagascar's central plateau, sitting at 1,500 metres elevation about 170 km south of Antananarivo. The altitude gives it a cooler climate than the coast, and Norwegian missionaries established it as a health resort in the 19th century. Today it is one of Madagascar's busiest market towns and an important centre for the [gem trade](/africa/madagascar/antsirabe/gem_market): street vendors and stalls sell precious and semi-precious stones, and in some places you can watch them being cut and polished.

The most memorable aspect of arriving in Antsirabe is the immediate swarm of [pousse-pousse](/africa/madagascar/antsirabe/pousse_pousse) drivers — operators of the rickshaw-like transport that is the town's signature. Competition is fierce and prices are negotiable; choose a young driver if you want to move at any speed. The [Our Lady of La Salette Cathedral](/africa/madagascar/antsirabe/our_lady_of_la_salette_cathedral), a red-brick neo-Gothic landmark with twin towers, anchors the town centre.

The town has several thermal springs; the [baths at Lac Ranomafana](/africa/madagascar/antsirabe/thermal_baths) (the name means "hot water") offer private cubicles and massages at low prices. The countryside beyond is worth a half-day: [Lac Andraikiba](/africa/madagascar/antsirabe/lac_andraikiba), 7 km west, is an easy cycle ride and good for swimming; Lac Tritiva, 20 km out, is more scenic but the road is rough and steep — start early. The small Merina village of Betafo is accessible by bicycle or taxi on a flatter road, and gives a sense of rural highland life.

Antsirabe is also a centre for [handicrafts](/africa/madagascar/antsirabe/handicrafts_market): the small shops on the main streets sell good-quality Malagasy artefacts, and bargaining is expected and normal.
