Manakara is the sea-end of the famous Fianarantsoa-Cote Est railway, the narrow-gauge line that drops from the highlands through forested hills to Madagascar's humid southeast coast. Most travellers arrive tired, dusty, and delighted that the train made it at all.

The town itself is low-key: a river mouth, a small port, coconut palms, sandy lanes, and long beaches where the Indian Ocean looks inviting but is often too rough for casual swimming. The real appeal is the combination of rail, coast, and canal. The [FCE railway station](/africa/madagascar/manakara/fce_railway_station) is the practical heart of town, while the [Pangalanes Canal](/africa/madagascar/manakara/pangalanes_canal) opens up a watery world of villages, pirogues, and narrow strips of land between lagoon and sea.

Manakara is not a beach resort, and that is useful to know before you come. It is better as a recovery stop after the rail journey, a base for a canal trip, and a place to feel the east coast's humid, battered, end-of-the-line character.
