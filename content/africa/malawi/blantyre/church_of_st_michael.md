The Church of St Michael and All Angels is one of the most remarkable buildings in Central Africa and the spiritual heart of Blantyre's Scottish mission heritage. Built between 1888 and 1891 entirely without nails, it was designed and constructed by the Reverend David Clement Scott, a Presbyterian missionary with no architectural training, and local labourers who had never built anything like it before.

The church is built from handmade brick — every brick fired on-site from local clay, in eighty-one different forms to accommodate the arches, domes, and flying buttresses of Scott's Latin cross design. The interior features a Byzantine arcade of six arches and a Moorish domed bell tower reached by a circular staircase. A plaque inside commemorates Lieutenant H. E. O'Neil's 1885 longitude determination, made here using 365 lunar observations.

Scott is said to have described the church as "the first permanent Christian church erected between the Zambezi and the Nile." Whatever the claim's accuracy, the building itself is extraordinary: a confident act of construction by people with no blueprints and no hardware store, deep in the African interior.
