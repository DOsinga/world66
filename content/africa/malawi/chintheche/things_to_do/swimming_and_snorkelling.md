Lake Malawi is one of Africa's great rift lakes — roughly 580 km long and up to 700 m deep — and the stretch of water off Chintheche is among its most inviting. The shore here is gently shelving white sand, the water is clear and warm (typically 24–28°C), and the lake barely has a tide, so conditions are calm almost every day during the dry season.

Snorkelling is excellent close to shore. Lake Malawi holds more species of fish than any other lake on earth, most of them cichlids endemic to the lake and found nowhere else. Even a basic snorkel and mask reveals an extraordinary range of colour: electric blue *Melanochromis*, yellow *Labidochromis*, and dozens of other mbuna species dart around the rocky areas just metres from the beach. Bring your own snorkel gear if you can — it is rarely available for hire locally.

Bilharzia (schistosomiasis) is a concern in many parts of Lake Malawi, but the risk around Chintheche and the wider northern lakeshore is generally considered low. The parasite thrives in shallow, slow-moving water with dense vegetation and snail populations; the cleaner, rockier northern shores are less hospitable to it. Most long-term residents swim freely here, but cautious travellers should seek up-to-date medical advice before arrival.
