Likoma Island is one of Lake Malawi's oddities: a Malawian island lying inside Mozambican waters, paired with smaller Chizumulu nearby. It is remote, slow, and more interesting than its size suggests, with sandy coves, baobabs, fishing villages, and a cathedral so large it seems to have arrived from another country.

The island's identity is split between lake life and missionary history. [St Peter's Cathedral](/africa/malawi/likoma_island/st_peters_cathedral) rises near the ferry landing, built from local stone in the early twentieth century. From there you can cross the island on foot in a couple of hours, climb small rocky viewpoints, or work around the shore by boat.

Likoma is not a polished resort island. The journey is part of the point: the old [MV Ilala](/africa/malawi/likoma_island/mv_ilala_ferry) ferry still gives the trip its most romantic and least predictable entrance. Come if you want a lake island with real village life and a sense of distance, not if you need everything to run on a tight schedule.
