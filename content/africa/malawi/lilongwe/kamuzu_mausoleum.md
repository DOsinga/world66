The Kamuzu Mausoleum in Lilongwe's New Town is the resting place of Hastings Kamuzu Banda, Malawi's first president and longtime dictator, who ruled from independence in 1964 until 1994. The mausoleum is an imposing structure set in formal gardens — a piece of Cold War-era African statecraft in concrete and marble — and is worth a visit for anyone interested in the country's complicated political history.

Banda's legacy in Malawi remains genuinely contested. He built the infrastructure of the modern state, moved the capital from Zomba to Lilongwe, and maintained stability of a sort — while also running a repressive one-party state with a notorious secret police force. The mausoleum does not dwell on these tensions, but standing here, with the parliament building nearby and the ghost of the Banda era still visible in the city's layout and architecture, invites reflection. Entrance is free; dress modestly.
