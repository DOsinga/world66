Malawi's third-largest city sits at 1,000 metres on a cool, open plateau in the Northern Region, about 370km north of [Lilongwe](/africa/malawi/lilongwe). It's a proper working city — a [university](/africa/malawi/mzuzu/mzuzu_university) town with a functioning airport and a commercial centre that has been growing steadily — rather than a tourist destination in its own right. Most travellers pass through on their way north or south, and that's not a bad instinct: Mzuzu is a useful base, not a highlight.

What makes it worth a stop is its position. The [Nyika Plateau](/africa/malawi/nyika) lies to the north, a vast 3,000-metre grassland with good wildlife, excellent hiking, and some of the finest views in Central Africa. The [Vwaza Marsh Game Reserve](/africa/malawi/vwaza) is to the northwest. The historic [Livingstonia Mission](/africa/malawi/livingstonia) sits dramatically on an escarpment above Lake Malawi to the northeast, and [Nkhata Bay](/africa/malawi/nkhata_bay) — with its lake beaches and diving — is a couple of hours to the southeast. Mzuzu airport has flights to Lilongwe, [Blantyre](/africa/malawi/blantyre), and Karonga, and twice-weekly flights serve Nyika directly.

The city itself has everything you need: banks, fuel, supermarkets, and decent restaurants. The [main market](/africa/malawi/mzuzu/mzuzu_main_market) in the centre is the most interesting place to spend an hour, and the [Mzuzu Coffee Den](/africa/malawi/mzuzu/mzuzu_coffee_den) — the flagship shop of the country's only Fairtrade coffee cooperative — is the obvious place to wait for a bus or meet other travellers. Roads to both Nyika and Vwaza can deteriorate badly in the rainy season (November to April), so check conditions before heading out.
