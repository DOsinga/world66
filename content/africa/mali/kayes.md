Kayes holds the unenviable distinction of being one of the hottest inhabited cities on Earth, with temperatures regularly exceeding 45 degrees Celsius in the dry season. The city sits on the Sénégal River in western Mali, near the Senegalese border, and served as the colonial capital of French Sudan before Bamako took over. It was an important node on the Dakar-Niger Railway — one of colonial Africa's great engineering projects — and that history is still visible in the buildings and infrastructure around the old train station.

The Sénégal River is the lifeblood of the region. Fifteen kilometres upstream, the [Chutes du Félou](/africa/mali/kayes/chutes_du_felou) drop 13 metres across a rocky shelf nearly a kilometre wide — one of the more dramatic waterfalls in West Africa, and historically the point where river navigation ended and overland travel began. Twelve kilometres from town, [Fort de Médine](/africa/mali/kayes/fort_medine) was the first French military post in the interior of West Africa, scene of a famous three-month siege in 1857. The old [Kayes train station](/africa/mali/kayes/kayes_train_station) remains a fine piece of late-colonial architecture and a reminder of the railway's central role in the region's history.

Come between November and February when temperatures are merely very hot rather than completely unbearable. Check current security conditions carefully before travelling — Mali has experienced significant instability in recent years, though Kayes in the far west has generally been more stable than the centre and north of the country.
