Built in 1855 by French General Louis Faidherbe on the left bank of the Sénégal River, twelve kilometres upstream from Kayes, Fort de Médine was the first permanent French military installation in the interior of West Africa. Its purpose was to control access to the river beyond the falls at Félou and to establish a foothold for the push inland into what is now Mali.

The fort's mud-brick walls, guard towers, and officers' quarters still stand in reasonable condition given their age. The site commands views over the river from a rocky promontory. The surrounding village of Médine is quiet and traditional. Combined with a visit to the nearby Félou Falls, the fort makes for a rewarding half-day excursion from Kayes. Local transport runs between Kayes and Médine; the road is rough but passable in dry season.
