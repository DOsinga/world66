Mopti is about halfway from [Bamako](/africa/mali/bamako) to [Timbuktu](/africa/mali/timbuktu), spread across three islands at the junction of the Niger and Bani rivers. Mostly inhabited by Fulani traders, it is a busy, colourful market town where various ethnic groups come to trade fish, salt, cloth, leather goods, masks, jewellery, amber, and intricately woven [Fulani wedding blankets](/africa/mali/mopti/fulani_blankets).

Mopti is the jumping-off point for [Dogon Country](/africa/mali/mopti/dogon_country) — a must-see on any Mali trip — and a necessary stop for planning the journey to [Timbuktu](/africa/mali/timbuktu). But the town is more than just a transit point. The [Grand Mosque](/africa/mali/mopti/grand_mosque), built in 1935 and modelled on the famous one in [Djenné](/africa/mali/djenne), is an impressive sight. The [Grand Marché](/africa/mali/mopti/grand_marche) in the old town (best on Thursday, market day) is full of dried fish and salt from the mines near Timbuktu. A pirogue trip on the river at dusk, easily arranged at the Bar Bozo waterfront near [Mopti Port](/africa/mali/mopti/mopti_port), is one of the most atmospheric experiences in West Africa.

Mopti is a lively, pleasant place to spend a few days, with good food and a laid-back atmosphere — though it is a haven for mosquitoes.

**Note**: The security situation in central Mali has been volatile since 2012 and has worsened significantly since 2021. Check current travel advisories before visiting.
