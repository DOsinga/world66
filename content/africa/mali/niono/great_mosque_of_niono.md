The entire community participates in this annual maintenance, making the preservation of the mosque a collective ritual rather than a professional task.

One of the finest examples of Sudano-Sahelian mosque architecture in Mali, the Great Mosque of Niono is a dramatic mud-brick structure with the characteristic tapering minarets and protruding wooden toron beams of the tradition. While less famous than the Grand Mosque of Djenné, it is architecturally significant and draws scholars and travellers interested in West African Islamic architecture.

The mosque was substantially rebuilt in its current form in 1948 by local craftsmen working in the same tradition that has shaped mosque building in this part of Mali for centuries. Non-Muslim visitors may not be permitted inside the prayer hall; respectful observation of the exterior and the surrounding mosque compound is generally welcomed. The building is at its most photogenic in the afternoon light.
