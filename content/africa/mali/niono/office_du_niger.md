story: >
  The Office du Niger was originally conceived in the 1920s as a scheme to grow cotton for French textile mills using Malian labour and the waters of the Niger River. The cotton plan largely failed — the soil and climate proved better suited to rice — and the scheme pivoted to food production after independence. Today the Office du Niger manages over 100,000 hectares of irrigated land and produces roughly a third of Mali's domestically consumed rice, making this flat, canal-laced landscape one of the country's most important food-producing regions.
---

The Office du Niger is a semi-autonomous government agency headquartered in Niono that administers one of the largest irrigation schemes in West Africa. The network of canals and rice fields extends across hundreds of kilometres of the Ségou Region, drawing water from the Niger River and turning otherwise marginal land into productive rice paddies. The landscape it creates — flat, green, laced with irrigation channels — is striking in contrast to the dry Sahel that surrounds it.

For visitors to Niono, the Office du Niger explains the character of the town: an administrative and agricultural hub rather than a historic trading centre. The canals and paddy fields are visible from any road in the area. Those interested in agricultural development and colonial-era hydraulic engineering will find the scheme worth understanding.
