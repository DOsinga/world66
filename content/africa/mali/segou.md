Segou is the historic capital of the Bambara people, a calm, tree-lined city of about 65,000 on the banks of the Niger River, some 235 kilometres northeast of [Bamako](/africa/mali/bamako). After the dust and congestion of the capital, Segou feels like a deep breath — wide streets, friendly people, and a pace of life that invites you to slow down.

The city is best known through Maryse Condé's historical novel *Segu*, which chronicles the Bambara kingdom. The remains of Mbella, the ancient Bambara capital, lie nearby. Monday is market day at the [Grand Marché](/africa/mali/segou/market), and it is the best time to find the bright hand-woven fabrics for which Segou is famous. The [Nieli Rug Cooperative](/africa/mali/segou/nielirugcoop) lets you buy those fabrics directly from the weavers who make them.

The [Niger River bank](/africa/mali/segou/niger_river_bank) is the heart of the city's daily life — pirogues, fishermen, and the extraordinary light on the water at sunset. The [pottery villages](/africa/mali/segou/segou_pottery) south of town produce distinctive red-brown ceramics using techniques unchanged for generations. A rewarding day trip runs to [Niono](/africa/mali/niono), locally known as the Venice of Mali, whose mud mosque rivals the more famous one in Djenné.
