**Segu** by Maryse Condé (1984) is the novel most closely associated with the city. Set in the late 18th century, it follows several generations of a Bambara family through the period of Islamic jihad, slave trading, and the collapse of the Bambara kingdom. Condé — a Guadeloupean writer — spent years researching the history of Segou, and the novel brings the ancient capital vividly to life. It is one of the most important novels to come out of the francophone African literary tradition.

**Travelling the Sahara** and the broader genre of West Africa travel literature that covers Mali tends to position Segou as the river city before the harder road north to Djenné and Mopti. Ryszard Kapuściński's essays on West Africa touch on the region's rhythms and landscapes in ways that are helpful context before or after a visit.
