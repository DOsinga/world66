Akjoujt is a small mining town in western [Mauritania](/africa/mauritania), set on the long paved road between [Nouakchott](/africa/mauritania/nouakchott) and [Atar](/africa/mauritania/atar). Most travellers see it through a dusty windscreen: a fuel stop, a place to buy water, and a sign that the capital's sprawl has finally given way to the open Sahara.

The town's life revolves around the copper and gold deposits at [Guelb Moghrein](/africa/mauritania/akjoujt/guelb_moghrein_mine), a few kilometres west of the centre. Mining here is not new; the Akjoujt area has traces of ancient copper working, and modern extraction has shaped the town's fortunes since the late twentieth century. It is not a pretty place, but it is a revealing one if you are interested in the resource economy behind Mauritania's desert roads.

For visitors, Akjoujt is best treated as a practical halt rather than a destination. Use it to break the journey north, drink tea in the market area, and check road conditions before continuing toward the Adrar plateau or back to the coast.
