Nouadhibou is Mauritania's second city and its main port, sitting on the narrow [Cap Blanc peninsula](/africa/mauritania/nouadhibou/cap_blanc) that juts into the Atlantic on the border with Western Sahara. For most travellers it is a transit point — the entry from Morocco after the long desert crossing, or the departure point for the [iron ore train](/africa/mauritania/nouadhibou/iron_ore_train) to [Zouerate](/africa/mauritania/zouerate).

The city itself is functional rather than beautiful, but it has one of the world's most remarkable sights for the adventurous: a vast [ship graveyard](/africa/mauritania/nouadhibou/ship_graveyard) offshore, where dozens of rusting hulks lie beached in the shallow waters. The [fishing port](/africa/mauritania/nouadhibou/fishing_port) is busy and atmospheric, and the Cap Blanc peninsula has seal colonies that are among the last populations of Mediterranean monk seals in the world. South of the peninsula tip lies [La Güera](/africa/mauritania/nouadhibou/la_guera), an abandoned Spanish town slowly being swallowed by the dunes.
