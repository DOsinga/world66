  The Marché Marocaine was established by Moroccan traders who came south during the colonial and post-colonial periods. Morocco and Mauritania have historic ties — much of northern Mauritania was once part of what the Moroccan sultans considered their sphere — and there remains a significant Moroccan commercial community in Nouakchott today.
---

The Moroccan market in southern Nouakchott has a different character from the Marché Capitale — smaller, more organised, with a stronger emphasis on clothing, textiles, and household goods. It's less overwhelming than the main market and often easier to navigate for visitors who want to shop or observe without the full intensity of the central souk.

The adjacent Marocaine Mosque, which gives the district its name, is one of the larger mosques in this part of the city. The neighbourhood around it has a slightly more prosperous feel than the densely populated central districts — a reminder that Nouakchott's internal geography reflects exactly who arrived when, and from where.
