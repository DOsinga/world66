The most remarkable thing about Oualata is what's on its walls. Every house in the old town is decorated by women with intricate geometric patterns in red, white and black pigments made from natural materials — ochre, limestone, and charcoal. The patterns are not purely decorative: they carry genealogical and social meaning, passed from mother to daughter across generations.

Walking through the mud-brick lanes, you can watch the art change from house to house, doorway to doorway. The most elaborate decorations frame the main entrance; interiors are often even more elaborately painted than the exteriors. Ask a resident politely and you may be invited inside to see.
