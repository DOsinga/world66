Rosso is a border town on the southern bank of the Senegal River, sitting directly across the water from the Senegalese town of the same name. It is Mauritania's main land crossing into Senegal and, for most travellers, little more than a day's business — a visa stamp, a wait for the ferry, a handshake with a fixer. But the town has a character worth pausing for, even briefly.

It lies in the Trarza region, Mauritania's fertile southwestern strip where most of the country's agricultural production is concentrated. The landscape around Rosso is noticeably greener than the desert interior, and the river itself is wide and slow-moving. The population is predominantly Black Mauritanian — Wolof, Halpulaar, and Soninke communities whose ties to Senegal are closer, in many cases, than to the Arab-Berber north. The ferry landing is the liveliest corner of town: pirogues, taxis-brousse, vendors selling cold drinks, and a steady procession of people navigating one of West Africa's busier informal border crossings.

If you are crossing into Senegal, Saint-Louis is about an hour south by road — a genuinely beautiful city worth at least a night. In the other direction, Nouakchott is roughly four hours north on a well-paved road.
