Al Hoceima is a small Mediterranean beach town on the Rif coast of northern [Morocco](/africa/morocco), a pleasant place to rest between the mountain roads of the Rif. The town was built by the Spanish in 1926, after they suppressed the Rif rebellion led by Abd el-Krim, and Spanish influences are still visible in the architecture and the town's relaxed character.

The [Plage Quemado](/africa/morocco/alhoceima/plage_quemado) sits right in the centre of town, wedged between two massive sea cliffs. Quieter beaches lie a few kilometres out on the road to [Nador](/africa/morocco/nador). From certain points along this road you can catch a glimpse of the [Peñón de Alhucemas](/africa/morocco/alhoceima/penon_de_alhucemas), a tiny Spanish-held island with a white church and tower sitting in the Mediterranean blue — still technically sovereign Spanish territory, garrisoned by a small military force. The [Al Hoceima National Park](/africa/morocco/alhoceima/al_hoceima_national_park), protecting coastline and forested mountains to the west, is worth exploring for its sea caves, cliff-nesting falcons, and isolated beaches accessible only by boat.
