The abandoned tuberculosis sanatorium at Ben Smime, twenty kilometres north of Azrou on the road to Meknes, is one of the more eerie places in the Middle Atlas. Built by the French in the 1930s to take advantage of the cool, dry mountain air, it operated until the 1970s and has stood empty ever since.

The buildings are sprawling and largely intact — wards, corridors, the chapel, the kitchens — and locally are linked, fairly or not, to a conspiracy around the death of King Mohammed V in 1961 and reports of paranormal activity. The genuine interest is the architecture and the setting. The site is unsecured but enter with care; floors and stairs are in poor condition, and it is best visited in daylight with company.
