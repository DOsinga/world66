---
title: "Bars and Cafes"
type: section
---

Morocco's relationship with alcohol is complex, and Casablanca is the city where that complexity is most visible. The big hotels have proper bars; the Ain Diab Corniche has licensed beach clubs and late-night venues; and the city has a genuine bar culture, mostly in the downtown and Corniche areas, that operates with a certain discretion but is not hard to find.

The café culture is entirely different — and more Moroccan. Street-level cafés serving mint tea, coffee, and *msemen* (flatbreads) are everywhere, and sitting in one for an hour is one of the better ways to watch the city. The cafés around the Marché Central and along Boulevard Mohammed V are the most convivial.

Rick's Café (Old Medina, near Rue Sour Djdid) is the famous one — a recreation of the Bogart film set, now a good restaurant and bar with live piano. La Sqala, in the fortification walls at the edge of the medina, has a beautiful courtyard garden and good Moroccan food alongside drinks. For something more local, the bars along the Corniche at Ain Diab get lively on weekend evenings and stay open late.
