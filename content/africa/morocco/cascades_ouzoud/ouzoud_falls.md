The Ouzoud Falls are the tallest waterfalls in Morocco — three separate streams tumbling 110 metres over a red-rock cliff into a wide emerald pool below. The approach through the olive groves from the car park above gives little warning of the scale; the moment the falls come into view is genuinely striking.

The path down to the base takes about twenty minutes and passes several café terraces with views of the falls. Barbary macaques have made the falls their territory — habituated to tourists, they are bold enough to approach tables and will steal food without hesitation. Do not feed them; it is bad for the monkeys and bad for your lunch. At the bottom, the pool is swimmable from May to October and the water is cool even in summer. A network of paths along the riverbank leads past smaller cascades and swimming holes that are quieter than the main pool.
