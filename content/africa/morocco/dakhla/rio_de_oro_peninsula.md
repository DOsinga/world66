The Río de Oro Peninsula is the narrow spit of land on which Dakhla sits. It extends about 40 km into the Atlantic Ocean from the coast of Western Sahara, creating the sheltered lagoon on its eastern side and leaving the open Atlantic on the west. The peninsula is mostly flat, sandy Saharan desert — remarkable for its emptiness and for the quality of light.

Driving or cycling along the peninsula rewards patience: the beaches on the Atlantic side are completely empty for long stretches, and the dunes near the tip are dramatic. The sandscapes change colour through the day, and the silence is absolute once you are away from the kitesurfing camps. If you have your own transport, exploring the length of the peninsula is one of the better days out from the city.
