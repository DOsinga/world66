  When Spain abandoned the territory in 1975 after the Green March, the cathedral was left intact and locked. For decades after, a rusting iron sign hung from the chain on the gate reading "Propiedad del Gobierno Español" — "Property of the Spanish government". It was technically true; no agreement on the building was ever reached. The sign is gone now but the building remains.

The Cathedral of Saint Francis of Assisi was built by Spain in the 1950s when Western Sahara was still the Spanish colony of Río de Oro and Saguía el-Hamra. The plain white modernist building, with a tall slim bell tower and a stone-flagged forecourt, sits in the southern "lower town". For decades after the Spanish withdrawal it was kept locked and minimally maintained; in recent years it has been used occasionally for services by the small Catholic community of African migrants and UN staff.

The building is more interesting from outside than in. Pair a visit here with a walk through the surrounding Souk Djmaa neighbourhood, where the distinctive eggshell-domed white houses Spain built for the Sahrawi quarters remain.
