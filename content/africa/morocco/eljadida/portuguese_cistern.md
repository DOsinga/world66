The Portuguese Cistern is the most extraordinary interior space in El Jadida, and one of the strangest rooms in all of Morocco. Built in the early sixteenth century as a water storage vault inside the Portuguese fortress, it fell into disuse and was only rediscovered in 1916, when a local merchant knocked through a wall and revealed the vaulted chamber beyond. It had been sealed, and partly filled with rubble, for over a century.

What you find inside is a forest of Gothic columns rising from a floor that is usually covered by a shallow mirror of water. The reflections double the arches and vaults, creating an infinite recession of stone and light that has an almost hallucinatory quality. The cistern is lit partly through a single circular opening in the ceiling, which sends a shaft of light down into the dimness. Even on a crowded day it is a genuinely arresting place. Allow time to simply stand in it.
