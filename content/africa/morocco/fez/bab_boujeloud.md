This is the gate you will see on every postcard of Fez, and it earns it. The horseshoe arch framed in blue-and-white zellige tilework is genuinely beautiful, especially in the late afternoon light when the tiles deepen in colour. Stand on the small square outside and you are looking at the entrance to the oldest part of the medina; step through and you are on the threshold of a thousand years of continuous habitation.

Two streets fork just inside the gate. Talaa Kebira, the main artery, climbs steeply to the right past [Bou Inania Madrasa](/africa/morocco/fez/bou_inania_madrasa) and [Dar al-Magana](/africa/morocco/fez/dar_al_magana). Talaa Seghira takes the quieter, lower route. Both lead eventually down to [Al-Qarawiyyin](/africa/morocco/fez/al_qarawiyyin). The square outside the gate has several cheap restaurants with rooftop terraces that look back at it — decent for a mint tea, not much else.
