The hilltop fortress north of the medina is worth the uphill walk for the view alone. From the ramparts you get a sweeping panorama across Fes el-Bali that rivals the one from the [Merinid Tombs](/africa/morocco/fez/merinid_tombs) but with fewer hawkers. On a clear day you can pick out the green-tiled roof of [Al-Qarawiyyin](/africa/morocco/fez/al_qarawiyyin) and trace the labyrinth of streets around it.

Inside, the arms museum covers Moroccan military history from prehistoric flints to ornate nineteenth-century sabres. The star piece is the massive cannon from the Battle of the Three Kings, but the collection of Berber daggers and Ottoman firearms is also worth lingering over. The museum was renovated in 2016 and the displays are well-presented. About twenty minutes on foot from Bab Boujeloud, following the road that climbs along the northern ridge.
