Where the Merinid madrasas dazzle with dense ornament, the Cherratine Madrasa impresses by sheer scale. Built in 1670 by Sultan Moulay Rachid, the founder of the Alaouite dynasty that still rules Morocco, it is the largest madrasa in Fez, with over a hundred student cells arranged around multiple courtyards. The decoration is restrained compared to [Bou Inania](/africa/morocco/fez/bou_inania_madrasa) or [Attarine](/africa/morocco/fez/attarine_madrasa) — carved plaster and zellige are present but sparser, letting the architecture speak for itself.

The difference in style tells a political story. The Merinid sultans built madrasas as showcases of dynastic prestige; the Alaouites, coming to power after a period of chaos, were making a statement about order and function. The result is a building that feels more like a working college than a jewel box. The Cherratine Madrasa is near the Qarawiyyin complex and is sometimes overlooked by visitors who have already seen the more decorated competitors — but the scale of the courtyards and the rows of tiny student rooms are worth seeing in their own right.
