This is one of the most atmospheric buildings in Fez, precisely because no one has cleaned it up. Dar Glaoui was one of several residences belonging to the Glaoui family, the powerful pashas of Marrakesh who controlled much of southern Morocco during the French Protectorate. After independence, the family was disgraced, and the palace was left to decay. Whole rooms survive with their zellige floors and carved plaster walls intact, while others are open to the sky.

A caretaker will show you through the complex of courtyards and salons. Some of the decorative work rivals anything in the [Bou Inania Madrasa](/africa/morocco/fez/bou_inania_madrasa) — intricate carved cedar, painted ceilings, fountain basins — but here it is cracked and peeling, with weeds pushing through the tiles. The effect is melancholy and beautiful. The palace is in the southern part of Fes el-Bali, not far from [Dar Batha](/africa/morocco/fez/dar_batha). A tip to the caretaker is expected.
