The leather shops clustered around and below the Chouara Tannery sell the finished products of the trade you just watched from the terraces above: bags, belts, jackets, wallets, ottomans, and the soft pointed babouche slippers in every colour imaginable. Quality varies enormously. The best leather is vegetable-tanned using the traditional methods you can see in the vats below — it is supple, smells of leather rather than chemicals, and ages well. Cheaper goods are chrome-tanned and have a harsher chemical smell that never fully goes away. Check the stitching, feel the weight, and trust your nose. Prices start absurdly high; bargain hard and expect to pay roughly a third of the opening ask. Walking away is the most effective negotiating tool you have.
