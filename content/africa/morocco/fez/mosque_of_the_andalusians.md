Founded in 859 or 860 by Mariam al-Fihri, sister of the woman who founded [Al-Qarawiyyin](/africa/morocco/fez/al_qarawiyyin), this mosque anchors the Andalusian Quarter on the east bank of the Fez River. The two mosques and the two quarters they serve have defined the geography of Fes el-Bali for over a thousand years. Non-Muslims cannot enter, but the monumental north gate — added by the Almohads in the thirteenth century — is one of the finest pieces of carved stonework in the city.

The Andalusian Quarter around the mosque is quieter and less touristed than the area west of the river. The streets are narrower, the commercial activity is more local, and you are far less likely to be approached by unofficial guides. [Sahrij Madrasa](/africa/morocco/fez/sahrij_madrasa) is nearby and worth seeing. The walk over from the [Place Seffarine](/africa/morocco/fez/place_seffarine) side crosses the river on a small bridge and gives you a sense of how the medina divides into its two historic halves.
