You hear Place Seffarine before you see it. The rhythmic clang of hammers on copper and brass carries through the surrounding alleys, and when you emerge into the open square, you find men working exactly as their predecessors did centuries ago — crouching over cauldrons and trays, shaping the metal by hand. This is one of the oldest squares in the medina and one of the most atmospheric, precisely because nothing about it is staged for visitors.

The square is bordered by the entrance to the Qarawiyyin Library and the fourteenth-century Saffarin Madrasa, both worth a look. Nearby is [Hammam Seffarine](/africa/morocco/fez/hammam_seffarine), one of the oldest working bathhouses in Morocco. If you want to buy copperwork, this is the place — you are buying directly from the makers, not from middlemen, and the prices reflect that.
