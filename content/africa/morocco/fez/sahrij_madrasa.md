Named for its large rectangular ablution pool — *sahrij* in Arabic — this madrasa was built in 1321 by Sultan Abu al-Hasan, the same ruler responsible for much of the finest Merinid architecture in Fez. The pool dominates the courtyard and gives the space a calm, reflective quality that the more famous madrasas lack. The carved stucco and zellige work is refined without being overwhelming.

Sahrij Madrasa sits on the east bank of the Fez River in the Andalusian Quarter, a part of the medina that sees far fewer visitors than the streets around [Al-Qarawiyyin](/africa/morocco/fez/al_qarawiyyin). It is paired with the neighbouring Sbai'yyin Madrasa, and the two were designed as a complementary set. If you are visiting the [Mosque of the Andalusians](/africa/morocco/fez/mosque_of_the_andalusians), the madrasa is a short walk away and well worth combining.
