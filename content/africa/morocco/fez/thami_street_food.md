The area around Bab Rcif, on the eastern edge of Fes el-Bali, is where Fez's street food scene comes alive. From late afternoon onwards, stalls and carts set up selling grilled kefta and merguez sandwiches, fried sardines, bowls of snail soup (babouche, served with a toothpick and heavily spiced broth), and msemen — the flaky, layered flatbread cooked on a griddle, sometimes stuffed with cheese or honey. A full meal here costs 15-30 dirham, making it the cheapest eating in the city.

The snail soup is worth trying even if the idea puts you off. The small snails are cooked in a peppery, anise-flavoured broth and served in a bowl with the shells — you fish them out with a toothpick and drink the broth. It is a Fassi street food staple and the vendors near Bab Rcif do it well. The fried fish stalls are also excellent. Come in the evening when the area is busiest and the food is freshest. This is not sanitised tourist food — expect to eat standing up or on a shared bench, surrounded by locals.
