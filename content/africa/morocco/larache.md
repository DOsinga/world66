Larache is a relaxed Atlantic coast town where few tourists venture, making it a good place to experience everyday Moroccan life without the hustle of the more popular destinations. The town sits beside an estuary that opens into the Atlantic, with sea cliffs to the west and tangerine and orange groves to the south.

The old [medina and Spanish colonial new town](/africa/morocco/larache/medina) are worth a wander, as is the [Château de la Cigogne](/africa/morocco/larache/chateau_de_la_cigogne) (Stork Castle) on its clifftop above the estuary mouth. The crumbling walls that once surrounded the medina and kasbah add character, and the new town beyond has attractive Spanish colonial architecture in white and blue. A pleasant morning activity is taking a boat taxi from the [port](/africa/morocco/larache/port_and_fish_market) across the estuary to the [beach](/africa/morocco/larache/plage_de_larache) on the far side.

Four to five kilometres outside town are the Roman remains of [Lixus](/africa/morocco/larache/lixus), one of the oldest settlements in North Africa — and, according to ancient legend, the site of the Garden of the Hesperides. The site is less monumental than [Volubilis](/africa/morocco/volubilis), but watching the sunset from the Phoenician temple above the river is a memorable experience.

Larache is also where Jean Genet, the French novelist, chose to be buried. His grave in the [Spanish Cemetery](/africa/morocco/larache/spanish_cemetery) on the southern edge of town draws a quiet stream of literary visitors. It is as good a reason as any to pause here rather than pass through.
