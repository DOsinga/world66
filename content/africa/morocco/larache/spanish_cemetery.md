On the southern edge of town, the Spanish Cemetery holds the graves of the Spanish colonists who stayed and died in Larache during the protectorate years and after. The cemetery is calm and well-kept, with whitewashed walls and rows of modest headstones shaded by trees.

The reason most visitors come is Jean Genet, the French novelist and playwright, who chose Larache as his final resting place. Genet lived in the town intermittently during his last years and is buried in a simple grave in the Christian section. The grave is easy to find — ask the caretaker or look for the small bunch of flowers that admirers occasionally leave.

The cemetery is open during daylight hours and entry is free. It is a short walk or taxi ride from the medina.
