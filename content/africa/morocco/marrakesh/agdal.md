The Agdal is the vast royal garden district that stretches south of the Kasbah walls, covering an area of some 400 hectares that has been cultivated continuously since the 12th century. Created by the Almohad rulers as a working agricultural estate and pleasure garden, the Agdal contains olive orchards, citrus groves, and vineyards irrigated by a complex system of channels fed from the Atlas mountains. At its heart is the Sahraj el Hana, a large ornamental lake surrounded by pavilions where the sultans retreated from the heat of summer.

The gardens are royal property and access is restricted to Fridays and Sundays when they are open to the public, and to the rare occasions when the king is not in residence. On those open days, Marrakshis come in family groups to walk under the olives, picnic beside the lake, and rent pedaloes. The scale of the gardens — you can walk for an hour through uninterrupted groves — is extraordinary, and the contrast between the lush green interior and the hot ochre city outside the walls is dramatic.
