The Sahraj el Hana is the great ornamental lake at the heart of the Agdal royal gardens — a vast rectangular basin originally constructed for irrigation that became, over the centuries, a royal pleasure ground. The pavilions at either end of the lake were used by successive sultans for picnics and regattas; one of these pavilions is where the Alaouite sultan Mohammed IV drowned in 1873 when the boat he was sailing capsized in a sudden squall.

When the Agdal is open to the public on Fridays and Sundays (and when the king is not in residence), families rent pedaloes on the lake and spread picnics under the olive trees. The scale of the basin — some 200 metres across — and the surrounding orchards create an impression of rural abundance in the middle of a dusty city. On a clear day the Atlas mountains are reflected in the water.
