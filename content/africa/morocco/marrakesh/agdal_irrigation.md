The Agdal gardens survive because of an extraordinary piece of pre-industrial hydraulic engineering: the khettara, a network of underground channels that brings water from the foothills of the Atlas mountains to the Marrakesh plain. The system was constructed over several centuries beginning in the Almoravid period and represents one of the great engineering achievements of medieval North Africa.

The khettara channels run underground for up to 40 kilometres, tapping the water table along their route and delivering it by gravity to the gardens and fountains of Marrakesh. Surface inspection holes — small covered shafts visible as a dotted line across the landscape — mark the route. The system has been in continuous operation for nearly a thousand years, though modern groundwater extraction has reduced its flow significantly. Walking along the route of one of the khettara channels, visible in the landscape south of the Agdal, gives a sense of the engineering ambition that made Marrakesh possible.
