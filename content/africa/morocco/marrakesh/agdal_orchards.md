The Agdal's 400 hectares contain some of the oldest cultivated olive trees in Morocco — gnarled, multi-century specimens in orchards that have been continuously tended since the Almohad era. Between the olive groves are orange and pomegranate orchards, vegetable gardens, and the channels of the ancient khettara irrigation system that brings water from the Atlas foothills to these gardens.

The royal garden still functions as a working agricultural estate producing olive oil, citrus, and other crops. Walking through the orchards on an open day — the smell of orange blossom in spring, the cool shade of the olives in summer — is a genuinely sensory experience and a reminder that Marrakesh's red walls enclose not just a city but a managed landscape designed for pleasure and production simultaneously.
