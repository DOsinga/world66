The Agdal gardens contain several royal pavilions built at different periods to provide shaded retreats beside the lake. The Dar el Hana (House of Pleasure) at the north end of the Sahraj el Hana is the most significant — a restored Almohad structure with painted wooden ceilings and tiled floors that give a sense of the architectural refinement applied even to these garden buildings.

The pavilions are not routinely open for interior visits, but the exteriors are visible on public open days and the relationship between the pavilion architecture and the landscape — each building positioned to give maximum views across the water and orchards — is instructive about the traditional Moroccan understanding of garden design as an art form integrated with architecture rather than merely a setting for it.
