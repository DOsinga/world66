The southern wall of the Agdal marks the outer limit of the historic city and runs for several kilometres through what is now the outer residential districts of Marrakesh. This wall, less celebrated than the medina ramparts, is in many places in remarkably good condition — the pisé (rammed earth) construction has survived centuries because of both its quality and the relative remoteness of this southern edge.

Walking along the external face of the Agdal wall — accessible from the roads south of the Kasbah — gives a sense of the scale of the royal garden complex and the relationship between the city and the agricultural landscape that surrounds it. The Atlas mountains are visible at the end of the straight roads running south from the walls on clear winter days, giving the best mountain panorama available from within the city.
