The square immediately inside the Bab Doukkala gate has several cafes that serve the local market traders, taxi drivers, and residents — practical places with cheap mint tea and coffee, msemen, and beghrir (Moroccan pancakes) in the morning, and simple tagines and sandwiches through the day. The clientele is almost entirely Moroccan.

This is the kind of cafe that exists throughout Morocco but becomes hard to find when tourism has colonised an area: no English menu, no tourist prices, no concession to the expectations of foreign visitors. It functions well as a resting point between walking the northern walls and heading south into the souks, and the tea here tastes the same as the tea in the medina's tourist cafes at one-fifth of the price.
