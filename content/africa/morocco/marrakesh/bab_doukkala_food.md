The streets around Bab Doukkala have a good concentration of inexpensive street food — the kind of neighbourhood eating that exists throughout Morocco but is often crowded out of tourist areas by more expensive alternatives. Kefta sandwiches, msemen filled with honey, fried fish from the market, and fresh orange juice are the staples, sold from carts and small open-fronted shops.

The evening food scene around the gate square is particularly lively — traders set up around sunset with grills and hot tajines, the square fills with local people, and the atmosphere is convivial without the performance element that characterises Jemaa el-Fnaa. Eating here costs a fraction of the tourist restaurants and the food is essentially the same Moroccan cooking in less elaborate contexts.
