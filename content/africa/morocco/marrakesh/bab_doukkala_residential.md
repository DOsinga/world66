The residential streets south of the Bab Doukkala mosque and market are among the least touristed in the medina — a working neighbourhood where several thousand people live in the kind of compact, layered urban fabric that Marrakesh developed over a thousand years. The lanes are narrow, shaded, and oriented around the neighbourhood mosque and hammam rather than towards any tourist attraction.

Walking here requires neither a map nor a purpose — the streets eventually lead either back to a known gate or forward to the main souks. The experience of getting mildly lost in the Bab Doukkala residential quarter, where the chances of encountering another tourist are low, gives a better sense of medina life than the curated visitor circuits. Children, cats, motorbikes, the smell of bread and spices, the sound of a television from a courtyard — the medina at its most ordinary.
