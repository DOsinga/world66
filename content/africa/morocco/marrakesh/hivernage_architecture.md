The French laid out Hivernage in the 1920s and 1930s as a European-style resort district with a particular aesthetic vision: wide avenues, Spanish-Moroccan hybrid villas, and the kind of ordered calm that was meant to signal colonial competence and comfort. The architecture is a fusion of art deco European residential design with Moroccan decorative elements — zellige tilework, horseshoe arches, decorated plasterwork — applied to houses that in their basic structure would not look out of place in a Parisian suburb.

The finest surviving examples are concentrated in the streets between the Mamounia and the Theatre Royal. Several have been converted to use as boutique hotels or riad-style guesthouses, preserving the interiors in reasonable condition. Architectural enthusiasts will find more variety in Gueliz's commercial buildings, but Hivernage's residential fabric is historically significant as a document of the Protectorate era's architectural ambitions.
