The Kasbah at night has a quality that is almost impossible to find elsewhere in the medina: silence. Once the Saadian Tombs close and the day visitors retreat north towards Jemaa el-Fnaa, the Kasbah's lanes are left to the neighbourhood itself. Streetlamps illuminate the mosque minaret; cats move through empty alleys; occasionally a door opens and you glimpse the lit courtyard of a riad.

The Kasbah square at night hosts a small food market less theatrical than Jemaa el-Fnaa. Grilled chicken, harira soup, and fresh orange juice from carts that serve the neighbourhood rather than tourists. A walk through the Kasbah after dinner, heading south towards the walls where you can see the Atlas silhouette against the dark sky, is one of the quieter pleasures of Marrakesh.
