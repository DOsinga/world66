The Mellah has become one of the better areas in Marrakesh for finding good food outside the tourist circuit. Several restaurants have opened in the lanes around the Bahia Palace catering to a mix of Marrakshi residents and savvy visitors — the prices are reasonable and the cooking tends to be more straightforwardly Moroccan than the theatrical presentations of the medina's palace restaurants.

The area around Place des Ferblantiers (the tinsmithing square at the northern edge of the Mellah) has the highest concentration of eating options. Several stalls on the square serve simple Moroccan food — merguez sandwiches, fried fish, kefta — at the kind of prices that suggest a local rather than tourist clientele. The square itself, ringed by workshops where lanterns and metalwork are made, is one of the more authentic corners of the southern medina.
