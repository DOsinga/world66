The Mellah was established in the 16th century by the Saadian sultan as a designated quarter for Marrakesh's Jewish community — one of the largest in Morocco. At its peak in the early 20th century the Mellah housed around 20,000 people in conditions of extraordinary density. Following Moroccan independence and successive waves of emigration to Israel and France, the Jewish population has almost entirely gone, but the physical fabric of the quarter survives.

The distinctive Mellah architecture — Andalusian-influenced houses with wooden-balconied upper floors projecting over the narrow lanes, a building style unique in Morocco — is well preserved along the main streets. The old synagogues, some restored and some derelict, punctuate the residential streets. The Lazama Synagogue is open to visitors and contains a beautiful courtyard garden. Understanding the Mellah requires some historical knowledge — there is little signposting — but the Maison de la Photographie and several guidebooks provide the context.
