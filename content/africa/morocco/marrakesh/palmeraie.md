The Palmeraie is the vast palm grove that stretches north and east of Marrakesh for some 13,000 hectares, encircling the city with a landscape of date palms that legend attributes to the pits discarded by soldiers of the Almoravid army. The palms are real, though the grove is considerably less dense than tourist photographs suggest — urban encroachment, groundwater depletion, and the Red Palm Weevil have all thinned it significantly since the mid-20th century.

What the Palmeraie offers today is the city's most luxurious accommodation — a string of boutique riads, resort hotels, and private villas hidden behind high walls and reached by bumpy piste roads through the palms. Quad biking and camel rides operate from the main roads for day visitors. The area is also home to several upscale restaurants and spa retreats that serve both guests and non-residents. For those wanting to experience the Palmeraie without staying here, a bicycle or scooter journey out from Gueliz along the Route de Fès in the early morning, before the dust of the quad bikes, gives the best sense of the grove.
