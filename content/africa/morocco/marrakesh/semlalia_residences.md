Semlalia is primarily a residential neighbourhood of middle-class Marrakshi families — apartment buildings from the 1970s and 1980s alongside older French Protectorate villas now divided into flats. The neighbourhood has the character of a place that functions entirely for its own residents: schools, pharmacies, hardware shops, and the ordinary commerce of a city district that does not need to perform itself for visitors.

Walking through Semlalia on a weekday morning gives a more honest picture of modern Marrakesh than either the medina's historic intensity or Gueliz's cosmopolitan new town. The apartment blocks here house teachers, civil servants, shopkeepers — the Marrakshi middle class that makes the city function. It is not a destination in the conventional travel sense, but it is an interesting lens through which to see the city.
