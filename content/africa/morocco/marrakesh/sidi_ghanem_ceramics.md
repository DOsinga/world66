Marrakesh's ceramic tradition produces some of Morocco's most distinctive work — the characteristically patterned zellige tiles, the terracotta tajines, the boldly coloured bowls and vases — and Sidi Ghanem is where the contemporary evolution of this tradition is happening. Several ceramic studios in the district work with traditional Moroccan forms and materials but produce work that is clearly rooted in contemporary design thinking.

The studios welcome visitors who want to see the production process. Zellige tile-cutting, in particular, is a skill that takes years to learn and is fascinating to watch — each tile individually cut and shaped with a special hammer and chisel before assembly into the geometric patterns that cover the walls of Marrakesh's architecture. The studios also sell finished work directly, and buying ceramics in Sidi Ghanem rather than the medina souks means provenance, quality control, and prices that reflect the actual cost of production.
