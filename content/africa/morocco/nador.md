Nador is not the prettiest town on Morocco's Mediterranean coast, but it is one of the most interesting. It sits beside the broad, shallow [Marchica Lagoon](/africa/morocco/nador/marchica_lagoon), close enough to the Spanish enclave of [Melilla](/europe/spain/melilla) that Spanish, Riffian, Arabic, and ferry-port pragmatism all mix in the streets.

The city is a working Rif capital rather than a polished resort. The corniche fills in the evening, the souks are busy with cross-border goods, and the hills behind town rise quickly toward [Mount Gurugu](/africa/morocco/nador/mount_gurugu). Travellers usually come through Nador on the way to Melilla, Al Hoceima, or the eastern Rif, but it rewards a night if you like borderland cities with more texture than postcard charm.

The main reason to linger is the lagoon. Marchica is being reshaped by resort development, but it remains a huge saltwater wetland with birdlife, fishing boats, and big skies. Use Nador as a practical base: walk the waterfront, take a taxi out to Atalayoun, and save time for the mountain views before moving on.
