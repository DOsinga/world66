The Ourika Valley is one of the finest day trips from [Marrakesh](/africa/morocco/marrakesh) — a narrow gorge cutting into the High Atlas where Berber villages cling to red rock cliffs above a fast-running river. It takes about an hour to get there, and the contrast with the city is immediate: cooler air, rushing water, terraced gardens bright with vegetables.

The first stretch of road out of Marrakesh is flat and unremarkable. But after the village of Asgaour — where a lively Sunday souk takes over the roadside — the landscape opens up dramatically. Snow-capped peaks appear, the valley narrows, and the air cools. Roadside restaurants dangle fresh trout from hooks to attract passing traffic; stopping for grilled fish above the river is one of the valley's reliable pleasures.

Setti Fatma, at the end of the paved road around 65km from Marrakesh, is the main destination. From here you can walk up into the gorge to a series of seven waterfalls. The lower falls are easy enough; the higher ones involve some scrambling and are genuinely slippery on the way down. Wear proper shoes. In spring, snowmelt turns the river into a torrent and the track can wash out completely.

The valley has a difficult history. In August 1995 a catastrophic flash flood swept through, killing over 200 people and destroying entire villages. Most of what you see today was rebuilt afterwards — the neatly reconstructed settlements give little hint of what happened, but locals remember it clearly.

For serious trekkers, Setti Fatma is also a starting point for routes into the Atlas. You can reach Toubkal from here in a few days, or make a three-day crossing of the Zat Valley, ending below Taddert on the Tizi n'Tichka pass. The Djebel Yagour plateau, reachable in a long day, has an extraordinary collection of prehistoric rock carvings — thousands of engravings of animals, humans, and symbols etched into the rock face. For guides, the Café Azagza in Setti Fatma is the place to ask.
