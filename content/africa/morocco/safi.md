Safi is Morocco's pottery capital, a working Atlantic port city where craft and industry sit side by side. The [Colline des Potiers](/africa/morocco/safi/colline_des_potiers) — an entire hillside neighborhood of kilns, workshops, and showrooms — produces the distinctive blue-and-white glazed ceramics sold across Morocco. You can buy directly from the potters here at prices well below what you'd pay in Marrakech or Fez.

The city has Portuguese bones. A circuit of 16th-century walls still rings the medina, and [Dar el Bahar](/africa/morocco/safi/dar_el_bahar) (the Sea Fortress) stands on the headland at the ocean's edge, offering sweeping views down the coast. The [Kechla Fortress](/africa/morocco/safi/kechla_fortress) sits at the top of the old city and holds the best panoramic views in Safi. The [medina](/africa/morocco/safi/medina_safi) itself is unglamorous but genuine — this is a town Moroccans live in, not one dressed up for tourists.

The port handles phosphate exports and fishing, which means the waterfront is working infrastructure rather than a promenade. City beaches are industrial and best skipped. Head 11 km north to [Cap Beddouza](/africa/morocco/safi/cap_beddouza) or 4 km south to [Sidi Bouzid](/africa/morocco/safi/sidi_bouzid_beach) for clean Atlantic surf and sand.
