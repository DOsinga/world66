Sefrou is a small ancient walled town at the foothills of the Middle Atlas, 28 kilometres south of Fes. Most travellers pass it by — which is exactly what makes it worth stopping for. The medina is well-preserved and almost entirely free of tourist shops, giving you a genuine feel for a working Moroccan town.

The town has an unusually deep history. Long before the Arab conquest, Sefrou was home to a Berber-Jewish community. By the early 20th century, Jews made up roughly a third of the population. The [Mellah](/africa/morocco/sefrou/mellah) — the Jewish quarter — survives with its distinctive tall houses and covered alleyways, though the community itself has largely gone. The [medina](/africa/morocco/sefrou/medina) around it is one of the least-touristed in Morocco, which is a genuine virtue.

Summers here are noticeably cooler than Fes, and the surrounding countryside is pleasant for walking. The [Cascade de Sefrou](/africa/morocco/sefrou/cascade_de_sefrou) sits a short walk above the town, and the [Kef el-Moumen caves](/africa/morocco/sefrou/kef_el_moumen) in the hills to the south hold rock-cut tombs and good views. In June, Sefrou holds a cherry festival that draws Moroccans from across the region — if you happen to be passing through, it's worth timing your visit around it.
