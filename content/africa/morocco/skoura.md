Skoura is a small oasis town in the Draa Valley, about 42 km east of [Ouarzazate](/africa/morocco/ouarzazate), famous for its dense [palm grove](/africa/morocco/skoura/skoura_palm_grove) and the ancient kasbahs scattered within it. The town itself is unremarkable, but the palm grove that surrounds it — thousands of palm trees, olive groves, rose gardens, and ancient irrigation channels — is the point: a labyrinth of mud walls and shaded paths that rewards unhurried exploration.

The two most celebrated kasbahs are [Kasbah Amridil](/africa/morocco/skoura/kasbah_amridil) and [Dar Ait Sidi El Mati](/africa/morocco/skoura/dar_ait_sidi_el_mati), both accessible on the north bank of the Dades. They are not easy to find on your own, which is part of the appeal. Hiring a local guide for a half-day walk through the grove is genuinely worthwhile — not a tourist performance, but a practical necessity in a place where every path looks like every other path. Kasbah Amridil is particularly celebrated: it appears on the 50-dirham note and is the best-preserved example of the Draa Valley kasbah tradition still partially inhabited by the original family.

The drive from Ouarzazate is mostly flat, but about 19 km out the road skirts the Dades and the views across the reservoir toward a half-submerged kasbah are worth a stop.
