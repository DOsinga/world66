A small Anglican church just off the Grand Socco, consecrated in 1894 on land donated by the Sultan of Morocco to the British expatriate community. The exterior follows English country-church conventions but the interior is a remarkable hybrid: a Moorish horseshoe arch behind the altar, the Lord's Prayer in Arabic carved into the chancel, and decorative tilework patterned after the local zellij.

The churchyard contains the graves of several notable Tangier figures, including the *Times* correspondent Walter Burton Harris (1866-1933), who described pre-protectorate Morocco in *Morocco That Was* and effectively invented the foreign-correspondent tradition. The custodian gives informal tours by donation. Daily except Sunday morning services.
