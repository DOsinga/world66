The tomb of Ibn Battuta — arguably the greatest traveller of the medieval world — sits in a small whitewashed mausoleum down a narrow lane in the medina, just below the kasbah. After thirty years on the road covering more ground than Marco Polo, Battuta returned to his native Tangier and dictated his *Rihla* (Travels) at the sultan's request. He died around 1369 and is believed to be buried here, though the precise authenticity of the site is debated.

The simple shrine is open during daylight hours; the caretaker often opens the door if it is locked and accepts a modest tip. Nearby walls bear plaques marking Battuta's known routes. The site is more affecting for its quietness than its grandeur — a fitting tribute to a man who spent his life moving and is now still.
