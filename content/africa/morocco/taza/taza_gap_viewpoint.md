The **Taza Gap** is the narrow pass between the Rif Mountains to the north and the Middle Atlas to the south, and the view from Taza's medina hilltop across this corridor is the reason the city exists in the position it does. On a clear day from [Bab Er Rih](/africa/morocco/taza/bab_er_rih) or the medina walls, you can see both mountain ranges simultaneously — an unusual perspective that makes the strategic geography immediately legible.

The valley floor below carries the modern N6 highway and railway line, both following the same route that traders and armies have used for millennia. Looking east, the flat agricultural land of the gap extends toward Taourirt and Oujda. The mix of irrigated farmland, olive groves, and the sprawl of Taza-Bas below the medina is visible in its entirety from the walls' highest points. This is the view that makes Taza worth understanding rather than passing through.
