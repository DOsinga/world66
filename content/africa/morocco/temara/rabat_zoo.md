The Rabat Zoo (Jardin Zoologique National de Rabat), located in the northern part of Temara, is one of the larger and better-maintained zoos in Morocco. Established in 1973 on the grounds of a former royal hunting reserve, it covers about 70 hectares of wooded grounds and houses a substantial collection of African and international species.

The zoo is notable primarily for its Barbary lion breeding programme. The Barbary lion — the subspecies native to North Africa, once kept by Roman emperors for the arena and later by the Moroccan royal family — is extinct in the wild, and the zoo maintains one of the few captive populations in the world. Other notable residents include African elephants, giraffes, cheetahs, and a variety of Moroccan native species. The grounds are well maintained and spacious by regional standards. A visit takes about two to three hours.
