  The Basilica at Volubilis still has both of its semicircular apses standing — an unusual survival. In most other Roman cities, basilicas were converted to Christian churches in late antiquity, and then often to mosques, each conversion stripping away or altering the original structure. Volubilis was abandoned before those transformations happened, which is why so much of the original plan survives.

The Basilica and Forum are the civic heart of Volubilis, set on a raised platform at the centre of the city. The Basilica — a long hall used for judicial proceedings and commercial dispute resolution — is the most impressive standing structure in the site, with two semicircular apses still upright at each end and the remains of its nave columns lying on the ground alongside. The attached Forum, a rectangular open square used for public gatherings, still shows its paved surface and the base of a central Temple of the Capitoline Triad.

Together with the adjacent Capitol temple, dedicated to Jupiter, Juno, and Minerva, the complex formed the administrative and religious centre of the Roman city. The placement on a slight rise, with the Decumanus stretching away toward the Triumphal Arch, was deliberate — you can still read the urban logic of the place from this vantage point.
