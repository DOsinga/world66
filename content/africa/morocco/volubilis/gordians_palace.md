story: >
  The Palace of Gordian is named after the Emperor Gordian III, who ruled from 238 to 244 AD, though the building may predate his reign. It was the largest private building in Roman Volubilis, with over 32 rooms, its own baths, and a colonnaded garden. When the Romans withdrew from Mauretania Tingitana in 285 AD, the palace was simply abandoned — its owners apparently left everything behind.
---

The Palace of Gordian occupies a commanding position at the northern end of the site, overlooking the Decumanus and the plain beyond. It was the governor's residence and official seat of Roman administration for the region, built on a scale that announced the empire's authority clearly. The remains of its colonnaded courtyard, private bath complex, and series of formal reception rooms give a clear sense of how a senior Roman official would have lived at the edge of the empire.

Standing in the ruins of the courtyard, looking out over the olive groves on the plateau, it is not hard to imagine why the Romans found this a reasonable place to build a city. The land is fertile, the spring water plentiful, and the views across the Moroccan plain to the hills beyond are genuinely beautiful.
