Ibo is one of the original Swahili trading settlements of the East African coast, now a quiet, crumbling jewel in the [Quirimbas Archipelago](/africa/mozambique/iboisland/quirimbas_archipelago). The island has barely 3,000 inhabitants, a handful of beautifully restored colonial mansions repurposed as lodges, and the ruins of three Portuguese forts including the large [Fort São João](/africa/mozambique/iboisland/fort_sao_joao), built in 1791.

Walking [Ibo](/africa/mozambique/iboisland/old_town) is the point. Silver jewellery has been crafted here for centuries — the island's [silversmiths](/africa/mozambique/iboisland/silversmiths) work with scrap metal and old coins, creating intricate pieces using techniques passed down through generations. The mangrove channels surrounding the island make for excellent [kayaking and dhow sailing](/africa/mozambique/iboisland/kayaking_dhow), and the birdlife is exceptional.

Getting here requires effort: fly to [Pemba](/africa/mozambique/pemba), then take a small boat or charter plane to the island. Most visitors stay at Ibo Island Lodge or the boutique Ibo Safari Lodge — both occupying beautifully restored colonial buildings — and this is very much a destination for those who've planned it in advance. Note that parts of Cabo Delgado province have seen security issues since 2017; check current travel advisories before visiting.
