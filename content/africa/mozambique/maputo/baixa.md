The **Baixa** is what everyone in Maputo calls the colonial-era downtown — the grid of streets that runs from the railway station and the port up to Avenida 24 de Julho, and from the **Cathedral** across to the **Fortaleza**. It is the oldest part of the city, the densest, the most architecturally interesting, and the most worn-down. The 1903 cast-iron **Mercado Central**, the 1910 **Railway Station**, the Art Deco cinemas, the colonnaded banks of Praça 25 de Junho and the colonial-era post office all sit within ten minutes' walk of each other.

By day the Baixa hums — pavement traders, office workers, the constant horns of chapas. By night it empties out completely and is best avoided on foot. Pickpocketing is a real risk in the busier areas around the Mercado Central; keep your phone out of sight. Most travellers stay further north in Polana and come down here for sightseeing and the markets.
