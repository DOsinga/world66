title: "Books"
type: section
---

A few books will give you Maputo at the level a guidebook cannot.

**Mia Couto, *Sleepwalking Land* (1992).** The novel that put Mozambican literature on the map. Set during the civil war, it follows an old man and a boy on a road of burnt-out buses; the language is dreamlike and the country is half-myth. Couto, a white Mozambican born in Beira, is the country's best-known writer and lives in Maputo.

**Mia Couto, *Confession of the Lioness* (2012).** A more recent novel, set in a northern village beset by man-eating lions — partly a real event Couto witnessed as a consulting biologist. Shorter and more accessible than *Sleepwalking Land*.

**Henning Mankell, *Chronicler of the Winds* (1995).** The Swedish crime writer ran a theatre in Maputo for two decades. This is his novel about a Mozambican street child — a hard, beautiful book that conveys the texture of post-war Maputo as well as anything in English translation.

**Lília Momplé, *Neighbours* (1995).** A short novel built around the South African commando raid on Maputo in 1981 that targeted suspected ANC activists. Three families' stories interweave in the hours before the attack.

**Malyn Newitt, *A Short History of Mozambique* (2017).** Compact, readable single-volume history by the leading English-language historian of the country, from the Swahili coast period through Portuguese colonisation, the war of independence, civil war and reconstruction.
