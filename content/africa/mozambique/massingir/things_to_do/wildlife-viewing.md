---
title: "Wildlife Viewing"
type: poi
---

Massingir and the surrounding lowveld offer some of the most accessible big-game viewing in Mozambique, built on a conservation comeback that began in the early 2000s. When Limpopo National Park was formally established in 2001 and integrated into the Great Limpopo Transfrontier Park, fences separating it from Kruger National Park were progressively removed. Elephants were among the first to respond, crossing into Mozambique in significant numbers; the population, which had been decimated by the civil war, has since recovered dramatically and now numbers in the thousands.

The dry season — May through October — is the best time to watch wildlife. As water sources contract, animals concentrate around the Massingir reservoir and the Shingwedzi and Olifants river channels inside the park. Game drives in the early morning and late afternoon yield the best sightings: elephants in large groups, buffalo herds, waterbuck, impala, and warthog are everyday encounters, while lion and leopard require more patience and a degree of luck. Night drives from Covane Community Lodge offer a chance to spot nocturnal predators and bush babies.

Walking is possible in the buffer zones around the park with a certified local guide, a completely different experience from the vehicle-based safaris common across the border in Kruger. The landscape — flat lowveld savannah of mopane and acacia, broken by the wide river valleys — is characteristic of the broader Limpopo basin and feels genuinely wild. Visitor numbers remain low compared to the South African side, which means encounters with other tourists are rare.
