Mozambique Island is one of the great historic places of the Indian Ocean. This narrow coral island was the capital of Portuguese East Africa before Maputo, but its deeper story is Swahili, Arab, Indian, and African as much as Portuguese. The result is a dense, weathered town where coral-stone mansions, mosques, churches, and forts sit within a few walkable kilometres.

The north end, [Stone Town](/africa/mozambique/mozambiqueisland/stone_town), holds the grand colonial architecture: the [Fortress of São Sebastião](/africa/mozambique/mozambiqueisland/fortress_sao_sebastiao), the [Chapel of Nossa Senhora de Baluarte](/africa/mozambique/mozambiqueisland/chapel_nossa_senhora_de_baluarte), and the old governor's palace. The south end is Macuti Town, built more from palm, coral rag, and daily island life than from monuments. You need both halves to understand the place.

This is not a polished beach island. Buildings crumble, services are basic, and the heat can be heavy. But for travellers who care about history and atmosphere, Mozambique Island is essential: small enough to cross on foot, layered enough to occupy several days, and unlike anywhere else in the country.
