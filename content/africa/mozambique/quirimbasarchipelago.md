The Quirimbas Archipelago is a chain of around thirty coral islands stretching 250 kilometres down the northern Mozambican coast from near the Tanzanian border to just north of Pemba. The islands vary from barely-there sandbars exposed only at low tide to larger inhabited islands with centuries of Swahili history. Most fall within the **Quirimbas National Park**, established in 2002, which protects one of the least-disturbed coral reef systems in the western Indian Ocean.

The most historically significant island is **[Ibo](/africa/mozambique/iboisland)**, a Swahili trading settlement that later became one of the most important Portuguese colonial towns on the East African coast. Its three Portuguese forts, crumbling colonial mansions, and silversmiths working with techniques unchanged for generations give it a texture rare anywhere in Africa. North of Ibo, the islands become progressively more remote: **Quirimba Island** (which gives the archipelago its name), **Matemo**, and **Medjumbe** have varying levels of tourist infrastructure, from low-key fishing communities to exclusive lodge operations.

The reef system that runs the length of the archipelago is in excellent condition compared to much of the East African coast. The Quirimbas has been less impacted by bleaching events than the reefs of Kenya or Tanzania to the north, and the fishing villages on the islands have traditionally used reef fish sustainably. Diving visibility is typically 20-30 metres; the walls outside the main channels drop steeply and attract large pelagics. Whale sharks pass through between November and March, and humpback whales are present from July to October.

The main base for the archipelago is **[Pemba](/africa/mozambique/pemba)**, the provincial capital of Cabo Delgado, 30 kilometres to the south. Pemba has an airport with connections to Maputo, and boat or charter plane transfers to the islands. Note that Cabo Delgado has experienced a serious insurgency since 2017 in its northern districts; the Quirimbas islands and the Pemba area have been less directly affected, but travellers should check current security advice before visiting.
