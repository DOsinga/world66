The Quirimbas National Park was established in 2002 and covers around 7,500 square kilometres, encompassing the southern 11 islands of the archipelago (from Ibo south to just north of Pemba), a strip of the adjacent mainland coast, and the marine area between them. It is managed jointly by the Mozambican government and a conservation organisation, and entry fees go partly to community development programmes in the villages within the park boundary.

The marine zone protects the most important reef areas of the archipelago and regulates fishing within its boundaries. The mainland section contains the Quirimbas Forest, a coastal forest community with several endemic bird species, and open savanna where elephants, buffaloes, and various antelope species have returned following decades of poaching pressure. The park is not set up for conventional wildlife viewing drives; the appeal is the marine environment and the combination of marine and terrestrial conservation in a single landscape.
