The Barra Peninsula curves north from Inhambane and ends at Ponta da Barra, where a functioning colonial-era lighthouse marks the tip. The peninsula is a narrow strip of sand between the Inhambane Bay on one side and the Indian Ocean on the other, covered with towering coconut palms. The beaches are quieter than Tofo and the snorkelling off the point is consistently good.

Getting there from Tofo requires either a short drive or a boat crossing from Inhambane — the simplest option is a hired chapa or taxi to the Barra turn-off and then a walk or local transport to the lighthouse. The lighthouse itself is not generally open to visitors, but the surrounding beach, the clear water, and the sense of being at the end of a quiet peninsula are the reasons to come. Several small pousadas (guesthouses) on the peninsula make it possible to stay overnight if you want to escape the social scene at Tofo.
