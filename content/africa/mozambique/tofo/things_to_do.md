title: Things to Do
type: section
---

The overwhelming reason to come to Tofo is the marine life in the bay. [Whale shark encounters](/africa/mozambique/tofo/whale_shark_diving) are the headline act — snorkelling or diving with these enormous filter-feeders is one of the more extraordinary wildlife experiences on the continent. [Manta rays](/africa/mozambique/tofo/manta_ray_diving) are present year-round at cleaning stations on the reef. [Humpback whales](/africa/mozambique/tofo/humpback_whales) pass through from June to November, and dolphins are common in the bay at most times of year.

On land, Tofo is a small place. The beach itself is the main attraction: broad, white, and backed by dunes covered in indigenous coastal vegetation. [Surfing](/africa/mozambique/tofo/surfing) is popular — the bay has a consistent reef break, and several schools offer lessons. At the north end of town, a [Toca do Tartaruga (Turtle Point)](/africa/mozambique/tofo/turtle_point) is where loggerhead and leatherback turtles come ashore to nest from October to February. You can join monitored night walks during nesting season.

The [Barra Peninsula](/africa/mozambique/tofo/barra_peninsula) is a day trip that adds variety — a narrow sandbar between the bay and the Inhambane estuary, with its own beach, the lighthouse at Ponta da Barra, and good snorkelling off the tip.
