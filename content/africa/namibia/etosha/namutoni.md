Namutoni is the easternmost of Etosha's three main rest camps, situated near the King Nehale Gate and a historic German colonial fort that has been incorporated into the camp's infrastructure. The fort dates to 1906 and its white-washed walls and towers give Namutoni a theatrical quality unlike anywhere else in the park.

The eastern section of the park around Namutoni is known for its dense mopane woodland and high densities of zebra, wildebeest, and elephant. The nearby Fischer's Pan is a shallow seasonal lake that attracts flamingos in wet years, and the roads around it can be excellent for lion sightings. The waterhole at Namutoni is smaller and quieter than Okaukuejo's, making it a favourite with those who prefer their wildlife viewing without a crowd.
