Outjo is a small, dusty town in northern [Namibia](/africa/namibia) that serves as the main gateway to [Etosha National Park](/africa/namibia/etosha). Most travellers pass through on their way to the park's Anderson Gate, about 100 kilometres to the north, but Outjo is worth a stop in its own right — if only to stock up on supplies and get a feel for small-town Namibian life.

The town has a Germanic colonial character typical of northern Namibia, with a few well-preserved buildings from the early 1900s and a small but friendly main street. The [Outjo Museum](/africa/namibia/outjo/outjo_museum) gives good context for the region's colonial history. The [Töpfers Konditorei](/africa/namibia/outjo/topfers_konditorei) bakery is famous across Namibia for its German-style strudel and bread. The [Khorab Memorial](/africa/namibia/outjo/khorab_memorial), 7km north of town, marks the spot where Germany surrendered South West Africa in 1915.

If you are driving the classic Namibian circuit from [Windhoek](/africa/namibia/windhoek) north to Etosha, Outjo sits at a natural stopping point on the B1/C38 route. Stock up on water, fuel, and food here — facilities inside Etosha are limited and expensive. The [Waterberg Plateau National Park](/africa/namibia/outjo/waterberg_plateau) is a worthwhile detour about 120km southeast — white rhinos, rare antelope, and a landscape haunted by the 1904 Herero genocide.
