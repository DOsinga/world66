The bush around Baleyara holds a number of villages of Bella Tuareg — communities descended from people who were enslaved by Tuareg nomads and who formed their own settlements after emancipation in the colonial period. The villages are visually distinct from the farming villages of the Djerma people in the area: the architecture, the clothing, and the social organisation all reflect the community's particular history.

Visiting requires local guidance — the villages are not on marked roads, and arriving without an introduction can be awkward. The best approach is to ask in Baleyara's market on market day, where Bella traders are present and where a local guide can be arranged. With a guide, visits are typically welcomed, and the community's craftwork — particularly leatherwork in the Tuareg tradition — is available for purchase.

The landscape around the villages, framed by tall acacias and with the Sahel scrub stretching to a flat horizon, has a stark beauty that is most dramatic in the early morning or at sunset. For travellers with time and the right introduction, this is the most memorable experience the Baleyara area offers.
