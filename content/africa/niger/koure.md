Kouré is a small Sahel village about 60 km southeast of Niamey, and it has one extraordinary reason to visit: the last wild population of West African giraffes. These are not zoo animals, not a game reserve in any conventional sense — the giraffes roam freely through the acacia scrubland as they always have, moving between village fields and bush, largely indifferent to the humans who watch them from a respectful distance.

The West African giraffe (*Giraffa camelopardalis peralta*) was once widespread across the region. By the 1990s, hunting and habitat loss had reduced the population to under 50 individuals. A community-led conservation effort, backed by the Nigerien government and international partners, reversed the collapse: the population has since grown to several hundred animals. Kouré is the last place on earth where this subspecies survives in the wild, which lends the visit a weight beyond ordinary wildlife spotting.

Trained local guides know the giraffes' movements and will take you to them on foot. Encounters are often extraordinarily close — the animals are habituated to people and may walk within a few metres of you. You'll need your own four-wheel drive to reach the area; guides do not provide transport. Avoid the unofficial touts who approach vehicles on the Niamey–Dosso road before you reach the village.

**Security note:** Niger's security situation has been volatile in recent years, including a military coup in July 2023. Check current Foreign Office or State Department advisories before planning any visit. Travel outside Niamey, including to Kouré, requires up-to-date local advice.
