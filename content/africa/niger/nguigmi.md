N’Guigmi sits at the farthest reach of eastern Niger, the last town on the road to Chad, 925 km from Niamey. It is a grey, dusty frontier outpost that feels very much like the end of the road — because it is. The area is primarily populated by nomadic Kanuri and Toubou peoples, and the [camel market](/africa/niger/nguigmi/camel_market) to the south does a substantial trade in livestock and in the distinctive leather and metalwork — sharp knives in colourful red scabbards — that the Toubou are known for.

The [Lake Chad shore](/africa/niger/nguigmi/lake_chad_shore) lies roughly 30 km to the east; the lake has retreated dramatically over the past 50 years, but fishing communities still work its shallow waters. The greatest draw for serious travellers is the [Tal Desert](/africa/niger/nguigmi/tal_desert_trek) to the northwest — three hours by camel, a pristine landscape of tall dunes and absolute silence. Security conditions in the Lake Chad basin are volatile; check current advisories before travelling here.
