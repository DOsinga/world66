The Grand Mosque of Niamey dominates the city's skyline with its green-tiled minaret, visible from most of the city and the main visual landmark after the Niger River itself. It was built in the 1970s, funded by Saudi Arabia, and holds several thousand worshippers in its main prayer hall.

Non-Muslim visitors can usually enter outside prayer times by asking permission at the entrance. Dress conservatively — long sleeves, full trousers or a skirt covering the knee — and bring something to cover your head if you can. The interior is spacious and cool, a relief after the Niamey heat. On Friday afternoons, when the mosque fills for Jumu'ah prayers, the surrounding streets are a spectacle in themselves — traffic gives way to an ocean of white robes and prayer mats spreading out onto the tarmac.
