Zinder was founded in the early 17th century by Hausa tradesmen from [Kano](/africa/nigeria/kano) and grew into a major trading hub on the route between Nigeria and [Agadez](/africa/niger/agadez). When the French arrived, they made it their administrative capital until 1926, when it was replaced by [Niamey](/africa/niger/niamey). Zinder is still Niger's second-largest city, and it has the best market in the country.

The old quarters are the highlight. The [Birnin Quarter](/africa/niger/zinder/birninquarter) has some of the finest Hausa architecture anywhere — ornately decorated mud-brick facades with geometric patterns that rival anything across the border in Kano. The [Zengou Quarter](/africa/niger/zinder/zengouquarter) is less grand but livelier, full of workshops and street life. The [Grande Mosquée](/africa/niger/zinder/grandemosquee) and the [Sultan's Palace](/africa/niger/zinder/sultanspalace) anchor the historic core; with a bit of luck and a local guide, you can arrange an audience with the Sultan.

The [Grand Marché](/africa/niger/zinder/grand_marche) is the best market in Niger and deserves at least a morning. Zinder is a natural stop on the way between Nigeria and the desert north. The road to [Kano](/africa/nigeria/kano) is direct and well-travelled; the road to [Agadez](/africa/niger/agadez) is rougher but sees regular traffic.
