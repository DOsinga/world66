  Zinder's market tradition stretches back centuries, rooted in its position on the trans-Saharan trade route linking the Hausa cities of northern Nigeria with Agadez and the desert north. The market was the engine of the city's founding in the 17th century, and it remains Niger's most important commercial hub outside Niamey — the place where goods from Chad, Libya, and Nigeria all find their way to the same stalls.
---

Zinder's Grand Marché is widely considered the best market in Niger — a genuine working market, not a curated tourist bazaar. It sprawls through the centre of town and is busiest in the morning, drawing traders and buyers from across the region: livestock, grain, cloth, spices, kola nuts, hardware, and the indigo-dyed fabric for which this part of Niger is known.

The fabric stalls are the most memorable section. Tuareg silver jewellery and leather goods also appear, along with an enormous variety of dried goods. The market is busiest on Sunday, which is the traditional market day when villagers arrive from surrounding areas. Give yourself at least two hours to wander properly, and hire a local guide if you want to navigate the deeper lanes without getting turned around.
