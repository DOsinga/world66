Asaba is the capital of Delta State, perched on the western bank of the River Niger in southern Nigeria. The [Niger Bridge](/africa/nigeria/asaba_delta_state/niger_river_bridge) connecting Asaba to Onitsha on the eastern bank is one of Nigeria's most important crossings — a landmark that links the west and east of the country, and which carries a history going well back to the Biafran War of the 1960s.

Since becoming the state capital, Asaba has grown rapidly, with modern developments and commercial activity transforming what was once a quiet riverine town. The [Niger waterfront](/africa/nigeria/asaba_delta_state/niger_waterfront) remains the most atmospheric part of the city — fishing canoes, river traders, and the sheer scale of the Niger at this point make it worth an early-morning visit. The [main market](/africa/nigeria/asaba_delta_state/asaba_market) near the town centre is a significant trading hub between western and eastern Nigeria, and the [Delta State Cultural Centre](/africa/nigeria/asaba_delta_state/delta_state_cultural_centre) offers a window into the region's many ethnic traditions. The city is a useful stopover for travellers crossing between western and eastern Nigeria, and more interesting than its transit-hub reputation suggests.

Asaba's traditional culture is richly layered. The Asaba people celebrate several major festivals through the year — **Ulo**, **Aja**, **Iwaji**, **Ine**, and **Oli Oma** — each tied to the agricultural calendar and the spiritual life of the five villages that make up the historic settlement. The festivals are not staged for tourists; if you happen to be here during one, the military-style dances and communal ceremonies of Ine are particularly striking.
