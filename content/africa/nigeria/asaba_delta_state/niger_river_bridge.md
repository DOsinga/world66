  The original Niger Bridge, completed in 1965, was designed by an Italian firm and built at a time when connecting the east and west of Nigeria felt like an act of national unity. During the Biafran War (1967–70), it became a frontline — the bridge was the crossing point that both sides fought over, and the western bank at Asaba was the site of one of the worst massacres of the conflict, when several hundred Asaba civilians were killed by Nigerian federal forces in October 1967.
---

The Niger Bridge connects Asaba on the west bank to Onitsha on the east — a crossing that has enormous practical and symbolic importance in Nigeria. The original suspension bridge, completed in 1965, carries one of the busiest road corridors in the country and has been supplemented in recent years by a second bridge under construction to ease the notorious congestion.

Walking or cycling across (vehicles pay a toll) gives dramatic views of the river — wide, brown, and busy with fishing canoes and commercial barges at all hours. The crossing is especially striking at sunset, when the Niger turns gold and the silhouettes of the canoes are visible against it for kilometres in either direction. The bridge is the single most obvious landmark in Asaba and the reason the city exists as a commercial hub.
