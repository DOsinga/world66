The National Museum on King's Square houses what remains of the great Benin bronze tradition in Nigeria — cast bronze figures, intricate plaques, terra-cotta busts, and iron sculptures that depict the court, military campaigns, and ceremonial life of the Benin Empire (1440–1897). The work is extraordinary: technically sophisticated, artistically powerful, and deeply historical.

The collection includes both ancient court art and modern work by Edo artists, providing a sense of continuity across centuries. The museum also holds carved ivory, wooden sculptures, and documentary material about the 1897 British Punitive Expedition. Visiting is both an aesthetic and a political experience — you cannot stand in front of these objects without thinking about what was taken and what remains. Allow at least two hours.
