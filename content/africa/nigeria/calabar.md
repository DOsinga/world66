Calabar is a tropical river port in the far south-east of Nigeria, sitting on a ridge above the Calabar River just upstream of the Bight of Biafra. It is by some margin the most pleasant city in Nigeria — green, well-laid-out, cleaner and safer than most of the country, and with a long history as a trading centre stretching back to the 15th century, when Portuguese traders first arrived here. In the 19th century it was the headquarters of the British Niger Coast Protectorate and a major embarkation point for the Atlantic slave trade — an estimated 200,000 enslaved Africans passed through this port. Presbyterian missionaries, most famously the Scot **Mary Slessor**, arrived in the 1870s and eventually helped put a stop to it.

The best part of town is [Duke Town](/africa/nigeria/calabar/duke_town) and the neighbouring Henshaw Town, where colonial-era buildings, the old Presbyterian church, and tin-roofed Efik houses look down onto the river. The [Old Residency Museum](/africa/nigeria/calabar/calabar_museum), housed in a prefabricated 19th-century building shipped out from Scotland in pieces, has excellent exhibits on the slave trade, the palm-oil trade, and the colonial period. Down at Marina Beach, the [Slave History Museum](/africa/nigeria/calabar/slave_history_museum) stands on the actual site of a former slave-holding barracoon — one of the most affecting memorials in West Africa. [Mary Slessor's grave and memorial](/africa/nigeria/calabar/mary_slessor_grave) are nearby, on the hillside cemetery above Duke Town.

On the outskirts, the [Drill Ranch](/africa/nigeria/calabar/drill_ranch), run by the Pandrillus conservation foundation, rehabilitates rescued drills — one of the most endangered primates in Africa — and accepts visitors. The [Calabar Carnival](/africa/nigeria/calabar/calabar_carnival), held every December, bills itself as "Africa's biggest street party" and draws hundreds of thousands of visitors in a week-long explosion of parades, costume bands, and music. For the adventurous, Calabar is also the gateway to [Cross River National Park](/africa/nigeria/calabar/cross_river_national_park) — one of the last strongholds of the Cross River gorilla, the world's rarest great ape — reached via Ikom, three to four hours inland.
