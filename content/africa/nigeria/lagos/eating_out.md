title: Eating Out
type: section
---

Lagos eats well at every price point. Nigerian classics — jollof rice, egusi soup with pounded yam, ofada with ayamase sauce, suya, goat-meat pepper soup — are everywhere from street-side joints to restaurant menus. Victoria Island and Ikoyi hold the fancier restaurants ([Yellow Chilli](/africa/nigeria/lagos/yellow_chilli), [Nkoyo](/africa/nigeria/lagos/nkoyo), [Terra Kulture](/africa/nigeria/lagos/terra_kulture), the Bukka Hut chains); Lagos Island holds the cheap canteens ([Ghana High](/africa/nigeria/lagos/ghana_high), Sweet Sensation) where office workers eat lunch. The roadside *suya* — beef or chicken grilled over wood and dusted with yaji spice — is the city's late-night signature; the best vendors are around Glover Court and on Awolowo Road in Ikoyi.
