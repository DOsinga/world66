title: Things to Do
type: section
---

Sightseeing is not why you come to Lagos. Most travellers end up doing a mix: a half-day at the [Nigerian National Museum](/africa/nigeria/lagos/national_museum) on Lagos Island, a wander through [Freedom Park](/africa/nigeria/lagos/freedom_park) (the former colonial prison now a cultural centre), a serious look at contemporary Nigerian art at the [Nike Art Gallery](/africa/nigeria/lagos/nike_art_gallery), a pilgrimage to the [Kalakuta Republic Museum](/africa/nigeria/lagos/kalakuta_museum) where Fela Kuti lived and died, a ferry out to [Tarkwa Bay](/africa/nigeria/lagos/tarkwa_bay), or a lazy afternoon at the [Lekki Conservation Centre](/africa/nigeria/lagos/lekki_conservation_centre) canopy walk. The harder, more rewarding option is to take a guided walk through [Lagos Island](/africa/nigeria/lagos/lagos_island) — markets, mosques, the Marina — or a respectful guided visit to the lagoon-side community at [Makoko](/africa/nigeria/lagos/makoko). And then they spend the evenings on what Lagos really does, which is live music, clubs, and food. Ask where the night is happening; it changes.
