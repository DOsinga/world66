Sokoto is the spiritual capital of Muslim northern Nigeria, the city of Usman dan Fodio, Muhammad Bello, and the caliphate that reshaped Hausaland in the 19th century. It sits near the edge of the Sahel, hot and sandy, with a slower rhythm than [Kano](/africa/nigeria/kano) but a deeper sense of religious authority.

The main reason to come is the old caliphal centre around the [Sultan's Palace](/africa/nigeria/sokoto/sultans_palace), the [Sokoto Central Mosque](/africa/nigeria/sokoto/sokoto_central_mosque), and the [Hubbaren Shehu](/africa/nigeria/sokoto/hubbaren_shehu), where Usman dan Fodio is buried. This is not casual sightseeing territory: dress conservatively, ask before photographing, and use a local guide if you want to understand what you are looking at.

Sokoto is also a working northwestern state capital, with a busy [Central Market](/africa/nigeria/sokoto/central_market) and long-distance transport links toward [Kano](/africa/nigeria/kano), Kaduna, and the Niger border. It rewards patience more than speed. Come for the history, stay alert to local conditions, and do not treat religious spaces as backdrops.
