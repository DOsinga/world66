The palace of the Emir of Zazzau sits at the heart of the old walled city, a commanding complex of earthen walls and traditional Hausa architecture that signals the emirate's long political authority. The main structure is a fine example of the Sudano-Sahelian building style — thick mud walls, rounded buttresses, and elaborate decorative plasterwork on the entrance facade. The palace is a working royal court, not a museum, so visitors may only enter the outer courtyard and view the exterior. During the annual Durbar festival, the Emir rides out from here in full ceremonial regalia, and the square in front of the palace fills with horsemen in chainmail.
