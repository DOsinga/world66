Cyangugu (officially renamed Rusizi, though the old name persists in everyday conversation) sits at the southern tip of Lake Kivu on Rwanda's border with the Democratic Republic of Congo. It is a small, unhurried border town with a genuinely lovely position — the lake gleaming to the north, the steep forested hills closing in from the east, and the Congolese city of Bukavu visible just across the Ruzizi River.

Most travellers come here as a gateway to [Nyungwe National Park](/africa/rwanda/nyungwe_national_park), one of Africa's largest and least-visited montane rainforests. The park is about an hour's drive east and offers chimpanzee tracking, a canopy walkway suspended 50 metres above the forest floor, and colobus monkeys in extraordinary numbers. Rusizi National Park, at the northern edge of town where the Ruzizi River fans into a delta, gives a very different experience: flat papyrus wetlands, pods of hippos, Nile crocodiles, and boat trips that are relaxed enough for anyone.

Lake Kivu itself is worth time. Unlike most African lakes it is free of bilharzia, which makes swimming genuinely enjoyable. The water is warm, the light on the hills is spectacular in the late afternoon, and sunset from the waterfront is one of those views that quietly justifies the long bus ride from Kigali. Kamembe International Airport, a few kilometres north of town, makes Cyangugu accessible by air from Kigali if time is short.
