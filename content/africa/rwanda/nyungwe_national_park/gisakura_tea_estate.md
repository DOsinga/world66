Tea plantations form a startling green border around the western edge of Nyungwe, the manicured rows of bushes meeting the wall of native forest with no transition. Gisakura is the largest of these estates and a working factory tours can sometimes be arranged through guesthouses in the area.

The juxtaposition is one of the iconic sights of southwestern Rwanda — bright cultivated tea below, dense dark forest above. Late afternoon, when the light slants across the slope, is the best time for photographs. The small forest patch directly across the road from the Gisakura ranger post holds a habituated troop of black-and-white colobus monkeys and is a popular short outing.
