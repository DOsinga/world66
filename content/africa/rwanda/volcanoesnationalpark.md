Volcanoes National Park in northwestern Rwanda is one of the most important conservation areas in Africa — the home of the mountain gorilla, the subject of Dian Fossey's 18-year study, and one of the few places on earth where an encounter with a great ape in the wild is accessible, if expensive, to any traveller willing to make the journey. The park protects the Rwandan slopes of the Virunga Mountains, a chain of eight volcanoes straddling the borders of Rwanda, the Democratic Republic of Congo, and Uganda. The volcanic peaks visible from the park headquarters at Kinigi — Karisimbi, Bisoke, Muhabura, Gahinga, and Sabyinyo — form a green wall that changes colour from dark jungle to bare volcanic rock as you look upward.

The mountain gorilla population across the Virungas now stands at around 600 individuals (out of a worldwide total of around 1,000), split into about 40 habituated and non-habituated families across the three countries. In Volcanoes National Park, twelve gorilla families are habituated for trekking, each visited by a single group of eight trekkers per day. The experience — spending one hour with a gorilla family in the forest, watching the silverback eat nettles and the infants tumble over each other in the undergrowth — is extraordinary. [Gorilla trekking](/africa/rwanda/volcanoesnationalpark/gorilla_trekking) is the primary reason to visit, and the permit cost (US$1,500 in 2024) reflects the conservation value, the exclusivity, and, frankly, the demand.

The park is more than gorillas. [Mount Bisoke](/africa/rwanda/volcanoesnationalpark/mount_bisoke) offers an excellent full-day summit hike to a spectacular crater lake. [Golden monkey trekking](/africa/rwanda/volcanoesnationalpark/golden_monkey_trekking) is cheaper and more acrobatic — a different and thoroughly enjoyable experience. The [Karisoke Research Center](/africa/rwanda/volcanoesnationalpark/karisoke_research_center) site, where Fossey conducted her work and is buried, is accessible on a special hike. And the park's bamboo forest, through which you approach the gorilla families, is beautiful in its own right — cool, green, and densely alive.

Most visitors base themselves in the town of [Musanze](/africa/rwanda/ruhengeri) (formerly Ruhengeri), about 12km from the park headquarters at Kinigi. The journey from Kigali is around two and a half hours on a well-maintained road.
