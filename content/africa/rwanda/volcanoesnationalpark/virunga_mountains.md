The Virunga Mountains are a chain of eight volcanoes running roughly north-south along the borders of Rwanda, the Democratic Republic of Congo, and Uganda. From the Kinigi park headquarters, the Rwandan peaks form a continuous green skyline to the north and west — Karisimbi (4,507m), Bisoke (3,711m), Muhabura (4,127m), Gahinga (3,474m), and Sabyinyo (3,634m) are all accessible via climbing permits from the park.

The volcanic soils of the Virунgas support extraordinary biodiversity. The forests are genuinely ancient — the same Afromontane ecosystem has existed here for millions of years without the disturbance of significant glaciation. The cloud forest above 2,500m is thick with epiphytes, moss, and tree ferns; the hagenia-hypericum woodland above 3,000m has a specific character unlike any African forest at lower altitudes.

The political geography of the Virунgas is complicated: the DRC side is technically Virunga National Park (a separate protected area), which has been extremely difficult to visit due to ongoing instability in North Kivu. The Uganda side is managed as Mgahinga Gorilla National Park. All three sectors protect the same mountain gorilla population, which moves freely across international borders in the forest.
