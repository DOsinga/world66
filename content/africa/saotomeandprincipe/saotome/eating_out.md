---
title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

São Tomé's cuisine reflects its island setting and Portuguese heritage. Grilled fish, *calulu* (a stew of dried fish or smoked meat with palm oil, vegetables, and okra), and *moqueca* (fish cooked with coconut milk and spices) are the staples. The fresh tuna and barracuda landed daily are excellent; the best restaurants take their fish from the morning catch.

For a sit-down meal, the restaurants along the waterfront and around the Marginal promenade are the main options. *Roça Belo Monte*, a restored plantation estate outside town, offers memorable lunches in a colonial setting. For cheap, local food, the market area near the port has stalls serving rice, fish, and plantain at low prices — unpretentious and good.

The island grows excellent cocoa — some of the finest in the world — and several producers have begun selling bean-to-bar chocolate. Look for Claudio Corallo's products, which are produced entirely on the island from estate-grown beans and have a fermented, complex flavour unlike commercial chocolate.
