The beach at Cap Skirring is a long arc of pale golden sand backed by coconut palms, running for several kilometres along the Atlantic coast of the southern Casamance. The water is warm year-round — rarely below 24°C even in the cool season — and the beach is wide and uncrowded except during French holiday periods, when tour groups from Club Med and other package operators fill the beachfront hotels. Outside of those windows, the beach has a genuinely unhurried character.

The beach is the centre of life in Cap Skirring. Vendors sell grilled fish and cold drinks from small stalls at the north end; fishing pirogues are launched from the shore each morning and return in the afternoon with the day's catch. Swimming is generally safe in the calmer sections, though the Atlantic surf picks up in certain seasons and local advice about conditions is worth heeding.
