---
title: When to Go
type: section
---

The dry season — November through May — is the right time to visit. Days are warm but not unbearable thanks to the Atlantic breeze, humidity is low, and rain is rare. December and January are the coolest months, with nights down to 18°C; this is high season and also when most of the cultural events happen, including the Dak'Art biennale of contemporary art in even-numbered years.

The rainy season runs roughly July to October, with the heaviest rain in August and September. Storms can be torrential and flooding is common in the older neighbourhoods, but downpours are usually short and the green that comes with the rains is welcome. May and October are intermediate months — warm, increasingly humid, with rising prices for hotels as the year-end approaches.

The Magal de Touba pilgrimage, the annual Mouride gathering, falls 48 days after the Islamic New Year (so the date moves each year by the lunar calendar); it has little direct effect on Dakar but transport in and out of the city is busy on either side. Ramadan and Eid also reshape the rhythm of the day — many restaurants close at lunch and reopen for *iftar* at sundown.
