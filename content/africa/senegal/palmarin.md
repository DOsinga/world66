Palmarin is a quiet run of Sereer villages stretched along a narrow peninsula between the Atlantic and the Sine-Saloum Delta. It is not a resort strip in the Saly sense. The reward here is the mix of beach, mangrove, salt pans, shell mounds, fishing villages, and slow village life.

The eastern side is protected as the [Reserve Naturelle Communautaire de Palmarin](/africa/senegal/palmarin/reserve_naturelle_communautaire_de_palmarin), a locally managed landscape of mangroves, salt flats, grassland, forest, and tidal channels. Birdlife is strong, and the reserve also protects mammals, reptiles, shell mounds, and the cultural practices tied to the delta. Go with a local eco-guide if you want the place to open up properly.

The villages themselves are part of the attraction. Traditional Sereer wrestling, salt harvesting, clam and oyster gathering, and pirogue trips into the bolongs all keep Palmarin grounded in the delta rather than in beach-hotel routine. It is a good choice for travellers who want Senegal's coast to feel rural, ecological, and lived-in.
