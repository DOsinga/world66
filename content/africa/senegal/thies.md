Thiès is Senegal's third-largest city, about 70 km east of Dakar on the main road and rail line to the interior. It is an important transport and commercial hub, but it also has a distinct cultural identity — most travellers pass through it without stopping, which is a mistake.

The [Manufacture Nationale de Tapisseries](/africa/senegal/thies/manufacture_nationale_tapisseries) is the most compelling reason to stop: a working atelier where weavers translate paintings by African artists into monumental tapestries. It is unique in West Africa and one of the most interesting craft institutions on the continent. The [Musée Régional](/africa/senegal/thies/musee_regional) is small but worthwhile for context on the Thiès region and its peoples.

Thiès also has a remarkable historical weight: its [railway station](/africa/senegal/thies/gare_de_thies) was the nerve centre of the 1947 railway workers' strike that Ousmane Sembène immortalised in God's Bits of Wood — one of the foundational events of Senegalese political consciousness. The [central market](/africa/senegal/thies/marche_central) is lively and one of the best cloth markets in the country. The city makes a practical base for visiting the Bandia Wildlife Reserve and the Lac Rose (Pink Lake) area.
