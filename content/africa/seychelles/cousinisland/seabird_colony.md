The seabird densities on Cousin Island are extraordinary by any measure. Over 300,000 birds breed here on 27 hectares. Wedge-tailed shearwaters burrow in tunnels underfoot — visitors learn quickly to watch where they step. Fairy terns nest in the open on branches, their single white eggs balanced on bare bark without any nesting material. Brown noddies pack the pisonia trees so densely that the branches are white with birds. White-tailed tropicbirds nest in rock crevices along the coast.

The island is also an important site for rarer species. The Seychelles fody and Seychelles turtle dove breed here. The Seychelles brush warbler — once reduced to around 30 individuals on this island alone — has recovered significantly through conservation work and translocation to other islands. It remains one of the conservation success stories of the Indian Ocean.
