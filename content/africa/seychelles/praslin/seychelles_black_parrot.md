The Seychelles black parrot (Coracopsis barklyi) exists only on Praslin. It is not actually black — adults are dark brown with a slightly paler face and a reddish bill — but the name has stuck since the early naturalists. The bird feeds almost entirely on the fruit of the coco de mer palm, which ties its entire existence to the Vallée de Mai and the surrounding palm forest. Around 600 individuals remain.

The Vallée de Mai is the best place to see it, but the parrots also range across the forested higher ground of central Praslin. They are most active in early morning and late afternoon, calling loudly and moving through the canopy in small groups. The call — a loud, slightly manic whistle — is distinctive once you know it and easy to follow. Sightings are common; patience is the only requirement.

Praslin National Park, which covers the interior of the island, is managed specifically to protect the parrot's habitat. The park encompasses the Vallée de Mai and the surrounding forested hills, and entry to the Vallée also gives access to the wider park trails.
