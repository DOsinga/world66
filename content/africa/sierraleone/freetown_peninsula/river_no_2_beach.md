  River No. 2 Beach is owned and run by the surrounding community as a development association. The lodges, restaurants, and rental income are pooled and reinvested in the village — schools, wells, and a clinic. The model has held up through the civil war, the Ebola epidemic, and the lean tourism years, and is one of the few coastal community projects in Africa to have done so.

The most famous beach on the peninsula and the picture-postcard image of Sierra Leone's coast. River No. 2 is a long arc of white sand where a freshwater river runs into the Atlantic, hemmed in by forested hills. Swimming is excellent on both the sea and the river, and the calm conditions make it a good first stop with children.

The community-run development association manages a row of simple beach lodges and restaurants behind the dunes, where grilled fish and palm wine are reliable orders. Day visitors are welcome; pay a small fee at the gate and the rest of the beach is essentially empty.
