type: section
---

There is essentially nothing in Dhuusamarreeb that would justify a journey on its own. The town is a small administrative centre in the dry interior, set among low scrub and occasional acacia, with a market, a few mosques, and a scatter of government buildings since it was chosen as capital of [Galmudug](/africa/somalia) state. The surrounding region has been the scene of repeated fighting between federal forces, clan militias, and al-Shabaab.

Anyone who does end up here will find the usual rhythms of a small Somali town: tea shops along the main street, camel and goat trading at the market, and a steady traffic of dusty pickups heading north and south on the inland highway. Travel is unsafe without serious local advice — most foreigners passing through do so with a security escort.
