The Governor's Palace is the most architecturally significant of Mogadishu's Italian colonial buildings, built in the early 20th century to serve as the residence and administrative centre of the colonial governor. The building is in the Shangani district near the waterfront, and its Italianate design — arched loggias, white render, formal courtyard — was intended to project the permanence and authority of colonial rule. It did not achieve that ambiguity of purpose, and its subsequent history has been as tangled as the city itself.

The building's exterior is visible from the street and remains impressive despite the damage of the civil war years. The surrounding area, known as the Shangani district, was the colonial-era centre of power and contains other Italian-period buildings in various states of repair. Access to the interior is subject to whatever government functions are currently housed there.
