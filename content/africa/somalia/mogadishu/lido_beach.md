Lido Beach is a long white-sand beach on the Indian Ocean coast of Mogadishu, north of the old city centre. In the evenings and on Fridays — the Somali weekend — it draws families and young people who come to walk, swim, and eat at the small beach restaurants. The beach is the most ordinary public leisure space in the city, and seeing it busy with ordinary life is a reminder that Mogadishu is a city of two and a half million people going about their lives, not merely a dateline from a news agency.

The beach has been the target of al-Shabaab attacks more than once, and security at its entrance points reflects that reality. Check current conditions before going. In calmer periods it is open, staffed, and functioning, with food and drink available. It is genuinely one of the more beautiful stretches of Indian Ocean coastline in East Africa — wide, clean, and fringed with the kind of turquoise water that would draw tourists by the thousand if the security situation were different.
