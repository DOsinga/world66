The Mogadishu Cathedral is one of the most striking ruins in East Africa — a roofless, shell-damaged Italianate church standing in the heart of the old colonial quarter. Built between 1924 and 1928 by the Italian colonial administration in a style that combined Romanesque and Byzantine elements, it was once one of the largest churches in East Africa. The twin towers still stand, and the coral stone arcade of the nave still rises to the open sky.

The cathedral has not functioned as a church since the civil war began in 1991. In the years since, it has been occupied, stripped, damaged by shellfire, and partially restored as a heritage site. International donors including UNESCO have funded structural stabilisation work. Viewing the exterior from the street is possible without special access, and the building's scale and the contrast between its architectural ambition and its current state make it one of the most affecting sights in the city. The surrounding streets are part of the old colonial quarter, with Italian-era buildings in various states of ruin and reconstruction.
