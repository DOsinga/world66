The National Theatre of Somalia stands in central Mogadishu and represents one of the more determined symbols of the city's cultural recovery. The original theatre was built in the Italian colonial period; the current building dates from the post-independence era. For decades it hosted Somali drama, music, and poetry — art forms with deep roots in Somali culture, where oral performance has traditionally been the primary medium of public expression.

The theatre was closed for much of the civil war period and was used for various non-cultural purposes. Its restoration and reopening have been prioritised by the federal government as a symbol of normalisation, and performances do take place. The building is also used for government events and official gatherings. Whether any performance is scheduled during your visit requires local inquiry, but if something is on, attending would give you access to a performing arts tradition of considerable richness.
