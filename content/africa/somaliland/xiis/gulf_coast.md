  Dhows have been crossing between this coast and the Arabian Peninsula for at least three thousand years. Ancient Egyptian texts mention a land called Punt — a source of frankincense, myrrh, ebony, and gold — that scholars have long associated with the Horn of Africa. The merchants and sailors who loaded those goods onto boats somewhere along this shore were the ancestors of the people who still fish these waters today.
---

The coastline at Xiis is dramatic in the way that remote, unspoiled coasts always are — not manicured or announced, just there. Rocky headlands push out into the Gulf, the water between them running from deep blue to clear green over the rocks. The beaches are narrow and stony in places, sandy in others, and almost always empty. The scale of it, with the dry mountains rising directly behind the town and the open Gulf stretching north toward Yemen, is hard to absorb on first arrival.

There are no organised beach activities and no facilities of any kind. Swimmers should be cautious — currents can be strong and there is no one to help if something goes wrong. But for the traveller who simply wants to sit on a beach that feels like the edge of the world, and watch the same waters that have carried trade and people across to Arabia for millennia, Xiis delivers that completely.
