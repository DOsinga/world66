Camps Bay is Cape Town's most glamorous beach suburb, a narrow coastal strip wedged between the cold Atlantic and the dramatic wall of the Twelve Apostles ridge. It is expensive, beautiful and self-consciously stylish — the promenade opposite the beach is a continuous row of restaurants, bars and cafés that fill up on every sunny afternoon from October to April.

The beach itself is wide, white-sanded and consistently excellent. The water is cold — the Benguela current keeps Atlantic beaches significantly cooler than False Bay — but the setting, with the mountain directly above and Lion's Head to the north, is unmatched. On summer weekends the beach gets very crowded. A fifteen-minute walk north along the promenade leads to Clifton's four sheltered coves, which are smaller, more private, and even colder.

The residential streets behind the beach road are steep and expensive, with large contemporary villas and occasional Cape Dutch houses. Glen Beach, a smaller beach at the far southern end of the strip, is good for bodyboarding. For those wanting to escape the main strip's prices, a walk up into the Kloof Nek or De Waal Park area offers spectacular views with zero queuing.
