story: Chapman's Peak Drive was hewn out of the cliff face between 1915 and 1922 by Italian prisoner-of-war labour, following the contour of the mountain on a narrow ledge above the Atlantic. The road has had to be partly rebuilt and netted after rockfalls and now operates as a toll road, but engineering aside, it remains one of the most spectacular coastal drives in the world.
---

Chapman's Peak Drive snakes for nine kilometres along the cliff edge between Hout Bay and Noordhoek, with the Atlantic dropping away to your right and sheer sandstone cliffs rising to your left. There are several signposted viewpoints with parking, and the road is the centrepiece of any Cape Peninsula day-trip itinerary.

A small toll applies. The road occasionally closes after heavy rains or rockfalls; check before you go. The viewpoints fill up at sunset, when the road glows orange and the surf far below catches the light. Cyclists love it for the same reason — every year the Cape Town Cycle Tour pulls 35,000 riders along this stretch.
