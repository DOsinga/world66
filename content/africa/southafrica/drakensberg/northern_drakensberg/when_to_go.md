---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

The Northern Drakensberg can be visited year-round, but the experience varies significantly by season.

**Winter (June–August)** is the most popular time for serious hiking. Days are clear, cool, and dry. Nights on the escarpment can drop below freezing and snow is common on the high peaks. The Tugela Falls sometimes freeze. Visibility is exceptional and the trails are generally dry. Accommodation books out well in advance for this period, particularly July school holidays.

**Summer (October–March)** brings the rains. Mornings are often clear, but thunderstorms build rapidly over the escarpment from early afternoon — often explosive electrical storms. Flash flooding can close gorge trails. That said, the mountains are green, wildflowers are abundant, and there are far fewer hikers. Start every day hike very early and be off exposed ridges by midday.

**Spring (September–October)** and **autumn (April–May)** offer the best of both: stable weather, clear skies, and moderate temperatures. These are the sweetest months in the Berg.
