---
title: "Books"
type: section
---

**The Garden of Bad Flowers** by Sandile Memela. A Durban-set novel of post-apartheid disillusionment by a writer who knows the city's neighbourhoods and politics from inside.

**Cry, the Beloved Country** by Alan Paton (1948). Paton was a Natal schoolteacher and his great novel runs from the Ixopo countryside south of Durban through the city itself; it remains a foundational South African text and explains a great deal of the country's geography of race.

**Mahatma Gandhi: His Life and Ideas** by Joseph Doke. Doke was a Durban Baptist minister and Gandhi's friend; his short 1909 biography was the first ever written and was largely composed during Gandhi's South African years at Phoenix Settlement.

**Black Diamond** by Zakes Mda. A KwaZulu-Natal noir set in Durban and Pietermaritzburg, fast and funny, with a fine ear for the city's many languages.

**Indaba, My Children** by Credo Mutwa. Zulu folklore retold by the great sangoma writer, much of the action set in the Natal hinterland. Strange, beautiful, and impossible to put down once you start.
