The Huguenot Memorial Museum tells the story of the French Protestant refugees who settled in the Franschhoek valley from 1688 onwards, fleeing Louis XIV's revocation of the Edict of Nantes. The museum sits at the end of Huguenot Road beside the Huguenot Monument — a large allegorical sculpture erected in 1948 to commemorate the 250th anniversary of the settlers' arrival.

The collection covers the genealogy of the Huguenot families (many Cape families can trace their surnames here — De Villiers, Du Plessis, Du Toit, Le Roux), their role in establishing viticulture in the valley, and the broader history of French Protestant culture and its suppression in France. The archival material on individual families is the museum's most distinctive offering.

The museum also houses a collection of Cape Dutch furniture and decorative arts from the colonial period. Opening hours are standard museum hours; the monument grounds are always accessible. Entry is inexpensive and well worth the time for anyone curious about the Cape's settler history.
