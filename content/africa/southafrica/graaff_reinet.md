Graaff-Reinet is one of South Africa's great Karoo towns: whitewashed houses, wide streets, church spires and dry mountains pressing close around the grid. Founded in the 18th century, it has a depth of built history that most small South African towns cannot match, but it never feels like a museum set. It is still a working town in the middle of sheep country.

The real punch comes just outside town at the [Valley of Desolation](/africa/southafrica/graaff_reinet/valley_of_desolation), where dolerite columns rise above the plains of [Camdeboo National Park](/africa/southafrica/graaff_reinet/camdeboo_national_park). Go near sunset if you can: the Karoo opens out below you in huge, dry folds, with Graaff-Reinet sitting green in the basin.

In town, the [Reinet House](/africa/southafrica/graaff_reinet/reinet_house) and [Dutch Reformed Church](/africa/southafrica/graaff_reinet/dutch_reformed_church) give a clear sense of the old Cape frontier. Graaff-Reinet works as a strong overnight stop between the coast and the interior, but it rewards a full spare day.
