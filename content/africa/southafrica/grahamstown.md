Grahamstown, officially Makhanda, is a small Eastern Cape city with an outsized cultural life. It has the feel of an old university town: stone churches, museum buildings, students, festival posters, and a complicated frontier history that still needs to be read with care.

The city is most alive during the [National Arts Festival](/africa/southafrica/grahamstown/national_arts_festival), when venues across town fill with theatre, music, dance, comedy, and visual art. Outside festival time, the best anchors are quieter: the [Cathedral of St Michael and St George](/africa/southafrica/grahamstown/cathedral_of_st_michael_and_st_george), the [Albany Museum](/africa/southafrica/grahamstown/albany_museum), and the odd little [Observatory Museum](/africa/southafrica/grahamstown/observatory_museum).

It is not a polished tourist town, and that is part of the interest. If you are nearby on the N2 or coming inland from the Sunshine Coast, Grahamstown gives you a day of South African history and culture that feels very different from another beach stop.
