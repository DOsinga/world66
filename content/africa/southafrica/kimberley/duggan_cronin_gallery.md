The Duggan-Cronin Gallery holds a remarkable and contested photographic archive: roughly 8,000 photographs taken between 1919 and 1954 by Alfred Martin Duggan-Cronin, a De Beers security guard who spent his spare decades documenting the appearance, dress, customs, and ceremonies of southern African indigenous peoples across much of the subcontinent.

The photographs carry the complex legacy of their era — taken with a colonial gaze that is now recognised as problematic, and some subjects were posed or costumed for effect. Nonetheless the collection is historically unique and visually extraordinary. The gallery is worth visiting for the photographic quality and for the opportunity to engage with what these images mean, how they were made, and what they tell us about the relationship between the photographer and the photographed. It is one of the few places in South Africa where early ethnographic photography is presented with this degree of depth and honesty.
