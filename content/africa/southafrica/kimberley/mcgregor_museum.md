The McGregor Museum is the principal natural and cultural history museum of the Northern Cape, housed in a grand colonial building that began life as a luxury hotel in the 1890s. The collections cover the geology and palaeontology of the Karoo, the archaeology and history of the Griqua people, the Anglo-Boer War, and the natural history of the region's arid landscapes.

The Boer War gallery is particularly detailed, covering the Siege of Kimberley — the 124-day blockade, the improvised defences, the role of Cecil Rhodes (who was trapped in the city), and the eventual relief. The outdoor courtyard holds an impressive collection of large mineral specimens and geological samples from the Northern Cape. The museum manages several satellite sites around Kimberley including the [Duggan-Cronin Gallery](/africa/southafrica/kimberley/duggan_cronin_gallery) and the [Sol Plaatje Museum](/africa/southafrica/kimberley/sol_plaatje_museum).
