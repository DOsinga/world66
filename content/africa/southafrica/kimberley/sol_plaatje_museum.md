Sol Plaatje was one of the most remarkable South Africans of the early twentieth century: journalist, novelist, linguist, political activist, and first Secretary-General of what became the ANC. The museum occupies the house in the Malay Camp neighbourhood where he lived during his final years, and it tells a story that gets little space in the more prominent Kimberley heritage sites.

Plaatje documented the Siege of Kimberley in a diary that is one of the great first-hand accounts of the Anglo-Boer War, written from the perspective of Black Kimberley residents who were caught in the siege while remaining largely invisible to official histories. His novel *Mhudi*, published in 1930, was the first novel written in English by a Black South African author. The museum presents his life and work with intelligence and care, and his story provides a necessary counterpoint to the Cecil Rhodes and De Beers narratives that dominate most of Kimberley's heritage trail.
