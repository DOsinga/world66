  The Pafuri region was inhabited by the Makuleke people for generations before they were forcibly removed in 1969 to make way for park expansion. After democracy came to South Africa, the Makuleke launched a successful land claim — and then, in an unusual and forward-thinking decision, chose to lease the land back to conservation rather than develop it, creating a community partnership with SANParks. The Makuleke concession now generates tourism income for the community while preserving one of Kruger's most ecologically important areas.

The Pafuri region in Kruger's far north is the most remote and least visited section of the park, and also one of the most ecologically distinctive. The confluence of the Limpopo and Levuvhu rivers creates a dense, tropical riverine forest — fever trees, nyala berries, and figs — that supports species you won't find in the more open south: the spectacular Narina trogon, the massive Pel's fishing owl, and the secretive African finfoot. The area has Kruger's highest bird diversity and regularly delivers sightings unavailable elsewhere in the park.

The Pafuri Picnic Site is a beautiful spot at the Levuvhu River crossing, where enormous fig trees provide shade and you can watch elephants crossing the river. Day walking trails are available in the Makuleke concession, guided by rangers from the local community. The region is best reached from the Punda Maria camp or by flying into the Pafuri airstrip. It rewards travellers who are willing to make the journey — this is Kruger without the crowds, in a landscape that feels genuinely wild.
