Sabi Sand is the most famous of the private game reserves that adjoin Kruger's western boundary — an unfenced 65,000-hectare property where animals move freely between the reserve and the national park. The private reserves operate under different rules to SANParks: night drives are permitted, guides can follow animals off-road, and the ratio of vehicles to individual animal sightings is strictly controlled. The result is the most intensive wildlife experience available in southern Africa — a leopard encounter here means following on foot as the cat hunts, not watching from a distant road.

The lodges within Sabi Sand are among the most celebrated in Africa — MalaMala, Londolozi, Singita, Lion Sands — and the prices reflect this. But even for visitors who stay in the national park, the story of Sabi Sand is worth knowing: it demonstrates what wildlife watching looks like when conservation is funded by high-end tourism rather than government budgets, and the contrast with the national park experience illuminates what each model does well.
