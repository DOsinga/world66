The Drakensberg — "Dragon's Mountains" in Afrikaans — forms the western spine of KwaZulu-Natal, rising steeply along the border with Lesotho to peaks above 3,400 metres. The range has been designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site, recognised for both its dramatic landscape and the extraordinary concentration of San rock art sheltered in its caves and overhangs.

Three main areas draw most visitors: Royal Natal National Park in the north, where the Amphitheatre — a 5 km basalt wall — drops the Tugela River in a five-stage cascade; Giant's Castle in the centre, excellent for hiking and birdwatching; and the Cathedral Peak area, popular for day walks and multi-day trails. The landscape shifts from subtropical forest in the foothills to high alpine grassland at altitude.

Hiking here is serious business. The trails are long, the altitude matters, and afternoon thunderstorms roll in quickly in summer. The rewards — enormous views, quiet valleys, and San paintings that feel genuinely intimate rather than roped-off museum pieces — are worth the effort.
