The Afrikaans Language Monument stands on the slopes of Paarl Mountain and is one of South Africa's more striking pieces of post-war architecture. The three interconnected concrete columns rise dramatically from the hillside, and the views across the Paarl valley from the monument terrace are exceptional. On a clear day you can see Table Mountain in the distance.

The monument was built to mark the centenary of Afrikaans being recognized as a distinct language from Dutch — a recognition that took place in Paarl in 1875. The site is well-maintained with a visitor centre explaining the history of the language, and the walk around the base gives you different angles on the sculptural forms. It is the kind of landmark that looks better in person than in photographs, and the view alone justifies the drive up the mountain.
