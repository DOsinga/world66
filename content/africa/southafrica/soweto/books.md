**Long Walk to Freedom** by Nelson Mandela (1994) — Mandela's autobiography covers his childhood, his years in Orlando West, his legal career, the formation of the ANC's armed wing, and his 27 years in prison. The Soweto chapters give a vivid sense of the life Mandela led on Vilakazi Street and why it was so significant.

**Kaffir Boy** by Mark Mathabane (1986) — A memoir of growing up in Alexandra township, Johannesburg, in the 1960s and 1970s under apartheid. Not Soweto specifically, but an essential document of black township life in the apartheid era and essential context for understanding what you see in Soweto's museums.

**Playing the Enemy** by John Carlin (2008) — The account behind the film *Invictus*, this book follows Mandela's extraordinary use of the 1995 Rugby World Cup to promote national reconciliation. It gives a sense of Mandela's political genius and the mood of South Africa in the years immediately after apartheid's end.

**Twelve Disciples of Nelson Mandela** by Benjamin Pogrund (2006) — A collection of portraits of twelve people whose lives were shaped by Mandela and the struggle. Several of the subjects grew up in Soweto and the book fills in the human texture behind the memorial sites.
