  The photograph that made Hector Pieterson famous was taken on 16 June 1976 by Sam Nzima, a photojournalist working for The World newspaper. Nzima captured the moment moments after the 12-year-old was shot: his classmate Mbuyisa Makhubu carrying his body, and his sister Antoinette running alongside screaming. The image was published around the world and became the defining image of apartheid's brutality. Nzima's employer confiscated his negatives, fearing government reprisal, and the original print was held for decades.

The Hector Pieterson Museum is the principal memorial to the Soweto Uprising of 16 June 1976, when students protesting the imposition of Afrikaans as a language of instruction in black schools were met with police gunfire. Hector Pieterson, 12 years old, was one of the first to be killed. The photograph of his body being carried by a classmate, published worldwide, became the iconic image of apartheid resistance.

The museum is located on the site of the shooting, in Orlando West. Its exhibitions use photographs, film footage, and personal testimonies to document the events of June 1976 and their context in the broader struggle against apartheid. The building also contains a memorial garden. Well-designed and emotionally powerful, it is one of the most important historical museums in South Africa.

A short walk from the museum is the Hector Pieterson Memorial Stone, marking the exact spot where he fell. Entry fee around R80 for adults. Open Tuesday to Sunday.
