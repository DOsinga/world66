Regina Mundi is the largest Catholic church in South Africa, located in Moroka, Soweto. Built in 1962, the church became one of the most important meeting places for anti-apartheid activists throughout the 1970s and 1980s — a space where large gatherings could take place under the nominal protection of religious sanctuary when other venues were closed to political organisation.

The church's history is inscribed on its fabric: bullet holes in the ceiling, left there deliberately as a reminder of the police assault on worshippers seeking refuge on 16 June 1976, make the interior a sober memorial. The church remains active and services are held regularly. Visitors are welcome at other times and the building's scale and history make it one of the most moving sites in Soweto. Guided tours can be arranged.
