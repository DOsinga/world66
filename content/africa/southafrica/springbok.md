Springbok is the practical capital of Namaqualand, but its appeal is not just fuel, groceries, and the N7 north to Namibia. The town sits in a dry bowl of granite hills, and in good flower years the empty-looking veld around it turns suddenly bright after winter rain.

This is copper country. Springbok grew from nineteenth-century mining, and the old [Blue Mine](/africa/southafrica/springbok/blue_mine) still marks the start of commercial copper mining in South Africa. In the centre, [Klipkoppie](/africa/southafrica/springbok/klipkoppie) gives you the town's best quick view and a visible link to the South African War.

The real reason to stay a day is [Goegap Nature Reserve](/africa/southafrica/springbok/goegap_nature_reserve), a short drive out of town. It has the spring flower display, but also hiking, mountain-bike and 4x4 routes, succulents, granite outcrops, and desert wildlife when the flowers are gone.

Springbok is at its best from late winter into spring, roughly August and September if the rains have been kind. Outside that window it is quieter and harsher, but still a worthwhile spare-day stop for travellers who like mining history and dry-country landscapes.
