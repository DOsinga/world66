Juba is South Sudan's capital and the only real urban base for anyone who ends up travelling in the country. It is not an easy city and it is not a conventional leisure stop. Security is volatile, infrastructure is rough, prices are high, and most foreign visitors are here for work, reporting, aid, research, or diplomacy.

Still, Juba matters. It is where the world's youngest country declared itself, where the White Nile cuts through the heat and dust, and where South Sudan's hopes and disappointments are visible in the same streets. The city grew fast after becoming the southern capital in 2005 and national capital in 2011, and it still feels unfinished.

For travellers who are already here and can move around safely with local advice, the meaningful stops are concentrated: the [John Garang Mausoleum](/africa/southsudan/juba/john_garang_mausoleum), [St Theresa Cathedral](/africa/southsudan/juba/st_theresa_cathedral), the markets, and the riverside. Do not wander casually, do not walk at night, and do not treat this as a normal city-break destination.
