Jebel Barkal is the landmark that explains Karima. The mountain rises abruptly from the flat desert near the Nile, a sandstone table with a sheer face and a strange pinnacle at one end. For ancient Egyptians and Kushites it was sacred to Amun, and the temples at its foot turned Napata into a religious centre that mattered across the Nile world.

The climb is short but steep and sandy. Go late in the afternoon if conditions allow: the light warms the temples below, the river turns silver, and the desert around Karima suddenly makes sense. Do not underestimate the heat, and avoid the loose edges near the top.
