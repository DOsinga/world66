title: "Things to Do"
type: section
---

Kassala has more atmosphere than ticked sights — the experience is in walking the souq at dusk, climbing the rocks behind Khatmiya, drinking coffee at the foot of the mountains, and watching the city slide into the evening cool.

The [Kassala souq](/africa/sudan/kassala/kassala_souq) is one of the most distinctive markets in Sudan, with the Rashaida silver jewellery, Beja swords, clay jebbana coffee pots, and the famously sweet local fruit. The historic [Khatmiya mosque and tomb of Sayyid Hassan](/africa/sudan/kassala/khatmiya_mosque), at the foot of the mountains, is the spiritual heart of the city — visit on a Friday evening for the Sufi dhikr at the nearby Burhaniya tariqa.

Above Khatmiya, the [Totil rock cafes](/africa/sudan/kassala/totil_cafes) serve the famous Kassala coffee spiced with ginger and cinnamon among the giant boulders of the lower slopes. Beyond, the dramatic granite domes of the [Taka Mountains](/africa/sudan/kassala/taka_mountains) are home to a colony of hamadryas baboons that descend at sunset to drink at the well.

Running through the city is [the Gash River](/africa/sudan/kassala/gash_river), a wide expanse of sand for most of the year and a brief, dramatic torrent during the summer rains. The other side of the river is mainly residential but worth a walk — particularly the predominantly Hausa Gharb el-Gash quarter, with its lively food market and evening agashe stalls, and the British-era railway quarter of Sikka Hadeed beyond.
