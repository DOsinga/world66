Shaab Rumi is a large reef about 40 km north of Port Sudan, best known as the site of Cousteau's Conshelf II experiment in 1963. The structures from that experiment — an underwater station where five aquanauts lived and worked for a month — remain on the reef at around 10 to 30 metres depth. They are one of the most historically significant underwater sites in the world.

The reef itself is also outstanding: a large lagoon surrounded by spectacular walls and pinnacles, with abundant marine life including grey reef sharks, Napoleon wrasse, and dense fish populations. It is a regular stop on liveaboard itineraries. Reaching it requires a boat from Port Sudan, typically as part of a multi-day dive trip.
