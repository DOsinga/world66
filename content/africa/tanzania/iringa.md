Iringa is a provincial capital in the Tanzanian Southern Highlands, set on a dramatic escarpment at 1,600 metres. The altitude keeps it cooler than most of Tanzania, the scenery is striking, and the town itself is relaxed and largely tourist-free — a rare thing in this part of the world.

What makes Iringa worth a stop is its position as a gateway to three of southern Tanzania's highlights: [Ruaha National Park](/africa/tanzania/ruaha_national_park), one of Tanzania's largest and least crowded reserves; [Udzungwa Mountains National Park](/africa/tanzania/udzungwa_national_park), a biodiversity hotspot of rainforest and waterfalls; and the [Isimila Stone Age site](/africa/tanzania/iringa/isimila_stone_age_site), where erosion has exposed stone tools and fossils from 60,000 years ago in an otherworldly landscape of eroded pillars. Closer to town is [Kalenga](/africa/tanzania/iringa/kalenga), the seat of Chief Mkwawa, the Hehe leader who held off the Germans for seven years.

In town, the market is one of the best in Tanzania — pick up the distinctive Iringa baskets woven from sisal and palm leaf. Neema Crafts, a workshop and cafe employing more than 60 disabled people, is genuinely not to be missed: you can tour the workshops (paper made from elephant dung, handwoven textiles, recycled-glass beadwork) and then eat very well in the gallery cafe. The climb up [Gangilonga Rock](/africa/tanzania/iringa/gangilonga_rock) rewards you with views over the escarpment; go in a group rather than alone.
