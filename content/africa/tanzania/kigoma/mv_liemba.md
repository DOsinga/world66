  The Liemba was built in Papenburg, Germany in 1913, dismantled and shipped in 5,000 crates to East Africa, and reassembled on Lake Tanganyika as the gunboat Graf von Götzen. Scuttled by her crew in 1916 to keep her out of Allied hands, she was raised by the British in 1924 and converted to a ferry. C. S. Forester is said to have used her story as inspiration for "The African Queen".

The MV Liemba is the most famous ship on Lake Tanganyika, and probably the oldest ferry still in regular passenger service anywhere in the world. She runs (when she runs — schedules are unreliable, breakdowns are frequent) between Kigoma and Mpulungu in Zambia, stopping at remote lakeside villages where there is no jetty. Passengers and cargo are loaded and unloaded from a fleet of canoes that swarm out from shore, often in the middle of the night.

A two- or three-day journey on the Liemba is a slow, unhurried way to see the lake and the small communities along its eastern shore. First-class cabins are basic but adequate; second-class is dormitory bunks; third-class means finding deck space. Even if you do not plan to sail, a walk along the Kigoma waterfront when she is in port is worthwhile: there are few working ships of this age left anywhere.
