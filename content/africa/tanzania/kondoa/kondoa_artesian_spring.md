Long before roads or buses, the caravan routes from the Indian Ocean coast to the interior of Africa depended on reliable water sources, and the artesian spring at Kondoa was one of the most important. Caravans carrying ivory and slaves — and later, returning with trade goods — stopped here because water was plentiful and reliable even in the dry season, a rarity in this part of the semi-arid Central Plateau.

The spring still flows, and locals still use it, though it now lies within an unremarkable corner of the town rather than being a dramatic feature in open bush. What makes it worth finding is the quiet historical weight of the place: this trickle of water shaped one of the great trade networks of nineteenth-century Africa, and Kondoa's position on the route explains why the town exists at all.

Ask at the market or the district office for directions — the spring is not signposted, but well-known. It is a short walk from the bridge.
