  Mikindani's streets once carried the trade of the entire Lake Malawi hinterland down to the coast — ivory, slaves, copper, and beeswax moved through this harbor for centuries before the Europeans arrived. The coral-stone buildings you walk past today were merchant houses and warehouses in that trade. The town's quiet decline after the German colonial period shifted focus to Mtwara has paradoxically preserved what would otherwise have been demolished and rebuilt.

Mikindani's old town is a compact quarter of coral-stone buildings, narrow lanes, and carved wooden doorways that speak to centuries of Swahili merchant culture. It is smaller and less famous than Zanzibar Stone Town or Lamu, which means you can walk it without crowds and find genuine quiet in what are still living streets.

The mosques, the crumbling warehouses, the occasional carved lintel above a doorway — none of it is packaged for tourism, and that's the point. Trade Aid's restoration work has helped stabilize several buildings, but the town retains an authentic patina of use and age. A guided walk of the old town takes about an hour and can be arranged through the Old Boma.
