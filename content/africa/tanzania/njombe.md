Njombe sits at about 2,000 metres in the Southern Highlands of Tanzania, making it one of the coolest towns in the country — in June to August it can be genuinely cold by East African standards. The climate attracted missionaries in large numbers, and the town's single main street is lined with an unusual concentration of [churches](/africa/tanzania/njombe/diocese_of_njombe).

The approach from the north passes through extensive [wattle and tea plantations](/africa/tanzania/njombe/tea_estates) on a surprisingly flat plateau — part of the ancient Gondwana erosion surface. South and southeast of Njombe, the landscape changes dramatically to beautiful rolling hills covered in grassland, with views stretching for miles.

Njombe is not a tourist destination in itself, but it is a pleasant stop on the road between [Dar es Salaam](/africa/tanzania/daressalaam) and the southern highlands, and the obvious base for a trip out to [Kitulo National Park](/africa/tanzania/njombe/kitulo_national_park) when the wildflowers are in bloom. The surrounding countryside rewards exploration, and the cool air is a welcome change from the lowland heat.
