A **dhow** is the traditional lateen-rigged wooden sailing vessel that has connected the Swahili coast, the Arabian Peninsula, Persia, and India for over a thousand years. Zanzibar was one of the great dhow ports of the Indian Ocean — monsoon winds allowed reliable round trips between Arabia and East Africa, carrying slaves, ivory, spices, and cloth. The monsoon economy shaped everything about the island, from its language to its architecture to its cuisine.

Today the traditional dhow industry has shrunk considerably, but it has not disappeared. Working cargo dhows still move between Zanzibar, Dar es Salaam, and the Kenyan coast. Smaller tourist dhows offer the best way to experience the island from the water: **sunset cruises** from Stone Town's waterfront, snorkelling trips to sandbanks, and full-day excursions around the island's southern tip.

The most popular sunset dhow trip leaves from the waterfront below the Old Fort at around 4pm, sailing north with the evening breeze and returning after dark with fresh fish grilled on a small charcoal fire on board. Snorkelling stops at sandbanks and reef sections break up longer daytime trips. The experience of sitting on a dhow's raised prow with the Island behind you and the Indian Ocean ahead is one of Zanzibar's most satisfying.

Several operators in Stone Town run these trips; the price should include snorkelling equipment and food. Be sceptical of operators offering unusually low prices, which often means overcrowded boats and a rushed schedule.
