Zanzibar was for centuries the world's leading producer of cloves, and the island's fertile interior still grows cloves, vanilla, nutmeg, cinnamon, cardamom, black pepper, lemongrass, and jackfruit in profusion. The spice tour is a World66 cliché — almost every visitor to the island does one — but the good ones are genuinely worthwhile if you choose a smaller, locally-run operation over the tourist-bus versions.

The best farms are in the central belt of the island, around Kizimbani and Kidichi. A typical two-hour walk with a knowledgeable guide involves identifying plants by smell and taste before you see them, chewing cinnamon bark, smelling vanilla flowers, and watching cloves being dried on mats in the sun. Guides often fashion makeshift hats, rings, and flowers from palm leaves as they go. There is usually a meal of Zanzibar biryani and spice-scented rice at the end.

The Kidichi Persian Baths — a small, roofless bathing pavilion built by Sultan Seyyid Said in the 1850s for his Persian wife — are usually included on spice tour routes and add a historical dimension to what is otherwise an agricultural excursion. The Sultan planted much of Zanzibar's clove cultivation himself, transforming the island's economy in the 1820s.

For the most authentic experience, book through a local operator in Stone Town rather than from a hotel, and ask for a farm that still operates commercially rather than existing solely for tourism.
