Aneho sits on a narrow sand spit between a lagoon and the Gulf of Guinea, 45 kilometres east of [Lomé](/africa/togo/lome) along the coastal road. It was the capital of Togo during the German colonial period until 1897, and the faded colonial architecture from that era still defines the town's character. The location near the border with [Benin](/africa/benin) makes it a natural stopping point for travellers crossing between the two countries.

The town offers a handful of things: the [Cathedral of Aneho](/africa/togo/aneho/cathedral_of_aneho) and other crumbling colonial-era buildings, the [Aneho Market](/africa/togo/aneho/aneho_market) that serves the surrounding Lacs Prefecture, the [Gulf of Guinea beach](/africa/togo/aneho/gulf_of_guinea_beach) where fishing boats come and go, and the [Lake Togo lagoon](/africa/togo/aneho/lake_togo_lagoon) on the northern side of the spit. From the lagoon shore, you can take a pirogue across to [Togoville](/africa/togo/togoville), where the chief signed the colonial protectorate agreement in 1884. It is a quiet, atmospheric place that sees few tourists — most people pass through rather than linger, but it rewards a few hours of wandering.
