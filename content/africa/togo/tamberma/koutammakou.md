The Koutammakou is the larger cultural landscape — about 50,000 hectares across northern Togo and into Benin — within which the takienta compounds, sacred groves, agricultural fields, and ritual sites form a single integrated whole. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2004, expanded in 2023 to include more of the Benin side.

What "visiting Koutammakou" means in practice is wandering between villages on dirt tracks, with a local guide pointing out shrines under particular trees, sacred groves where rituals are performed, irrigation channels, and the millet, sorghum, and fonio fields the Batammariba have cultivated for centuries. The landscape feels organised and ancient in a way that is hard to describe. Allow at least a full day. Kande is the standard base.
