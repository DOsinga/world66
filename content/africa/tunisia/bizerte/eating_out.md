---
title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

Bizerte is a fishing port, and the food reflects it: grilled fish, seafood couscous, octopus salads, calamari, and the local speciality *poissonade* (a tuna-and-onion sandwich on a hollowed-out baguette). The best eating is along the old port quay and on the corniche; the cheap eating is in the medina near Place Bouchoucha.

**Le Petit Mousse** on the corniche has been Bizerte's reliable seafood restaurant for decades — bouillabaisse, grilled sea bream, full menus for around 40–60 dinars. **Restaurant Sport Nautique** on the old port has tables right on the quay and serves a good salade méchouia (grilled vegetable salad) and fresh fish; a dinner with wine runs 50–80 dinars. **Eden** on the harbour does grilled fish and chips for less.

In the medina, the small canteens around the Great Mosque do *lablabi* (chickpea breakfast soup), *koucha* (oven-baked mutton with potatoes), and *brik à l'oeuf* (fried egg-and-tuna pastry) for under 10 dinars. The fish-grill stands on the corniche in summer fry sardines and red mullet to order with chips and harissa.

Most places are open through the day; almost nowhere serves alcohol except the older restaurants on the corniche, where it is added discreetly to the bill.
