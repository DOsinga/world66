The Carthage Palace is the official residence of the President of Tunisia, occupying a substantial seafront site between the Antonine Baths and the cluster of foreign embassies on the Avenue Habib Bourguiba. It dates from the 1950s in its current form, on the site of an older Beylical palace, and has been the headquarters of every Tunisian president since independence.

The palace is not open to the public and the surrounding area is heavily secured — armed guards, road closures, and check points along most of the seafront stretch. You can see the white perimeter wall and the formal gardens from a distance, but photography is discouraged near the entrance gates. The proximity to the major Carthage archaeological sites makes the security presence noticeable — expect plain-clothes officers in plain view.
