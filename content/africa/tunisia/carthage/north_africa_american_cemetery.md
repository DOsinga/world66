The North Africa American Cemetery and Memorial sits north of the main Carthage archaeological zone on a quiet stretch of suburban Tunis. It is the only American military cemetery in Africa from World War II and holds the graves of 2,841 American servicemen who died during the North African Campaign — primarily in the fighting against the Afrika Korps in Tunisia in the spring of 1943.

The site is impeccably kept by the American Battle Monuments Commission. White marble headstones radiate out in geometric arcs from a central memorial wall inscribed with the names of 3,724 missing in action. The accompanying chapel includes a large mosaic-floor map of the campaign, marking the major engagements. Visiting is free, the grounds are open daily, and the staff are knowledgeable and unhurried.
