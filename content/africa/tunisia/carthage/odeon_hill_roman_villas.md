The Hill of the Odeon and the park of the Roman villas show a quieter side of Carthage than the Baths of Antoninus or Byrsa Hill. Here the attraction is the spread of foundations, mosaics, terraces, and partial reconstructions that hint at how wealthy Roman Carthage once lived.

It rewards patience more than spectacle. Walk slowly, use the site as part of a wider Carthage circuit, and let the open ground connect the fragments: theatre, houses, baths, and sea-facing suburbs rather than one isolated ruin.
