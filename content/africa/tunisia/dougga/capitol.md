The temple thus stands as a private monument to a public hope that did not quite hold."

The Capitol is Dougga's most photographed monument, and rightly so: a six-columned Corinthian portico, fluted shafts in honey-gold limestone, supporting a complete pediment with traces of carved relief. The interior cella once held colossal statues of Jupiter, Juno, and Minerva — the Capitoline triad — of which only fragments survive in the Bardo Museum in Tunis.

Built in the 2nd century AD on the south side of the Forum, the temple stands on an artificial terrace cut into the hillside. The view from the steps down onto the forum and out across the olive groves is one of the great Roman views in the Mediterranean. The image of the Capitol appears on the Tunisian 5-dinar banknote — try to spot it before you arrive.
