Borj El Hissar — "the fortress on the spit" — sits at the western tip of Gharbi island, where the land tails off into a long sandbar pointing toward Sfax. The visible ruin is a small fort built in stages from the Roman period through the Aghlabid and Ottoman eras, with thick walls and the remains of a watchtower. The location is what makes it: cut off from the village by a stretch of beach, lapped by extremely shallow turquoise water, and almost always deserted.

The site is unguarded and free to walk through. A few cisterns and column drums lie around the ruins, and at low tide you can wade out along the sandbar for surprisingly long distances. The drive or cycle out from Mellita through palm groves and small villages is part of the appeal. Take water and a hat — there is no shade and no café anywhere nearby.
