La Marsa is the most elegant of the seaside suburbs strung along the cliff coast north of **[Tunis](/africa/tunisia/tunis)** — a leafy, well-off beach town that has been the summer escape of the Tunisian capital since Ottoman times. It sits at the northern end of the **TGM** light-rail line that runs from Tunis through Carthage and **[Sidi Bou Said](/africa/tunisia/sidibousaid)**, and the half-hour ride is the easiest way for visitors to get out of the heat of the medina to the sea.

The town centres on a long, palm-lined **[Corniche](/africa/tunisia/lamarsa/corniche)** along a sandy beach, busy on summer evenings with families, ice-cream vendors, and the foreign embassies' weekend crowds. The Bey of Tunis spent his summers here in the 19th century, and the old beylical palaces — most notably the **[Abdellia Palace](/africa/tunisia/lamarsa/abdellia_palace)** — still stand among the modern villas. The **[Café Saf-Saf](/africa/tunisia/lamarsa/cafe_saf_saf)**, set under an ancient fig tree around a working draw-well, has been pouring mint tea since the 18th century and is the obvious place to stop. The atmosphere is more French than Arab — La Marsa is where many of Tunis's diplomatic corps and bourgeoisie live, and the cafés, bakeries, and bookshops reflect it.

There is not a lot of sightseeing — La Marsa is for walking the corniche, lying on the beach, eating well in the evening, and using the place as a base for visits to **[Carthage](/africa/tunisia/carthage)** and **[Sidi Bou Said](/africa/tunisia/sidibousaid)** a few minutes' train ride south. It is also the most relaxed and cosmopolitan place to stay in greater Tunis, and a sensible alternative to a hotel in the centre.
