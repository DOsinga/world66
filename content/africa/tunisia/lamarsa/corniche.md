The Corniche is the spine of La Marsa and the closest thing the city has to an unmissable sight. It is a wide, palm-lined promenade running for about two kilometres along the beach, with cafés, restaurants, ice-cream shops, and a strip of sand wide enough to spread out a towel. In the cooler months it is a place to walk; in summer it becomes the main social space of greater Tunis, packed in the evenings and on weekends.

The atmosphere is unmistakeably Mediterranean and entirely Tunisian — couples promenading, families picnicking, teenagers on motor scooters. Cafés range from cheap-and-cheerful to upmarket; the best ones have terraces directly above the beach. Most have free wi-fi and stay open late. The view across the Gulf of Tunis is at its best in the late afternoon, when the light goes amber and the **[Sidi Bou Said](/africa/tunisia/sidibousaid)** cliffs to the south stand out clearly.
