Medenine is the regional capital of southeastern Tunisia, sitting on the main road between **[Gabes](/africa/tunisia/gabes)** and the Libyan border. It is a working transit town of around 80,000 people, not a destination in itself — but it is the natural pivot point for anyone exploring the **ksour country** of the south. The old town was once a remarkable concentration of **ksour** — fortified Berber granaries built of stacked vaulted cells called ghorfas — and although most were demolished in the 1960s to make way for modern housing, the partial survival of the central ksar gives a sense of what was lost.

The town itself has little to offer beyond a few hours' wander: the **[Ksar Medenine](/africa/tunisia/medenine/ksar_medenine)** in the centre, with its arcaded courtyards now occupied by craft shops and cafés, and a small museum of Berber traditions. The market on Mondays is busy and worth catching if your timing is right. Otherwise this is a place to fuel up, stock up on supplies, and move on.

The reason to stop is the surrounding country. **[Metameur](/africa/tunisia/medenine/metameur)**, 6 km west, has a beautifully preserved three-storey ksar still in active use. **[Ksar Hadada](/africa/tunisia/medenine/ksar_hadada)** to the south doubled as Luke Skywalker's slave quarters in *The Phantom Menace*. The fortified Berber villages of **[Chenini](/africa/tunisia/chenini)** and **[Ghomrassen](/africa/tunisia/ghomrassen)** are accessible day trips, and **[Tataouine](/africa/tunisia/tataouine)** — the larger town further south and the gateway to the best ksour — is only an hour away. Travellers heading south to the desert almost always pass through Medenine; spending half a day here, rather than just changing buses, repays the effort.
