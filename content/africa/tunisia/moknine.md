Moknine is a town in the Tunisian Sahel, surrounded by vast fields of olive trees and positioned between the tourist centres of [Sousse](/africa/tunisia/sousse) (35 km), [Monastir](/africa/tunisia/monastir) (20 km), and Mahdia (25 km). It is known throughout [Tunisia](/africa/tunisia) as the centre of Sahel pottery.

The [pottery tradition](/africa/tunisia/moknine/pottery_workshops) here goes back centuries, sustained by abundant local clay deposits and a natural fuel called grig, made from non-olive wood. Pottery was once used in every domain of daily life — for storing food, cooling water, and cooking. While everyday use has faded, the decorative tradition remains strong, and the [potters' workshops](/africa/tunisia/moknine/pottery_workshops) in and around Moknine are the principal source of the ornamental ceramics you will see across the Sahel. The town's [old medina](/africa/tunisia/moknine/moknine_medina) is compact and authentic, a good example of a working Sahel town that has not been reshaped for tourism.
