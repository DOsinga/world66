Moknine is Tunisia's principal centre for traditional Sahel pottery, and the workshops on the edge of town are the town's most distinctive attraction. Potters here use local clay and traditional kick-wheels to produce the characteristic earthenware of the region — wide-bellied storage jars, decorated plates, and smaller household pieces in terracotta with geometric incised patterns. Firing is done in traditional kilns using grig, a fuel made from non-olive wood.

Visitors are usually welcome to watch the potters at work. The quality of pieces varies — the most careful potters produce work that is genuinely impressive, while more basic production pieces are available at low prices. Buying directly from the workshop supports the tradition. The best time to visit is morning, when the kilns are active.
