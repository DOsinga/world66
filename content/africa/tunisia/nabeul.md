Nabeul is the pottery capital of Tunisia, and it wears that title openly. The town on the Cap Bon peninsula, about 65 km from Tunis, is lined with [workshops and shops](/africa/tunisia/nabeul/pottery_workshops) selling the distinctive blue-and-white glazed ceramics, terracotta pots, and tiles that have been made here for centuries. The weekly Friday market draws traders from across the region.

Beyond the ceramics, Nabeul has a pleasant old medina and a good beach. The [Nabeul Museum](/africa/tunisia/nabeul/nabeul_museum) holds important archaeological finds from the Phoenician site at Kerkouane and from Roman Neapolis — the ancient predecessor of the modern town — and is more rewarding than it looks from the outside. The town is quieter and more authentic than the resort strip further along the coast. It also serves as a useful base for exploring Cap Bon — the vineyards, citrus groves, and the Kerkouane ruins (a UNESCO World Heritage Site) are all within easy reach.

Be prepared for persistent salesmanship in the market and pottery shops. Bargaining is expected, and prices rarely start where they end.
