Tabarka sits in the far northwest of Tunisia, squeezed between the [Kroumirie Hills](/africa/tunisia/tabarka/kroumirie_hills) and the Mediterranean. The setting is genuinely pretty, and the town has grown into a low-key resort without losing too much of its character.

The waters offshore are rich in fish and coral — Tabarka has a reputation as one of Tunisia's better spots for [scuba diving](/africa/tunisia/tabarka/scuba_diving) and underwater photography. The [Tabarka Rocks](/africa/tunisia/tabarka/tabarka_rocks) — a cluster of dramatic limestone stacks rising from the sea just west of town — create an underwater reef that divers explore on calm days. Above the waterline, the [Fort de Gênes](/africa/tunisia/tabarka/fort_de_genes), built by Genoese coral merchants in 1542, commands the rocky promontory above the harbour and is worth the short climb for the views alone.

There's good hiking in the hills behind town, and the Romans were clearly fond of the area too: the underground villas of [Bulla Regia](/africa/tunisia/tabarka/bulla_regia), with their mosaic floors and cool subterranean rooms, lie about 70 kilometres south and are among the most unusual Roman ruins in North Africa.
