The small site museum sits at the entrance, in a one-storey building that doubles as the ticket office. The displays are modest but useful — Phoenician pottery, Punic stelae, Roman lamps, glassware, and a few mosaics lifted from the houses on the hill for protection.

Labels are in Arabic and French. The collection is small enough to read in twenty minutes but worth doing before you walk the site, because it helps make sense of what is left in the ground. The richest finds from Utica are in the Bardo in Tunis; what remains here are the everyday objects, which in some ways are the more telling.
