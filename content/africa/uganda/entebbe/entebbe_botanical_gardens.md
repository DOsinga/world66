The Entebbe Botanical Gardens occupy 100 acres of landscaped grounds that slope down to a kilometre-long stretch of Lake Victoria shoreline. Established in 1898 as a colonial agricultural research station, the gardens now contain over 300 plant species and function as the most beautiful green space in the Entebbe area.

The gardens are excellent for birdwatching — grey parrots, hornbills, herons, and numerous species of weaver birds nest in the tall trees, and the lakeside section attracts water birds year-round. The towering fig trees and mahogany along the main paths give the grounds a genuinely primeval atmosphere. Several clearly marked walking trails cover the gardens in an hour or two. Entry is cheap and the gardens are rarely crowded. They sit within walking distance of the Uganda Wildlife Education Centre, making the two an easy combined visit.
