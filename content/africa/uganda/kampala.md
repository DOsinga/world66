Kampala is Uganda's capital, a sprawling, hilly, noisy city of around 1.7 million people a few miles north of Lake Victoria. Built across seven hills — the colonial-era joke that it was another Rome was taken seriously by town planners — it is the busy, matatu-clogged hub of a country that has rebuilt itself from the wreckage of Idi Amin's rule and the decades of civil war that followed.

The city has a handful of sights worth a morning each. The [Kasubi Tombs](/africa/uganda/kampala/kasubitombs), burial place of the Buganda kings, are a UNESCO World Heritage Site (rebuilt after a 2010 fire) and a window into the pre-colonial kingdom that still dominates life in central Uganda. The [Kabaka's Palace](/africa/uganda/kampala/kabakaspalace) and the haunting Idi Amin torture chambers in the old military compound beneath it give a sobering counterweight. The [Uganda Museum](/africa/uganda/kampala/nationalmuseum) in Kamwokya is a slightly dusty but fascinating collection of ethnographic and musical-instrument displays from across the country, and the [Ndere Cultural Centre](/africa/uganda/kampala/ndere_cultural_centre) is the best place to see traditional Ugandan music and dance performed well.

The hilltop [Uganda National Mosque](/africa/uganda/kampala/gaddafi_mosque) — still widely known as the Gaddafi Mosque — offers the best panoramic views in the city from its minaret. The rival cathedrals of [Rubaga](/africa/uganda/kampala/rubaga_cathedral) and [Namirembe](/africa/uganda/kampala/namirembe_cathedral) face each other from their respective hills, a reminder of the Catholic-Anglican missionary competition that shaped modern Uganda. Further out, the serene [Bahá'í House of Worship](/africa/uganda/kampala/bahai_temple) on Kikaya Hill is one of only eight such temples in the world.

Most travellers use Kampala as a one- or two-day stop before heading out to the national parks of the west and south (Bwindi, Queen Elizabeth, Murchison Falls) for gorilla trekking and safaris. But spend a night in the city itself — the food is lively, the [markets](/africa/uganda/kampala/owino_market) are a spectacle, and the Ugandans you meet will be among the friendliest on the continent.
