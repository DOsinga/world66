The main viewpoint sits on the Zambian rim a few metres back from the edge, a simple platform of rock with no railings and a long uninterrupted view of the gorge. A narrower path leads further along the cliff to a second outcrop where you can see the falls in profile against the canyon wall. The Tanzanian side is directly opposite — the international border runs down the centre of the river.

In the wet season (December to April) the river carries enough water to make the falls genuinely spectacular, with mist rising hundreds of metres up the gorge. In the dry season the flow is reduced but the falls never stop. Marabou storks nest on the cliffs below and circle in the updrafts; the gorge is also a stopover for migrating bee-eaters and raptors. Bring water, sun protection, and good shoes — the rock is loose and there is no shade.
