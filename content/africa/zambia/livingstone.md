Livingstone is the town that grew up in the shadow of Victoria Falls, and it has never quite been able to compete with the spectacle at its doorstep. That is no criticism — the falls are overwhelming, and few towns could hold their own against one of the world's largest waterfalls. What Livingstone offers is the Zambian side of the experience: a relaxed, functional base for visiting the falls and the surrounding national park, with fewer crowds and lower prices than its glossier neighbour Livingstone (Zimbabwe) across the border.

The town was established by the British South Africa Company in 1905 as a commercial hub, named after the explorer-missionary David Livingstone who had arrived at the falls in 1855 and became the first European to record them. It served as the capital of Northern Rhodesia until 1935, which explains the remnants of colonial-era infrastructure — a decent museum, a handsome park, and some early 20th-century buildings that give the town more character than a purely utilitarian border settlement would have.

The [Mosi-oa-Tunya National Park](/africa/zambia/livingstone/mosi_oa_tunya_national_park) begins just south of town and runs to the falls. Within the park is the only population of white rhinos in Zambia, protected and regularly visited on game drives. The [Livingstone Museum](/africa/zambia/livingstone/livingstone_museum) is the best in Zambia and provides useful context on the region's history before you head to the falls. The [Railway Museum](/africa/zambia/livingstone/railway_museum) is small but unexpectedly engaging, with relics of the BSAC railway network that opened up this part of southern Africa.

Most visitors use Livingstone as a two- or three-day base. The falls are essential; the national park game drive is worthwhile; the town itself can be absorbed in an afternoon. It is not a place to linger for its own sake, but it is a comfortable and unpretentious place to be.
