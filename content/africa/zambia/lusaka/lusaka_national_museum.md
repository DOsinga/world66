The Lusaka National Museum sits on Independence Avenue in the Cathedral Hill district and covers the history and culture of Zambia across four linked sections: ethnography, witchcraft, political history, and contemporary art. It is one of the better-presented museums in sub-Saharan Africa, housed in a striking building opened in 1996.

The ethnography collection documents traditional ways of life across Zambia's 73 ethnic groups — tools, textiles, musical instruments, and ceremonial objects arranged with enough context to make them meaningful rather than merely decorative. The contemporary art gallery showcases paintings, sculpture, and ceramics by Zambian artists, with work that ranges from formal academic pieces to expressive street-influenced art. The political history section covers independence in 1964 and the decades since, including the copper boom and subsequent economic difficulties.

Admission is inexpensive and the museum is rarely crowded, which makes it an easy and rewarding half-day stop.
