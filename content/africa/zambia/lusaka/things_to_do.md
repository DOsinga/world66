---
title: "Things to Do"
type: section
---

Lusaka's main attractions are its museums, markets, and craft centres. You won't need more than a day or two to see the highlights, but the city's markets alone can absorb a good few hours.

The [Lusaka National Museum](/africa/zambia/lusaka/lusaka_national_museum) sits on Independence Avenue and covers the full arc of Zambian history across four sections: ethnography, witchcraft, political history, and contemporary art. Its witchcraft exhibit — featuring objects confiscated by police, presented as cultural history — is unlike anything in most museums.

The Lusaka City Market, a huge single-roof marketplace west of Cairo Road, houses over 4,000 stalls selling clothes, local art, jewellery, and food. For crafts and gifts, head to [Kabwata Cultural Village](/africa/zambia/lusaka/kabwata_cultural_village) on Burma Road, where artists work on-site in thatched workshops, or the Sunday craft market at Arcades Shopping Centre.

Beyond the city, [Lusaka National Park](/africa/zambia/lusaka/lusaka_national_park) — one of the only national parks within a capital city anywhere in the world — sits 30 kilometres east with white rhinos, giraffes, and zebras. [Munda Wanga Environmental Park](/africa/zambia/lusaka/munda_wanga_environmental_park) to the south has a small zoo, botanical gardens, and natural swimming pools. For something more unusual, [Kalimba Reptile Park](/africa/zambia/lusaka/kalimba_reptile_park) north of the city lets you get close to pythons, crocodiles, and monitor lizards.

The [Freedom Statue](/africa/zambia/lusaka/freedom_statue) on Independence Avenue and the [Embassy Park Presidential Burial Site](/africa/zambia/lusaka/embassy_park) near Cathedral Hill are both worth a stop for anyone interested in the country's post-independence history.
