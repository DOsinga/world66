Ndola is the capital of [Zambia](/africa/zambia)'s Copperbelt, tucked into the crook of the country against the Congolese border. It is the second-largest city in Zambia and the main stop between [Lusaka](/africa/zambia/lusaka) and Kitwe. The economy runs on copper — the mines have been here since the colonial era, and copper products are everywhere in the markets.

The city has a lively [central market](/africa/zambia/ndola/ndola_central_market) where you can find chitenge (wrap cloth), carved animals, jewellery, and all manner of copper goods. It is a friendly, walkable city, considerably more relaxed than Lusaka. The [Dag Hammarskjöld Memorial](/africa/zambia/ndola/dag_hammarskjold_memorial), about 13 km outside town, marks the spot where the UN Secretary-General's plane crashed in 1961 under circumstances that remain disputed — a sombre but historically significant site.

The [Copperbelt Museum](/africa/zambia/ndola/copperbelt_museum) provides the best overview of the region's mining history. The [Slave Tree](/africa/zambia/ndola/slave_tree), a national monument, stands in the city centre — a living relic of the East African slave trade. Ndola serves mainly as a transit point on the way to or from the Congolese border, but it is a pleasant enough place to spend a night.
