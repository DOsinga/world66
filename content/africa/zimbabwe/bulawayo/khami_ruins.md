Khami Ruins, about 22 km west of Bulawayo, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of Zimbabwe's most important archaeological sites. The ruins are the remains of a capital city that flourished between the 15th and 17th centuries as the seat of the Torwa dynasty, successor to the Great Zimbabwe civilisation. The site covers a large area of terraced stone walls, platforms, and decorated facades that demonstrate sophisticated construction techniques.

The ruins are less visited and less famous than Great Zimbabwe, which makes them more rewarding in some ways — quieter, more peaceful, and easier to explore at your own pace. A small museum at the entrance explains the history and archaeology of the site. The setting is attractive: the ruins sit above the Khami River, and the views from the upper terraces are pleasant. Allow a couple of hours. The site is accessible by car from Bulawayo on a road that continues to become less maintained further from the city.
