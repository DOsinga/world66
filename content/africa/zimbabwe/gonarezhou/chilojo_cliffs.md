The Chilojo Cliffs are the visual signature of Gonarezhou — great walls of Clarens sandstone, banded in deep red and ochre, rising 180 metres above a sweeping bend in the Runde River. They run for roughly 20 kilometres along the south bank, their colour shifting from burnt orange at midday to something close to blood-red in the last hour before sunset. This is the moment to be at the cliff-top viewpoint above Chipinda Pools, watching elephant herds move single-file along the white sand of the river below.

The geology is the same Triassic sandstone that forms the Drakensberg further south and the Waterberg in South Africa — ancient desert dunes, compressed and stained by iron oxide. The rock faces are layered and horizontally banded, the lines broken here and there by cracks and overhangs where Pel's fishing owl and various raptors nest. From the water's edge, the scale is genuinely humbling; from the ridge above, the view stretches across the Mozambique lowveld on the far bank.

Access is easiest from Chipinda Pools camp in the northern sector. The main viewing point is a short drive from camp on a track that requires a high-clearance 4WD, particularly after rain. Walking on the cliff faces themselves is not permitted, but guided walks to the base are sometimes available through park management. Early morning and late afternoon are the best times — not only for the light but because the heat at midday in the lowveld can be extreme.
