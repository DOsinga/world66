The Great Enclosure is the defining monument of Great Zimbabwe — a massive oval structure with walls reaching eleven metres high and up to five metres thick, enclosing an area of roughly 270 metres in circumference. It is the largest ancient structure in sub-Saharan Africa south of the Sahara, and it was almost certainly the royal enclosure where the Mwene Mutapa (the ruler) lived and received tribute.

The walls are dry-stone masonry of extraordinary quality — granite blocks shaped and stacked without mortar, with decorative chevron and herringbone patterns near the top of the outer wall. Inside, a narrow passage between two concentric walls leads to the famous Conical Tower, a solid stone cylinder whose purpose remains mysterious. The overall effect is of a space designed to awe and impress — which was clearly the intention.
