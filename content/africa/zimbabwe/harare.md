Harare is Zimbabwe’s capital and largest city, set at about 1,500 metres on the central plateau. It has been through very difficult times — hyperinflation, political repression, economic collapse — and the city shows the scars. But it remains a surprisingly green and pleasant place, with wide tree-lined avenues, parks, and a more relaxed atmosphere than you might expect.

The [National Gallery of Zimbabwe](/africa/zimbabwe/harare/nationalgallery) is one of the best in southern Africa, with an outstanding collection of Shona stone sculpture. The [Harare Gardens](/africa/zimbabwe/harare/hararegardens) next door make for a pleasant stroll. The [Natural History Museum](/africa/zimbabwe/harare/queenvictoria) covers local wildlife and geology. For crafts and art, [Chapungu Kraal](/africa/zimbabwe/harare/chapungu_kraal) sculpture park on the outskirts is worth a half-day. Don't miss the [Eastgate Centre](/africa/zimbabwe/harare/eastgate_centre) on Robert Mugabe Road — a famous work of biomimicry architecture that cools itself without air conditioning, modelled on termite mounds.

Day trips from Harare include the [Balancing Rocks at Epworth](/africa/zimbabwe/harare/balancing_rocks) — granite formations that appeared on every Zimbabwean banknote — plus the Lion and Cheetah Park and Lake Chivero Game Park. The Tengenenge Sculpture Community, a longer drive north, is a remarkable open-air workshop where hundreds of sculptors work in stone.
