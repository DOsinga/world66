Mana Pools is Zimbabwe's great walking-safari wilderness, a lower-Zambezi landscape of floodplain pools, river islands, albida woodland, and the Zambezi escarpment rising blue in the distance. It is remote, hot, and not especially easy to reach, which is part of why it remains so special.

The name means "four pools" in Shona, referring to the permanent oxbow pools left by old Zambezi channels. In the dry season, wildlife moves down from the escarpment toward the river and the pools, concentrating elephant, buffalo, impala, eland, waterbuck, hippo, crocodile, lion, leopard, wild dog, and extraordinary birdlife in a relatively open floodplain. The light under the winterthorn trees is famous among photographers for good reason.

Mana Pools has a freedom that few African parks still allow. Walking is central to the experience, though rules change and permits or professional guides may be required. The point is not bravado; it is the rare chance to experience big-game country on foot, reading tracks, wind, and distance rather than watching everything through a windscreen.

This is not a casual add-on from [Harare](/africa/zimbabwe/harare) or [Victoria Falls](/africa/zimbabwe/victoriafalls). Roads are long and rough, fuel and supplies must be planned, and the heat from September to November is serious. But for travellers who want wild Zimbabwe at its most elemental, Mana Pools is one of the country's finest places.
