---
title: When to Go
type: section
---

Bamiyan sits at about 2,500 metres in the central Hindu Kush, and the climate is determined by altitude.

**Summer (May to September)** is the only practical season for most visitors. Days are warm and clear, the valley is green and the apricot trees are in fruit, the road to Band-e Amir is open, and the air is dry. June through August is the peak.

**Winter (November to March)** is severe — heavy snow closes the high passes between Bamiyan and Kabul, often for weeks at a time, and the cold drops well below -20°C. Don't try to drive in.

**Shoulder months (April, October)** are unpredictable — sometimes warm and clear, sometimes snowy.

The annual **Buddha Day** events around 22 March (Nowruz, the Afghan New Year) draw local crowds to the cliff. Security conditions across Afghanistan can change rapidly; check the situation close to your travel date.
