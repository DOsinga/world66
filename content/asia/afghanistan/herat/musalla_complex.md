The Musalla complex was the great architectural achievement of Queen Gawhar Shad, wife of the Timurid ruler Shah Rukh, built in the 15th century. It comprised a congregational mosque, a madrassa, and a mausoleum, all decorated in the extraordinary tilework of the Timurid school. What remains today is a fragment of that glory: two minarets of the original six, still covered in brilliant mosaic faience, and the mausoleum of Gawhar Shad herself.

The minarets lean at alarming angles — subsidence and earthquake damage — but the tilework remains visible, cobalt and turquoise geometric patterns on a background of pale stone. The mausoleum interior, when accessible, contains some of the finest Timurid stucco work in existence. The site is northwest of the old city, a short drive from the citadel.
