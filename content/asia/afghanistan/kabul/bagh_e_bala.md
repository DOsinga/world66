Bagh-e Bala is a hilltop palace and park in northern Kabul, built in the late 19th century for Amir Abdur Rahman Khan. The setting is the draw: a royal building above the city, with trees, open ground, and views that help explain why Kabul's rulers kept choosing elevated garden sites.

The palace has had several lives, including royal residence, guesthouse, restaurant, and public landmark. Current access in Kabul should always be checked locally, and security conditions matter, but as a historic site it sits comfortably alongside Darul Aman, Tajbeg, and Bala Hissar in the city's royal and military geography.
