The grid of covered streets and small shops radiating out from the Blue Mosque is one of the busiest commercial districts in northern Afghanistan. Mazar sits on the old trade routes between Iran, Central Asia and the Indian subcontinent, and the bazaar still reflects that — Uzbek traders, Turkmen carpet sellers, Hazara silversmiths, and Pashtun fruit merchants all work within a few blocks of each other.

The most distinctive local craft is the Mazari carpet, particularly the small fine-knotted prayer rugs in deep reds and blues. The fruit and dried-nut stalls are extraordinary even by Afghan standards — Mazar's pomegranates, melons and almonds are renowned. Bring a local guide and dress conservatively; cameras attract attention and the security situation can be tense.
