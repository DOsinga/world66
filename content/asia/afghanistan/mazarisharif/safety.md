---
title: "Safety"
type: section
---

Afghanistan is one of the most difficult and dangerous countries in the world for foreign travellers, and Mazar-i-Sharif is no exception. Since the Taliban takeover in 2021, the active conflict has largely ended but the threat from Islamic State Khorasan Province (ISKP) remains real — the city has been the site of multiple bombings of Shia mosques in recent years.

Most Western governments advise against all travel. There is effectively no consular assistance available to foreign nationals. Kidnapping for ransom and arbitrary detention are both serious risks.

If you do travel, work through an established local fixer, register with any embassy that will still accept registrations, dress and behave conservatively, never travel after dark, and avoid concentrations of people — particularly around Shia religious sites on Fridays and holy days. Women face severe restrictions on movement, dress and public presence. Photography of military installations, checkpoints or government buildings will get you arrested.
