Bab al-Bahrain — the Gate of Bahrain — was built by the British in 1945, on what was then the customs waterfront at the edge of the sea. It served as the formal ceremonial entrance to Manama and the start of the main commercial street. The building now houses Bahrain's tourism directorate and a small visitor information office. Its construction reflects the hybrid aesthetic of British colonial administration in the Gulf: a low-rise stone structure with arched windows and crenellations, gesturing at Arabesque design while remaining thoroughly functional.

The gate is best understood as a threshold. Walk through the archway and you're immediately in the old souq network — narrow covered alleyways that split into the gold souq, the spice souq, and the fabric market. The gold souq is the most active: hundreds of stalls stacked with 21-carat and 22-carat gold jewellery, priced by weight with a workmanship charge negotiated separately. Evening is the right time to visit, when the lights are on and the stalls are at their busiest. The surrounding streets have changed dramatically from their original layout through land reclamation and road building, but the souq core behind the gate is the most intact piece of pre-oil Manama that survives.
