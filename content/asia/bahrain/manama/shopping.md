---
title: "Shopping"
type: section
---

The gold souq behind Bab al-Bahrain is the main traditional shopping destination — hundreds of stalls dealing in 21-carat and 22-carat gold jewellery, priced by the weight of the gold with a separate workmanship charge. Bargaining is expected on the workmanship component; the gold weight price is fixed by the daily market rate. The adjacent alleyways have strong fabric sections, particularly Indian and Pakistani textiles sold by the metre. Spice shops occupy their own part of the souq, selling saffron, dried limes, and Gulf spice blends. The souq is best visited in the evening.

For modern retail, Seef Mall and City Centre Bahrain are the two largest shopping centres, both in the Seef district northwest of the city centre. They carry the standard international brands. Bahrain has no sales tax — unlike the UAE, which introduced 5 percent VAT in 2018 — making electronics and luxury goods modestly cheaper than in Dubai or Abu Dhabi. This gap has narrowed but still attracts some cross-border shopping traffic from Saudi Arabia, which also has VAT.

A craft and produce market runs on Wednesday afternoons near the Adhari Hotel, selling local food products, handicrafts, and artisan goods. It is small but worth checking if you're around on a Wednesday.
