Bagerhat is a small, low-key town in southwest Bangladesh with one very good reason to come: the ruins of Khalifatabad, the 15th-century mosque city founded by the Sufi saint and administrator Khan Jahan Ali. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, but it still feels more like a provincial pilgrimage stop than a polished monument zone.

The headline sight is the [Sixty Dome Mosque](/asia/bangladesh/bagerhat/sixty_dome_mosque), a long brick prayer hall whose name undersells it: the roof carries a grid of low domes, and the heavy walls and corner towers give it the feel of a fortress as much as a mosque. Around it are ponds, smaller mosques, and traces of the old city plan.

The other half of Bagerhat's identity is devotional. The [mausoleum of Khan Jahan Ali](/asia/bangladesh/bagerhat/khan_jahan_ali_mausoleum) still draws worshippers, and the smaller [Bibi Begni Mosque](/asia/bangladesh/bagerhat/bibi_begni_mosque) shows the same Bengal Sultanate brick style on a more intimate scale.

Bagerhat is best treated as a day trip from Khulna or Mongla rather than as a place to linger. Come for the old brick architecture, the ponds, and the calm rhythm of a town that has never been fully turned into a tourist site.
