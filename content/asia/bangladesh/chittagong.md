Chittagong (officially Chattogram) is Bangladesh's second city and its main port, sitting on the Karnaphuli River near where it meets the Bay of Bengal. It is a working city rather than a tourist one — crowded, commercial, and a gateway to other places more than a destination in its own right.

The old town has some genuine points of interest: the [Bayazid Bostami shrine](/asia/bangladesh/chittagong/bayazid_bostami_shrine) with its large tank of soft-shelled turtles, the 17th-century [Anderkilla Shahi Jame Mosque](/asia/bangladesh/chittagong/anderkilla_mosque), the 14th-century [Qadam Mubarak Mosque](/asia/bangladesh/chittagong/qadam_mubarak_mosque) said to contain a footprint of the Prophet, and the Mughal tank of [Laldighi](/asia/bangladesh/chittagong/laldighi) at the heart of the bazaar district. The World War II [Commonwealth War Cemetery](/asia/bangladesh/chittagong/commonwealth_war_cemetery) is well maintained and worth visiting. The 16th-century Portuguese quarter at [Firingi Bazar](/asia/bangladesh/chittagong/firingi_bazar) and the harbour area at [Sadarghat](/asia/bangladesh/chittagong/sadarghat) — the city's oldest waterfront — are worth a wander, as is the climb up [DC Hill](/asia/bangladesh/chittagong/dc_hill) for views over the old town.

Outside the city, the [Shipbreaking Yards](/asia/bangladesh/chittagong/shipbreaking_yards) at Sitakunda are one of the most extraordinary industrial sights in South Asia — the place where the world's ocean-going ships are run aground and broken up by hand. [Patenga Beach](/asia/bangladesh/chittagong/patenga_beach) and [Foy's Lake](/asia/bangladesh/chittagong/foys_lake) are the main local outings for families.

Chittagong is the jumping-off point for [Cox's Bazar](/asia/bangladesh/coxsbazar) to the south and the Chittagong Hill Tracts to the east. Most travellers pass through rather than linger.
