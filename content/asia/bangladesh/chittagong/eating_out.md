---
title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

Chittagong's food is among the most distinctive in Bangladesh. The city's Chittagonian cuisine is heavier on fish, dried fish, fresh coconut and red chilli than the food of the rest of the country, and uses techniques borrowed from the Arakanese, Portuguese and Mughal traders who passed through over the centuries.

**Mezban** is the city's signature feast — large communal lunches built around beef, served with a thick red curry over rice. Originally a wedding and political-rally tradition, it is now also served at city restaurants. **Mejbani** in the GEC Circle area is one of the better mezban specialists.

**Boli Khelar restaurants** along the SS Khaled Road do good Chittagonian-style fish — *shutki* (dried-fish curries), *mach bhuna* (slow-fried fish) and *chui jhal* (whole hot prawns).

**Handi Restaurant** in the Agrabad commercial area is a long-standing place for Bengali-Mughlai dishes — biryani, kebabs and the local speciality of *kacchi* (mutton biryani layered with potatoes).

**Wholesale fish market** at Fishery Ghat opens before dawn and is worth visiting just to see the catch landed; the early-morning food stalls grill the freshest fish to order.

For sweets, **Hazi Biryani's** version of biryani is famous beyond Chittagong, and **Bonny Sweets** at Anderkilla makes excellent *roshogolla* and *chom chom*.
