story: >
  Curzon Hall was built as a town hall for Dhaka, but the Bengali partition of 1905 changed its purpose. The hall became part of the new University of Dhaka in 1921 and is where the Central Shaheed Minar protest movement had many of its earliest meetings. In 1952, students gathering here and at nearby Dhaka Medical College were among those who marched to protest the Urdu-only language policy — a march that ended in police gunfire and four deaths.
---

A beautiful example of Indo-Saracenic architecture built in 1904, combining Mughal arches and decorative brickwork with an English country house roofline. It now houses the science faculty of the University of Dhaka. The building is named after the Viceroy Lord Curzon who laid the foundation stone, though construction was completed well after his departure.

The exterior is the main draw — particularly the north and south facades with their layered arched galleries — and can be appreciated from the street or the adjacent grounds. The university campus around it has a pleasant, slightly worn quality and is a good place to observe Dhaka student life.
