story: >
  The original Shaheed Minar was built overnight in February 1952, two days after police shot dead students protesting the government's decision to make Urdu the sole official language of Pakistan. The Pakistani authorities demolished it within days and arrested the builders. A second was built and demolished again. The current structure dates from 1963 and has been expanded since independence. Every 21 February — International Mother Language Day, a UNESCO designation established in 2000 — Bangladeshis lay flowers here beginning at midnight.
---

The national monument to the martyrs of the 1952 Language Movement, in which students and activists died protesting Pakistan's attempt to impose Urdu as the only official language of the country, erasing Bengali. The movement that began here led directly to the assertion of Bengali cultural identity and ultimately to the 1971 Liberation War and independence.

The monument consists of a curved white structure representing a mother's outstretched arms sheltering her children, set in a paved plaza near Dhaka Medical College. On 21 February (Ekushey February), enormous crowds gather for a candlelit procession beginning at midnight, and the plaza is carpeted in flowers by dawn. At other times it is a quieter, reflective space — one of the most politically charged sites in Bangladesh.
