  The Star Mosque's original 18th-century structure was built by a nawab named Mirza Golam Pir (known as Mirza Ahmed Jan Ispahani). The blue and white star motifs covering every surface were assembled from imported plates and bowls — early Dhaka recycling.

A small but extraordinarily decorated mosque in Armanitola, the old Armenian quarter of Dhaka, whose every surface — walls, domes, interior arches — is encrusted with star-shaped mosaic patterns made from Chinese porcelain fragments. The effect is unlike any other mosque in Bangladesh: more like entering a jewellery box than a place of worship.

The mosque dates from the early 18th century but was radically transformed in the 19th century when a local merchant commissioned the tile decoration. Non-Muslim visitors are generally welcome outside of prayer times; dress modestly and remove shoes at the entrance. It is a ten-minute walk from Ahsan Manzil through the lanes of old Dhaka.
