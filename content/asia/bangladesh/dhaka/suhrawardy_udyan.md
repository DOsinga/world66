  On 7 March 1971, Sheikh Mujibur Rahman stood in this park and addressed an estimated two million people. His closing words — "Ebarer sangram amader muktir sangram, ebarer sangram swadhinatar sangram" (The struggle this time is for our freedom, the struggle this time is for our independence) — are considered the de facto declaration of independence, though the formal declaration came later. The speech was recognised as a UNESCO Memory of the World in 2017.

The large park in central Dhaka where two defining moments of Bangladeshi history occurred: Sheikh Mujibur Rahman's 7th March 1971 speech calling for independence, and the Pakistani military's surrender to Indian and Bengali forces on 16 December 1971. Both events took place on the same ground, now marked with monuments, including the Eternal Flame, an eternal flame set within a lotus-shaped structure.

The park is a popular green space for Dhaka residents — families picnicking on weekends, couples on shaded benches — and the historical weight of the location sits lightly on ordinary days. Visit on Independence Day (26 March) or Victory Day (16 December) for the commemorations.
