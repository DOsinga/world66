Sylhet sits in the northeast of Bangladesh in a region of tea estates, rivers, and lush green hills rolling up toward the Khasi and Jaintia mountains of Meghalaya. It is Bangladesh at its most scenic — very different from the flat delta that covers most of the country.

The city itself is a major pilgrimage centre, anchored by the [Shah Jalal Dargah](/asia/bangladesh/sylhet/shah_jalal_dargah) — the shrine of the 14th-century Sufi saint who brought Islam to the region. Around the dargah and the Surma River are the colonial-era [Keane Bridge](/asia/bangladesh/sylhet/keane_bridge), [Ali Amjad's Clock](/asia/bangladesh/sylhet/ali_amjads_clock), and the Mughal-era [Shahi Eidgah](/asia/bangladesh/sylhet/shahi_eidgah). Tea estates begin within the city limits at [Lakkatura](/asia/bangladesh/sylhet/lakkatura_tea_garden) and [Malnicherra](/asia/bangladesh/sylhet/sylhet_botanical_garden), the latter founded in 1849 and the oldest in the subcontinent.

The tea estates around [Srimangal](/asia/bangladesh/sylhet/srimangal) (about 80 km south of Sylhet) are the main draw for visitors heading further afield. You can walk through the plantations, visit a tea factory, and drink some of the best tea in the region. [Lawachara National Park](/asia/bangladesh/sylhet/lawachara_national_park), nearby, is one of the few places in Bangladesh where you can see hoolock gibbons in the wild. [Ratargul Swamp Forest](/asia/bangladesh/sylhet/ratargul_swamp_forest) — accessible by small boat and only properly accessible during the wet season — is an eerie, beautiful place: flooded jungle in shades of green.

The Sylhet valley is formed by the [Surma](/asia/bangladesh/sylhet/surma_river) and Kushiara rivers. From October to February, migratory birds from Siberia and the Himalayas winter in the wetlands and haors (shallow seasonal lakes) of this region. The [Tanguar Haor](/asia/bangladesh/sylhet/tanguar_haor) is one of the most important wetland ecosystems in Bangladesh and a Ramsar site. To the northeast, [Jaflong](/asia/bangladesh/sylhet/jaflong), [Bichanakandi](/asia/bangladesh/sylhet/bichanakandi), and [Lalakhal](/asia/bangladesh/sylhet/lalakhal) are popular border-area trips where rivers from the Khasi Hills cross into the country in dramatic stone-strewn beds.
