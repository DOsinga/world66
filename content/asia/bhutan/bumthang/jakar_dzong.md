  The dzong's name, Jakar Yugyal, means "castle of the white bird." According to tradition, when the site was being chosen in the 17th century, a large white bird suddenly appeared and circled the hilltop before landing — taken as an auspicious sign that this was the correct location. Jakar translates simply as "white bird."

Jakar Dzong is the administrative and religious fortress of Bumthang District, perched on a ridge above the Chokhor Valley with sweeping views in every direction. The dzong was originally built in the 17th century on the site of a smaller earlier structure, and expanded significantly in the 19th century. Like all Bhutanese dzongs it functions as both a monastery and an administrative centre — the government offices of Bumthang District still operate here alongside a monk body.

The dzong is impressive from a distance and worth visiting for the architecture and the views from the approach path. Visitors can enter the courtyard. Dress modestly; shoes must be removed when entering the temple sections.
