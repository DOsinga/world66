Lhuntsi is one of Bhutan's most remote districts, tucked into the northeast corner of the country amid jagged peaks and deep valleys. Not many visitors make it here — the journey from Thimphu takes about three days — but those who do find a quiet, largely unchanged part of the kingdom.

The [Lhuntse Dzong](/asia/bhutan/lhuntsi/lhuntse_dzong), perched above the Kuri Chhu river, is the main landmark. The district is also known for *kishuthara*, an intricate hand-woven silk brocade produced in the village of [Khoma](/asia/bhutan/lhuntsi/khoma_village) and prized across Bhutan; the ancestral home of the Wangchuck royal family lies a little further upriver at [Dungkar](/asia/bhutan/lhuntsi/dungkar). The [Lhuntse Tsechu](/asia/bhutan/lhuntsi/lhuntse_tsechu), held in mid-December, is a colourful festival worth timing a visit around if you are in the region, and the multi-day [Singye Dzong trek](/asia/bhutan/lhuntsi/singye_dzong_trek) leads north into some of the wildest country in eastern Bhutan.
