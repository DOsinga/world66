---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

The British Indian Ocean Territory consists of the Chagos Archipelago, dominated by the joint UK/US military base on Diego Garcia. There is no civilian tourism: the territory has been closed to the original Chagossian population since their forced removal in the 1960s/70s, and the only authorised visitors are military personnel, contractors, and the rare permitted sailor or researcher.

The climate is tropical maritime, hot and humid year-round. Temperatures sit between 25°C and 32°C; sea temperatures stay around 28°C.

**Drier season (May–October)** is the better window, with the southeast trade winds blowing steadily and lower rainfall.

**Wet season (November–April)** brings heavier rains. The Chagos sits well south of the main Indian Ocean cyclone belt, so direct hits are unlikely, but heavy weather is not.

**Yacht permits** to anchor in the lagoons of the outer atolls have historically been issued on a limited basis to vessels in transit; check the current status with the BIOT Administration in London before planning anything. **Diego Garcia itself remains closed** to all visitors who are not military or military-affiliated.

The Chagossians' decades-long legal effort to return to their islands is the defining political question for the territory.
