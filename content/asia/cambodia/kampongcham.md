Kampong Cham is a port city on the banks of the Mekong River, about 120 kilometres northeast of [Phnom Penh](/asia/cambodia/phnompenh). It is Cambodia's third-largest city and one of the more pleasant provincial towns to spend a day or two — relaxed, walkable, and less visited than the Angkor circuit.

The town's most significant sight is [Wat Nokor](/asia/cambodia/kampongcham/wat_nokor), a well-preserved Khmer temple complex a short tuk-tuk ride from the centre. Nearby, the twin hills of [Phnom Pros and Phnom Srei](/asia/cambodia/kampongcham/phnom_pros_phnom_srei) are topped by a stupa and shrines and offer views over the Mekong plain. A sobering reminder of the Khmer Rouge era, mass graves lie close to town — sites that draw visitors seeking to understand Cambodia's difficult recent history.

The [Mekong waterfront](/asia/cambodia/kampongcham/mekong_riverfront) is the heart of local life, with a [bamboo bridge](/asia/cambodia/kampongcham/koh_paen_bamboo_bridge) connecting the town to the island of Koh Paen during the dry season — a remarkable feat of annual reconstruction by local villagers. Upriver, the pre-Angkorian brick sanctuaries at [Wat Hanchey](/asia/cambodia/kampongcham/han_chey_temple) sit on a high bluff above the Mekong; downriver, the surviving teak pagoda of [Wat Maha Leap](/asia/cambodia/kampongcham/wat_maha_leap) is one of the last of its kind in the country.
