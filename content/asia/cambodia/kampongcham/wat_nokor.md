Wat Nokor — sometimes Wat Nokor Bachey — is a Khmer temple complex on the western edge of Kampong Cham, dating from the late 11th century and originally built in honour of the Buddha-king Jayavarman VII. The grey sandstone gopuras, blackened with age, are still in good condition, and the carved lintels and corner pediments show clear traces of their original Mahayana iconography.

The unusual feature of the site is that an active modern pagoda was built within the ancient walls in the 1950s, so visitors pass continually between weathered grey stone and brightly painted modern shrines. The monks are welcoming and the small surrounding courtyard is shaded by frangipani trees. Entry is free for foreigners as of mid-decade. The temple is two kilometres west of town along National Road 7.
