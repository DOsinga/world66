The salt fields between Kampot and Kep are best seen from the road in the dry season — January to April — when the pans are active and salt is piled in white mounds along the bunds. Workers use wooden rakes to push the salt into pyramids, and the geometric pattern of the flooded pans, reflecting the sky, is excellent for photography in the right light.

The fields are visible from the main road to Kep, about 8 kilometres east of Kampot. Cyclists can detour off the road along the field tracks. Salt production here uses traditional methods — sun evaporation over several weeks — and the resulting flakes are coarser and more mineral-rich than industrial salt. A small amount is sold locally; most is marketed in Phnom Penh. A few operations sell directly to visitors.
