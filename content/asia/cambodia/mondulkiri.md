Mondulkiri is Cambodia's largest and least densely populated province, occupying the northeastern highlands where the Mekong lowlands give way to rolling hills, pine forests, and waterfalls. At 800 metres elevation, the provincial capital Sen Monorom is one of the coolest places in Cambodia, a relief after the heat of Phnom Penh and the coast. The province borders Vietnam to the east and is home to the Bunong (Mnong) people, an indigenous highland community with a long tradition of elephant keeping.

The main draw for visitors is a combination of wildlife, waterfalls, and responsible elephant experiences. The [Elephant Valley Project](/asia/cambodia/mondulkiri/elephant_valley_project) and several other operations offer the chance to spend time with rescued working elephants in a forest setting, without riding — a model increasingly adopted as the ethical standard for elephant tourism in Southeast Asia. [Bou Sra Waterfall](/asia/cambodia/mondulkiri/bou_sra_waterfall), about 40 kilometres from Sen Monorom, is the most impressive waterfall in the province, dropping in two stages into forested gorges.

The landscape itself is the other attraction: pine forests on hilltops (unusual in tropical Cambodia), rolling grasslands, coffee and pepper plantations, and a general atmosphere of space and quiet absent from the rest of the country. Community-based trekking to [Bunong villages](/asia/cambodia/mondulkiri/bunong_villages) offers a window into highland life — weaving, traditional agriculture, and the animist ceremonies that coexist with Buddhism in the highlands — that feels entirely different from the Khmer heartland.

Getting here takes effort. The road from Phnom Penh is a 6–8 hour bus ride, and the province has no rail connection. Most travellers come for three or four days and wish they had more time.
