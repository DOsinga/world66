title: "Books"
type: section
---

Cambodia's recent history haunts most writing about Phnom Penh, and the canon is unusually rich.

- **First They Killed My Father** by Loung Ung. A child's memoir of the Khmer Rouge years — Loung was five years old when Phnom Penh fell in April 1975. Quiet, devastating, indispensable.

- **The Gate** by François Bizot. The French ethnologist's account of being captured by the Khmer Rouge in 1971, interrogated at length by the future commandant of S-21, and released. The chapters set in Phnom Penh during the fall of the city are extraordinary.

- **A History of Cambodia** by David Chandler. The standard one-volume history, written by the dean of Cambodian historians. Even-handed, readable, and the best context you can buy in a paperback.

- **In the Shadow of the Banyan** by Vaddey Ratner. A novel based on the author's childhood in royalist Phnom Penh and her family's experiences after April 1975. Lyrical where Loung Ung is austere.

- **Cambodia's Curse** by Joel Brinkley. A harsher, more politically engaged account of post-war Cambodia — useful as a counterweight to the more sentimental reconstruction-era reporting.
