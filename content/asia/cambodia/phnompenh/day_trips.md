Phnom Penh has fewer obvious day trips than Siem Reap, but a couple of half-days will get you outside the city. The [Killing Fields of Choeung Ek](/asia/cambodia/phnompenh/choeung_ek), 15 km south, are usually combined with [Tuol Sleng](/asia/cambodia/phnompenh/tuolslengmuseum) into a single sobering morning. The Mekong island of [Koh Dach (Silk Island)](/asia/cambodia/phnompenh/silk_island), 30 minutes by ferry from the city, is a low-key day of silk weaving and rural cycling. The 11th-century pre-Angkorian temple of Phnom Chisor, 60 km south, is a long climb in the heat but rewarding for archaeology enthusiasts. The [Phnom Tamao Wildlife Rescue Centre](/asia/cambodia/phnompenh/phnom_tamao_wildlife_rescue_centre), also south of the city, is the most ethical animal encounter near Phnom Penh.

The riverside town of [Udong](/asia/cambodia/udong), 40 km north, was Cambodia's capital before Phnom Penh and has a hilltop crowned with royal stupas and shrines — an easy and rewarding half-day for anyone interested in the country's pre-colonial history.

For anyone with more time, [Kampot](/asia/cambodia/kampot) (3 hours south by road) and the colonial hill station of [Bokor](/asia/cambodia/kampot/bokor_national_park) make a worthwhile overnight, as does the riverside town of [Kratie](/asia/cambodia/kratie) (5–6 hours north) for a chance to see the freshwater Irrawaddy dolphins.
