**Banteay Srei** is widely considered to have the finest stone carving in the entire Angkor complex. Built in 967 AD from rose-pink sandstone, its towers and lintels are covered in an almost impossibly intricate network of foliage, celestial nymphs, guardian figures, and mythological scenes from the Ramayana and Mahabharata. The scale is intimate — the towers are much smaller than those at Angkor Wat — which means the carving feels close and accessible rather than monumental and overwhelming.

The temple is located about 25 kilometres north of Siem Reap, which keeps it slightly less visited than the main Angkor circuit, though it has become increasingly popular. The road passes through countryside and can be dusty or muddy depending on the season. Combine it with the **Cambodia Landmine Museum** and perhaps a stop at **Kbal Spean** (the "River of a Thousand Lingas," where ancient carvings are submerged in a forest stream) — both lie on the same road. Arrive before 9am if possible: the inner enclosure is small and fills quickly.
