Hefei is Anhui's provincial capital and, by population, one of the larger cities in eastern China — over nine million people and growing fast. It is not a destination in the conventional sense. The city has no dramatic old town, no ancient temples of international standing, no scenery that would make it worth a detour. What it has is broad expressways, expanding tech campuses, and the restless energy of a Chinese city that has reinvented itself in a single generation from a provincial backwater into a hub for semiconductors, electric vehicles, and university research.

That said, Hefei has parks worth an afternoon. [Lord Bao Park](/asia/china/anhui/hefei/lord_bao_park) commemorates Bao Zheng, the famously incorruptible Song Dynasty magistrate who was born here, and the grounds around his tomb are pleasant enough for a walk. [Xiaoyaojin Park](/asia/china/anhui/hefei/xiaoyaojin_park), named for a battle site from the Three Kingdoms period, is the city's main recreation ground. The [Anhui Provincial Museum](/asia/china/anhui/hefei/anhui_provincial_museum) covers the province's history and archaeology with reasonable thoroughness, including some fine Han dynasty bronzes that justify the visit.

The food is Hefei's strongest argument for lingering. The city has a justified reputation for crayfish — xiaolongxia, cooked with garlic, chili, or black pepper depending on your mood — and the restaurants along [Ninguo Road](/asia/china/anhui/hefei/ninguo_road_food_street) and in the old wholesale district serve them from late evening until well after midnight. [Li Hongzhang hotpot](/asia/china/anhui/hefei/li_hongzhang_hotpot), named for the 19th-century statesman from the city, is the other dish worth ordering.

Practically, Hefei is a useful hub. Xinqiao International Airport connects to major Chinese cities, and high-speed rail reaches Shanghai in two hours, Nanjing in under one, and Huang Shan in about ninety minutes. If you're touring southern Anhui's villages and mountains, you'll likely pass through.
