  Chaohu is one of China's five great freshwater lakes and was historically the heart of a wealthy agricultural basin. Its name means "Nest Lake," from an ancient legend of a settlement swallowed by rising waters — a story that turns out to have some geological basis: seismic subsidence over long periods has changed the lake's boundaries significantly. Silver fish from Chaohu — a small freshwater species unique to the lake — appear in dried form in markets throughout Anhui.

Chaohu Lake, roughly 60 kilometers southeast of central Hefei, is one of China's five great freshwater lakes and one of the less-visited. At 780 square kilometers it's substantial, and the shoreline landscape of flat agricultural plain and reed beds has a quiet, unhurried quality quite different from the manicured scenic areas of southern Anhui. Boat trips on the lake are available from the town of Chaohu.

The lakeside town of Zhongyaomiao on the southwestern shore has temple complexes and the best-preserved stretch of historical streetscape in the area. Guyi Island, accessible by ferry, is a small forested hill rising from the lake with a Buddhist temple complex — the approach by water with the island silhouetted against the sky is the best way to arrive. The lake is at its most atmospheric in early morning when mist lies over the water.
