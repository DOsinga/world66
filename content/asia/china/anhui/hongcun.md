Hongcun is the more photographed of southern Anhui's two UNESCO-listed ancient villages, and the reason is immediately apparent: the half-moon pond at the village entrance, with its mirror reflection of whitewashed walls and ink-dark roofs, is one of the most composed natural photographs in China. The village appeared in Ang Lee's Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon and has been the subject of innumerable landscape paintings by the art students who descend on it in spring and autumn, easels set up along the pond bank.

The layout of the village is historically deliberate. Hongcun was designed in the shape of an ox, with a canal system functioning as a circulatory network: water flows from a spring through channels cut along the lanes, fills the Moon Pond, continues to the larger South Lake, and exits the village. This irrigation and drainage system, conceived in the Ming dynasty, still functions and keeps the village remarkably clean. The lanes are paved with cobblestones and too narrow for anything but foot traffic.

The great building here is Chengzhi Hall, a late Qing mansion built by a wealthy salt merchant. The hall has forty-eight rooms across an elaborate compound, and every surface that can be carved has been: beams, screen panels, window lattices, and column bases display scenes from nature, mythology, and Qing dynasty domestic life. The carving is exceptional by any standard. Several smaller mansions and ancestral halls are included in the entrance fee and warrant exploration.

Hongcun is 85 kilometers from Tunxi (Huang Shan city) and 11 kilometers from Yixian. Tourist buses run from Huangshan North station. Entrance costs around ¥104, and the ticket permits multiple days. The village fills up entirely on weekends and during national holidays; a weekday visit, or arriving before 8am, makes an enormous difference.
