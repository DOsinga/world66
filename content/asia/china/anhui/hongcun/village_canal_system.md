  The canal system is remarkable for being a living piece of Ming dynasty infrastructure still in daily use after five hundred years. The water enters the village from a mountain spring every morning, flows through the network of stone-lined channels, passes through the Moon Pond, and exits through South Lake — completing a circuit designed to provide fresh water to every household in sequence. Before 7am, the water is clean enough for washing vegetables; later in the day it's used for laundry.
---

Hongcun's canal network is as interesting as any of the individual buildings — a Ming dynasty hydraulic engineering project that still functions as designed, channeling water from a mountain spring through the village in a precisely calculated sequence. The channels run along every major lane, stone-lined and covered with flat stones at road crossings, with small sluice gates that regulate flow between sections.

Walking the village while paying attention to the water — where it enters, how it flows, what it passes — gives a completely different reading of the place than simply looking at the buildings. The system was designed not just for water supply and fire protection but according to feng shui principles that linked the flow of water with the flow of good fortune. Whether or not you believe the latter, the practical engineering still impresses after five hundred years of continuous operation.
