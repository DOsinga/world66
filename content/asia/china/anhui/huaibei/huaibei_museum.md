  The stone pictorial slabs from Han dynasty tombs in the museum's collection are among the most complete assemblages outside Beijing. The practice of carving narrative scenes into the stone chambers of tombs — scenes of feasting, hunting, mythological confrontations, and the journey to the afterlife — was concentrated in precisely this corner of northern China, where the limestone outcrops provided suitable material and local patrons had the wealth to commission elaborate burial programs.

The Huaibei City Museum holds a strong collection of local archaeological finds, with particular emphasis on the Han dynasty pictorial stone slabs that are the region's most significant contribution to Chinese art history. These carved limestone panels, recovered from tomb complexes in the surrounding countryside, depict scenes of aristocratic life, mythological combat, and ceremonial procession in a vigorous, narrative style quite different from the court painting that survives from the same era.

The galleries also cover the Grand Canal period, when Linhuan served as a major waypoint on the north-south waterway, and include bronze weapons, ceramics, and jade from Chu state tombs dating to the Warring States period. The museum building itself is reasonably modern and the collections are well-organized, though English labeling is limited. Allow ninety minutes.
