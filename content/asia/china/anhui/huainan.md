Huainan is an industrial city in north-central Anhui — coal, chemicals, electricity, and pharmaceuticals are its economic foundations, and it makes no great effort to disguise this. The landscape along the railway reflects the city's history: processing plants, cooling towers, and the flat agricultural plain that stretches across the Huaihe valley. Most travelers pass through without stopping, which is not unreasonable.

What gives Huainan a claim on attention is Shou County, a historic district on the city's western edge that was once one of the major capitals of ancient China. Shouchun served as the capital of the Chu state in the final decades before the Qin unification, and the [old city walls](/asia/china/anhui/huainan/shou_county_walls) — largely intact, some sections dating to the Song dynasty though on much older foundations — enclose a surprisingly well-preserved historic core. The [Shou County Museum](/asia/china/anhui/huainan/shou_county_museum) holds fine bronzes, lacquerware, and jade from Chu tombs found in the surrounding fields.

The city is also associated with the legendary origins of tofu, supposedly invented here by [Liu An](/asia/china/anhui/huainan/liu_an_tofu_origin), the Han dynasty prince of Huainan, around 150 BCE. The claim is contested but maintained with enthusiasm; Huainan hosts an annual tofu festival and the local variety, made from water from a specific spring, is considered particularly fine.

Huainan Beef Soup — bai tang niu rou tang, with a milky white broth served at breakfast — is the dish that residents are most proud of. Multiple train stations serve the city, with high-speed connections available via Huainan East and South stations.
