The Xuantian Grand Temple is the spiritual heart of Qiyun Mountain and the culmination of the main pilgrimage route up from the base. Built into a deep overhang in the red sandstone cliffs, the complex of shrines, incense halls, and offering platforms occupies a natural rock shelter that keeps it perpetually in shadow and creates an atmosphere of concentrated devotion entirely unlike open-air religious sites.

The architecture layers centuries of addition and repair without losing coherence. The front platforms are crowded with incense burners and votive offerings; behind them, deeper within the rock, the inner sanctum holds the principal image of Xuantian Shangdi in gilded bronze. The scale is intimate — this is not a temple built to impress through size, but one that works on the visitor through accumulated detail and the sheer strangeness of worshipping beneath an overhanging cliff face. Resident Taoist monks maintain the site, and their presence gives it an authenticity that many tourist-oriented religious sites have surrendered.
