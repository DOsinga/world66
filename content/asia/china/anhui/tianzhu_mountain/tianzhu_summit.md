story: >
  The character "Wan" in Anhui's informal abbreviated name derives from this peak — Tianzhu Mountain was considered so spiritually central to the region that the province took part of its identity from the summit. Emperor Wu of Han designated it the Southern Sacred Mountain in 106 BCE, a title it held until the Han court transferred the honor to Heng Mountain in Hunan, leaving Tianzhu with the consolation title of "Subordinate Sacred Mountain" — an administrative demotion that did nothing to diminish its hold on the local imagination.
---

The Tianzhu summit stands at 1,760 meters, and the approach to it from either the eastern or western routes makes the height feel earned. The final section of trail winds through granite formations that increase in scale and drama as altitude is gained — the upper mountain is a landscape of massive towers and natural arches that appear to have been arranged by a sculptor with a taste for the theatrical. On clear days the view extends across the Dabie Mountains to the northwest and down into the river valleys of the Yangtze plain.

The summit area itself is surprisingly spacious for a high granite peak. A small temple occupies one of the highest points, maintaining a religious presence that dates to the mountain's long history of imperial designation as a sacred site. The wind at the top can be severe, and temperatures run significantly cooler than the base. The eastern ascent, which passes the best of the granite formations, is the preferred route for the climb; the western descent provides variety without repeating the scenery.
