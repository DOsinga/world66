Beijing — "Northern Capital" — has been China's political centre for most of the last eight hundred years, and the architecture and atmosphere of the city still reflect that. The [Forbidden City](/asia/china/beijing/forbidden_city) at the heart of the old town, the [Temple of Heaven](/asia/china/beijing/temple_of_heaven) to the south, the [Summer Palace](/asia/china/beijing/summer_palace) to the northwest, and the [Great Wall at Mutianyu](/asia/china/beijing/great_wall_mutianyu) running along the mountains an hour north are the most famous set pieces of imperial China anywhere in the world, and together they fill the better part of a week.

But Beijing is also the capital of the modern Chinese state, and modern Beijing is everywhere: the ring roads and tower-blocks that have swallowed most of the old city, [Tiananmen Square](/asia/china/beijing/tiananmen_square) and the great government compounds along it, the [Bird's Nest](/asia/china/beijing/birds_nest) and [Water Cube](/asia/china/beijing/water_cube) from the 2008 Olympics, the [798 Art District](/asia/china/beijing/798_art_district). The old city is disappearing fast — what was a dense grid of grey-brick hutongs and courtyard houses thirty years ago is now mostly new buildings, with pockets of the old city preserved around [Gulou](/asia/china/beijing/gulou), [Nanluoguxiang](/asia/china/beijing/nanluoguxiang), and the lake district at [Houhai](/asia/china/beijing/houhai).

Beijing is a hard city to love on a short visit. It is enormous, sprawling, often polluted, and the language barrier is real. But it rewards time — a week or two spent walking the hutongs, eating [Peking duck](/asia/china/beijing/quanjude) and regional cuisines, and taking day trips to the [Great Wall at Jinshanling](/asia/china/beijing/jinshanling) or the [Ming Tombs](/asia/china/beijing/ming_tombs) makes it one of the most rewarding cities in Asia.
