  The white dagoba on Jade Island was built in 1651 to commemorate the first visit of the Fifth Dalai Lama to Beijing. Empress Dowager Cixi was so fond of Beihai that she had a private boat and a private kitchen built on the grounds. The park restaurant still serves a version of her imperial kitchen recipes, marketed as "Fang Shan" cuisine.

One of Beijing's oldest imperial gardens, dating from the 11th century when it was first developed as a pleasure ground for the Liao dynasty court. The park surrounds a large lake and centres on Jade Island, a small hill topped by a gleaming white Tibetan dagoba visible from much of the northern city. Pavilions, stone bridges, and covered walkways connect the island to the north shore, where the Nine Dragon Screen — a 27-metre glazed tile wall depicting nine dragons — is one of the finest examples of imperial decorative arts in Beijing.

The park is popular with Beijingers of all ages throughout the year: ice skating on the frozen lake in winter, lotus viewing in summer, and constant kite flying from the open areas near the main gate. Less crowded than Jingshan Park directly to the south, and a more pleasant place to spend time. Allow two hours.
