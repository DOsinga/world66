Fujian is a coastal province in southeastern China, facing Taiwan across the Taiwan Strait. It is a mountainous province — most of the interior is steep forested hills — and historically its population has been oriented toward the sea. Fujian was the origin of a large proportion of the Chinese diaspora in Southeast Asia, and the province has long had stronger economic ties with the outside world than much of inland China.

The provincial capital [Fuzhou](/asia/china/fujianprovince/fuzhou) is over 2,000 years old and worth exploring. [Xiamen](/asia/china/fujianprovince/xiamen), on the southern coast, is the most developed destination — a pleasant city with a good international airport and a car-free colonial island, [Gulangyu](/asia/china/fujianprovince/gulangyu), offshore. [Quanzhou](/asia/china/fujianprovince/quanzhou), once the busiest port in the medieval world, is a UNESCO World Heritage city with remarkable remains from its days as the eastern terminus of the Maritime Silk Road. The [Wuyi Mountains](/asia/china/fujianprovince/wuyishan) in the northwest are another UNESCO site, known for dramatic bamboo-raft gorges and the finest oolong tea in China. Deep in the western interior, the [Fujian Tulou](/asia/china/fujianprovince/tulou) — the great circular earthen fortress-houses of the Hakka people — are unlike anything else you will see.
