  The twin stone pagodas of Kaiyuan Temple are the most recognisable skyline in
  Quanzhou. They were built in the Tang dynasty in timber, then rebuilt in stone
  in the Song — the material change being a practical response to fires. The east
  pagoda, Zhenguo Pagoda, was completed in 1238 and stands 48 metres. The stone
  carvings on each of the five tiers show figures from Buddhist iconography with
  a sculptural quality that reflects the prosperity and craft skill of Song-dynasty
  Quanzhou at its peak. Each of the 48 reliefs on the west pagoda has a different
  face, no two the same.

Kaiyuan Temple is the largest Buddhist complex in Fujian and the spiritual anchor of Quanzhou's old town. Founded in 686 CE during the Tang dynasty, it covers 78,000 square metres and encompasses the main hall — supported by 86 stone pillars, some of them repurposed from earlier Hindu temples — a mulberry garden, and the famous twin pagodas that have stood above the city for nearly eight hundred years.

The main Mahavira Hall is one of the finest surviving examples of Song-dynasty Buddhist architecture in China, particularly the corbelling system under the eaves and the stone pillar bases that show traces of the original Hindu design they were adapted from. The temple is an active monastic site with hundreds of resident monks; the morning chanting services can be attended by visitors if you arrive early. Plan at least two hours for the full complex. It is UNESCO-inscribed as part of the Quanzhou maritime heritage sites.
