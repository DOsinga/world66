  The owner of Shangshudi — Li Chunye — was a Ming dynasty official who rose to
  the rank of Shangshu (equivalent to a cabinet minister), which gave both his

  a compound of 120 rooms and five interconnected courtyards that were completed
  between 1596 and 1623. The scale was permissible for a man of his rank; the
  elaborate wood carvings on every interior surface were a form of investment,
  since skilled craftsmen were cheaper in Fujian than almost anywhere in China.
  The mansion survived the subsequent four centuries largely intact — unusual for
  a private residence this size.

Shangshudi Mansion in Taining town is the largest preserved Ming dynasty residential complex in Fujian: 120 rooms across five linked courtyards, built by a retired imperial official over a period of nearly thirty years at the turn of the 17th century. The compound's scale gives a clear sense of how prosperous local gentry lived in the late Ming period — not in the formal grandeur of a palace but in an elaborate domestic comfort, with separate spaces for different functions, private gardens, and a level of carved and painted decoration throughout that would not look out of place in a major temple.

The mansion is open to visitors and employs guides (mostly in Chinese) who explain the social hierarchy of the compound — which rooms were for which family members, how the kitchen and storage areas related to the reception halls, how the defensive features functioned. The wood carvings of the interior screens and partition panels are particularly fine. The mansion is in the old town a short walk from the main bus terminal and makes a natural complement to the natural scenery of the geopark.
