Xiamen is the most immediately appealing city in Fujian — a compact island city with a subtropical climate, clean streets, and a genuine beachside personality that sets it apart from most of industrial coastal China. It was one of China's first Special Economic Zones, which brought prosperity and a cosmopolitan air, and its overseas Chinese connections have given it a more outward-looking character than many cities of similar size.

The undisputed highlight is **Gulangyu**, a small island a ten-minute ferry ride from the waterfront. Cars and motorcycles are banned entirely, which makes it feel like nowhere else in China: quiet residential lanes, elaborate colonial mansions built by foreign merchants and returned overseas Chinese, bougainvillea spilling over stone walls. The island became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2017. It's busy with tourists, but wander away from the main souvenir drag and the residential backstreets are genuinely lovely. The **Piano Museum** and **Organ Museum** reflect the island's unlikely reputation as a centre of classical music — brought here in the 19th century by missionaries.

Back on Xiamen Island, **Xiamen University** is one of the most beautiful campuses in China, its traditional rooflines blending with subtropical gardens along the waterfront. The **Nanputuo Temple** next door is a working Buddhist monastery backed against Wulao Mountain, and you can hike up the hill behind it for good views over the city. The **Huandao Lu** coastal road makes for an excellent cycling route around the island's southern and eastern shores.

Xiamen's food culture is notable. **Shacha noodles** — a peanutty satay broth with oysters or pork — are the city's signature dish, with a flavour that traces its origins to Southeast Asia via the emigrant diaspora. The **oyster omelette** (fried with sweet potato starch) is another local specialty worth tracking down in the night markets around Zhongshan Road.

Getting here is easy: Xiamen Gaoqi International Airport has direct connections to major Asian cities and some European routes. High-speed trains link Xiamen to Fuzhou in about an hour and to Shanghai in eight.
