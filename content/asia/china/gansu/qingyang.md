Qingyang is the easternmost city in Gansu, set on the Loess Plateau in a landscape of eroded yellow earth, [cave dwellings cut into cliff faces](/asia/china/gansu/qingyang/loess_plateau_landscapes), and deep ravines carved by seasonal streams. It is a region of considerable antiquity — Chinese legend places the birthplace of the mythological ancestor Xianzu in the Huanxian area of the prefecture — and the loess environment has preserved archaeological sites and traditional building forms with unusual clarity.

The [Northern Stone Cave Temple (Beidao Shiku)](/asia/china/gansu/qingyang/north_stone_window_grottoes) in the surrounding countryside contains Tang and Song dynasty carvings in a relatively unvisited setting. Qingyang is also the nearest city to the Mausoleum of the Yellow Emperor in neighbouring Shaanxi — the major ancestral shrine to the semi-mythological founder of Chinese civilisation — which draws enormous crowds during the Qingming Festival.

Within the city, the [Qingyang Municipal Museum](/asia/china/gansu/qingyang/qingyang_municipal_museum) covers the region's history from neolithic settlement through the Silk Road period. The streets of the old city retain some traditional courtyard architecture characteristic of the Loess Plateau, and [paper-cutting](/asia/china/gansu/qingyang/paper_cutting_market) — a local folk art tradition of particular refinement in this area — is practised and sold throughout the markets.

Qingyang connects by bus and train to [Lanzhou](/asia/china/gansu/lanzhou) in about five hours and to Xi'an in a similar time via [Pingliang](/asia/china/gansu/pingliang). It sits in a part of Gansu that most Silk Road itineraries bypass entirely, which gives it an unhurried quality that contrasts with the tourist infrastructure of the Hexi Corridor.
