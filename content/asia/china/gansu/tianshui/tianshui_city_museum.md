  The Yangshao and Majiayao painted pottery at Tianshui City Museum was made by farming
  communities that settled this valley six or seven thousand years ago — among the
  earliest sedentary populations in Northwest China. The swirling geometric and figural
  designs on the vessels, made without a potter's wheel, represent an artistic tradition
  of sustained sophistication that lasted over two thousand years. Some of the pieces
  here come from sites within the city limits, meaning the same ground on which Tianshui
  stands today was occupied by neolithic potters of the first rank.

Tianshui City Museum holds a well-regarded collection of painted pottery from the Yangshao and Majiayao neolithic cultures, along with bronzeware and other artefacts spanning the Zhou and Han periods. The neolithic material is the highlight — the range and quality of the painted vessels is considerable, and the museum's presentation contextualises the different cultural phases clearly enough to make sense of them even without specialist knowledge.

The bronzeware section includes chariot fittings and ceremonial vessels from the Western Zhou period that reflect Tianshui's importance as a major centre on the Wei River during the early historical period. Admission to the museum is free, which makes it one of the better free cultural institutions in Gansu. The building itself is modern and the displays are well-lit.
