Shantou is the coast-facing half of the Teochew heartland: busier, saltier, and more outward-looking than its elegant neighbour [Chaozhou](/asia/china/guangdong/chaozhou). It was one of China's original treaty ports, and that history still shows around [Small Park](/asia/china/guangdong/shantou/small_park), where arcaded shophouses and old banking facades lean into a web of lanes that feels far older than the city's modern high-rises.

Come for the food as much as the architecture. Teochew cooking is delicate by Guangdong standards, built around seafood, braised goose, beef hotpot, oyster omelettes, rice-noodle rolls, and tiny cups of strong kung fu tea. The best meals are often in plain-looking neighbourhood restaurants where the display of live fish and chilled shellfish matters more than the decor.

Shantou also has a handsome harbour setting. [Queshi Scenic Area](/asia/china/guangdong/shantou/queshi_scenic_area) rises across the bay, [Mayu Island](/asia/china/guangdong/shantou/mayu_island) sits in the estuary, and the bridge road east leads eventually to [Nan'ao Island](/asia/china/guangdong/shantou/nanao_island), one of Guangdong's better coastal escapes. Pair Shantou with Chaozhou rather than choosing between them: together they explain Teochew culture far better than either city alone.
