Zhanjiang's charcoal-grilled oysters are the definitive version of a dish found across Guangdong's coast. The oysters come from the shallow bays of the Leizhou Peninsula, where the combination of warm, nutrient-rich water and tidal flow produces large, plump shellfish with a distinctive salinity. They arrive at the table still in their shells, charred black from the grill, with condiments of soy sauce, garlic paste, and chilli oil applied at the last moment.

This is street food rather than restaurant food. The best oyster stalls are clustered near the fishing harbours and the night market areas, operating from evening into the early hours. The combination of the grilled oysters, cold beer, and the open-air setting constitutes an experience that tastes specifically like this city and is not replicated anywhere else in China. Zhanjiang chicken — the city's other food obsession, raised slowly on the peninsula and served whole — appears on the same stall menus and is worth eating alongside the oysters.
