Chengyang is a cluster of eight Dong minority villages in the mountains of northern Guangxi, connected by footpaths and famous for the Wind-and-Rain Bridge completed in 1912 — a covered wooden structure spanning the Linxi River, supported on stone piers, with pavilions along its length where people sit and talk out of the rain. It is one of the finest examples of Dong architecture anywhere, and the villages around it, with their drum towers rising above a landscape of terraced fields and forested ridges, make for a whole that is more satisfying than any individual element.

The eight villages are small and unhurried in a way that Longsheng and Yangshuo are not. Each has a drum tower at its centre — the traditional gathering point for community meetings, singing, and festivals — and the Dong are among the great choral traditions of China, developing polyphonic singing in isolation from outside influence. With some planning it is possible to hear performances, though these are most accessible during festivals. The autumn season, when the rice is ripe and the terraces turn from green to gold, is the best time to visit.

The hike between the villages and up to Ji Chang takes two to three hours return, passing through tea plantations and more rice terraces, with views back down to the river valley. The trail is well marked and the walking is straightforward. Most visitors come for a day trip but staying overnight in one of the village guesthouses, priced very reasonably, changes the experience significantly: the village empties of day visitors by mid-afternoon and the evenings are quiet.

Getting to Chengyang requires a high-speed train from Guilin to Sanjiang in 35 minutes, then a local bus for another 30 minutes. The entrance fee to the scenic area is reasonable and the infrastructure is unobtrusive.
