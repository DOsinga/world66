The Friendship Pass sits in a gap in the mountains on the Sino-Vietnamese border, 18 kilometres south of Chongzuo city. The fortified stone gateway — massive, ornate, and considerably older than its name suggests — has guarded this mountain pass since the Ming dynasty, and the current structure was rebuilt and enlarged several times as the strategic importance of the crossing grew. From the upper battlements of the gate you can look south into Vietnam, where the road descends toward Lang Son and eventually Hanoi.

The surrounding hillsides are honeycombed with fortifications, most of them from the Qing dynasty or earlier, and accessible on foot trails that take an hour or two to complete. The hill immediately behind the gate has the best views and the most intact defensive walls. It is a genuinely atmospheric place — not touristic in any heavy-handed way — and the contrast between the ancient gate and the busy modern border traffic on either side of it is striking. Buses from Chongzuo run to the pass regularly, or you can hire a driver for the morning.
