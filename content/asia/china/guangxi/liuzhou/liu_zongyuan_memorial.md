  Liu Zongyuan was exiled to Liuzhou in 815 AD as punishment for his involvement
  in a failed political reform movement, having already spent a decade exiled
  to Yongzhou (modern Yongzhou, Hunan). He died here in 819, four years into
  his Liuzhou posting. During these years of punishment he wrote some of the
  most celebrated prose essays and poetry in Chinese literature, developing a
  style that combined precise observation of landscape with philosophical
  meditation. He planted hundreds of trees in Liuzhou and reformed local
  practices he found unjust — both acts that outlasted his short tenure.

The memorial park on the site associated with Liu Zongyuan, the great Tang dynasty poet-official who was exiled to Liuzhou in 815, provides a quiet counterpoint to the city's industrial character. Liu Zongyuan is one of the Tang Eight Prose Masters — a group of writers credited with reforming classical Chinese prose — and his Liuzhou period, though experienced as a form of punishment, produced work that has been read continuously for 1,200 years.

The park includes a reconstructed memorial hall with historical exhibits, an artificial lake, and a grove of trees that references Liu's practice of planting trees throughout the city. It is a place for locals to walk and sit as much as a heritage site, and the atmosphere is correspondingly relaxed. For anyone with an interest in Tang dynasty literature, the park provides useful context; for those without that interest, it is a pleasant green space with good riverside views.
