---
title: When to Go
type: section
---

The subtropical climate at Libo means the area is green and accessible year-round, but the best conditions depend on what you want to see. Spring (March–May) brings fresh growth, fewer visitors, and morning mists that hang over the forest and river valleys before burning off by mid-morning. Summer is lush and warm, with higher water levels in the pools and rivers, but is also the wettest season — the boardwalks can be slippery and afternoon thunderstorms are common.

Autumn (September–November) is the most comfortable season: drier, cooler, and with the clearest water visibility in the karst pools. The turquoise colour is most vivid in clear conditions, and autumn tends to produce longer periods of cloud-free light. The local Buyi and Miao festivals also cluster in autumn and early spring — the Water-Splashing Festival and other celebrations at nearby villages are worth timing a visit around if you can.

Winter is quiet and cool, around 8–12°C in December–January. The forest stays green and the water clarity is excellent, but visitor numbers are low, which means some shuttle services run less frequently.

The National Day Golden Week holiday (first week of October) brings very large crowds to the scenic zones — worth avoiding if you have flexibility.
