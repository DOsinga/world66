Mount Fanjing, known in Chinese as Fanjingshan, is the highest point in Guizhou at 2,570 metres and has been a sacred Buddhist mountain since the Ming Dynasty. The UNESCO World Heritage designation, awarded in 2018, recognised both the exceptional biodiversity of its [cloud forests](/asia/china/guizhou/mount_fanjing/cloud_forest) — the mountain is one of the last habitats of the Guizhou snub-nosed monkey, a species found nowhere else — and the cultural significance of the [temples at its summit](/asia/china/guizhou/mount_fanjing/jinding_summit_temples). The combination of conservation biology and mountain Buddhism makes Fanjing unusual even among China's many holy mountains.

The summit geology is what distinguishes Fanjing from other high peaks in southern China. The mountain top splits into two separate rock pillars, rising almost vertically from the ridge, and each pillar has a temple at its crown: Jinding on the main peak and Laojin on the adjacent one. A narrow stone bridge between them, the Golden Knife Gorge, is where the two temples meet. From below, the silhouette of the two pinnacles with their red-walled temples against sky or cloud is the image that has defined Fanjing in Chinese Buddhist iconography for five centuries. Getting up to them involves either the cable car (which covers much of the elevation) and then a very long staircase — 8,888 steps, the number chosen for its auspicious resonance — or the full ascent past [Huguo Temple](/asia/china/guizhou/mount_fanjing/huguo_temple) from the base.

The forest below the summit is where the biodiversity lies. Red fir and birch dominate the higher slopes; below the treeline, subtropical mixed forest with over 2,000 vascular plant species. Mist covers the mountain for much of the year, and the cloud forest in morning light — mosses and ferns on every surface, visibility dropping to a few dozen metres — has the quality of somewhere that hasn't quite decided to exist in the ordinary world.

Fanjing is most directly reached from Tongren (see /asia/china/guizhou/tongren), 90 kilometres to the northeast. Daily tourist buses run from Tongren's tourist bus station to the Jiangkou base area. The entry fee covers transport from the gate to the cable car base. Allow a full day for the return trip.
