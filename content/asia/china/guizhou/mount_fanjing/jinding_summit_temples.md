  The two temples at the summit were first constructed in the Ming dynasty, but the original structures burned in the early 20th century and were rebuilt — then demolished during the Cultural Revolution, when the Red Guards who made the climb found them and destroyed what remained. The current temples were reconstructed in the 1990s from historical records and photographs. They stand on pinnacles that are genuinely difficult to access and required extraordinary labour to build even in their current simplified form. The Cultural Revolution-era destruction was not unique to Fanjing, but it is one of the few places where the effort required to destroy something was roughly equal to the effort required to build it.

The summit of Fanjingshan splits into two rock pillars rising almost vertically from the ridge, and each carries a temple at its crown. Jinding (Golden Summit) on the main peak houses a hall dedicated to Sakyamuni; the adjacent pillar, Laojin, carries the Maitreya Hall. The narrow stone passage between them — the Golden Knife Gorge — is crossed by a stone footbridge that connects the two peaks. From the approach path below, the silhouette of the two red-walled temples against sky or cloud is one of the definitive images of Chinese sacred architecture.

Reaching the temples involves either the cable car from the base — which covers most of the elevation and deposits you near the lower ridge — followed by 8,888 stone steps to the summit, or the full ascent on foot from the base that takes the better part of a day. The number 8,888 was chosen for its resonance in Chinese numerology (8 being auspicious), but the steps are real and the climb is genuinely tiring. Allow 2–3 hours from the cable car terminus to the summit and back.

The views from the summit are exceptional in clear weather and entirely absent in cloud, which covers the mountain for much of the year. The cloud also has its own quality: the temples disappearing into mist and re-emerging is the experience that recurs in historical paintings of the mountain. Come for both conditions.
