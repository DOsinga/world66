The Catholic church at Qingyan is among the most architecturally incongruous buildings in Guizhou — a stone Gothic church inserted into the grid of a Ming Dynasty garrison town, its bell tower rising above the wooden rooflines of the surrounding residences. It was built by French missionaries who had established the first Christian community in the area in the 1830s, during the period when Western missionaries moved into Guizhou's frontier regions.

The church is still an active parish. The interior is modest but maintained, with wooden pews, painted plaster walls, and a small altar. It's open to visitors during non-service hours, and the contrast between the Gothic stone facade and the carved wooden doorways of the neighbouring houses is best appreciated from the lane immediately opposite.
