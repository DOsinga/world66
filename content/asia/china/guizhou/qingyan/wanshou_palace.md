Wanshou Palace is a Qing Dynasty guild hall built by merchants from Jiangxi province who settled in Qingyan during the commercial expansion of the 18th and 19th centuries. Guild halls like this were built throughout China wherever merchant communities from the same region gathered: they served simultaneously as lodging houses for travelling merchants, community centres for the settled population, and ceremonial spaces for the patron deity.

The architecture is more elaborate than the town's temples — the wooden carved panels on the facade and the stonework at the entrance represent a higher level of investment than most of Qingyan's other historic buildings. The compound has been restored and is accessible as a heritage site; the courtyards and halls give a sense of the social organisation behind the physical building.
