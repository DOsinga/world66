  The Sister Festival's courtship logic works through a coding system embedded in sticky rice. A woman gives a lacquered box of glutinous rice to a man she is interested in; inside the rice, hidden objects convey her intentions. Chilli peppers mean rejection. A pair of chopsticks means she accepts him. Garlic means she wants him to work harder before she will consider him. The man must interpret correctly — and respond appropriately — or face public embarrassment. The practice is centuries old and still functions.

The Miao Sister Festival (苗族姊妹节, Miáozú Jiěmèi Jié) takes place on the 15th to 17th day of the third lunar month — usually late April or early May — primarily in the Shidong area of Taijiang County along the Qingshui River. It is one of the most important festivals in Miao social life, centred on the courtship traditions of young women, and it predates the Qing Dynasty by an unknown period.

The public dimension of the festival includes dragon boat racing on the Qingshui River, lusheng (bamboo pipe organ) music, collective dancing, and the assembly of several thousand people in full traditional dress — layered silver jewellery, elaborate headdresses, and indigo-dyed pleated skirts. The silver alone worn by a young Miao woman at a major festival can weigh several kilograms. The Shidong gathering is not a performance staged for visitors but a community event that visitors may attend. The difference is perceptible.
