Dongpo Academy is a memorial complex built in honour of Su Dongpo (Su Shi, 1037–1101), one of the greatest literary figures of the Song dynasty, who spent three years in Danzhou during a period of imperial exile at the end of the eleventh century. The Zaijiutang pavilion — the building where Su Dongpo lived and gave lectures during his exile — is the oldest surviving structure on the site, established in 1098. The broader academy complex was built in his memory in subsequent centuries.

Su Dongpo arrived in Danzhou in 1097 at the age of 60, stripped of office by political enemies and sent to what was then China's outermost frontier. He lived in conditions of considerable hardship — limited food, no adequate medical care, uncertain shelter — but continued to write, teach, and engage with the local Li and Miao communities. The poems and essays he produced during his Hainan exile are among his most personal work. The academy preserves a collection of historical manuscripts, calligraphy, and paintings relating to this period, and the series of memorial halls are arranged around the site with an unhurried contemplative quality appropriate to the subject. It is designated a Major National Historical and Cultural Site.

The academy sits about 40 kilometres from central Danzhou, near Zhonghe Town, in a setting of trees and gardens that gives some sense of how the place must have felt during Su Dongpo's time here. Allow two hours.
