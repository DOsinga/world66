Wenchang sits on Hainan's northeast coast and has a strong claim to being the island's cultural capital — the ancestral home of a significant part of the overseas Hainanese diaspora in Southeast Asia, and the seat of what passed for island learning for centuries. The Confucius Temple, built in the 11th century, is among the best-preserved historical buildings on Hainan, and the old qilou arcaded shophouses along Wennan Street give a flavour of 1920s provincial prosperity that the south coast resort towns have long since bulldozed away.

The other side of Wenchang's identity is thoroughly modern: the Wenchang Satellite Launch Center, China's newest and southernmost spaceport, was built here partly because the low latitude reduces the fuel needed to reach geostationary orbit. Launches are not generally open to the public, but the facility has shaped the town's self-image considerably. The coconut groves at Dongjiao, a long peninsula north of town with half a million palms and fishing villages selling grilled seafood between the trees, are a more accessible and considerably more relaxed draw.

Wenchang's teahouse culture is the real reason to linger. Unlike Haikou, the city has largely avoided mass tourism, and the local morning tea ritual — dim sum, gossip, and multiple pots of strong tea drawn out over two hours — still functions as actual social infrastructure rather than a heritage performance. Prices are low by any Chinese standard, and the beaches on the peninsula's outer coast, while not set up for tourists, are genuinely unspoilt.
