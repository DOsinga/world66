story: >
  The boat-shaped house — a low, curved structure with a thatched roof that sweeps almost to the ground on both sides — was the traditional dwelling of the Li people for centuries. The form was adapted to the highland climate: the thick thatch insulated against both the summer heat and the highland chill, and the low entrance discouraged wild animals. Most such houses were demolished during the 1960s and 1970s when concrete arrived, but Chubao has maintained a cluster as living architecture rather than museum pieces.
---

Chubao Village, a few kilometres from Wuzhishan city, retains examples of the traditional Li boat-shaped houses in a context that feels less contrived than most "ethnic minority tourism" destinations in China. Some of the structures are genuinely old; others have been maintained or rebuilt in the traditional style. Either way, the village is inhabited and functioning rather than being a heritage display, which makes a significant difference to the experience.

The surroundings are also genuinely attractive — the village sits in a valley with terraced fields and a small river, and the combination of traditional architecture, working agriculture, and mountain backdrop makes for a compelling afternoon. Local guides from the village can be arranged to explain the building methods and the social organisation that the spatial arrangement of the houses reflects.
