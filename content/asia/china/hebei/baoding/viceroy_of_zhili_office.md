The Office of the Viceroy of Zhili is the best-preserved Qing-era governor's complex in China — a substantial compound of ceremonial halls, working offices, and residential quarters that functioned as the administrative headquarters for the most powerful provincial post in the empire. The Viceroy of Zhili governed the region around Beijing and Tianjin, making this the second seat of power in the dynasty after the capital itself.

The complex is arranged along a series of axial courtyards in the standard hierarchical pattern of Chinese official architecture, with the most formal and imposing spaces at the ceremonial front and the more intimate residential and garden areas at the rear. The main audience hall retains its original woodwork and furnishings. The attached museum is well curated and explains both the administrative machinery of the Qing and the specific history of the famous viceroys — Li Hongzhang, Zeng Guofan, Yuan Shikai — who worked here during the dynasty's turbulent final century.
