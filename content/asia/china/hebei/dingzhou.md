Dingzhou holds a record that most Chinese cities would advertise more aggressively: the [Liaodi Pagoda](/asia/china/hebei/dingzhou/liaodi_pagoda), completed in 1055 during the Northern Song dynasty, is the tallest surviving pre-modern pagoda in China, rising 84 metres above the surrounding plain. It was built not for purely religious purposes but partly as a military watchtower — the flat terrain of the North China Plain meant that a tall structure here could monitor movement for a considerable distance in every direction. The combination of devotional and defensive function is reflected in the building itself, which has a more austere character than purely ceremonial pagodas of the same period.

The pagoda stands within the grounds of what was once the Kaiyuan Monastery, and its sheer height makes it visible from much of the city long before you reach it. Climbing the interior staircase gives a genuine sense of the engineering ambition involved: each of the eleven stories is fully accessible, and the views from the top over the flat farmland toward the Taihang foothills are expansive. The surrounding monastery has been rebuilt at various periods and lacks the accumulated authenticity of the pagoda itself, but the whole compound is well maintained.

The rest of Dingzhou's historical centre rewards a short walk. A [Confucius Temple](/asia/china/hebei/dingzhou/confucius_temple) in reasonable condition occupies a courtyard near the pagoda, and the city has some preserved street sections from the Ming and Qing periods that function more as ordinary residential neighbourhoods than as tourist reconstructions. Dingzhou's recent fame in China rests partly on the Ting Hsien Experiment, a rural development programme conducted here between 1926 and 1937 that became internationally influential as a model for community literacy and healthcare before the Japanese occupation ended it.

Dingzhou is on the Beijing–Guangzhou high-speed rail line with three stations, making it accessible from both Beijing to the north and Shijiazhuang to the south.
