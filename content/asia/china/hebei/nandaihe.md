Nandaihe sits on the Bohai coast just south of the better-known Beidaihe resort, and its main appeal is precisely that it is the quieter option. Where Beidaihe has been crowded since the 1890s and carries generations of resort infrastructure and summer-party energy, Nandaihe developed later and more gently. The beach is broad and sandy, the water is calm and shallow enough for children, and the pace is slower than its neighbour to the north.

The resort area is centred on a stretch of developed coastline with hotels, seafood restaurants, and the standard equipment of a Chinese summer beach town. What it lacks, compared to Beidaihe, is the political backstory — Beidaihe is where senior Communist Party officials have traditionally gone for their summer breaks, lending it a certain notoriety. Nandaihe has no equivalent mythology, which is either a disadvantage or a relief depending on what you are looking for.

The area is part of the Qinhuangdao municipality, which also administers [Shanhaiguan](/asia/china/hebei/shanhaiguan) and [Beidaihe](/asia/china/hebei/bei_dai_he), so the three can be combined into a single coastal visit. Shanhaiguan to the northeast is where the Great Wall meets the sea at the Dragon Head Rock, and the old walled city there has a completely different character from the beach resorts — more historical, less festive. The combination of wall, beach, and quiet resort town makes the Qinhuangdao coast one of the more varied stretches of Chinese coastline accessible from Beijing by train in under three hours.

The season runs roughly from June through September. Outside those months most facilities close and the coast has a deserted winter quality that some visitors find appealing in its own right.
