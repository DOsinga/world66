Zhengding is a small walled town just north of Shijiazhuang that spent over a thousand years as one of the most important Buddhist centres in northern China. From the Sui dynasty through the Qing, it was a place of genuine religious significance rather than just administrative convenience, and the physical evidence of that history is still standing in extraordinary concentration. Four distinct pagodas rise above the town's rooflines — Tang, Song, and later structures, each with a different architectural character — and several major temple complexes survive in varying states of preservation.

The most important is [Longxing Temple](/asia/china/hebei/zhengding/longxing_temple), sometimes called the Great Buddha Temple, a Sui dynasty foundation that houses one of the finest collections of Tang and Song Buddhist sculpture in northern China. The centrepiece is a 22-metre bronze statue of the Thousand-Armed Guanyin, cast during the Northern Song period, standing inside a pavilion that was built specifically around it. The scale is theatrical and the craftsmanship precise. Nearby, the [Linji Temple](/asia/china/hebei/zhengding/linji_temple) is the founding site of the Linji school of Chan Buddhism, one of the main traditions that spread to Japan as Rinzai Zen. The temple that stands today is much rebuilt, but the site carries real weight for practitioners of that tradition.

The four pagodas are all within walking distance of each other, which makes for an unusual architectural tour. The [Xumi Pagoda](/asia/china/hebei/zhengding/xumi_pagoda), 48 metres of Tang brickwork, is the tallest and most elegant. The [Lingxiao Pagoda](/asia/china/hebei/zhengding/lingxiao_pagoda) mixes timber and brick construction in a way that was fashionable in the Northern Song period and is rare anywhere. The [Hua Pagoda](/asia/china/hebei/zhengding/hua_pagoda) is the strangest: its lower section is encrusted with sculpted figures and decorative elements in a way that looks nothing like conventional Chinese pagoda design.

Zhengding is close enough to Shijiazhuang to visit as a half-day excursion, though the concentration of sites rewards a longer stay. Unlike many historic towns in China, it has not been aggressively commercialised.
