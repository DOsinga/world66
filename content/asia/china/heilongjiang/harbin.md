Harbin is the provincial capital of Heilongjiang, China's northernmost province, sitting on the Songhua River just a few hundred kilometres from the Russian border. That proximity shaped the city: Russian engineers and traders flooded in during the early 20th century to build the Chinese Eastern Railway, leaving behind a district of Orthodox churches, art nouveau facades, and broad European-style boulevards unlike anything else in China. The [Daoli district](/asia/china/heilongjiang/harbin/daoli_district) preserves the densest concentration of this architecture.

The city is famous worldwide for its [International Ice and Snow World festival](/asia/china/heilongjiang/harbin/ice_and_snow_world), held every January and February on the banks of the Songhua River. The scale is extraordinary — entire palaces and cathedrals carved from river ice, lit from inside at night with coloured LEDs, alongside snow sculptures the size of buildings. It draws millions of visitors, and for good reason. Winter temperatures routinely drop to -20°C or colder, so come prepared.

Outside of winter, Harbin is a quieter, liveable city with a strong café culture, good northeast Chinese food (think hearty dumplings, braised pork, and fresh river fish), and well-maintained parks along the [Songhua River embankment](/asia/china/heilongjiang/harbin/songhua_river). [Zhongyang Dajie](/asia/china/heilongjiang/harbin/zhongyang_dajie) (Central Street) is a pedestrian boulevard lined with European-era buildings and is the obvious place to start any visit. The [Saint Sophia Cathedral](/asia/china/heilongjiang/harbin/saint_sophia_cathedral) is the city's most iconic landmark, while the [Unit 731 Museum](/asia/china/heilongjiang/harbin/unit_731_museum) at the southern edge of town confronts one of the darkest chapters of the Second World War.
