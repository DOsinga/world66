Every January, a city rises from the ice on the banks of the Songhua River. Harbin Ice and Snow World is the largest ice-and-snow festival in the world, covering over 600,000 square metres with sculptures, slides, castles, and cathedral-scale structures carved from river ice. After dark, internal LED lighting turns the whole complex into something hallucinatory — entire buildings glowing blue, red, and green against -20°C temperatures.

The scale is the first surprise; the second is the quality of the carving. Alongside enormous architectural pieces, there are detailed ice sculptures in separate competitions, including student entries and international teams. The festival typically runs from early January through February. Come prepared for extreme cold — the experience is best at night when the lighting is at its most dramatic, but that is also when temperatures plummet. Rental parkas, hand warmers, and ice-grip shoe covers are available at the entrance.
