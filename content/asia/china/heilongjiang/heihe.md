Heihe sits on the Chinese bank of the [Amur River](/asia/china/heilongjiang/heihe/amur_river_crossing) — the Heilong Jiang itself — staring directly across at the Russian city of Blagoveshchensk. The two cities are close enough that on a clear day you can read the Cyrillic signs on the opposite shore, and the relationship between them defines everything about Heihe: the town exists, in large part, to sell things to Russians and to let Chinese tourists marvel at the proximity of Russia without needing a visa. In summer, ferries make the crossing in minutes; in winter, when the river freezes solid, buses drive across on the ice.

The border economy is visible everywhere. The main commercial streets are full of shops catering to Russian buyers — furs, electronics, clothing, traditional Chinese medicine — and the staff in hotels and trade centres near the waterfront often speak reasonable Russian. The [cross-border trade markets](/asia/china/heilongjiang/heihe/cross_border_markets) — the Great Island International Trade Centre and the Russian International Trade Centre — are essentially covered markets, and wandering them offers an odd window into how small-scale cross-border commerce actually works in this corner of the world. The free-trade zone designation gives the whole place a slightly transactional energy, but that is also part of what makes it interesting.

History has weight here too. The [Aihui History Museum](/asia/china/heilongjiang/heihe/aihui_history_museum), about 33 kilometres south of the city centre in the old town of Aigun, documents the complicated Sino-Russian territorial negotiations of the 19th century, when China ceded enormous tracts of land under the Treaty of Aigun in 1858. The museum is bilingual, thorough, and notably un-triumphalist by Chinese museum standards. The ancient earthworks of the old Aigun city are still partially visible nearby.

Heihe is most logically visited as part of a journey through northern Heilongjiang rather than as a destination in its own right. The train from Harbin takes around ten hours; flights are faster but infrequent. Summer is the obvious season — the river is open, the days are long, and the town is animated by trade — though there is something genuinely strange and memorable about seeing the ice road to Russia in January.
