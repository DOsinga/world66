Mohe's Ice and Snow Festival runs through January and February, built around the theatrical possibilities of -40°C cold rather than any architectural ambition. The centrepiece demonstrations — throwing a cup of boiling water into the air and watching it vaporise instantly into a cloud of ice crystals, or soaking a piece of clothing and watching it freeze into a sculpture within minutes — are genuinely spectacular rather than gimmicky. At these temperatures, water behaves in ways that are visually startling even when you know the physics.

The festival also includes more conventional cold-weather activities: ice fishing on the frozen river, snowmobile rides, dog sledding, and competitions for who can endure the most time outdoors in minimal clothing (a tradition taken seriously by local participants). The atmosphere is festive rather than solemn, and the combination of extreme cold and communal warmth — everyone retreating to heated guesthouses for noodle soup between activities — gives the festival an unusual character. It is one of the few winter events in China that feels genuinely rooted in local conditions rather than imported from elsewhere.
