Qiqihar is Heilongjiang's second city and, to most visitors, a staging point for something far more compelling: the [Zhalong Nature Reserve](/asia/china/heilongjiang/qiqihar/zhalong_nature_reserve), one of the finest wetland birding sites in all of China. The reserve lies about an hour from the city centre by bus and protects a vast expanse of reedy marshland along the Wuyuer River. Around 260 bird species have been recorded here, including six of the world's fifteen crane species — the red-crowned crane, the rarest and most emblematic, breeds here and can be seen at close range from spring through early autumn. Dawn is the best time, when the cranes move through the reeds in small groups and the flat light makes everything look slightly unreal.

The city itself is older than it looks. It was established as a walled military outpost in 1691 and grew into a proper town when the Chinese Eastern Railway came through in the early 1900s. That railway history is still legible in the urban fabric: the older districts have a low-rise, slightly faded Russian-era quality that Harbin has largely rebuilt away. The [Dacheng Temple](/asia/china/heilongjiang/qiqihar/dacheng_temple), a Buddhist complex finished in 1943, is worth an hour; the grounds are spacious and quiet in a way that urban Chinese temples rarely manage.

Qiqihar's local food culture tilts toward barbecue, and the night markets that operate through the warmer months are genuinely good. The city draws on a mixed Manchu, Han, and Daur heritage, and the cooking reflects that: you'll find game, river fish, and smoked meats alongside the standard northeastern dumplings and braised pork. The [Longsha Park](/asia/china/heilongjiang/qiqihar/longsha_park) on the city's edge fills up with locals in summer evenings and gives a useful sense of how a mid-sized northeastern Chinese city actually functions day to day.

Getting here from Harbin is easy — frequent trains take two to three hours — and the combination of city and reserve makes a two-night stop more rewarding than a day trip. The reserve entry fee is modest and the buses run reliably; the main variable is weather, which in Heilongjiang always is.
