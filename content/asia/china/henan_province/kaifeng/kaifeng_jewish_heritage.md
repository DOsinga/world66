Kaifeng's Jewish community is one of the most remarkable diaspora stories in the world. Jews are believed to have settled here during the Song dynasty, possibly as merchants or traders, and over the following centuries developed a distinct Sino-Jewish culture that preserved Hebrew prayers and Torah study while absorbing Chinese customs, architecture, and language. The community built a synagogue in the thirteenth century and maintained it for over five hundred years before the building was destroyed by a Yellow River flood in 1849.

The site of the former synagogue, on what is now a street in the old city, is marked by a small stone monument. A heritage museum nearby documents the community's history, and the Sacred Heart Cathedral — a Catholic church with its own complex history in the city — is visible nearby. Access to the Jewish heritage sites is sometimes restricted; local guides or the Kaifeng Jewish Study Center can arrange visits. The story of how this community maintained its identity in isolation for centuries is more surprising and moving than any museum display; reading about it before visiting makes the modest physical remains more resonant.
