Guanlin is the burial site of the head of Guan Yu, the Three Kingdoms general who became one of China's most venerated deities. The temple and mausoleum complex, built during the Ming Dynasty, sits about 7 kilometres south of the city centre on the road to Longmen Grottoes. The site consists of a series of grand halls, a tall mound covering the head, and hundreds of ancient stelae inscribed with calligraphy and historical records.

The temple is genuinely significant in Chinese religious culture: Guan Yu is worshipped as a god throughout China, Taiwan, Hong Kong, and among overseas Chinese communities. The incense and offerings at Guanlin are not tourist performance — they are sincere. The mausoleum courtyard has a forest of stelae that reward slow reading if you have any classical Chinese. Combined with a visit to Longmen Grottoes 6 kilometres further south, Guanlin makes a logical half-day excursion from the city.
