  Before the tunnel was built, the only way up to Guoliang village was a series of 720 hand-cut steps carved into the cliff face — the "Sky Ladder." In 1972, after the village's requests for a government road were rejected, thirteen villagers began carving their own highway through solid rock using hammers and hand-drills. They sold their livestock to buy tools, drilled fifteen months to complete the main bore, and emerged with a tunnel just wide enough for a single vehicle and windows cut directly through the cliff wall to the canyon below.
---

The Guoliang Tunnel is one of the most remarkable pieces of hand-built infrastructure in the world: 1.2 kilometres of road carved through solid rock by the 300-odd inhabitants of Guoliang village, who spent five years from 1972 drilling and chiselling their way through a Taihang cliff because no government road budget was forthcoming. The tunnel is barely wide enough for one car, with low clearance in places, and its most striking feature is the series of windows cut directly through the cliff face — "unaligned holes," as they are sometimes called — that open onto the canyon 300 metres below. Driving or walking through while sunlight slants in through these apertures is genuinely strange.

The village above the tunnel sits on a high terrace of the Taihang range with views down the canyon and across to the opposite cliffs. It is small — a few dozen stone-built houses, some converted into guesthouses — and gets a manageable amount of visitors compared to the mass-market scenic spots in the gorge below. The surrounding Wanxianshan Scenic Area (¥80 entry) includes the canyon, the village, and a network of paths along the cliff edges above the tunnel. The scenery here — sheer limestone walls, clear streams, and the abrupt transition from flat plain to mountain that characterises the Taihang's southern edge — is excellent.
