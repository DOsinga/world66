Yuntaishan — Cloud Terrace Mountain — rises to 1,308 metres on the southern edge of the Taihang range in Xiuwu County, near Jiaozuo in northern Henan. It is one of China's AAAAA-rated scenic areas, the highest classification given by the national tourism administration, and it earns that designation on the basis of its geological formations: a series of spectacular gorges, carved by water through layered Precambrian quartzite and limestone, producing the kind of deep slot canyons and sheer red-rock faces that make central Henan's mountain scenery so different from anything in the flat river plains to the south.

The park's signature feature is a waterfall in [Tianmen Gorge](/asia/china/henan_province/yuntaishan/tianmen_gorge) said to drop 314 metres in a single uninterrupted fall — claimed, with typical Chinese tourism authority confidence, to be the tallest continuous waterfall in the country. Whether or not the superlative survives scrutiny, the fall is undeniably dramatic, especially in spring and after summer rains when the volume is highest. The [Red Stone Canyon](/asia/china/henan_province/yuntaishan/red_stone_canyon), a gorge carved through vividly coloured rock, is the other main draw: a narrow walk through walls that shift from rust to ochre to deep burgundy depending on the light and the angle. [Zhuyu Mountain](/asia/china/henan_province/yuntaishan/zhuyu_mountain), within the same park system, offers a somewhat quieter alternative with good ridge walking and views north toward the Yellow River plain.

The park encompasses the [Wangwushan-Yuntaishan UNESCO Geopark](/asia/china/henan_province/yuntaishan/wangwushan_geopark), recognised by UNESCO for its exceptional geological record stretching back over a billion years. The layered rock formations visible in the gorge walls tell a story of ancient seabeds, continental shifts, and the patient erosion work of water — a story that most visitors absorb intuitively without needing to know the technical details. The canyon walks are well-marked and involve some steps cut into cliff faces, but are not technical; comfortable shoes and a reasonable level of fitness are sufficient for the main routes.

The nearest city is Jiaozuo, 40 kilometres south, which has good rail connections to Zhengzhou (about an hour by high-speed train). Most visitors do Yuntaishan as a day trip from Zhengzhou, though staying overnight in Jiaozuo gives you the option of arriving early before the day-trip crowds. The park charges ¥80 for entry and operates shuttle buses between the main scenic zones within the site.
