  Zhuyu Mountain has a connection to one of the most-quoted lines in Tang poetry: Wang Wei's ninth-century poem "On the Ninth Day of the Ninth Month, Thinking of My Shandong Brothers" — "Alone in a strange land, I feel doubly the pull of home / when the Double Ninth comes and the brothers all climb with cornel." The Chongyang festival (Double Ninth Day) tradition of climbing hills and wearing zhuyu (cornel) sprigs is believed to have been observed on this mountain, and Wang Wei may have had it specifically in mind.

Zhuyu Mountain, in the western section of the Yuntaishan park, offers a somewhat quieter alternative to the main gorge zones — better for ridge walking with long views than for canyon drama. The summit area gives good views north over the Taihang escarpment and south toward the Yellow River plain, a panorama that makes the geological contrast between the mountain ranges and the flat lowlands immediately tangible. The name comes from the zhuyu — Cornus officinalis, a tree with small red berries — that grows on the slopes.

The mountain is reached by shuttle bus from the main park entrance and has its own circuit of well-marked trails. The walk from the shuttle terminus to the summit takes about ninety minutes at a comfortable pace. In autumn, when the zhuyu trees are fruiting and the foliage is turning, the mountain is at its most attractive. The Chongyang festival in late autumn traditionally involves hill-climbing, and Zhuyu Mountain sees a spike of local visitors on that day.
