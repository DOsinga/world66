The Yuwang Temple commemorates Yu the Great, the semi-mythical engineer-king who is said to have organised the taming of the Yellow River's catastrophic floods around 2000 BCE, founding the Xia dynasty in the process. Yu's story is one of the most persistent in Chinese culture — the selfless leader who sacrificed personal comfort for public duty — and the Yuzhou temple is one of several sites in Henan that claims a connection to the man or his work.

The temple complex includes memorial halls, stele pavilions with inscribed praise from various dynasties, and the ancient tree-shaded courtyard typical of Henan's more modest heritage sites. It is not a major sight in the way the Jun Royal Kiln Museum is, but as a piece of the deep mythological map of the Yellow River region — a landscape where every town seems to have a claim on some founding figure of Chinese civilisation — it rewards a short visit.
