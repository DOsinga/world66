The Kowloon Walled City was, for most of the twentieth century, one of the most extraordinary urban environments on earth: a densely packed settlement of some 33,000 people occupying a tiny enclave that fell through the cracks of British-Chinese sovereignty disputes. For decades it operated outside Hong Kong law — no police, no building codes, no government — with its own internal economy of dentists, unlicensed doctors, drug dens, and factories.

After demolition in 1993, archaeologists discovered the original Qing-era military fort beneath the accretions of ninety years of anarchic construction. These remnants are now preserved in a formal Jiangnan-style garden — quiet and well-maintained, with pavilions, ornamental rocks, and planted courtyards. A small exhibition near the entrance documents the Walled City's history with photographs and a remarkable scale model. The contrast between the serene garden and what stood here before is the entire point of the visit.
