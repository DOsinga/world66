  The stone columns in the Qixingzhai section of the Grand Canyon rise up to 150 metres from the valley floor. Locals call them "sword peaks" — the rock has been carved by water erosion into shapes so narrow and regular that they do look, from the right angle, like blades standing in a rack. The best view is from the observation deck at the northern end of the park in early morning, before the valley fills with haze.

The Enshi Grand Canyon is 108 kilometres of gorges and karst formations running through the Wuling Mountains southwest of the city. This is the signature landscape of western Hubei: limestone towers rising from forested valley floors, sheer canyon walls dropping to clear rivers, and an air quality that, at this altitude, is noticeably better than the Yangtze plain. The Qixingzhai section at the northern end has the most dramatic peak forest scenery, with needle-peaked towers clustering like organ pipes from the valley floor — a landscape type more familiar from Guilin or Zhangjiajie but less visited here.

The Yunlong River section offers a combination of canyon walking and river drifting: a stretch where the gorge walls close in enough that you navigate the river by bamboo raft with walls rising overhead. Allow two days to do both sections justice; trying to rush through both in a single day means spending most of it on shuttle buses. The park entry is split between sections; budget ¥200 or more for the full experience. Accommodation is available inside the park for those who want to see the morning light in the canyon before the day visitors arrive.
