The Chuwang Chariot and Horse Formation Park, a national archaeological park a short taxi ride from the Jingzhou city centre, contains one of the most complete Warring States period elite burial complexes ever excavated in China. The site encompasses 138 burial tombs, 40 chariot pits, and over 200 sacrificial pits — the physical expression of what it meant to bury a Chu aristocrat with the equipment he would need in the afterlife.

The chariots — intact bronze-fitted wooden vehicles complete with horses — are displayed in situ in protective shelters, allowing visitors to see the burials as they were found rather than as reconstructed displays in a museum case. The scale is sobering: this is not a single spectacular tomb but an entire social ecosystem of death, with servants, horses, vehicles, and ritual objects arranged according to a precise Chu cosmological logic that archaeologists are still working to fully understand. The park is less visited than the Jingzhou Museum and rewards visitors who have already absorbed the broader context there.
