story: >
  The Chinese proverb "carelessness lost Jingzhou" (大意失荊州) refers directly to this city. Guan Yu, the general of Shu, held Jingzhou for Liu Bei's kingdom until he made the strategic error of leaving the city under-garrisoned while campaigning north — and Eastern Wu took it. The phrase entered everyday Chinese as a warning about overconfidence and complacency at the moment of apparent success.

The Guandi Temple — a major memorial site dedicated to the deified Three Kingdoms general Guan Yu — stands near the north gate of the old walled city and is one of four principal Guan Yu temples in China. The site's significance here is particular: Jingzhou is where Guan Yu served as military governor for the kingdom of Shu, and where his eventual defeat and execution gave rise to the enduring proverb about the consequences of complacency.

The temple complex is well-maintained and functions as an active place of worship as well as a historical site. The main hall contains a large statue of Guan Yu in his characteristic red-faced, long-bearded form, flanked by his lieutenants. The incense burns continuously and the atmosphere is genuine rather than touristic — local people come to pray, not just look. The surrounding buildings contain inscribed steles and painted narratives of Guan Yu's campaigns, making it a useful companion to the Three Kingdoms sites across this part of central Hubei. Admission is modest.
