The Changde Poem Wall runs for three kilometres along the Yuan River embankment, a flood levee faced entirely with carved calligraphy and relief artwork. The Guinness World Records certified it as the world's longest wall of engraved art, which is exactly the kind of claim that sounds like marketing until you actually walk its length. The scale is genuinely impressive: 1,267 poems engraved in stone, spanning five thousand years of Chinese literary history, with forty-three thematic pictorial panels between them depicting scenes from Hunanese history and mythology.

The best time to visit is the late afternoon and evening, when the slab-cut characters catch the slanting light and the carvings gain depth. At night, illuminations along the wall make a good backdrop to the riverside promenade. The adjoining parks are where locals come for the slow rituals of Chinese urban leisure: group dancing, kite-flying in the open spaces, old men playing chess at folding tables, tea sellers with thermos flasks. Walking the full wall takes about an hour at a relaxed pace; the section near the Yalisi Coffee building (a Qing dynasty-style pavilion now occupied by a cafe) is the most photographed.
