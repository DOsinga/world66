  Jia Yi was banished to Changsha in 174 BC for political reasons, at age 23,
  after his rapid rise at the Han court provoked the resentment of older
  officials. He spent three years here, deeply unhappy, and wrote a series of
  essays and elegies that became foundational texts of Chinese political
  thought. The "Owl Rhapsody," written after an owl (a death omen) flew into
  his room, meditates on fate and mortality with an urgency that still comes
  through in translation. He died at 33, still in political exile.

The Former Residence of Jia Yi preserves the site of the house where the Han dynasty scholar and statesman Jia Yi lived during his political exile in Changsha in the 170s BC. The Qing-dynasty building that stands on the site today is a reconstruction rather than Jia Yi's actual dwelling — his residence was approximately two thousand years earlier — but the courtyard compound has been sensitively furnished as a memorial to one of the Han dynasty's most influential and tragic intellectual figures.

Jia Yi arrived in Changsha as a young prodigy who had provoked powerful enemies at court. His essays from this period — on state reform, on the treatment of princes, on the nature of governance — were read by subsequent emperors and officials for centuries. The memorial hall contains a small museum tracing his life and influence, with reproductions of his major works. The Du Fu River Pavilion nearby commemorates the Tang poet Du Fu, who also spent years in Changsha and wrote movingly about the city. Entry is free. The residence is in the old city centre, a short walk from Wuyi Square.
