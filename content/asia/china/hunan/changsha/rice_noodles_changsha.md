Rice flour noodles — mifen — are the foundation of Changsha breakfast culture. The noodles come in several preparations: mala (spiced broth with beef), assorted (mixed toppings), or dry-mixed with oil and condiments. The best shops open at five or six in the morning and may run out of broth by mid-morning. A bowl costs ¥8–15.

The key indicator of a serious noodle shop is the queue of locals, which forms reliably from around seven to nine in the morning at the best establishments. These are not restaurants with tables and service — they are counter-service operations with a dozen stools and an outdoor extension of folding tables in the lane. Ordering is by pointing at the menu board and holding up fingers for the number of bowls. The entire transaction takes about two minutes. No English menus exist at the serious places, but the format is simple enough that language is not a real barrier.
