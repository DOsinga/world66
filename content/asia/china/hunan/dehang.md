Dehang is a Miao minority village set in a gorge about an hour north of [Fenghuang](/asia/china/hunan/fenghuang) in western [Hunan](/asia/china/hunan). Where Fenghuang has been polished into a heavily visited riverside showpiece, Dehang retains something closer to functional village life alongside its tourism facilities: people grow rice on the terraced slopes above the gorge, the silversmithing and embroidery sold in the market are produced locally rather than imported from factories, and the hiking trails lead into terrain that requires genuine effort to reach. It is a useful corrective to the more choreographed experience downstream.

The [gorge walk](/asia/china/hunan/dehang/dehang_gorge_walk) is the main event. A trail follows the river through increasingly narrow rock walls, past waterfalls dropping from the plateau above, to a series of pools and viewpoints at the upper end of the canyon. The full circuit takes three to five hours depending on pace and conditions; the waterfall at the head of the gorge, fed by springs on the plateau, runs strongest in late summer and autumn. The walking is not technically demanding but involves river crossings on stepping stones and some steep sections, and the path is rougher than the manicured walkways of [Wulingyuan](/asia/china/hunan/wulingyuan). The [rice terraces](/asia/china/hunan/dehang/rice_terraces) above the village are at their most beautiful in May and June when the paddies are flooded.

Miao cultural performance — silver headdresses, traditional music, ceremony — is offered at the village entrance for tourist groups, which is easy to dismiss as staged. It is staged, but the craftsmanship is real: Miao silverwork is among the most complex in China, with headdresses containing hundreds of separate components assembled by individual silversmiths. Buying directly from [workshop vendors](/asia/china/hunan/dehang/miao_village) in the village, rather than from the souvenir stalls at the gate, produces both better pieces and a more useful exchange.

Dehang is reached by bus from Jishou or from [Fenghuang](/asia/china/hunan/fenghuang), with the journey taking 45 minutes to an hour. Accommodation in the village is simple but adequate. Visiting Dehang and Fenghuang together on a two-night loop from Jishou (a high-speed rail stop) gives a more rounded picture of Xiangxi — the Tujia and Miao Autonomous Prefecture — than either town alone provides.
