Shen Congwen's former residence in the old town is a traditional Qing dynasty courtyard house where the writer was born in 1902 and spent his early childhood. The house contains original furniture, family photographs, manuscript reproductions, and a small museum documenting Shen's life and work. The rooms are modest by any standard — a working-family dwelling of the Miao and Han borderland — and the modesty suits the writer, whose fiction consistently found beauty and tragedy in ordinary lives.

Shen's novels and stories set in western Hunan — "The Border Town" being the most celebrated — brought the Miao and Tujia cultures of Xiangxi into the Chinese literary mainstream and remain the best literary introduction to this landscape. His grave is accessible on a hillside path fifteen to twenty minutes' walk from the old town, marked by a rough stone bearing an inscription he wrote for himself: "Not sad, not rejoicing, understanding, acknowledging the love that lies behind all things." Entry to the residence costs ¥10 (separate from the town ticket in some configurations).
