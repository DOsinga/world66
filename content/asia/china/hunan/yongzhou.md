Yongzhou occupies the southern end of [Hunan](/asia/china/hunan) where the Xiang River begins its northward run, a city with origins around 201 BC that most travellers skip on their way to [Fenghuang](/asia/china/hunan/fenghuang) or [Guangxi](/asia/china/guangxi). That is a pity, because Yongzhou has one of Chinese literature's most celebrated connections: Tang dynasty writer Liu Zongyuan was exiled here from 805 to 815 AD and spent ten years exploring the landscape while producing the "Eight Records of Yongzhou," a series of nature essays that became foundational texts of classical Chinese prose. The Liuzi Temple, dedicated to his memory in Lingling District, is an elegant complex with a ¥25 admission charge that attracts scholars and the occasional literary pilgrim.

More compelling to most visitors is the Nüshu connection. [Jiangyong County](/asia/china/hunan/jiangyong), administered under Yongzhou, is the only place on earth where Nüshu developed — a syllabic script invented by women, used exclusively by women, passed from mother to daughter and between sworn sisters, and kept deliberately opaque to men. The script was created to give voice to things that couldn't be said in the official language: hardship, longing, the interior life of rural women in a society that denied them formal literacy. It flourished during the Qing dynasty, went underground during the Japanese occupation, and declined through the 20th century; the last fully proficient speaker, Yang Huanyi, died in 2004. The [Nüshu Cultural Village](/asia/china/hunan/yongzhou/nushu_cultural_village) opened in Jiangyong in 2002, and cultural transmitters now work to preserve the tradition.

The food of Yongzhou prefecture is notably distinct even within Hunan's highly varied culinary landscape. Dongan Chicken — poached chicken finished with aged vinegar, dried chilli, and ginger, a dish that has been traced to a Tang dynasty origin story — is the local specialty and worth seeking out. Drinking Snails, a street food said to be 2,000 years old, are snails braised with herbs and eaten by suction; snake wine, infused with actual snakes, is sold as a tonic throughout the region.

Yongzhou is about an hour from [Changsha](/asia/china/hunan/changsha) by high-speed train. The city itself is pleasant and unrushed; the main urban sights are the [Liuzi Temple](/asia/china/hunan/yongzhou/liuzi_temple) and the free [Yongzhou Museum](/asia/china/hunan/yongzhou/yongzhou_museum), and Jiangyong makes a worthwhile day trip for travellers interested in the Nüshu story.
