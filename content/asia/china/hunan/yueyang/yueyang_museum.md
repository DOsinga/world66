  Among the museum's most significant exhibits are bronze artifacts recovered from the Dongting Lake basin, including pieces from the late Zhou period when the Chu state dominated this region. Chu bronzes are considered among the finest in ancient China; their presence in the Yueyang collection reflects the city's position at what was once the strategic centre of Chu territory, a fact that the lake's 20th-century drainage and industrial development has somewhat obscured.
---

The Yueyang Museum presents the city's long history from Neolithic settlement through the Republican period in a modern building near the city centre. Admission is free. The collection covers archaeological finds from the Dongting Lake region, including bronze-age artifacts, porcelain from the Song and Ming periods, and exhibits on the lake's ecology and historical extent — the lake itself once covered twice its current area, and the drainage history is presented without glossing over its environmental consequences.

A section on Qu Yuan and the Dragon Boat Festival provides the best available English-language context for the festival's origins, with the historical and legendary strands clearly separated. For visitors stopping in Yueyang on the Beijing-Guangzhou rail corridor, the museum provides 90 minutes of genuine substance rather than the souvenir experience that the Yueyang Tower area offers.
