---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

Zhangjiajie is worth visiting year-round, but the experience varies considerably by season. Autumn (September to November) gives the clearest skies and most manageable crowds — the best combination for photography and comfortable walking. Spring (March to May) brings frequent mist and fog; the fog is visually spectacular and the park's signature sea-of-clouds effect is most common in April, but visibility from high viewpoints is unreliable. 

Summer (June to August) is the park's busiest period, with the walkways genuinely congested during Golden Week and school holidays. The heat and humidity are significant at valley level, though the plateau areas are cooler. Winter is the least visited season; snow on the columns is one of the most photographed scenes in Chinese nature photography, and the crowds are minimal, but cable cars and some walkways close in ice or high wind. Checking conditions before a winter visit is essential.

Weekday visits are significantly better than weekend visits at any season — the trails and viewpoints are much less crowded.
