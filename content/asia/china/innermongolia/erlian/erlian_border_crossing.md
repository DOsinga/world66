  The bogie exchange at Erlian is a piece of railway engineering theatre that happens every time the Trans-Mongolian train crosses the border. Chinese track is standard gauge (1,435mm); Mongolian and Russian track is broad gauge (1,520mm). At the border station, the carriages are lifted by hydraulic jacks, the wheel assemblies rolled out and replaced, and the train lowered back down — the entire operation takes several hours and can be watched from the platform. The process dates from Stalin's strategic decision in the 1940s to keep Soviet railway gauge different from Western Europe's, a decision that still shapes travel logistics across Eurasia.
---

The Erlian border crossing is the land connection between China and Mongolia, and the Trans-Mongolian Railway passes through it on its way between Beijing and Ulaanbaatar. Even if you are not crossing, the infrastructure of the border zone is interesting to observe: the freight lorries lined up for customs inspection, the Chinese and Mongolian merchants doing business in both languages, and the general atmosphere of a place where two very different economies and cultures meet at a gap in the fence.

If you are crossing by rail, the bogie exchange at Erlian station takes several hours and the carriages are formally sealed during the process — passengers cannot leave the train, but there is usually enough window-watching to pass the time. The road crossing (for private vehicles and buses) is more straightforward. Walking across the border is not permitted; the crossing is 10 kilometres north of town and requires a vehicle. Zamiin-Uud on the Mongolian side has basic accommodation if you arrive too late for onward connections.
