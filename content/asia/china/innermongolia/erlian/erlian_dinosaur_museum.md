story: >
  The Erlian Basin has produced fossils of some of the largest dinosaurs ever documented, including Huaxiaosaurus aigahtens and several massive titanosaur sauropods. The 1990s excavations by joint Chinese and Canadian teams here changed palaeontologists' estimates of maximum dinosaur body size upwards significantly. What makes Erlian special is not just the size of the animals found but the completeness of some specimens — articulated skeletons rather than scattered bones — which allowed reconstruction of posture and gait that was not possible from earlier finds.
---

The Erlian Dinosaur Museum sits about seven kilometres from the town centre and contains well-presented exhibits on the Late Cretaceous fauna of the Erlian Basin, one of the most productive fossil sites in Asia. The indoor displays include reconstructed skeletons, cast specimens, and geological context that explains why this particular patch of desert is so extraordinarily rich in dinosaur remains — the Cretaceous lake system that once covered the basin preserved bones in enormous numbers. The English-language labelling is better than average for Inner Mongolia.

Outside, a large garden of life-size skeletal replicas gives scale to animals that are otherwise impossible to visualise. The titanosaur models are enormous by any standard. There is also a working excavation site nearby where, in summer, you can observe palaeontologists in progress — the dig is active and new specimens are uncovered most seasons. The museum has a modest entrance fee and a small shop selling fossil-themed gifts and surprisingly good reproductions of local finds.
