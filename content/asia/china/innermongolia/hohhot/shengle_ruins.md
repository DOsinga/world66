story: >
  Shengle was the first capital of the Northern Wei dynasty (386–534 CE), established by the Tuoba clan of the Xianbei people — the same ethnic group that had brought down the Western Jin dynasty and displaced the Han from northern China. The Tuoba's decision to build a permanent capital at Shengle rather than continuing as nomads was a pivotal moment in Chinese history: it initiated a two-century process of Sinicisation that eventually produced the sui and Tang dynasties. Modern China is in some ways the direct heir of this steppe people's decision to go Chinese.
---

The ruins of Shengle, about 50 kilometres west of Hohhot, are all that remains of the first capital of the Northern Wei dynasty — one of the most significant states of the Northern and Southern Dynasties period (4th–6th century CE). The site consists of earthen walls and building platforms spread across the open farmland, now partially incorporated into a small archaeological park. It is not spectacular in the way that the Forbidden City or the Great Wall are spectacular, but for travellers interested in the period when the distinction between "Chinese" and "nomadic" civilisation was being actively negotiated, Shengle is a compelling site.

The on-site museum has finds from the excavations: coins, pottery, bronze fittings, and architectural remains that document the Tuoba court's rapid adoption of Chinese administrative forms while maintaining its own military and cultural identity. The site is accessible by taxi from Hohhot; allow a half-day for the round trip.
