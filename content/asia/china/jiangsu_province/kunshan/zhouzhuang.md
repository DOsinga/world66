  Zhouzhuang's fame is partly the work of one painting. Chen Yifei's 1984 oil painting "Memory of Hometown," depicting the town's Double Bridge reflected in canal water, was acquired by Armand Hammer and presented to Deng Xiaoping during a state visit. The painting's international exhibition made Zhouzhuang internationally known and set off a wave of development that ultimately transformed the town into the tourist destination it is today.

Zhouzhuang is China's most famous water town — a designation that is both a recommendation and a caution. The Ming and Qing dynasty canal architecture is genuine and impressive: white-washed walls rising from dark water, stone bridges connecting the narrow islands, and the famous Double Bridge (Shide Bridge and Yongan Bridge together) that has appeared in countless images of Jiangnan canal culture. The Shen and Zhang family mansions from the Ming period are among the best-preserved examples of wealthy merchant domestic architecture in China.

The problem is the visitors: at peak times, particularly on weekends and national holidays, Zhouzhuang is genuinely overcrowded, with tourist boats filling the canals and souvenir shops occupying every available frontage. The experience at those moments is more theme park than historical village. Arriving early on a weekday morning in low season is transformative — the light is better, the crowds are thin, and the town's genuine character surfaces from under the tourism overlay. Admission is charged at the entrance; boat rides through the canals are available separately.
