The John Rabe House is the preserved home of John Rabe, the German businessman who led the International Safety Zone during the Nanjing Massacre of 1937-1938. Rabe created the zone in defiance of the Japanese military occupation, sheltering hundreds of thousands of Chinese civilians in his compound and in a larger protected district that he administered with other foreigners.

The house has been restored and turned into a small memorial museum. It sits in the university district of Gulou, and a short exhibition covers Rabe's life, his diaries, and the International Safety Zone. The site is compact — an hour is enough — but it provides essential context for the larger Massacre Memorial Hall, and the intimate scale gives Rabe's personal story more immediacy than the monumental museum can.
