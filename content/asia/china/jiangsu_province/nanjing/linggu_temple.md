story: The Beamless Hall was built in 1381 entirely from brick — no wooden beams — using a barrel-vault construction technique unusual in Chinese architecture of the period. Zhu Yuanzhang's motivation may have been practical (brick doesn't burn as easily as wood) or symbolic (the absence of beams supposedly signifies the absence of corruption). The hall has survived intact for over 640 years.

Linggu Temple (灵谷寺) sits in the eastern section of Purple Mountain, a short distance from the Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum. The main draw is the Beamless Hall (无梁殿), a Ming-dynasty brick structure of exceptional engineering — its vaulted interior spans 22 metres wide and 16 metres high with no internal supports. After 1929 it was repurposed as a memorial to soldiers who died in the Northern Expedition.

The broader temple complex includes a nine-storey pagoda (Linggu Pagoda) that can be climbed for views over the forested hillside, as well as several reconstruction halls and a pleasant garden. The forest setting is particularly beautiful in April when the wisteria is in bloom and in November when the maple leaves turn. The Pine Wind Pavilion, a ten-minute walk further up the hill, has a fine view back toward the city.
