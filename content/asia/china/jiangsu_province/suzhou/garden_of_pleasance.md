The Garden of Pleasance is among the lesser-known of Suzhou's classical gardens, which means it is often quieter than the big four. This in itself is a good reason to visit: you can walk the covered corridors and linger at the pavilions without navigating tour groups. The garden dates from the late Qing dynasty and is particularly celebrated for its leakage windows — decoratively shaped openings in the garden walls through which glimpses of other areas are framed like paintings.

The design makes heavy use of grey tile lattice in patterns of different geometries — hexagons, octagons, flowers, and abstract shapes — which were intended to suggest the variety of experience in a single stroll. The central pool is small but well-proportioned, and the surrounding corridors are pleasantly shaded even in midsummer. Admission is inexpensive and the crowds rarely overwhelming.
