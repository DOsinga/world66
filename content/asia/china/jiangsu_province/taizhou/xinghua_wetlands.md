  The Xinghua wetlands were shaped by centuries of a distinctive farming technique unique to this area: farmers built raised platforms called daoban out of the lake mud and planted crops on top, leaving narrow water channels between them. Seen from the air in April when the rapeseed blooms, the result is an abstract pattern of yellow rectangles on dark water that has become one of China's most reproduced aerial photographs.
---

The canal town of Xinghua, about 30 kilometres north of Taizhou city, sits in the middle of a vast freshwater wetland formed by the rivers draining into the East Sea coastal plain. In early to mid-April, the surrounding farmland transforms into an extraordinary spectacle: the raised-bed fields are planted with rapeseed, and as they flower simultaneously, the landscape becomes a mosaic of vivid yellow platforms floating above the dark water channels.

Boat tours depart from Xinghua town and navigate the channels between the blooming fields — the experience from water level is different from the aerial photographs but equally striking. The peak lasts two to three weeks; outside that window the wetlands are still worth visiting for their birds and quiet rural character, but the flower crowds (which can be large) are absent. The town itself has a small historic street with canal architecture worth an hour's exploration.
