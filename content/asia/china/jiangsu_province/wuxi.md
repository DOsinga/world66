Wuxi is a canal city on the southern shore of [Lake Tai](/asia/china/jiangsu_province/wuxi/lake_tai) (Taihu), sitting between Shanghai and Nanjing on one of China's busiest rail corridors. The lake — one of the largest freshwater lakes in the country — defines the city's character: it is a place of water, willow-shaded bridges, and classical gardens, with a pace noticeably slower than the two great cities that bracket it on either side.

The main draw for visitors is the lake itself and the Taihu shoreline parks, particularly [Turtle Head Isle](/asia/china/jiangsu_province/wuxi/turtle_head_isle) (Yuantouzhu), a peninsula of gardens and pavilions that juts into the water and offers good views of the islands offshore. The city also has well-preserved sections of traditional canal-side architecture in the [Qingming Bridge area](/asia/china/jiangsu_province/wuxi/qingming_bridge_historic_district), which rewards a slow afternoon on foot. [Li Garden](/asia/china/jiangsu_province/wuxi/li_garden) (Liyuan) is the most photographed of the classical gardens, a compact composition of rockeries, pavilions, and water arranged in the Jiangnan style.

Wuxi is easy to visit as a day trip from Shanghai (about 45 minutes by high-speed train) or en route to [Suzhou](/asia/china/jiangsu_province/suzhou), which is just 20 minutes further down the line. If you stay overnight, you get the canal district largely to yourself in the evenings when the day-trippers have gone. The [Lingshan Grand Buddha](/asia/china/jiangsu_province/wuxi/lingshan_grand_buddha) to the west of the city and the [Sanshan Islands](/asia/china/jiangsu_province/wuxi/sanshan_islands) in the middle of the lake are both worth the extra half-day.
