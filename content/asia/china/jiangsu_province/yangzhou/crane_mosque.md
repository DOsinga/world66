The Crane Mosque, located in Yangzhou's historic Muslim Quarter, is one of the oldest mosques in China, its founding traditionally dated to the early Tang Dynasty. The mosque's architecture is a study in cultural synthesis: the buildings follow traditional Chinese temple architecture, with tiled roofs, red columns, and enclosed courtyards, yet the spatial organisation is dictated entirely by Islamic practice — the qibla wall oriented southwest toward Mecca, the wudu (ablution) pool in the forecourt.

The surrounding Muslim Quarter retains a distinct character from the rest of Yangzhou's historic district — the food stalls outside the mosque serve halal lamb skewers and sesame flatbreads, a reminder that Arab and Persian merchants lived and traded here for centuries. The mosque is open to non-Muslim visitors outside prayer times, and the combination of Chinese and Islamic architectural elements is unique enough to reward a detailed look.
