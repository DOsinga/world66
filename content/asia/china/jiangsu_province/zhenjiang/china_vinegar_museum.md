  Zhenjiang black vinegar is made by a three-stage process — brewing, ageing in clay pots, and final maturation — that takes a minimum of three years and ideally five or more. The longer the ageing, the more complex the final flavour becomes. A bottle of ten-year Zhenjiang vinegar has a depth that makes ordinary wine vinegar taste simple; a twenty-year bottle is genuinely astonishing. The museum's best exhibit is a row of pots from different production years, each available for tasting.
---

The China Vinegar Culture Museum is housed in part of the Hengshun Vinegar factory complex — a functioning factory that has been producing Zhenjiang black vinegar since 1840. The exhibition covers the history and technology of Chinese vinegar production, with particular focus on the four great regional vinegars of China (Shanxi, Fujian, Sichuan, and Zhenjiang) and what distinguishes each.

The tasting section is the main draw: samples from different ages and producers allow comparison of how the flavour develops, and the difference between a standard three-year vinegar and a mature five-year product is immediately detectable. The factory shop sells the full range, including limited editions aged over a decade that are worth buying in quantity if you enjoy cooking. The museum is not far from the central city and can be combined with a walk to Xijindu street.
