  Sanqinggong's founding is traditionally dated to the Eastern Jin dynasty (317–420 AD), making it one of the oldest Taoist sites in Jiangxi. The temple is dedicated to the Three Pure Ones (三清), the highest deities in the Taoist pantheon, and the mountain's three peaks — Yujing, Yushui, and Yuhua — represent each of the three deities in physical form. The temple was rebuilt multiple times after damage, most recently in the 1990s following Cultural Revolution destruction.

The Sanqinggong (三清宫) temple complex sits among the granite formations of the mountain's upper slopes, its stone halls and carved arches embedded so naturally into the rock that the boundary between natural landscape and constructed site is deliberately blurred. This integration of architecture with geology is characteristic of the finest Taoist sacred mountain sites in China, and Sanqingshan does it well.

The main complex includes several interconnected halls dedicated to the Three Pure Ones, a natural spring enclosed within a stone pavilion, and carving inscriptions from various periods of the temple's history. The complex is active — monks conduct ceremonies and pilgrims make the climb specifically to worship — and the combination of functioning religious life and spectacular mountain setting is genuinely moving. The approach path winds through formations that feel designed to prepare the visitor for the temple ahead.
