Poyang Lake is China's largest freshwater lake and one of the most important wetland ecosystems in East Asia. Fed by five major river systems and draining northward into the Yangtze, it occupies a basin in northern Jiangxi that expands dramatically during the summer flood season and contracts to a fraction of its peak size in winter droughts — a fluctuation that creates a mosaic of open water, exposed mudflats, and reed beds that shifts with the seasons. In normal wet years the lake stretches over 3,200 square kilometers; in severe drought years it has fallen below 200.

The winter months, counterintuitively, are the best time to visit. The falling water levels concentrate migratory birds onto the remaining wetlands in extraordinary numbers, and Poyang Lake has become one of the premier birdwatching destinations in Asia. The [Siberian crane wintering grounds](/asia/china/jiangxi/poyang_lake/siberian_crane_reserve) hold up to ninety percent of the world's critically endangered Siberian cranes, alongside hundreds of thousands of [other migratory birds](/asia/china/jiangxi/poyang_lake/migratory_birds). The lake holds Ramsar Wetland designation, and [lake boat tours](/asia/china/jiangxi/poyang_lake/lake_boat_tours) from [Wucheng Town](/asia/china/jiangxi/poyang_lake/wucheng_town) offer the most dramatic viewing at dawn. The scale of the winter migrations is genuinely remarkable.

The lake sits near [Jiujiang](/asia/china/jiangxi/jiujiang) in the north and is accessible from [Nanchang](/asia/china/jiangxi/nanchang) by bus. The [1363 Battle of Poyang Lake](/asia/china/jiangxi/poyang_lake/battle_of_poyang_lake_site), fought between rival rebel armies contesting the collapsing Mongol Yuan dynasty, is considered by some historians the largest naval engagement in history — an unlikely distinction for an inland Chinese lake, but the scale of the fleets involved bears it out. Water quality and lake levels have come under pressure from upstream dam operations and agricultural runoff, making a visit now — before further degradation — more urgent than it might otherwise seem.
