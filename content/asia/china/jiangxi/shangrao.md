Shangrao is the prefecture-level city in northeastern Jiangxi that contains, within its administrative reach, two of the most dramatic mountain landscapes in the province: [Sanqingshan](/asia/china/jiangxi/mount_sanqingshan) and the rural villages of [Wuyuan](/asia/china/jiangxi/wuyuan) county. As a city it is compact and fairly ordinary, but its position on high-speed rail lines connecting Shanghai to Kunming has made it an increasingly viable base for exploring a region that rewards slow travel. Borders with Zhejiang, Fujian, and Anhui provinces converge nearby, and the cultural blending is evident in the architecture and cuisine.

[Guifeng Scenic Area](/asia/china/jiangxi/shangrao/guifeng_scenic_area), accessible by bus from the city center, showcases the Danxia landforms typical of this part of southeastern China — clusters of reddish sandstone pillars that include a naturally formed giant reclining Buddha visible from the main trail viewpoint. The area is less visited than the province's headline mountains and accordingly more peaceful. [Xinzhou old town](/asia/china/jiangxi/shangrao/xinzhou_old_town), within the city's urban core, preserves a section of old town walls and some Ming-dynasty streetscapes that would be significant heritage sites in less well-endowed parts of the country.

The city's most useful role for most visitors is as an overnight stop that breaks up the journey between [Wuyuan's villages](/asia/china/jiangxi/wuyuan) and [Sanqingshan](/asia/china/jiangxi/mount_sanqingshan). There is decent accommodation at various price points and no shortage of Jiangxi-style restaurants. Spring is the prime season for the region: Wuyuan's canola fields peak in March and April, and the mountains above are clear and cool before summer humidity sets in.
