  The carved wooden panels inside Likeng's old merchant houses are among the finest surviving examples of Huizhou woodcarving in China. Local craftsmen developed a tradition of three-dimensional carved wooden screens, window lattices, and beam decorations that became the defining luxury of these wealthy merchant homes — a display of learning and artistry in places that the merchants themselves visited rarely, having spent most of their lives trading in distant cities.

Likeng (李坑) is one of the most accessible and photographed villages in Wuyuan, set along a canal that reflects the whitewashed walls and dark tile roofs in the early morning light. The village was founded by the Li family during the Song dynasty and prospered through the Ming and Qing periods when the Huizhou merchant network controlled much of China's inland trade. The surviving architecture — two-story wood-and-plaster buildings with characteristic horse-head gable walls, carved wooden interiors, and central courtyards — is genuine Song-to-Qing dynasty construction.

The village is busy enough to have souvenir shops and tea houses crowding the main canal alleys, but the buildings themselves are authentic and worth examining carefully. Arrive before 9am to beat the tour groups. The upper part of the village, away from the entrance ticket area, is quieter and gives a better sense of how people still live in these old buildings.
