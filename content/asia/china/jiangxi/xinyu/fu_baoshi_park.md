  Fu Baoshi (傅抱石, 1904–1965) was born in Xinyu into a family so poor his parents repaired umbrellas for a living. He taught himself painting by copying works in the Nanchang Confucian temple and won a scholarship to study in Japan on the strength of work he had never formally been taught. He became one of the twentieth century's most significant Chinese ink painters, known particularly for his distinctive "Fu's wrinkle" brushstroke technique that evokes the texture of mountain rock with an economy of line that no previous painter had achieved.

Fu Baoshi Park (傅抱石公园) was built around the birthplace of the painter Fu Baoshi, one of the most celebrated Chinese ink painters of the twentieth century and the city's most famous native son. The park combines traditional pavilion gardens, stone paths, lotus ponds, and seasonal plantings with the Fu Baoshi Memorial Museum at its center — a purpose-built space holding a substantial collection of original works, along with documentation of his artistic development and his collaboration with the Nanjing school of modern Chinese painting.

The museum presents Fu Baoshi's work with genuine curatorial intelligence, tracing the development of his technique from early academic paintings to the bold, expressive late works that established his reputation. Even visitors who arrive knowing nothing of Chinese ink painting tend to find the best works arresting. The park itself is pleasant enough to justify a half-day without entering the museum.
