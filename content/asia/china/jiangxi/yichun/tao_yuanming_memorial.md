Tao Yuanming — also known as Tao Qian — was among the most celebrated poets of classical China, and Yichun claims him as a native son. The memorial hall dedicated to him in the city collects manuscripts, calligraphy based on his poems, portraits from different dynasties, and explanatory material placing him in the literary tradition of the Jin dynasty (roughly 4th–5th century CE). His most famous work, "The Peach Blossom Spring," describes a hidden community living in pastoral simplicity, cut off from the political chaos of his era — an image that resonated through every subsequent dynasty and still carries meaning in contemporary China.

The memorial is modest in scale but well-curated for a city of Yichun's size, and the surrounding park area includes plantings meant to evoke the pastoral themes of his poetry. For anyone travelling with an interest in classical Chinese literature, stopping here connects the city's landscape — forested mountains, rivers, the unhurried pace — to the literary tradition it actually produced.
