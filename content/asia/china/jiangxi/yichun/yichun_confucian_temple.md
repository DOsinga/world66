The Confucian Temple in Yichun — known locally as the Wen Miao — is one of the more complete temple complexes in this part of Jiangxi, occupying a site that has served as the centre of traditional learning in the prefecture since the Song dynasty. The main hall follows the standard hall-of-great-achievement plan, with heavy tiled roofs and a central courtyard planted with ancient trees that were already old when the current buildings were last substantially rebuilt.

The interior houses the traditional arrangement of ritual tablets and ancestral portraits associated with Confucian ceremony, and the temple remains an active cultural site rather than a pure museum — local ceremonies and study events still take place here. The quiet courtyard and the sense of accumulated scholarly purpose give the place more presence than it might have from descriptions alone. It is a short walk from the city centre and makes a good starting point for a morning in Yichun.
