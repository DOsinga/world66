  Jiangjiadian sits at the point where northeast China's agricultural plain gives way to the Mongolian steppe — a transition that happens gradually across the horizon rather than at a marked line. Standing here, you can see why nomadic and settled peoples fought over this corridor for centuries: it is the last good grazing land before the cultivated fields begin.
---

The Jiangjiadian Grassland on Baicheng's western outskirts is the most accessible version of the steppe landscape that begins just beyond the city. The grassland has been developed with modest tourist facilities — yurt accommodation, a small racecourse, fishing spots on the adjacent wetlands, and horse-riding arrangements — aimed primarily at Chinese domestic visitors who want a taste of Inner Mongolian life without the longer journey.

It is not wilderness in the Mongolian sense, but the openness is genuine and the facilities are low-key enough not to overwhelm the landscape. In summer, the grassland is green and the horizon carries the distinctive horizontal quality of steppe country. The racecourse hosts occasional events that attract local participation. For visitors based in Baicheng who want to spend a half-day outside the city without driving to the nature reserves, the grassland makes a practical option.
