Changchun is Jilin's capital, and its history is stranger and more layered than almost any city in northeast China. From 1932 to 1945 it was Hsinking, the capital of Manchukuo — the Japanese puppet state — and the seat of Puyi, China's last emperor, who nominally ruled under Japanese supervision while having no real power at all. The [Museum of the Imperial Palace of Manchukuo](/asia/china/jilin/changchun/manchukuo_imperial_palace) sits where Puyi lived and worked, and it is one of the most sobering colonial-era sites in China: the buildings are grand in a peculiar hybrid Japanese-Chinese neoclassical style, and the exhibits document both the theatre of imperial pretension and the brutal reality underneath it. The [Eight Former Manchukuo Government Buildings](/asia/china/jilin/changchun/eight_grand_ministries), repurposed as university campuses after 1949, are scattered around the city and easy to stumble across.

Changchun recovered after 1949 to become China's automotive industry capital — the first [Red Flag limousines](/asia/china/jilin/changchun/red_flag_automobile_museum) were built here by the [First Automobile Works](/asia/china/jilin/changchun/first_automobile_works), and the city still carries its manufacturing identity with some pride. The [Changchun Film Studio](/asia/china/jilin/changchun/changchun_film_studio) produced the first films of the People's Republic and remains active. The city centre is organised and reasonably navigable, with a metro system that handles the main arteries, and the scale feels manageable compared to the great crushing metropolises further south.

[Jingyuetan National Forest Park](/asia/china/jilin/changchun/jingyuetan_national_forest_park) on the city's southeastern edge is genuinely large — claimed to be the largest artificial forest park in Asia — and it becomes a ski destination in winter when the cold here is serious, regularly touching minus fifteen or colder. The [Changchun World Sculpture Park](/asia/china/jilin/changchun/changchun_world_sculpture_park) gathers several hundred international sculptures around a central lake and is better than it sounds: a good place to walk for an afternoon without any particular agenda. The [Northeast Tiger Park](/asia/china/jilin/changchun/northeast_tiger_park) houses Siberian tigers and other animals in conditions that are worth researching before you visit, as opinions on the facilities vary considerably.

Getting to Changchun is straightforward: it is a major stop on the high-speed rail network linking Beijing, Shenyang, and Harbin, with the journey from Beijing taking under four hours. From here, [Jilin City](/asia/china/jilin/jilin_city) is forty minutes by fast train, and [Changbaishan](/asia/china/jilin/changbaishan) is reachable in a day with an early start.
