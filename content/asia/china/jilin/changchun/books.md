**The Last Emperor** by Aisin-Gioro Puyi (autobiography, 1964) — Puyi's account of his own extraordinary life, from the Forbidden City through Manchukuo to his re-education as an ordinary citizen under Communist rule. The Changchun chapters cover his years at the Imperial Palace of Manchukuo in vivid, sometimes self-justifying detail. The book was published in revised form under the title "From Emperor to Citizen" and is the primary source for understanding the Manchukuo period from the inside.

**Manchukuo: The Creation of a State** by Bill Sewell — a scholarly but readable account of how the Japanese puppet state was built, operated, and eventually collapsed. Essential context for understanding what you see when walking the Manchukuo sites in Changchun.

**Under a Cruel Star** by Heda Margolius Kovaly — not about Changchun specifically, but a memoir of life under totalitarianism that illuminates the same mechanisms of collaboration and resistance that characterised Manchukuo. Reading it alongside accounts of the Puyi years gives a clearer sense of how people navigate power they cannot refuse.

**Pu Yi's Later Years** by Li Wenda — covers the period from Puyi's Soviet capture through his time in a Chinese re-education facility and his final years as a worker in Beijing. Less well known than the autobiography but provides the outside perspective on a story Puyi told only from inside.
