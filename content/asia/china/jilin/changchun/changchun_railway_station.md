  Changchun's railway station was the main arrival point for millions of migrants who came to Manchuria in the late Qing and early Republican periods — Shandong and Hebei farmers recruited or desperate enough to take the train north into the unknown. The station area was designed by Japanese colonial architects to impress arriving passengers with the modernity of the Manchukuo project, and the building retains its original imposing scale.
---

The area around Changchun Railway Station preserves some of the most intact Manchukuo-era urban fabric in the city. The station building itself dates to the Japanese colonial period and was built to receive the South Manchurian Railway — the Japanese-controlled rail system that was the backbone of Manchuria's economic development. The station plaza and the surrounding commercial streets show the scale at which Japanese planners imagined the city's gateway.

The immediate station area is lively in the way that all Chinese train station districts are lively: street food, shops, crowds in transit, the energy of arrival and departure. Look past the commercial activity and the architectural history is visible in the older buildings on the surrounding streets, many of which retain their Manchukuo-era facades despite interior conversions.
