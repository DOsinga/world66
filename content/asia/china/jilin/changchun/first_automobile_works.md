  When First Automobile Works assembled its first vehicle — a Liberation (Jiefang) brand truck — in July 1956, Mao Zedong personally attended the ceremony. The factory had been built with Soviet assistance as a Five-Year Plan flagship project, and its first truck was presented as evidence that China could build its own industrial capacity without dependence on foreign technology. The ceremony was photographed, filmed, and circulated widely as a symbol of socialist construction.

The First Automobile Works district in northern Changchun is where China's automotive industry was born. The FAW complex, established in 1953 with Soviet technical assistance, produced the Jiefang (Liberation) truck, the Red Flag limousine, and generations of vehicles that motorised the People's Republic. The factory district is a substantial area of industrial architecture and worker housing from the 1950s — a complete Soviet-influenced planned industrial city-within-a-city that is gradually being absorbed into the general urban fabric.

The FAW Museum (see separate entry) is the accessible focal point for visitors. The broader factory district, with its Soviet-style workers' housing blocks and the remnants of 1950s industrial urbanism, is visible from the street and represents a layer of Chinese socialist construction history that is being increasingly recognised as architecturally and historically significant.
