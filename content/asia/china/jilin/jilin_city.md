Jilin City shares its province's name, which causes some confusion, but the city has a distinct identity firmly rooted in the Songhua River that bisects it. The river matters here in a way that rivers rarely do in modern Chinese cities: it flows through the urban core, it shapes the winter microclimate, and it is the reason the city became famous for one of northeast China's genuinely spectacular natural events. When the temperature drops in December and January, warm water released from the Fengman hydroelectric dam upstream keeps the river from fully freezing, and the resulting moisture meets the frigid air to coat the riverside trees in thick white rime ice — crystalline formations that can last for weeks and turn the riverside promenade into something from a fairytale. Jilin's rimmed trees are counted among China's four major natural wonders, and this is not mere civic boasting: people do come specifically to see them.

The rest of the year, Jilin City is a pleasant mid-sized northeastern city with a few things worth finding. The Jilin City Museum holds what is reportedly the world's largest stone meteorite — a genuinely odd exhibit that gets less attention than it deserves. The Confucian Temple (Wen Miao) is the largest in Manchuria and significantly quieter than equivalent temples further south, which makes it easier to appreciate properly. Beishan Park has an active temple complex, seasonal lotus displays, and in winter becomes part of the broader ice landscape that takes over the city.

Beidahu Ski Resort, about fifty kilometres from the city, hosted the 6th Asian Winter Games and is the most developed ski destination in Jilin province — a real resort with multiple runs and modern infrastructure, not the improvised facilities that pass for ski areas in some northeast Chinese cities. Songhua Lake (the Fengman Reservoir itself) is a significant body of water about fifteen kilometres from the centre, popular for boat cruises in summer and ice fishing in the deep cold of winter.

Jilin City is forty minutes from Changchun by high-speed train and sits naturally in the path of anyone travelling toward Changbaishan from the capital. The city is compact enough to cover comfortably on foot along the riverbanks, and the core attractions — the river walk, Beishan Park, the museum — can be done in a day if you are moving through.
