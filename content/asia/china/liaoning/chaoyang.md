Chaoyang is known in paleontology circles worldwide, and it deserves to be. The hills around this western Liaoning city produced one of the most significant fossil discoveries of the twentieth century: a sequence of feathered dinosaurs and primitive birds from the Early Cretaceous period that fundamentally changed scientific understanding of avian evolution. Sinornithosaurus, Microraptor, and dozens of other species were pulled from these mudstone formations, and the [Chaoyang Bird Fossil National Geopark](/asia/china/liaoning/chaoyang/chaoyang_bird_fossil_national_geopark) — reopened in 2025 after a major upgrade — houses the results in a serious museum built around actual excavation sites.

The geopark on Longniao Street is the main draw, and it earns the trip on its own. The exhibits are well-curated and the fossils — including specimens with feather impressions still visible — are among the most scientifically important objects on public display anywhere in China. The surrounding hills are riddled with fossil-bearing formations, and the museum provides context for the broader geological story of the Yixian and Jiufotang formations that made this area so significant.

Chaoyang's other historical layer is Buddhist. The city served as a centre of Buddhism from the fourth century onward, and the Three Pagodas — the [North Pagoda](/asia/china/liaoning/chaoyang/chaoyang_north_tower), [South Pagoda](/asia/china/liaoning/chaoyang/chaoyang_south_tower), and [Phoenix Pagoda](/asia/china/liaoning/chaoyang/phoenix_tower) — represent a concentrated record of pagoda construction from the Northern Yan through the Liao Dynasty. The North Pagoda in particular, rebuilt multiple times over fifteen centuries, carries relics that were rediscovered during a 1984 restoration and are now displayed in the attached museum.

The surrounding region contains some of the oldest evidence of Chinese civilisation — a Neolithic culture here dates back almost seven thousand years — and the [Niuheliang archaeological site](/asia/china/liaoning/chaoyang/niuheliang_archaeological_site), a Hongshan culture ceremonial complex from around 3500 BCE, is accessible from the city. Chaoyang sits in the western highlands and is a roughly three-hour drive from Shenyang.
