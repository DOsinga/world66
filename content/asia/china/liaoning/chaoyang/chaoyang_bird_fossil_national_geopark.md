story: >
  In 1996 a farmer near Chaoyang cracked open a mudstone slab and found Sinosauropteryx, the first non-avian dinosaur ever discovered with preserved evidence of feathers. The find demolished a century of debate about bird origins — palaeontologists had argued for decades whether birds descended from dinosaurs, and this single specimen answered the question in one stroke. The geopark was built around the formations that produced it and dozens of related species.
---

The Chaoyang Bird Fossil National Geopark is one of the most scientifically significant natural history museums in China, built directly on the Early Cretaceous Yixian and Jiufotang geological formations that produced an extraordinary sequence of feathered dinosaurs and primitive birds. Reopened in 2025 after a substantial upgrade, the museum sits on Longniao Street and houses actual excavation sites alongside display cases of specimens — including specimens with feather impressions still visible in the rock matrix after 125 million years.

The exhibits are well-curated by the standards of Chinese provincial museums. Star specimens include Microraptor gui, a four-winged gliding dinosaur; Confuciusornis, the oldest known bird with a true beak; and Sinornithosaurus, a feathered dromaeosaurid. The palaeontological story told here — the transition from feathered dinosaur to bird, played out across multiple genera in a single geographic sequence — is one of the most compelling in evolutionary biology, and the museum presents it with appropriate seriousness. Allow two to three hours to do it justice.
