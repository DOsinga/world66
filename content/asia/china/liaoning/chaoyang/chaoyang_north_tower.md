The Chaoyang North Tower is one of the oldest Buddhist pagodas in China still standing above ground, and its layered history is visible in its fabric. The tower's core dates to the Northern Wei Dynasty (386–534 CE), making it contemporaneous with the earliest Buddhist monuments in China, though it was substantially rebuilt during the Sui, Tang, and Liao periods. Each reconstruction added its own layer, and the current thirteen-storey structure — rising around forty metres above the surrounding neighbourhood — is essentially a palimpsest of fifteen centuries of Buddhist patronage.

The attached museum houses the relics recovered from the 1984 excavation alongside contextual displays on the city's Buddhist history. Chaoyang served as a major Buddhist centre from the fourth century onward, and the concentration of pagodas here — North Tower, South Tower, and Phoenix Tower within a few kilometres — reflects a sustained level of patronage that survived the decline of each successive dynasty. The North Tower stands in a well-maintained park and is visible from much of the city centre.
