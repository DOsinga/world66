  The Hushan section was officially designated the eastern terminus of the Great Wall in 1992, resolving a long historical debate. Earlier scholarship had placed the wall's end at Shanhaiguan on the coast, but archaeological work at Hushan confirmed that the Ming fortifications extended considerably further northeast, following the Yalu River valley to this ridge overlooking the Korean border.
---

Hushan Great Wall sits twenty-five kilometres northeast of Dandong city and represents the easternmost section of the entire Ming Dynasty wall system. The location is obscure by Great Wall standards — far removed from the famous sections near Beijing — and that obscurity is its main appeal. The reconstruction is partial and the crowds are thin, which means you can walk stretches that feel genuinely rugged and get an unmediated sense of why the Ming emperors built a wall here: the ridge commands a clear view of the Yalu River valley and the Korean hills beyond.

The trail covers about 1.5 kilometres of wall and takes two to three hours to walk at a comfortable pace. North Korean territory is visible throughout the walk, separated only by the river below. The site also includes a small museum documenting the wall's history in this region, and a boat trip on the Yalu is available from the base for visitors who want a closer look at the North Korean side. Taxis from central Dandong take about forty minutes.
