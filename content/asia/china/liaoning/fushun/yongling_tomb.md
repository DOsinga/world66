Yongling Tomb, located in Xinbin County about ninety kilometres southeast of Fushun city, is one of the three Manchu royal tombs that collectively hold UNESCO World Heritage status. It is the burial site of Nurhachi's great-great-grandfather, great-grandfather, grandfather, and father — the four generations whose tombs became the dynastic foundation when the Qing consolidated power in China.

The complex is smaller and less famous than the Qing tombs near Beijing or Shenyang's Fuling Tomb, which makes it significantly less visited. The site includes a main hall with ancestral tablets, a spirit way lined with stone animals, and the tomb mounds themselves, set against a forested hillside in a valley that feels genuinely remote from the industrial northeast. Xinbin County also contains the Hetuala site where Nurhachi declared the Later Jin Dynasty in 1616; combining the two in a day trip from Fushun is straightforward.
