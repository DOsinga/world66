Panjin is an oil city built on a wetland delta, and its defining attraction is a geographic accident: every autumn, the vast reed marshes at the mouth of the Liaohe River turn a saturated crimson red, the colour coming from Suaeda salsa seaweed that stains the mudflats as the season turns. The Red Beach Scenic Area covers twenty square kilometres of this and is unlike anything else in China — a landscape that looks more like a colour-saturated photograph than a real place, set against the grey of the Bohai Sea.

The timing matters enormously. Peak colour runs from late September through October; visit in summer and you will find green reeds and mud. The wetland is also a significant stopover on the East Asian–Australasian Flyway, and during migration season the marshes fill with red-crowned cranes, black-faced spoonbills, and several other globally threatened species. A boardwalk system threads through the reserve, and boat trips are available on the interior waterways.

Away from the Red Beach, Panjin is a working oil-producing city with little to recommend it architecturally, but the local agricultural products are worth seeking out. Panjin rice — grown in the saline marshland soil — has a reputation across China for its particular texture and flavour, and the river crabs fattened in the estuary waters are a seasonal delicacy that locals take seriously. The combination of crab and local rice is the meal to have here.

The city is a straightforward day trip from Shenyang — high-speed trains cover the distance in under an hour — though staying overnight lets you catch the Red Beach at dawn, when the light is right and the tour buses have not yet arrived.
