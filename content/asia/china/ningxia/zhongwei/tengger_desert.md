  The Tengger is the fourth largest desert in China, covering 36,000 square kilometres. Its name in Mongolian means "sky" — a reference to the way the flat desert horizon merges with the sky when seen from the dune crests. The desert sits in a shallow basin between the Helan Mountains to the east and the mountains of Gansu to the west, and its southern edge, where it meets the Yellow River at Shapotou, is its most accessible and most dramatic point.
---

The Tengger Desert stretches north and west from Zhongwei, part of the vast arid interior of northern China. At Shapotou it reaches the Yellow River and stops — the desert edge here is steep and abrupt, with high dunes dropping directly toward the water. But the desert extends well beyond the resort area, and for those willing to go further into it, the landscapes away from Shapotou's crowds are quieter and more elemental.

Camel treks into the interior beyond Shapotou are available through operators in Zhongwei, ranging from half-day rides to overnight camps with fire and stargazing. The night sky in the desert interior, away from any artificial light, is exceptional — this part of northwestern China has extremely low light pollution. Operators can arrange everything including camping equipment; prices vary but a full overnight desert experience typically costs several hundred yuan per person.

The desert surface varies from pure sand dunes in the south and east to rockier, more gravelled plains toward the centre. Spring (April–May) and autumn (September–October) are the best seasons; summer is very hot and the tourist infrastructure at Shapotou is overwhelmed; winter brings cold but clear days and a near-total absence of other visitors.
