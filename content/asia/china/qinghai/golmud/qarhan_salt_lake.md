story: >
  Qarhan is the largest salt lake in China and one of the largest in the
  world — around 5,800 square kilometres of brine flats. The state-owned
  potash plant on the lake produces roughly a quarter of China's potassium
  fertiliser, and the salt crust is solid enough that the G3011 expressway
  and the Qinghai–Tibet railway both run straight across it on causeways.
---

A hundred kilometres east of Golmud the desert opens out onto an immense salt pan, white to the horizon. Qarhan is best seen by driving the G3011 toward Da Qaidam, where the road crosses thirty kilometres of unbroken salt. Pull over at any of the maintenance laybys to walk on it.

There is a small visitor centre and an "ten-thousand zhang salt bridge" tourist area at the eastern end of the lake, run by the potash company, with mock saltboats and viewing platforms. It is touristy and avoidable; the real spectacle is the empty road itself, the railway running parallel on its own causeway, and the bright sky reflected off white salt at midday.
