  The Jade Emperor Pavilion complex crowns a steep red sandstone butte that was already considered sacred before the Taoist structures were built. Local legend holds that the site was chosen by a wandering Taoist monk who placed a bowl of water on the summit and declared that wherever the water remained undisturbed overnight — undisturbed by wind or spirit — was the holiest place in the valley. The water stilled here, and the first pavilion was built the following spring.

Yuwang Guan is a Taoist complex perched on a dramatic red sandstone butte above Guide's old town, offering the most commanding panoramic view of the Yellow River valley in the area. The climb is steep — a set of stone steps cut into the cliff face, with iron chains to grip in slippery conditions — but the reward at the top is the sight of the "crystal river" bending through its red sandstone gorge far below, clear enough to see the riverbed.

The complex itself contains several pavilions and shrines from different periods, maintained by a small resident monastic community. The oldest structures date to the Ming dynasty, though most of what stands now is Qing-era or later. Incense smoke drifts through the courtyards, and prayer flags from the nearby Tibetan tradition hang alongside distinctly Chinese deity paintings, reflecting the mixed religious landscape of this part of Qinghai. The best light for the valley views is early morning or late afternoon.
