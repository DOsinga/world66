Maduo is the highest county seat in China at roughly 4,300 metres, sitting on the Tibetan Plateau in Qinghai's Golog Prefecture near the headwaters of the Yellow River. The town is small and functional, dominated by a large central square with a [monument to the legendary King Gesar](/asia/china/qinghai/maduo/gesar-monument) — the hero of the world's longest epic poem, deeply revered across the Tibetan world. Yaks graze on the outskirts of town, prayer flags snap in the wind, and the horizon in every direction is nothing but plateau.

The main draw for travellers is the Yellow River itself. About four kilometres from town, the [Yellow River Source Area](/asia/china/qinghai/maduo/yellow-river-source) begins; about 50 kilometres north lie the twin sacred lakes of [Ngoring](/asia/china/qinghai/maduo/ngoring-lake) and [Kyaring](/asia/china/qinghai/maduo/kyaring-lake), the primary sources of the Yellow River. These enormous high-altitude lakes, set at 4,400 metres, are among the highest large lakes in the world. They sit in an open plateau landscape of breathtaking emptiness — no trees, very few people, and the low-angle plateau light giving everything a slightly unreal quality. Getting out there requires hiring a private car (around 900 to 1,000 yuan return); a visit to the lakeside ruins and the small temple is worth combining with the journey.

The town itself is connected to Xining by bus — one daily departure from the Nanchuan West Bus Terminal at around 8am, taking roughly 10 hours for about 95 yuan. Accommodation is available in mid-range hotels at 300 to 400 yuan for a double, or budget options at 150 to 250 yuan. The altitude is severe enough to cause real problems: headaches and breathlessness are common on arrival, and physical exertion should be minimal for the first day. Acclimatise in Xining and ideally Yushu before pushing this high.

Maduo is a staging point for travellers heading deeper into Golog Prefecture and toward the [Amnye Machen](/asia/china/qinghai/amnye-machen) mountain range. For those simply crossing the plateau, the town offers an unforgettable night at the edge of somewhere genuinely wild.
