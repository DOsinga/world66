The King Gesar Monument in Maduo's central square is the dominant feature of the town and one of the most prominent tributes to the epic's hero anywhere in China. King Gesar (Gesar of Ling) is the central figure of the world's longest epic poem — a vast oral tradition running to over one million verses in its complete form, celebrating the warrior-king who unified the Tibetan people and defeated the forces of chaos. In the Golog region of Qinghai, where the landscape of the epic is said to be set, Gesar is a living cultural presence, not just a literary figure.

The monument is a large bronze figure in martial posture, with prayer flags and offering cairns accumulated around its base by local people. The surrounding square, with its views of the plateau horizon and the vast sky, gives the statue a genuine grandeur. The town's Tibetan character — visible in the architecture, the monastery on the edge of town, and the yak herds on the outskirts — provides context for understanding why this is the place where the Gesar tradition feels most alive.
