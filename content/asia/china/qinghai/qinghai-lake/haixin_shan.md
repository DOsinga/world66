  Haixin Shan is sacred in both Tibetan Buddhist and Mongolian traditions. The Mongolians who controlled this region in the 13th and 14th centuries called the lake Koko Nor — Blue Lake — and the island was their most venerated site. Tibetan pilgrims walk the clockwise circuit of the lake specifically to pay respect to this island, which they see as the home of a naga dragon king who regulates the lake's waters and weather.

Haixin Shan — Heart of the Sea Island — sits in the centre of Qinghai Lake, a small rocky island crowned with a Tibetan Buddhist monastery. It is one of the most sacred sites in the lake region, approached by boat from the southern shore and receiving a steady stream of pilgrims throughout the summer months.

The island is about 1 square kilometre, rising to 3,266 metres above sea level — higher than the surrounding lake surface. The monastery on its summit is modest in scale but sits in a position of extraordinary isolation. Boat trips from the shore take around 45 minutes each way. The lake surface is intensely blue under clear skies; the journey across it, looking back at the mountains ringing the lake, is worth the excursion even if the monastery itself is small.
