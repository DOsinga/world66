story: >
  Xining was founded as a military garrison in 111 BCE, when the Han dynasty emperor Wu Di sent soldiers west to secure the Silk Road corridors against the Xiongnu nomads. The city has served as a military garrison and frontier post for most of its history; the walls that enclosed it were not just for show but for genuine defence against the plateau peoples to the west and south. The modern city has obliterated almost all trace of the original walls.
---

Xining served for centuries as the primary Chinese military garrison on the edge of the Tibetan plateau, and its history as a frontier garrison is recorded in the Provincial Museum and the City Museum even if the physical evidence is largely gone. A section of the old city wall survives near the central bus station area — a fragment of tamped earth construction that has been incorporated into a small park and labelled as a historical monument.

The garrison history gives Xining a different character from Chinese cities that developed as trading towns or administrative capitals. It was always a place where the Chinese state maintained its presence by force in a region that did not naturally belong to it — a character that persists in the city's modern relationship to Tibetan and Muslim communities.
