  The most famous piece in the museum is the He Zun — a bronze ritual vessel from around 1042 BCE that bears an inscription containing the oldest known use of the word "China" (中国, Zhongguo). The inscription records a speech by the Zhou king at the founding of his new capital; among other things, he declares that the Zhou have received the mandate to rule "the centre of the world." The vessel sat in the ground for three thousand years before being found by a farmer in 1963.

The Baoji Bronzeware Museum holds one of the finest collections of Zhou dynasty bronze vessels in China and one of the most important concentrations of Bronze Age metalwork in the world. The city and its surrounding countryside have produced an exceptional number of Zhou bronzes because this area was the original heartland of the Zhou state — Baoji is where the Zhou people developed before their conquest of the Shang dynasty in 1046 BCE and their founding of a kingdom that lasted 800 years.

The collection includes ritual vessels (ding tripods, gui tureens, zun goblets, and bells), weapons, chariot fittings, and inscribed objects of the type that historians use to reconstruct the political and religious life of early China. The museum building is dramatic — a modern structure incorporating the form of traditional bronze vessels — and the curation is excellent. Admission is free; the museum is on the eastern section of Binhe Avenue along the Wei River.
