Hancheng is a small city on the Yellow River in eastern Shaanxi with a claim to historical significance disproportionate to its size: this is where Sima Qian was born. Sima Qian, the Han Dynasty historian who completed the Shiji — Records of the Grand Historian — is considered the father of Chinese historiography; his work, covering Chinese history from the Yellow Emperor to his own time, set the template for every dynastic history that followed. The [Sima Qian Memorial Temple](/asia/china/shaanxi/hancheng/sima_qian_memorial), in the hills above the city, is a major pilgrimage destination for scholars and students.

The [Hancheng Old Town](/asia/china/shaanxi/hancheng/hancheng_old_town) is one of the best-preserved examples of Ming and Qing urban planning in Shaanxi: a grid of lanes lined with courtyard residences, the wooden facades decorated with carved panels, mostly intact and still inhabited. [Dangjia Village](/asia/china/shaanxi/hancheng/dangjia_village), a few kilometres outside the city, is an even better preserved example — a Yuan and Ming Dynasty village of extraordinary architectural coherence that sees significant tourist numbers.

The Yellow River here flows through a deep gorge, and the coal deposits on either bank have made this stretch of the river one of the most heavily industrialised in China. This contrast — between the literary and architectural heritage of the old city and the industrial landscape around it — is more interesting than it sounds, a visible record of what different centuries have done to the same river valley.

Hancheng has limited rail connections; most access is by bus from Xi'an in about three to four hours, or from Weinan. The city is a reasonable destination for those interested in Chinese historiography or well-preserved traditional architecture outside the main tourist circuits.
