Dangjia Village, about nine kilometres northeast of Hancheng's old city, is one of the best-preserved traditional village complexes in China. Founded in the Yuan Dynasty and developed through the Ming and Qing periods, it contains more than 120 four-sided courtyard compounds arranged on a hillside grid of stone-paved lanes. The grey brick walls, carved timber brackets, doorway screens with painted decorations, and family ancestral halls are largely intact and still inhabited.

What distinguishes Dangjia from other preserved villages is its coherence: every element — the siting on the slope, the defensive walls and watchtowers at the perimeter, the communal water cisterns, the private garden compounds behind the main residences — follows a consistent plan, executed in similar materials over three centuries. The village is a UNESCO Preparatory List site and receives moderate tourist numbers, but its scale means you can walk the outer lanes in near-solitude even when the centre is busy. Spring and autumn are the better seasons to visit.
