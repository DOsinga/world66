The Sima Qian Memorial Temple sits on a hilltop about ten kilometres south of Hancheng, where the great Han Dynasty historian was born around 145 BC. The complex includes a ceremonial gate, stele pavilions with inscribed stone tablets commemorating the historian, and at the summit a round earthen burial mound shaded by ancient cypress trees. The setting is austere and dignified in the way of Chinese scholar shrines, with views over the loess landscape and the Yellow River valley below.

Sima Qian completed the Shiji — Records of the Grand Historian — in approximately 91 BC. The work comprises 130 chapters covering over three thousand years of Chinese history from the Yellow Emperor to Sima Qian's own time, using a biographical format he invented that subsequent historians copied for two millennia. For scholars of Chinese civilisation, this site carries genuine weight: visiting feels like standing at the origin of an entire historiographical tradition. The temple sees steady pilgrimage traffic from university students and scholars alongside general tourists.
