  This temple predates the more famous Wuhou Temple in Chengdu by fifty years. Built in 236 AD, six years after Zhuge Liang's death at the Battle of Wuzhang Plains, it was constructed on imperial order at the site closest to his final campaign base. Zhuge Liang spent the last six years of his life in this valley, launching five northern expeditions from Hanzhong against the Wei Kingdom — all of which failed to restore the Han, and all of which cemented his reputation as the model of loyal service to a failing cause.

The Wuhou Temple in Mianxian County, about forty-five kilometres west of central Hanzhong, is the principal memorial site for Zhuge Liang, the strategist whose brilliance defined the Shu Han kingdom during the Three Kingdoms period. The complex sprawls across a considerable area and includes the main memorial hall, the adjacent tomb mound, side pavilions containing inscribed steles, and older buildings in various states of repair. The atmosphere is one of genuine historical weight — this is not a Qing reconstruction but a site that has accumulated pilgrimages and donations over seventeen centuries.

The main hall contains large statues of Zhuge Liang and his principal subordinates, surrounded by votive offerings and incense burners that speak to his ongoing status as a cultural deity for military excellence, loyalty, and strategic intelligence. The surrounding grounds have cedars and cypresses of considerable age. Mianxian's small town centre is nearby, and the temple is one of the most visited sites in the Hanzhong region; weekend crowds can be substantial.
