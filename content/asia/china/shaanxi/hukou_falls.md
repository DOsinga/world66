Hukou Falls is where the Yellow River, having crossed the broad loess plateau of northern Shaanxi, is suddenly forced through a narrow basalt gorge barely thirty metres wide — squeezing a river that is three hundred metres across into a channel the width of a large building, then dropping fifteen metres in a roar of yellow-brown water and spray. The name means "kettle spout" and the hydraulics justify it: the water does not so much fall as funnel, under enormous pressure, through a geological obstruction. It is the second largest waterfall in China by volume.

[The falls](/asia/china/shaanxi/hukou_falls/the_falls) are on the boundary between Shaanxi and Shanxi province, with the Shanxi bank offering the most photogenic angle. The Shaanxi bank provides closer access to the upper viewing platform where you can stand within a few metres of the water and feel the percussion in your chest. The surrounding [loess plateau landscape](/asia/china/shaanxi/hukou_falls/loess_landscape) is eroded yellow earth, terraced fields on the upper slopes, poplar and willow trees along the river banks — there is nothing in the world that looks quite like this particular combination of landscape and river.

Seasonal variation is significant: spring snowmelt produces the most spectacular flow, with the highest water volumes; summer rainstorms add to the volume but reduce visibility with spray. Autumn, when the surrounding trees turn and the water level drops to reveal more of the rock formations, is considered the best time for photography. In winter the falls partially freeze, producing ice formations on the surrounding rock faces.

The falls are most easily reached from Yan'an, about one hundred and sixty kilometres to the south, with public buses or private tours. The Shanxi-side access from Jixian County is better developed for tourism.
