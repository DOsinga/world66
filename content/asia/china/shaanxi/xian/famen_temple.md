A working Buddhist temple 120 km west of Xi'an in Fufeng County, Famen has been a pilgrimage site since the Tang dynasty because of one object: a fingerbone said to belong to the historical Buddha, brought here by traders from India in the early centuries AD. It is displayed only on the most important Buddhist holidays.

The original 8th-century crypt is now a museum housing the relic and the Tang treasures found around it — gold and silver tea sets, glass from the Eastern Mediterranean, silk woven with patterns that match exactly with painted scrolls of the period. A separate, dramatic modern complex built in 2009 encloses the precinct in a vast lotus-shaped processional hall designed by Taiwanese architect C.Y. Lee.

Famen is far enough from Xi'an to make for a long but rewarding day trip. Most travellers combine it with the Han imperial tombs at Maoling on the same circuit.
