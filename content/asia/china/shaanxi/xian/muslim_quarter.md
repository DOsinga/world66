The Muslim Quarter is what most travellers remember about Xi'an. A dense grid of alleys spreading west and north from the [Drum Tower](/asia/china/shaanxi/xian/drum_tower), it has been home to a Hui Muslim community for over a thousand years — descendants of Persian and Central Asian traders who settled here when Xi'an was the eastern terminus of the Silk Road.

The food is the reason to come. Hand-pulled lamb noodles, mountains of skewered lamb grilled over coals, persimmon cakes fried in pans the size of bicycle wheels, paomo (a torn-bread-and-mutton stew you assemble yourself), pomegranate juice pressed in front of you, and roujiamo — the closest thing China has to a hamburger, made with cumin-spiced shredded meat in a flat wheaten bun.

Beiyuanmen is the busiest food street and the most touristed; Huajue Lane is calmer and runs along the back of the [Great Mosque](/asia/china/shaanxi/xian/great_mosque). Wander after dark when the woks are at full tilt and the alleys are full of steam. The quarter is also the best part of the city for souvenir hunting — silver, calligraphy, paper-cuts, jade.
