Dongying is one of China's youngest cities, established in 1974 after the discovery of the Shengli oil field — the second-largest in the country — and it has the personality of a place built around a single industry in a hurry. The urban fabric is modern and utilitarian, but the natural setting is extraordinary: the city sits at the mouth of the Yellow River, where China's second-longest waterway deposits its sediment load into the Bohai Sea. The Yellow River Delta grows measurably every year as new land is created, and the resulting wetlands have become a major stopover for migratory birds on the East Asian flyway.

The [Yellow River Delta Nature Reserve](/asia/china/shandong_province/dongying/yellow_river_delta_nature_reserve), at the river's current mouth, is the draw. Every spring and autumn, hundreds of species of migratory birds pass through on routes connecting Siberia and Australia, and the reserve's reed beds and mudflats host concentrations that are genuinely impressive even by the standards of dedicated birders. The [Yellow River Estuary Scenic Area](/asia/china/shandong_province/dongying/yellow_river_estuary) offers boat trips and observation platforms where you can watch the river deposit new land into the sea in real time — one of the stranger sights in eastern China.

The city itself offers a [museum](/asia/china/shandong_province/dongying/dongying_museum) covering the region's history from Neolithic times through the oil era, and in Guangrao county a Song Dynasty wooden hall at the [Guandi Temple](/asia/china/shandong_province/dongying/guandi_temple_guangrao) is reputedly the oldest surviving wooden structure in Shandong. Dongying is not a destination that requires a long stay, but travelers moving along the northern Shandong coast who have any interest in wetland ecology or the landscapes of the Yellow River will find it more rewarding than its industrial reputation suggests.
