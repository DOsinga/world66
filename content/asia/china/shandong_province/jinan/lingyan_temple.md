story: >
  The temple's famous collection of Song Dynasty painted clay sculptures is the reason to make the journey. Forty lifelike monk figures, each roughly life-size, were modelled between the 10th and 12th centuries with such individual personality and physiognomic detail that they look like portraits — and they may be. The sculptor is unknown, but the figures include one apparently asleep, one absorbed in reading, and several with expressions that are quite unlike the idealised serenity of most Buddhist sculpture.
---

Lingyan Temple is one of the four great Buddhist monasteries of Tang Dynasty China, built in a deep valley in the Taishan mountain range about 80 kilometres southwest of Jinan. Though the main Tang structures were rebuilt in the Song and later dynasties, the complex retains an extraordinary collection of Song Dynasty painted clay sculptures of Buddhist monks — among the finest examples of realistic figure sculpture in Chinese history.

The site also contains the Pizhi Pagoda, a nine-storey Song Dynasty brick structure, and a wooded valley with streams and carved rock inscriptions accumulated over fifteen centuries of pilgrimage and scholarly visits. The setting is beautiful and the visit rewards a half day; combine it with an overnight in Tai'an if you are also visiting Mount Tai, as Lingyan is roughly on route. Bus connections from Tai'an are more convenient than from Jinan city.
