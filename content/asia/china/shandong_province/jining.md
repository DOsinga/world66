Jining occupies the southwestern corner of Shandong, sitting astride the Beijing-Hangzhou Grand Canal at the point where it begins its descent through Shandong toward the Yangtze. For centuries the city was one of the great canal entrepôts, growing wealthy on the grain, silk, and salt that moved north and south through its locks, and the poet Li Bai spent two years here in the 730s in circumstances that suggest it was a place of some sophistication. The canal heritage is less visible now than in the restored stretches to the south, but the city's position at this historical crossroads gives it a weight beyond its current size.

The more obvious draw is that Jining prefecture contains Qufu, the hometown of Confucius — administratively separate, but effectively the reason most visitors come to this corner of Shandong. Zoucheng, the birthplace of Mencius, Confucius's most important intellectual successor, is also within the prefecture; the temple and memorial hall there are far less visited than Qufu and correspondingly more atmospheric. The Mengfu Ancient City in Zoucheng has been substantially restored and is worth the short detour if you are already in the area.

Jining itself has a functional Confucius Temple, a tower commemorating Li Bai's residence, and the canal-front architecture of its old districts. The practical appeal is its rail connections: the high-speed line puts Beijing two and a half hours away, and the city serves as a logical overnight base for travelers covering both Qufu and the canal heritage sites of southern Shandong without wanting to change accommodation daily.
