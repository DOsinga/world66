story: >
  The Beijing-Hangzhou Grand Canal, at 1,794 kilometres, is the longest artificial waterway in the world and was for over a millennium the economic spine of China, moving grain, silk, salt, and soldiers between the capital and the Yangtze Delta. Jining occupies a critical position in the canal's history: the section through Shandong required the construction of a complex system of locks, aqueducts, and water conservation reservoirs to maintain a sufficient depth across the ridge between the northern and southern watershed — an engineering achievement that took two centuries to perfect.
---

Jining's canal heritage district preserves sections of the old waterway and the historic architecture that grew up along its banks during the Yuan, Ming, and Qing dynasties. The canal-front area has been developed as a scenic walk with restored warehouses, canal-facing pavilions, and sections of original stone-paved towpath. The UNESCO-listed Grand Canal runs through the city, and the locks and sluice gates that control water flow through the Shandong ridge are still in operation nearby.

The Canal Museum (Jining Canal Cultural Museum) covers the canal's history comprehensively, from the Sui Dynasty origins to the present day, with models of the lock systems, historical maps, and material culture from the canal trade. The best time to see the canal itself is early morning, when barge traffic is active and the light is good. The museum is near the main canal bridge in the central district; entry is free.
