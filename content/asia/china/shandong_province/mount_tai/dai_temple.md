The Dai Temple at the foot of Mount Tai in Tai'an city is the principal temple dedicated to the mountain deity — the god of Mount Tai (Dongyue Dadi), traditionally believed to preside over life and death and to receive the petitions of the dead. The temple complex covers 96,000 square metres and follows the layout of an imperial palace, reflecting the mountain's status as a site of state ritual.

The main Tiankoang Palace, built in 1009 CE, is one of the most important surviving buildings in China — its scale, age, and the continuous Song Dynasty mural that covers three interior walls place it in the first rank of Chinese architectural heritage. The mural alone justifies the visit: a panoramic procession of several hundred figures painted between 1009 and 1012 CE, it is one of the longest figurative mural sequences to survive from medieval China. Entry is included in the general Mount Tai ticket (¥127).
