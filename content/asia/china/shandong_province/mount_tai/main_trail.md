The main ascending trail is the reason to come to Mount Tai. The path is continuous stone steps from the Red Gate palace at the mountain base to the South Gate of Heaven near the summit — 7,500 steps, 7.5 kilometres, 1,400 metres of ascent. Along the way: the Red Gate Palace, the Wan Xian Tower (Ten Thousand Immortals Pavilion), the Middle Heaven Gate at the halfway point, and the Zhong Tianmen cable car station for those who prefer to let machinery do the upper section.

The standard approach for Chinese pilgrims is to start after midnight and climb through the night, reaching the summit for sunrise — a tradition that predates the current staircase by millennia. The dawn over the clouds, when they are present, justifies the ordeal. Rent the military surplus padded jackets from the stalls near the summit; the temperature drops dramatically and the wind is fierce even in summer. The climb takes 4–6 hours at a comfortable pace; coming down by cable car is more pleasant on the knees.
