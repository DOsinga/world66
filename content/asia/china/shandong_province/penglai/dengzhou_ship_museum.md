  The ships in the Dengzhou Museum were excavated from the ancient harbor of Dengzhou (the old name for Penglai) where they had lain buried in the harbor mud for 600–700 years. The preservation in anaerobic mud was extraordinary: the hull timbers, iron nails, and rigging fittings are largely intact. The largest ship, a Yuan Dynasty vessel, carried cargo between Penglai and Korea on the Maritime Silk Road route that made this harbor one of the most important in East Asia.
---

The Dengzhou Ancient Ship Museum houses four ships excavated from Penglai's ancient harbor between 1984 and 2005 — genuine Yuan and Ming Dynasty vessels preserved in harbor mud, displayed in a climate-controlled building on the waterfront. The ships range from a small coastal patrol boat to a large cargo vessel of the type that operated on the Maritime Silk Road routes between China, Korea, and Japan.

This is an unusual museum experience: the ships are real, large, and visibly seaworthy in their original design. The context of Penglai as a major medieval naval base and trading port is clearly explained. The museum is smaller than major ship museums in other countries but the authenticity of the objects gives it a weight that many larger institutions lack. Entry is around ¥60, included with the Penglai Pavilion complex ticket.
