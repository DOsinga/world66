  After Germany lost Qingdao in 1914, the Governor's Residence served various purposes through Japanese occupation and the Chinese Nationalist period. In 1957, Mao Zedong stayed here for an extended working retreat — one of the few times he visited the coast. His bedroom is preserved as he left it.
---

The former residence of the German Governor of Jiaozhou sits on Xinhao Hill above the old concession, a chunky Bavarian villa completed in 1905 at a cost that scandalized Berlin (the Kaiser reportedly called it an "absurd extravagance"). Today it functions as a museum of the German colonial period.

The building is the most distinctive example of German colonial architecture in Qingdao — turrets, steep roofs, stone trim, and a ballroom with original parquet floors. The interior has been meticulously restored, with period furniture, maps, and photographs of the concession. The hilltop garden offers a fine view over the red-roofed German quarter below. Open Tuesday to Sunday, 8:30am–5pm.
