story: >
  When Confucius died in 479 BCE, his disciples planted trees over his grave. His descendants continued the tradition, generation by generation, for 2,500 years. The result is a forest of more than 100,000 trees covering 200 hectares — the oldest privately-maintained grove in China. The family planted specific species to mark different levels of the Kong family hierarchy; the oldest trees, near the original burial mound of Confucius himself, are thought to be over 1,000 years old.
---

The Cemetery of Confucius (Konglin, or Kong Forest) occupies 200 hectares north of Qufu's city wall — a 2.5-kilometre sacred avenue lined with ancient cypresses leads from the north gate to the burial area. Inside, the cemetery contains the graves of more than 100,000 members of the Kong family buried here over 2,500 years, with stone turtles, inscribed tablets, and ceremonial spirit paths in accumulating layers of historical complexity.

The grave of Confucius himself is a modest earthen mound at the center of the oldest section, behind an inscribed stone reading "The Throne of the Sage." The simplicity is intentional: the original grave was a simple mound, and the family chose to maintain this in contrast to the elaborate monuments of lesser figures around it. The forest is most atmospheric in autumn when the ancient ginkgos and cypresses turn color; it is also, counterintuitively, best early in the morning when the tourists have not yet arrived and the ancient trees have the silence they deserve. Allow two hours for a proper walk.
