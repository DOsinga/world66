story: >
  The Battle of Taierzhuang in March–April 1938 was the first significant Chinese ground victory over Japan in the war — a six-week engagement that killed over 20,000 Japanese soldiers. News of the victory was broadcast internationally and provided a temporary lift to Chinese morale at a time when the Nationalist government had already lost Beijing, Shanghai, and Nanjing. The battle's commander, Li Zongren, became a national hero, though he ended his life in exile.
---

The Battle of Taierzhuang Memorial Hall documents the 1938 engagement in careful detail: maps of the fighting over six weeks, photographs taken by war correspondents on both sides, weapons, personal effects, and accounts from participants. The hall is a 4A-rated national-level attraction and free to enter — one of the better war museums in eastern China in terms of historiographical seriousness. It neither sanitizes the violence nor retreats into triumphalism.

The memorial complex includes a panoramic painting gallery with a 360-degree representation of the battle, a monument to the fallen, and display sections on the foreign journalists who covered the fighting. The contrast between the careful historical record inside and the commerce of the reconstructed ancient town just outside the walls is worth sitting with. The site is on the south side of the canal, a short walk from the ancient town entrance.
