story: >
  Lu Ban (507–444 BCE) is the patron saint of Chinese craftsmen — carpenters, masons, and builders invoke his name today as they have for 2,500 years. He is credited with inventing the carpenter's saw, the square, the plane, and the ink-line marker, as well as designing the first siege machinery used in Chinese warfare. Zaozhuang claims him as a native son, and the claim has reasonable historical basis.
---

The Lu Ban Memorial Hall in Zaozhuang's Yicheng district honors the legendary craftsman of the Spring and Autumn period who became the patron of Chinese artisans. The memorial combines a biographical museum on Lu Ban's life and attributed inventions with demonstrations of traditional woodworking techniques that trace their lineage to the methods he supposedly developed.

For visitors interested in the history of technology and craft, the memorial offers a condensed introduction to how Chinese tradition remembers its great craftsman-innovators. The adjacent workshops occasionally host demonstrations of traditional carpentry. The site is in Yicheng district, about 20 kilometers from Zaozhuang's city center; accessible by local bus.
