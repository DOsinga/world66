Shanghai's hinterland — the Yangtze delta — is dense with classical canal towns and historic gardens, all reachable in under an hour by high-speed rail. [Suzhou](/asia/china/jiangsu_province/suzhou) (25 minutes by train) is the obvious one: a world heritage city of Ming-dynasty scholar gardens and silk workshops. [Hangzhou](/asia/china/zhejiang/hangzhou) (45 minutes) sits around the famously beautiful West Lake. The smaller water towns of [Zhouzhuang](/asia/china/jiangsu_province/zhouzhuang), [Wuzhen](/asia/china/zhejiang/wuzhen) and **Tongli** preserve canal-and-bridge layouts that look exactly like Ming dynasty paintings — best visited as half-day trips on weekdays before the bus tours arrive. [Nanjing](/asia/china/jiangsu_province/nanjing) (1 hour) is the old Ming capital, with city walls, Sun Yat-sen's mausoleum, and the sobering Nanjing Massacre Memorial.

Closer to home, and far quieter, [Xinchang](/asia/china/xinchang) is a Ming-Qing salt town in southern Pudong whose old street and stone bridges have escaped the tourist polish of the bigger canal towns — an easy, unhurried half day for anyone who has already seen the famous ones.
