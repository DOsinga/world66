Datong sits on the dry, windy plateau of northern Shanxi, close to the Mongolian border and far from the polished tourist circuits of eastern China. For centuries it was a frontier garrison town, and the landscape still has that stark, edge-of-empire feel.

The reason to come here is the [Yungang Grottoes](/asia/china/shanxiprovince/datong/yungang_grottoes), a complex of 53 cave temples carved into a sandstone cliff in the 5th century. The sheer scale of the carvings — some Buddha figures stand over 15 metres tall — makes this one of China's most impressive ancient sites. The old walled city centre has been extensively restored, with the [Nine Dragon Screen](/asia/china/shanxiprovince/datong/nine_dragon_screen) and the [Huayan Temple](/asia/china/shanxiprovince/datong/huayan_temple) and [Shanhua Temple](/asia/china/shanxiprovince/datong/shanhua_temple) worth a visit. Beyond town, the [Hanging Monastery](/asia/china/shanxiprovince/datong/hanging_monastery) at Hengshan clings improbably to a cliff face and can be combined with a day trip to the sacred peak itself.
