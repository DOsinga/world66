The Hukou Waterfall is the largest waterfall on the Yellow River and the second largest in China — not by height, but by the sheer volume and drama of a river 300 metres wide suddenly forcing itself through a gorge only 20 metres across before dropping 30 metres into the gorge below. The name means "flask mouth," and the analogy is apt: the river pours through the constriction with a roar audible a kilometre away, the water below turning white and spray-brown against the yellow loess cliffs. It sits on the Shanxi-Shaanxi provincial border, and the most practical access is from the Shanxi side via Linfen, about 150 km to the east.

The waterfall changes character dramatically with the seasons. In summer flood season the river is at its most violent, the spray creating permanent rainbow mist above the gorge and the water turning the pale yellow of dissolved loess. In winter, ice formations build along the cliffs and the flow narrows, exposing the raw geometry of the rock channel. Spring melt produces a middle ground that many consider the best combination of volume and clear light. There is no particularly bad time to visit — the falls are impressive year-round — but the dawn and dusk hours are notably better than midday when tour groups are at their densest.

The surrounding landscape is loess plateau country at its most stark and beautiful: eroded yellow cliffs, dry gullies, terraced fields cut into the hillsides, and the wide river making its bend through the canyon. Basic guesthouses operate near the falls for those who want to stay overnight and catch the waterfall in quieter morning light. From Linfen, the approach takes in characteristic Shanxi farming countryside — yaodong cave dwellings cut into the loess banks, apple and persimmon orchards, and the occasional coal-truck convoy that reminds you this is still working Shanxi.
