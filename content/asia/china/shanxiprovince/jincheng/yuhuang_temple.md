story: >
  The 28 lunar mansion sculptures at Yuhuang Temple are among the very few surviving examples of Song-dynasty three-dimensional astronomical representation in China. Each figure embodies one of the 28 constellations that Chinese astronomy used to divide the sky — corresponding roughly to the lunar mansions used in other Asian astronomical traditions. They were made by professional sculptors working in the Taoist iconographic tradition of the 12th century and were lost to scholarly attention for centuries before being identified in the early 20th century.
---

The Yuhuang Temple in Jincheng city is a large Song-dynasty Taoist complex whose most extraordinary feature is a set of polychrome clay sculptures representing the 28 lunar mansions and celestial officials arranged around the main hall. These are among the finest examples of Song religious carving anywhere in Shanxi, executed with a precision and expressiveness that reflects the high craft standards of the period. Each figure is individualised — different postures, costumes, and attributes — and the overall ensemble reads as a three-dimensional map of the Taoist cosmos.

Beyond the astronomical sculptures, the temple complex contains several large Song and Yuan halls in good structural condition, with surviving painted decoration on some of the interior surfaces. The main courtyard is pleasant and not overcrowded. The temple is walkable from the city centre, making it an easy starting point before or after the longer drive to the mountain village sites outside the city.
