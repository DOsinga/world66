  The Taiyuan Ancient Mosque is one of the oldest mosques in northern China, with a founding tradition tracing to the Tang dynasty when Arab and Central Asian Muslim traders settled along the Silk Road arteries that passed through Shanxi. The current buildings are Ming and Qing, but the community they serve has been continuous for over a thousand years — Taiyuan's Hui Muslim community is among the oldest settled Muslim populations in China.
---

The Taiyuan Ancient Mosque in the Xinghualing district is a well-preserved example of the Chinese mosque architectural tradition, in which Islamic religious spaces are designed in a Chinese timber-frame idiom rather than a Middle Eastern one. The entrance gate, main hall, and prayer spaces follow the same courtyard-and-axis organisation as a Buddhist temple or Confucian academy, with the Qibla direction (toward Mecca) determining the orientation of the main prayer hall rather than following the conventional Chinese east-west axis.

The mosque is still in active use by Taiyuan's Hui Muslim community and is not primarily a tourist site, though visitors are welcome outside prayer times. The courtyard garden is quiet and well-maintained, and the main prayer hall's carved wooden interior decoration — adapting Chinese decorative motifs to Islamic non-figurative convention — is worth seeing.
