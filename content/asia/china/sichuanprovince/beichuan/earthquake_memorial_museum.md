The 5/12 Wenchuan Earthquake Memorial Museum, opened in 2011 in the former Beichuan High School building near the ruins, documents the earthquake and its aftermath through photographs, personal effects, testimony, and technical data. The museum is well-organised and free to enter. It does not avoid the difficult aspects of the disaster — the building quality controversy, the gap between official and independent death toll estimates, the political tensions around parental grief — though it frames these in the language of reconstruction success rather than accountability failure.

The strongest sections are the documentary photography — the scale of the destruction across the whole earthquake zone, not just Beichuan — and the personal objects: schoolbags, shoes, letters, and the small objects that were found and preserved. The rescue operations section records the response in considerable detail, including military involvement, international assistance, and the improvised civilian rescue work that preceded the arrival of organised teams. Allow two to three hours for the full museum.
