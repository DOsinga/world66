**Traces of Heaven** by Paul Theroux (in *Riding the Iron Rooster*, 1988) — Theroux's account of crossing China by train in the 1980s includes time in Chengdu and Sichuan; its portrait of the province before the economic transformation is an invaluable before-and-after lens.

**The River at the Centre of the World** by Simon Winchester (1996) — Winchester travels the length of the Yangtze from Shanghai to Tibet, passing through Sichuan. An intelligent, enthusiastic account of a changing China.

**Land of Fish and Rice** by Fuchsia Dunlop (2016) — Dunlop is the most authoritative writer on Chinese food for Western audiences, and this volume covers Jiangnan cooking. Her earlier book *Sichuan Cookery* (2001) is the essential reference for the food that defines Chengdu.

**Oracle Bones** by Peter Hessler (2006) — Hessler spent years in China as a writer for the New Yorker; his account of the country's transformation in the 2000s includes considerable time in Sichuan and remains one of the best portraits of the country's modernisation.
