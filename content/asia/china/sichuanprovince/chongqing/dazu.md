Dazu is a workaday district west of central Chongqing with one extraordinary reason to go: the Dazu Rock Carvings, a UNESCO-listed group of cliffside sculptures carved from the 9th to the 13th centuries. The best panels are not just rows of Buddhas. They mix Buddhist, Daoist, Confucian, and everyday subjects with a confidence that makes the stone feel almost theatrical.

Most visitors come for [Baodingshan](/asia/china/sichuanprovince/chongqing/dazu/baodingshan_rock_carvings), the most famous and best-presented site. It has the grand set pieces: the wheel of life, hell scenes, the reclining Buddha, and the Thousand-Hand Guanyin. [Beishan](/asia/china/sichuanprovince/chongqing/dazu/beishan_rock_carvings), closer to town, is quieter and rewards slower looking, with dense Tang and Song dynasty niches along the cliff.

The wider district matters because the carvings are spread across several hills. [Nanshan](/asia/china/sichuanprovince/chongqing/dazu/nanshan_rock_carvings) is strongest for Daoist sculpture, while [Shimenshan](/asia/china/sichuanprovince/chongqing/dazu/shimenshan_rock_carvings) shows the same habit of mixing traditions in one carved landscape. Add the [Dazu Rock Carvings Museum](/asia/china/sichuanprovince/chongqing/dazu/dazu_rock_carvings_museum) if you want context before or after Baodingshan.

Dazu can be done as a long day from Chongqing, but it is better if you stay overnight and see Beishan without rushing. Buses and high-speed rail reach the district, then local buses and taxis handle the last legs to the carving sites.
