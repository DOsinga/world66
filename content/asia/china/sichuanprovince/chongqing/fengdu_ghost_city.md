  According to legend, Fengdu became the city of ghosts through a naming accident. During the Han dynasty, two officials named Wang and Yin lived here as hermits on Ming Mountain. Their combined name — Yinwang, meaning "King of Hell" — was taken literally by later generations, and the mountain gradually accumulated temples, statues, and rituals associated with death and the afterlife. The original town of Fengdu was submerged by the Three Gorges Dam reservoir; the ghost city on the mountain survived.

Fengdu Ghost City sits on Ming Mountain above the Yangtze River, about 170 kilometres downstream from Chongqing. It is a complex of temples, pavilions, and statues dedicated to the Chinese concept of hell — a vivid, sometimes alarming vision of what awaits the wicked after death, with graphic depictions of torture administered to the souls of the damned.

The site has been a place of pilgrimage and spiritual tourism for over a thousand years. It expanded rapidly after the original town of Fengdu was flooded by the Three Gorges Dam reservoir and residents were relocated. Most Yangtze cruise itineraries include a stop here, and the site is a highlight for those interested in Chinese folk religion and the remarkable sincerity with which hell is depicted. Cable cars operate to the summit. The views back over the Yangtze are excellent.
