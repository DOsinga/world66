The Huguang Guild Hall (湖广会馆) is a complex of Qing dynasty buildings on the south bank of the Yangtze, among the best-preserved examples of this architectural type in China. Guild halls served as community centres for merchants and migrants from particular regions — a combination of business association, temple, theatre, and guesthouse. This one was built in the early 18th century to serve immigrants from Hunan and Hubei provinces.

The complex includes several interconnected courtyards, a theatre stage where opera performances are still staged, and temple buildings dedicated to various deities. A museum within the complex traces the history of the Great Migration into Sichuan — one of the largest population movements in Chinese history. The tiled rooflines, carved timber screens, and stone courtyards are lovely. Allow 90 minutes.
