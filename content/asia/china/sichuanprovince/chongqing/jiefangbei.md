  The Liberation Monument at the centre of Jiefangbei was originally built in 1945 to commemorate the end of the Anti-Japanese War — then demolished and rebuilt in 1950 after the Communist victory. It is now surrounded by the most expensive retail real estate in inland China, with flagship stores of every major luxury brand arrayed around its base. The monument once dominated the skyline; now you can barely see it through the glass towers.

Jiefangbei (解放碑, Liberation Monument) is the commercial heart of Chongqing and one of the busiest shopping districts in interior China. The clock tower monument at its centre gives the district its name, and the pedestrian streets radiating outward are lined with department stores, restaurants, and the flagship outlets of Chinese and international brands.

It is worth visiting as much for the spectacle as for the shopping. The density of people, lights, and commerce is intense — particularly at night when the whole district blazes with LED screens and neon. Below the streets, the Hongya Cave complex on the Jialing riverfront is a vertically stacked shopping and restaurant development built into the cliff face, made famous by a photograph that circulated widely online comparing it to the bathhouse in the film Spirited Away.
