story: >
  Jinyun Mountain's forest has been protected since the Tang dynasty, when a Buddhist monastery established on the summit imposed a prohibition on tree-cutting in the surrounding area. The prohibition was enforced for over a millennium, which is why the mountain retains one of the largest areas of old-growth subtropical broadleaf forest in the Chongqing region — an ecological legacy of a religious conservation ethic.
---

Jinyun Mountain National Nature Reserve, about 50 kilometres north of central Chongqing in Beibei district, protects a remnant patch of subtropical broadleaf forest that is among the oldest and most diverse in the region. The reserve is home to over 1,700 plant species and is particularly noted for its metasequoia — the dawn redwood, a "living fossil" tree species once known only from fossils before living trees were discovered in China in 1944. Jinyun is one of the few places where they grow naturally.

The reserve has walking trails at various levels of difficulty, from easy forest walks to a climb to the summit ridge at 952 metres. Several Buddhist and Taoist temples are scattered through the forest. The mountain is a reliable cool refuge from Chongqing's summer heat and is popular with locals for this reason. Combined with a visit to Beibei's hot springs, it makes an excellent full-day outing.
