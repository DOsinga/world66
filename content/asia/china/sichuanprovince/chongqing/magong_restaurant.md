story: >
  Mao Zedong was famously fond of red-braised pork (红烧肉, hóngshāo ròu) — belly pork slow-cooked in soy sauce, rice wine, and sugar until it collapses into glossy, trembling slabs. His cooks developed a version with chilies that became the signature dish of Hunan cuisine. The dish is now found in restaurants across China under various names claiming Mao's heritage, each one asserting its version is the authentic original.
---

Chongqing sits close to the Hunan border and Hunanese cuisine is well-represented in the city alongside the local Sichuan-Chongqing tradition. Mao-style Hunan restaurants serve the dishes associated with China's revolutionary history: red-braised pork, steamed preserved pork with dried mustard greens, and fiery dishes built on fresh rather than dried chilies.

The Hunan style differs from Sichuan in important ways: the heat comes from fresh chilies and garlic rather than from Sichuan peppercorn, and the dishes tend to be oilier and more pungent. Look for restaurants in the Guanyinqiao area of Jiangbei or along the Nanbin Road south bank strip, where the concentration of good restaurants makes it easy to explore different styles.
