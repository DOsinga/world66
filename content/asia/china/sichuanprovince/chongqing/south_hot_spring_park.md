  The South Hot Spring was developed during the Republican period as a resort for officials and wealthy families — it was far enough from the city to feel removed from the wartime chaos while remaining accessible. Chiang Kai-shek used it as one of his retreats. The natural hot spring pools, at temperatures ranging from 36 to 42°C, were among the few genuine luxuries available in wartime Chongqing.

The South Hot Spring Park (南温泉) is a public park and hot spring resort about 18 kilometres south of central Chongqing. Natural hot spring water feeds the pools and bathhouses throughout the park, which is set in a river valley with bamboo groves and forested hills above.

The park is busiest on winter weekends, when Chongqing's notoriously damp and chilly weather makes hot spring bathing particularly appealing. There are public pools at various temperatures, as well as private bathing rooms and full spa facilities. The surrounding park has walking trails along the river, with some pleasant karst scenery in the valley sides. Easy to reach by metro and bus from the city centre.
