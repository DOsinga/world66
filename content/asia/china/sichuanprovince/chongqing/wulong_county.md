  The Wulong karst landscape was selected as a UNESCO World Heritage Site as part of the South China Karst inscription in 2007. The assessment described it as "one of the world's most spectacular examples of humid tropical to subtropical karst landscapes." The combination of natural bridges, sinkholes, and cave systems concentrated in a relatively small area is extraordinary — geologically speaking, this landscape has very few equals anywhere on earth.
---

Wulong County, about 200 kilometres southeast of Chongqing, contains some of the most dramatic karst scenery in China. The UNESCO-listed landscape includes the Three Natural Bridges (three enormous stone arches spanning a river gorge), the Furong Cave (one of China's finest show caves), and the Longshui Gorge, a canyon cut through limestone with sections navigable on foot.

The area has been developed for tourism but remains relatively uncrowded compared to more famous Chinese scenic areas. The combination of geological features concentrated in a manageable area makes it an excellent full-day or overnight trip from Chongqing. High-speed rail runs to Wulong station, from which shuttle buses serve the main sites. Spring and autumn are the best seasons, when the vegetation in the gorges is at its most vivid.
