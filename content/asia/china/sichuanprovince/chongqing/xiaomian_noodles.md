Chongqing xiaomian (小面, small noodles) is the city's breakfast, lunch, and late-night dish — thin noodles in a heavily spiced broth with complex layers of heat and savour. Unlike hotpot, which is social and expensive, xiaomian is fast, cheap, and eaten everywhere, including at small street counters where the cook manages six or eight pots simultaneously.

The broth relies on chili oil and Sichuan peppercorns for heat and numbing, but what distinguishes good xiaomian is the secondary seasonings: the quality of the soy sauce, the ratio of vinegar, the fresh scallions and peanuts on top. Order at the counter, state your preferred heat level (even mild is fairly spicy), and find a seat. The noodles arrive in under five minutes. Many visitors eat it every morning for breakfast.
