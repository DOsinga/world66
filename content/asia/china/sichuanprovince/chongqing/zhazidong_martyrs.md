  Zhazidong (渣滓洞) was a Kuomintang detention centre on the outskirts of Chongqing where hundreds of Communist Party members and left-wing activists were imprisoned and many executed in the years before 1949. The site became famous through the novel Red Crag (红岩, Hóng Yán), published in 1961, which fictionalised the experiences of prisoners here. The novel was a massive bestseller and shaped popular understanding of the Communist martyrs for a generation.
---

The Zhazidong Martyrs Memorial preserves a former Kuomintang detention centre in the hills west of Chongqing where political prisoners — mostly Communist Party members and sympathisers — were held and executed in the late 1940s as the Civil War turned against the Nationalist government.

The memorial museum reconstructs the prison conditions and tells the stories of specific prisoners through photographs and personal effects. The exhibition emphasises sacrifice and revolutionary commitment in a style typical of Chinese revolutionary memorials, but the physical site — the actual cells, the interrogation rooms — has a genuine historical weight. The memorial is set in hills outside the city centre and can be combined with a visit to nearby Gele Mountain park.
