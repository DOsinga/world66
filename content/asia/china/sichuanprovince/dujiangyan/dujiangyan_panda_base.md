The Dujiangyan facility of the China Conservation and Research Center for Giant Panda is a different operation from the well-known Chengdu Research Base. Where Chengdu is a polished tourist attraction with large crowds and a fixed viewing circuit, Dujiangyan is primarily a research and rewilding centre where pandas live in larger, wilder enclosures and some are being trained to return to nature. The tourist infrastructure is intentionally modest.

The main draw for visitors is the volunteer programme, which lets you spend a morning working alongside keepers — preparing food, cleaning enclosures, and spending close-up time with the animals. This is one of the few places in China where paying visitors can get genuine hands-on access to pandas rather than just photographing them through glass. Booking in advance is essential; slots fill quickly and walk-ins are rarely accommodated.

Non-volunteer visits are also possible on a standard admission ticket, with viewing of outdoor enclosures where pandas go about their business largely undisturbed. The facility sits north of the city centre; taxi or DiDi is the easiest way to reach it.
