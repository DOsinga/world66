Ganzi (also spelled Garzê) is a rough Tibetan town at 3,400 metres on the high plateau of western Sichuan, and it functions primarily as a staging post for exploring the surrounding region. The bus connections radiate out in all directions — south to Litang, north to Dege, east back to Kangding — and Ganzi sits at the hub of a network of routes through the Kham region, historically one of Tibet's three traditional provinces. It is the kind of place where travellers tend to arrive planning one night and stay three.

The [Ganzi Monastery](/asia/china/sichuanprovince/ganzi/ganzi_monastery) is the town's centrepiece, a large working monastery with murals and a gilded roofline visible from across the valley. Entry is free and the monastery is active — you are as likely to encounter monks conducting a ceremony as you are tourists taking photographs. More extraordinary, and considerably harder to reach, is [Yarchen Gar Monastery](/asia/china/sichuanprovince/ganzi/yarchen_gar) about 80 kilometres south of Ganzi, one of the largest Buddhist academies in the world. Thousands of monks and nuns live in a sprawling settlement of small wooden dwellings on the plateau, and the scale of it — a temporary-looking city devoted entirely to religious practice — is unlike anything else in China.

The [surrounding grasslands](/asia/china/sichuanprovince/ganzi/ganzi_grasslands) around Tagong and Maren, accessible from routes through the region, are high-altitude pastoral country where Tibetan nomadic herders still move with their yak herds through the summer months. Horse riding is available and the landscape on clear days, with snowpeaks cutting the horizon, is genuinely spectacular. The physical demands of travel here should not be underestimated — the elevation affects everyone differently, and buses on mountain roads at this altitude take far longer than distances suggest.

Ganzi has basic accommodation and restaurants. The local food runs to yak meat hot pot and butter tea, both of which require some acclimatisation of taste as well as altitude. The town has limited internet access and ATMs may not accept foreign cards — the practical rhythms of western Sichuan apply here as they do throughout the plateau.
