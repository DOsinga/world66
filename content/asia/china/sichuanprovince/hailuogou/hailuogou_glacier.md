  Hailuogou glacier descends from Minya Konka to approximately 2,900 metres above
  sea level — lower than almost any glacier in Asia outside the Himalayas. It reaches
  this altitude because Minya Konka receives extraordinarily heavy snowfall from monsoon
  moisture that rises against the eastern face of the plateau. The glacier's snout is
  visible from within forested terrain that elsewhere would have no ice nearby.
  In the 1930s, Joseph Rock and a party of Western explorers came here specifically
  to attempt Minya Konka; several members of a subsequent expedition died on its slopes.

The Hailuogou glacier is the central feature of the park and the reason most people make the journey. The glacier descends from the permanent ice fields of Minya Konka to an unusually low altitude — you can stand at the glacier's lower terminus in a landscape that still has large trees and hear the creak and crack of ice movement above you. The approach from the entrance involves shuttle buses and then a walkway that leads to the viewing platform and glacier access point.

At close range the glacier presents a wall of blue-white ice riddled with crevasses and topped by seracs — unstable ice towers that break periodically and sometimes audibly. The surface ice is grey with moraine debris but the interior, where it catches the light, has the particular deep blue that makes glacial ice unlike any other substance. Do not attempt to walk on the glacier without authorised guidance; the surface crevasses are real hazards. The cable car provides an alternative elevated view and is the better option for those with less mobility or time.

Clear mornings — typically before 10 a.m. before cloud builds from the valley — offer the best light on both the glacier and the peak above. Bring layers regardless of the season; the temperature drops significantly at the glacier's altitude even in summer.
