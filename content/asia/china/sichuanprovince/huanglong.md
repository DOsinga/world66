Huanglong — officially Huanglongsi National Park — is a UNESCO World Heritage Site in northern Sichuan, about 60 kilometres north of Songpan, featuring a valley of travertine mineral pools that descend a limestone mountainside in vivid shades of turquoise, gold, and white. The deposits have accumulated over millennia as calcium-rich water flows down from the snowfields above, leaving behind terraced formations that look, in photographs, almost too saturated to be real. In person the colour is genuine and the scale is larger than images convey.

The park's main feature is a [4.2-kilometre wooden boardwalk](/asia/china/sichuanprovince/huanglong/huanglong_boardwalk) that climbs about 700 metres from the entrance at roughly 3,000 metres to the [Five Colored Pool](/asia/china/sichuanprovince/huanglong/five_colored_pool) near the summit at 3,700 metres. The ascent takes two to three hours and passes pools, a small Buddhist temple, and nearly horizontal waterfalls that are rare enough to be worth stopping for. The cable car, available for ¥80 one-way, covers the upper section and is worthwhile for acclimatisation or on the descent. Budget four to six hours for the full circuit.

The altitude is the principal consideration. The entrance alone is at 3,000 metres and most visitors arrive by bus from Jiuzhaigou or Chengdu having not spent much time at elevation. Altitude sickness is common — headaches and breathlessness are normal, more serious symptoms require descent. The park is a day trip only, with no accommodation available inside, and the last bus back to Jiuzhaigou departs in mid-afternoon.

Entry costs ¥200 between April and November. Outside those months, when the pools take on a different, frost-edged quality and visitor numbers drop sharply, the price falls to ¥60 — a genuinely good deal for one of China's more extraordinary natural sites. Bring food and water from town; prices at the few concessions inside the park are punitive.
