The Thousand-Buddha Cliff is a long sandstone escarpment along the ancient Shu Road between Mingyang and Guangyuan, carved with Buddhist figures that accumulated over several dynasties. The name is modest — there are significantly more than a thousand individual figures, ranging from small votive niches containing a single Buddha to large compositions with multiple figures and attendants. The most impressive sections have figures reaching several metres in height.

The cliff site is less famous internationally than the Leshan Giant Buddha but is arguably more historically evocative because it sits directly alongside the road that gave rise to its existence. The carvings were made by people travelling one of the most significant routes in Chinese history, at a time when the journey was genuinely dangerous and the walls of rock above the gorge provided both the material and the motivation for religious expression. The integration of the carvings into the living landscape of the gorge — where the road still runs and where vegetation grows in and around the niches — gives it a quality that more formally preserved sites sometimes lack.

Access from the main Jianmen park requires a short detour toward Guangyuan and is best managed with private transport or a taxi from Jiange.
