Kangding sits at 2,600 metres where the Sichuan plain abruptly ends and the Tibetan plateau begins. It is the largest city in western Sichuan and has served for centuries as the principal trading post between Han China and Tibet — a role you can still sense in its streets, where Tibetan monks, Yi minorities, and Han shopkeepers share the same narrow lanes. The air is noticeably thinner than Chengdu and the relief from that city's haze and density is almost physical.

The town is famous across China through a 1939 folk song, Kang Ding Qing Ge, which romanticises its mountain setting and the love lives of its inhabitants. [Paoma Mountain](/asia/china/sichuanprovince/kangding/paoma_mountain), the forested peak that rises directly above the centre, is the subject of that song and an easy climb — either on foot in half an hour or by cable car. A temple complex on the summit looks out over the Zheduo River gorge and the surrounding snowpeaks that define the skyline in every direction.

[Mugecuo National Park](/asia/china/sichuanprovince/kangding/mugecuo_park), 21 kilometres out of town, is Kangding's best day trip: an alpine plateau at 3,200 metres ringed by glaciated peaks, with lakes, grasslands, and forest. The park is far less visited than Jiuzhaigou and rewards those willing to walk rather than stick to the shuttle bus circuit. Back in town, the hot spring resort at Er Dao Qiao offers an unremarkable but welcome soak after a day at altitude.

Kangding is primarily useful as a transit point for deeper western Sichuan — the gateway to [Dege](/asia/china/sichuanprovince/dege), [Ganzi](/asia/china/sichuanprovince/ganzi), and the Tibetan heartland — but it is worth an overnight stay in its own right. The [Xinshi Dian Jie](/asia/china/sichuanprovince/kangding/xinshi_dian_jie) street sells Tibetan jewellery, silverware, and religious objects of genuine quality, and the evening square dancing around the central plaza is a peculiarly Chinese form of communal life that Kangding performs with particular enthusiasm.
