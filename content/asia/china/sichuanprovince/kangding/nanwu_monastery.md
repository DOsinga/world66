Nanwu Monastery, the largest and oldest in Kangding, sits on the western edge of town about 2 kilometres from the centre. Founded in the seventeenth century, it belongs to the Nyingma school of Tibetan Buddhism and has been the principal religious institution of the Kangding valley through centuries of political change. The main assembly hall is substantial, with a collection of large gilded Buddhas and well-preserved murals in the Kham style. The outer walls bear carved mani stones accumulated over centuries.

The monastery grounds are quiet and accessible by foot from the centre of Kangding along the river path. Unlike the more tourist-focused sites in some Chinese cities, Nanwu is a working monastery where the daily routines of monastic life — prayers, study, maintenance — are visible to respectful visitors. The best time to visit is early morning, when the low light catches the gilded rooftops and the sound of prayers from the assembly hall drifts across the courtyard. Entry is free.

From the monastery grounds, the view up the Zheduo River valley — with the high peaks of the plateau closing off the western end — gives a clear sense of why Kangding has always felt like the end of one world and the beginning of another.
