---
title: Getting There
type: section
---

Langmusi is genuinely remote and the journey in is part of the experience. There is no airport nearby and no high-speed rail.

The two most common approaches are from **Xiahe in Gansu** and from **Jiuzhaigou in Sichuan**. From Xiahe (home of Labrang Monastery, a major Tibetan Buddhist site), buses or shared taxis take 3–4 hours on a road that climbs across high grasslands before dropping into the Langmusi valley. This is the route most travellers take if doing a Gansu-Tibet circuit.

From [Jiuzhaigou](/asia/china/sichuanprovince/jiuzhaigou) or Songpan to the south, buses run roughly daily and take 4–5 hours. The road passes through some of northern Sichuan's most characteristic plateau terrain. This approach makes Langmusi a natural extension of a Sichuan itinerary including Jiuzhaigou and Huanglong.

From Chengdu the journey is long — typically 10–12 hours with changes — and most efficiently broken at either Jiuzhaigou or Songpan. There is no direct long-distance bus from Chengdu to Langmusi.

Langmusi village itself is tiny and everything is walkable. There are no taxis or moto-taxis. Guesthouses and small hostels cluster along the main village street on both sides of the provincial boundary.
