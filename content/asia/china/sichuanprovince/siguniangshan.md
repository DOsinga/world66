Siguniang Mountain — Four Girls Mountain in translation, named for the four peaks that dominate its skyline — is a UNESCO World Heritage Site 220 kilometres west of Chengdu in the Qionglai Mountains, offering some of the most dramatic alpine scenery in Sichuan without requiring the multi-day journey that the Tibetan plateau destinations demand. The highest peak reaches 6,250 metres and is one of the more serious technical climbs in China; the surrounding terrain, accessible through three separate valleys, is open to ordinary visitors.

[Shuangqiao Valley](/asia/china/sichuanprovince/siguniangshan/shuangqiao_valley) is the most visited, with a 32-kilometre boardwalk and paved road access, a shuttle bus circuit, and mountain views that work well in clear weather. [Changping Valley](/asia/china/sichuanprovince/siguniangshan/changping_valley) is longer, wilder, and motorised vehicles are not permitted — a six-kilometre boardwalk through old-growth forest leads to progressively more remote terrain, and beyond the boardwalk the path continues toward high-altitude grazing areas. [Haizi Valley](/asia/china/sichuanprovince/siguniangshan/haizi_valley), closest to the main town of Rilong, is a short hike with several glacial lakes and is the most practical option for those with limited time or energy.

Wildlife in the park is real rather than nominal. Sichuan golden monkeys, clouded leopards, and occasional giant pandas have all been documented here, and the bird population includes bearded vultures and Chinese monal pheasants. The chances of seeing large mammals are low but not negligible, particularly in the less-trafficked valleys early in the morning. The best seasons run from spring through autumn; winter temperatures drop to -15°C and summer afternoons bring reliable thunderstorms.

Rilong town, the base for the park, has accommodation ranging from basic guesthouses to mid-range hotels, and is accessible from Chengdu by bus in about five hours via Dujiangyan and Wolong. The Wolong Giant Panda Research Base sits on the same route and adds a logical reason to break the journey. Valley entrance fees run from ¥40 in off-season to ¥80 in peak season — reasonable given the quality of the scenery.
