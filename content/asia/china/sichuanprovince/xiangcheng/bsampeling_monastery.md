  Bsampeling Monastery's full name is Ganden Sampe Norbuling — roughly, "The Wish-Fulfilling Jewel Garden of Ganden." It was built in 1669 on the site of a much older Kagyu school monastery, and the yellow hat (Gelug) school that replaced it was in some cases quite literally built over the foundations of the earlier tradition. The monks here are part of the Dalai Lama lineage, a fact that gave the monastery a complicated status through the twentieth century.
---

Bsampeling Monastery sits on the hillside above Xiangcheng town, a traditional complex covering more than 20,000 square metres of monks' residences, assembly halls, and Buddhist shrines reached by a prayer-flag-lined path climbing from the valley floor. The architecture is traditional Tibetan — whitewashed stone walls, red-banded upper stories, gilded roof ornaments — and recently enough constructed to be in good repair while old enough to feel genuinely lived-in.

The monastery is active, with resident monks whose daily schedule structures the complex's rhythms. Admission is ¥15 and the fee supports the monastery directly. The murals in the main assembly hall, painted in a style that mixes Tibetan and Chinese iconographic traditions, are particularly fine. The hillside position gives views down the valley to the white cubic houses below — a view worth lingering over, particularly in the late afternoon when the light catches the mountains behind the town.
