Simatai is one of the few sections of the Great Wall that has been left largely unrestored, and that is exactly its appeal. The wall here is steep and crumbling in places, with watchtowers perched on narrow ridges that drop away sharply on both sides. It feels like the real thing in a way that the polished sections at Badaling never will. Located about 120 kilometres northeast of [Beijing](/asia/china/beijing), in Miyun District, Simatai offers the Great Wall experience that most visitors imagine but rarely find.

A hiking trail connects Simatai to the [Jinshanling](jinshanling) section to the west, a roughly three-hour walk along the wall with some of the best views anywhere on the structure. The terrain is genuinely demanding — some sections involve scrambling on steep, crumbling stone, and a few towers are accessible only to confident walkers. This is not the wall you visit with a tour bus; it rewards those who come prepared.

Since 2014, visits require advance reservations and visitor numbers are capped, which keeps the crowds manageable. Simatai is also one of the only sections of the Great Wall open for night visits, when the watchtowers are lit up against the dark mountains. The ancient town of [Gubei Water Town](/asia/china/simatai/gubei_water_town) at the base of the wall makes a convenient overnight stop and lets you get up on the wall early in the morning before the day visitors arrive.
