Shigatse is the second largest city in Tibet, sitting at an elevation of 3,840 metres in the Yarlung Tsangpo valley. It is the seat of the Panchen Lama, and the [Tashilhunpo Monastery](/asia/china/tibet/shigatse/tashilhunpo_monastery) — built in 1447 — is the reason most travellers come here. At its height the monastery housed more than 4,000 monks; today around 600 remain, and the great gilded [mausoleum halls](/asia/china/tibet/shigatse/panchen_lama_mausoleum) are among the most impressive religious structures in Tibet.

The [Shigatse Dzong](/asia/china/tibet/shigatse/shigatse_dzong), the old fortress partially destroyed during the Cultural Revolution, sits atop a rocky hilltop above the city. The silhouette of the reconstructed walls against the mountains is a striking landmark. The modern city below is a Chinese administrative centre with little character, but the [old Tibetan quarter](/asia/china/tibet/shigatse/shigatse_traditional_street) around the monastery still has the feel of traditional life, with pilgrims spinning prayer wheels and a good [Tibetan market](/asia/china/tibet/shigatse/tibetan_market) nearby.

Shigatse lies about 280 kilometres southwest of [Lhasa](/asia/china/tibet/lhasa) on the Friendship Highway, which continues west to the Nepal border at Gyirong. Most travellers visit as part of an overland journey rather than staying long. Day trips to [Gyantse](/asia/china/tibet/gyantse), [Shalu Monastery](/asia/china/tibet/shigatse/shalu_monastery), and [Narthang Monastery](/asia/china/tibet/shigatse/narthang_monastery) are all feasible from here.
