Xinchang (新场) is a water town that most visitors to Shanghai never reach, which is precisely its appeal. It sits in the southern reaches of the Pudong New Area, close enough to be a genuine day trip and far enough from the famous canal towns of the western suburbs to have escaped their crowds, their ticket gates, and their souvenir uniformity. The town grew up around the salt trade during the Yuan and Ming dynasties, when this stretch of low coastal plain produced much of the salt that made the region wealthy, and the merchant houses and stone bridges that money built are still standing.

The heart of Xinchang is its old street, a long lane of timber-fronted shops, tea houses, and food stalls running beside a narrow canal. The waterway is crossed by original stone arch bridges, some of them several centuries old, and lined with courtyard houses whose carved wooden facades and private jetties have survived more or less intact. Because the town was never comprehensively restored for tourism, the wear is real: peeling whitewash, laundry strung over the water, old residents playing cards in doorways. It looks much closer to what the Yangtze Delta water towns actually were before they were polished for visitors.

If you have already seen Zhujiajiao or one of the bigger Jiangnan towns and want something quieter, Xinchang rewards an unhurried half day. Wander the old street for the snacks — sticky rice, fresh tofu, sweet osmanthus cakes — pause at a canal-side tea house, and let the pace of the place set your own. Film crews have noticed it too; parts of Ang Lee's *Lust, Caution* were shot here, drawn by the same unguarded authenticity.

Xinchang is best visited on a weekday, when the lanes are close to empty. Reach it by Metro Line 16 to Xinchang station followed by a short local bus or taxi, or drive south from central Pudong in under an hour. There is little need to plan beyond arriving; the town is small, walkable, and made for drifting.
