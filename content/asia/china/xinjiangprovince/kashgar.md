The oasis of Kashgar, with a view of the last peaks of the western Himalayas, is the last stop in China on the Silk Road. From here you can cross to [Pakistan](/asia/pakistan) over the [Karakoram Highway](/asia/china/xinjiangprovince/kashgar/karakoram_highway) or to [Kyrgyzstan](/asia/kyrgyzstan) over the Torugart Pass.

Kashgar is famous for its [Sunday livestock market](/asia/china/xinjiangprovince/kashgar/sunday_livestock_market). It is big — no, it is huge. You will feel like you are back in the heyday of the Silk trade. Everywhere, donkeys are sold and taken for a test drive, sheep are bought and put on the back of a bicycle to take them home. Fur hats, silver daggers, and ancient Korans are on display in small shops. Men and women shout as they haggle over prices.

The [old town (Kona Sheher)](/asia/china/xinjiangprovince/kashgar/old_town), centred on the [Id Kah Mosque](/asia/china/xinjiangprovince/kashgar/id_kah_mosque), is one of the few remaining Uyghur neighbourhoods in Xinjiang that still has something of its original character, though much has been rebuilt. The [Afaq Khoja Mausoleum](/asia/china/xinjiangprovince/kashgar/afaq_khoja_mausoleum) — the holiest Islamic site in Xinjiang, with its brilliant tiled dome — lies five kilometres northeast of the centre. Getting something to eat is simple: the [food stalls around Id Kah Square](/asia/china/xinjiangprovince/kashgar/id_kah_square_food_stalls) do shish kebab, lagman noodles, and samsa pastries from early morning until late at night. Rent a bike and ride out into the oasis — the landscape is flat and green, the people friendly, and there are a few saints' tombs to visit along the way.

Kashgar was also a centre of remarkable intellectual life: both [Yusuf Khass Hajib](/asia/china/xinjiangprovince/kashgar/yusuf_khass_hajib_mausoleum), who composed a great philosophical treatise in Turkic here in 1069, and [Mahmud Kashgari](/asia/china/xinjiangprovince/kashgar/mahmud_kashgari_mausoleum), who compiled the first systematic dictionary of Turkic languages, lived and worked here. Their mausoleums are among the most significant cultural sites in the region.
