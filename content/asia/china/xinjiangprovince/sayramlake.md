Sayram Lake lies at 2,073 metres in Bortala Prefecture, near the Kazakhstan border in northwestern Xinjiang, on the main road between Yining and Ürümqi. It is the largest alpine lake in Xinjiang — about 45 kilometres long and brilliantly blue in the manner of high-altitude bodies of water with little suspended sediment. The surrounding landscape is grassland and bare ridgeline with almost no tree cover, giving views across the lake that feel enormous. In early summer, wildflowers fill the margins and the snowfields on the peaks above the western shore are still white against the sky.

The lake's character is defined by the Kazakh pastoralists who have grazed their animals here for generations. In summer, yurts appear around the shore as families move up from the valley towns for the grazing season. The pastoral economy is genuine rather than performative: the horses, sheep, and cattle are here because the grass is here, not because tourists want to photograph them. [Horse riding can be arranged directly with local families](/asia/china/xinjiangprovince/sayramlake/kazakh_horseback). The lake has not been heavily commercialised — facilities are minimal, accommodation is yurts and basic guesthouses, and the experience depends almost entirely on the landscape itself.

The road from [Yining](/asia/china/xinjiangprovince/yining) runs east through the [Guozigou valley](/asia/china/xinjiangprovince/sayramlake/guozigou_valley) — a dramatic gorge with a river running through it and tunnels cut through the cliff faces — before climbing to the lake. This valley road is itself worth the journey: it alternates between narrow walls of rock, sudden openings onto meadows, and views down to the river. From the lake, the highway continues east through a high pass and descends toward Ürümqi, a total drive of about seven to nine hours from the city. The lake makes a natural break in the journey and a reason to choose the northern route through the Tian Shan rather than the faster southern option.

June through September is the best time to visit; outside those months the altitude makes conditions genuinely cold and accommodation scarce. Sayram Lake is well served by the G30 expressway and G312 national highway, both of which skirt the lake's eastern shore. From Yining the drive is about two hours; a hop-on hop-off bus service loops the lake during summer.
