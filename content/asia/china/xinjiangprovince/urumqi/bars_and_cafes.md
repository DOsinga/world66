---
title: "Bars and Cafes"
type: section
---

Ürümqi's nightlife is limited, and that is not surprising given the city's social context. The Uyghur population is predominantly Muslim, and the security environment since 2017 has made public gatherings feel less spontaneous than they once did. That said, the city is not joyless: the Han quarter has Western-style bars and coffee chains, and the Erdaoqiao area has something far more interesting.

The [Uyghur teahouses](/asia/china/xinjiangprovince/urumqi/uyghur_teahouses) near the bazaar are the most atmospheric place to sit down in the city. Green tea arrives with a small plate of dried fruit — raisins, apricots, walnuts — and life outside carries on at its own pace. These are not tourist traps; they are where local men come to sit and talk, and you are welcome to join the rhythm. No alcohol is served, and none is needed.

For a conventional bar experience, the streets around Nanmen (South Gate) and the university districts in the north have the usual range of beer bars and karaoke establishments that appear in any Chinese city of this size. They are unremarkable but functional if you want a cold beer at the end of a long day.
