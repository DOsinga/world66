  Khorgos is one of the busiest land border crossings between China and Central Asia, and its recent transformation into a special economic zone reflects China's ambition to revive the Silk Road as a trade corridor. The border market — technically in a duty-free zone straddling the Chinese and Kazakh sides — allows Chinese and Kazakh citizens to cross without visas for shopping. It is, in a compressed and thoroughly commercial form, exactly what the Silk Road was: a place where goods moved between very different economies.
---

Khorgos, about 90 kilometres west of Yining near the Kazakh border, has grown rapidly as a special economic zone and international trade hub. The combination of a China–Kazakhstan free-trade zone, a new railway connection to Kazakhstan, and Chinese investment in the Belt and Road Initiative has turned a small border crossing into a significant commercial centre within a few years.

For the traveller, Khorgos offers a glimpse of how the Silk Road functions in the 21st century: lorries queuing at the border, container yards, a duty-free shopping district where Chinese tourists buy Kazakh goods and Kazakh traders buy Chinese electronics. It is not a beautiful place, but it is a genuinely interesting one — a hinge between two economic systems and two cultures, animated by exactly the commercial logic that drove the original Silk Road.

The border crossing is open to third-country nationals under normal tourist visa conditions. Crossing into Kazakhstan through Khorgos is a straightforward way to continue west if your itinerary takes you there.
