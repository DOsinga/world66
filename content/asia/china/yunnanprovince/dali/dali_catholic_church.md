story: "Built by French missionaries in 1927, Dali's Catholic Church is an unusual hybrid: a fully functional Gothic Revival cathedral floor plan executed entirely in traditional Bai architectural style. The roof is upturned tile, the eaves are carved wood, the structure is timber-and-stone in the Bai vernacular — but the interior is a basilica with a central nave, side aisles, and a Gothic-style altar. The missionaries deliberately built in the local style as a gesture of integration; the result is a building that exists nowhere else in the world."
---

A small Catholic church just off Renmin Lu in the old town — one of the most architecturally interesting buildings in Dali for the way it fuses Bai roof and exterior design with a Western basilica interior. Easy to miss from outside, since it looks like just another courtyard compound; the cross above the gate is the only hint.

Sunday morning Mass is still celebrated here, and visitors are welcome outside service hours. The interior is small but striking — wooden beams, painted icons, and the Gothic floor plan inside what looks from outside like any other Bai temple. Free; opening hours vary, but mornings are usually safe.
