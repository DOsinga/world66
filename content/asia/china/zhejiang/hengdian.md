Hengdian is a small town in Dongyang that became, somewhat improbably, one of the world's largest working film studios. The complex grew from a single set built in the 1990s for a historical epic into a 330-hectare backlot of period recreations: a full-scale replica of the Forbidden City, a reconstruction of the first emperor Qin Shihuang's palace, Ming and Qing dynasty streets, and old Shanghai and Hong Kong neighbourhoods. Over 1,200 films and television series have been shot here, including *Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon* and the 2020 Disney *Mulan*, and the sets are in near-constant use.

The studios are open to visitors while production continues, which means wandering between shooting locations where extras in Tang-dynasty armour stand next to camera cranes, directors shout through megaphones, and costumed actors lounge on mobile phone breaks in the shade of a Qing imperial gateway. Tens of thousands of would-be actors have moved to Hengdian to work as extras, and the town around the studios has its own peculiar economy and social life built around the film industry.

The admission price covers access to the main set areas, and the Qin imperial palace and the Forbidden City replica are the most photogenic. There is an amusement park element to the whole enterprise — costume rental, live performances, themed restaurants — but the underlying reality of a working studio town, with all its chaos and hustle, is more interesting than anything on the official programme. Hengdian is about two hours south of Hangzhou and most conveniently reached by bus from Dongyang or Yiwu.
