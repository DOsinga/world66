  The Hengdian replica was built for a single 1996 television production but proved so popular that it became permanent. It is larger in some respects than the original Beijing complex, and the production company that commissioned it kept building — the finished structure cost more than the original Forbidden City's contemporary equivalent.
---

The centrepiece of Hengdian World Studios is a full-scale reproduction of Beijing's Forbidden City, built to serve as a set for historical dramas and imperial epics. Unlike the Beijing original, you can walk into the throne rooms, pace across the main courtyards without crowd barriers, and watch film crews at work on the staircases. The scale is immediately arresting — the scarlet walls, yellow-tiled roofs, and stone imperial walkways are reproduced in detail, and the absence of the Beijing crowds makes it easier in some ways to read the architecture.

The set is used continuously for filming, so any visit involves some choreography around production schedules. Camera cranes appear over the gatehouse, wires are strung between pavilions, and directors' assistants wave crowds of extras in period costume through rehearsals. The whole experience is more candid film industry than museum, and for anyone interested in how Chinese historical drama is actually made, watching a morning's shooting from a permitted vantage point is worth the admission price alone.
