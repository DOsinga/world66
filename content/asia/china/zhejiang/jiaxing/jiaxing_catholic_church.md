Jiaxing's Catholic church is a substantial red-brick Gothic Revival building that sits in the older residential quarter north of the city centre, a relic of the strong Jesuit presence in the Jiangnan region from the sixteenth century onwards. The church dates from the late nineteenth century and follows the typical mission-hall style of the period: pointed arch windows, a modest bell tower, and a nave that holds several hundred worshippers.

The congregation is active and Sunday masses draw a visible local Catholic community — a reminder that Christianity has deep roots in this part of China predating the modern missionary era. The building is not always open outside services, but the exterior and surrounding lane are worth a short detour from the South Lake area. The Catholic church stands not far from the Jiaxing Vincent Abbey, which adds a further layer of ecclesiastical history to what is, by Chinese standards, an unusually Christian corner of town.
