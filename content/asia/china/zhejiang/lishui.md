Lishui sits in southern Zhejiang, where the province turns hillier, greener, and slower than the Hangzhou-Shaoxing corridor. The city itself is not a polished showpiece, but it works well as a base for the Ou River countryside, Ming-era pagodas, and scenic villages that give this corner of Zhejiang its appeal.

The strongest half-day trip is [Ancient Weir Painting Village](/asia/china/zhejiang/lishui/ancient_weir_painting_village), a riverside scenic area of old landing stages, camphor trees, ferry crossings, and studios where painters come for the soft water-and-mountain views. Closer to town, [Lishui City Museum](/asia/china/zhejiang/lishui/lishui_city_museum) gives useful context on the region's pottery, sword-making, stone carving, and mountain cultures.

Lishui is best when you let the hills shape the day. [Baiyun Mountain](/asia/china/zhejiang/lishui/baiyun_mountain) is the easy local escape, with paved paths climbing above the northern edge of the city. [Shahe Pagoda](/asia/china/zhejiang/lishui/shahe_pagoda) is rougher and quieter, but the reward is a Ming pagoda with a good sunset view back across the urban valley.
