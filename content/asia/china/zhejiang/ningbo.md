Ningbo is Zhejiang's second city and one of China's oldest ports, opened to foreign trade as a treaty port in 1842 and already thriving as a maritime hub centuries before that. Where Hangzhou attracts tourists, Ningbo attracts business, and the city has a grittier, more self-sufficient character — less manicured, more purposeful. It sits at the southern edge of the Yangtze Delta, connected to Shanghai and Hangzhou by high-speed rail, and faces the East China Sea across a coastline of estuaries and islands.

The TianYi Pavilion, built in 1561, is the oldest surviving private library in China, a remarkable structure designed with drainage channels and firebreaks to protect its collection of Ming-dynasty books. The surrounding garden is one of the more tranquil spots in the city. Baoguo Temple, dating back over a thousand years, preserves wooden halls built without a single nail — an engineering feat still studied by architects today.

Beyond the city itself, Ningbo is the jumping-off point for the Zhoushan archipelago and the sacred island of Mount Putuo. Dongqian Lake, Zhejiang's largest natural freshwater lake, stretches southeast of the city and rewards a half-day excursion by bike or boat. The city's food culture leans heavily on seafood and yellow rice wine, and the night markets around the old town are among the province's liveliest.
