Ningbo is Zhejiang's second city and one of China's oldest ports, opened to foreign trade as a treaty port in 1842 and already thriving as a maritime hub centuries before that. Where Hangzhou attracts tourists, Ningbo attracts business, and the city has a grittier, more self-sufficient character — less manicured, more purposeful. It sits at the southern edge of the Yangtze Delta, connected to Shanghai and Hangzhou by high-speed rail, and faces the East China Sea across a coastline of estuaries and islands.

The [TianYi Pavilion](/asia/china/zhejiang/ningbo/tianyi_ge), built in 1561, is the oldest surviving private library in China, a remarkable structure designed with drainage channels and firebreaks to protect its collection of Ming-dynasty books. The surrounding garden is one of the more tranquil spots in the city. The [Qing'an Guildhall](/asia/china/zhejiang/ningbo/qingan_guildhall) on the [Laowaitan waterfront](/asia/china/zhejiang/ningbo/laowaitan) is one of the finest examples of Qing dynasty commercial architecture in China. [Baoguo Temple](/asia/china/zhejiang/ningbo/baoguo_temple), dating back over a thousand years, preserves wooden halls built without a single nail — an engineering feat still studied by architects today. Further into the hills, [Tiantong Temple](/asia/china/zhejiang/ningbo/tiantong_temple) and [Ayuwang Temple](/asia/china/zhejiang/ningbo/ayuwang_temple) are two of the five great Song dynasty Buddhist temples in a forested mountain setting east of the city.

The architect Wang Shu, who won the Pritzker Prize in 2012, has left a remarkable collection of buildings in Ningbo, most notably the [History Museum](/asia/china/zhejiang/ningbo/ningbo_museum) in Yinzhou, built from twenty million salvaged bricks and documenting a city history that begins with the [Hemudu Neolithic site](/asia/china/zhejiang/ningbo/hemudu_site) seven thousand years ago. Beyond the city itself, Ningbo is the jumping-off point for the Zhoushan archipelago and the sacred island of [Mount Putuo](/asia/china/zhejiang/mount-putuo). [Dongqian Lake](/asia/china/zhejiang/ningbo/dongqian_lake), Zhejiang's largest natural freshwater lake, stretches southeast of the city and rewards a half-day excursion by bike or boat. The city's food culture leans heavily on seafood and yellow rice wine, and the [Chenghuangmiao night market](/asia/china/zhejiang/ningbo/chenghuangmiao_night_market) around the old town are among the province's liveliest.
