Guanzong Temple is one of the oldest Buddhist sites in the Ningbo area, its origins traced to the Eastern Jin dynasty in the fourth century CE. The temple belongs to the Tiantai school of Buddhism, which found Ningbo an important centre of transmission to Japan — several Japanese monks studied here in the Tang and Song dynasties and carried Tiantai teachings back to form the basis of Japanese Tendai Buddhism.

The current buildings date from various periods of reconstruction, with the main hall containing a substantial collection of Buddhist sculpture. The temple sits in a residential neighbourhood near the old city centre and retains an active congregational life: early morning and evening chanting services draw local worshippers, and the courtyard has the lived-in quality of a temple still embedded in its community rather than managed as a tourist attraction. The attached scripture repository houses a collection of Buddhist texts maintained through continuous copying for over a millennium.
