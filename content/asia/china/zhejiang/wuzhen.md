Wuzhen is China's most famous water town, and it has paid a price for that fame: the canal-side wooden buildings are immaculate, the stone bridges are perfectly lit at night, and admission fees ensure that the crowds are manageable but the atmosphere is controlled. It is, in other words, a theme park of itself — but a very beautiful one. The town sits astride the Beijing-Hangzhou Grand Canal in northern Zhejiang, and the preserved streets along its smaller waterways give a reasonable impression of what this entire region looked like before concrete arrived.

The town is divided into two ticketed sections, the Eastern and Western Quarters. The West Quarter, developed more recently, is the more atmospheric of the two: you can stay in guesthouses built over the water, watch silk looms and winemaking demonstrations, and eat at canal-side restaurants under the eaves of buildings that have been here, in one form or another, for several centuries. The East Quarter is older and more compact, with the main sights clustered close together. Both are best in the early morning or evening, when the day-trip crowds have thinned.

The writer Mao Dun was born in Wuzhen and is celebrated here with a small museum and restored residence. His novels documented the transformation of Jiangnan society in the early twentieth century — the very world that Wuzhen now recreates for tourist consumption. The coincidence is not without irony, and the museum is worth a visit for the perspective it adds.
