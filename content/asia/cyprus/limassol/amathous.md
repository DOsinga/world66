story: >
  Amathous was one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in Cyprus, with roots stretching back to the 11th century BC. Its most famous mythological connection is to Theseus: according to legend, Ariadne — who had helped Theseus escape from the Minoan labyrinth — was abandoned here on her way back to Athens when Theseus's ship was blown off course. She died at Amathous giving birth, and a shrine in her honour was maintained in the city for centuries.
---

Ancient Amathous, east of Limassol along the coast road toward Ayia Napa, is one of the nine ancient kingdoms of Cyprus and an important archaeological site. The ruins occupy a commanding hilltop position above the sea, with sweeping views along the coast and a lower city area that runs down toward the water.

The site is extensive — covering both an acropolis with the remains of temples to Aphrodite and Hera, and a lower city with an agora, baths, and Byzantine remains. The famous giant storage vessels (pithoi) found here are now in major museums around the world, and a reconstruction of one stands at the site. The coastal setting is lovely, and the site is never overcrowded. It is easily combined with a beach stop at Governors Beach, about ten kilometres further east.
