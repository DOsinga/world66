  The ancient city of Kourion was destroyed by a catastrophic earthquake in 365 AD — one of the largest ever recorded in the Eastern Mediterranean, possibly magnitude 7.5 or greater, triggered by the rupture of the same fault zone responsible for modern seismic activity in the region. Archaeologists excavating in the 1980s discovered the skeletal remains of a family — man, woman, and child — huddled together in a building, apparently having embraced each other in their final moments as the roof fell in. The poignancy of this discovery made international news.
---

Ancient Kourion, perched on a dramatic cliff top 20 kilometres west of Limassol, is the finest ancient site in Cyprus and one of the most impressive in the Eastern Mediterranean. The city was occupied continuously from the Bronze Age through the Byzantine period, and the remains are extraordinarily well preserved — partly because of the very earthquake that destroyed it in 365 AD, which buried many structures before they could be pillaged for building materials.

The highlights are the Greco-Roman theatre, beautifully restored and still used for summer performances, with sweeping views over the sea; the House of Eustolios with its magnificent floor mosaics; and the Early Christian basilica complex. The nearby Sanctuary of Apollo Hylates, a few hundred metres from the main site, preserves an Archaic-period sacred grove with temples, baths, and a stadium. Allow at least three hours for both sites, and visit in the morning before the heat builds.
