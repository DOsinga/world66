Famagusta is the best-preserved medieval walled city in the eastern Mediterranean, and one of the strangest urban time capsules on the island. The old city — enclosed by massive Venetian walls built in the 15th and 16th centuries — is packed with Gothic churches, Ottoman mosques converted from those same churches, and ruins whose scale hints at a place that was once among the wealthiest ports in the Levant.

The city sits in the northern part of Cyprus, under Turkish Cypriot administration, and entry requires crossing the Green Line from the south or arriving directly from Turkey. Its political status complicates visits but does not diminish what is here. The Lala Mustafa Pasha Mosque — the former Cathedral of Saint Nicholas, built by the de Lusignan dynasty in the 14th century — is the great set piece, its Gothic facade standing intact despite centuries of conversion and conflict. The nearby ruins of Saint George of the Greeks, the Venetian Palace, and the land walls with their distinctive bastions can fill a full day.

Outside the walls, the ghost town of Varosha is a peculiar feature of modern Famagusta: an entire resort district sealed since 1974, now partly reopened to visitors. The beaches of Famagusta Bay remain excellent.
