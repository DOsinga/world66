Varosha (Maraş in Turkish) is the abandoned southern suburb of Famagusta that was fenced off by Turkish military forces in 1974 and left to decay for nearly half a century. Before 1974 it was one of the most fashionable resort destinations in the Mediterranean — a strip of modern hotels and beach apartments used by Elizabeth Taylor, Brigitte Bardot, and other figures of the era. The abrupt abandonment left everything in place.

Parts of Varosha were officially reopened in 2020 and 2021, and it is now possible to walk through some of the streets and see the remains — partly collapsed hotels, overgrown gardens, streets still bearing the signs of businesses from 50 years ago. The opening is politically contested: the Republic of Cyprus, Greece, the EU, and the United Nations have criticised it as a violation of UN resolutions. Whether to visit is a decision each traveller makes for themselves; simply walking the beach south of the old town and looking north at the decaying hotels visible behind the wire is enough to understand why this place has not been forgotten.

The beach at Varosha (Maraş Beach) is long, sandy, and accessible.
