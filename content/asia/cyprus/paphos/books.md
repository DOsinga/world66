A short shelf to take to Paphos.

**Bitter Lemons of Cyprus** by Lawrence Durrell. Durrell bought a house in the Kyrenia hills in 1953, just as the Greek-Cypriot independence struggle was getting under way. The book is part memoir, part lament for a way of life he could feel slipping away. Indispensable for understanding modern Cyprus.

**The Aphrodite Inheritance** by Evelyn Anthony. A thriller that uses the Aphrodite cult, the Akamas peninsula and the Paphos coast as its backdrop. Light reading, but pleasingly local.

**Aphrodite and the Others** by Petros Markaris. A short story collection in which the Greek goddess and her companions wander through modern Mediterranean towns. Useful for the way it reframes the mythology that still attaches itself to Paphos.

**Echoes from the Dead Zone** by Yiannis Papadakis. A Cypriot anthropologist's memoir of crossing the Green Line and meeting people on both sides. The clearest single account of what division has done to the island.
