Alibag (also Alibaug) is a coastal town in Maharashtra, about 95 kilometres south of Mumbai by road and roughly an hour by catamaran from the Gateway of India. It has been the default seaside escape for Mumbai residents for decades, and weekend crowds can make it feel like an extension of the city. Come on a weekday and it's much more relaxed.

The town takes its name from the Kolaba Fort, a sea fort built by the Maratha king Shivaji in 1680 to guard against Siddi and British naval power. The fort sits just offshore and is accessible on foot at low tide or by small boat at high tide. Alibag beach itself is long and flat with hard, slightly dark sand — good for walking. Several quieter beaches lie within easy reach: Varsoli is a mile away and gets fewer visitors; Akshi, 6 km out, is calmer still; Mandwa to the north has regular ferry connections from Mumbai.

The surrounding Konkan coast has a distinctive cuisine: fresh seafood, rice dishes, and coconut-heavy preparations that differ from both Goan and Mumbai cooking. The monsoon (June–September) transforms the landscape dramatically, turning everything intensely green, though sea crossings can be rough.
