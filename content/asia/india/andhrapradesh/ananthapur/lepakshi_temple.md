  The Veerabhadra Temple has 70 carved pillars, one of which — the famous "hanging pillar" — does not fully touch the ground. It can be demonstrated by passing a thin cloth or piece of paper under its base. British engineers in the colonial period attempted to move the pillar to investigate the structural mystery; they shifted it slightly and dislodged the pillars it carries, and hurriedly replaced it. The slight misalignment is still visible.

The Veerabhadra Temple at Lepakshi, about 120km southeast of Anantapur (and 15km east of Hindupur), is one of the finest examples of Vijayanagara-period temple architecture in South India, built in the sixteenth century during the reign of Achyuta Deva Raya. The entire temple complex is carved from a single large granite outcrop, with mandapas (pillared halls), corridors, and shrines interconnected in a labyrinthine plan.

The ceilings of the main mandapa carry some of the best-preserved Vijayanagara mural paintings in existence — vivid, detailed scenes from the Puranas, the Ramayana, and the Mahabharata executed in natural mineral pigments. The colours have maintained their vibrancy over five centuries. The complex also contains a massive monolithic Nandi bull, one of the largest in India, carved from a single rock and sitting in the open a short distance from the main temple entrance. Lepakshi is reachable from Anantapur by road in about two to two and a half hours; it deserves at least half a day.
