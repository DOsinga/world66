The Sita Ramachandraswamy Temple is one of the most revered Vaishnavite shrines in South India, dedicated to Lord Rama and believed to mark the site where he spent part of his fourteen-year forest exile. The complex stands on a high bank above the Godavari River, a position that gives it commanding views of the water and the forested hills beyond. Even for visitors with no connection to Hinduism, the scale of devotion here — pilgrims arriving by the busload from across Telangana and Andhra Pradesh, the smell of jasmine garlands and incense, the sound of Carnatic devotional music — is something memorable.

The temple was built in its present form in the 17th century and expanded over the following centuries. Beyond the main sanctum, the complex includes smaller shrines to Hanuman, Lakshmana, and the presiding goddess, as well as a small museum displaying jewelled ornaments donated to the deities and painted panels depicting scenes from the Ramayana. Prasadam — food consecrated by offering to the deity — is distributed after worship and is worth trying: the pulihora (tamarind rice) in particular.

The Rama Navami festival in March–April transforms the town entirely, drawing several hundred thousand pilgrims over ten days of music, processions, and ritual. The celebration of the divine wedding of Rama and Sita is enacted in full on the final night.
