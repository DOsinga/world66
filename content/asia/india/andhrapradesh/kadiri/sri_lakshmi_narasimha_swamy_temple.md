The Sri Lakshmi Narasimha Swamy Temple is Kadiri's reason for being. One of the most venerated Vaishnava shrines in Andhra Pradesh, it is dedicated to a manifestation of Vishnu as Narasimha — the man-lion form he took to defeat the demon Hiranyakashipu. The temple is believed to date from the Vijayanagara period under the Rayas, and its presiding deity draws pilgrims from across Andhra Pradesh and Telangana.

The main sanctum houses the self-manifested idol, considered exceptionally powerful on account of its swayambhu status. Pilgrims arrive throughout the day, and the queue for darshan can extend for hours during major festivals. Behind the main sanctum lies a pushkarini — the sacred tank common to large South Indian temples — whose still waters and carved stone banks provide a moment of calm within the temple complex. The gopuram gateway tower is visible from much of the surrounding town.

The temple is most crowded during the Brahmotsavam festival each year, when lakhs of devotees arrive for a week-long celebration with processions and ritual bathing in the pushkarini. If you are not visiting for religious reasons, early morning is the quietest time to come.
