story: "In the Ramayana, the demon king Ravana abducts Sita from the forest and carries her south to Lanka in his flying chariot, the Pushpaka Vimana. The vulture-king Jatayu tried to intercept and was mortally wounded near here; the dying bird is said to have collapsed at this very spot. The name Lepakshi itself comes from 'le pakshi' — 'rise, bird' — supposedly Rama's command to the dying Jatayu when he arrived too late."
---

Inside the temple compound is a large damp footprint pressed into the granite floor, water seeping into it continually from a spring beneath the rock. The footprint is said to be Sita's, left when she was held briefly here on her way to Lanka, and the water is regarded as sacred — pilgrims dab it on their foreheads.

The geology probably explains it perfectly well: a natural fissure in the granite outcrop carrying ground water to the surface, the depression worn into a vaguely foot-shaped basin by centuries of feet and hands. But the legend is the better story, and you may want to wait a few minutes after a tour group leaves to see the print in quiet.
