The Veerabhadra Temple is one of the great surviving monuments of Vijayanagara India. Built around 1530 on a low granite outcrop the locals call Kurma Saila — Tortoise Hill — it covers an extraordinary range of stone-carving: hundreds of intricately worked pillars, friezes of dancers, musicians, and gods, and a sequence of ceiling murals that survive almost nowhere else in India in this state of preservation. The temple is dedicated to Veerabhadra, the fierce form of Shiva.

Walk slowly through the three concentric prakaras. Look for the carved Nataraja column in the natya mandapa (dance hall), the seven-headed naga sheltering the linga in the open-air shrine, and the unfinished kalyana mandapa where the wedding of Shiva and Parvati was to be carved — work stopped abruptly, and the rough-hewn pillars stand mid-carving as if the masons had walked off the previous day. Shoes off at the entrance, no photography of the ceiling murals.
