Rajahmundry sits on a wide, busy bend of the Godavari, where coastal Andhra starts to feel like river country. It is an old literary and cultural centre, but the first thing you notice is the water: ghats, bridges, boats, and the long sweep of the river splitting toward the delta.

The city is worth a spare day if you are already moving through Andhra Pradesh. Start at [Pushkar Ghat](/asia/india/andhrapradesh/rajahmundry/pushkar_ghat) in the morning, cross or view the [Godavari Bridge](/asia/india/andhrapradesh/rajahmundry/godavari_bridge), then head downriver toward Dowleswaram for the [Sir Arthur Cotton Museum](/asia/india/andhrapradesh/rajahmundry/sir_arthur_cotton_museum) and the barrage that changed the irrigation history of the delta.

Rajahmundry also works as a practical base for river trips toward the Papikondalu hills and for short excursions into East Godavari's temple towns and nurseries. It is not as polished as a big-ticket Indian heritage city, but the Godavari gives it a real sense of place.
