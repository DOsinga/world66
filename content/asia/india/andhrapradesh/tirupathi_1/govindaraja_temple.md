story: "When the great Vaishnavite philosopher Ramanuja consecrated the Tirumala temple in the 12th century, he installed the lesser-known Govindaraja form of Vishnu down in the plains below as a complementary deity. The two temples are theologically linked — local tradition holds that worship at Tirumala is completed by paying respects to Govindaraja in the town. The temple's enormous gopuram, visible from across central Tirupati, is one of the oldest still standing in southern India."
---

The most important temple in Tirupati town itself, distinct from the hill temple. The seven-storey gopuram dominates the town centre and is visible from the railway station; the temple complex inside includes shrines to Vishnu in his reclining (Bhujanga Sayana) form and to several attendant deities.

Many pilgrims include a visit to Govindaraja before or after their trip to Tirumala. The queues are far shorter than on the hill — usually 30 minutes to an hour — and the temple offers a more contemplative experience than the industrial throughput of Tirumala. Photography is prohibited inside the inner shrines. Open from early morning until late evening with breaks for ritual closings.
