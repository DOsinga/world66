Vemulawada is a small pilgrimage town in the Rajanna Sircilla district of Telangana, 150km northeast of Hyderabad and 36km from Karimnagar. It is known throughout south India as "Dakshina Kashi" — the Kashi of the South — a title that signals its standing as one of the most important Shiva shrines in the region. The Sri Raja Rajeshwara Swamy Temple is the town's reason for existing, and for most visitors the temple, its large sacred tank, and the ghats surrounding it are the entirety of the visit.

The temple is notable beyond its religious significance for something rarer: it is a genuine example of communal devotion, with both Hindu and Muslim worshippers offering prayers here, a tradition maintained across generations. In terms of revenue it ranks second in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana only to Tirupati, a measure of the scale of pilgrimage it draws. Maha Shivaratri, the main festival, brings three to four lakh devotees to the town for an all-night ceremony including the Ekadasa Rudrabhishekham performed at midnight by eleven priests together.

Vemulawada is not a destination with much to offer beyond the temple complex, and that is exactly what makes it what it is: a focused pilgrimage town where the sacred landscape is compact and the purpose is undiluted. Come for the early morning rituals at the tank, stay for the atmosphere of the ghats, and time a visit around Shivaratri if you want to see south Indian pilgrimage at its most intense.
