The Sri Raja Rajeshwara Swamy Temple is the reason Vemulawada exists. It is one of the most venerated Shiva temples in south India — ancient, architecturally handsome, and busy with pilgrims on almost every day of the year. The presiding deity is Rajeshwara, a form of Shiva, and the temple's epithet "Dakshina Kashi" — the Kashi of the South — signals its standing: this is considered to be as sacred as Varanasi for Shaivite devotees across Telangana and beyond.

The temple is notable for something unusual even by the standards of syncretic India: both Hindu and Muslim devotees worship here, offering their prayers to Rajeshwara and Allah side by side. This tradition of shared devotion has persisted for generations and gives the temple a quality of openness that is rare at major pilgrimage sites. Free darshan is available, and the priests are accustomed to visitors of all backgrounds.

Architecturally the complex features a large gopuram gateway and colonnaded mandapas typical of south Indian temple construction. It ranks second only to Tirupati among Andhra/Telangana temples by revenue, a measure of the scale of devotion it commands. Arrive before 7am to see the morning abhishekam and to move through the inner sanctum before the queues lengthen.
