---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

**Maha Shivaratri** (February or March, depending on the lunar calendar) is the defining event in Vemulawada's calendar. The festival draws three to four lakh devotees and the town becomes an extraordinary spectacle of faith, crowds, and colour. If you want the full pilgrimage experience — all-night rituals, illuminated temple, the energy of hundreds of thousands of people gathered for a shared purpose — this is the time to come. Accommodation books out early and roads into town can be blocked for hours; arriving the previous evening and leaving the morning after the main night is a sensible approach.

**Karthika month** (October and November) also sees a significant increase in pilgrims, as this is a sacred month for Shiva devotion across south India. The atmosphere is more measured than Shivaratri, but still livelier than an ordinary visit.

**Everyday visits** are calmer and in some ways more rewarding — you can move freely through the temple, take time at the tank, and see the community side of the pilgrimage town without the festival press. Mornings are the most peaceful time to visit the temple, with the best light on the tank and the opening rituals well worth witnessing.
