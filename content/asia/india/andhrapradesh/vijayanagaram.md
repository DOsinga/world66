Vizianagaram (also spelled Vijayanagaram) is a mid-sized town in the northern part of Andhra Pradesh, situated between Visakhapatnam and the Odisha border. The town was the seat of the Vizianagaram princely state, and the legacy of that era is more substantial than most travellers expect: an 18th-century [fort](/asia/india/andhrapradesh/vijayanagaram/vizianagaram_fort), [Maharajah's College](/asia/india/andhrapradesh/vijayanagaram/maharajahs_college) founded in 1879, and the [Government College of Music and Dance](/asia/india/andhrapradesh/vijayanagaram/college_of_music_and_dance), which has trained Kuchipudi dancers and Carnatic musicians for over a century.

The Vizianagaram royal family were significant patrons of South Indian classical arts, and the town retains a cultural weight that surprises visitors who arrive expecting nothing more than a railway junction. The fort marks the history of the princely state's turbulent end — in 1757, a succession dispute played out in personal combat between two rival claimants, leaving both men dead and the British East India Company an opening to absorb the region.

For most travellers, Vizianagaram is a stop on the Chennai–Kolkata rail line, and Visakhapatnam (Vizag) is the main event to the south. But if you have a few hours between trains, the old town near the fort and the [Maharajah's College](/asia/india/andhrapradesh/vijayanagaram/maharajahs_college) campus repay a wander.
