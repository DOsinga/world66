The Thousand Pillar Temple at Hanamkonda, built in 1163 CE under King Rudra Deva of the Kakatiya dynasty, is one of the finest examples of Kakatiya architecture in Telangana. The temple is a trikuta — three shrines on a single platform — dedicated to Shiva, Vishnu, and Surya, arranged in a star-shaped plan that is a signature of the Kakatiya style.

The interior forest of carved basalt columns is extraordinary. Each pillar is distinct, carved with figures, patterns, and narrative panels of exceptional fineness. The bracket figures — particularly the female figures known as *salabhanjikas* — show the sculptural sophistication that made Kakatiya art influential across the Deccan. A large Nandi bull carved from a single block of stone sits in front of the Shiva shrine. The temple is in excellent condition and is an Archaeological Survey of India protected monument. Entry is free; remove shoes before entering.
