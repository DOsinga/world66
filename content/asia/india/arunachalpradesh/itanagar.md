Itanagar is the capital of Arunachal Pradesh, India's wildest and least-visited northeastern state. The city sits in the foothills of the eastern Himalayas, surrounded by subtropical forest and low mountains. It is small, unhurried, and genuinely off the tourist circuit.

The main historical draw is [Ita Fort](/asia/india/arunachalpradesh/itanagar/ita_fort), a 14th-century brick fortification that gives the city its name. The ruins are scattered across a forested hillside and worth an hour of exploration. Nearby, [Ganga Lake](/asia/india/arunachalpradesh/itanagar/ganga_lake) (also called Gekar Sinyi) is a pretty spot for a walk and a place of local significance.

Itanagar has a handful of Buddhist monasteries, including the yellow-roofed [Itafort Buddhist Temple](/asia/india/arunachalpradesh/itanagar/itafort_buddhist_temple), which reflect the Tibetan cultural influence that runs through much of the state. The [Jawaharlal Nehru State Museum](/asia/india/arunachalpradesh/itanagar/jawaharlal_nehru_state_museum) offers a decent introduction to the more than 25 tribal cultures of Arunachal Pradesh — a good first stop before heading deeper into the state.

Foreign visitors and even Indian nationals from outside the state need an Inner Line Permit (ILP) to enter Arunachal Pradesh. The permit is straightforward to obtain online but you need to sort it out before arrival. Foreigners additionally require a Protected Area Permit through a registered tour operator.
