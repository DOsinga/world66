Patna is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world. As Pataliputra, it was the capital of the Maurya and Gupta empires -- the seat of Ashoka and Chandragupta -- and one of the great cities of the ancient world. Today it is the capital of Bihar, a sprawling, chaotic, and fascinating city stretched along the southern bank of the Ganges.

The city is not conventionally beautiful, and the infrastructure can be challenging, but it rewards the curious. The [Patna Museum](/asia/india/bihar/patna/patna_museum) and the newer [Bihar Museum](/asia/india/bihar/patna/bihar_museum) together have an excellent collection of Mauryan-era sculptures, including the polished sandstone Didarganj Yakshi -- one of the finest pieces of ancient Indian art. The [Golghar](/asia/india/bihar/patna/golghar), a beehive-shaped granary built by the British in 1786, offers panoramic views from its spiral staircase. The Sikh pilgrimage site of [Takht Sri Patna Sahib](/asia/india/bihar/patna/takht_sri_patna_sahib), birthplace of Guru Gobind Singh, is one of the five seats of Sikh authority.

The [Mahatma Gandhi Setu](/asia/india/bihar/patna/mahatma_gandhi_setu) across the Ganges, once the longest river bridge in the world, connects Patna to Hajipur in the north. The [ghats along the river](/asia/india/bihar/patna/patna_ghats) are busy in the mornings, and during Chhath Puja -- Bihar's most important festival -- the entire riverbank fills with devotees making offerings to the setting and rising sun.
