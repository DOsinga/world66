The formal colonial city built by Edwin Lutyens and Herbert Baker between 1911 and 1931, organised around the great ceremonial axis of **Rajpath** (now Kartavya Path) running from **India Gate** to the Presidential Palace. This is the Delhi of broad, tree-lined avenues, colonial bungalows behind compound walls, and the concentrated institutions of the Indian state — Parliament, the Supreme Court, the ministries, and the official residences of the President and Prime Minister.

The public spaces are genuinely impressive in scale. The **India Gate** war memorial marks the eastern end of the ceremonial axis; the **Rashtrapati Bhavan** (Presidential Palace), designed by Lutyens with Mughal-derived gardens, is visible from the boulevard but requires a separate booking to visit. **Connaught Place**, Lutyens' commercial hub, is the main shopping and dining district with its circular colonnade of white buildings still functioning much as intended.
