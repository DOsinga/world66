The oldest continuously inhabited area of Delhi, with settlements going back to the 8th century. Mehrauli anchors the southern end of the city and its historical layer cake: the **Qutb Minar complex** — the 72-metre minaret begun in 1193 and the ruins of India's oldest mosque — sits at its heart, surrounded by further medieval ruins scattered through what was once the first Sultanate capital. The **Mehrauli Archaeological Park** adjacent to the Qutb complex contains dozens more medieval monuments in varying states of preservation.

The village of Mehrauli itself, squeezed between the archaeological sites, has survived as a working settlement. The **Adham Khan's Tomb** on the main road is a domed 16th-century Mughal structure now used as a police station and accessible to visitors. The shopping village has become gentrified since the 2000s, with boutiques and cafes serving the south Delhi residential areas nearby.
