Shahjahanabad — Old Delhi — is the 17th-century Mughal city built by Shah Jahan, bounded by the Red Fort on the east and the Jama Masjid at its heart. The narrow lanes of **Chandni Chowk** were once the most prestigious market street in the subcontinent; today they remain one of the most intense, sensory-overloading urban environments anywhere, with lanes specialising in textiles, spices, wedding supplies, electronics, and street food that has been served from the same spots for generations.

The **Jama Masjid** — India's largest mosque, built 1644–1656 — overlooks the neighbourhood from a broad flight of steps that becomes a social gathering point on Fridays. The **Red Fort** marks the eastern end: Shah Jahan's palace complex and the symbolic heart of Mughal power. The street food here is the oldest in Delhi — **Paranthe Wali Gali** has been frying stuffed parathas since the 1870s; **Karim's** near the Jama Masjid has been cooking Mughal-style meat dishes since 1913.
