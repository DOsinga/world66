A seven-kilometre arc of soft, butter-coloured sand running from the rocky headland at Aguada up to Baga, with Calangute town sitting roughly in the middle. The sand is good, the water is warm year-round, and the slope into the sea is gentle enough for swimming. At low tide the beach is enormous; at high tide it shrinks to a thin strip in front of the shacks.

The atmosphere is unapologetically commercial. Beach shacks rent out sunbeds and umbrellas for a few hundred rupees a day, hawkers cycle through with drums, sarongs and henna kits, and parasailing boats buzz offshore. Sunset is the moment — the crowd thickens, the shacks light their fairy lights, and you can walk for an hour along the wet sand with the Arabian Sea on one side and the surf strung with people on the other.

For a quieter swim, walk twenty minutes south toward [Candolim](/asia/india/goa/candolim) or north toward Baga, both of which start to thin out at the ends.
