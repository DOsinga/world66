The bronze statue in front of the Adil Shah Palace catches Abbé Faria mid-trance — he leans forward over a kneeling woman, his hand extended, in the moment of inducing what he called *sommeil lucide* or "lucid sleep." The pose is theatrical and unmistakable; the statue is one of the most photographed monuments in central Panaji.

Faria is a curiously underappreciated Goan figure — born here in 1756, ordained a priest, exiled to Portugal for revolutionary sympathies, and finally writing the first serious treatise on hypnotism in Paris in 1819. Dumas borrowed his name for Edmond Dantes's tutor in *The Count of Monte Cristo*. A short five minutes' detour from the Church of the Immaculate Conception.
