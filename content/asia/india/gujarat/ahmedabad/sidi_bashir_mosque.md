The mechanism has been studied for centuries; the engineer Robert Murphy attempted to dismantle one in the 1840s to understand it and failed. Access to the top has been closed since the 1960s."

The Sidi Bashir Mosque was built around 1452 and its main marvel — the curious flexible minarets — was once a tourist attraction recorded by every European visitor. The mosque itself was largely destroyed in the wars of 1753 and only the prayer-hall facade and the two slender minarets survive, marooned beside the railway tracks near Sarangpur Gate.

A second pair of similar **shaking minarets** stand at the **Raj Bibi Mosque** in the same neighbourhood, less famous but equally interesting. Both can be visited; neither can be climbed.
