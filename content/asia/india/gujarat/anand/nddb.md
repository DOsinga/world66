The NDDB campus sits on the outskirts of Anand and is the institutional headquarters of India's White Revolution — the national programme that replicated the Amul cooperative model across hundreds of districts and transformed India from a milk-deficient country into the world's largest dairy producer. The campus itself is a pleasant, well-maintained complex with the slightly purposeful feel of somewhere that has always taken its work seriously.

A small museum on campus documents the history of Operation Flood, the three-phase programme launched in 1970 that connected village dairy cooperatives to urban markets across India. The exhibits include photographs, maps of the expanding cooperative network, and material on Dr Verghese Kurien, who served as chairman of the NDDB from its founding in 1965 until 1998 and is the person most responsible for what Indian dairy became. The museum is modest — this is not a polished tourist attraction — but for anyone interested in development economics, cooperative movements, or the history of Indian agriculture, it is genuinely illuminating. Check in advance about access, as the campus is still a working institution and visitor hours can vary.
