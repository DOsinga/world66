Champaner-Pavagadh is one of Gujarat's great surprises: a ruined Sultanate capital at the foot of a sacred volcanic hill, with mosques, stepwells, gateways, palaces, fortifications, and pilgrimage paths scattered through fields and forest. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, but it still feels less manicured than many better-known Indian monuments.

The lower city was expanded in the late 15th century by Sultan Mahmud Begada after he captured Pavagadh, and its mosques show a beautiful fusion of Islamic planning and Gujarati carving. [Jami Masjid](/asia/india/gujarat/champaner/jami_masjid) is the masterpiece, but the quieter [Kevada Masjid](/asia/india/gujarat/champaner/kevada_masjid), [Nagina Masjid](/asia/india/gujarat/champaner/nagina_masjid), and [Sahar ki Masjid](/asia/india/gujarat/champaner/sahar_ki_masjid) reward slow wandering.

Above the ruins, [Pavagadh Hill](/asia/india/gujarat/champaner/pavagadh_hill) pulls in Hindu pilgrims heading to the [Kalika Mata Temple](/asia/india/gujarat/champaner/kalika_mata_temple). That combination is what makes Champaner memorable: Sultanate stonework below, devotional energy above, and a landscape big enough that you should allow a full day rather than treating it as a quick photo stop from Vadodara.
