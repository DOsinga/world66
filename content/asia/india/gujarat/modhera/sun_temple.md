The Sun Temple at Modhera is one of the great monuments of medieval India, built in 1026 AD by the Solanki king Bhimdev I at the height of the dynasty's power and ambition. It is dedicated to Surya, the solar deity, and every element of its design reflects a deep understanding of solar movement. The temple faces east so that the rising sun enters the inner sanctum; on the solstices and equinoxes the alignment is exact.

The architecture belongs to the Māru-Gurjara tradition — a style developed in western India between the 8th and 13th centuries that combines extraordinary structural complexity with almost overwhelming decorative richness. The exterior walls of the main shrine (gudhamandapa) are covered from base to shikhara with carved panels depicting gods, goddesses, apsaras, mythological scenes, and scenes from everyday life. The quality and precision of the carving rivals anything at Khajuraho. Inside, the pillars of the assembly hall are intricately worked and arranged so that they create kaleidoscopic patterns of shadow and light as the sun moves across the sky.

In front of the main temple, the Surya Kund is a vast stepped tank — a rectangular reservoir surrounded by over a hundred small shrine niches, each one carved, each one slightly different. Its geometry is perfect, and its reflection of the temple complex on a still morning is one of the most beautiful sights in Gujarat. The setting near the Pushpavati River adds a further layer of atmosphere. The whole complex is maintained by the Archaeological Survey of India and is a UNESCO Tentative World Heritage Site.
