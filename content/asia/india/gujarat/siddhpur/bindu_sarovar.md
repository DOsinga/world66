Bindu Sarovar is a large artificial tank at the southern edge of the old town, considered one of Hinduism's five holiest lakes (the Panch-Sarovar) and mentioned in the Rig Veda. The tank is especially significant for the ritual of Matrushraddh — memorial ceremonies performed for one's deceased mother — and draws large numbers of pilgrims during the Kartika month (October–November). Outside of pilgrimage season it is a quiet and atmospheric spot, its ghats framed by old temples and the low skyline of the town.

The Shristhal Sangrahalay (Siddhpur Museum) stands adjacent to the tank and is worth combining with a visit to the ghats. The museum's architecture is designed around the axis of the sarovar, with water features that echo the tank's ritual significance. Inside, three galleries cover the religious history of Siddhpur (including the shraddh tradition), the history of the region, and the social life of its communities.
