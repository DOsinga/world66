The Rudra Mahalaya is one of Gujarat's great lost monuments — a vast temple complex dedicated to Shiva that took nearly two hundred years to build and was largely destroyed within two centuries of its completion. What remains today is a haunting fragment: two carved torans (ceremonial gateways), four original pillars of the central shrine, and stretches of carved stonework that hint at the scale of what once stood here. The western section of the complex was converted into a congregational mosque, the Jami Masjid, and both the ruins and the mosque are protected as ASI Monuments of National Importance.

The site is not formally open to visitors, but a guard is usually present and it is generally possible to walk in and look around. The Maru-Gurjara architectural style — the same tradition behind Modhera and Mount Abu — is visible in the surviving carved columns and gateway piers. The Shristhal Museum nearby contains a replica of one of the torans alongside photographs and artefacts that help visitors understand what the full complex would have looked like.
