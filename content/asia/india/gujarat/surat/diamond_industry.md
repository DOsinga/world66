story: >
  Surat's dominance in diamond cutting began in the 1960s when Gujarati entrepreneurs who had migrated to the city developed new techniques for cutting smaller, lower-grade stones that the Belgian and Israeli cutters declined to work with. By making these "commercial" stones profitable, they built an industry that eventually absorbed the high-end trade as well. Today even stones destined for Antwerp and New York are cut in Surat.
---

Roughly 90 per cent of the world's diamonds pass through Surat to be cut and polished before reaching retail markets. The city processes around 8,000 tonnes of rough diamonds annually, from tiny industrial-grade chips to gems destined for major jewellery houses. The Surat Diamond Bourse, opened in 2023 near the airport, is the largest office complex in the world and houses over 4,000 diamond merchants.

Visiting the diamond district is possible with advance arrangement through tour operators or hotel concierges. The workshops are concentrated in areas like Varachha, Katargam, and Mahidharpura, where dozens of workers sit at polishing wheels in small storey-buildings. Photography inside the workshops is almost never permitted. The sight of thousands of people working on gems that will end up in engagement rings around the world — many of them migrants from Saurashtra and other parts of Gujarat — is genuinely unlike anything else in India.
