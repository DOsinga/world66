Ambala is one of north India's great junction towns: part old trading city, part military cantonment, and part railway crossroads between Delhi, Punjab, Himachal Pradesh, and Chandigarh. It is not polished as a tourist stop, but it has the layered feel of a place that has had armies, merchants, pilgrims, and trains passing through for a long time.

The two halves matter. Ambala City is older, busier, and more local, with Sikh shrines such as [Gurudwara Manji Sahib](/asia/india/haryana/ambala/gurudwara_manji_sahib) and market streets that still feel tied to the Grand Trunk Road. Ambala Cantonment is wider and greener, with railway yards, parade-ground geometry, churches, cemeteries, and the old British imprint visible around [Holy Redeemer Church](/asia/india/haryana/ambala/holy_redeemer_church) and the [European Cemetery](/asia/india/haryana/ambala/european_cemetery).

Most travellers pass through rather than plan a night here, and that is fair. But if you are changing trains or driving between Delhi and the hills, Ambala is worth a slow half day. The cantonment has the best atmosphere, especially around [Rani Ka Talab](/asia/india/haryana/ambala/rani_ka_talab) in the evening, while the older city gives you the religious and trading side of Haryana that the highways usually hide.
