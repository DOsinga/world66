Sirsa sits in far western Haryana, close to the borders with Punjab and Rajasthan. It is a dry, agricultural city rather than a conventional tourist stop, but it has the edge-of-state feel that makes Haryana more varied than the Delhi-Chandigarh highway suggests.

The city is best understood through its religious life and its position near the Ghaggar-Hakra river system. Gurdwara Shri Chilla Sahib, on the Sirsa side of Rania Road, is the most important Sikh site in town and is associated with Guru Nanak's meditation here. Tara Baba Kutiya is another well-known local pilgrimage stop, especially for visitors travelling within Haryana rather than on an all-India circuit.

For travellers, Sirsa is a short stop, not a destination to build a whole trip around. If you are moving between Hisar, Bathinda, Hanumangarh, or the Rajasthan border, it is worth pausing for the shrines and a look toward the Ghaggar at [Ottu Barrage](/asia/india/haryana/sirsa/ottu_barrage), where irrigation history and the older river landscape meet.
