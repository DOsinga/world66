The Khajji Nag temple gives the village its name. It is a small 12th-century shrine on the eastern edge of the lake, built in the local pahari style with a low pitched roof and a deep wooden veranda. The exterior is plain, almost domestic, but the interior is unexpectedly rich — heavily carved pillars, painted ceiling panels, and the five life-sized wooden Pandava figures around the inner sanctum.

The temple is still actively worshipped and the priests are usually happy for visitors to step inside. Remove shoes at the door. There is no entry fee but a small donation is customary.
