Manali sits at 2,050 metres in the Kullu Valley of Himachal Pradesh, where the Beas River cuts through pine and deodar forests backed by snow-capped peaks. It is one of the most popular hill stations in northern India — a gateway to the high Himalayas that draws trekkers, honeymooners, and backpackers in roughly equal measure.

The main attraction is the setting itself: the valley is green and lush in summer, snow-covered in winter, and the surrounding mountains offer everything from gentle day walks to serious high-altitude treks. The 450-year-old [Hidimba Temple](/asia/india/himachalpradesh/manali/hidimba_temple) (Dhungri Temple) is the town's most distinctive landmark, a four-tiered wooden pagoda set in a cedar forest and dedicated to the goddess Hadimba. The [Tibetan Monastery](/asia/india/himachalpradesh/manali/tibetan_monastery) in the centre of town has good thangka paintings and a peaceful atmosphere.

[Old Manali](/asia/india/himachalpradesh/manali/old_manali), a short walk uphill from the main bazaar, is where most backpackers settle — it has cheap guesthouses, cafes, and a more relaxed pace than the crowded town centre. The [hot springs at Vashisht](/asia/india/himachalpradesh/manali/vashisht_hot_springs), about three kilometres north, are worth the walk. For a longer excursion, the trek to [Bhrigu Lake](/asia/india/himachalpradesh/manali/bhrigu_lake_trek) (4,300 metres) is one of the best accessible high-altitude hikes in the region.

Manali is also the starting point for the road to Leh over [Rohtang Pass](/asia/india/himachalpradesh/manali/rohtang_pass) — one of the great Himalayan drives, open roughly from June to October.
