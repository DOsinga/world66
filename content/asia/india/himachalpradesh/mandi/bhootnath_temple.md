Bhootnath is the most important temple in Mandi and the building around which the rest of the old town arranges itself. The single-cell sikhara-roofed shrine sits at the head of the main bazaar, a few minutes' walk from the river — small, stone, blackened by centuries of butter-lamps, and rarely empty. The outer walls carry low-relief panels of Shiva, Parvati and Ganesh; the threshold stone is worn deep with five hundred years of footsteps.

The temple comes alive at the **Shivratri Fair** in February or March, when the deities of dozens of village temples across Mandi district are carried in procession to Bhootnath to pay homage to Shiva. The week-long festival fills the bazaar with palanquins, drummers and shawl-traders; if you can time a visit for it, it is one of the most colourful events in Himachal.
