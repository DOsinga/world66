The village walk is not a formal attraction, but it is one of the main reasons to be in Nako. The old lanes, flat-roofed houses, chortens, stacked firewood, and small fields say more about the place than a fast stop at the lake ever could.

Be patient and polite. People live here, and the best way to enjoy Nako is to move quietly, ask before photographing anyone, and let the details accumulate: painted doors, mani walls, mountain light, and the sudden opening of views toward the valley.
