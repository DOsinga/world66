Shimla is the capital of Himachal Pradesh and, at 2,130 metres above sea level, India's most famous hill station — the summer capital of British India from 1864 until Independence, where the Raj's administration decamped each year to escape the plains heat. The colonial legacy is visible everywhere: in the mock-Tudor architecture of the [Viceregal Lodge](/asia/india/himachalpradesh/shimla/viceregal_lodge), the cast-iron bandstand on the Ridge, the pedestrianised [Mall Road](/asia/india/himachalpradesh/shimla/mall_road) with its tea shops and bookstores, and the remarkable narrow-gauge [Kalka-Shimla Railway](/asia/india/himachalpradesh/shimla/kalka_shimla_railway) that climbs through 102 tunnels and 864 bridges over 96 kilometres to reach the town.

The centre of Shimla is walkable and pleasant. The Ridge — a broad open promenade — connects the Mall to [Christ Church](/asia/india/himachalpradesh/shimla/christ_church) (1857), one of the oldest churches in northern India. From Scandal Point, the walk up to [Jakhoo Hill](/asia/india/himachalpradesh/shimla/jakhu_temple) (about 2 km) leads to the Hanuman Temple at the summit, the highest point in the immediate area, with views over the town and the forested slopes of the outer Himalaya. The [Lower Bazaar](/asia/india/himachalpradesh/shimla/lower_bazaar) below the Ridge is more authentically Indian: wool shops, Tibetan jewellery, and small restaurants.

Shimla is also the starting point for treks into the Himalayan foothills and for road journeys to Spiti, Kinnaur, and Manali. The [Gaiety Theatre](/asia/india/himachalpradesh/shimla/gaiety_theatre) on Mall Road and the [State Museum](/asia/india/himachalpradesh/shimla/state_museum) with its Pahari miniature painting collection are worth seeking out beyond the main promenade.
