Spiti Valley is one of the most dramatic landscapes in India — a cold desert plateau at 3,800 to 4,500 metres in the Trans-Himalayan zone of Himachal Pradesh, enclosed by stark brown mountains streaked with mineral colours and bisected by the turquoise Spiti River. This is India at its most remote and most spectacular: the landscape looks more like Tibet than anything else in the Subcontinent, which makes sense since it was part of the Tibetan Kingdom until the 16th century. The population is small, Buddhist, and deeply traditional, and the villages cling to cliff faces and river bends in ways that seem almost implausible from a distance.

Getting here is a commitment. The valley is accessible from two directions: via [Manali](/asia/india/himachalpradesh/manali) over the Rohtang and Kunzum passes (open roughly June to October), or year-round via the Kinnaur route through Shimla and Reckong Peo, though the roads are narrow, unpaved in stretches, and regularly disrupted by landslides and snow. The effort is precisely the point. Spiti rewards patience with ancient monasteries perched on impossible ridges, fossil beds full of marine creatures laid down when this was all seabed, and skies so clear that the stars feel like they belong to you personally.

The main hub is Kaza, a small market town with guesthouses, ATMs (bring cash anyway), and enough cafes to feel moderately civilised. From Kaza, day trips reach the major monasteries at [Key](/asia/india/himachalpradesh/spiti/key_monastery) and [Tabo](/asia/india/himachalpradesh/spiti/tabo_monastery), the fossil-rich cliffs at [Langza](/asia/india/himachalpradesh/spiti/langza), and the ancient village of [Kibber](/asia/india/himachalpradesh/spiti/kibber). The short but steep hike to [Chandratal Lake](/asia/india/himachalpradesh/spiti/chandratal_lake), a crescent of impossibly blue glacial water at 4,300 metres, is the single most memorable excursion in the valley.

Permits are required for foreign nationals in some restricted zones — check current requirements in Kaza or Shimla before heading toward the Pin Valley or crossing toward Spiti's northern reaches. The best time to visit is June to September; the valley is effectively snowbound the rest of the year.
