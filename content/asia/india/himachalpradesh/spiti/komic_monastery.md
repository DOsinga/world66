Komic is a cluster of houses at around 4,587 metres — one of the highest motorable villages anywhere — sitting on a broad plateau with extraordinary views across Spiti. The small Buddhist monastery here belongs to the Sakyapa order and contains some notable old paintings and a resident monk community, though it is much simpler than the major monasteries at Key or Tabo.

The village itself is the attraction: fewer than twenty families live here year-round, herding yaks and growing barley in the short growing season. In winter, most residents remain despite temperatures that regularly drop to -30°C. The road from Kaza to Komic via Langza and Hikkim makes for an excellent half-day circuit, visiting fossil fields, the world's highest post office, and this monastery — three very different experiences within a few kilometres of each other at the edge of what feels like the roof of the world.
