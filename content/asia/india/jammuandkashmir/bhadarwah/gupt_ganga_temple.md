Gupt Ganga is the most atmospheric of Bhadarwah's temples — an old stone Shiva shrine standing right on the bank of the Neeru river on the eastern edge of town, where the sound of the water never stops. The temple's peculiarity, and the source of its name, is the spring that rises inside the building itself: the water is regarded as a hidden (gupt) emergence of the Ganga, and pilgrims bathe in the stone tank it feeds before entering the sanctum.

The setting rewards even the unreligious visitor. The Neeru here runs fast and cold straight off the mountains, deodar-covered slopes rise on both sides, and the riverside path makes a pleasant short walk from the bazaar. Come early in the morning when the temple is busiest with locals and the light is on the water. During the August yatra season the temple is a staging point for pilgrims heading to Mount Kailash, and the riverbank fills with sadhus and camp smoke.
