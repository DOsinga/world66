Drass is a small town in Kargil district, sitting in the [Dras River valley](/asia/india/jammuandkashmir/ladakh/drass/dras_river_valley) between the Suru and Zanskar valleys in the northern Himalayas. At 3,300 metres, it is often cited as the second coldest inhabited place on earth — winter temperatures have been recorded below −45°C. The town's population is predominantly Shina-speaking and Muslim, and it has a quietly frontier quality: stone houses, bare brown mountains, a fast pale river, and military traffic on the main road.

The town gained wider attention during the 1999 Kargil War, when fighting concentrated on the peaks immediately to the north, including [Tololing](/asia/india/jammuandkashmir/ladakh/drass/tololing_peak) and Tiger Hill. The [Kargil War Memorial](/asia/india/jammuandkashmir/ladakh/drass/kargil_war_memorial), five kilometres east on the highway, is one of the most visited sites in Ladakh and an unusually honest monument — you can look directly at the peaks where the battles took place while reading the names of the soldiers killed there.

Most visitors pass through on the Srinagar–Leh highway and stop for a few hours. The road crosses [Zoji La](/asia/india/jammuandkashmir/ladakh/drass/zoji_la_pass) west of town — the gateway between Kashmir and Ladakh, and one of the few mountain passes on a national highway that is genuinely dangerous in bad conditions. Traffic is regulated one direction at a time and the road closes at 6pm. Between November and May, the pass closes entirely and the valley is cut off from Kashmir.
