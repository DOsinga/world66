Lamayuru is a small village on the Srinagar-Leh highway, about 115 km west of [Leh](/asia/india/jammuandkashmir/ladakh/leh), but it feels less like a road stop than a place the landscape has deliberately staged. The village sits below [Lamayuru Monastery](/asia/india/jammuandkashmir/ladakh/lamayuru/lamayuru_monastery), with pale, eroded ridges behind it that have earned the tourist nickname "Moonland".

The monastery is the reason to come. It is one of Ladakh's oldest and most important gompas, associated with Naropa and the Drikung Kagyu school, and its white buildings are stacked against the hillside in the classic Ladakhi way. From the courtyards you look down over barley fields, chortens, dry cliffs, and the highway curling through the valley.

Lamayuru also works as a slower alternative to treating western Ladakh as a blur from a jeep window. The [Moonland viewpoint](/asia/india/jammuandkashmir/ladakh/lamayuru/moonland_viewpoint) is simple but memorable, especially in low light, and [Wanla Monastery](/asia/india/jammuandkashmir/ladakh/lamayuru/wanla_monastery) makes a good short detour if you have a vehicle. Longer trekking routes into Zanskar also start or pass near here, including the demanding [Lamayuru to Padum trek](/asia/india/jammuandkashmir/ladakh/zanskar/padum/lamayuru_to_padum).

Most travellers visit as a long day trip from Leh or stop between Leh and Kargil. Staying overnight is better if you want the village after the day vehicles leave and the monastery in early morning light. The altitude is about 3,500 metres, so do not treat even a short walk uphill as casual if you have just arrived in Ladakh.
