---
title: Bars and Cafes
type: section
---

Alcohol is available in Leh — this is not a dry destination — but the scene is low-key. Most travellers drink at their guesthouses or at the handful of restaurants that serve beer. Chang, the local barley beer, is worth trying if you encounter it at a local household; it is rarely on restaurant menus.

**Cafe Jeevan** near the main bazaar is a good daytime stop for espresso and decent filter coffee, which is genuinely hard to find in the mountains. It also does good breakfast and light lunches.

**The Bookworm Cafe** in Changspa has a library of well-worn paperbacks, strong tea, and a sociable terrace where trekkers compare routes and compare altitude horror stories. Beer is available.

**Lehchen Bar** is one of the few places specifically set up as a bar rather than a restaurant that also serves drinks. Local and Indian beer, some spirits, and a terrace that makes the most of the clear mountain evenings.

Most serious drinking happens at guesthouse rooftops after dark. Kingfisher and Tuborg are ubiquitous; ask for Seabuckthorn juice if you want something local and non-alcoholic — it is made from the bright orange berries that grow wild along the Indus riverbanks.
