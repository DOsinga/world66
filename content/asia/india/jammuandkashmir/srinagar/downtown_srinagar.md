"Downtown" is what Srinagar residents call the old city — the historic urban core north of Lal Chowk on the western bank of the Jhelum. The Kashmiri name is Shahar-e-Khaas, meaning "the special city," and it was indeed the political and commercial heart of Kashmir for centuries before modern Srinagar expanded south and east. The contrast with the newer parts of the city is immediate: the streets narrow, the architecture becomes traditional carved wood, and the mosques and shrines multiply.

Walking Downtown without a fixed itinerary is one of the better things to do in Srinagar. The neighbourhood mosques (most lanes have at least one) serve as landmarks and gathering points. The craft workshops are visible through open doors — weavers at looms, men shaping papier-mâché, women doing needlework. The neighbourhood bazaars are oriented toward local life rather than tourists: spice sellers, butchers, bread bakers, fabric merchants. The area around Nowhatta, the Jama Masjid, and the older bazaar lanes toward Habba Kadal is the most atmospheric. Hari Parbat Hill rises at the northern edge, with the fort walls visible on its summit.
