Hazratbal — "Majestic Place" — is Kashmir's most sacred Islamic site, set on the western shore of Dal Lake with a pristine white marble façade reflected in the water. The mosque's sanctity derives from the Moi-e-Muqaddas, a relic claimed to be a hair of the Prophet Muhammad, brought to Kashmir from Medina in the 17th century. The relic is displayed publicly on major Islamic holidays, drawing enormous crowds from across the valley and beyond.

The current marble structure was completed in 1979, replacing an earlier wooden mosque. Its white dome and minarets are visible from much of the lake and make a particularly striking sight in early morning light. Non-Muslims may visit the grounds (remove shoes at the gate) though access to the inner prayer hall depends on prayer times and the current security situation. The mosque sits at the junction of Hazratbal Road and Boulevard Road on the northern lake shore; it is about 6 km from Lal Chowk.
