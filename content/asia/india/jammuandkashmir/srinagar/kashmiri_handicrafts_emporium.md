The government-run arts emporium on Residency Road is the most reliable single stop for Kashmiri handicrafts, particularly useful as a first purchase or as a reference point for quality and pricing. The stock covers the full range of Kashmiri crafts — pashmina shawls, silk and wool carpets, papier-mâché, crewel embroidery, walnut woodwork, and copper items — and carries a Government of India quality certification that provides some protection against the widespread adulteration problem in the pashmina trade.

Prices are fixed, which is both the main advantage and the main limitation: you cannot negotiate, but you also know what you are paying is the marked price rather than the opening gambit in a long negotiation. The selection is conservative — the emporium stocks what sells reliably rather than the more unusual or innovative craft pieces that occasionally appear at smaller workshops. For a first-time visitor to Kashmir who wants to buy something without extensive research and negotiation, this is the practical starting point. For experienced buyers looking for specific quality levels, the emporium prices serve as a useful benchmark when shopping elsewhere.
