Belgaum (also known as Belagavi) is a large city in northern Karnataka, sitting on the Deccan Plateau near the borders of Goa and Maharashtra. It has historically been a meeting point of cultures — the city has significant Kannada-, Marathi-, and Urdu-speaking populations, and the architecture reflects layers of Hindu, Muslim, and colonial influence. The old fort, built by the Chalukyas and extended under the Bahmani Sultans, encloses two mosques and a Jain temple within its walls.

The city is also a major military cantonment — the Indian Army's officers' training academy is based here — which gives it a tidier, more organised feel than many comparably sized Indian cities. The climate is pleasant year-round by Deccan standards: the altitude moderates the heat, and the monsoon arrives reliably in June.

Belgaum is a transit point for travellers moving between Goa and Bangalore or Pune. The food reflects the cultural mix: biryani from the Hyderabadi tradition sits alongside Maharashtrian snacks and Karnataka's own coastal-influenced cuisine. The main bus station area concentrates most of the practical amenities.
