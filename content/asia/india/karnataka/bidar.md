Bidar sits on the northern edge of Karnataka, closer in feeling to the Deccan plateau of Telangana than to the greener south of the state. It is a small, dry, workaday city, but its monuments are far better than its low profile suggests. If you are tracing the old Bahmani and Barid Shahi capitals after [Gulbarga](/asia/india/karnataka/gulbarga) and [Bijapur](/asia/india/karnataka/bijapur), Bidar is an essential stop.

The reason to come is the compact run of medieval Islamic architecture. [Bidar Fort](/asia/india/karnataka/bidar/bidar_fort) spreads across the old city with palaces, gates and the huge [Solah Khamba Mosque](/asia/india/karnataka/bidar/solah_khamba_mosque) inside its walls. In town, the ruined [Mahmud Gawan Madrasa](/asia/india/karnataka/bidar/mahmud_gawan_madrasa) still shows flashes of blue tile and Central Asian ambition, while the [Bahmani Tombs](/asia/india/karnataka/bidar/bahmani_tombs) stand out in the open country east of town.

Bidar is not polished. Streets are dusty, distances are just awkward enough to make an auto-rickshaw useful, and summer heat can make sightseeing a test of will. Go early, carry water, and give the city a full day rather than treating it as a quick detour. The best approach is usually by road or rail from Hyderabad, about three to four hours away; Gulbarga is roughly 110 km southwest.
