The Mahmud Gawan Madrasa is the most elegant ruin in Bidar. Much of the building is damaged, but the surviving facade still has tall pointed arches, traces of blue-green tile and a vertical grace that feels imported from far beyond the Deccan. It once functioned as a residential Islamic college with lecture halls, a mosque and rooms for students.

The site stands close to the old city centre, so it is easy to combine with the Chaubara watchtower and Bidar Fort. It does not take long to see, but linger for the details: fragments of calligraphy, patterned brickwork and the way the broken minarets still dominate the street.
