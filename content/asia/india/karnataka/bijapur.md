Bijapur, now officially known as Vijayapura, is a historic city in northern Karnataka that was once the capital of the Adil Shahi sultanate. Between the 16th and 17th centuries the city accumulated some of the finest Islamic architecture in India, and what remains today is remarkable — a skyline of domes, minarets, and monumental gateways rising from the Deccan plain.

The [Gol Gumbaz](/asia/india/karnataka/bijapur/gol_gumbaz) is the centrepiece: a massive tomb whose central dome, when completed in 1656, was the second largest in the world. The interior is famous for its Whispering Gallery — a circular balcony where the slightest sound travels the full circumference of the dome and echoes back with uncanny clarity. The [Ibrahim Rauza](/asia/india/karnataka/bijapur/ibrahim_rauza), built a generation earlier, is smaller but more beautiful — its minarets and fine carvings influenced the design of the Taj Mahal. The [Citadel](/asia/india/karnataka/bijapur/citadel) at the city centre once contained palaces and mosques, most now in ruins but still atmospheric, and the [Jama Masjid](/asia/india/karnataka/bijapur/jama_masjid) is one of the largest mosques in the country. The unfinished tomb of [Bara Kaman](/asia/india/karnataka/bijapur/bara_kaman) and the giant Persian cannon [Malik-E-Maidan](/asia/india/karnataka/bijapur/malik_e_maidan) are the other essential stops.

Bijapur is an easy day trip or overnight from [Badami](/asia/india/karnataka/badami) or [Hampi](/asia/india/karnataka/hampi), though it rewards spending a full day if you want to explore the dozens of lesser-known mosques, tombs, and tanks scattered across the old city.
