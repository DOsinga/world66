Chitradurga is a city in central Karnataka built among and around an extraordinary landscape of giant granite boulders. The rocks have been here for perhaps three billion years — some of the oldest exposed geology on earth — and they give the city its name: in Kannada, *Chitradurga* means "picturesque fort." That fort, a multilayered hill fortress threading between and over the boulders, is the main reason to come.

The [Chitradurga Fort](/asia/india/karnataka/chitradurga/chitradurga_fort) is one of the most impressive in the Deccan. It was built and extended by a succession of rulers — the Nayak Palegars, Haider Ali, and Tipu Sultan — over several centuries, and its seven concentric rings of walls enclose temples, granaries, palaces, water tanks, and secret passageways. The fort is a serious half-day walk. Inside, the [Hidimbeswara Temple](/asia/india/karnataka/chitradurga/hidimbeswara_temple) is the oldest, dedicated to the deity that local mythology ties to the Mahabharata's giant Hidimbasura, who supposedly lived on these very boulders.

Beyond the fort, the [Chandravalli Caves](/asia/india/karnataka/chitradurga/chandravalli_caves) and the [Ankali Math](/asia/india/karnataka/chitradurga/ankali_math) lie in a quiet valley south of the city. The Archaeological Museum near the fort has coins, inscriptions, and finds that give context to the layers of history stacked up here.

Chitradurga sits on the Bangalore–Pune highway (NH48), making it a practical stop on a road trip. Davangere is 60km north; Bangalore is about 200km south.
