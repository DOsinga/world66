Coorg — officially known as Kodagu — is a hilly district in southwestern [Karnataka](/asia/india/karnataka), draped in coffee plantations and spice estates. It sits in the Western Ghats at elevations between 900 and 1800 metres, which gives it a cool, misty climate that comes as a relief after the heat of the Indian plains.

The district has no single city to speak of — [Madikeri](/asia/india/karnataka/coorg/madikeri) is the administrative centre, but Coorg is better understood as a landscape: winding roads through shade-grown arabica, rushing rivers, and forested ridges that shelter elephants and leopards. The Kodava people, the indigenous community here, have a distinct culture, language, and culinary tradition quite different from the rest of Karnataka.

A short drive from Madikeri brings you to the [Namdroling Monastery](/asia/india/karnataka/coorg/namdroling_monastery) at Kushalnagar, also called the Golden Temple — a large Tibetan Buddhist complex with golden-roofed prayer halls that feels entirely incongruous with its surroundings, and all the more striking for it. [Abbey Falls](/asia/india/karnataka/coorg/abbey_falls) and the viewpoint at [Raja's Seat](/asia/india/karnataka/coorg/rajas_seat) are pleasant stops for those who want to stretch their legs without committing to a full trek. More serious walkers head to [Tadiandamol](/asia/india/karnataka/coorg/tadiandamol), Coorg's highest peak, or explore the edges of [Nagarhole National Park](/asia/india/karnataka/coorg/nagarhole) to the east. [Talakaveri](/asia/india/karnataka/coorg/talakaveri), the source of the Kaveri river, and the elephants of the [Dubare camp](/asia/india/karnataka/coorg/dubare_elephant_camp) round out the standard Coorg circuit.
