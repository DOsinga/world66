  The Queen's Bath has a structural peculiarity that puzzled
  archaeologists for years: a continuous corridor running around its
  outer perimeter with balconies overlooking the central pool, but no
  visible connection between the outer corridor and the inner area.
  It is now believed that the corridor was used by guards, with the
  royalty accessing the bath through a separate underground passage
  that has not yet been found.

One of the most elegant secular structures in the royal enclosure: a large square bathing pavilion built in an unusual blend of Hindu and Islamic architectural elements. The exterior is plain and fortress-like; the interior reveals a central pool surrounded by arched corridors with lotus-shaped water jets and decorative balconies projecting over the water.

The pool measures roughly 15 metres square and would have been filled by an aqueduct. The contrast between the austere exterior and the ornate interior is deliberate — this was a private space for the royal household, hidden from public view. It sits about a kilometre east of the Lotus Mahal, at the southern edge of the royal enclosure.
