Sravanabelagola is home to the [Gommateshwara statue](/asia/india/karnataka/sravanabelagola/gommateshwara_statue), a 17-metre monolithic figure of Lord Bahubali carved in 981 AD and widely considered the tallest freestanding monolithic statue in the world. It stands on top of Vindhyagiri Hill and can be seen for miles across the surrounding plains. Climbing the 614 rock-cut steps to the top is part of the experience.

The town is one of the most sacred Jain pilgrimage sites in India, and every 12 years hosts the Mahamastakabhisheka — an anointing ceremony in which the statue is bathed in milk, curds, saffron, and gold coins from scaffolding erected for the occasion. It draws hundreds of thousands of people. The facing [Chandragiri Hill](/asia/india/karnataka/sravanabelagola/chandragiri_hill) has a quieter cluster of Jain temples and is associated with the emperor Chandragupta Maurya, who according to Jain tradition abdicated his throne and came here to die. The [Akkana Basadi](/asia/india/karnataka/sravanabelagola/akkana_basadi) in the town itself is a well-preserved 12th-century Jain temple with fine Hoysala-era carving.

Located on the Bangalore–Hassan highway, Sravanabelagola makes an easy stop on the way to Hassan or Belur, and can be combined with the great Hoysala temple towns in a single day trip from Bangalore.
