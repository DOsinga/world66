Kasaragod is Kerala's far-northern edge, closer in feel to coastal Karnataka than to the backwater postcard version of the state. Malayalam, Tulu, Kannada, Konkani, and Beary culture overlap here, and the landscape shifts between river mouths, laterite forts, coconut groves, mosques, and temple towns.

The headline sight is [Bekal Fort](/asia/india/kerala/kasaragod/bekal_fort), south of town on the Arabian Sea, but Kasaragod is not a one-stop fort excursion. [Chandragiri Fort](/asia/india/kerala/kasaragod/chandragiri_fort) watches the river mouth, [Ananthapura Lake Temple](/asia/india/kerala/kasaragod/ananthapura_lake_temple) sits in a tank north of town, and Malik Dinar Mosque points to the old Indian Ocean trade routes that shaped the Malabar coast.

The town itself is workaday, so do not expect polished resort Kerala. Come with a car or a hired driver, build a day around the forts and sacred sites, and Kasaragod becomes a rewarding northern counterpoint to Kochi, Varkala, and the backwaters.
