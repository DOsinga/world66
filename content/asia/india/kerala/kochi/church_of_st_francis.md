type: poi
story: Vasco da Gama, who opened the sea route to India in 1498, died in Kochi in 1524 and was buried in this church. His body remained here for 14 years before being exhumed and shipped back to Lisbon — the slot that held his tomb slab can still be seen in the floor, a rectangular hollow in the ancient stone.

The Church of Saint Francis, built in 1503, is the oldest European church in India. It stands near the Fort Kochi waterfront in a quiet compound of old trees. The original structure was built by Portuguese Franciscan friars and has been rebuilt and modified several times — by the Dutch, who converted it to a Protestant church in the 17th century, and later by the British.

The interior is whitewashed and simple, with colonial-era wooden rafters and a few memorial tablets to Dutch and British officials. Punkahs — large cloth fans that were once swung by ropes attached to servants outside — still hang from the ceiling, a detail that is both practical and historical. The church is an active Anglican congregation.
