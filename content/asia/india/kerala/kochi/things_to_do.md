[Fort Kochi](/asia/india/kerala/kochi/fort_kochi) is the heart of historic Kochi — a small peninsula with Portuguese, Dutch, and British colonial architecture layered over centuries. The [Chinese Fishing Nets](/asia/india/kerala/kochi/chinese_fishing_nets) along the waterfront are the most photographed sight in Kerala, best seen at dawn when they are actually being worked.

[Mattancherry Palace](/asia/india/kerala/kochi/mattancherry_palace) contains some of the finest Hindu mural painting in India, covering the walls of three rooms with scenes from the Ramayana and Mahabharata in vivid detail. Nearby, the [Paradesi Synagogue](/asia/india/kerala/kochi/paradesi_synagogue) in the old Jewish quarter (Jew Town) is one of the oldest synagogues in the Commonwealth, still in use by the tiny remaining Jewish community.

The [Church of Saint Francis](/asia/india/kerala/kochi/church_of_st_francis) is historically significant as the site of Vasco da Gama's original burial before his remains were moved to Lisbon. The [Indo-Portuguese Museum](/asia/india/kerala/kochi/indo_portuguese_museum) covers the Catholic heritage of the region in depth.

The [Kochi-Muziris Biennale](/asia/india/kerala/kochi/kochi_biennale), held every two years (December to March), is Asia's largest contemporary art festival and has transformed Fort Kochi into a significant arts destination.
