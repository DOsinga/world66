Thiruvananthapuram — still widely known by its colonial name Trivandrum — is the capital of Kerala, perched on a series of hills near the southern tip of India. It is not a city that announces itself loudly, but it rewards close attention. The old city clusters around the [Padmanabhaswamy Temple](/asia/india/kerala/thiruvananthapuram/padmanabhaswamy_temple), one of the wealthiest and most important Vaishnava temples in the world, its seven-storey gopuram visible from most of the city centre. Around it spreads a tangle of narrow lanes, pilgrims, flower sellers, and the particular intensity of a sacred place embedded in an active metropolis.

The city has a different character from Kerala's more tourist-oriented towns. It is a government capital, a university city, and an IT hub in the making — pragmatic and ambitious without being overrun. The cultural life is genuinely strong: classical Kathakali and Bharatanatyam performances are regular events, and the [Napier Museum](/asia/india/kerala/thiruvananthapuram/napier_museum) in the north of the city houses a good collection of bronzes and temple carvings in a fantastically improbable Indo-Saracenic building. Next door, [Sree Chithra Art Gallery](/asia/india/kerala/thiruvananthapuram/sree_chithra_art_gallery) displays the Kerala and Tanjore paintings that define the region's visual tradition.

The beach at [Kovalam](/asia/india/kerala/kovalam) is about 16 km south and is the reason many travellers stop here at all. But the city itself deserves at least a day, and the [Thiruvananthapuram Zoo](/asia/india/kerala/thiruvananthapuram/thiruvananthapuram_zoo) — one of India's best, set in spacious grounds in the museum compound — is genuinely excellent if you have any interest in wildlife. The hilltop fort area, the old stone streets around the temple, and the evening light on the Arabian Sea from [Shankumugham Beach](/asia/india/kerala/thiruvananthapuram/shankumugham_beach) make the city worth time in its own right.
