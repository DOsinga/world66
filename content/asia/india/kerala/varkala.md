Varkala is Kerala's cliff-top beach town, and it looks like nothing else on this coast. A long laterite cliff drops straight down to the Arabian Sea, its red-brown face lined with coconut palms and topped by a path of restaurants, shops, and guesthouses. The beach below — [Papanasam](/asia/india/kerala/varkala/papanasam_beach), the "destroyer of sins" — stretches golden and wide beneath the bluff.

The town has been a Hindu pilgrimage site for centuries, centred on the 2,000-year-old [Janardhana Swami Temple](/asia/india/kerala/varkala/janardhana_swami_temple). That spiritual heritage gives Varkala a different feel from Goa's party beaches: pilgrims performing rituals at the water's edge share the beach with backpackers in swimwear, and somehow it works. About 3 km inland, [Sivagiri Mutt](/asia/india/kerala/varkala/sivagiri_mutt) — the ashram of the social reformer Sree Narayana Guru — is one of Kerala's most important spiritual sites and draws hundreds of thousands of pilgrims for the December festival.

The [North Cliff](/asia/india/kerala/varkala/north_cliff_area) path is where most visitors spend their time — yoga shalas, Ayurvedic clinics, and cliff-edge restaurants with sunset views. Six kilometres north, [Kappil Beach and Lake](/asia/india/kerala/varkala/kappil_beach_and_lake) offers a quieter alternative with backwater boat trips through coconut groves. Varkala is more developed than it used to be, but the dramatic coastline — red cliffs, crashing waves, coconut groves — still delivers.
