Kavaratti is the administrative capital of [Lakshadweep](/asia/india/lakshadweep), India's smallest union territory -- a scattering of coral islands in the Arabian Sea about 400 km off the Kerala coast. The island is small enough to walk across in an hour, fringed by white sand and shallow turquoise [lagoons](/asia/india/lakshadweep/kavaratti/kavaratti_lagoon).

The island's history goes back centuries. Around the 7th century, a Muslim saint was shipwrecked on nearby Amini, and the islanders eventually converted to Islam. Kavaratti is dotted with 52 mosques, the most striking being the [Ujra Mosque](/asia/india/lakshadweep/kavaratti/ujra_mosque). Its entrance is a marvel of carved driftwood -- flowing lines, sinuous scrolls and floral pillars, all shaped from timber washed ashore. A well in the mosque grounds is said to have curative powers.

Beyond the mosques, the [Kavaratti Aquarium](/asia/india/lakshadweep/kavaratti/kavaratti_aquarium) displays the reef's remarkable marine life. The real draw is the water: swimming, snorkelling, kayaking and sailing are all available on the [lagoon](/asia/india/lakshadweep/kavaratti/kavaratti_lagoon), and the coral reefs just offshore are among the best-preserved in India. Permits are required for all visitors to Lakshadweep and are arranged through tour operators or the Lakshadweep tourism office in Kochi.
