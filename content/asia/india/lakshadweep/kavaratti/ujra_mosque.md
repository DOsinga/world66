The Ujra Mosque is the most architecturally remarkable building on Kavaratti — a whitewashed structure whose entrance is framed by columns of intricately carved driftwood. The carving covers the timber in flowing organic patterns: spirals, floral motifs, and geometric forms intertwined without visible joins, the work of craftsmen who used timber salvaged from the sea because the island has no trees large enough for structural timber.

The mosque dates from the early centuries of Islam in Lakshadweep and reflects the island culture's adaptation of mainland Indian and Arab architectural traditions to the materials available on a coral atoll. It is one of 52 mosques on Kavaratti — a remarkable number for an island of about 14,000 people, reflecting the depth of Islamic culture here.

Non-Muslims may be able to view the exterior and entrance portico; it is worth asking locally about current access arrangements. The mosque is in the centre of the island, easily walkable from the main jetty.
