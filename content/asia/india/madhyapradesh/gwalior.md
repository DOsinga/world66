Gwalior is one of central India's great fortress cities, dominated by a long sandstone ridge that rises abruptly above the plain. The fort is visible from half the city and still feels like the place's organising fact: palaces, temples, Jain carvings and ramparts all sit on or around that dramatic slab of rock.

The essential visit is [Gwalior Fort](/asia/india/madhyapradesh/gwalior/gwalior_fort). Inside and around it are the tiled [Man Mandir Palace](/asia/india/madhyapradesh/gwalior/man_mandir_palace), the hybrid [Teli ka Mandir](/asia/india/madhyapradesh/gwalior/teli_ka_mandir), the delicately carved [Sasbahu Temples](/asia/india/madhyapradesh/gwalior/sasbahu_temples), and the rock-cut Jain figures of [Gopachal Parvat](/asia/india/madhyapradesh/gwalior/gopachal_parvat). Down in the city, [Jai Vilas Palace](/asia/india/madhyapradesh/gwalior/jai_vilas_palace) shows the Scindia dynasty in full 19th-century European flourish.

Gwalior is busy, dusty and not as tourist-softened as Rajasthan's famous fort towns. That is part of the attraction. You get major monuments without the same level of performance around them, and the city works well as a stop between [Agra](/asia/india/uttarpradesh/agra), [Datia](/asia/india/madhyapradesh/datia), Orchha and [Khajuraho](/asia/india/madhyapradesh/khajuraho). Give it at least one full day; two is better if you want to see the fort slowly and still have time for music history around Tansen's tomb.
