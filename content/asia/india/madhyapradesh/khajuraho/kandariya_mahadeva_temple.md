The Kandariya Mahadeva Temple is the largest and most ornate temple in the western group, dedicated to Shiva. Built around 1030 CE during the reign of the Chandela king Vidyadhara, it represents the pinnacle of the Nagara style of North Indian temple architecture, with a shikhara (tower) that soars in a series of ascending peaks.

The temple is covered with an estimated 900 sculptures on its outer walls, arranged in three horizontal bands. The figures include celestial maidens, gods, mythological scenes, and the explicitly erotic couples for which Khajuraho is known. The quality of carving is extraordinary — bodies rendered with soft flesh, drapery that seems to billow, expressions of genuine emotional intensity. Inside the dark sanctum, a marble Shiva lingam sits on a platform.

Walk slowly around the exterior in the early morning light when the sandstone glows amber and the carved surfaces reveal their depth. The temple is still used for active worship — remove shoes before entering the inner sanctum.
