Mandsaur is a district town in western Madhya Pradesh with a surprisingly rich archaeological heritage. The main attraction is the [Pashupatinath Temple](/asia/india/madhyapradesh/mandsaur/pashupatinath_temple) on the bank of the Shivna River — its rare eight-faced Shiva idol has a parallel only in the famous Pashupatinath temple in Kathmandu.

The town sits in the Malwa plateau region, where the local language is Malwi, a blend of Rajasthani and Hindi. The surrounding area is one of India's major opium-producing regions, cultivated legally under government license for the pharmaceutical industry. Ancient stone inscriptions and ruins scattered around the district point to a long history of settlement. The [Yashodharman Pillar at Sondhni](/asia/india/madhyapradesh/mandsaur/yashodharman_pillar) commemorates a sixth-century king who defeated the Huna invaders, and the [Dharmarajeshwara Temple](/asia/india/madhyapradesh/mandsaur/dharmarajeshwara_temple) — a rock-cut Shaiva shrine sometimes called the Ellora of Madhya Pradesh — is within easy reach of the town.
