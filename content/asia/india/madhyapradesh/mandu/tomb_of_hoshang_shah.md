The Tomb of Hoshang Shah is Mandu's finest marble monument, a cool white mausoleum inside a walled complex beside the Jami Masjid. Built in the 15th century, it has a quiet balance of domes, arches, and carved screens that makes it feel smaller and more intimate than Mandu's palaces.

It is also important for the story of Indo-Islamic architecture in India. The tomb's polished marble and restrained geometry make it one of the places where Mandu most clearly points toward later Mughal building.
