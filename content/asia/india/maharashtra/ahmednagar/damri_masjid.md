  The Damri Masjid's name translates roughly as "half-pice mosque" — a reference to its supposedly low construction cost, said to have been funded by a single small coin. The Damri Masjid is a partially ruined mosque from the Nizam Shahi period, standing in the older part of Ahmednagar as a reminder of the city's medieval Islamic heritage. The building's elegant stonework, particularly the carved arcading and the remains of its minaret, shows the sophistication of the Deccani architectural tradition before the Mughal conquest.

The mosque is no longer in active use but can be viewed from outside and, when the gate is open, entered for a closer look. It sits in a quiet residential neighbourhood and is less visited than Ahmednagar Fort, which makes it a more contemplative experience. The surrounding streets have old Deccani houses and the general texture of a part of the city that has changed slowly.
