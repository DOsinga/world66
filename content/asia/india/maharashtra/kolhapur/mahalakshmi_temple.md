  The temple is one of the six Shakti Peethas — sacred sites associated with Sati, the goddess whose body is said to have fallen in pieces across the subcontinent after her death. At Kolhapur, her feet are believed to have landed. The sanctum holds a black stone idol of the goddess, considered svayambhu (self-manifested), and the idol is said to be bathed in sunlight through a specially positioned window on three days each year in March.

The most important temple in Kolhapur, and one of the most significant Shakti Peethas in India. The goddess Mahalakshmi — known locally as Ambabai — draws pilgrims from across Maharashtra and beyond. The temple complex occupies a prominent position in the old city and has been a centre of religious life here since at least the 7th century, with the current structure built under the Shilaharas and expanded by later dynasties.

The architecture is Hemadpanthi style — black stone, intricately carved, with geometric patterning on the tower. Non-Hindus are welcome but should dress modestly and check current access rules for the inner sanctum. The courtyard is photogenic and the atmosphere around major festivals — especially Navratri — is electric.
