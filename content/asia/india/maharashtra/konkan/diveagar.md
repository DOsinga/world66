Diveagar is a quiet beach village on the Konkan coast of Maharashtra, about 165 km south of Mumbai. It sits on a stretch of coast known for its clean, uncrowded beaches, coconut groves, and the easy pace of village life that has largely been pushed out of the more popular Goa resorts.

[Diveagar Beach](/asia/india/maharashtra/konkan/diveagar/diveagar_beach) is long and sandy, backed by casuarina trees and coconut palms. The sea here can be rough during the monsoon, but from October to May it is calm enough for swimming. The [Suvarna Ganesh Temple](/asia/india/maharashtra/konkan/diveagar/suvarna_ganesh_temple), built around a gold Ganesh idol unearthed in a betel-nut garden in 1997, draws a steady stream of pilgrims and gives the village its modern claim to fame. The older [Rupnarayan](/asia/india/maharashtra/konkan/diveagar/rupnarayan_temple) and [Uttareshwar](/asia/india/maharashtra/konkan/diveagar/uttareshwar_temple) temples are quieter five-minute stops on a walk through the village.

The village is small, and that is the point. There is no nightlife, no beach bar scene, and almost no tourist infrastructure beyond a handful of guesthouses and homestays. If you want a weekend away from the city without the crowds of Alibaug or the distance of Goa, Diveagar delivers.
