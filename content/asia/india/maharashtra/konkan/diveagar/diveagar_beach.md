Diveagar's beach is the reason almost everyone makes the drive from Mumbai or Pune. It runs for roughly six kilometres in a slow curve from a fishing settlement at the south end to a tidal creek at the north, where migratory gulls and waders gather in the cooler months. The sand is fine and pale, the slope into the water is gentle, and the rows of casuarina trees behind the beach throw enough shade to flop down for an afternoon.

The sea is calm and swimmable from October to May. During the monsoon (June to September), the water turns brown and the rip currents make swimming a bad idea — but the empty grey beach in the rain has its own appeal. Sunsets here are reliably good: the headland to the north cuts a clean silhouette and there is almost nothing on the sand to spoil the view.

Beach activities are low-key. A few local operators run jet-skis, banana boats, and parasailing at the central stretch on weekends, but walk a hundred metres in either direction and you are alone again. There are no beach shacks of the Goan kind; food and drink come from the village a few hundred metres inland.
