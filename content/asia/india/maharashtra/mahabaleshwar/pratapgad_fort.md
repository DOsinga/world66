story: >
  On 10 November 1659, Shivaji met the Bijapur Sultanate's general Afzal Khan here for 
  a peace meeting — supposedly unarmed, as both had agreed. Afzal Khan was enormous, 
  Shivaji slight. As Khan reached to embrace him, he tightened his grip to crush the 
  smaller man. Shivaji responded with the weapons he had concealed: steel tiger-claw 
  blades (wagh nakh) on one hand and a dagger (bichhwa) in the other. Afzal Khan died 
  in the meeting, and the Maratha force that had been waiting in the forest routed the 
  Bijapur army. The fort's bronze statue of Shivaji, installed in 1957 and visible from 
  the approach road, celebrates this as the founding moment of the Maratha Empire.

Pratapgad Fort is a hilltop Maratha fortress 24 km from Mahabaleshwar, built in 1656 on the orders of Chhatrapati Shivaji as part of his campaign to push the Bijapur Sultanate back from the Konkan passes. It is one of the best-preserved Maratha forts in Maharashtra and ranks among the most historically significant — the site of one of the most celebrated incidents in Maratha history (see story above).

The fort divides into an upper and lower section. The **lower fort** contains the **Afzal Khan Tomb** (a simple structure where the Bijapur general was buried, maintained out of historical respect) and the gateway with its original cannons. The **upper fort** has the main bastions, a Bhavani Devi temple, and panoramic views over the Koyna valley and the Sahyadri ranges. The path from the car park to the top takes about 20–30 minutes and is steep in places but well maintained. The fort is a state protected monument and draws large crowds on weekends and public holidays.
