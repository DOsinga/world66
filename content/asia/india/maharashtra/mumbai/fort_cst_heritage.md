Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus (formerly Victoria Terminus or VT) is one of the finest examples of Victorian Gothic architecture in Asia, a UNESCO World Heritage Site designed by Frederick William Stevens and completed in 1887. The building combines Gothic Revival forms with Indian decorative elements in an exuberant hybrid that the architect called "Italian Gothic in an Indian setting." Stone carvings of peacocks, monkeys, and gargoyles cover every surface.

The station remains one of the busiest railway stations in India, handling tens of thousands of suburban commuters and long-distance passengers daily. Visitors can walk through the main hall and observe the extraordinary ornamental ironwork, stained glass, and stone carving while the living city moves around them. The exterior is best appreciated from the traffic island opposite where the full facade is visible.
