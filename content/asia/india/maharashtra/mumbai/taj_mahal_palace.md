The Taj Mahal Palace is Mumbai's most iconic building, a grand Edwardian-Saracenic hotel completed in 1903 by the industrialist Jamsetji Tata. Legend holds that Tata built it after being refused entry to a European hotel; the result was a building that set new standards for hospitality in India. The elaborate dome, Moorish arches, and Florentine cloister make the exterior one of the most photographed in Mumbai.

The hotel faces the Gateway of India across the waterfront promenade. Non-guests can visit the Sea Lounge café for afternoon tea overlooking the harbour, or walk through the heritage wing lobby to admire the architecture. The Sea Lounge's filter coffee and sandwiches are an affordable way to experience one of Mumbai's most celebrated addresses. The hotel was a target in the 2008 attacks and has been immaculately restored.
