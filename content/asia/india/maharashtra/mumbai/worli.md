A neighbourhood in the middle of the city that has undergone the most visible transformation in Mumbai over the past two decades — from a fishing village and industrial area to one of the primary locations for luxury residential towers, corporate offices, and the new Mumbai that the city's developers are building for its finance-sector elite. The contrast between the old **Worli Koliwada** fishing village — a settlement of the original Koli fisherfolk, with painted boats drawn up on the beach and fish drying on racks — and the gleaming towers immediately behind it is as sharp as anything in the city.

The **Bandra-Worli Sea Link** arrives here from the north, the six-kilometre cable-stayed bridge across Mahim Bay that transformed travel times between the western suburbs and South Mumbai. The bridge itself is most dramatic after dark when it is lit up. The **Haji Ali Dargah** — the white marble tomb of a Muslim saint, sitting on a rocky islet accessible only at low tide via a narrow causeway — lies off the Worli seafront, one of the most photographed images in Mumbai.
