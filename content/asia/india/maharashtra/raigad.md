[Raigad Fort](/asia/india/maharashtra/raigad/raigad_fort), in the Sahyadri ranges of the Western Ghats, was the capital of the Maratha warrior king Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj. Perched on a sheer-sided plateau roughly 820 m above the surrounding valley, it was one of the most formidable fortresses of 17th-century India. Shivaji was crowned here in 1674 and died here in 1680.

The fort is now largely ruined — the British destroyed much of it after the collapse of Maratha power — but the scale of the original construction remains impressive. The [Raigad ropeway](/asia/india/maharashtra/raigad/raigad_ropeway) (cable car) connects the base to the top; the walk up takes about two hours through forest. The iconic equestrian statue of Shivaji, the ruins of the market area, the [Maha Darwaja](/asia/india/maharashtra/raigad/maha_darwaja) gate, the [Jagdishwar Temple](/asia/india/maharashtra/raigad/jagdishwar_temple), and the tomb of Shivaji are all within the fort complex. The execution cliff at [Takmak Tok](/asia/india/maharashtra/raigad/takmak_tok) commands the western edge.

The views of the Konkan coast and the surrounding Sahyadri ranges are spectacular on a clear day. Raigad is about 120 km from Mumbai and can be visited as a day trip.
