Shillong, the capital of [Meghalaya](/asia/india/meghalaya), sits at around 1,500 metres in the Khasi Hills. The British called it the "Scotland of the East" for its pine-covered hills, cool climate, and colonial-era architecture. Today it is a lively university town with a character quite unlike anywhere else in India — the Khasi people have a matrilineal society, and the blend of tribal, colonial, and modern Indian cultures gives the city a distinctive feel.

The music scene is the first thing many visitors notice. Shillong has an outsized reputation for rock and blues, with live gigs in small venues and cafes around [Police Bazar](/asia/india/meghalaya/shillong/police_bazar) and [Laitumkhrah](/asia/india/meghalaya/shillong/laitumkhrah). Bob Dylan's influence runs deep here, and the city has produced several well-known Indian rock bands. The bustling [Bara Bazaar](/asia/india/meghalaya/shillong/bara_bazaar) is worth exploring for its displays of local produce, betel nut, dried fish, and hand-woven Khasi textiles.

Beyond the city, Shillong serves as the gateway to some of Meghalaya's most remarkable landscapes. The living root bridges of Cherrapunji and Mawlynnong — bridges grown over generations from the aerial roots of rubber fig trees — are a few hours to the south. Cherrapunji (Sohra) and Mawsynram compete for the title of wettest place on earth, and the waterfalls in the area during the monsoon are spectacular. [Ward's Lake](/asia/india/meghalaya/shillong/wards_lake) and [Shillong Peak](/asia/india/meghalaya/shillong/shillong_peak) offer pleasant outings closer to town, while [Umiam Lake](/asia/india/meghalaya/shillong/umiam_lake) on the road from [Guwahati](/asia/india/assam/guwahati) is a good spot for boating and picnics. The seven-storey [Don Bosco Museum](/asia/india/meghalaya/shillong/don_bosco_museum) is the best primer in India on the cultures of the Northeast.
