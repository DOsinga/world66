The Dasashwamedh Ghat is the ritual focal point of Jajpur, a broad flight of laterite steps descending into the slow brown Vaitarani — the river Hindus believe souls must cross on the way to the afterlife. Pilgrims from across Odisha and beyond come here specifically to offer pinda (rice balls) and tarpan (water libations) for ancestors, in the belief that rites performed on the Vaitarani's banks ease the soul's passage.

The atmosphere is unlike a typical Indian temple town. There are no chanting tour groups; instead, families sit cross-legged on the steps under the supervision of a panda (ritual priest), going through the long sequence of mantras with quiet concentration. The best time to visit is early morning when the river mist still hangs low. A small cluster of shrines and a sacred fig tree above the steps make the whole site feel like a discrete sanctuary, even as the town presses against its edges.
