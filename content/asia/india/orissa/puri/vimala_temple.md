Within the vast Jagannath Temple complex, in the southwestern corner of the compound, stands the Vimala Temple — dedicated to the goddess Vimala, the *shakti* (divine feminine power) of Lord Jagannath. Vimala is considered the presiding goddess of Puri itself, and the temple is classified as one of the 51 *Shakti Pithas* — the sacred goddess sites spread across South Asia, each marking a place where a part of the goddess Sati's body fell to earth after her death. This gives the site a significance in the Shakta tradition that runs entirely parallel to its role within the Vaishnava Jagannath cult.

The dual identity of the Jagannath complex — simultaneously one of the great Vaishnava pilgrimage sites and a major Shakti Pitha — is theologically unusual and explains something of the temple's pan-Hindu appeal. Non-Hindus cannot enter the compound to visit the Vimala Temple, but understanding its presence there enriches the sense of what the whole complex represents: not a single-tradition shrine but a site where Vaishnavism and Shaktism have coexisted and intertwined for centuries. The *mahaprasad* of the Jagannath Temple achieves its sacred status partly because Vimala has first consumed the offered food — only after her acceptance does the offering become *prasad* for distribution to pilgrims.
