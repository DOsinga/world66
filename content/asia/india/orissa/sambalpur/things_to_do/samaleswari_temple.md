---
title: "Samaleswari Temple"
type: poi
---

The Samaleswari Temple is the most revered religious site in western Odisha and one of the most important shrines in the entire state. It stands at the edge of the Mahanadi River in Sambalpur town and is dedicated to the goddess Samalei, who is regarded as the presiding deity and protector of the Sambalpuri people. The temple gives Sambalpur its name — the city was historically known as Sambalpur after Samalei. Pilgrims come from across western Odisha throughout the year, and during major festivals the ghats along the river are packed with worshippers.

The temple complex is built in the Kalinga architectural style, with a curvilinear shikhara rising above a jagamohana (assembly hall). The inner sanctum contains the idol of Samalei carved from stone, and the rituals follow a strict daily schedule from early morning aarti through to the evening service. Non-Hindu visitors are generally permitted to enter the outer precincts and observe the ceremonies; the courtyard atmosphere during morning prayer — with incense, the sound of bells, and the backdrop of the river — is genuinely striking.

The two most important festivals here are Nuakhai, the harvest festival of western Odisha celebrated in late August or September, and the Samaleswari Puja during Navratri in October. Both draw enormous crowds and the city essentially stops for the celebrations. If you visit during these periods, the scale of devotion on display is impressive, though accommodation should be booked well in advance.
