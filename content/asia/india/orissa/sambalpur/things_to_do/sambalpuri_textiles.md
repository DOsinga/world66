---
title: "Sambalpuri Textile Weaving"
type: poi
---

Sambalpur is the capital of one of India's most distinctive handloom traditions. Sambalpuri ikat — known locally as bandha — is a double-ikat resist-dyeing technique in which both the warp and weft threads are tie-dyed in complex geometric and floral patterns before weaving, so that the finished fabric displays perfectly aligned designs on both sides. The patterns draw on traditional motifs: the shankha (conch), chakra (wheel), phula (flower), and fish scales appear repeatedly, often in deep reds, blacks, and blues against natural or cream backgrounds. Sambalpuri saris and dress fabric are prized across India and the technique has a geographical indication (GI) certification.

The weaving villages around Sambalpur — particularly Sonepur, Bargarh, and Barpali — are the main production centres, but the fabric is sold throughout the city. The Odisha State Handloom Cooperative showroom in town is a reliable place to buy authentic, fairly priced Sambalpuri work without the pressure of the private market; the staff can explain what separates genuine bandha from machine-printed imitations. The main market streets near the Samaleswari Temple area also have several established textile shops.

If you want to see the weaving itself, some weavers in the surrounding villages welcome visitors. The process is slow and intricate — a complex sari can take a skilled weaver several weeks — and watching the yarn being stretched, knotted, dyed, and then gradually transformed into fabric on a pit loom is one of the more memorable craft experiences in Odisha. Ask at your hotel or the cooperative for introductions to weaving families willing to show visitors their work.
