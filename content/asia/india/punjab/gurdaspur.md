Gurdaspur is a border district in northern Punjab, sitting close to the Pakistani frontier and serving historically as a gateway to Kashmir. The district's history runs deep — from ancient habitation through Mughal rule to the Sikh kingdoms and the British Raj. The 1947 Partition cut through it with particular violence; the new border drew families, friends, and communities apart in ways still felt today.

The main draw for visitors is Sikh pilgrimage. [Dera Baba Nanak](/asia/india/punjab/gurdaspur/dera_baba_nanak) on the Ravi River is where Guru Nanak, the founder of Sikhism, spent his final years and died in 1539. Since 2019, the Kartarpur Corridor from Dera Baba Nanak allows Indian pilgrims to cross to the Pakistani shrine of Kartarpur Sahib without a visa — a remarkable opening on one of the world's most fraught borders. [Sri Hargobindpur](/asia/india/punjab/gurdaspur/sri_hargobindpur), named after the sixth Sikh Guru, has its own historic gurdwara on the Beas River.

[Batala](/asia/india/punjab/gurdaspur/batala), the district's second city, has an old town with gurdwaras associated with the early life of Guru Nanak and a reputation as a manufacturing centre for hand tools. Pathankot, in the north of the district, is the main transit point for travellers heading to Dharamsala, Dalhousie, and the Himalayan hill stations. The landscape is classic Punjab — flat agricultural plains watered by the Ravi and Beas rivers, green in the growing season and golden at harvest.
