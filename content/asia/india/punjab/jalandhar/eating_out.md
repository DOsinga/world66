---
title: Eating Out
type: section
---

Jalandhar's food is straight Punjabi: heavy on dairy, slow-cooked, and not subtle. The classic local dishes are sarson da saag with makki di roti (mustard greens with maize flatbread), Amritsari kulcha, butter chicken, and a long roster of paneer dishes. Sweets — pinnis, ladoos, gulab jamun, peda — are taken seriously.

**Bhojan Bhandar** in Ladowali Road is a long-running pure-vegetarian thali restaurant that does the classic Punjabi spread well at modest prices. **Haveli**, on the GT Road bypass on the way to Phagwara, is a tourist-aimed but genuinely good Punjabi village-style restaurant with live music and a wide menu (popular for weddings; book ahead at weekends). **Mahajan Sweets** in the bazaar near Devi Talab is the source for pinnis (winter), barfi, and good gulab jamun. **Punjabi Rasoi** near the bus station does fast, cheap chole bhature and aloo paratha breakfasts; come hungry.

For Amritsari-style fried fish, try the small stalls on Bhagat Singh Chowk in the evenings — Punjabis from across the Doaba come here for it.
