  Lalgarh Palace was built by Maharaja Ganga Singh as a memorial to his father Lal Singh, commissioned around 1902 and completed over two decades. Ganga Singh was one of the more remarkable rulers of his era — he represented India at the Paris Peace Conference in 1919 and was instrumental in getting Bikaner connected to the rail network. The palace's architect, Samuel Swinton Jacob, blended Indo-Saracenic, Rajput, and European styles to produce something that feels thoroughly sui generis.

Lalgarh Palace stands north of the old city as a monument to early-20th-century Rajasthani ambition. Built from deep red sandstone with elaborate carved screens (jali work), the palace combines Rajput tradition with European grandeur — a ballroom sits alongside zenana quarters, and carved pillars alternate with wrought-iron balconies.

Part of the palace now operates as a heritage hotel, but a significant wing houses the Shri Sadul Museum, dedicated to the life and times of Maharaja Sadul Singh. The museum contains archival photographs, hunting trophies, and personal effects that offer an unexpectedly intimate window into Bikaner's royal history. The gardens are open and pleasant to walk through; the overall complex is worth at least an hour.
