  Bundi has over 50 step-wells — more than any other town in Rajasthan — built over several centuries as a public infrastructure for water storage in a semi-arid landscape. The Raniji ki Baori, built in 1699 by the queen regent Nathavati Ji, is the largest and most elaborately carved. Its three storeys of galleries descend to a narrow well shaft that reaches the water table, and every surface is covered in sculptural detail.

The Raniji ki Baori (Queen's Step-well) is the finest of Bundi's many step-wells and one of the most beautiful in India. Built in 1699, it descends nine storeys to the water level, its sides carved with friezes of elephants, apsaras, and scenes from Hindu mythology. The proportions are remarkable: the step-well is engineered so that sunlight reaches the water surface at the bottom for only a few hours each day, dramatically illuminating the carvings.

Visiting on a quiet morning — before the tour groups arrive — is the best experience. Climb down to the lowest accessible level and look up: the stepped galleries frame the sky above in a perfect geometric rectangle. The site is managed by the Archaeological Survey of India and is free to enter.
