[Chittorgarh Fort](/asia/india/rajasthan/chittorgarh/chittorgarh_fort) is the largest fort in India, sprawling across a 180-metre-high plateau for over 13 km in circumference. It is also the most historically charged site in Rajasthan — the fort was besieged three times, and each defeat ended with the women performing jauhar (mass self-immolation) rather than face capture.

The fort contains ruined palaces, temples, towers, and reservoirs spread over a vast area. The [Vijay Stambha](/asia/india/rajasthan/chittorgarh/vijay_stambha) (Tower of Victory) and [Kirti Stambha](/asia/india/rajasthan/chittorgarh/kirti_stambha) (Tower of Fame) are the most iconic structures. The [Rani Padmini Palace](/asia/india/rajasthan/chittorgarh/rani_padmini_palace) marks the spot associated with the most famous of the fort's tragic stories, and the [Meera Temple](/asia/india/rajasthan/chittorgarh/meera_temple) commemorates the poet-saint Mirabai who lived here in the sixteenth century. The town below has little to detain you, but the fort itself deserves a half day. An unusual day trip is the [Bhojunda Stromatolite Park](/asia/india/rajasthan/chittorgarh/bhojunda_stromatolite_park), where 1.6-billion-year-old fossils are exposed in a field six kilometres from town. Chittorgarh is well connected by rail to [Ajmer](/asia/india/rajasthan/ajmer), [Jaipur](/asia/india/rajasthan/jaipur), Ratlam, and [Udaipur](/asia/india/rajasthan/udaipur).
