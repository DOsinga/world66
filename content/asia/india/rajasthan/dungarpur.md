Dungarpur is one of those Rajasthani towns that tourism forgot. Tucked into the Aravalli foothills in southern Rajasthan, about 110 km from [Udaipur](/asia/india/rajasthan/udaipur) and close to the Gujarat border, it has no luxury heritage hotels drawing weekend crowds and no tour buses clogging the lanes. What it does have is two extraordinary palaces, a landscape dotted with Bhil tribal villages, and a pace of life that feels decades removed from the rest of India.

The [Juna Mahal](/asia/india/rajasthan/dungarpur/juna_mahal), the Old Palace, is the real draw. This seven-storey fortress rising above the town centre looks forbidding from outside, but the interiors are astonishing: room after room of miniature paintings, mirror work, and coloured glass that have survived remarkably intact since the 17th century. On the edge of [Gaib Sagar lake](/asia/india/rajasthan/dungarpur/gaib_sagar_lake), the [Udai Bilas Palace](/asia/india/rajasthan/dungarpur/udai_bilas_palace) mixes Rajput grandeur with Victorian flourishes, its pale stone reflecting in the water.

Beyond the town, the countryside rewards exploration. The [Beneshwar shrine](/asia/india/rajasthan/dungarpur/beneshwar_temple) sits at the confluence of the Som and Mahi rivers and comes alive during the Beneshwar Fair in February, one of Rajasthan's largest tribal gatherings. The [Deo Somnath temple](/asia/india/rajasthan/dungarpur/deo_somnath_temple) is worth the short drive. This is Bhil heartland, and the weekly markets in surrounding villages offer a glimpse of a Rajasthan that most visitors never see.
