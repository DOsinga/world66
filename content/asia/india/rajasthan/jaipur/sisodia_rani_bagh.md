Built in 1728 by Maharaja Sawai Jai Singh II for his second wife, a Sisodia Rajput princess from Udaipur, this terraced garden palace on the Agra Road is a formal Mughal-style garden — fountains, water channels, and tiered pavilions — with frescoes inside the garden chambers depicting the life of Krishna.

The garden is rarely crowded and offers a pleasant contrast to the fortified monuments closer to the city. The murals in the main hall have suffered over the years but remain vivid in places, particularly the scenes of Krishna's childhood in Vrindavan. The garden layout, with its multilevel terraces and central fountain axis, is elegantly proportioned.

About 8 km east of the city center on the Agra Road, making it convenient to combine with Galtaji Temple nearby.
