If the Blue City is Jodhpur's main attraction, Navchokiya is its heart — the quarter where the indigo paint is thickest on the walls and the lanes become so narrow that two people with loaded bags can barely pass. The name means "nine crossings" and refers to the nine small squares connected by lanes that form the neighbourhood's informal structure. In practice, the grid dissolves within minutes of entering.

Getting deliberately lost here is the point. The lanes open unexpectedly onto small temples, chai stalls, workshops where craftsmen repair bicycles or hammer brassware, and sudden terraces with views over a sea of blue rooftops to the fort. The best time is early morning, when the light is soft and the lanes belong to people going about their day rather than to other visitors.
