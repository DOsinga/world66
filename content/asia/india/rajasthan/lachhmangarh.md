Lachhmangarh (also spelled Laxmangarh) is a small town in the Shekhawati region, founded in 1862 by Rao Raja Laxman Singh of Sikar. It is part of the string of Shekhawati towns known for their painted havelis -- merchant mansions decorated with elaborate frescoes depicting everything from mythology to European trains and telephones.

The [town's hilltop fort](/asia/india/rajasthan/lachhmangarh/lachhmangarh_fort), built on scattered boulders, is privately owned and closed to visitors, but you can climb the ramp to a temple near the entrance for good views over the town. The real attractions are at street level: the [Char Chowk Haveli](/asia/india/rajasthan/lachhmangarh/char_chowk_haveli) has well-preserved paintings including some explicit erotic imagery, and the [Rathi Family Haveli](/asia/india/rajasthan/lachhmangarh/rathi_haveli) near the clock tower features delightfully over-the-top paintings of European women and British soldiers. Lachhmangarh fans of modern fiction may recognize it as the model for the hometown of Balram Halwai in Aravind Adiga's Booker Prize-winning novel *The White Tiger*.
