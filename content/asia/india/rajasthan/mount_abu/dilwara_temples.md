Five marble temples built between 1031 and 1582 by Jain devotees on a forested hillside 2.5 km from Mount Abu town. The exteriors are plain, almost domestic-looking — Jain temples were deliberately disguised to avoid attracting attention during Muslim incursions. Inside is some of the finest stone carving anywhere in India: ceilings carved into petal-thin lotuses, pillared halls where every surface is incised with figures and floral patterns, doorways framed in pierced marble screens. The **Vimal Vasahi** (1031) and the **Luna Vasahi** (1230) are the two masterpieces. Photography, leather items, and phones are not allowed inside. Open afternoons to non-Jain visitors; arrive after noon and allow 90 minutes.
