Pushkar Lake is a sacred lake surrounded by 52 bathing ghats (stepped platforms descending to the water) and a skyline of temples. It is the central spiritual focus of Pushkar and one of the most sacred bodies of water in India. Pilgrims come year-round to bathe in its waters, and the ghats are busy from before dawn until after dusk with offerings, prayers, and ritual bathing.

The lake is fed partly by underground springs and partly by seasonal rainfall. Its levels have dropped over the decades due to overuse and reduced rainfall, and it is a source of ongoing conservation concern. The water is considered holy regardless of its physical condition — pilgrims continue to bathe and conduct puja ceremonies throughout the year.

Walking the ghats in the early morning, before the day heats up, is one of the best experiences Pushkar offers. The light at dawn is remarkable, the activity on the water intense, and the priests at the main ghats conduct elaborate rituals as the sun rises over the surrounding Aravalli hills. This is not a place for photographs without considerable sensitivity — many devotees object strongly.
