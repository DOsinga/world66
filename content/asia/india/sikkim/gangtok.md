Gangtok, Sikkim's capital, is a surprisingly lively hill town perched at 1,650 metres in the Himalayan foothills. At first glance it has the noise and bustle of any Indian city, but a little further into town the setting takes over — prayer flags flutter between buildings, monasteries cling to ridges above, and on clear days the massive bulk of Kangchenjunga fills the northern horizon.

The main drag runs along [MG Road](/asia/india/sikkim/gangtok/mg_road), a pedestrianised stretch with restaurants, shops, and views down the valley. Above the city, [Enchey Monastery](/asia/india/sikkim/gangtok/enchey_monastery) is worth the steep climb for its quiet atmosphere and panoramic outlook. The [Do-drul Chorten](/asia/india/sikkim/gangtok/do_drul_chorten) stupa in the town centre, encircled by 108 prayer wheels, is the easiest Buddhist site to visit and buzzes with activity morning and evening. The [Namgyal Institute of Tibetology](/asia/india/sikkim/gangtok/namgyal_institute_of_tibetology), south of town, houses one of the finest collections of Tibetan manuscripts outside Lhasa.

Most visitors use Gangtok as a transit hub on the way to the more remote villages of [Pelling](/asia/india/sikkim/pelling) or Yuksom, but it deserves a day or two in its own right. Rumtek Monastery, one of the most important centres of Tibetan Buddhism outside Tibet, is an easy half-day trip, and [Tsomgo Lake](/asia/india/sikkim/chaggutsomgolake), a sacred glacial lake at 3,780 metres, is about two hours away by jeep.
