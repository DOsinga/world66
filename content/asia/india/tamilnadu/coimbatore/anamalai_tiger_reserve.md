  The reserve contains one of the last viable populations of the Nilgiri tahr — a stocky wild goat unique to the Western Ghats that was hunted nearly to extinction by colonial-era sportsmen. The population at Anamalai has recovered since protection began, and the animals can sometimes be seen from the roads through the forest.

The Anamalai Tiger Reserve covers 958 sq km of Western Ghats forest between Tamil Nadu and Kerala, beginning about 50 km south of Coimbatore. The reserve holds tigers, elephants, leopards, gaurs, and an exceptional range of birdlife — it is one of the better places in India to see wild Asian elephants in open forest. Jeep safaris into the Valparai plateau section of the reserve can be arranged through the forest department or local tour operators.

The road from Coimbatore to Pollachi and then into the hills is itself scenic, climbing through tea and coffee estates on the approach to the forest. The Valparai area has good guesthouses and can be used as a base for multiple days. The reserve is at its best from October to March; the monsoon from June to September closes some tracks.
