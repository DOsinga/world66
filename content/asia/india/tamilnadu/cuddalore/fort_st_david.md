Fort St David is one of the earliest British settlements in India, established by the East India Company in the 1690s on land purchased from a local ruler. It served as the Company's main stronghold on the Coromandel Coast for decades, surviving multiple sieges before finally being demolished by the French in 1758. Today only scattered ruins remain — stretches of wall, earthworks, and the outline of the old fortifications — but the site has historical weight for anyone interested in the colonial history of south India.

The ruins sit on the seafront south of the main town. They are not formally maintained or interpreted as a tourist site, and there is no museum or signage to speak of, but the atmosphere is atmospheric. The views over the Bay of Bengal and the surrounding landscape give a sense of why this particular stretch of coast was so strategically valued by successive European powers — Dutch, Portuguese, French, and British all held territory in the Cuddalore area at different times.
