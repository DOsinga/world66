The filter coffee stalls near the bus stand and the main temple entrances are among the most useful spots in Kanchipuram — not because they are destinations, but because they are the hinge between temple visits and the rest of the day.

Tamil filter coffee is made in a two-part stainless steel filter: coarsely ground dark-roast coffee mixed with chicory is packed into the upper chamber, hot water poured over, and the concentrate drips through into the lower over twenty minutes or so. The stall keeper mixes a measure of concentrate with hot milk and sugar, pours it back and forth between tumbler and davara (the wide saucer that doubles as a cup) to cool it and build a slight froth, and hands it to you. The whole ritual takes about thirty seconds.

Drink it standing at the counter — that's the convention. Sitting is sometimes available but the standing format suits the pace of the town. The cost is negligible.

There is no single best stall to recommend; the quality across the town's coffee vendors is relatively consistent, and finding one is not difficult. Look for the cluster near the Ekambaranathar Temple's east gopuram, or ask any auto-rickshaw driver where he drinks his coffee.
