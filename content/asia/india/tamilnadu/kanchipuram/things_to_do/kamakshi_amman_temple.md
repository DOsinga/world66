The most actively worshipped temple in Kanchipuram, and one of the three most important Shakti shrines in south India — the others being Meenakshi Amman in Madurai and Visalakshi in Varanasi. Kamakshi is a form of Parvati, and the goddess here is considered especially powerful; pilgrims come from across Tamil Nadu and beyond to seek her blessing.

The temple dates in parts to the early Pallava period, though it has been heavily rebuilt and expanded over the centuries. Architecturally it is not as pure as Kailasanatha or Vaikuntha Perumal, but that misses the point: this is a living, breathing religious centre, not a monument. The inner sanctum, accessible to Hindus only, is perpetually crowded with worshippers. The outer precincts and the golden gopuram are open to all.

The atmosphere is very different from the ASI-managed Pallava temples. Priests, flower sellers, and devotees fill the corridors; the smell of camphor and jasmine is constant; rituals happen at close quarters throughout the day. For visitors interested in Hinduism as a living practice rather than as archaeology, Kamakshi Amman is the most illuminating place in Kanchipuram.

The temple is in the town centre, walkable from most hotels. Dress codes apply; remove shoes at the entrance.
