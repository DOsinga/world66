Srirangam is a river island in the Cauvery near Tiruchirappalli, and almost the entire island is occupied by the [Sri Ranganathaswamy Temple](/asia/india/tamilnadu/srirangam/ranganathaswamy_temple) — one of the largest functioning temple complexes in the world. The temple has seven concentric enclosure walls, the outermost of which is over 4 km in circumference and contains shops, houses, and streets. The innermost sanctum houses the reclining form of Lord Vishnu as Sri Ranganatha, and the site is one of the most sacred Vaishnava shrines in South India.

The Rajagopuram, the southern gateway tower, is 73 metres tall with 13 tiers and is considered the tallest temple tower in Asia. Construction began in the medieval Chola period and continued under successive dynasties — the Rajagopuram itself was started in the 14th century and not completed until 1987. The temple is listed as a UNESCO Tentative World Heritage Site.

The [Jambukeswarar Temple](/asia/india/tamilnadu/srirangam/jambukeswarar_temple) in Thiruvanaikaval, a short distance from the island, is one of the Pancha Bhuta Stalas — five Shiva temples representing the classical elements. This one represents water: the primary lingam stands permanently submerged in a natural spring. The two temples together are the main reason to visit the area. The [Rock Fort temples](/asia/india/tamilnadu/srirangam/rockfort_temples) in Trichy city, across the river, are an easy addition for a full day.

Srirangam is a short auto-rickshaw or bus ride from Tiruchirappalli city centre, making it an easy half-day excursion, though a full day comfortably covers all three sites.
