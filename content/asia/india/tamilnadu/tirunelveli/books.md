**A Comparative Grammar of the Dravidian or South-Indian Family of Languages** by Robert Caldwell is the foundational work of comparative Dravidian linguistics, written by the long-serving Anglican bishop of Tirunelveli and published in 1856. Long, dense, and astonishingly modern in its method; the only book of its time to put Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, and Malayalam on a firm scientific footing.

**The Hungry Tide of History** by V. Ramachandran is a recent local history of the Thamirabarani valley from prehistoric times to the present, weaving together archaeology, temple inscriptions, and oral tradition. Best read after a visit to the Porunai Museum.

**My God Died Young** by Sasthi Brata — although not specifically about Tirunelveli — is one of the most candid memoirs of growing up in a south Indian Brahmin family in the colonial period, and gives a vivid sense of what mission-college towns like Palayamkottai were like in the early 20th century.

For broader Tamil context, Vikram Sampath's *Splendours of Royal Mysore* and A.R. Venkatachalapathy's *The Province of the Book* are both useful, even though their main focus lies further north.
