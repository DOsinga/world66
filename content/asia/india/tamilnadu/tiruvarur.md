Tiruvarur is a small town in the Cauvery Delta between Thanjavur and Nagapattinam, known primarily for the **[Thyagaraja Temple](/asia/india/tamilnadu/tiruvarur/thyagaraja_temple)** — one of the Pancha Bhuta Stalas, the five ancient Shiva temples each representing one of the classical elements. Tiruvarur's temple represents earth (prithvi). The complex is genuinely large and ancient, with a 36-hectare temple tank that serves as one of the town's most distinctive landmarks.

The town is also the birthplace of the Carnatic music composer Thyagaraja (1767–1847), who spent most of his working life in Thiruvaiyaru but is closely associated with this town; the annual Thyagaraja Aradhana festival attracts classical musicians from across south India to perform in his honour. Tiruvarur has a second musical connection: M.S. Subbulakshmi, the most celebrated Carnatic vocalist of the twentieth century, was born here in 1916.

Beyond the great temple, the surrounding Cauvery Delta countryside is flat and intensively cultivated — paddy fields, banana groves, and small villages connected by narrow roads. The [Amirthakadeswarar Temple](/asia/india/tamilnadu/tiruvarur/amirthakadeswarar_temple) at Thirukadaiyur, about 10 km west, is a well-known marriage blessing temple; the [Oppiliappan Temple](/asia/india/tamilnadu/tiruvarur/oppiliappan_temple) at Thirunageswaram to the north is a significant Vaishnava site. Together with the great temple in Kumbakonam, these form a natural temple circuit for visitors interested in Dravidian religious architecture.

Tiruvarur is 55 km east of Thanjavur by road and rail, and makes a reasonable stop on the route to Nagapattinam. Accommodation is basic — day visitors from Thanjavur or Nagapattinam is a sensible strategy.
