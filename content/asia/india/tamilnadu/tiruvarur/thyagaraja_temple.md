The Thyagaraja Temple (also called the Thiru Thyageswara Temple) is one of the most important Shiva temples in Tamil Nadu, dedicated to the lord as Thyagarajaswamy and classified as one of the Pancha Bhuta Stalas — the five temples associated with the five classical elements. This temple represents earth, and the main shrine is said to stand directly on the element it embodies.

The complex is enormous: a 36-hectare walled precinct containing multiple shrines, mandapams, and one of the largest temple tanks in south India. The main gopuram (gateway tower) rises dramatically over the flat delta landscape and is visible from considerable distance. The temple is a working religious centre with regular puja ceremonies; non-Hindus are generally permitted in the outer courtyards but not all inner sanctums. Early morning and evening pujas are the best times to witness temple life. The surrounding tank — used for temple festivals and ritual bathing — is worth walking around for the views back towards the temple towers.
