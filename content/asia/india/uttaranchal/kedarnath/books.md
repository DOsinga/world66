title: "Books"
type: section
---

**Bill Aitken, *The Nanda Devi Affair*** — by a Scottish-Indian writer who lived in the Garhwal hills for decades. Not specifically about Kedarnath but the best portrait in English of the western Himalayan landscape and its religious geography.

**Ruskin Bond, *The Roads to Mussoorie*** — gentle essays and stories by Garhwal's most beloved writer-in-residence. Bond never visited Kedarnath himself but conveys the texture of the surrounding hill country better than any guidebook.

**Stephen Alter, *Sacred Waters: A Pilgrimage Up the Ganges River to the Source*** — a perceptive account of one season on the Char Dham circuit, written by an American who grew up in Mussoorie. Includes a full section on the Kedarnath pilgrimage.

**Wilfrid Noyce, *Climbing the Fish's Tail*** — older but still readable account of climbing in the Garhwal Himalaya in the 1950s, with good descriptions of the approach valleys.

**Hugh Thomson, *Nanda Devi: A Journey to the Last Sanctuary*** — an attempted modern pilgrimage to one of the great closed-off mountains of the region. Conveys the politics and the spiritual weight of the western Himalayas.
