The Triveni Sangam is the confluence of three rivers: the Ganges, the Yamuna, and the mythical underground Saraswati, visible to the faithful but invisible to the merely physical eye. For Hindus, bathing at the Sangam washes away sin and breaks the cycle of rebirth — the theological claim is absolute, and the devout act on it. The ghats are busy year-round; during the Magh Mela (held annually in January and February) and the Kumbh Mela (every twelve years), they become among the most intensely populated places on the planet.

The physical confluence — where the brown Ganges and the greener Yamuna meet — is visible from the ghats or from a hired boat. Boatmen row pilgrims out to the exact meeting point of the waters, where people immerse themselves, pour offerings, and float oil lamps. The ceremony is ancient, sincere, and genuinely moving to witness regardless of your own beliefs. Go at dawn for the quietest experience, and hire a boat to see the confluence from the water.
