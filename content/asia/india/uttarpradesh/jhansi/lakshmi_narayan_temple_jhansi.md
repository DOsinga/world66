The Lakshmi Narayan Temple is one of Jhansi's most active Hindu pilgrimage sites, a Vaishnava temple with an ornate shikhara that stands out against the flat roofscape of the old city below the fort. The main shrine is dedicated to Vishnu and Lakshmi; subsidiary shrines hold Hanuman and Shiva images. The temple precinct is busy through most of the day and genuinely crowded at dawn and dusk puja.

The architecture dates primarily from the 18th century and shows the regional Bundela style — bold proportions, detailed carving on the mandapa, and a plainer sanctum than the elaborate exterior might suggest. The temple is freely open to non-Hindu visitors but remove footwear at the gate and avoid photography of the main idol. The lane leading to the temple from the fort-side bazaar passes through one of the older quarters of the city and is worth walking slowly.
