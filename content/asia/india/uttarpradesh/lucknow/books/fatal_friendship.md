Rosie Llewellyn-Jones is the foremost living historian of Nawabi Lucknow — a British scholar who has worked the city's archives for forty years — and "A Fatal Friendship: The Nawabs, the British and the City of Lucknow" is the best single-volume account of how the city rose and fell in the 18th and 19th centuries. Her writing is less literary than Dalrymple's but more authoritative on Lucknow specifically.

Several of her other books — "A Very Ingenious Man" on Claude Martin and his strange house at Constantia, and "The Last King in India" on Wajid Ali Shah — are equally rewarding for anyone serious about the city.
