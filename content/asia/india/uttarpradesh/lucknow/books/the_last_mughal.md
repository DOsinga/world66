Dalrymple's 2006 history of the 1857 Rebellion is centred on Delhi and the deposition of the last Mughal emperor, Bahadur Shah Zafar, but the most riveting chapters cover the simultaneous siege of Lucknow and the destruction of the Nawabi city. Drawing on Persian and Urdu sources that had never been used by Anglophone historians, Dalrymple reconstructs the siege day by day from inside the Residency and from the rebel camp outside.

Read it before visiting the Residency; the ruins acquire meaning they otherwise lack. Pair it with Mirza Ghalib's letters, which Dalrymple quotes throughout, for the most vivid contemporary voice on the events.
