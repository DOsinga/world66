Mirza Hadi Ruswa's 1899 novel is the foundational text on Nawabi Lucknow — the fictionalised confession of a courtesan named Umrao Jaan, dictated to the author in her old age, in the city's last years before the British annexation. The book is part lament for a lost world of poetry recitals and refined manners, part unflinching account of how that world treated the women at its centre.

It was twice made into Hindi films (1981 and 2006) that anyone who has spent time in India will have heard of. The English translation by Khushwant Singh and M.A. Husaini is the best entry point; the original Urdu rewards anyone with the patience.
