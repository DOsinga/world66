Mathura is one of the great pilgrimage cities of northern India, revered as the birthplace of Krishna and the heart of the Braj region. It sits on the Yamuna, close enough to Agra to be tempting as a side trip, but the mood is completely different: temple bells, river ghats, milk sweets, and streets thick with devotional traffic.

The city's strongest anchors are close together. [Shri Krishna Janmabhoomi](/asia/india/uttarpradesh/mathura/shri_krishna_janmabhoomi) is the central shrine complex; [Vishram Ghat](/asia/india/uttarpradesh/mathura/vishram_ghat) is where Mathura turns toward the river; and [Dwarkadhish Temple](/asia/india/uttarpradesh/mathura/dwarkadhish_temple) is the old-city temple that comes alive during Holi, Janmashtami, and Diwali.

Mathura is not a calm place. The lanes can be crowded, the traffic is wearing, and the sacred geography spills beyond the city into Vrindavan, Govardhan, Barsana, and Gokul. But even with one day, Mathura makes a strong case for itself. It is not just a gateway to Krishna country; it is one of the places where that devotion is most visible, noisy, and alive.
