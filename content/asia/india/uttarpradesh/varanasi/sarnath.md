Sarnath, 10 km northeast of central Varanasi, is one of the four most sacred sites in Buddhism. The Gautama Buddha came here after his enlightenment at Bodh Gaya and delivered his first sermon in the Deer Park — a teaching that set the doctrine of Buddhism in motion. The site remained a major Buddhist centre until it was sacked and largely destroyed in the 12th century.

The key monument is the Dhamek Stupa: a massive cylindrical structure 39 metres high, built around the spot where the first sermon was given and rebuilt multiple times over the centuries. The surrounding archaeological park contains excavated monastery foundations, an Ashokan pillar (the original capital now in the museum), and well-maintained gardens. The Sarnath Archaeological Museum holds some of the finest Buddhist sculpture in India, including the original lion capital.

The contrast with Varanasi is striking — Sarnath is quiet, green, and serene. Several Buddhist nations (Japan, China, Thailand, Sri Lanka) have built temples and monasteries here, adding a sense of international pilgrimage character. An auto-rickshaw from Varanasi takes 20–30 minutes.
