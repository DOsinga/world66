Vrindavan is one of the great Krishna pilgrimage towns of North India, a sacred place on the Yamuna where devotion is not a museum piece but the daily noise of the streets. Temple bells, rickshaw horns, flower sellers, monkeys, chanting pilgrims, and narrow lanes all press together here.

The town's religious geography is dense. [Banke Bihari Temple](/asia/india/uttarpradesh/vrindavan/banke_bihari_temple) is the emotional centre for many visitors, [Kesi Ghat](/asia/india/uttarpradesh/vrindavan/kesi_ghat) turns the town toward the river, and older shrines such as [Govind Dev Temple](/asia/india/uttarpradesh/vrindavan/govind_dev_temple) and [Rangji Temple](/asia/india/uttarpradesh/vrindavan/rangji_temple) show how many architectural languages have been pulled into Krishna worship.

Vrindavan is not restful. The lanes can be exhausting, temple crowds swell without warning, and scams around guides and donations are common enough to be worth a clear head. But if you want to understand the Braj region beyond the monuments of [Agra](/asia/india/uttarpradesh/agra) and the birthplace shrines of [Mathura](/asia/india/uttarpradesh/mathura), Vrindavan is essential.
