Asansol is West Bengal's second-largest city, in the Chota Nagpur coalfields about 200 km northwest of Kolkata. It grew in the 19th century as the British developed the coal seams of the Burdwan district — coal remains the city's defining industry, which locals sometimes call "black diamond." The city has [one of the oldest railway stations](/asia/india/westbengal/asansol/asansol_railway_station) in the state, still an important junction on the Howrah-Delhi main line.

It is primarily an industrial and transit city rather than a tourist destination. Those stopping here are usually passing between Kolkata and the hill stations, or visiting the nearby [Maithon](/asia/india/westbengal/asansol/maithon_dam) and [Panchet](/asia/india/westbengal/asansol/panchet_dam) dams on the Damodar River, which offer pleasant lake scenery and boating. The [Kalyaneshwari temple](/asia/india/westbengal/asansol/kalyaneshwari_temple) at Kalyanpur, about 25 km away, is a significant Hindu pilgrimage site in the region. Closer in, the [Ghaghar Burhi](/asia/india/westbengal/asansol/ghaghar_burhi) shrine sits in a wooded ravine on the eastern edge of town, and [Burnpur](/asia/india/westbengal/asansol/burnpur_iisco_works) — the captive township around the IISCO steel plant — has the city's most interesting urban character.
