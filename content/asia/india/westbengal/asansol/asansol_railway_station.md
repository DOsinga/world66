  The station opened in 1863 as part of the East Indian Railway's main line from Calcutta to Delhi. Asansol's value to the British was the coalfields immediately south; the railway needed coal for its locomotives, and the station grew into one of the busiest junctions in eastern India almost from the start.
---

Asansol Junction is one of the oldest and busiest railway stations in eastern India — seven platforms, both Howrah-Delhi mainline trains and a clutch of regional services, and a steady stream of long-distance passengers at almost any hour. It is the reason most travellers ever come to the city.

For trainspotters and railway enthusiasts, the adjacent Asansol electric locomotive shed is one of the largest in India. For everyone else, the station itself is a useful base for a few hours' wait: the food courts at the platforms are above average, and a short walk into Railpar will turn up the usual array of Bengali sweet shops.
