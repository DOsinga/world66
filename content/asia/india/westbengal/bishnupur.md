Bishnupur is the great terracotta temple town of western [West Bengal](/asia/india/westbengal). It was the capital of the Malla kings of Mallabhum, who turned brick, laterite, and local craft into one of the densest historic townscapes in the state.

The reason to come is the temple circuit. [Rasmancha](/asia/india/westbengal/bishnupur/rasmancha), built around 1600, is unlike anything else in Bengal: a stepped pyramidal pavilion ringed by rows of arched corridors. Nearby, [Shyamrai Temple](/asia/india/westbengal/bishnupur/shyamrai_temple) and [Jorbangla Temple](/asia/india/westbengal/bishnupur/jorbangla_temple) carry fine terracotta panels showing Krishna legends, court scenes, boats, soldiers, animals, and episodes from the epics.

Bishnupur is not only brick temples. [Madanmohan Temple](/asia/india/westbengal/bishnupur/madanmohan_temple), [Dalmadal Cannon](/asia/india/westbengal/bishnupur/dalmadal_cannon), the old stone gateways, and [Bishnupur Museum](/asia/india/westbengal/bishnupur/bishnupur_museum) fill out the Malla story. The town is also known for Baluchari silk saris and the Bishnupur gharana of Hindustani classical music, both reminders that this was a courtly cultural centre, not just a cluster of ruins.

One long day is enough for the main monuments if you hire a cycle rickshaw or toto between clusters. Stay overnight if you want softer light on the terracotta panels and time to look at the silk shops without rushing back to Kolkata.
