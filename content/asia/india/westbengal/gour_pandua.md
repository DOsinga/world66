Gour-Pandua is the real reason travelers stop around Malda in northern [West Bengal](/asia/india/westbengal). The modern town supplies beds, food, rail access, and hired cars; the reward is outside it, among the broken capitals of medieval Bengal.

The two sites are different but belong together. [Gour](/asia/india/westbengal/gour_pandua/dakhil_darwaza) has citadel gates, mosques, fragments of walls, and the [Firoz Minar](/asia/india/westbengal/gour_pandua/firoz_minar), spread along quiet roads near the Bangladesh border. [Pandua](/asia/india/westbengal/gour_pandua/adina_mosque) is anchored by the huge Adina Mosque and a cluster of tombs and dargahs in a greener, less urban setting.

This is not a polished archaeological park. Signage can be thin, distances are awkward, and Malda remains the practical base. But if you care about Bengal's sultanate history, terracotta ornament, ruined gateways, and old capitals absorbed into mango country, Gour-Pandua clears the bar easily.
