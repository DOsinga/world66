story: >
  The Rupnarayan River rises in the Chota Nagpur Plateau and travels some 150 km before meeting the Hooghly here at Gadiara. The point of confluence is considered sacred in the Hindu tradition; small offerings are sometimes placed on the water by locals, and the spot has an unhurried, contemplative quality that the larger river ghats to the north entirely lack.
---

The junction of the Rupnarayan and Hooghly rivers is the reason Gadiara exists as a destination. The view across the water is wide and open — the Hooghly at this point is broad enough that the far bank seems distant, and on clear mornings the light on the water is striking. Fishing boats work the confluence throughout the day.

Small wooden boats can be hired at the ghat for short river trips. The boatmen know the local currents and will take you out onto the water for an hour or two at a negotiated price. There is no formal booking system; you arrange it on arrival. This is the most direct way to experience why Gadiara draws visitors at all.
