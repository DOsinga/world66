Santiniketan is the university town Rabindranath Tagore made famous: a place of open-air classes, murals, red earth, music, seasonal festivals, and quiet experiments in how education might live with nature. It sits near Bolpur in West Bengal, close enough to Kolkata for a long day trip, but better with a night if you want the pace to slow down.

The heart of the visit is the Visva-Bharati campus and the Tagore houses of the [Rabindra-Uttarayana Complex](/asia/india/westbengal/santiniketan/rabindra_uttarayana_complex). The campus is not a normal sightseeing zone, and access can be restricted, but its best places make the town's identity clear: [Upasana Griha](/asia/india/westbengal/santiniketan/upasana_griha), [Kala Bhavana](/asia/india/westbengal/santiniketan/kala_bhavana), Chhatimtala, and the groves where the school tried to keep learning close to trees and seasons.

Santiniketan is strongest when you let the cultural life matter as much as the buildings. The [Poush Mela](/asia/india/westbengal/santiniketan/poush_mela), Baul music, Sonajhuri's weekend market, Santal culture, and nearby nature stops all add to the same story: a small town that became one of Bengal's great creative symbols.
