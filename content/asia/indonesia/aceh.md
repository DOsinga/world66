Aceh occupies the northern tip of Sumatra and has always been a place apart. It was the first part of Southeast Asia to convert to Islam, centuries before the rest of the archipelago, and its deeply conservative Islamic identity has shaped its character ever since. Aceh was also the point of Sumatra closest to the 2004 Indian Ocean earthquake epicentre; the tsunami that followed killed over 100,000 people here and reshaped the landscape of the coast. Recovery has been remarkable but the scars remain.

The provincial capital is [Banda Aceh](/asia/indonesia/aceh/banda_aceh), a functional city with a scattering of colonial-era buildings and the [Baiturrahman Grand Mosque](/asia/indonesia/aceh/banda_aceh/baiturrahman_grand_mosque), its white domes visible across the city, which survived the tsunami and became a symbol of the recovery. From Banda Aceh it is an hour by fast ferry to [Sabang Island](/asia/indonesia/aceh/sabang_island) (Pulau Weh), one of the best diving and snorkelling destinations in Sumatra, with clear water, healthy reefs, and the Kilometer Zero monument marking Indonesia's western starting point.

Inland, the [Gunung Leuser National Park](/asia/indonesia/aceh/sights) spans the border with North Sumatra and is one of the last places on earth where tigers, orangutans, elephants, and rhinos share the same forest. The [Alas River](/asia/indonesia/aceh/sights) cuts through it — a popular white-water rafting corridor. Aceh operates under a regional version of Islamic law (sharia) and travellers should dress conservatively and be respectful of local custom. Alcohol is not widely available.
