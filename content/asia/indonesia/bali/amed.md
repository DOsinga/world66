Amed is not a single neat town so much as a string of fishing villages stretched along Bali's dry northeastern coast. It is the island's antidote to Kuta: black volcanic beaches, outrigger canoes pulled above the tide line, salt pans beside the road, and Mount Agung rising behind everything. The pace is slow, the evenings are dark, and that is the point.

The water is the reason to come. [Jemeluk Bay](/asia/indonesia/bali/amed/jemeluk_bay) has the easiest snorkelling and a fine sunset viewpoint, [Lipah Beach](/asia/indonesia/bali/amed/lipah_beach) is good for a relaxed swim, and the [Japanese Shipwreck](/asia/indonesia/bali/amed/japanese_shipwreck) near Banyuning is a shallow wreck that even confident snorkellers can enjoy. Divers use Amed as a gentler base for the reefs here and for dawn trips to the [USAT Liberty wreck](/asia/indonesia/bali/tulamben/usat_liberty_wreck) in nearby [Tulamben](/asia/indonesia/bali/tulamben).

Amed is also a useful base for East Bali's inland sights. [Pura Lempuyang Luhur](/asia/indonesia/bali/amed/pura_lempuyang_luhur), high on the slope above the coast, is one of Bali's most important temple complexes, while [Tirta Gangga](/asia/indonesia/bali/amed/tirta_gangga) is the old Karangasem royal water palace in the rice fields to the southwest.

Come for a few nights, not a rushed day trip. The roads are long from south Bali, and Amed only makes sense when you slow down enough to watch the fishing boats leave before dawn and the volcano fade at sunset.
