Canggu is the neighbourhood that happened to [Bali](/asia/indonesia/bali) when the Airbnb generation arrived. Ten years ago it was a village of rice fields, surf breaks, and a handful of warung. Now it is one of Southeast Asia's most concentrated digital nomad hubs, with co-working cafes on every corner, boutique yoga studios in converted barns, and a beach road of beach clubs, smoothie bars, and Instagram-ready restaurants. It has somehow managed to remain genuinely pleasant despite all of this.

The surf is the anchor. [Echo Beach](/asia/indonesia/bali/canggu/echo_beach) and [Batu Bolong](/asia/indonesia/bali/canggu/batu_bolong_beach) are serious breaks that have been drawing intermediate-to-advanced surfers for decades; the point at [Berawa](/asia/indonesia/bali/canggu/berawa_beach) is gentler. Surf schools line the beach roads. At sunset the water is full of shortboarders and longboarders catching the last light, and the bars set back from the beach fill up to watch them. The sunsets here, facing due west, are exceptional.

Behind the beaches the neighbourhood extends into rice fields and smaller lanes where the old Bali persists alongside the new cafes. Pura Batu Bolong, a small temple on a black-rock promontory at the south end of the beach strip, is one of the prettiest sea temples on the island. The rice fields north of the main road are genuinely beautiful and largely unvisited by the tourists who stay on the coast.

Canggu sits between [Seminyak](/asia/indonesia/bali/seminyak) to the south and the quieter villages of Pererenan and Cemagi to the north. It is noisier and more crowded than either, but the combination of surf, social scene, and beautiful countryside is hard to resist. Most people who come for a week end up staying longer.
