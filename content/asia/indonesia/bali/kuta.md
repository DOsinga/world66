*Asia > Indonesia > [Bali](/asia/indonesia/bali) > Kuta*

Kuta is Bali's loudest, brashest beach town — a 1970s surfers' hideout that grew into the island's mass-tourism engine. The original village is a tangle of narrow gangs (lanes) running back from a long west-facing beach that catches a clean Indian Ocean swell. Surfers still come for the waves, but Kuta today is mostly bars, T-shirt shops, and the trail of holidaymakers walking between them with cold Bintangs.

The town blends into three neighbours along the coast. **Tuban**, to the south, sits between the beach and the airport — quieter, more package-tour. **Legian** is just north and softens the noise. Keep going and you hit **Seminyak**, the upmarket end where Kuta turns into beach clubs, restaurants with reservation books, and villa hotels. The four merge without obvious borders; a thirty-minute walk takes you across all of them.

What survives from old Kuta is the sunset routine. The beach faces straight west, the sand is wide and flat, and at dusk half the town walks down to watch the sun drop into the ocean. Tourists, families, surf kids, drink sellers — everyone ends up at the waterline. It is still the best free thing Kuta does.

Be honest with yourself before booking. Kuta is not the place for temple silence or rice-terrace mornings — for that, head to **Ubud** or further north. It is the place for surf, beer, traffic, and a base near the airport. Treated that way, it works.
