Lake Batur is a volcanic crater lake that fills a large portion of the Batur caldera floor. At about 16 kilometres long and 8 kilometres wide, it is the largest lake in Bali. The water is deep and clear, and the lake is encircled by the outer caldera wall — from Songan's shore you look across the lake to the volcanic peak of Mount Batur rising from its centre, an arrangement that makes for a striking view at almost any hour.

The lake supports a fishing community whose outrigger boats are out in the early mornings. The fish — a local freshwater species — is served at restaurants throughout the villages around the caldera. The water from the lake is considered sacred and connects, according to Balinese Hindu belief, with springs across the island; the Pura Ulun Danu Batur temple is dedicated to the lake deity. Swimming is possible but not especially common given the cultural significance of the water.
