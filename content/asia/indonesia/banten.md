Banten is the westernmost province of Java, separated from Sumatra by the narrow Sunda Strait. It was carved out of West Java as a distinct province in 2000, but its history runs much deeper: the Sultanate of Banten was one of the most powerful trading states in Southeast Asia in the 16th and 17th centuries, a cosmopolitan port that drew merchants from China, India, Arabia, and Europe. The ruins of that sultanate — the Surosowan and Kaibon palaces, the great mosque at Banten Lama, the Dutch Speelwijk fortress — survive in the old town of Banten near the northern coast.

The province is dominated by the western end of the Sunda volcanic arc. The active [Krakatoa](/asia/indonesia/banten/anak_krakatau) complex rises from the Sunda Strait itself — the islands visible on a clear day from the coast near [Carita](/asia/indonesia/banten/pandeglang/carita_beach). At the southwestern tip of Java sits [Ujung Kulon National Park](/asia/indonesia/banten/pandeglang/ujung_kulon), a UNESCO World Heritage Site and the last stronghold of the Javan rhinoceros, one of the rarest large mammals on earth.

Most travelers pass through Banten quickly on the way from Jakarta to the Ujung Kulon peninsula or to the surf and beach areas around Anyer and Carita. The provincial capital Serang is functional rather than interesting. [Pandeglang](/asia/indonesia/banten/pandeglang), the main regency covering the southwest of the province, is the gateway to the national park and to the area's quieter beaches. The Jakarta-Merak toll road makes the north coast accessible as a weekend escape from the capital — a two-hour drive.
