The limestone interior of Biak island is honeycombed with natural cave systems that the Japanese garrison used as fortifications, command posts, and final refuges during the American assault of May to June 1944. Several of these caves are accessible to visitors, most notably the complex known as Goa Jepang (Japanese Cave), located a few kilometres from Biak town along the coastal road.

The caves contain rusting equipment, shell casings, and in some places the visible marks of the fighting that ended here over eighty years ago. Local guides can navigate the cave systems and provide context about the battle — which was largely obscured by the near-simultaneous D-Day landings in Normandy and remained little-known in the West. The experience is sobering rather than spectacular; the scale of what happened in these narrow passages requires imagination rather than dramatic staging.
