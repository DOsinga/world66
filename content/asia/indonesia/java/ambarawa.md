A small town on the junction of the national road between [Yogyakarta](/asia/indonesia/java/yogyakarta) and [Semarang](/asia/indonesia/java/semarang), Ambarawa is mostly forgotten today but played an important role during the Indonesian independence struggle — the Battle of Ambarawa, commemorated at the [Palagan Ambarawa Monument](/asia/indonesia/java/ambarawa/palagan_monument), is still remembered every 15 December as Hari Juang Kartika.

The main draws for visitors are [Rawa Pening Lake](/asia/indonesia/java/ambarawa/rawa_pening_lake), beautiful at sunrise, and the [Ambarawa Train Museum](/asia/indonesia/java/ambarawa/ambarawa_train_museum) where you can ride colonial-era steam locomotives through mountain scenery on one of the last surviving cog railways in Asia. The vast 1834 Dutch fort, [Fort Willem I](/asia/indonesia/java/ambarawa/fort_willem_i), is half-overgrown and atmospheric. From here you can continue up the hill to the [Bandungan flower market](/asia/indonesia/java/ambarawa/bandungan_market) and the Hindu temple complex of [Candi Gedong Songo](/asia/indonesia/java/ambarawa/gedong_songo) on the slopes of Gunung Ungaran. [Salatiga](/asia/indonesia/java/salatiga) and Magelang are also easily reached.
