The Bogor Botanical Gardens (Kebun Raya Bogor) are one of the finest tropical botanical gardens in Asia — 87 hectares of lawns, lakes, and dense plantings in the heart of the city, home to over 15,000 species of tropical plants including palms, orchids, ferns, and the enormous Amorphophallus titanum, the giant corpse flower. The gardens were founded by the Dutch in 1817 and have served as a centre of tropical botanical research ever since.

Walking the grounds takes two to three hours at a comfortable pace. Highlights include the large lotus pond, the towering stands of mahogany and fig trees that have been growing here for two centuries, and the orchid house, which holds one of the largest collections of Indonesian orchids anywhere. The presidential palace sits in the northwest corner of the gardens and is visible through the perimeter fence — the lawns around it are home to a herd of spotted deer that wander freely. Entry to the gardens is very cheap by any measure; arrive in the morning before the tour groups from Jakarta.
