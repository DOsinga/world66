The Bogor Zoology Museum, part of the Research Center for Biology, houses one of the largest natural history collections in Southeast Asia — over 2.5 million specimens spanning insects, fish, birds, mammals, and reptiles, though only a fraction is on public display at any given time. The museum was founded in 1894, making it one of the oldest natural history institutions in Indonesia.

The public galleries present Indonesian fauna in somewhat dated but comprehensive dioramas, with a particular strength in the bird collection — Indonesia has extraordinary avian diversity, and the museum's holdings reflect this. The collection of Javan specimens is especially significant: many species documented here are now rare or extinct on the island. The museum is a short walk south of the main Botanical Gardens, next to the zoological research campus. Admission is inexpensive and crowds are light.
