Kudus is a compact city on the north side of Central Java, east of [Semarang](/asia/indonesia/java/semarang), with a stronger identity than its size suggests. It is one of Java's old Islamic towns, closely tied to Sunan Kudus and the Wali Songo saints who helped spread Islam across the island. It is also the place most Indonesians associate with kretek, the clove-scented cigarette that became one of the country's most distinctive mass products.

The centre of the visit is [Menara Kudus Mosque](/asia/indonesia/java/kudus/menara_kudus_mosque), a 16th-century mosque complex whose brick tower looks more like a Majapahit-era Hindu-Javanese shrine than a standard minaret. Pilgrims come for Sunan Kudus's tomb, but architecture-minded travellers should come too: the mosque is one of Java's clearest examples of religious change leaving older visual language intact.

Kudus's other half is industrial and everyday. The [Kretek Museum](/asia/indonesia/java/kudus/kretek_museum) explains how clove cigarettes grew from local craft to national business, while food stalls around the old centre serve soto Kudus and buffalo dishes shaped by the city's habit of avoiding beef out of respect for Hindu tradition.

This is not a polished tourist town. It works best as a deliberate stop on a north-coast Java route, especially if you are already linking Semarang, Demak, Jepara, and Rembang. Give Kudus a spare day and it rewards you with a very Javanese mix of saints, smoke, brickwork, and street food.
