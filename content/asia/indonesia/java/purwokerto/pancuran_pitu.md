Pancuran Pitu — literally "seven spouts" in Javanese — is a row of seven warm-water outlets that emerge from the rock high on the southern flank of Mount Slamet, about 2.5 km above the main Baturraden park. The water comes out at around 60–70°C, smelling distinctly of sulphur, and flows over orange and yellow mineral terraces before mixing with the cold stream below. There is no swimming pool here as such; visitors stand under the spouts for as long as they can stand the heat, or wallow in the warm mud below.

The walk up from Lokawisata Baturraden takes around 45 minutes through forest and is the most pleasant part of the experience — pine trees, occasional troops of long-tailed macaques, and views back down across the Banyumas plain. There is a small entrance fee paid at the gate near Pancuran Pitu, and a few warungs selling tea, instant noodles and pisang goreng. Bring a towel; the path back down is muddy and the air is cool once you stop walking.
