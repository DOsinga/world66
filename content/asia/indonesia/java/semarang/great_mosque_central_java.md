The Great Mosque of Central Java (Masjid Agung Jawa Tengah) is one of the largest mosques in Indonesia and one of the most architecturally ambitious. Completed in 2006, the complex blends Javanese architectural elements — a tiered joglo-style roof — with a large dome and minarets drawn from broader Islamic tradition. The result is distinctive and confident, neither derivative nor confused.

The outer courtyard is covered by six large retractable shade umbrellas modelled on the famous ones at the Prophet's Mosque in Medina. When deployed, they create a vast covered space that can accommodate thousands of worshippers during Friday prayers or special occasions. The mosque also contains a museum of Islamic history in Java and a library open to visitors.

Non-Muslim visitors are welcome outside of prayer times; appropriate dress (covered shoulders and legs, head covering for women) is required and lent at the entrance if needed. The mosque is about 4 km east of the city center, easily reached by Trans Semarang bus.
