Tugu Muda — the "Monument of Youth" — stands at the center of a traffic roundabout surrounded by some of Semarang's finest colonial government buildings, including the old Governor's Residence and the Central Java military command. The monument was built in 1953 to commemorate the Battle of Semarang, a fierce five-day clash between Indonesian nationalist youth and Japanese forces in October 1945 during the chaos following Japan's surrender.

The monument itself is a conical form representing a cornhusk flame — a symbol of Indonesian independence drawn from the country's agricultural heritage. The surrounding buildings give the square a stately, formal character at odds with the chaos of Indonesian traffic that swirls around it.

Tugu Muda is a useful orientation point in the city and is visible from Lawang Sewu, which stands directly opposite. Together with the colonial government buildings and the old railway headquarters, this area gives the best sense of what Semarang looked like at the height of Dutch colonial power.
