  Founded in 1916 by the Dutch naturalist H.F.K. Kommer, Surabaya Zoo was once one of the
  finest zoological gardens in Asia. It fell on hard times after independence and became
  notorious in the 2010s for the deaths of animals in its care — including a giraffe found
  with 20 kg of plastic in its stomach. Significant investment and management changes have
  since improved conditions substantially, though animal welfare standards remain contested.

Surabaya Zoo is the largest zoo in Southeast Asia by land area, home to more than 350 species including orangutans, Sumatran tigers, Komodo dragons, pygmy hippos, and an extensive bird collection. For visitors with children it is an impressive place, covering some 37 hectares in the south of the city.

The zoo's history and controversies make it more complex than a typical visit; for those interested in conservation and Indonesian wildlife, the animals on display represent species under genuine threat in the wild. The Komodo dragons and the Sumatran tigers are the highlights. Arrive early before the heat builds and bring water.
