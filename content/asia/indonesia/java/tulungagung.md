Tulungagung is a town and regency in southern East [Java](/asia/indonesia/java), inland from a rough stretch of Indian Ocean coast. It is known inside Indonesia for marble, but the better reason for travellers to pause here is the mix of remote beaches, small Majapahit-era temple remains, and a more local feel than the better-known East Java stops.

This is not a polished resort base. The beaches are scattered across the south of the regency, roads can be slow, and a car or motorbike makes a big difference. What you get in return is a quieter corner of Java: cliff-backed coves, strong surf, small waterfalls near the shore, and old brick-and-stone temple sites that rarely feel crowded.

Tulungagung makes most sense for travellers already crossing southern East Java, or for people who have seen [Malang](/asia/indonesia/java/malang), [Surabaya](/asia/indonesia/java/surabaya), and the volcanoes and want a less obvious day or two. It is a place to move around from, not a town where the main street itself does all the work.
