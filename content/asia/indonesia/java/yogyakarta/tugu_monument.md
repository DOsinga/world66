The Tugu is the white cylindrical pillar with a pointed top that marks the northern end of Yogyakarta's ceremonial axis — the invisible line running from Merapi through the Kraton to Parangtritis Beach on the Indian Ocean. This axis is one of the defining ideas of Javanese cosmology, and Hamengkubuwono I built the original monument in 1755, the year he founded Yogyakarta, to mark the line's northern boundary.

The monument stands in the middle of a busy roundabout just north of Jalan Malioboro, and reaching it requires navigating the traffic. But it repays the effort: the white pillar at dusk, with Merapi sometimes visible on the horizon to the north, makes the cosmic axis idea suddenly legible. The neighbourhood around it, Tugu, is a quiet, colonial-era district that has become a centre for boutique cafés and arts spaces — a good place to wander before or after a Malioboro visit.
