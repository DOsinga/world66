Banjamarsin is a city built on water. Its maze of canals and rivers thread through the urban fabric, and life plays out on and around the water — in stilt houses, on wooden boats, and at the famous floating market that draws visitors from across the island. It is the most accessible introduction to Kalimantan and a comfortable base for onward exploration.

The highlight is the early-morning floating market — [Lok Baintan](/asia/indonesia/kalimantan/banjamarsin/lok_baintan_floating_market) for the real thing, or the closer [Kuin](/asia/indonesia/kalimantan/banjamarsin/kuin_floating_market) for a quicker visit — where vendors in dugout canoes sell fruit, vegetables, and cooked food on the river. You can rent a speedboat at Jalan Pos to get out on the water and also reach the river islands of [Pulau Kaget](/asia/indonesia/kalimantan/banjamarsin/pulau_kaget) and [Pulau Kembang](/asia/indonesia/kalimantan/banjamarsin/pulau_kembang), both worth a visit. Back on land the [Sultan Suriansyah Mosque](/asia/indonesia/kalimantan/banjamarsin/sultan_suriansyah_mosque) and the modernist [Sabilal Muhtadin](/asia/indonesia/kalimantan/banjamarsin/sabilal_muhtadin_mosque) bracket Banjarese religious architecture across four centuries. From Banjamarsin you can head east to [Samarinda](/asia/indonesia/kalimantan/samarinda), the best starting point for trips up the Mahakam River to Dayak villages.
