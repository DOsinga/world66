Samarinda is the provincial capital of East Kalimantan and a major river port on the Mahakam River. The city of around 800,000 people is the main starting point for travel into the Bornean interior — [the Mahakam valley](/asia/indonesia/kalimantan/samarinda/mahakam_river_journey), with its Dayak longhouses, lake communities, and dense rainforest, begins effectively at [Samarinda's docks](/asia/indonesia/kalimantan/samarinda/sungai_kunjang_port).

The city itself sits on both banks of the Mahakam, which is broad and busy with traffic: coal barges, passenger ferries, longboats, and the occasional luxury houseboat heading upriver. The [waterfront near the Masjid Raya Darussalam](/asia/indonesia/kalimantan/samarinda/masjid_raya_darussalam) is the most atmospheric part of town, with food stalls and fishing boats at dusk. The [Islamic Centre of Samarinda](/asia/indonesia/kalimantan/samarinda/islamic_centre_samarinda) — a strikingly large mosque visible from the river — is the city's architectural landmark, completed in the 2000s and now one of the largest mosques in Southeast Asia.

Beyond the waterfront, Samarinda is a practical city rather than a scenic one. Coal mining and timber have made it prosperous, and the main interest for travellers is less the city itself than the Mahakam corridor that begins here. The [Pampang Cultural Village](/asia/indonesia/kalimantan/samarinda/pampang_cultural_village) on the northern edge of the city offers a window into Dayak culture without needing a boat ticket. The [Pasar Pagi](/asia/indonesia/kalimantan/samarinda/pasar_pagi) is the liveliest introduction to the city's rhythms — produce, spices, river fish, and rattan goods from upriver communities. The award-winning [Citra Niaga market](/asia/indonesia/kalimantan/samarinda/citra_niaga) is worth seeking out for its architecture and for *sarung Samarinda*, the city's celebrated handwoven textile.

Samarinda is also becoming a significant waypoint for travellers headed to Balikpapan or the new Indonesian capital Nusantara, located in East Kalimantan about 120 kilometres south. A half-day trip to [Tenggarong](/asia/indonesia/kalimantan/samarinda/tenggarong_day_trip) — the former Kutai royal capital — adds historical depth to any stay.
