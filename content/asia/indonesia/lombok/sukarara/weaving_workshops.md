  Sasak tradition long held that a young woman was not eligible for marriage until she could demonstrate proficiency at the loom. The custom faded in the 20th century but its legacy is the village itself — when nearly every girl had to learn to weave, the skill became universal, and Sukarara turned from an ordinary farming village into the island's textile capital. The most complex pieces, woven with as many as fifteen threads in parallel on a backstrap loom, still take three to six months to complete.

Throughout Sukarara, women sit at backstrap looms on the verandahs of their homes, leaning back against a strap that tensions the warp and working with a shuttle and a wooden beater. The looms are simple — no foot pedals, no metal parts — but the cloth they produce is among the most complex in Indonesia. The signature Lombok songket weaves gold or silver thread through silk or cotton in geometric patterns, while ikat pieces use pre-dyed warp threads to build up images of mountains, fauna, or ancestral motifs.

Visitors are encouraged to try the loom themselves, and many of the cooperatives offer a brief hands-on demonstration before the inevitable visit to the showroom. The lesson is humbling: weaving a centimetre of plain cloth takes most novices ten minutes, and a single mistake unravels the row. The point lands quickly — when a finished piece carries a price tag in the millions of rupiah, it is hard to feel it is too much.
