Ambon is the largest city in Maluku and the capital of South Maluku province, situated on the island of the same name in eastern Indonesia. Its transport connections — a busy airport, regular ferries, and fast boat services — make it the natural gateway to the wider Maluku archipelago.

The city itself has few grand sights, but the island surrounding it has considerable appeal: colonial Dutch forts, world-class diving waters, and quiet villages with strong spice-trade histories. Ambon was one of the most bitterly contested prizes of the 17th-century spice trade — the Dutch massacred English merchants here in 1623 and held the clove and nutmeg monopoly for over a century. [Fort Victoria](/asia/indonesia/maluku/ambon/fort_victoria) in the city centre survives from that era, and the [Museum Siwalima](/asia/indonesia/maluku/ambon/siwalima_museum) on a hill above the bay collects the region's ethnographic and natural history.

The waters around Ambon Bay are among the best muck diving destinations in the world. The [Laha area](/asia/indonesia/maluku/ambon/laha_muck_diving) on the bay's northern shore hosts an extraordinary density of rare and bizarre marine life — frogfish, rhinopias scorpionfish, blue-ringed octopus, and ghost pipefish attract underwater photographers from around the world. [Natsepa Beach](/asia/indonesia/maluku/ambon/natsepa_beach) on the northern coast is the island's most popular beach, famous for its rujak fruit salad sellers and calm swimming water. The [Martha Christina Tiahahu Monument](/asia/indonesia/maluku/ambon/martha_christina_tiahahu) on Karang Panjang hill offers the best sunset view over the city and bay.
