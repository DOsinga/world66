Masohi is a practical, rough-edged town on Elpaputih Bay, on the south coast of Seram. It is not the prettiest place in Maluku, but it is the island's main service centre and the usual jumping-off point for some of Seram's best wild country: Manusela National Park, Mount Binaiya, freshwater springs, caves, mangroves, and the beaches on the north coast.

The town itself was founded in 1957 and its name is tied to the Indonesian idea of gotong royong, mutual cooperation. That planned-town origin gives Masohi a different feel from the older spice-island ports elsewhere in Maluku. It is more useful than romantic, but useful matters on Seram.

Give Masohi a day if you are already on Seram and want to reach beyond the ferry pier. [Mount Binaiya](/asia/indonesia/maluku/masohi/mount_binaiya) is a serious expedition rather than a casual stroll, while [Ora Beach](/asia/indonesia/maluku/masohi/ora_beach) and the springs and caves around Tehoru show why Seram is worth the extra trouble after Ambon.
