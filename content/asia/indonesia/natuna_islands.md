The Natuna Islands sit far out in the South China Sea, closer in feeling to Borneo and the Malay peninsula than to the busy resort islands of western Indonesia. They are not easy islands: flights are limited, boats are slow, and English is thin on the ground. That distance is also the point.

Most visitors base themselves around Ranai on Great Natuna, then work outward to granite beaches, mangrove boardwalks, fishing villages, and small offshore islands. [Aleph Stone Park](/asia/indonesia/natuna_islands/aleph_stone_park) is the quick visual hit, with huge rounded boulders set beside clear water. [Senua Island](/asia/indonesia/natuna_islands/senua_island) and [Genting Island](/asia/indonesia/natuna_islands/genting_island) are the stronger day-out choices if you have arranged a boat.

Natuna also has a frontier edge. The islands are Malay, Muslim, and maritime, with long links to Riau and the Johor world, but they now sit near one of Asia's most argued-over fishing grounds. You come here for reefs, slow village harbours, and the pleasure of being somewhere that has not been smoothed into a package.
