Nias is a large island off the west coast of Sumatra, separated from the mainland by a deep channel and culturally distinct from anywhere else in Indonesia. The Niha people developed in relative isolation for millennia, and the traces of that isolation are everywhere: the megalithic villages with their massive stone pavements and carved ancestor figures, the ceremonial stone-jumping tradition (fahombo), and the extraordinary chief's houses that can be 20 meters long with curved roofs rising like upturned boats.

The island's geography divides it naturally between north and south. The north is centered on [Gunungsitoli](/asia/indonesia/nias/gununsitoli), the capital and main port, a practical city rebuilt substantially after the 2005 earthquake. The south is centered on [Teluk Dalam](/asia/indonesia/nias_selatan/teluk_dalam), the gateway to the island's two great attractions: the traditional villages of Bawomataluo and Hilisimaetano, where stone-jumping performances are still held for important ceremonies, and the surf beaches of Lagundri Bay, which carry one of the best right-hand reef waves in the world. Surfers from Australia and Europe have been coming here since the 1970s.

The 2004 tsunami and the 8.6-magnitude earthquake of March 2005 caused devastating damage across the island, killing thousands and destroying most of Gunungsitoli. A decade of reconstruction with NGO support has largely rebuilt the infrastructure, though the emotional scars run deep. Getting to Nias requires either a flight to Binaka Airport (connections from Medan and Padang) or a ferry from Sibolga on the Sumatran mainland — a crossing of around twelve hours.
