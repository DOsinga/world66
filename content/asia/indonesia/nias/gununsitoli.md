Gunungsitoli (also written Gununsitoli) is the capital and largest city of [Nias Island](/asia/indonesia/nias), spread along the northeastern coast where the hills meet the sea. Founded as a Dutch fortification in the 1600s, it remained the only part of the island effectively under Dutch control until 1914. That history — colonial fort, missionary outpost, trading hub — still shows in the old missionary cemetery and century-old churches in the southern part of town.

The March 2005 earthquake, magnitude 8.6 and among the ten most powerful ever recorded worldwide, killed 220 people in Gunungsitoli and flattened nearly a third of its buildings. Reconstruction has reshaped much of the centre, and the Nias Earthquake Monument near the waterfront keeps the memory alive. What you see today is largely a new city laid over the footprint of the old one — functional rather than beautiful, but busier and more confident than you might expect.

Gunungsitoli is not a destination in itself, but for anyone coming to Nias it is the inevitable entry point. Binaka Airport connects to Medan and Padang; ferries from Sibolga on the Sumatran mainland dock at the port. The Museum Pusaka Nias, housing more than 6,000 objects of Nias cultural heritage, is the one attraction that genuinely warrants time before heading south. From here, roads run through the interior to [Teluk Dalam](/asia/indonesia/nias/teleuk_dalam) and the world-class surf at Lagundri, passing traditional megalithic villages along the way.
