Bawomataluo — "sun hill" in Nias — is the most spectacular of the traditional villages of South Nias, set on a ridge above Teluk Dalam and reached up a long flight of stone steps. The village is laid out around a long central plaza paved with megalithic stones: polished benches, vertical columns, a stone throne for the chief, and the famous two-metre stone tower used for fahombo, the warrior stone-jumping ritual that is the symbol of Nias culture.

The houses are extraordinary in their own right: enormous timber structures with steeply pitched roofs and curved gables, raised on rows of huge wooden columns, with the largest of them — the omo sebua, the chief's house — built in the late nineteenth century from interlocking beams without nails. Bawomataluo survived both the 2004 tsunami (it sits well above sea level) and the 2005 earthquake with relatively little damage, and is one of the best-preserved megalithic villages in Southeast Asia.
