Gorontalo is a low-key port city on the north coast of Sulawesi, far less visited than [Manado](/asia/indonesia/sulawesi/manado) but with a sharper local identity than most transit towns. It is Gorontaloan rather than Minahasan, Muslim rather than mainly Christian, and close to mangroves, lake country, old forts, and one of Indonesia's more unusual wildlife encounters.

The headline experience is early-morning [whale shark watching at Botubarani](/asia/indonesia/sulawesi/gorontalo/botubarani_whale_sharks), just outside the city. It is a short, practical outing rather than an expedition, but seeing the world's largest fish from a small boat is not a small thing. The encounter needs care and a light touch; choose operators who keep distance and do not crowd the animals.

Back on land, [Otanaha Fortress](/asia/indonesia/sulawesi/gorontalo/otanaha_fortress) gives Gorontalo its best view and a bit of history, while [Lake Limboto](/asia/indonesia/sulawesi/gorontalo/lake_limboto) shows the marshy inland landscape that shapes the city. The old centre still has scattered colonial-era houses and the [Baiturrahim Great Mosque](/asia/indonesia/sulawesi/gorontalo/baiturrahim_great_mosque), a useful marker of the city's religious life.

Gorontalo is still a gateway to the [Togian Islands](/asia/indonesia/sulawesi/togian_islands), but it is not only that. Give it a day before the ferry and you get a different Sulawesi from the one most travellers rush through.
