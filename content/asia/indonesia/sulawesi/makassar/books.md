title: "Books"

---

**A History of Modern Indonesia** by Adrian Vickers is the most readable single-volume introduction to how Indonesia came to be the country it is — the colonial period, independence, Suharto, and the reform era all covered with clarity. Essential background for anywhere in the archipelago, but especially useful given Makassar's complicated colonial history.

**Lord Jim** by Joseph Conrad draws on Conrad's own time as a sailor in Southeast Asian waters. Parts of the novel are set in the Makassar Strait and among the Bugis trading communities; the atmosphere, if not the exact geography, still resonates in the port city.

**Lonely Planet Indonesia** covers Makassar, Sulawesi, and the surrounding islands in practical detail. Not elegant reading, but useful for transport logistics, accommodation, and day-trip planning, particularly for the Spermonde archipelago and Tana Toraja.

**Sea Nomads of Southeast Asia** or similar works on Bugis and Makassarese maritime culture — ask at a bookshop in Makassar for current titles, as the local selection on Bugis seafaring history is often better than what is available abroad. The Bugis were among the most accomplished blue-water sailors in the world, and their story is told imperfectly in most general histories.
