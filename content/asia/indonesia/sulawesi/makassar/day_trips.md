---
title: "Day Trips"
type: section
---

**Rammang-Rammang** (45 km north) is one of the most dramatic landscapes in Sulawesi: a field of tall karst limestone towers rising from flat paddy fields, laced with mangrove rivers navigable by small wooden boat. Hire a driver from Makassar, take a boat through the karst channels, and walk to the small village at the centre. Best in the dry season but accessible year-round.

**Spermonde Archipelago** — a scatter of small islands 30 to 60 minutes by boat from Makassar — offers white sand, clear water, and snorkelling over coral reefs. The islands closest to the city are popular and can be crowded on weekends; go further out for a quieter experience. Boats leave from various points along the coast; arrange through a guesthouse or directly at the jetty. Dry season (April–October) only — the seas in the wet season are rough enough to make the crossing unpleasant.

**Malino** (80 km east) is a cool highland town at around 1000 metres, used as a retreat from the coastal heat since Dutch colonial times. There are waterfalls, tea plantations, and forested hills. The drive up is pleasant and the temperature drop on arrival is welcome. A comfortable day trip with your own transport.

**Sungguminasa and Gowa** (10 km south) is where the old Gowa Sultanate, once the most powerful kingdom in eastern Indonesia, had its centre. The Balla Lompoa (Great House) is a reconstructed royal palace with a museum of regalia and artefacts. The Bantimurung waterfall and butterfly park is nearby — touristy but worth it if you have children in tow or a genuine interest in entomology.
