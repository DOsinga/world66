Palu is the main city of Central Sulawesi, set where the Palu River meets a long, narrow bay. It is not a polished resort town, and the 2018 earthquake and tsunami are still part of the city's recent memory. But it has a stronger sense of place than a simple transit stop.

The landscape is the first surprise. Palu is one of Indonesia's driest cities, with brown hills around the bay and a waterfront that feels very different from the wet, green Sulawesi many travellers expect. [Datokarama Park](/asia/indonesia/sulawesi/palu/datokarama_park) and [Talise Beach](/asia/indonesia/sulawesi/palu/talise_beach) are the easiest places to take in that setting.

For culture, the [Central Sulawesi State Museum](/asia/indonesia/sulawesi/palu/central_sulawesi_state_museum) is the useful first stop, especially for the Kaili culture and the megalithic landscapes deeper in the province. [Sou Raja](/asia/indonesia/sulawesi/palu/sou_raja) gives a small but concrete link to Palu's local rulers, while the [Nosarara Nosabatutu Monument](/asia/indonesia/sulawesi/palu/nosarara_nosabatutu_monument) looks back over the bay and the city.

Palu is still best used as a base for Central Sulawesi rather than as an end in itself. But if you are already nearby, a spare day here is better spent on the museum, bayfront, and viewpoints than waiting indoors for the next bus or flight.
