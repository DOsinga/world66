Baiturrahman Grand Mosque is the building that anchors Banda Aceh. Its black domes, white walls, reflecting pools, and broad marble courtyard give the centre of the city a sense of ceremony, and the mosque is as important socially as it is visually. Families gather here in the evening when the heat drops and the lights come on.

The present structure was rebuilt by the Dutch in the late 19th century after the earlier mosque was burned during the Aceh War. That history makes it more complicated than a simple postcard monument: it is both a colonial-era reconstruction and the most beloved symbol of Acehnese Islam. Non-Muslim visitors are usually welcome outside prayer times, provided they dress modestly and behave quietly.
