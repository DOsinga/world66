Jambi is a river city in eastern [Sumatra](/asia/indonesia/sumatra), stretched along the Batanghari and better known to Indonesians than to foreign travellers. It is a provincial capital, so parts of it feel functional and spread out, but it has one very strong reason to stop: the ancient [Muaro Jambi Temple Compounds](/asia/indonesia/sumatra/jambi/muaro_jambi_temple_compounds), about 30 km from the city.

Those temple ruins lift Jambi above the ordinary. The site was tied to the Malayu and Srivijaya worlds and is usually described as the largest archaeological complex in Sumatra. It is flat, green, and best explored slowly by bicycle, with brick temple bases and reconstructed shrines appearing between trees and village lanes.

In the city itself, the Batanghari gives Jambi its shape. [Gentala Arasy](/asia/indonesia/sumatra/jambi/gentala_arasy) is a pedestrian bridge, tower, and museum that has become the modern city icon, while [Tanggo Rajo](/asia/indonesia/sumatra/jambi/tanggo_rajo) is the riverfront place to walk in the evening and eat snacks by the water. Jambi is not polished, but it rewards a traveller who wants Sumatra beyond the usual volcano and lake circuit.
