Medan is the gateway to northern Sumatra. Indonesia's fourth-largest city (population around 2.5 million) is loud, sprawling and chaotic, but most travellers pass through on their way to [Lake Toba](/asia/indonesia/sumatra/laketoba), the immense volcanic lake a few hours south. Give Medan a day before you move on -- it has more character than it gets credit for.

The old colonial quarter around Jalan Ahmad Yani rewards a morning's walk. The [Maimun Palace](/asia/indonesia/sumatra/medan/maimun_palace), built in 1888 for the Sultan of Deli, mixes Malay, Mughal and European styles and is still partly occupied by the royal family. Nearby, the [Grand Mosque](/asia/indonesia/sumatra/medan/grand_mosque_medan) (Masjid Raya Al-Mashun) is one of the most beautiful mosques in Indonesia. The [Tjong A Fie Mansion](/asia/indonesia/sumatra/medan/tjong_a_fie_mansion), the former home of Medan's most powerful Chinese merchant, is now an excellent guided museum — a window into the colonial plantation economy that made this city.

Medan's food scene is outstanding. [Tip Top Restaurant](/asia/indonesia/sumatra/medan/tip_top_restaurant) has been serving soto and ice cream since 1934 in a Dutch colonial interior that has barely changed. The night markets are even better — find Pasar Semalam on Jalan Semarang for cheap, excellent Batak, Chinese, and Indonesian cooking.

Medan is 875 km northwest of [Jakarta](/asia/indonesia/java/jakarta). Kualanamu International Airport has domestic connections across Indonesia and some international flights to Malaysia and Singapore. From the airport, it is about an hour's drive into the city centre.
