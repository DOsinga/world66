Sumba's Sandalwood pony (kuda Sandelhout) is one of the island's defining images. Small, compact, and extraordinarily hardy, the breed has been exported from Sumba for centuries — first to Java, Bali, and the Malay courts; later as far as Australia and Europe. Their name comes from the sandalwood trade that once made Sumba prosperous. Today they are everywhere: grazing on the dry savannahs of the east, used for transport in villages where no road goes, and — most dramatically — ridden at full gallop during the Pasola.

The horses are rarely corralled. They graze free on the open grasslands of East Sumba, which in the dry season turns to an almost African savannah — yellow grass, palm trees, and small herds of horses against a wide sky. The area around Melolo and the eastern plains offers the best landscapes for seeing them at large.

Horseriding trips can be arranged through guesthouses in both Waikabubak and Waingapu. A half-day ride through traditional village country is one of the more memorable ways to see the interior.
