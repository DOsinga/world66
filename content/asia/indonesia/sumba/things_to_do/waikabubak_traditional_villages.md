Waikabubak, the capital of West Sumba, is unusual among Indonesian towns in that traditional clan houses and megalithic tombs sit within the town itself. The hilltop kampungs of Tarung and Waitabar are both within walking distance of the central market — rows of high-peaked thatched houses facing stone tombs across swept earth courtyards, with chickens and children and daily life going on around them.

Tarung holds an important annual ceremony in November (Wula Podu, the sacred month), during which outsiders are not always welcome. At other times, visitors who approach respectfully are generally allowed to look around. A small donation to the village elder is appropriate.

The market itself, held daily but busiest in the mornings, sells local produce, sirih pinang (betel nut), and a rotating selection of ikat cloth and handicrafts. For a wider range of traditional goods, the permanent stalls along the main street carry cloth from across the west.
