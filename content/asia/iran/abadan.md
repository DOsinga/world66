Abadan is a city in the southwest of Iran, on an island at the confluence of the Shatt al-Arab waterway and the Persian Gulf. It was once home to the largest oil refinery in the world — built by the Anglo-Persian Oil Company in the early 20th century — and its fortunes have been entirely shaped by oil. At its peak in the 1970s, the city had a population of several hundred thousand and a prosperous, cosmopolitan character unusual for Iran: British engineers, Indian technicians, Armenian Christians, and Iranians from across the country all lived in its company neighbourhoods.

The Iran-Iraq War devastated Abadan. The city was encircled and besieged for over a year from 1980, reducing much of it to rubble, and the [Abadan Refinery](/asia/iran/abadan/abadan_refinery) was heavily damaged. Reconstruction has been slow and the city has never recovered its former vitality. The refinery still operates. The flat, hot landscape of the oil island is striking in its own way, and the city's history — both its oil-era glamour and the brutal siege — gives it a weight that few Iranian cities share.

For travellers, the draw is the history rather than conventional sightseeing. The [Cinema Rex Memorial](/asia/iran/abadan/cinema_rex_memorial) documents one of the most consequential atrocities of Iran's revolutionary period. The [Ranguniha Mosque](/asia/iran/abadan/ranguniha_mosque) and the [Saint Garapet Church](/asia/iran/abadan/saint_garapet_church) are physical reminders of the cosmopolitan city that existed here before 1979. Visitors are rare — come prepared for intense heat from May to October, and with a genuine interest in 20th-century history.
