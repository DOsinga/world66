Ahvaz is one of the least appealing cities in [Iran](/asia/iran). Located in Khuzestan province, it is mainly a commercial and industrial centre built around the Karun River, Iran's only navigable waterway. It was heavily bombed during the Iran–Iraq War and reconstruction has not made the place more attractive — summer temperatures regularly hit 50°C and dust storms are frequent.

The reason to come here is the surrounding province, which contains some of the most remarkable ancient sites in the Middle East. Within a 100 km radius of the city lie [Chogha Zanbil](/asia/iran/ahvaz/chogha_zanbil), a nearly 5,000-year-old Elamite ziggurat and UNESCO World Heritage site; [Shush](/asia/iran/ahvaz/shush_susa) (biblical Susa), one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world; [Haft Tappeh](/asia/iran/ahvaz/haft_tappeh), another Elamite royal site with a good on-site museum; and [Shushtar](/asia/iran/ahvaz/shushtar_hydraulic_system), whose ancient hydraulic system is UNESCO-listed as a masterpiece of engineering.

In the city itself, the [Karun River waterfront](/asia/iran/ahvaz/karun_river_waterfront) is where life happens in the evenings — families, vendors, and fishermen along a promenade that connects the city's famous bridges. [Pol-e Siah](/asia/iran/ahvaz/pol_e_siah), the old Black Bridge, is the most historically interesting of these; [Pol-e Sefid](/asia/iran/ahvaz/pol_e_sefid), the White Bridge, is the most photogenic.
