The Sheikh Safi al-Din Khanegah and Shrine Ensemble is Ardabil's reason to exist as a tourist destination. The complex grew out of the small khanegah (Sufi lodge) founded around the saint's tomb in the 14th century, expanded over two centuries into a sprawling spiritual and political centre. The successive Safavid shahs added gardens, courtyards, and richly tiled tomb chambers — including the burial of Shah Ismail I, founder of the dynasty.

The architecture is a masterclass in early Safavid design. The portal mosaics are deep cobalt blue and gold, the muqarnas vaults are intricate, and the interior tile work and carved wooden doors are well preserved. The Dar al-Hadis prayer hall and the Chini-khaneh — designed specifically to display Shah Abbas's gift of Chinese porcelain — are the highlights. Allow at least two hours; carpet sellers cluster outside, but the shrine itself is dignified and quiet.
