The Binalud range rises immediately west of [Mashhad](/asia/iran/mashhad), forming the green wall that separates the city from the plain of [Neyshabur](/asia/iran/neyshabur) beyond. Its high point is Mount Binalud at 3,211 m, and the range is the natural lung of the region — the place Mashhadis head when the summer heat settles over the city.

The lower slopes are easy to reach by car: the road over the pass between Mashhad and Neyshabur tops out at around 2,100 m, and from there the upper peaks are within reach of fit weekend hikers. Trails set off from the foothill towns of [Torghabeh](/asia/iran/torghabeh) and [Shandiz](/asia/iran/shandiz) and from the village of Zoshk, climbing through orchards and walnut groves into bare rock and high meadow. In good snow years the small ski field at Shirbad, on the northern flank of the range, operates from December to March.

For most visitors the Binalud is less a single objective than a backdrop and a refuge — the cool valleys at its feet hold the resort towns, the kebab restaurants, and the trailheads for shorter walks. Serious hikers tackle the summit as a long day or an overnight from the pass; everyone else comes for the orchards, the streams, and a few degrees of relief from the city below.
