A second 11th-century brick tomb tower in the old town, finished in 1056 CE — about thirty years after the [Pir-e Alamdar](/asia/iran/damghan/pir_e_alamdar) on the next block. The proportions are slightly broader and the Kufic inscriptions slightly more elaborate, but the conical roof and the fluted body follow the same Seljuq vocabulary.

The name — "Forty Daughters" — is a local fairy-tale attribution; the dedicatory inscription actually names a Seljuq prince called Abu Shoja. Free to visit.
