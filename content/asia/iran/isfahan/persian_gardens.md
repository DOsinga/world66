The most readable scholarly survey of the Persian garden tradition, tracing its development from the ancient Persian paradise garden through the great Safavid royal gardens of Isfahan — including Chehel Sotoun and the now-vanished gardens that lined Chaharbagh Boulevard. Lavishly illustrated, it gives a visitor the framework to understand what they are looking at when they stand in Isfahan's surviving green spaces. The book argues convincingly that the garden — walled, watered, and intensely ordered — is the organizing metaphor of Persian civilization, and you will feel that argument confirmed everywhere in Isfahan.
