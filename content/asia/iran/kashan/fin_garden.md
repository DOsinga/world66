Fin Garden — Bagh-e Fin — is the oldest surviving Persian garden in Iran and one of nine historic Persian gardens inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site. It lies about 9 kilometres southwest of Kashan city centre and makes a natural half-day excursion from any base in the city.

The garden dates to the Safavid period, though the site has been used since at least the 16th century. Its design follows the classic chahar bagh (four-garden) layout — a central axis divided by water channels into four quadrants, with cypress trees, fruit trees, and flowering plants arranged in formal geometry. Natural springs feed the water system, which flows without pumps through channels, pools, and cascades across the entire site.

The garden has a hammam, a teahouse, and several pavilions of varying periods. The overall effect — running water, shade from tall cypresses, the smell of roses — is one of genuine tranquillity. It is deservedly popular with Iranian visitors, particularly families on weekends.

**Getting there:** Taxis from central Kashan take about 20 minutes. Some city buses run to Fin village on the edge of the garden.  
**Hours:** Daily 8am–6pm  
**Admission:** Moderate entrance fee
