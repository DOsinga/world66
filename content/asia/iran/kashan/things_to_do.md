---
title: Things to Do
type: section
---

Kashan's greatest treasures are its traditional merchant houses, and no visit is complete without spending time inside at least two or three of them. The [Tabatabaei House](/asia/iran/kashan/tabatabaei_house) is the grandest — four courtyards, soaring windtowers, and stucco ceilings of extraordinary delicacy. The [Boroujerdi House](/asia/iran/kashan/boroujerdi_house) next door was built to impress a single bride and outdoes even its neighbour in the ambition of its painted decoration. Both are within a few minutes' walk of each other in the old merchants' quarter.

The [Agha Bozorg Mosque](/asia/iran/kashan/agha_bozorg_mosque) is the city's finest religious building — a sunken courtyard mosque and madrasa from the late 18th century whose cool geometries are a perfect counterpoint to the exuberance of the houses. The [Bazaar of Kashan](/asia/iran/kashan/bazaar_of_kashan) runs through the old city in a series of vaulted corridors connecting the mosque, a caravanserai, and the surrounding streets. It remains a working bazaar rather than a tourist arcade.

The [Sultan Amir Ahmad Bathhouse](/asia/iran/kashan/sultan_amir_ahmad_bathhouse) in the southern part of the old city is one of the best-preserved traditional hammams in Iran — its tiled domes and star-shaped skylights are best appreciated from the roof as much as from inside. Outside the city, [Fin Garden](/asia/iran/kashan/fin_garden) is the oldest surviving Persian garden in Iran, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and worth a half-day. The prehistoric mound of [Tepe Sialk](/asia/iran/kashan/tepe_sialk), on the city's western edge, is one of the oldest known settlements in Iran and an easy stop for anyone interested in the deeper past.
