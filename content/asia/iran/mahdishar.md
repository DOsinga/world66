Mahdishahr — also known by its older name Sangesar — is a small city in Semnan Province, sitting on the southern slopes of the Alborz mountains about 20 kilometres west of [Semnan](/asia/iran/semnan). At roughly 1,630 metres above sea level, it is a cool retreat from the lowland heat, with cold winters and heavy snowfall but mild summers. To the north the Alborz ridgeline marks the border with Mazandaran Province; to the south, the land drops away into the vast salt desert of Dasht-e Kavir.

The city's most significant sight is [Darband Cave](/asia/iran/mahdishar/darband_cave), a vast limestone cavern 3 kilometres to the north, whose main hall stretches 91 metres long and rises 20 metres high. The cave has yielded archaeological finds from the medieval Islamic period and is one of the largest in the province. A short distance up the mountain road lies [Shahmirzad](/asia/iran/mahdishar/shahmirzad), a cooler resort town famous for its enormous walnut orchard — the largest in the world according to the UN's Food and Agriculture Organization — and its tree-lined gardens.

The [Parvar Protected Area](/asia/iran/mahdishar/parvar_protected_area), about 35 kilometres north, is one of the more biodiverse conservation zones in northern Iran, supporting Persian leopard, Asiatic cheetah, red deer, bear, and dozens of bird species. Closer in, the [Sangesar Nomadic Museum](/asia/iran/mahdishar/sangesar_nomadic_museum) documents the remarkable Sangesari tribal culture: a semi-nomadic people with their own Northwestern Iranian language who traditionally undertook one of the world's longest seasonal migration routes. You can also arrange [horseback rides](/asia/iran/mahdishar/sangesar_horseback_riding) with tribal guides into the mountain valleys north of the city.

Mahdishar is quiet and unhurried. It suits travellers who want mountain air, genuine small-town Iranian life, and access to some unusual heritage — rather than the well-worn circuit of mosques and bazaars further west.
