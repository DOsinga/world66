Mashhad is the holiest city in Iran, tucked into the mountainous northeast corner of the country in the region known as Khorasan. The name means "place of martyrdom" — the martyr in question is Imam Reza, the eighth Shia imam, who was poisoned here in the 9th century. His tomb became a sacred place of pilgrimage, and today millions of Iranians visit the shrine each year, arriving by the tens of thousands daily on flights, trains, and buses.

The [Holy Shrine of Imam Reza](/asia/iran/mashhad/imam_reza_shrine) is the centrepiece of the city: a vast complex of courtyards, porticos, the magnificent [golden dome](/asia/iran/mashhad/golden_dome), and the [Goharshad Mosque](/asia/iran/mashhad/goharshad_mosque) dating from the Timurid period. The [attached museums](/asia/iran/mashhad/astan_quds_museum) hold priceless manuscripts and objects, and the [tomb of Harun al-Rashid](/asia/iran/mashhad/harun_al_rashid_mausoleum) lies inside the same complex. The shrine is overwhelming in scale and devotion — it is less a single building than a small city within the city.

Beyond the shrine, Mashhad has a number of large parks and several interesting excursions: the [tomb of Ferdowsi](/asia/iran/mashhad/tomb_of_ferdowsi), Iran's national poet, is in nearby [Tus](/asia/iran/mashhad/tus) (24 km); the [tomb of Khajeh Rabi](/asia/iran/mashhad/tomb_of_khajeh_rabi), with calligraphy by the renowned Safavid artist Reza Abbasi, lies 6 kilometres north; and the summer resorts of [Torghabeh](/asia/iran/mashhad/torghabeh), [Shandiz](/asia/iran/mashhad/shandiz), and the surrounding mountain valleys offer cool relief from the city heat. The climate is varied — cold winters, pleasant springs, mild summers, and beautiful autumns.
