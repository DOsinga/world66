Na'in is an old desert town between [Isfahan](/asia/iran/isfahan) and Yazd, close enough to the main route that it is easy to miss and good enough that you should not. Its historic quarter holds one of Iran's oldest mosques, a ruined pre-Islamic castle, traditional houses, a bazaar, and traces of the qanat system that made settlement here possible.

The [Jameh Mosque](/asia/iran/na_in/jameh_mosque) is the reason to stop. Its brick arcades, carved stucco, basement, and early minaret feel far older and more austere than the blue-tiled mosques of Isfahan. Across from it, the [Pirnia Traditional House](/asia/iran/na_in/pirnia_traditional_house) shows the domestic side of desert architecture.

Na'in is also known for fine carpets, often in pale blue and cream. You do not need a full day here unless you are deep into architecture, but as a break between Isfahan and Yazd it is far more rewarding than another roadside tea stop.
