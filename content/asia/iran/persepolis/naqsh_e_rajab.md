Naqsh-e Rajab is a small group of Sassanian rock reliefs cut into a limestone hollow a few kilometres north of Persepolis. The site is much smaller than Naqsh-e Rustam, but the carving is crisp and direct: royal investitures, mounted figures, and courtly scenes from the early Sassanian period.

It is best visited as a short stop between Persepolis and Naqsh-e Rustam. The scale is modest, so it will disappoint anyone expecting another full archaeological complex, but for travellers interested in how imperial imagery continued after the Achaemenids, it adds an important chapter to the Persepolis landscape.
