Naqsh-e Rustam is essential if you have come as far as Persepolis. Four royal Achaemenid tombs are carved high into a cliff face, traditionally associated with kings including Darius I and Xerxes, while later Sasanian reliefs below show emperors receiving investiture or triumphing over Rome.

The site is compact but monumental. Stand back from the cliff and you can read more than a thousand years of Persian imperial imagery in one view: the tombs of the ancient kings above, the horsemen and divine figures of late antiquity below, and the square Ka'ba-ye Zartosht tower standing in front like a stone riddle.
