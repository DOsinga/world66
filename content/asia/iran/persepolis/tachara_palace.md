Tachara, the palace of Darius the Great, is one of the essential buildings inside Persepolis. Its polished stone doorways, reliefs, and inscriptions make it easier to imagine the ceremonial power of the Achaemenid court than the more fragmentary parts of the terrace.

Do not rush it on the way to the bigger set pieces. The palace rewards close looking: the carved figures, the quality of the stone, and the way the building sits within the wider royal complex all help explain why Persepolis remains more than a collection of ruins.
