The Tomb of Artaxerxes II is cut into the cliff above Persepolis, one of the royal Achaemenid tombs that turn the palace terrace into a sacred landscape. From below, its cross-shaped facade is part architecture, part proclamation, with the king shown in relation to fire altar, platform, and empire.

It is worth looking up from the palaces and walking close enough to read the cliff as a whole. The tomb helps connect Persepolis with the royal necropolis at Naqsh-e Rustam, showing how death, kingship, and ceremony were staged in stone.
