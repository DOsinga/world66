The rock-hewn tomb attributed to Artaxerxes III sits in the cliffs above Persepolis, continuing the Achaemenid habit of turning royal burial into monumental stone theatre. The facade follows the familiar cross-shaped royal-tomb language: king, fire altar, attendants and the architecture of empire compressed into rock.

It is easy to miss if you rush only through the palace platforms. Look up and give the cliff tombs time; they connect Persepolis to nearby Naqsh-e Rustam and make the ceremonial city feel like part of a larger royal landscape.
