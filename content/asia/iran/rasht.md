Rasht is the capital of Gilan and the main inland gateway to Iran's Caspian coast. It feels nothing like the dry plateau cities most travellers associate with Iran: the air is damp, the countryside is green, rice paddies press close to the suburbs, and the food is reason enough to come.

The city has long been a trading hinge between Tehran, the Caspian ports, Russia, and the Caucasus. That history is still visible around [Municipality Square](/asia/iran/rasht/municipality_square), where early-20th-century civic buildings give Rasht a faintly European face, and in [Rasht Bazaar](/asia/iran/rasht/rasht_bazaar), a working market famous for fish, olives, herbs, pickles, and the loud confidence of Gilaki commerce.

Rasht is not a city of grand monuments. Its appeal is more local and sensory: rain on plane trees, lunch tables crowded with *mirza ghasemi* and sour stews, and side trips into the forests and wetlands of Gilan. The [Gilan Rural Heritage Museum](/asia/iran/rasht/gilan_rural_heritage_museum) south of town is the best single place to understand the province's wooden houses and village life, while [Eynak Lagoon](/asia/iran/rasht/eynak_lagoon) shows how close the city still sits to water and reeds.

Use Rasht as a base for [Bandar-e Anzali](/asia/iran/bandareanzali), Masuleh, Lahijan, and the Caspian foothills. It is practical, rainy, appetising, and more lived-in than polished - which is exactly its charm.
