Hedayat's short, hallucinatory novel — written in 1936, first published in India to avoid Iranian censorship — is the defining work of Iranian modernist fiction: the confession of a narrator who may be insane, dreaming, or dead, obsessed with a woman he may have killed or imagined, in a Tehran that shifts between the ancient and the contemporary without distinguishing between them. It is not a comfortable book, but it is unmistakably Iranian in its themes — the weight of the past, the unreliability of identity, the presence of death. Hedayat died by suicide in his apartment in Paris in 1951, aged 48. The book is banned in Iran.
