  The 182-carat *Darya-ye Noor* ("Sea of Light") is one of two parts of a stone that was once the largest diamond in the world. In 1739, Nader Shah invaded Mughal India and among his plunder was the legendary *Koh-i-Noor*, which he removed from the Peacock Throne. The stone was later divided; one half became the Koh-i-Noor (now in the British Crown Jewels), the other half the Darya-ye Noor (now in this vault). The pink colour of the Darya-ye Noor comes from trace elements of boron in the crystal structure.
---

The Treasury of National Jewels, housed in the vaults of the Central Bank of Iran, contains one of the most extraordinary collections of precious objects on earth — assembled over five centuries by Safavid, Afsharid, Zand, Qajar, and Pahlavi rulers and now displayed as state property of the Islamic Republic. The scale is genuinely staggering: crowns studded with thousands of individual diamonds, rubies, and emeralds; a globe made from 51,366 gemstones (gold ocean, ruby continents, emerald Europe); swords, shields, and throne furniture encrusted with precious stones.

The centrepieces are the *Darya-ye Noor* (a 182-carat pink diamond, the largest in the world), the *Taj-e Kiani* (the Imperial Crown of Nader Shah), and the original Peacock Throne — though "throne" barely conveys the scale of a jewel-encrusted platform that took 17 years to build. Photography of the most sensitive pieces is restricted. The treasury is only open limited hours on specific days of the week; verify times before visiting.
