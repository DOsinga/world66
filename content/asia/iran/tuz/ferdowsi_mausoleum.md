The mausoleum of Ferdowsi, author of the Shahnameh (Book of Kings, completed around 1010 AD), is the principal reason to come to Tus, and it is a genuinely moving place. The Shahnameh — a 60,000-verse epic retelling the myths and history of ancient Persia, from the creation of the world through the Arab conquest — is the national epic of Iran and of the Persian-speaking world, and Ferdowsi's status is something like Shakespeare and Homer combined. The mausoleum, rebuilt in the 1930s in a style evoking Achaemenid architecture, sits in a formal garden and has the quality of a national shrine.

Iranians come here from across the country, and the atmosphere of quiet reverence is unmistakable. Visitors leave flowers, recite verses from the Shahnameh, and sit in the garden. The interior of the mausoleum has carved reliefs depicting scenes from the epic. For anyone interested in Persian literature or Iranian culture, this is one of the most significant sites in the country. The mausoleum is open daily from early morning.
