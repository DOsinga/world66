An Nasiriyah is the capital of Dhi Qar Governorate in southeastern Iraq, on the Euphrates River. Founded in 1870, it is a provincial city of around 800,000 people and the main gateway for visiting the nearby ruins of [Ur](/asia/iraq/ur), one of the oldest cities in the world.

The city itself is a working Iraqi provincial town, but the [Nasiriyah Museum](/asia/iraq/annasiriyah/nasiriyah_museum) is worth a visit before heading to the archaeological sites — it holds a solid collection of Sumerian objects from Ur and the surrounding region that makes the ruins more legible.

To the east of Nasiriyah begin the [Mesopotamian Marshes](/asia/iraq/annasiriyah/mesopotamian_marshes) — one of the largest wetland systems in the world and home to the Marsh Arabs (Madan), who have lived here in reed houses on artificial floating islands for thousands of years. Saddam Hussein deliberately drained most of the marshes in the early 1990s as collective punishment against Shia communities who had rebelled after the Gulf War. After 2003, water was restored and large portions of the marsh have recovered. The area now has UNESCO World Heritage status. Boat trips through the marshes can be arranged in Nasiriyah, and they are one of the most remarkable travel experiences in the whole of Iraq.
