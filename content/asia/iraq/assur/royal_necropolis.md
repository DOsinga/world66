  The royal tombs of Assur yielded extraordinary finds when German archaeologists opened them in the early 20th century — gold jewellery, ivory carvings, bronze vessels, and elaborate burial furniture indicating that Assyrian kings went to their deaths attended by considerable luxury. Portions of the tomb contents are now in Berlin and Baghdad. Some tombs were looted in antiquity; others were found intact.
---

Beneath the ruins of the Old Palace at Assur, German archaeologists excavated a series of vaulted underground tombs in which Assyrian kings and members of the royal family were buried during the Middle Assyrian period (roughly 14th–11th centuries BC). The tombs are constructed of fired brick with barrel vaults — sophisticated masonry for their period — and were designed as permanent chambers beneath the palace floors.

The tombs are not open for visitors to enter, but the area above them is accessible as part of the general site visit. Explanatory panels (where present) describe the finds from each tomb and the burial practices of the Assyrian royal court. For visitors with a specialist interest in Bronze Age funerary practices, this is one of the most significant royal tomb complexes in the ancient Near East.
