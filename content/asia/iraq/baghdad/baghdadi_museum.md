The Baghdadi Museum is a modest but engaging museum near the Tigris, close to the old commercial heart of Baghdad. Rather than focusing on kings, empires, or archaeology, it recreates scenes of ordinary Baghdadi life with figures, shopfronts, tools, costumes, and street tableaux.

That makes it useful for travellers because it fills in the human scale of the city. After mosques, markets, and Abbasid-era remains, the museum gives you barbers, craftsmen, merchants, and domestic scenes: a reminder that Baghdad’s history is not only monumental but also intensely urban.
