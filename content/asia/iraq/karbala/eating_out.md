---
title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

Karbala's food is straightforward Iraqi pilgrim fare — grilled meats, big plates of rice, lentil stew, and bread from the clay oven. Around the shrines you will find an almost endless line of small eateries serving **masgouf** (Euphrates carp split and grilled over an open fire), **quzi** (slow-cooked lamb on saffron rice), and **dolma** (vine leaves stuffed with rice and minced meat).

Look for the **kababji** stalls near the bazaar: shoulder-to-shoulder grills turning out skewers of lamb and chicken, served with raw onion, fresh herbs, and warm samoon bread. For dessert, the syrup-soaked **zalabiya** is everywhere during pilgrim season. Restaurants close briefly during the main prayers but stay open very late on busy nights.

Alcohol is not served. Tap water is best avoided — bottled water is universal and cheap.
