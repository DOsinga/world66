---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

The Karbala plains are brutally hot in summer — daytime temperatures in July and August routinely exceed 45°C, and the city's marble plazas reflect the heat without mercy. The shrine area is most pleasant between **November and March**, when daytime highs sit around 15–22°C and evenings are crisp.

The religious calendar matters more than the climate. **Muharram** (the first month of the Islamic year) and especially **Ashura** — the tenth day of Muharram, commemorating Husayn's death — bring vast crowds and intense mourning rituals. **Arbaeen**, forty days after Ashura, draws between fifteen and twenty million pilgrims, most of them walking from Najaf or further afield. Either is unforgettable but logistically demanding: accommodation is almost impossible, the city is sealed off in places, and security is heavy.

For a calmer visit, come in late winter or early spring, well outside the pilgrimage peaks. Avoid Friday afternoons if you dislike crowds — locals from across central Iraq make a weekly visit.
