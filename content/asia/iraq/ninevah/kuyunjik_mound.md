The larger of Nineveh's two great mounds, Kuyunjik rises about 30 metres above the plain on the east bank of the Tigris. Beneath it lie the remains of the Palace of Sennacherib and the later Palace of Ashurbanipal, excavated by French and British archaeologists from the 1840s onwards. The scale of what was found here was extraordinary: room after room lined with carved stone reliefs, colossal winged bulls (*lamassu*) guarding doorways, and an archive of cuneiform tablets that transformed understanding of the ancient world.

Most of the major finds left Iraq long ago — the British Museum and the Louvre hold the finest pieces — but the mound itself remains immensely atmospheric. Walking across it, you stand on what was once the heart of the largest city on earth. The site is managed as part of the wider Nineveh archaeological zone and can be visited with local guides from Mosul, though some areas remain restricted as post-ISIS restoration and excavation continues.
