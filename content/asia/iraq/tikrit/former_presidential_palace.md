Tikrit's former presidential palace complex is a difficult, politically loaded sight rather than a conventional attraction. Built under Saddam Hussein, the riverside compound became a symbol of Baathist power and later of the violence and occupation that followed the 2003 invasion.

Any visit depends heavily on current security, access rules, and local guidance. It clears the bar because it is a real, specific place tied to modern Iraqi history, but it should be approached with caution and without treating the ruins as spectacle.
