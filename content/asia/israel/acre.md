Acre (Akko) is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world, and its old city is a UNESCO World Heritage Site of remarkable density: Crusader vaults, Ottoman mosques, Bahá'í gardens, and a working Arab fishing harbour all coexist within the old walls. It is about 20 km north of [Haifa](/asia/israel/haifa) and 130 km from [Jerusalem](/asia/israel/jerusalem).

The old city is built on a promontory that juts into the sea. Beneath the Ottoman-era buildings lies an almost complete Crusader city — the underground halls, refectories, and passages of the [Hospitaller Fortress](/asia/israel/acre/hospitaller_fortress) are astonishingly well preserved and can be visited. Above ground, the 18th-century [El-Jazzar Mosque](/asia/israel/acre/el_jazzar_mosque) with its green dome dominates the skyline, and the [Turkish Baths Museum](/asia/israel/acre/turkish_baths_museum) next door holds remarkable archaeological collections.

Napoleon besieged Acre in 1799 but failed to take it — the defenders held out for 61 days. The [city walls](/asia/israel/acre/city_walls) he attacked still stand and can be walked for free. The [Khan el-Umdan](/asia/israel/acre/khan_el_umdan) caravanserai near the harbour and the atmospheric [Old City Market](/asia/israel/acre/old_city_market) are both worth exploring. A few kilometres north, the [Bahá'í Shrines and Gardens](/asia/israel/acre/bahji_gardens) at Bahji are the holiest site in the Bahá'í faith — among the most peaceful spots on the coast.
