Be'er Sheva (Beersheba) is the capital of the Negev — a university city, military center, and the main transport hub for Israel's south. It's not a conventional tourist destination, but it's a useful base for exploring the Negev desert and has a few things worth seeing in its own right.

The [Old City](/asia/israel/beer_sheva/old_city) has buildings from the Ottoman period that have been partially restored, including the [Great Mosque](/asia/israel/beer_sheva/great_mosque) now serving as a museum and the beautifully kept [Negev Museum of Art](/asia/israel/beer_sheva/negev_museum_of_art). The [Bedouin market](/asia/israel/beer_sheva/bedouin_market), held on Thursdays, is smaller than it once was but still an authentic slice of regional life. [Ben-Gurion University](/asia/israel/beer_sheva/ben_gurion_university) brings a student population that keeps the city livelier than its utilitarian reputation suggests.

Be'er Sheva is notable as a chess city — it has produced more grandmasters per capita than anywhere in the world, largely due to the large wave of Soviet Jewish immigration in the 1990s.

The [Ottoman Railway Station](/asia/israel/beer_sheva/ottoman_railway_station) and [Atatürk Plaza](/asia/israel/beer_sheva/ataturk_plaza) add unexpected layers of history — memorials to the 1917 Battle of Beersheba that turned the tide of the First World War in this region. [Abraham's Well](/asia/israel/beer_sheva/abrahams_well) in the Old City commemorates the story that gave the city its name.

From Be'er Sheva, buses and shared taxis reach Mitzpe Ramon, the Dead Sea, and [Eilat](/asia/israel/eilat). The train line connects north to Tel Aviv. It's 80 km from Jerusalem.

The biblical Be'er Sheva — mentioned in connection with Abraham, Isaac, and the patriarchs — is at [Tel Be'er Sheva](/asia/israel/beer_sheva/tel_beer_sheva), a UNESCO-listed archaeological site a few kilometers east of the modern city.
