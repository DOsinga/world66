The Great Mosque of Be'er Sheva is the most architecturally prominent building from the Ottoman founding of the city. Built in 1906 in a simplified Ottoman style with a single minaret, it was the religious and social centre of the original town. After Israeli forces captured Be'er Sheva in 1948, the building was converted to secular use and has housed parts of the Be'er Sheva Museum collection.

The building is visually striking in the context of its surroundings and worth seeing from the outside even if the interior is not accessible. It anchors the northern end of the Old City and is a good starting point for a walk through the Ottoman quarter.
