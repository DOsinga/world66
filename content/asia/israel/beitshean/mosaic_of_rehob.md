In the floor of an ancient synagogue at Kibbutz Ein HaNetziv, south of Beit Shean, lies one of the longest Hebrew inscriptions from the Byzantine period found anywhere in the world. The Mosaic of Rehob is a 6th-century synagogue floor mosaic containing an elaborate halakhic text — rabbinic legal rulings about the boundaries of the Land of Israel, details about Shemita (the sabbatical year), and lists of towns exempt from various agricultural laws.

The mosaic is significant not just for its length but for what it tells us: that complex rabbinic legal texts were displayed publicly in synagogues, presumably read aloud and used as community reference. The text names dozens of towns and villages in the region, providing a snapshot of early Byzantine-period Jewish settlement in the Jordan and Jezreel valleys.

The site is maintained as a small outdoor display with protective covering. It lacks the drama of the Roman ruins but rewards those with an interest in early Judaism or Byzantine epigraphy. Combined with the national park, it rounds out the picture of Beit Shean Valley's remarkable historical density.
