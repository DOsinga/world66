  Copper was mined at Timna for at least five thousand years, making these
  among the oldest mines in the world. Egyptian pharaohs sent expeditions
  here from at least 1400 BC, and a small temple to the goddess Hathor was

  dyed with colours still vivid after three thousand years.

An extraordinary geological and archaeological site in the Negev desert, about 30 kilometres north of Eilat. The park is centred on an ancient copper-mining district that was worked for thousands of years — first by the Egyptians, then the Nabataeans, then successive powers through the medieval period. The landscape is dramatic: a natural amphitheatre of eroded sandstone formations, including the famous King Solomon's Pillars, a row of towering rock columns carved by millennia of wind and water.

A reconstructed Tabernacle (based on the biblical description) sits inside the park and is illuminated dramatically in the evening. The park also has a small artificial lake, a mini-golf course, and mountain biking trails — an unexpected range of activities for a desert setting. The combination of ancient history and unusual geology makes it one of the best half-day trips from Eilat. Open year-round; evening visits are especially atmospheric.
