Eilat is Israel's southernmost city, a resort town wedged into the northern tip of the Red Sea between Jordan to the east and Egypt's Sinai Peninsula to the west. The city exists almost entirely for tourism — beaches, diving, snorkeling, and winter sun. Israelis come for their own holidays, Jordanians cross the border at Aqaba a few kilometres away, and European charter flights arrive throughout the cooler months.

The Red Sea here is spectacularly clear, and the coral reef that runs along the southern part of the bay is genuinely excellent. Snorkeling directly off the beach near the Coral Beach Nature Reserve puts you in the water above some of the best coral in the region. Diving is even better — the underwater observatory marine park between Eilat and the Egyptian border lets non-divers observe the reef through large windows below the waterline. The [Red Sea Star](/asia/israel/eliat/the_red_sea_star), a restaurant partly built underwater on the southern promenade near Le Meridien, offers a more passive way to watch fish while having dinner.

The border crossings with both Egypt (Taba) and Jordan (Wadi Araba) are open and heavily used. A two-day excursion to [Petra](/asia/jordan/petra) from Eilat is a very popular itinerary — the border is close, and organized day trips or easy independent crossings make it feasible. Egypt's Sinai resorts at Dahab and Nuweiba are similarly accessible. The city itself is modern and functional without much architectural charm, but the mountains behind it — Israel's Negev desert slopes and Jordan's red sandstone cliffs across the bay — provide constant and dramatic backdrop.
