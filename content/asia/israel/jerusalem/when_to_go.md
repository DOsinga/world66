---
title: When to Go
type: section
---

Jerusalem sits at 800 metres elevation, which moderates what would otherwise be a harsh desert climate. Summers (June-August) are hot and dry — daytime temperatures of 28-32°C, cool nights — and hotels fill up completely with tourists and pilgrims. Spring (April-May) and autumn (October-November) are the best times to visit: warm days, cool evenings, and manageable crowds.

The Jewish calendar drives the rhythm of the city in ways that visitors should understand. **Rosh Hashanah** and **Yom Kippur** (September or October) see the city almost completely shut down for 25 hours — no cars, no shops, no public transport. The city during Yom Kippur becomes an enormous pedestrian space, with children cycling in the middle of empty highways. **Passover** and **Sukkot** (spring and autumn) bring massive domestic tourism to Jerusalem's hotels.

**Easter and Holy Week** bring Christian pilgrims from around the world and significant crowding in the Old City, particularly in the Church of the Holy Sepulchre. The Saturday before Easter, the **Holy Fire ceremony** draws enormous crowds to the church — plan accordingly or avoid.

**Ramadan** transforms the Muslim Quarter's atmosphere dramatically after sunset, when the Ottoman streets fill with family gatherings and special foods. It is a genuinely festive time in the Old City. Winter (December-January) can bring frost and occasionally snow, which turns Jerusalem into a magical ice-white city for a day or two, though it also brings rain and grey skies.
