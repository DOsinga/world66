Tiberias sits low and hot on the western shore of the Sea of Galilee, with the lake on one side and the brown hills of the Lower Galilee climbing behind it. It is one of Judaism's four holy cities, a practical base for Christian pilgrimage sites around the lake, and a good jumping-off point for the Roman ruins at [Beit Shean](/asia/israel/beitshean).

The city has never been especially polished. Modern Tiberias is a mix of lakefront hotels, working neighbourhoods, religious tombs, hot springs, and half-visible ruins from the Roman, Byzantine, Crusader, Ottoman, and British Mandate periods. That roughness is part of the appeal: you can walk from the [Tomb of Maimonides](/asia/israel/tiberias/tomb_of_maimonides) to [St. Peter's Church](/asia/israel/tiberias/st_peters_church), eat grilled fish by the water, and then stand over the zodiac mosaic at [Hammat Tiberias](/asia/israel/tiberias/hammat_tiberias).

Tiberias works best if you use it as a base rather than a single sight. North of town are [Magdala](/asia/israel/tiberias/magdala) and the cliffs of [Mount Arbel](/asia/israel/tiberias/mount_arbel); south are the hot springs and the Jordan Valley road to Beit Shean. Summer heat can be punishing, so start early, carry water, and leave the lakefront stroll for sunset.
