Bizen is not a city of big sights piled on top of each other. Its appeal is craft, old schools, and quiet hills on the eastern edge of Okayama Prefecture. The name is best known in Japan for Bizen ware, a hard, unglazed pottery fired in wood kilns until flame and ash mark the clay by chance.

Imbe, the pottery district, is the natural place to start. Workshops, galleries, climbing kilns, and the Bizen City Museum of Art sit close enough together for a slow walk. A short trip away, Osafune keeps another famous craft alive at the sword museum, where visitors can see blades, fittings, polishing, and forging demonstrations on selected days.

Bizen also has one of Japan's best preserved early schools: [Old Shizutani School](/asia/japan/bizen/old_shizutani_school), built in the 17th century for commoners rather than samurai. Add the thatched farmhouses of [Hattoji Furusato Village](/asia/japan/bizen/hattoji_furusato_village), and Bizen becomes a good spare-day stop for travelers who want old Japan without another castle town.
