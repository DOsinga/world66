Nagoya sits at the heart of central Honshu, about 90 minutes by shinkansen southwest of Tokyo and an hour north of Kyoto. It's Japan's fourth-largest city, heavily industrial (Toyota's headquarters are just outside), and usually skipped by visitors racing between the big hitters. That's a mistake: Nagoya has enough to fill two or three good days, especially for travellers with a serious interest in Japanese history.

[Nagoya Castle](/asia/japan/chubu/nagoya/nagoya_castle) is the headline sight — the white five-storey keep, topped with its famous golden dolphins, was destroyed in 1945 and rebuilt in concrete, but the reconstructed [Honmaru Palace](/asia/japan/chubu/nagoya/honmaru_palace) beside it is the real draw, meticulously recreated in traditional carpentry and painted screens. [Atsuta Shrine](/asia/japan/chubu/nagoya/atsuta_shrine), one of Shinto's most sacred sites, shelters the legendary Kusanagi sword.

[Osu](/asia/japan/chubu/nagoya/osu), just south of the castle, is a buzzing old neighbourhood of covered shopping arcades, temple grounds, and second-hand shops around the working [Osu Kannon](/asia/japan/chubu/nagoya/osu_kannon) temple. The [Shikemichi](/asia/japan/chubu/nagoya/shikemichi) quarter preserves a handful of Edo-era merchant houses. And for railway and industrial heritage fans, the [SCMaglev and Railway Park](/asia/japan/chubu/nagoya/scmaglev_railway_park) and the [Toyota Commemorative Museum](/asia/japan/chubu/nagoya/toyota_commemorative_museum) are both outstanding. The [Tokugawa Art Museum](/asia/japan/chubu/nagoya/tokugawa_art_museum) with the adjoining [Tokugawa-en](/asia/japan/chubu/nagoya/tokugawa_en) garden makes a quieter morning.

Nagoya's food is one of the reasons to come. Miso-katsu (the original at [Yabaton](/asia/japan/chubu/nagoya/yabaton)), hitsumabushi or grilled eel over rice (at [Hourai-ken](/asia/japan/chubu/nagoya/hourai_ken)), and tebasaki crispy chicken wings ([Sekai no Yamachan](/asia/japan/chubu/nagoya/sekai_no_yamachan) or [Furaibo](/asia/japan/chubu/nagoya/furaibo)) are the local specialties — distinct enough to have their own style name, Nagoya-meshi.

Day trips open up considerably from here: [Inuyama](/asia/japan/chubu/inuyama) with its original 16th-century castle, the preserved post town of Magome in the Kiso Valley, and the sacred Ise Shrine are all within easy reach.
