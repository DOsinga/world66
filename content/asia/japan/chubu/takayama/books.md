**The Inland Sea, by Donald Richie.** Not about Takayama specifically, but Richie's masterpiece of post-war Japanese travel writing is the right tonal preparation: an unhurried, sympathetic, slightly melancholy account of small-town and rural Japan, written by an outsider who came to know it better than most insiders. Travellers heading into Hida do well to read this first.

**Memoirs of a Mountain Hermit (Hosshinshu), by Kamo no Chōmei.** The 12th-century Buddhist hermit's reflections on impermanence and the rural mountain life echo strongly in the temple groves of the Higashiyama walk and the cedar shrines around Takayama.

**Snow Country, by Yasunari Kawabata.** Kawabata's Nobel-winning novel of an affair in a snowed-in mountain inn is set in nearby Niigata, but the atmosphere it captures — the way mountain Japan turns inward in winter — is exactly what Takayama feels like under a foot of snow.
