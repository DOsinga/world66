**John Hersey, *Hiroshima* (1946)** — The original and still essential account. Six survivors, their lives in the hours before the bomb, and their experiences in the aftermath. Hersey wrote it for the *New Yorker*, which dedicated its entire issue of 31 August 1946 to the piece. It is brief, devastating, and restrained. Read it before you go; nothing else will prepare you as well.

**Tamiki Hara, *Summer Flowers* (1947)** — A short story cycle by a poet who survived the bombing and wrote about it with extraordinary economy and clarity. Translated into English alongside other Hiroshima literature in the anthology *Hiroshima: Three Witnesses*. Hara died by suicide in 1951, unable to continue living in a world that could produce such a weapon.

**Masuji Ibuse, *Black Rain* (1966)** — A novel about Yasuko, a young woman trying to marry years after the bomb, and the stigma that survivors (*hibakusha*) faced from a society afraid of radiation contamination. Ibuse uses a fictional diary to recreate the days immediately after the blast with the density and texture of a documentary. One of the great Japanese novels of the 20th century.

**Setsuko Thurlow, *Setsuko Hiroshima* (2022)** — Memoir by one of the most prominent hibakusha activists, who was 13 years old on 6 August 1945 and survived by chance. She later became a central figure in the International Campaign to Abolish Nuclear Weapons and accepted the Nobel Peace Prize on its behalf in 2017. Honest about trauma, and clear-eyed about politics.
