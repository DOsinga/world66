Hamamatsu sits between the Pacific and Lake Hamana, west of Shizuoka city. It is a working Japanese city rather than a polished showpiece, but that is part of the appeal: Yamaha, Kawai, and Roland give it a real musical-instrument identity, Tokugawa Ieyasu gives it historical weight, and the coast breaks into a broad belt of sand dunes.

Start near the station at the [Hamamatsu Museum of Musical Instruments](/asia/japan/hamamatsu/hamamatsu_museum_of_musical_instruments), the clearest expression of the city's "City of Music" claim. From there it is an easy hop to [Hamamatsu Castle](/asia/japan/hamamatsu/hamamatsu_castle), where Ieyasu lived before becoming shogun. The keep is reconstructed, but the park and stone base still give the place a proper centre of gravity.

Hamamatsu is strongest when you mix the city with its edges. [Nakatajima Sand Dunes](/asia/japan/hamamatsu/nakatajima_sand_dunes) feel surprisingly wild for somewhere reached by bus, while Lake Hamana brings onsen, eel restaurants, boat rides, and small side trips. It makes a good one-day stop between Nagoya and Shizuoka if you want something less obvious than another castle town.
