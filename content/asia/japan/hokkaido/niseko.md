Niseko is Hokkaido's best-known international ski town, spread around the lower slopes of Mount Annupuri with Mount Yotei standing across the valley like a smaller Fuji. The appeal is simple: cold Siberian weather drops dry powder on a linked set of ski areas, then the day ends in hot spring baths instead of a long drive back to a city.

The name covers several bases rather than one tidy town centre. Hirafu has the busiest village scene and the largest ski area; Annupuri is quieter, lower-key, and closer to old onsen inns; Niseko Village and Hanazono fill out the mountain with more terrain. Together they make a winter destination with enough variety for beginners, powder hunters, and people who mainly want snow, food, and baths in the same day.

Niseko is not only a winter stop. Summer brings rafting on the Shiribetsu River, cycling, farm cafes, and hiking around the Niseko volcanic group. Still, the place makes most sense from December to March, when the snow is the point and the villages feel fully awake.
