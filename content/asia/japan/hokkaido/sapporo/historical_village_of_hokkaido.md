About 15 minutes from central Sapporo by subway and tram, the Historical Village of Hokkaido is an open-air museum containing 52 original buildings from the Meiji and Taisho periods, relocated here from across the island when their home communities could no longer maintain them. The collection covers Hokkaido's pioneer era in unusual breadth: farmhouses, a fish-processing plant, a mine office, a Shinto shrine, a rural railway station, a provincial newspaper office, and a two-storey merchant's house from Otaru, among others. Staff in period dress demonstrate traditional crafts in several of the buildings through the day.

The buildings are arranged into four themed zones — town, farm, fishing village, and mountain village — and the grounds are large enough that you need 2-3 hours to walk through properly. Unlike recreated heritage villages that build reproductions, everything here is authentic, and the mix of structures gives a more honest picture of what pioneer Hokkaido actually looked like than the standard tourist narrative of adventure and frontier spirit. The museum has good English signage. Open year-round; in winter the grounds are covered in snow and sleigh rides are available, which sounds gimmicky but works well with the period architecture.
