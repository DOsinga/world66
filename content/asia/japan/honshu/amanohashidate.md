Amanohashidate — the "Bridge to Heaven" — is one of the Nihon Sankei, the Three Great Views of [Japan](/asia/japan). It is a 3.6 km pine-covered [sand spit](/asia/japan/honshu/amanohashidate/the_sandbar) stretching across Miyazu Bay on the northern coast of Kyoto Prefecture.

The traditional way to appreciate the view is to turn your back to it, bend over, and look at the sandbar upside down between your legs — framed against the sky, it is said to resemble a bridge ascending to heaven. The [Amanohashidate Cable Car](/asia/japan/honshu/amanohashidate/amanohashidate_cable_car) at the northern end of the bay takes you to the classic Kasamatsu Park viewpoint, where you can perform this ritual. Near the top, [Kono Shrine](/asia/japan/honshu/amanohashidate/kono_shrine) is one of the oldest in the Tango region, dedicated to the deities of sea and safe travel.

Walking or cycling along the spit itself is pleasant, with over 5,000 pine trees providing shade. At the southern end, [Chionji Temple](/asia/japan/honshu/amanohashidate/chionji_shrine) houses one of Japan's most important images of Monju Bosatsu. A boat service crosses the bay if you prefer not to walk back. The hill above the northern viewpoint is also home to [Nariai-ji](/asia/japan/honshu/amanohashidate/nariai_ji), a venerable temple on the Saigoku Kannon Pilgrimage. A short train ride away, [Miyazu Castle](/asia/japan/honshu/amanohashidate/miyazu_castle) adds a feudal layer to the visit. The area's ryokan and onsen are excellent, and the seafood — especially matsuba crab in winter — is superb.
