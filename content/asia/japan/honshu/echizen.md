Echizen is a workbench of a city in southern Fukui, better known by its old centre, Takefu. It does not have the instant drama of a castle town, but it has something more tactile: forged kitchen knives, handmade washi, old shrine craft traditions, soba shops, and a literary link to Murasaki Shikibu, the author of *The Tale of Genji*.

The best reason to come is the craft circuit east of the centre. [Takefu Knife Village](/asia/japan/honshu/echizen/takefu_knife_village) lets visitors look down into a shared workshop where Echizen blades are forged, polished, and sold by local makers. Farther east, [Echizen Washi Village](/asia/japan/honshu/echizen/echizen_washi_village) gathers paper museums, workshops, and small makers in the district where the craft has been passed down for centuries.

The paper story also has a shrine. [Okamoto and Otaki Shrine](/asia/japan/honshu/echizen/okamoto_and_otaki_shrine), dedicated to the goddess of paper, is one of the most distinctive cultural stops in the city. Back near Takefu, [Murasaki Shikibu Park](/asia/japan/honshu/echizen/murasaki_shikibu_park) gives the city a quieter Heian-period note, with a formal garden and a view toward Mount Hino.

Echizen works best if you arrive with a clear plan and enough time to move between spread-out sights. A car helps, but buses run from Takefu toward Washi no Sato. Treat it as a day for watching people make things, not as a place to tick off monuments.
