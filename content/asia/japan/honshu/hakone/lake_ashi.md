Lake Ashi (Ashino-ko) lies at the southwestern end of the Hakone caldera, 725 metres above sea level, and provides the most celebrated view in the area: on a clear day, Mount Fuji rises above the cedar forests on the far shore in a composition that looks almost too good to be real. The lake is accessible by sightseeing boat from the town of Togendai at the northern end, crossing to Moto-Hakone and Hakone-machi on the southern shore.

The boats are theatrical — modelled on European galleons and operated by competing companies with different paintwork. The crossing takes about 25 minutes. The view of Fuji is most reliable in the early morning, before haze builds; it is often completely obscured by midday in summer.

Near the Hakone-machi pier, the torii gate of Hakone Shrine stands in the shallow water — a photogenic marker at the water's edge, with the shrine itself in the cedars behind it.
