  Hikone Castle is one of only four castles in Japan designated a National Treasure — the others are Matsumoto, Inuyama, and Himeji. Built in 1622 using stones salvaged from the demolished Sawayama Castle nearby, the three-storey keep has survived every earthquake, war, and modernisation drive since. In 1877, Emperor Meiji's minister Ōkuma Shigenobu ordered it demolished; only the personal intervention of the emperor himself, who had visited and admired it, spared the castle from the wrecking crew.
---

One of the twelve original castles remaining in Japan and one of the country's four National Treasures. The three-storey keep dates from 1622 and sits on a forested hill above Hikone city, with Lake Biwa glittering to the west. Compact by the standards of Himeji or Osaka, it is all the better for it — the proportions feel human, the timber interiors are beautiful, and the view from the top floor takes in the full expanse of Japan's largest lake.

The approach climbs through stone-walled compounds, past the Tenbin-yagura gatehouse and along cherry-lined paths that make early April the most popular visiting time of year. The grounds are extensive and include the reconstructed Nihonmatsu-maru enclosure. Allow at least two hours to see the castle properly, or longer if cherry blossoms are out.
