Built on wooden piers out over the tidal waters of Hiroshima Bay, Itsukushima Shrine appears to float at high tide — a deliberate illusion maintained since the original shrine was founded in 593 CE. The vermilion *torii* gate standing in the sea is one of Japan's most photographed images, and with good reason. At low tide you can walk out to its base.

The shrine complex (admission ¥500) includes several halls connected by covered walkways and corridors built at water level. Beyond it, the island itself is worth exploring — deer wander freely, the hillside temple of **Daishō-in** is rich with stone lanterns and Buddhist imagery, and **Mount Misen** can be reached by ropeway for wide views across the Seto Inland Sea.

Getting there: take Hiroden Line 2 to Hiroden-miyajimaguchi (about 70 minutes from the city centre), then the JR/Miyajima ferry (10 minutes, ¥200 each way). The Hiroshima Visiting Area Pass covers this entire journey.
