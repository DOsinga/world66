---
title: "Things to Do"
type: section
---

Hiroshima divides naturally into two parts: the memorial landscape around Peace Park, and the rest of the city that has quietly rebuilt itself. Both are worth your time.

The **Peace Memorial Park** occupies the triangular sandbar where the Motoyasu and Honkawa rivers meet — the area once known as Nakajima, which was directly below the hypocenter. The park's monuments are arranged along a single axis. Stand at the **Cenotaph for the Victims** and look through its curved stone arch: the flame of peace burns in the pool before you, and the **A-Bomb Dome** stands framed in the distance exactly as intended. The dome — the only structure left standing near the hypocenter — was a deliberate choice. Its gutted skeleton is the most powerful thing in the park.

The **Hiroshima Peace Memorial Museum** is one of the essential museums in Japan. It does not flinch. The exhibits show in specific, unflinching detail what a nuclear weapon does to a city and its people — burned clothing, melted roof tiles, the shadow of a man etched into stone steps. Allow two hours minimum. Entry ¥200.

Away from the Peace Park, **Hiroshima Castle** — destroyed in 1945 and reconstructed in 1958 — houses a good museum on the castle town and samurai culture. The moats and gardens around it are pleasant to wander.

**Shukkei-en** is a miniature landscape garden dating to 1620, compressed into a small space so that hills, bridges, and islands create the impression of a much larger scene. Damaged in the bombing, it was restored; an unusual survivor.

Across the bay, **Miyajima** is a 10-minute ferry ride from Miyajimaguchi (tram Line 2 from the city centre). The Itsukushima Shrine's orange *torii* gate standing in the water at high tide is one of the most recognisable images in Japan. The island is small enough to walk, and the deer are genuinely tame.
