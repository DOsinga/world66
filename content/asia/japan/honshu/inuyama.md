Inuyama is a small castle town on the Kiso River in Aichi Prefecture, sometimes called the "Japanese Rhine" for the wooded gorge the river cuts through upstream. The town's centrepiece is [Inuyama Castle](/asia/japan/honshu/inuyama/inuyama_castle), one of only twelve original castles remaining in Japan and a national treasure — the oldest surviving wooden castle keep in the country, built in 1537, perched on a bluff with a panoramic view across the river.

Below the castle, the [Uraku-en garden](/asia/japan/honshu/inuyama/uraku_en_garden) contains the Jo-an teahouse, a national treasure built in 1618 by Oda Uraku (younger brother of the warlord Oda Nobunaga) and one of the finest surviving examples of tea-ceremony architecture. The Honmachi street running up to the castle gate preserves a handsome stretch of old townscape: sake breweries, traditional shops, and a small shrine. In summer (June through October), [traditional ukai cormorant fishing](/asia/japan/honshu/inuyama/kiso_river_ukai) takes place on the river after dark — fishermen work by torchlight from wooden boats with Inuyama Castle silhouetted on the hill behind them, an experience that has been running for 1,300 years.

A few kilometres to the east, [Museum Meiji-mura](/asia/japan/honshu/inuyama/meiji_mura) preserves 67 Meiji-era buildings in an open-air setting including Frank Lloyd Wright's Imperial Hotel lobby. [Little World Museum of Man](/asia/japan/honshu/inuyama/little_world_museum), another open-air museum nearby, features full-scale reconstructed dwellings from 20 countries. Inuyama is an easy day trip from [Nagoya](/asia/japan/honshu/nagoya), about 30 minutes by train on the Meitetsu line.
