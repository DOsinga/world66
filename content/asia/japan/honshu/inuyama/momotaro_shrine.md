story: >
  Momotaro — the Peach Boy — is one of Japan's most beloved folk heroes: a child born from a giant peach floating downstream, who grows up to lead a band of animals against a demon island. The legend is set on the Kiso River, and the shrine here at Momotaro Park claims to mark the spot where the peach was found. The local connection to the folk tale runs deep enough that the town's name, Inuyama ("dog mountain"), references the loyal dog that accompanied Momotaro on his quest.
---

About three kilometres upstream from Inuyama Castle along the Kiso River, Momotaro Shrine is dedicated to the hero of Japan's most famous folk tale. The shrine complex sits in a park on the riverbank and contains several buildings, a collection of demon masks and figurines donated by worshippers, and a small museum dedicated to Momotaro legends from across Japan. The atmosphere is cheerfully kitsch — this is a popular spot for families, and the surrounding park fills with picnickers on weekends.

The walk upstream from Inuyama-Yuen Station along the river takes about 40 minutes and passes the Ohaguro-bashi footbridge, which offers good views back toward the castle. Cycling the riverside path is an even better option. The shrine's collection of demon-related offerings is oddly compelling — rows of ceramic oni (demon) masks donated by businesses seeking protection from evil, which somehow manages to feel both solemn and absurd.
