The second-ranked of Kamakura's Five Great Zen Temples, Engaku-ji spreads across a forested valley just minutes from Kita-Kamakura Station. Founded in 1282, its grounds encompass a remarkable collection of Zen architecture: the Sanmon (main gate), the Butsuden (Buddha hall), the tea house above the pond, and the Shariden, one of the oldest surviving wooden structures in Japan. The autumn maple foliage here is among the finest in Kamakura.

The temple is active and serene rather than touristy. Zazen sitting meditation sessions are open to visitors on weekend mornings. Admission around 500 yen.
