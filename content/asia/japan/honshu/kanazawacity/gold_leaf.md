  Kanazawa produces about 99% of all the gold leaf made in Japan. The reason is partly historical (the Maeda clan actively promoted craft industries to keep skilled workers in the city and away from Edo) and partly climatic: the high humidity of the Sea of Japan coast is ideal for gold leaf production, because the moisture in the air prevents the leaf from sticking to itself during the beating process.
---

Kanazawa has been Japan's gold leaf capital for over 400 years. The Kanazawa Yasue Gold Leaf Museum, near the northern part of the city, tells the story of this craft tradition and displays extraordinary examples of gold leaf work across textiles, ceramics, lacquerware, and even food.

The museum is small but interesting, with demonstrations of the beating process that turns gold into leaf thin enough to be translucent. The gift shop sells gold leaf products including the city's famous gold leaf ice cream — a sheet of edible gold wrapped around soft serve, which photographs well and costs about ¥700. The craft workshops in the Higashi Chaya area and around the market also offer gold leaf crafting experiences.
