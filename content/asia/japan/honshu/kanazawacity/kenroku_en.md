Kenroku-en is consistently ranked among Japan's three great landscape gardens, alongside Kairaku-en in Mito and Koraku-en in Okayama. The garden was built by the Maeda clan over two centuries, and the result is a remarkably varied landscape: ponds, streams, stone lanterns, cherry trees, and pine trees in every direction.

Each season transforms the garden. Spring brings cherry blossoms in late March and early April. Summer is green and lush. Autumn produces outstanding foliage — the maples turn red and orange in late October and November. Winter is perhaps the most famous: the garden staff spend weeks installing yukitsuri, rope supports for the pine tree branches to protect them from snow. The pattern of ropes spreading down from a central pole became one of the iconic images of Japanese winter.

The garden is open daily and has a modest entrance fee. It is at its best in early morning before the tour groups arrive, and in late afternoon in autumn.
