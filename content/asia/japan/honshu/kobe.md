Kobe sits between the Rokko mountains and Osaka Bay, a port city that has been open to foreign influence longer than almost anywhere else in Japan. Together with [Osaka](/asia/japan/honshu/osaka) and [Kyoto](/asia/japan/honshu/kyoto) it forms the heart of the Kansai region, and it is about three hours and twenty minutes from [Tokyo](/asia/japan/tokyo) by Shinkansen.

The [Kitano-chō](/asia/japan/honshu/kobe/kitano_cho) district is where foreign traders settled in the late 1800s, and their "ijinkan" — Western-style houses with Japanese touches — still line the hillside streets. Some are open to the public, others have been converted into boutiques and restaurants. The area has a pleasantly un-Japanese atmosphere, with wrought-iron balconies and bay windows looking out over the harbour. The [Kobe Mosque](/asia/japan/honshu/kobe/kobe_mosque), Japan's oldest, stands nearby.

The [Nada sake breweries](/asia/japan/honshu/kobe/nada_sake_breweries) in the eastern part of the city make Kobe one of Japan's great sake-producing districts. The breweries use water from the Rokko mountains, and several are open to visitors for tours and tastings. [Rokko Mountain](/asia/japan/honshu/kobe/rokko_mountain) itself can be reached by cable car and provides panoramic views over the city and Osaka Bay.

[Arima Onsen](/asia/japan/honshu/kobe/arima_onsen), tucked into the mountains behind the city, is one of Japan's oldest spa resorts. Its mineral-rich waters come in two types: "gold" (iron-rich, rust-coloured) and "silver" (clear, carbonated). The town around the springs is compact and pleasant, with traditional ryokan and the Zuihoji Park where the warlord Hideyoshi Toyotomi once held tea ceremonies.

[Kobe beef](/asia/japan/honshu/kobe/kobe_beef) needs no introduction, but it is worth noting that the city is also excellent for Chinese food — [Nankin-machi](/asia/japan/honshu/kobe/nankinmachi), the Chinatown, is one of Japan's three great Chinatowns — and for Indian and Southeast Asian cuisine brought by the port's long trading connections. The [Kobe Luminarie](/asia/japan/honshu/kobe/kobe_luminarie) light festival each December draws millions of visitors, and the [Kobe City Museum](/asia/japan/honshu/kobe/kobe_city_museum) holds the finest collection of Namban art in Japan.
