  Nada's sake production depends on a specific local water source called Miyamizu —
  "shrine water" — from springs in the Nishinomiya area. Miyamizu has an unusually
  high mineral content that accelerates the fermentation process, producing a dry,
  clean sake style known as Nada-mono. When this connection was discovered in the
  early 19th century, the Nada brewers rapidly became the dominant force in the
  Japanese sake industry, and they have remained so ever since.

Nada, the eastern district of Kobe, is one of Japan's most important sake-producing areas. The combination of excellent water from the Rokko mountains, rice from the surrounding plains, and cold maritime air from Osaka Bay has made Nada-sake synonymous with quality for over two centuries. Several of the major breweries are open to visitors for tours and tastings.

The three main brewery museums in Nada — Hakutsuru, Kikumasamune, and Hamafukutsuru — are all within walking distance of each other near Uozaki station on the Hanshin Line. The tours explain the sake-making process from rice polishing through fermentation and pressing. Tastings are included, and the brewery gift shops are a good place to buy sake at competitive prices. The sake styles produced here tend toward the dry and robust — food-friendly rather than delicate.
