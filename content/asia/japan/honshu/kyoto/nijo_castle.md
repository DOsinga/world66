Built in 1603 as the Kyoto residence of the first Tokugawa shogun, Nijo Castle is one of the best-preserved examples of feudal Japanese architecture in the country. The famous feature is the nightingale floors of the Ninomaru Palace: the boards were constructed to creak at any weight, preventing silent approach. The paintings on the sliding screens are original 17th-century works. The garden was redesigned repeatedly under different shoguns. Go early to walk the palace before the tour groups arrive.
