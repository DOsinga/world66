Kawabata's most celebrated novel follows a Tokyo dilettante through his seasons-long liaison with a geisha in a mountain hot-spring town. Not set near Fuji, but essential for understanding the Japanese cultural landscape — the relationship between tradition and modernity, the beauty of transience (mono no aware), and the seasonal rhythms that make Japan what it is. The Nobel Prize committee cited it when awarding Kawabata in 1968.
