  In 1898 a British colonial archaeologist excavated a small stone reliquary from a stupa in Piprahwa, India, with an inscription identifying the contents as the cremated remains of the Buddha himself. The find was divided between Thailand and a list of Buddhist nations; Japan's share was eventually settled here in 1904. The relics are kept in the inner sanctuary of the Hokyobu pagoda and shown to the public on the 21st of each month.

Nittaiji — literally "Japan-Thailand Temple" — was founded in 1904 specifically to house relics of the Buddha donated to Japan by the King of Siam (now Thailand). It is a non-sectarian Buddhist temple, formally outside the major Japanese schools, and the closest thing in Japan to a national religious site for the historic Buddha himself.

The grounds in the eastern hills above Kakuozan are extensive and lightly visited compared to the central sights. A monthly antique market on the 21st (the day of Kobo Daishi's death) draws collectors and the temple street fills with stalls. The five-storey Gohonzon Hokyobu pagoda is a 1997 reconstruction. Kakuozan Station (Higashiyama subway line), then a 10-minute uphill walk.
