Kōraku-en is one of the three gardens traditionally ranked as Japan's finest, alongside Kenroku-en in Kanazawa and Mito's Kairakuen. It covers about 13 hectares on a bend of the Asahi River opposite Okayama Castle, and the design incorporates views of the castle across the water as a borrowed landscape element. The garden is a stroll garden (kaiyushiki) with a circuit path leading through tea houses, ponds, rice paddies — unusual in a ceremonial garden — crane aviaries, and grassed promenades.

The combination of the open lawn areas, the mature pine groupings, and the castle visible beyond the river gives Kōraku-en a spacious, confident quality. Cherry blossoms and irises in spring and azaleas in autumn provide seasonal colour. The garden is generally busy but large enough to find quiet corners. The best approach is early morning before the tour groups arrive; the tea houses within the garden offer matcha and seasonal sweets at reasonable prices.
