Okayama Castle stands on the western bank of the Asahi River with Kōraku-en garden on the opposite bank, and the two are typically visited together on the same half-day circuit. The current castle keep is a faithful exterior reproduction of the original Momoyama-period structure, with the distinctive black-lacquered wooden boards and the unusual moon-viewing turret (tsukimi yagura) that survived the bombing intact. The interior museum displays period armour, weapons, and historical items related to the castle and the Ikeda clan who controlled Okayama domain through most of the Edo period.

The castle sits on a low rise above the river with good views from the upper floors — particularly over the garden to the east and the city to the west. The grounds include reconstructed earthworks and a small garden. Entry to the castle requires a ticket; the outer grounds and garden promenade along the river are free. The walk from Okayama Station to the castle crosses the Asahi River on the stone Tsurumi Bridge and takes about 20 minutes on foot.
