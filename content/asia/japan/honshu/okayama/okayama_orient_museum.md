The Okayama Orient Museum holds one of Japan's most significant collections of ancient Middle Eastern and Eastern Mediterranean art — an unlikely specialisation for a provincial Japanese city that reflects Okayama's pre-war academic connections to archaeological work in the region. The collection spans objects from ancient Mesopotamia, Egypt, Persia, and the Byzantine world, with particular strength in ancient West Asian artefacts.

The museum occupies a purpose-built building near the castle district and is rarely crowded, which makes it an unusually contemplative experience for a collection of this quality. Cuneiform tablets, ancient glass vessels, cylinder seals, and Islamic ceramics are among the highlights. For visitors interested in ancient history who find themselves in western Japan, this is a genuinely unexpected stop. The museum is within easy walking distance of Okayama Castle and Kōraku-en.
