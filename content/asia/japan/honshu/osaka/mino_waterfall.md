The 33-metre Mino-o Falls at the end of a wooded 2.7-kilometre trail through Meiji-no-Mori Mino-o Quasi-National Park, north of Osaka. The path up to the falls follows the Mino-o River through cedar and maple forest, passing several small restaurants and sweet shops selling momiji (maple leaf) tempura — a snack unique to this area, in which fresh maple leaves are pickled for a year and then battered and deep-fried.

The trail is completely walkable in ordinary shoes and is suitable for families. Autumn is the most popular season when the maples turn bright red and orange along the entire path — crowds are enormous in October and November. The falls themselves are impressive after rain but can be reduced to a trickle in dry summer months. The Minoh Insectarium at the trailhead is worth stopping at for the butterfly house. The area is accessible from Osaka in about 40 minutes by Hankyu Minoh Line.
