Okonomiyaki — a thick savoury pancake of batter, shredded cabbage, and various additions cooked on an iron griddle — is one of the dishes most associated with Osaka, and the city has a distinct regional style. Osaka-style (also called Kansai-style) okonomiyaki mixes all the ingredients together in the batter before cooking; Hiroshima-style layers them separately, and the two cities have an ongoing and completely serious argument about which is correct.

The best way to eat it in Osaka is at a restaurant where you cook it yourself on a griddle sunk into your table — the cook brings you the prepared ingredients and leaves the rest to you. The Dotonbori area and the lanes around Shinsaibashi have numerous restaurants with table-top grills. Standard additions include shrimp, pork, kimchi, and cheese; the authentic finishing touches are a thick sweet-salty sauce, Japanese mayonnaise, bonito flakes, and nori powder.
