The Tsukuba Botanical Garden is a branch of the National Museum of Nature and Science, opened in 1976 as part of the science city's founding institutions. Its 16 hectares are divided into themed zones — temperate forest, tropical greenhouse, alpine plants, and an extensive collection of Japanese native species — making it more interesting to walk through than the average municipal park.

The seasonal calendar is one of the main draws. Cherry blossoms arrive in late March and April, followed by wisteria in May. Summer brings the lotus pond to life. Autumn is the quietest but most colourful season, when the maple and zelkova trees turn before the city's other parks. The tropical greenhouse stays warm and green year-round, which makes it a useful refuge on a cold winter day.

Admission is modest, and the garden is largely uncrowded even on weekends, since most visitors to Tsukuba head straight for Mount Tsukuba or the JAXA centre. The connecting bus from Tsukuba Station takes about 15 minutes. Comfortable walking shoes are advisable since the paths vary between paved and gravel.
