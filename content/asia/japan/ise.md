Ise is a pilgrimage city in Mie Prefecture, best known for [Ise Jingu](/asia/japan/ise/ise_jingu), the most important Shinto shrine in Japan. The shrine is not one building but a pair of sacred precincts, Geku and Naiku, set among old trees and rebuilt every 20 years in a ritual that keeps the architecture ancient and new at the same time.

The city works because the sacred sites have a real setting around them. Naiku leads into Oharai-machi and Okage Yokocho, where pilgrims eat Ise udon, buy sweets, and wander wooden shopfronts along the Isuzu River. The old merchant lanes of [Kawasaki Kaiwai](/asia/japan/ise/kawasaki_kaiwai) are quieter, but they show another side of Ise: river trade, warehouses, and old inns.

Ise is easy to visit from Nagoya, Osaka, or Kyoto, though it deserves more than a quick shrine stop. Add [Meoto Iwa](/asia/japan/ise/meoto_iwa) on the coast at Futami if you want the classic seaside image of two sacred rocks tied by a heavy rope.
