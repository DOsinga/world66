Iwate is the second largest prefecture in Japan by area and one of the least densely populated, covering a wide stretch of northern Honshu between the Kitakami Mountains and the Pacific coast. It sits at the heart of the [Tohoku](/asia/japan/tohoku) region — remote, mountainous, culturally distinct, and largely overlooked by foreign tourists.

The capital, Morioka, is a compact river city with some excellent Meiji-era architecture, the ruins of a feudal castle draped in cherry trees, and a food culture that punches well above its size. The [Morioka Castle Ruins](/asia/japan/iwate/morioka_castle_ruins), the extraordinary [Rock-Breaking Cherry Tree](/asia/japan/iwate/rock_breaking_cherry_tree), and the Meiji-period [Bank of Iwate Red Brick Building](/asia/japan/iwate/bank_of_iwate_red_brick_building) can all be covered in half a day on foot. Morioka is also the home of three famous noodle dishes — Wanko Soba, Morioka Reimen, and Jajamen — and eating all three in a single day is considered a respectable local achievement.

South of Morioka, the town of Hiraizumi was one of the great cultural centres of 12th-century Japan, when the Fujiwara clan lords built a Buddhist paradise to rival Kyoto. [Chusonji](/asia/japan/iwate/chusonji), with its gilded Konjikido hall, and the Pure Land garden of [Motsuji](/asia/japan/iwate/motsuji) are among the finest historic sites in northern Japan, now listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The [Takadachi Gikeido](/asia/japan/iwate/takadachi_gikeido) chapel on a bluff above the Kitakami River marks the site where Japan's most romantic warrior, Minamoto no Yoshitsune, met his end — and where the poet Matsuo Basho stood in 1689 and wrote the haiku that made Hiraizumi famous.

Between Morioka and Hiraizumi lies Hanamaki, birthplace of the poet and writer Kenji Miyazawa. The [Kenji Miyazawa Memorial Museum](/asia/japan/iwate/kenji_miyazawa_memorial_museum) in the forests above the town is the most evocative memorial to a Japanese literary figure you are likely to find, and the [Tenshochi Cherry Blossoms](/asia/japan/iwate/tenshochi_cherry_blossoms) along the Kitakami River here rank among the finest in Tohoku.

To the east, the [Sanriku Coast](/asia/japan/iwate/sanriku_coast) offers some of the most dramatic scenery in Japan — deep ria inlets, fishing villages, and clifftop views over the open Pacific. The [Kitayamazaki Cliffs](/asia/japan/iwate/kitayamazaki_cliffs) near Tanohata are the highlight: 200 metres of sheer basalt dropping straight into the sea. The coast was devastated by the 2011 earthquake and tsunami and is still rebuilding; coming here is a richer experience for the awareness of what happened and what the communities have done since.

Inland, [Mount Hayachine](/asia/japan/iwate/mount_hayachine) provides excellent hiking from midsummer, with ridge walks through alpine flowers to a summit view that on clear days takes in both the mountains and the coast. The [Genbikei Gorge](/asia/japan/iwate/genbikei_gorge) near Ichinoseki is a more gentle destination — a narrow basalt ravine with a famous bucket-and-rope mochi delivery system that needs to be seen to be believed.
