Chusonji is a Tendai Buddhist temple complex on a forested hill above the town of Hiraizumi, 130 kilometres south of Morioka. Founded in 850 and expanded by the Fujiwara clan lords in the 12th century, it was the spiritual and cultural heart of a northern kingdom that briefly rivaled Kyoto in wealth and ambition. The complex is a UNESCO World Heritage Site as part of the Historic Monuments and Sites of Hiraizumi.

The centrepiece is the Konjikido, a small but extraordinarily rich hall completed in 1124. Every surface inside is covered with gold leaf, lacquer inlay, and mother-of-pearl — the accumulated wealth of the Tohoku gold and amber trade poured into a single chamber. The hall now sits inside a protective modern enclosure, but the shimmer of it in the dim light is genuinely breathtaking. The Sankoden treasury alongside holds the temple's collection of National Treasures, including Buddha images, ritual implements, and the embroidered cloth canopies that once hung in the Konjikido. Budget at least two hours for a thorough visit.
