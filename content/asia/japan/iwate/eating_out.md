title: Eating Out
type: section
---

Iwate has three foods worth travelling for. Wanko Soba is the theatrical version: small mouthfuls of buckwheat noodles served in rapid succession by a server who keeps refilling your bowl until you cover it with the lid. The competitive record runs past 500 bowls; a normal meal might be 30–60. The best places to try this are in Morioka, where it originated as a form of hospitality.

Morioka also claims Morioka Reimen — a cold noodle dish with Korean origins, served in a clear beef broth with watermelon, kimchi, and boiled egg. It is refreshing and slightly odd in the best possible way. The third local speciality is Jajamen, a Chinese-derived dish of thick wheat noodles topped with a savoury meat and miso paste sauce; after eating, you add raw egg and a splash of broth to make a small soup from the residue.

For fresh seafood, the Sanriku coast is the source: sea urchin, abalone, and oysters from the cold Pacific waters of the rias. Kamaishi and Miyako have waterfront restaurants serving the day's catch simply prepared.
