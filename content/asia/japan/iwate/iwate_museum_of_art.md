The Iwate Museum of Art (Iwate Kenritsu Bijutsukan) opened in 2001 on the western outskirts of Morioka, in a purpose-built building set in parkland above the Shizukuishi River valley. The collection focuses on artists connected to Iwate Prefecture, with particular strength in Western-style oil painting from the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

The permanent collection includes works by Yasui Sotaro and other painters of the Meiji and Taisho periods who bridged Japanese and European traditions. The museum also holds a good collection of sculpture by Funakoshi Yasutake, a Morioka native who trained in France. Temporary exhibitions bring in significant works from other institutions. The museum is about 20 minutes by bus from Morioka Station and makes for a worthwhile half-day alongside a visit to nearby Takizawa.
