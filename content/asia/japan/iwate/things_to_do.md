title: Things to Do
type: section
---

Iwate rewards travelers who look beyond the obvious. Morioka, the prefecture capital, packs a great deal into a compact riverside centre — ruined castle walls, a cherry tree growing from a boulder, a graceful Meiji-era bank building. South of Morioka the town of Hanamaki was home to Kenji Miyazawa, Japan's beloved poet-naturalist, and his memory permeates the place.

The real draws, though, are the outliers. Hiraizumi, in the south, was a genuine rival to Kyoto in the 12th century, and the remains of that golden age — the gilded Konjikido hall, the Pure Land garden of Motsuji — are among the finest historic sites in the Tohoku region, now listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site. To the east, the Sanriku coast offers rugged ria scenery, remote fishing towns, and one of the most dramatic stretches of the Pacific shore in Japan.
