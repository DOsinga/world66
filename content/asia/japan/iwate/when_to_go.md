---
title: When to Go
type: section
---

Spring (late April to early May) brings cherry blossoms to Morioka's castle ruins and the famous Rock-Breaking Cherry Tree. The season is short and intense, and the city gets busy during Golden Week.

Summer (June to August) is warm and relatively dry, good for hiking on Mount Hayachine and exploring the Sanriku coast. The Chagu Chagu Umako horse festival in Morioka takes place on the second Saturday of June — a procession of decorated farm horses that has been going on for over 200 years.

Autumn (October to November) colours the mountains and the castle park in reds and golds. This is arguably the best season for a visit, with comfortable temperatures and fewer crowds.

Winter is cold — Morioka regularly sees snow — but the ski resort at Shizukuishi operates from December through March and the Wanko Soba noodle-eating tradition is a fine excuse to stay warm indoors. The coast is raw and wind-battered but has a bleak beauty of its own.
