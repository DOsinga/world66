Himeji is a city in Hyogo Prefecture, about 90 minutes west of Osaka by Shinkansen, and it has one of the finest castles in Japan. [Himeji Castle](/asia/japan/kansai/himeji/himeji_castle) — also called White Heron Castle for its gleaming white plaster walls — is a UNESCO World Heritage site and one of only twelve original castles in the country to have survived intact, never destroyed or rebuilt.

The castle sits on a hill visible from the Shinkansen tracks as you pull into the station. Walk straight down [Otemae-dori](/asia/japan/kansai/himeji/otemae_dori) for about 15 minutes to reach it, or take the [Miyuki-dori arcade](/asia/japan/kansai/himeji/miyuki_dori) running parallel if the weather turns. The grounds are extensive and worth taking slowly; the main keep rises six storeys and the view from the top over the city and surrounding plain is excellent.

The [Koko-en garden](/asia/japan/kansai/himeji/koko_en), reconstructed in the Edo period style just next to the castle grounds, makes a pleasant addition to the visit. Allow at least half a day for both. If you have a full day, the mountain temple of [Engyo-ji on Mount Shosha](/asia/japan/kansai/himeji/mount_shosha_engyoji) — the Last Samurai filming location — is the obvious second stop.
