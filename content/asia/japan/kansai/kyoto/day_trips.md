Kyoto is in the middle of the Kansai region, which means nearly every important historic city in western Japan is within day-trip range by train. [Nara](/asia/japan/kansai/nara), an hour away on the Kintetsu line, was the capital before Kyoto and is home to the colossal bronze Buddha at Tōdai-ji and a deer park where the tame deer bow to visitors for biscuits. [Osaka](/asia/japan/kansai/osaka), 15 minutes on the shinkansen, is Japan's great food city and a total change of pace from Kyoto. [Himeji](/asia/japan/kansai/himeji), an hour the other way, has the country's most spectacular surviving castle. Further afield, [Hiroshima](/asia/japan/chugoku/hiroshima) and the shrine island of Miyajima are reachable on the shinkansen (about 100 minutes) for a long but entirely feasible day. And the quiet castle town of [Hikone](/asia/japan/kansai/hikone) and the pine-covered sandbar of [Amanohashidate](/asia/japan/kansai/amanohashidate) on the Sea of Japan coast are pleasant alternatives to the better-known sights.

Closer in, several worthwhile half-day escapes are really single sights rather than whole towns: the Phoenix Hall at Byōdō-in in the tea town of Uji, the riverside Kifune Shrine and its lantern-lit steps in the northern mountains, and the moss gardens of Sanzen-in in the village of Ōhara.
