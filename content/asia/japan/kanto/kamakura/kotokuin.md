The Kōtoku-in Daibutsu — the Great Buddha of Kamakura — is a 13-metre bronze figure of Amida Buddha that has been sitting cross-legged in the open air since 1498. It was cast in 1252, making it slightly older than the famous Great Buddha of Nara. The scale is impressive: the head alone is over 2 metres from chin to crown, the face has a particular quality of calm abstraction that photographs badly and requires seeing in person. Inside, you can pay a small extra fee to enter the hollow bronze body.

The statue has been repaired many times over the centuries and stands today in good condition, green-bronze from weathering. The surrounding garden is pleasant, with lotus ponds in the approach.

The Kōtoku-in is in the Hase district of western Kamakura, accessible by the Enoden railway line from Kamakura Station (get off at Hase Station, then a ten-minute walk). Arriving before 9am avoids the main tour groups.
