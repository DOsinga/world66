---
title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

Tokyo has more Michelin-starred restaurants than any other city in the world — more than Paris and New York combined — but the most revelatory eating often happens at the other end of the price scale: a bowl of ramen at midnight, a sushi counter with eight seats, a yakitori joint under the railway tracks at Yurakucho. The city is serious about all of it.

The key disciplines to seek out: **ramen** (the regional varieties are dramatically different; Tokyo style is shoyu-based, Sapporo style is miso, Hakata is tonkotsu — try at least one of each); **sushi** (the Tsukiji outer market area and Ginza have excellent lunch-price counters); **izakaya** (Japanese pub-restaurants with sake, beer, and small plates — the best evenings in Tokyo happen here); **yakitori** (grilled chicken on skewers, eaten under the train tracks around Yurakucho and Shimbashi); and **tempura** (best at the counter, watching the chef work).

Breakfast at a standing soba shop near any major train station, costing under ¥500, is one of Tokyo's great pleasures.
