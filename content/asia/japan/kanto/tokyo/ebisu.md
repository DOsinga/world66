Ebisu is one of Tokyo's most likeable neighbourhoods — upscale but not flashy, with excellent restaurants and a relaxed atmosphere that sets it apart from the sensory overload of nearby [Shibuya](/asia/japan/kanto/tokyo/shibuya). The district takes its name from the Shinto god of fishermen, but the more relevant origin story is the Yebisu beer brewery that operated here until 1988.

The brewery's legacy lives on at Yebisu Garden Place, a plaza at the east exit of the station that houses the [Yebisu Beer Museum](/asia/japan/kanto/tokyo/yebisu_beer_museum), a Mitsukoshi department store, and the [Tokyo Photographic Art Museum](/asia/japan/kanto/tokyo/tokyo_photographic_art_museum). Just east of the station, [Ebisu Yokocho](/asia/japan/kanto/tokyo/ebisu_yokocho) is a covered alley of tiny open-fronted izakaya stalls, open into the small hours — Ebisu's loud, cheap, late-night side. [AFURI](/asia/japan/kanto/tokyo/afuri), the ramen shop credited with popularising the lighter yuzu-shio style, has its original branch here.

The narrow lanes west of the station, around Ebisu-Nishi, feel almost village-like by Tokyo standards — small French and Italian bistros, sake bars, and hidden spots like [Bar Trench](/asia/japan/kanto/tokyo/bar_trench), a craft cocktail bar built around absinthe and herbal liqueurs that you need to already know about to find. This is a neighbourhood for eating and drinking well, not for sightseeing, and that is precisely the point.
