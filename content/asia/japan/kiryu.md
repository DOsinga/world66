Kiryu is an old textile city in Gunma, close enough to Tokyo for a long day but far enough from the standard route to feel local. For centuries it was known for silk and woven cloth, and that trade still shapes the streets: sawtooth-roofed mills, former warehouses, merchant houses, specialist shops, and small museums survive across the old town.

This is not a city of blockbuster sights. Kiryu works because its identity is unusually coherent. You can start at Kiryu Tenmangu, walk the Honcho historic district, look for old textile buildings, then spend time with looms, dyes, and silk at the city's museums. On the right first Saturday, markets and mechanical doll performances give the old festival culture a visible pulse.

Kiryu is also a good reminder that industrial Japan is not only steel, ships, and concrete. Here the story is softer and more tactile: cloth, wood, brick, narrow streets, and the stubborn survival of local craft.
