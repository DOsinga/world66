Fukuoka is the largest city on the island of Kyushu and one of Japan's most livable cities by most measures — compact, green, genuinely friendly, and well-served by food. It sits at the northern tip of Kyushu facing the Korean Strait, which has given it a cosmopolitan, outward-looking character for centuries. The city was historically divided into Hakata (the merchant and port quarter) and Fukuoka (the castle town), and today the area around Hakata Station remains the commercial core.

The food culture is the main reason to come specifically for Fukuoka rather than just passing through. Hakata ramen — tonkotsu broth, thin noodles, sliced pork, and pickled ginger — was developed here and tastes best at one of the city's yatai, the open-air street stalls that line the river banks in Nakasu. Mentaiko (spicy marinated pollock roe) is the other obsession. The seafood, from the nearby Sea of Japan, is excellent.

Canal City is the central shopping and entertainment complex, but the city also has large malls in the suburbs — Marinoa City Fukuoka in the west is one of the bigger ones. Ohori Park, with a lake at its centre, is the city's green lung and a popular spot for morning runs. The Dazaifu Tenmangu shrine, easily reached from central Fukuoka, is one of the most atmospheric in Kyushu.
