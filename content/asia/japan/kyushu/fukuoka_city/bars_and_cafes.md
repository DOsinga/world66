---
title: Bars and Cafes
type: section
---

Tenjin is the main nightlife district, with bar-lined alleys concentrated around the Watanabedori and Nishitetsu Fukuoka Station areas. The drinking culture leans toward izakayas — Japanese pub-restaurants where ordering food alongside drinks is the standard approach. The Nakasu island between the Naka and Hakata rivers is Fukuoka's entertainment quarter: denser, more expensive, and more varied, mixing hostess bars, live music venues, and ordinary restaurants on the same narrow streets.

The yatai stalls along the Naka River are technically eating establishments but function socially as bars: you sit shoulder to shoulder with strangers, order ramen and sake or beer, and the conversation tends to flow. They are open from around 6pm and close by midnight or 1am. The Momochi seafront has a different, more relaxed character: the Hakodate Beer hall at the Kanemori warehouses equivalent is the Nakamichi beach area in summer, where outdoor bars open from June to August along the artificial beach east of the Fukuoka Tower.
