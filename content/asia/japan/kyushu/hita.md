Hita sits in the river basin of western Oita Prefecture, close to the border with Fukuoka. It is often sold as a "little Kyoto", which is a bit generous, but the old merchant quarter of [Mameda-machi](/asia/japan/kyushu/hita/mameda_machi) does have real texture: white-walled storehouses, narrow lanes, sake shops, and the memory of a town once ruled directly by the Tokugawa shogunate.

The reason to give Hita a full day is that the town is not only one preserved street. [Kangien Academy](/asia/japan/kyushu/hita/kangien_academy) tells the story of an unusually open Edo-period school, while the surrounding mountains hold [Onta Pottery Village](/asia/japan/kyushu/hita/onta_pottery_village), where clay is still crushed by water-powered wooden hammers and fired in climbing kilns.

Hita is also a practical food-and-drink stop. The Mikuma River is known for ayu, the forests supplied the cedar for geta clogs, and [Sapporo Beer Kyushu Hita Brewery](/asia/japan/kyushu/hita/sapporo_beer_kyushu_hita_brewery) makes good use of the local water. It works best as a slow day between Fukuoka, Beppu, and the Aso area rather than as a rushed tick on a rail itinerary.
