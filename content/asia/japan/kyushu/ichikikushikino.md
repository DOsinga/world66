Ichikikushikino sits on the west coast of Kagoshima Prefecture, close enough to [Kagoshima](/asia/japan/kyushu/kagoshima) for a day trip but far enough away to feel like a working port town rather than a city suburb. Its identity is unusually clear: tuna boats, shochu, an old gold mine, and a small but important episode in the birth of modern Japan.

The best reason to come is the combination of [Satsuma Kinzangura](/asia/japan/kyushu/ichikikushikino/satsuma_kinzangura) and the [Satsuma Students Museum](/asia/japan/kyushu/ichikikushikino/satsuma_students_museum). One takes you into the former Kushikino gold mine, now used to age shochu in cool tunnels. The other tells the story of the young Satsuma men who secretly left from Hashima in 1865 to study in Britain, then returned with ideas and skills that mattered in the Meiji Restoration.

The coast gives the town a softer edge. [Terushima Shrine](/asia/japan/kyushu/ichikikushikino/terushima_shrine) sits on a small rocky island reached by a red bridge, while viewpoints and parks look west over the East China Sea. Come with a car if you can; the railway is useful for reaching town, but the sights are spread along the shore and hills.
