Kitakyushu sits at the northern tip of Kyushu, looking across the narrow Kanmon Strait to Honshu. It's a city built on steel and coal — one of Japan's great industrial heartlands — and it carries that history visibly in its architecture, its port districts, and its gritty, unpretentious character.

The city was created in 1963 through the merger of five distinct towns: Moji, Kokura, Wakamatsu, Yahata, and Tobata. Two million people live here, yet it feels nothing like Tokyo or Osaka. Each of the old towns has kept its own identity. [Kokura](/asia/japan/kyushu/kitakyushu/kokura_district) is the commercial heart, with covered shopping arcades and [Kokura Castle](/asia/japan/kyushu/kitakyushu/kokura_castle). [Moji](/asia/japan/kyushu/kitakyushu/mojiko_retro), near the strait crossing, has preserved its Meiji-era port architecture and reinvented itself as a heritage destination. [Yahata](/asia/japan/kyushu/kitakyushu/space_world_area) still wears its industrial past on its sleeves.

The [Matsumoto Seicho Memorial Museum](/asia/japan/kyushu/kitakyushu/matsumoto_seicho_museum) next to Kokura Castle honours the city's most famous crime writer. The [Wisteria Tunnel](/asia/japan/kyushu/kitakyushu/wisteria_tunnel) at Kawachi Fuji Garden draws visitors from across Japan in late April. The [TOTO Museum](/asia/japan/kyushu/kitakyushu/toto_museum) turns the history of Japan's most famous toilet company into a surprisingly engaging exhibit. The [Kitakyushu Municipal Museum of Art](/asia/japan/kyushu/kitakyushu/kitakyushu_municipal_museum), designed by Arata Isozaki in 1974, is worth visiting as much for its architecture as its collection. The [Kanmon Pedestrian Tunnel](/asia/japan/kyushu/kitakyushu/kanmon_pedestrian_tunnel) allows you to walk between Kyushu and Honshu in under 20 minutes. [Tanga Market](/asia/japan/kyushu/kitakyushu/kokura_tanga_market) in central Kokura is the best place to eat as the locals do.

Most visitors base themselves in Kokura and make day trips from there. It's an easy city to navigate and rewards those willing to explore beyond the obvious.
