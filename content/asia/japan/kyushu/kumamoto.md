The third-largest city in Kyushu after Fukuoka and Kitakyushu, Kumamoto sits on the central plains of the island with the ancient volcanic plateau of **Aso-san** rising to the east. The city suffered devastating earthquakes in 2016, but has rebuilt with impressive determination — and the ongoing restoration of its great castle has become a symbol of that resilience.

Kumamoto's most famous landmark is [Kumamoto-jō](/asia/japan/kyushu/kumamoto/kumamoto_castle), one of Japan's three great castles. Destroyed by fire during the Satsuma Rebellion of 1877, the current keep is a 1960 reconstruction, but its predominantly black exterior remains striking and the hilltop setting commands the city. Within the same enclosure sit the [Kato Shrine](/asia/japan/kyushu/kumamoto/kato_shrine), the modernist [Prefectural Museum of Art](/asia/japan/kyushu/kumamoto/kumamoto_museum_of_art), and the reconstructed Edo-period street of [Sakuranobaba Josaien](/asia/japan/kyushu/kumamoto/sakuranobaba_josaien). The castle district is pleasant to walk, even mid-restoration.

The second essential stop is [Suizenji-jōju-en](/asia/japan/kyushu/kumamoto/suizenji_garden), a 17th-century strolling garden in the southeastern suburbs. Its miniature landscape recreates the 53 stages of the old Tōkaidō highway, with a small replica of Mount Fuji at its centre. The vermilion [Izumi Shrine](/asia/japan/kyushu/kumamoto/izumi_shrine) stands at the head of the pond. The garden ranks among Japan's finest. North of the centre, the hillside temple of [Honmyō-ji](/asia/japan/kyushu/kumamoto/honmyo_ji) holds the tomb of castle-builder Katō Kiyomasa, and the small wooden [Lafcadio Hearn Residence](/asia/japan/kyushu/kumamoto/lafcadio_hearn_old_residence) and elegant [Kyū-Hosokawa Gyobutei](/asia/japan/kyushu/kumamoto/kyu_hosokawa_gyobutei) samurai residence are both worth a half hour each.

Devotees of Miyamoto Musashi can follow his trail to [Musashizuka Park](/asia/japan/kyushu/kumamoto/musashizuka_park), where the swordsman is buried, and on to the cave at [Reigandō](/asia/japan/kyushu/kumamoto/reigando) where he wrote The Book of Five Rings. Central shopping and dining cluster along the long [Shimotōri arcade](/asia/japan/kyushu/kumamoto/shimotori_arcade) and the alleys off it.

Kumamoto's most famous culinary speciality is *basashi* — raw horse meat served thin-sliced with ginger and garlic soy sauce. Most restaurants in the centre offer it, and it is worth trying at least once. Other Kumamoto staples are [karashi renkon](/asia/japan/kyushu/kumamoto/karashi_renkon) (deep-fried mustard-stuffed lotus root) and [Kumamoto ramen](/asia/japan/kyushu/kumamoto/kumamoto_ramen).
