Miyazaki is Kyushu at its sunniest: palms, broad streets, surf beaches, and a coastline where shrine mythology sits right beside resort Japan. It is less dense and less famous than Fukuoka or Nagasaki, but it has a relaxed southern pace and enough strong sights for a full day.

The classic first stop is [Aoshima Shrine](/asia/japan/kyushu/miyazaki/aoshima_shrine), set on a small island ringed by the folded rock formations nicknamed the Devil's Washboard. Inland, [Miyazaki-jingu](/asia/japan/kyushu/miyazaki/miyazaki_jingu) gives the city its oldest mythic identity, with a shrine dedicated to Emperor Jimmu, Japan's legendary first emperor.

Miyazaki is also good at easy pleasures. [Heiwadai Park](/asia/japan/kyushu/miyazaki/heiwadai_park) has its stone peace tower and haniwa figures, while the city's beaches pull surfers when the swell is right. Food helps too: chicken nanban, Miyazaki beef, mango desserts, and shochu make the city more rewarding after the sightseeing is done.
