Moji is where Kyushu begins — or ends, depending on which way you're crossing. The Kanmon Strait is narrow here, just a few hundred metres between Kyushu and Honshu, bridged above and tunnelled below.

The main draw is [Mojiko Retro](/asia/japan/kyushu/moji/mojiko_retro), the old port district preserved with its Meiji and Taisho-era brick buildings largely intact. It's genuinely atmospheric: outdoor cafes around the harbour, the chateau-like [Mojiko Station](/asia/japan/kyushu/moji/mojiko_station), the [Blue Wing Moji](/asia/japan/kyushu/moji/blue_wing_moji) drawbridge that rises six times a day, and the [old Mitsui Club](/asia/japan/kyushu/moji/old_customs_house) marking where Einstein stopped during his 1922 visit to Japan. The architecture here is a reminder that Moji was once a significant port, handling coal exports that fuelled Japan's industrial rise.

From Mojiko, a short ferry crosses to Shimonoseki on the Honshu side — or, more memorably, the [Kanmon Pedestrian Tunnel](/asia/japan/kyushu/moji/kanmon_pedestrian_tunnel) lets you walk under the strait. Go for the fugu — blowfish — which Shimonoseki considers its speciality and serves in every form imaginable.
