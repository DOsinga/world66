Miyako Island has some of the most beautiful beaches in Japan — white coral sand, turquoise water so clear you can see the bottom from the bridges that connect the island to its smaller neighbours. The island sits about 300 kilometres southwest of Okinawa's main island, far enough south that the water stays warm well into autumn.

The main beaches are [Yonaha Maehama](/asia/japan/okinawa/miyakoisland/yonaha_maehama_beach) on the southwest coast (a long, wide arc of sand rated Japan's best beach by TripAdvisor in 2019) and [Yoshino Beach](/asia/japan/okinawa/miyakoisland/yoshino_beach) on the east (better for snorkelling over the shallow reef, with sea turtles reliably present). [Sunayama Beach](/asia/japan/okinawa/miyakoisland/sunayama_beach) on the northwest has a dramatic natural stone arch and a sand-dune approach. The [Irabu Ohashi bridge](/asia/japan/okinawa/miyakoisland/irabu_ohashi_bridge) — 3.5 kilometres long and toll-free — connects Miyako to Irabu Island, where Toguchi-no-Hama beach is quieter and equally beautiful. [Higashi Hennazaki Cape](/asia/japan/okinawa/miyakoisland/higashi_hennazaki) at the island's southeastern tip is a National Place of Scenic Beauty — a narrow finger of reef-fringed land with ocean on both sides and a lighthouse at the end.

Miyako is flat — unusual for a Japanese island — so cycling is a good way to get around. The island's culture retains traces of the old Ryukyu Kingdom, visible in the traditional stone walls and the annual [Paantu festival](/asia/japan/okinawa/miyakoisland/paantu_festival), where mud-covered sacred figures walk through the village of Shimajiri touching people and doorways to drive away evil spirits — a ritual of genuine strangeness and age.
