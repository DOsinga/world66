Naha is Okinawa's capital and by far its largest city, home to about 320,000 people. It is where most visitors arrive and where the island's urban life is concentrated. [Kokusai Street](/asia/japan/ryukyuislands/okinawaisland/naha/kokusai_street) (International Street) is the main drag — a 1.6-kilometre stretch of souvenir shops, restaurants, and izakayas that fills up every evening. Duck into the covered [Makishi Public Market](/asia/japan/ryukyuislands/okinawaisland/naha/makishi_public_market) just off Kokusai for fresh seafood, tropical fruit, and Okinawan specialties like pig's face and sea grapes.

The reconstructed [Shuri Castle](/asia/japan/ryukyuislands/okinawaisland/naha/shuri_castle) sits on a hill overlooking the city, a reminder of Naha's centuries as the capital of the Ryukyu Kingdom. The [Tsuboya pottery district](/asia/japan/ryukyuislands/okinawaisland/naha/tsuboya_pottery_district), a short walk from Kokusai, is quieter and worth exploring for its traditional workshops and small galleries. [Fukushuen](/asia/japan/ryukyuislands/okinawaisland/naha/fukushuen), a classical Chinese garden near the old Kumemura district, reflects the deep historical ties between Okinawa and China.

Naha is the only part of Okinawa with decent public transport — the Yui Rail monorail connects the airport to the city centre and up to Shuri. Beyond that, you will need a car or bus.
