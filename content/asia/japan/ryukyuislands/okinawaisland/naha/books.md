**The Battle of Okinawa: The Last Epic Struggle of World War II** by Gerald Astor. A detailed account of the 82-day battle that destroyed much of what is now Naha and killed roughly a quarter of the island's civilian population. Essential context for understanding why so much of what you see in the city is reconstructed.

**Typhoon of Steel: The Battle for Okinawa** by James and William Belote. A thoroughly researched narrative of the battle from both American and Japanese perspectives, with good detail on what the island looked like before and after the fighting.

**The Island of Sea Women** by Lisa See. A novel about haenyeo (free-diving women) set on Jeju Island in Korea, but recommended because it engages seriously with the same post-war Pacific world and the disruptions of occupation and modernization that shaped Okinawa in the same period. Not about Okinawa specifically, but illuminating.
