Okinawan food is distinct from Japanese mainland cuisine, shaped by centuries of trade with China, three decades of American occupation, and a subtropical climate that produces different ingredients. Pork is central — used in every form — along with bitter melon (goya), sea grapes (umi-budou), and Okinawan soba, which is made with wheat noodles rather than buckwheat.
