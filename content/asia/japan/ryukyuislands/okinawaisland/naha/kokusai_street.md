  Kokusai Street — "International Street" — earned its name in the post-war period
  when it emerged from the rubble of 1945 as a chaotic strip of bars, cinemas, and
  shops catering to US military personnel. The nickname stuck long after the
  character changed, and what had been called "the Miracle Mile" for its improbable
  post-war revival is now Naha's main tourist drag.

Kokusai Street is a 1.6-kilometre pedestrianised-ish strip running through the heart of Naha, lined with souvenir shops, restaurants, izakayas, and the occasional traditional craft store. It is touristy and crowded in the evenings but not without charm — the density of activity, the smell of cooking, the Okinawan music drifting out of doorways, gives the street a genuine vitality.

The best way to use Kokusai is as a spine for exploring: turn off into the covered market arcades that run parallel (Heiwa-dori is the most atmospheric), duck into the Makishi Public Market at the eastern end for fresh produce and Okinawan specialties, and look for shops selling genuine awamori (the local spirit distilled from indica rice) among the rows of generic snacks. Street food vendors set up in the evenings, and the izakayas fill early with a mix of tourists and locals who work nearby.
