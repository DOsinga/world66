Tamaudun is the royal mausoleum of the Second Sho dynasty, tucked just west of Shuri Castle on the ridge above Naha. Built in the early sixteenth century, it looks more like a small stone palace than a tomb: three burial chambers, thick limestone walls, and a courtyard that keeps the noise of the city at a distance. It is one of the clearest surviving places to understand the Ryukyu Kingdom as something distinct from mainland Japan.

The visit is short but worthwhile, especially if you are already exploring Shuri. The stonework, guardian figures, and quiet enclosure give it a solemnity that balances the reconstructed castle nearby. Come early or late in the day, when tour groups thin out and the pale walls catch softer light.
