Nakagusuku Castle ruins occupy a ridge in central Okinawa with views stretching to both coasts on a clear day. One of six UNESCO World Heritage castle ruins on the island, Nakagusuku is among the best-preserved, with walls reaching ten metres in places and a layout that reveals the sophistication of 15th-century Ryukyuan fortification. Six baileys step up the ridge in a sequence designed to slow any attacker who breached the outer walls.

The dry-stone masonry technique used here — large coral limestone blocks shaped and stacked without mortar — is found throughout Okinawa's gusuku (castle) sites and represents a distinct building tradition that developed independently from Japanese castle architecture. Walking the circuit of the walls takes about 45 minutes; the inner enclosure has an atmospheric, overgrown quality. The site is less visited than Shuri Castle in Naha, which makes it a calmer and more atmospheric alternative for anyone interested in the Ryukyu Kingdom's history.
