Nakijin Castle ruins sit at the northern end of Okinawa Island on the Nakijin Cape, surrounded by subtropical forest and overlooking Motobu Bay. The site is the largest castle ruin in the Okinawa UNESCO cluster by area, and the walls — some sections still standing three to four metres tall — wind through dense vegetation in a way that feels genuinely remote despite being accessible by road.

The castle is best known for its cherry blossoms, which bloom in January and February, weeks earlier than anywhere else in Japan. The combination of pink blossoms against grey stone walls and blue sea below makes this one of the most photographed spots in Okinawa during the winter months. But outside cherry blossom season the crowds are thin, and the site rewards slow exploration. The museum at the entrance has good material on the Gusuku cultural landscape and the connection between Nakijin and the trading networks of medieval East Asia. Allow an hour for a proper visit.
