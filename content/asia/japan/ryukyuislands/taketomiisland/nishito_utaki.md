He was born on Taketomi and is buried here. After his death, the site of his home was converted into a sacred grove — an utaki — dedicated to his spirit, and he was formally enshrined as a guardian deity of the island. The designation of sacred sites in Okinawa traditionally follows this pattern: remarkable individuals become local deities, their dwelling places become holy ground.

The Nishito Utaki is a sacred Ryukyuan grove dedicated to the memory of Nishito, a 16th-century administrator and cultural hero of the Yaeyama Islands who was born on Taketomi. Like all utaki — the traditional sacred sites of the Ryukyu religion — it consists of a shaded clearing in the forest rather than a built shrine, accessed by a simple stone path. The site is designated a Prefectural Cultural Property.

The utaki is quiet and easy to miss if you are cycling quickly through the village. It sits within the settlement itself, clearly signposted. Visitors are expected to be respectful; the site is still actively used by local families for prayer. The broader island has around thirty utaki in total, distributed around the coast and interior, most of them unvisited by tourists. The Taketomi Folk Museum nearby (Taketomi Mingei-kan) provides background on the island's religious traditions and the minsa weaving craft that the island is also known for.
