  The sanshin, a three-stringed instrument related to the Chinese sanxian, is the soul of Okinawan music. The guides who drive the buffalo carts through Taketomi village learned to play in a tradition that stretches back centuries and survived the near-total destruction of the island's population during World War II. The cart rides are one of the few mass-tourism experiences in Japan that still carry a genuine cultural weight rather than a theme-park version of it.
---

The water buffalo cart tour through Taketomi village is the island's most photographed experience: a large wooden cart pulled by a single patient buffalo through the sandy lanes, while the guide sits at the front playing the sanshin (a three-stringed Okinawan instrument resembling a banjo) and singing traditional Yaeyama folk songs. The pace is glacial, the route circles the village centre, and the whole thing takes about twenty minutes. It sounds trivial. It is actually charming.

The buffalo are calm, the music is genuine, and the low-angle view of the village from the cart — coral walls at eye level, bougainvillea overhead, the distant sound of waves — is a peaceful way to experience a place that is easy to rush through. Multiple operators run the carts; the queues are longest in late morning when the ferry crowds arrive. Going early or waiting until mid-afternoon means a shorter wait. Tickets are bought at stands near the village centre ferry drop-off point.
