Masaoka Shiki is Matsuyama's most celebrated native son — a poet who practically reinvented haiku in the late 19th century and died at 34 from tuberculosis, leaving behind an enormous body of work. The memorial museum near his birthplace displays manuscripts, personal effects, and detailed explanations of his life and influence.

Shiki's connection to Matsuyama is everywhere in the city — on lamp posts, in park plaques, in the names of coffee shops. This museum is the place to understand why he matters. Even if haiku is not your subject, the story of his final productive years while seriously ill is genuinely moving.
