Suo-Oshima is a long island town in eastern Yamaguchi Prefecture, linked to the mainland by the Oshima Bridge and looking south across the Seto Inland Sea. It is rural, spread out, and best with a car, but it has a clear identity: mandarin orchards, small fishing ports, beaches, and a surprisingly strong historic connection with Hawaii.

The island's first hook is the sea. [Mt. Dake Observation Deck](/asia/japan/shikoku/suo_oshima/mt_dake_observation_deck) gives the wide view, with the Seto Inland Sea broken into islands below, while [Katazoegahama Seaside Park](/asia/japan/shikoku/suo_oshima/katazoegahama_seaside_park) is the easiest place to turn a summer day into swimming, camping, or a lazy beach picnic.

Suo-Oshima's quieter, more distinctive story is told at the [Museum of Japanese Emigration to Hawaii](/asia/japan/shikoku/suo_oshima/museum_of_japanese_emigration_to_hawaii). Many islanders left for Hawaii in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, and the museum makes that Pacific connection feel local rather than abstract. Come for a spare day from Yanai, Iwakuni, or Hiroshima if you want a slower Seto Inland Sea outing rather than another big-name temple stop.
