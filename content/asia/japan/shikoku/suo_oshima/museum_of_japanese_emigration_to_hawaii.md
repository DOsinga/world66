The Museum of Japanese Emigration to Hawaii is Suo-Oshima's most distinctive cultural stop. It explains the island's late-19th and early-20th-century migration to Hawaii through family records, photographs, objects, and the recreated home of a returned emigrant.

The museum gives useful depth to the island's "Hawaii of Japan" branding. Without it, the palm trees and Hawaiian motifs can feel like a tourism slogan; with it, they point to a real story of rural hardship, Pacific movement, and families split between two island worlds.
