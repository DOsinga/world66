Iwaki is Fukushima's big coastal city, spread wide between fishing ports, hot springs, suburbs, and low hills. It is not a postcard town, but it has enough specific attractions to reward a day or two on the Pacific side of Tohoku.

The strongest route links [Aquamarine Fukushima](/asia/japan/honshu/iwaki/aquamarine_fukushima) at Onahama with the hot-spring and entertainment world of [Spa Resort Hawaiians](/asia/japan/honshu/iwaki/spa_resort_hawaiians). For older history, [Shiramizu Amida-do](/asia/japan/honshu/iwaki/shiramizu_amida_do) is the prize: a Heian-period temple hall and garden, and a National Treasure. [Iwaki City Coal & Fossil Museum](/asia/japan/honshu/iwaki/iwaki_city_coal_and_fossil_museum) explains why a Hawaiian resort ended up in a former coal district.

Iwaki works best if you lean into the mix. It is part seaside aquarium town, part onsen town, part post-industrial story, and that combination is more interesting than a simple stop on the Joban Line.
