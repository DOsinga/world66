A teenager flees Tokyo for Takamatsu while, in parallel, an old man in Tokyo can speak to cats and make fish fall from the sky. Murakami's Tokyo is a city of lonely apartments, jazz bars, and underground passageways — entirely recognisable and utterly strange. The novel captures something true about urban solitude in a city of 37 million people. Any of Murakami's novels set in Tokyo work equally well; this one has the most vivid sense of the city as dreamscape.
