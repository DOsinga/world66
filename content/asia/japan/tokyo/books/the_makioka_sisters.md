Tanizaki's great novel follows four sisters from an Osaka merchant family in the late 1930s as Japan slides toward war. Much of it is set in Tokyo, where the family navigates marriage negotiations, social obligations, and the slow dissolution of a way of life. It is an exquisitely observed portrait of Japanese social ritual, written partly as a deliberate act of cultural preservation while the militarist government was trying to erase the very culture Tanizaki was describing.
