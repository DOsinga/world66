The izakaya is the essential Tokyo evening: a Japanese pub-restaurant where food and drink are ordered continuously through the night, the dishes small and designed for sharing. At its best, an izakaya offers grilled chicken (yakitori), edamame, house-made tofu, grilled fish, pickles, cold noodles, and seasonal specials, all consumed with sake, beer, or shochu while the restaurant fills with after-work noise.

The best izakayas in Tokyo are not famous or easy to find — they are neighbourhood places with hand-written menus and regulars who have been coming for decades. The Shimbashi area has a high concentration of good ones. Look for red lanterns hanging outside, a noisy interior visible through frosted glass, and a handwritten menu board in Japanese. Pointing at neighbouring tables' dishes is an acceptable ordering method.
