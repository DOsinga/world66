Murakami's most widely read novel is set in 1960s Tokyo during the student revolution years — a coming-of-age story about loss, memory, and the city's particular quality of loneliness. It is not a guide to Tokyo but it captures something true about the city's emotional texture: the isolation possible in a crowd of millions, the way Tokyo neighbourhoods exist as emotional as much as geographical territories. If you want to understand why so many people feel both overwhelmed and deeply at home in Tokyo, this is the place to start.
