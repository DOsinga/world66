Snow Country (1956) is the novel that won Kawabata the Nobel Prize in 1968, the first Japanese writer to receive it. Set in a mountain hot-spring town on the Sea of Japan coast, it follows a wealthy Tokyo aesthete and his doomed love for a geisha. It is a short, elliptical, extraordinarily beautiful book about transience, longing, and the aesthetics of the ephemeral — concepts central to Japanese culture that Tokyo, for all its modernity, still embodies. Read it before you visit any ryokan or attend any traditional performance.
