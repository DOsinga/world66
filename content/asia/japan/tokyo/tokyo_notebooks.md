Donald Richie spent more than sixty years in Tokyo and became the foremost Western interpreter of Japanese film, aesthetics, and daily life. His journals and essays about the city — collected in several books including the Reader — are the most acute and specific account of Tokyo's transformation from postwar ruins to global metropolis. He writes about everything: the aesthetics of tempura, the sociology of the public bath, the meaning of the tearoom. Nothing about Tokyo is too small to deserve attention.
