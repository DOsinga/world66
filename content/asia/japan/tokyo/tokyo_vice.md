Jake Adelstein spent twelve years as a crime reporter on Japan's largest daily newspaper, the Yomiuri Shimbun — the only Westerner ever to do so — and this is his account of that career. Tokyo Vice is not a flattering portrait of the city: it deals with the yakuza, the sex industry, the mechanics of organised crime, and the gap between Japan's self-image of harmony and the reality underneath. It is compulsively readable and the Tokyo it describes — the underground of Kabukicho in Shinjuku, the police departments, the newsroom — is as specific as a city guide.
