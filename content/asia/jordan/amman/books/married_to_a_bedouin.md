A New Zealand nurse met a Bedouin jewellery seller at Petra in 1978, married him three weeks later, and spent the next fifteen years living in a cave in the rose-red city — before the Jordanian government relocated the Bedouin community to a modern settlement at Wadi Musa. Van Geldermalsen's memoir is funny, honest, and deeply affectionate toward the Bdoul tribe, and it captures the texture of traditional Bedouin life in Jordan with the precision of an insider-outsider.

It reads as a companion piece to any visit to Petra, and it gives context for the Jordanian Bedouin culture visible throughout the country — including in Amman, where the tribal values of hospitality, reputation, and family loyalty still shape urban life among the older families.
