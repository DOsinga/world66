Lawrence's account of the Arab Revolt of 1916–1918 — in which he served as a British officer advising the Hashemite forces under Emir Faisal — covers territory that is now Jordan in close detail. Aqaba, Wadi Rum, Azraq, and the Hejaz Railway that Lawrence spent years dynamiting all feature extensively. The book is part memoir, part history, part self-mythology, and deeply unreliable as a factual account, but as a piece of writing about the landscape and people of the region it remains unmatched.

Understanding Lawrence, and the Arab Revolt, is essential background for making sense of how Jordan came to exist as a state at all — the Hashemite kingdom that rules today is a direct product of the post-war settlement that Lawrence both shaped and bitterly opposed.
