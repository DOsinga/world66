Edward Said's 1979 argument for Palestinian rights and statehood, written from the perspective of a Palestinian intellectual who grew up partly in Jerusalem and Cairo before becoming a Columbia professor. It is the foundational text for understanding the Palestinian experience of displacement that has defined Amman more than almost any other factor — the city is majority Palestinian in origin, and the political and cultural tensions Said describes are present in everyday Amman life.

Said is a challenging read but precise and serious. This book, more than any other, explains why Amman looks and feels the way it does.
