Downtown Amman — known as Al Balad — is the oldest and busiest part of the city: the tangled valley streets around the Roman Theatre and the Grand Husseini Mosque, packed with souks, coffee houses, and the remnants of the city's pre-oil-boom character. The contrast with west Amman's quiet villa suburbs is stark. Here the streets are full, the traffic is aggressive, and the smells are of cardamom coffee, shawarma, and diesel.

The main draws are concentrated in a small area: the Roman Theatre, the Jordan Museum nearby, the gold souk and spice market off King Faisal Street, and the covered souks around Husseini Mosque. Wandering without a specific plan is the best approach — the streets are dense enough to reward slow exploration, and the city's history is visible in the layering of Roman columns, Ottoman stonework, and 1950s apartment blocks. This is the Amman that predates tourism.
