Al-Maghtas — 'the Baptism' in Arabic — is the Jordanian-side site where scholars and the Vatican now believe the baptism of Jesus by John the Baptist took place. The UNESCO World Heritage site (designated 2015) on the east bank of the Jordan River is a 40-minute drive north of the Dead Sea resort strip, and it is one of the most historically and spiritually significant sites in the Middle East.

The excavated archaeological complex includes the remains of **John the Baptist's cave** and associated churches dating to the Byzantine era, ancient baptismal pools, and the slow, muddy bend of the Jordan River itself where pilgrims still wade in for baptism. A boardwalk leads from the visitor centre through the excavated remains, and a guide (included in the entry fee) explains the layers of history — Edomite, Byzantine, Crusader, and Ottoman.

The site is managed and visited more professionally than many Jordanian historical sites. The church ruins and pools are well-preserved given their age, and the explanatory materials are thoughtful. The Jordan River here is narrow, brown, and barely moving — a physical letdown for anyone expecting a grand biblical waterway, but oddly moving given what the location represents. On the opposite bank, Israeli flags are visible; a few hundred metres and nearly three millennia separate them.

Visas for Jordan are now available on arrival at the Dead Sea resort area's border crossings. The site is genuinely worthwhile even for non-religious visitors interested in archaeology and history.
