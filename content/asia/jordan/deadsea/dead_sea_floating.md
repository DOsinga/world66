Floating in the Dead Sea is not a metaphor. You cannot sink. The water's density — roughly 1.24 kg/litre compared to ocean water's 1.025 kg/litre — is high enough that a human body simply sits on the surface. Lean back, lift your feet off the bottom, and you are instantly, completely supported. No effort required.

The experience is simultaneously disorienting and peaceful. The water is warm (around 32°C in summer), the shoreline is ringed by salt-streaked cliffs, and the mountains of the West Bank are visible across the water. The ritual involves coating yourself in the dark mineral mud from the shoreline, letting it dry to a crust in the sun, then rinsing off in the sea. The mud contains magnesium, potassium, and bromide compounds that have measurable therapeutic effects on certain skin conditions.

**Practical realities**: The water is extremely unpleasant in the eyes, nose, or mouth — it is not salty in the way that ocean water is salty, but caustic. Do not get it in your face. Any cut or scratch will sting intensely. Shaving before a visit is not recommended.

Beach access is available at the resort hotels, most of which charge a day rate for non-guests. The **Amman Beach public beach** is a government-operated alternative with lower entry fees. The beach area provides freshwater showers essential for rinsing before leaving — Dead Sea water left on the skin will crystallise uncomfortably and is corrosive to clothing.

The best time to go is early morning or late afternoon, when the light is extraordinary and the heat manageable. In midsummer the shore temperature regularly exceeds 40°C.
