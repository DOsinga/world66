Machaerus (also called Qala'at Mukawir) is a ruined hilltop fortress about 25 km south of the Dead Sea resort strip, at 700 metres above sea level with the Dead Sea visible far below. Built originally by the Hasmonean dynasty and rebuilt by Herod the Great, it served as a royal palace and military stronghold on the eastern frontier of the kingdom.

The site is most famous as the location of John the Baptist's imprisonment and execution, as recorded in the Gospels and confirmed by the historian Josephus, who describes the fortress in some detail. Archaeological work here has been largely Italian and has accelerated in recent years, revealing the extent of the palace complex including what may be the very hall where the fateful birthday banquet took place.

The ruins themselves are substantial but partially reconstructed: the walls of the upper citadel, cisterns, towers, and the outline of the palace rooms are visible. The hilltop position is dramatic in all directions, and the long views down to the Dead Sea and across to the West Bank give the site a geological grandeur that matches its historical weight. A small village of Mukawir lies at the base of the hill.

Machaerus is accessible by car (about 45 minutes from the Dead Sea highway) and is rarely crowded — a striking contrast to Petra and Jerash. This is a place for travellers who want to go slightly off the tourist circuit and find something equally compelling.
