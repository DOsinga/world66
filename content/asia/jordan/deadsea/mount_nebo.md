Mount Nebo rises above the Dead Sea from the Jordanian plateau, only 10 km west of Madaba and easily combined with a visit to the Dead Sea or the Baptism Site. The summit at 817 metres gives one of the most panoramic views in the Middle East: on clear days, the Dead Sea, the Jordan Valley, the hills of Jerusalem, and Bethlehem are all visible simultaneously.

The site has been venerated as Moses's deathplace since at least the 4th century CE. Byzantine Christians built a church and monastery here; the church was expanded several times and eventually abandoned in the 14th century. Today, the **Memorial Church of Moses** on the summit has been restored by the Franciscan Custody of the Holy Land and contains an extraordinary collection of **Byzantine floor mosaics** — some of the finest in Jordan, showing hunting scenes, pastoral landscapes, and exotic animals in vivid detail dating to the 5th–8th centuries CE.

A serpentine bronze sculpture near the church commemorates the bronze serpent that Moses raised in the wilderness (Numbers 21:9) — a symbol later linked to John 3:14 and to the international symbol of medicine. The memorial church interior, with its mosaic floors and sweeping valley views through the open windows, is one of the most serene spots in Jordan. Entry includes the museum.

Mount Nebo is 40 minutes from the Dead Sea resort strip by car, and worth combining with nearby Madaba (8 km) in a single day trip.
