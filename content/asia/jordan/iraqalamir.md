Iraq al-Amir is a small village in the Wadi Seer valley, about 30 kilometres west of [Amman](/asia/jordan/amman). It makes an easy half-day trip from the capital and offers one of the few Hellenistic sites in [Jordan](/asia/jordan) — a reminder that this part of the world was once ruled by the successors of Alexander the Great.

The centrepiece is [Qasr al-Abd](/asia/jordan/iraqalamir/qasr_al_abd) (Castle of the Slave), a partially reconstructed 2nd-century BC palace built by Hyrcanus of the powerful Tobiad family. The building is remarkable for its carved stone lions and eagles, and for the sheer ambition of its construction — some of the largest individual stone blocks in any ancient structure in the Middle East, fitted together without mortar.

The surrounding valley is green and pleasant, a sharp contrast to the arid plateau above. The [caves in the cliffs](/asia/jordan/iraqalamir/caves_of_iraq_al_amir) bear what may be the oldest inscriptions in Jordan — the name 'Tobiah' in Aramaic, cut into the stone millennia ago. A few simple restaurants near the site serve tea and lunch. The drive from Amman winds down through increasingly rural scenery and is part of the appeal.
