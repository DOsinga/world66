Located on the campus of Yarmouk University, the Museum of Jordanian Heritage is the best archaeological museum in northern Jordan and an underrated stop on any route through the region. Its collections span Jordan's entire human story, from Stone Age flint tools and Bronze Age pottery through Nabataean, Roman, Byzantine, and Islamic artefacts — all well-labelled and logically laid out.

The highlight for many visitors is the collection from the Decapolis cities: coins, mosaics, carved columns, and small bronzes that bring the Greco-Roman cities of Umm Qais, Gerasa, and Capitolias to life in a way that walking the ruins alone cannot. Admission is free for university students and very cheap for everyone else. Opening hours can be erratic — check locally before making a special trip.
