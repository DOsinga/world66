story: "Salt's streets are narrow enough that the municipality collects rubbish by donkey rather than truck — a practical solution that has been in use for decades and gives the old town a sound and smell unlike any other heritage site in Jordan. The donkeys navigate the lanes that no vehicle can enter, making their rounds through the limestone canyons of the UNESCO World Heritage zone. In 2021, UNESCO inscribed Salt as 'the Place of Tolerance and Urban Hospitality' — the first Jordanian city to receive World Heritage status for its social values rather than its archaeological remains."
---

Salt's designation as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2021 recognised something that visitors instinctively feel: the old town is not a museum but a living neighbourhood, and the quality of daily life here is woven into the architecture. More than 650 Ottoman-era buildings survive in the old city, most of them constructed between 1860 and 1920 from the distinctive yellow limestone quarried in the surrounding hills.

The style is a hybrid of Ottoman structural conventions and Levantine decorative taste: thick walls that stay cool in summer, arched windows that flood interiors with light, and ground-floor arcades designed to accommodate commerce. Many buildings have been in the same family for five or six generations. Walking through the town, you encounter laundry drying above doorways that have not changed since the nineteenth century, and small children playing on stairways that climb through the same limestone rock their great-grandparents carved.
