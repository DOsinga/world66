Umm Qais stands on a basalt-covered ridge in the far north of Jordan, looking out over one of the most dramatic panoramas in the Middle East: the Sea of Galilee (Lake Tiberias) glittering in the distance, the deep slash of the Yarmouk river gorge below, and the Golan Heights rising on the far side of the Syrian border. It is a view that makes the detour here entirely worthwhile, even before you have set eyes on the ruins.

The ancient city was known as **Gadara**, one of the ten cities of the Decapolis — the league of Hellenistic and later Roman towns spread across what is now Jordan, Syria, and Israel. Gadara was prosperous and cultured: it produced philosophers, poets, and rhetoricians. What remains today is remarkable. The ruins are built almost entirely in the local **black basalt**, which gives the site a brooding, distinctive quality absent from the pale limestone ruins of [Jerash](/asia/jordan/jerash) or [Amman](/asia/jordan/amman). The [West Theatre](/asia/jordan/umm_qais/west_theatre), carved into the hillside and still largely intact, could seat 3,000 people; a [colonnaded street](/asia/jordan/umm_qais/colonnaded_street) and forum stretch through the centre of the site; and a grand mausoleum with a white marble sarcophagus stands at the northern end.

What makes Umm Qais peculiar among Jordanian sites is that an **Ottoman village** was built directly on top of the ruins in the 18th and 19th centuries and occupied until the 1980s. The basalt houses — some still with their carved Roman door frames — were only cleared after the residents were relocated, and archaeologists have been excavating ever since. The **Beit Russan** complex, the former residence of the Ottoman village mukhtar, has been converted into the site's guesthouse and a restaurant with a terrace that commands that magnificent view. Lunch here — mezze dishes, grilled meats, local wine — with the Sea of Galilee spread below is hard to beat anywhere in Jordan.

Umm Qais is about 110 kilometres north of [Amman](/asia/jordan/amman) and 30 kilometres northwest of [Irbid](/asia/jordan/irbid), and is most easily visited as a day trip from either city. The [viewpoint](/asia/jordan/umm_qais/viewpoint) above the gorge is especially good at dusk, when the light turns the basalt columns gold. Allow two to three hours for the site itself.
