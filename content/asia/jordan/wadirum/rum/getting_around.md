---
title: Getting Around
type: section
---

You do not drive yourself around Wadi Rum. The protected area requires a Bedouin guide for vehicle access, and the unmarked desert tracks are genuinely confusing without local knowledge. There are no road signs once you leave the village.

Jeep tours are the standard way to explore. Open-back Toyota pickups bounce across the sand between the main sights, and your guide doubles as driver, storyteller, and tea-maker. Half-day tours (3-4 hours) cover the northern highlights; full-day tours push south toward Burdah and Umm ad Dami.

Camel treks move at walking pace and are best for shorter routes or sunset rides. Multi-day camel expeditions are possible but need advance arrangement.

Hiking is underrated here. Several routes near the village can be done independently — the trail around the Seven Pillars of Wisdom, for example — but longer hikes into the canyons or up the jebels require a guide. The Bedouin know every handhold on every rock face, and their knowledge of water sources is essential on hot days.

Hot air balloon rides operate at dawn, weather permitting, and offer a perspective on the landscape that nothing else matches.
