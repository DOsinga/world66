---
title: Things to Do
type: section
---

Wadi Rum is not a place for ticking off sights from a list. It is a place for being swallowed by landscape — for watching sandstone change colour over the course of an afternoon, for sleeping under more stars than you thought existed. The desert rewards patience and curiosity more than speed.

Most visitors explore on jeep tours arranged through Bedouin camps or the visitor centre. A half-day tour covers the greatest hits — rock bridges, inscriptions, sand dunes — while a full day pushes deeper into the less-visited southern reaches. But the real magic is in the spaces between stops: the silence, the scale, the light.

Serious hikers can tackle multi-day routes through the canyons and over the mountains, though you will need a Bedouin guide for anything beyond the marked trails near the village. Camel treks are slower but more atmospheric — the pace forces you to actually look at what you are passing through. Stargazing here is extraordinary; the lack of light pollution makes this one of the best dark-sky locations in the Middle East.
