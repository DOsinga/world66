story: "Aksu-Zhabagly was established in 1926, making it the oldest protected area in Central Asia — older than most national parks anywhere in the former Soviet Union. The reserve is home to the wild Greig tulip, the ancestor of nearly every cultivated tulip in the world, including the bulbs that fuelled the Dutch tulip mania of the 17th century."
---

The reserve covers about 1,300 square kilometres at the western end of the Tien Shan range, rising from 1,100 metres at the foothills to the 4,236-metre summit of Mount Sairam. It protects more than 1,200 plant species, around 240 bird species, and large mammals including the Tien Shan brown bear, Siberian ibex, Argali wild sheep, and a small population of snow leopards.

Entry is by permit only, in the company of a registered guide. The Zhabagly village ecotourism cooperative handles everything — permits, guides, transport, horses, and food. There is no infrastructure inside the reserve itself: no roads, no buildings, no fences. Treks are camping affairs, and the weather above 2,500 metres can turn from sunshine to snow within an hour even in July.
