The Central Mosque of Almaty was completed in 1999 and stands as the city's principal place of Islamic worship. The building combines a modern steel-and-glass structure with traditional Islamic architectural forms — four minarets of around 80 metres each, and a main dome visible from across the neighbourhood. Kazakhstan is a majority-Muslim country but with a notably secular public culture, a legacy of the Soviet period, and the mosque reflects both traditions: visually assertive but not dominating the cityscape.

The mosque is open to visitors outside prayer times; women should bring a headscarf. The interior is spacious and cooled, decorated with geometric tilework and Quranic calligraphy. Friday midday prayers draw the largest congregations. The mosque stands on the corner of Pushkin and Mametov streets, about ten minutes' walk north of Republic Square.
