story: >
  The museum's centrepiece is a recreation of the "Golden Man" — a full suit of golden armour discovered in 1969 in a burial mound at Issyk, about 50 km east of Almaty. The original belonged to a Saka warrior who lived around the 4th or 5th century BCE, and the intricacy of the craftsmanship — 4,000 individual gold pieces stitched to a leather undergarment — suggests a person of extraordinary status. The Golden Man has become the national symbol of Kazakhstan, appearing on the country's flag and independence monument.
---

The Central State Museum is the largest and most comprehensive museum in Kazakhstan, occupying a grand circular building near Republic Square. Its four floors cover the full sweep of Kazakh history: the early nomadic Saka culture, the medieval Silk Road trade, Russian colonisation, the Soviet period, and independence.

The permanent collection includes ethnographic objects — yurts, traditional dress, musical instruments, weaponry — alongside archaeological finds and art. A reconstruction of a traditional Kazakh yurt interior is particularly well done. The museum's signage is predominantly in Kazakh and Russian, with some English labels added in recent years. Allow two to three hours for a thorough visit. Closed on Tuesdays.
