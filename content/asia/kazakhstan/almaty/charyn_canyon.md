story: >
  The relict ash trees that fill the narrow inner canyon of Charyn are a biological curiosity — they survived the ice ages by sheltering in a microclimate created by the canyon walls, when the surrounding steppe was too cold and dry to support them. The Charyn ash forest is one of only two such relict stands in the world; the other is in North America. The trees look perfectly ordinary from a distance but carry 50 million years of history in their genetics.
---

Charyn Canyon is often called the Grand Canyon of Central Asia, though the comparison is imprecise — it is dramatically beautiful in its own right, a 154-kilometre gorge carved through vivid red sandstone and conglomerate, with walls rising 150–300 metres from the canyon floor. The most visited section is the Valley of Castles, where erosion has left pinnacles and spires of reddish rock in formations that are genuinely striking.

The canyon is about 200 kilometres east of Almaty, roughly three hours by car or marshrutka. Day tours are available from the city; self-drivers should allow a full day including travel time. The canyon floor can be reached by a steep path; a walk through the main section takes about an hour and a half. The river Charyn runs through the bottom, with small beaches where you can swim in summer. Carry water — the canyon floor gets very hot, and there is no shade in the Valley of Castles.
