story: >
  The 28 Panfilov Guardsmen who give the park its name are celebrated as heroes of the Battle of Moscow in November 1941. According to Soviet accounts, these soldiers held off a German tank assault for four hours before dying to a man. Later research established that the story was substantially mythologised — several of the "28 guardsmen" survived — but the myth had already entered Soviet national identity so deeply that it was never retracted. The Eternal Flame in the park burns for all of them.

Panfilov Park is the green heart of central Almaty, occupying a long rectangular block between Gogol and Qazybek Bi streets. It was laid out in the late 19th century and still feels like a place meant for leisure: wide paths, old trees, and benches where locals read or feed pigeons on summer afternoons.

The park contains two monuments that make it more than just a pleasant green space. The Zenkov Cathedral stands at its centre — see the separate entry for details. At the north end, the Memorial to the 28 Panfilov Guardsmen features an Eternal Flame flanked by stone reliefs and a lane of pedestals bearing the names of the soldiers. The flame has burned continuously since 1975 and the memorial is a genuine site of collective memory, not merely a tourist attraction. The park is free to enter and open at all hours.
