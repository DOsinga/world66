The Karaganda Ecological Museum is an unusual institution — a regional museum focused not on history or ethnography but on the natural environment and the damage done to it. It was established in the early 1990s in direct response to the ecological catastrophe of the Soviet period, when the steppe was mined, the groundwater polluted, and the land around the Semipalatinsk nuclear test site irradiated across an enormous area. The museum documents all of this with a directness that feels refreshing.

Exhibits cover the steppe ecosystem — the flora and fauna of the Kazakh grasslands, which are genuinely remarkable in their own right — alongside sections on mining contamination, water pollution, and the nuclear legacy of Semipalatinsk. The nuclear section is particularly striking: the test site is roughly 500km northeast of Karagandy, and the wind carried fallout across a wide area for decades. The museum treats this not as ancient history but as an ongoing health and environmental issue. For anyone interested in Soviet environmental history or Central Asian ecology, this is the most intellectually serious museum in the city.
