story: "Shymkent appears in written sources from the 12th century, when it was a small fortified town on the road from Samarkand to the steppe. It was sacked by the armies of Genghis Khan in 1220, rebuilt under the Timurids, sacked again by the Dzungars, and finally bulldozed in the Soviet period when the citadel hill was levelled into a park. The current reconstruction, finished in 2010, is built on the original archaeological footprint — but every stone of it is new."

The low hill at the northern edge of the old centre is the city's founding site: a Silk Road fortress occupied continuously from at least the 12th century until it was demolished in the Soviet era. A walled reconstruction of the fortress was built on the site between 2008 and 2010 as part of Shymkent's revival of its historical centre.

The reconstruction itself is more theatre than archaeology — clean new walls, a small museum, and a viewpoint at the top of the keep — but the climb up gives the best overview of the old city you can get on foot, and the small archaeological museum at the base of the hill is honest about what was lost.
