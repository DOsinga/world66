Inside the mausoleum complex, below ground level, is the khilvet — the underground hermitage cell where Khoja Ahmed Yasawi spent the final decades of his life in voluntary seclusion. When he turned 63, the age at which the Prophet Muhammad died, Yasawi felt he had lived too long and descended below ground, where he remained until his death in 1166. The decision was an act of profound humility that only deepened his reputation for sanctity.

The cell itself is small and austere: a low-ceilinged room reached by stairs, lit by a single opening. It is one of the most charged spaces in the complex — pilgrims queue to enter and emerge visibly moved. Whether or not you share the faith, the combination of the confined space, the weight of the building above, and six hundred years of unbroken veneration makes this a memorable place.
