---
title: "Things to Do"
type: section
---

Kuwait City's sights are concentrated along the waterfront corniche. The [Kuwait Towers](/asia/kuwait/kuwaitcity/kuwaittowers) are the unmistakable landmark — their observation deck gives the best view of the city. The [Souk Al-Mubarakiya](/asia/kuwait/kuwaitcity/souk_al_mubarakiya), a covered market near the old centre, is the most atmospheric place in the city for a morning wander: spice merchants, gold dealers, and food stalls in a compact, genuinely lived-in setting. The [Tareq Rajab Museum](/asia/kuwait/kuwaitcity/tareq_rajab_museum) holds one of the finest private collections of Islamic art in the world, and is free. The [National Museum](/asia/kuwait/kuwaitcity/nationalmuseum) tells the broader story of Kuwait's heritage, including the harrowing account of the 1990 Iraqi invasion and the looting of its collections. The [Grand Mosque](/asia/kuwait/kuwaitcity/grand_mosque) offers free guided tours for non-Muslim visitors. For a sense of Kuwait before oil, [Sadu House](/asia/kuwait/kuwaitcity/saduhouse) and [Bayt Al-Badr](/asia/kuwait/kuwaitcity/baytalbadr) offer glimpses of the pre-oil townscape. [Al Shaheed Park](/asia/kuwait/kuwaitcity/al_shaheed_park) provides welcome green space. The [Liberation Tower](/asia/kuwait/kuwaitcity/liberation_tower) at 372 metres is the highest vantage point in the country. For the genuinely unusual, the [House of Mirrors](/asia/kuwait/kuwaitcity/house_of_mirrors) in Rumaithiya — a private house covered entirely in mirror mosaics — is one of the most extraordinary things in Kuwait.
