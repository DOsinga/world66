  When the architect Larisa Sergeevna Lebedeva designed the circus in 1976 she chose a single hyperboloid shell of pre-stressed concrete — at the time one of the most ambitious thin-shell roofs in the Soviet Union. The result looks unmistakably like a flying saucer parked on a city block, and the building is occasionally photographed by visiting architecture enthusiasts who treat it as a small pilgrimage.

A late-Soviet circus building from 1976 that looks like a flying saucer — a single concrete-shell dome floating above a low ring of brickwork. The interior holds a 1,500-seat ring used for occasional performances, mostly visiting Russian and Central Asian circus troupes.

Performances are irregular and aimed primarily at children, but the exterior is worth a passing look for anyone curious about Soviet modernist architecture. Wedding parties often pose for photos on the front steps because the building photographs so well.
