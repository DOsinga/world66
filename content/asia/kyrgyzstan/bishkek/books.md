title: "Books"
type: section
---

**Jamilia** by Chingiz Aitmatov. The short novel that made Aitmatov's name internationally — a love story set in the Kyrgyz countryside during World War II. Louis Aragon called it "the most beautiful love story in the world".

**The Day Lasts More Than a Hundred Years** by Chingiz Aitmatov. Aitmatov's masterpiece, weaving together Soviet space programmes, central Asian folklore, and the daily life of a small railway station in the Kazakh steppe. The single book that does most to explain how a literate Soviet-era Central Asian thought about the world.

**Murder in Samarkand** by Craig Murray. Not about Kyrgyzstan, but the memoir of a British ambassador who fell foul of post-Soviet Central Asian dictatorship. Required reading for understanding the region's politics in the 2000s.

**Apples are from Kazakhstan: The Land that Disappeared** by Christopher Robbins. A wide-ranging travel book that takes in much of Central Asia, including Kyrgyzstan, written with affection and a sharp eye for the region's contradictions.

**Eurasian Crossroads: A History of Xinjiang** by James A. Millward. Heavy academic history of the great Silk Road region just over the mountains from Kyrgyzstan, useful background for understanding how the modern Kyrgyz state emerged.
