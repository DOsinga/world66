---
title: "Things to Do"
type: section
---

Bishkek is not a city that overwhelms visitors with sights. It is a city of squares, parks, museums and bazaars — best experienced on foot in summer, weaving between Soviet-era apartment blocks, tree-lined avenues, and the looming view of the Kyrgyz Ala-Too range to the south. Most of the headline attractions cluster around Ala-Too Square at the centre: the Kyrgyz State Historical Museum, the changing of the guard, the marble piles of government buildings, and the Lenin and Manas statues that bracket the square's recent history.

Two or three days is enough to see what the city itself has, and most travellers structure their visit around Bishkek as a base — using it to organise treks, hire drivers, and decompress between weeks in the mountains. The most rewarding things in Bishkek are also the most ordinary: a long browse through Osh Bazaar, a cup of black tea in Panfilov Park, a banya followed by a Korean restaurant.
