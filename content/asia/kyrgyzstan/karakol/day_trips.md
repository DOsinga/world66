Karakol is surrounded by some of the most dramatic scenery in Central Asia, and several outstanding destinations lie within a half-day's drive or a full day's walk. The valleys immediately south and west of town are the obvious choices: [Karakol Gorge](/asia/kyrgyzstan/karakolgorge) rises straight from the edge of town into spruce forest and doubles as the gateway to the Ala-Kul trek; [Altyn-Arashan](/asia/kyrgyzstan/altynarashan) is a high valley of hot springs and yurt camps ringed by 4,000-metre peaks; and [Jeti-Oguz](/asia/kyrgyzstan/jetioguz), west along the lakeshore, is famous for its red sandstone Seven Bulls and flower-filled meadows.

Closer to town, the [Przhevalsky Museum](/asia/kyrgyzstan/karakol/przhevalsky_museum) and mausoleum sit on a quiet promontory on the lakeshore, a short taxi ride from the centre.

Most guesthouses can arrange 4WD transport to the main valley trailheads for around 1,000–2,000 som, depending on destination. Marshrutkas from the bazaar cover Jeti-Oguz and some other western valleys in the morning and return in the afternoon. For the Przhevalsky Museum on the lakeshore, a short taxi ride suffices.
