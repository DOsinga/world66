The Jeti-Oguz Gorge, about 30 kilometres west of Karakol, is one of the most scenic spots on the southern shore of Issyk-Kul. At the valley's entrance, seven rounded red sandstone pinnacles rise above the plain — hence the name, which means "Seven Bulls" in Kyrgyz. A little further along, a solitary red formation called the Broken Heart rises in a jagged crest against the sky.

Beyond the formations, the valley opens into broad subalpine meadows. Jeti-Oguz is a well-known sanatorium area — a Soviet-era health resort sits near the entrance, still operating — and the meadows beyond are popular with Kyrgyz families for summer picnics and horse riding. The valley also serves as a trekking access point, with routes climbing into the Terskey Ala-Too range. A day trip from Karakol combines the rock formations, a walk in the meadows, and a horse ride; marshrutkas run from Karakol's bazaar in the morning, or a shared taxi can be arranged through any guesthouse.
