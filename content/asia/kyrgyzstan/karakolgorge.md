The Karakol Gorge is the valley that rises immediately south of [Karakol](/asia/kyrgyzstan/karakol), carved by the Karakol River as it tumbles down from the Central Tian Shan. The lower reaches are filled with dense Tian Shan spruce — tall, dark old-growth conifers that thin as the valley climbs, giving way to open meadows and then to bare rock and glaciers above 3,000 metres. The river runs cold and fast beside the trail the whole way up.

As a day out, the gorge rewards a straightforward walk of four to six hours up into the spruce zone and back, with the views opening steadily as you gain height. It is also the standard approach to the [Ala-Kul trek](/asia/kyrgyzstan/karakol/ala_kul_trek), so you will share the lower trail with multi-day hikers heading up to the pass and the turquoise lake beyond, and with trekkers coming down footsore at the end of the circuit. Yurt camps operate in the upper valley through the summer and serve tea and simple meals.

The trailhead is about 20 minutes from town by shared taxi, easily arranged through any Karakol guesthouse. The walk needs no special equipment beyond decent boots and a layer for the wind, but the weather changes fast at altitude, so carry waterproofs even on a clear morning. There is a small national-park entrance fee at the gate.
