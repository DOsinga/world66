Norus is a small ski resort tucked into the foothills of the Kyrgyz Ala-Too range, about 35 kilometres southeast of [Bishkek](/asia/kyrgyzstan/bishkek). It sits in the Ysyk-Ata valley, where a stream cuts through pine-clad slopes rising to snow-capped ridges. For most visitors it is a half-day or day trip from the capital rather than a destination in itself, but it delivers what it promises: a quiet patch of mountain air and a modest ski run without the long drive to [Karakol](/asia/kyrgyzstan/karakol).

The resort runs a couple of drag lifts serving groomed slopes that are well-suited to beginners and intermediate skiers. The vertical is limited, but the setting is beautiful — on clear days the whole sweep of the Chüy Valley spreads out below. A small lodge at the base serves shashlik, lagman and hot tea, and it fills with local families on weekends.

Outside the ski season the valley is equally worth visiting. In summer the meadows above Norus are carpeted with wildflowers, and the road into the mountains makes a good base for day hikes. The nearby Ysyk-Ata resort, a few kilometres up the same valley, has thermal baths that are popular with Bishkek residents year-round.

There is no ski or snowboard rental on site, so arrange equipment in Bishkek before you come. The drive from the capital takes 45 minutes to an hour by taxi, costing around 2,000–3,000 KGS one way. Make sure the driver has snow chains in winter — the road into the valley can be icy.
