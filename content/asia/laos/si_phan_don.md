Si Phan Don — "four thousand islands" in Lao — is where the Mekong, approaching the Cambodian border, spreads across a vast delta of islands, channels, and rapids. At the peak of the dry season the river is 14 kilometres wide here. The landscape is one of the most dramatic in Southeast Asia: multiple waterfalls, braided channels between forested islands, rice paddies, fishing villages, and an atmosphere so unhurried that it has a reputation for extending intended stays by days or weeks.

Most travellers base themselves on [Don Det](/asia/laos/si_phan_don/don_det) or [Don Khone](/asia/laos/si_phan_don/don_khone), the main islands connected by a French colonial-era railway bridge (the rusting locomotive survives). The pace on these islands is extremely slow and deliberately so — hammocks, river views, cold Beer Lao, and the kind of days where nothing gets done but nothing needs to. The two headline sights are the [Khon Phapheng Falls](/asia/laos/si_phan_don/khon_phapheng_falls) — the widest waterfall in Southeast Asia by volume, impressive enough to have stopped every French colonial engineering project for a railway connection to Yunnan — and the [Irrawaddy dolphins](/asia/laos/si_phan_don/irrawaddy_dolphins) that survive in a small stretch of river near Don Khone.

From [Pakse](/asia/laos/pakse), buses and minivans reach the islands in about three hours, with the connection usually via the ferry landing at Ban Nakasang opposite Don Det. From the Cambodian border, Si Phan Don is the last stop in Laos on the overland route; the border crossing at Don Det to Stung Treng is the most used route between the two countries.
