  Don Khone has the most tangible evidence of the French colonial period in Si Phan Don — not just the railway bridge from Don Det but the locomotive itself, a narrow-gauge steam engine left on the tracks at the southern end of the island after the railway closed. Don Khone is quieter and less developed than neighbouring Don Det, connected by the old French railway bridge. The island is larger but has fewer guesthouses, which gives it a more local atmosphere. The paths wind through small villages, rice fields, and forest to the southern tip of the island where the dolphin-watching boats depart.

The Li Phi Falls (also known as Tad Somphamit) on the western side of Don Khone are a series of rapids and small waterfalls in a narrow gorge — spectacular in the wet season when the flow is full. The rusted locomotive at the site of the old French railway terminus near the southern tip is the most evocative colonial relic in the area. Cycling around Don Khone takes a couple of hours and passes all the main sights.
