  The Don Det-Don Khone railway was built in 1897 to carry cargo around the Khon Phapheng Falls — at 7 kilometres, it was one of the shortest railways in Indochina and possibly the most expensive per kilometre, given the extraordinary difficulty of building anything in the swampy delta. The railway closed in 1940. The locomotive remained on the tracks, and today stands at the southern terminus of Don Khone, covered in moss and completely open to inspection.

The French colonial railway that once connected Don Det and Don Khone is now one of the most unusual heritage sites in Laos. The bridge between the two islands — 7 kilometres of narrow-gauge track — is now a pedestrian walkway, and the locomotive at the Don Khone terminus has been sitting on the tracks for over 80 years, rusting magnificently in the humid heat.

The railway was built to solve a specific logistics problem: the Khon Phapheng Falls blocked river navigation, and goods needed to be transferred from one section of the Mekong to another. The solution — build a tiny railway across two islands — worked for about 40 years before river transport was reorganised. Walking across the bridge between the two islands is a pleasant stroll, and the locomotive at the far end is worth seeking out.
