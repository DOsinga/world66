Irrawaddy dolphins in the Mekong near Don Khone are one of the rarest wildlife sightings in Southeast Asia — a small population of a critically endangered species, distinguished from other dolphins by their rounded foreheads and short beaks, adapted to life in freshwater. Sightings are not guaranteed but remain possible; the dolphins are most active in the early morning and early evening.

The standard approach is to hire a small wooden boat from Ban Hang Khone at the southern tip of Don Khone and instruct the boatman to take you to the dolphin pool. You wait in the boat on the river, watching. The dolphins surface to breathe at intervals, blowing visible puffs of mist. The encounter, when it happens, is quiet and brief — but the combination of the broad Mekong, the distant Cambodian shore, and the presence of a nearly-gone species makes it one of the more affecting wildlife experiences in the region.
