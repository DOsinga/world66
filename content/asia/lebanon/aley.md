Aley is a mountain town perched above [Beirut](/asia/lebanon/beirut) in the Mount Lebanon range, popular as a summer retreat when the coastal heat becomes oppressive. The cool mountain air, traditional stone houses and hillside restaurants make it a pleasant escape from the city, just 30 minutes up the winding mountain road.

The town has a long history as a resort -- Ottoman-era Lebanese and foreign residents fled here in summer, and the tradition continues today. You will find good restaurants with terraces overlooking the valley, some surviving traditional Lebanese architecture — including the late-Ottoman [Grand Serail](/asia/lebanon/aley/grand_serail) on the [main street](/asia/lebanon/aley/main_street_promenade) — and a relaxed pace that contrasts sharply with Beirut's intensity. The restored [Aley Synagogue](/asia/lebanon/aley/aley_synagogue) is a quiet reminder of the older mix of communities that summered here, and the nearby [Bsous Silk Museum](/asia/lebanon/aley/bsous_silk_museum) is the best small museum in this part of Mount Lebanon.

Aley is also a useful base for exploring the Chouf Mountains and visiting [Beiteddine](/asia/lebanon/beiteddine) and [Deir el Qamar](/asia/lebanon/deirelqamar), both of which are within easy reach to the south.
