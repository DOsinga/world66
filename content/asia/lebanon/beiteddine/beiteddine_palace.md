The Palace of Beiteddine is one of the masterpieces of 19th-century Arab architecture — a sprawling complex of terraced courtyards, marble fountains, vaulted halls, and richly decorated rooms set on a saddle in the Chouf mountains. Built between 1788 and 1818 by Emir Bashir II, designed by Italian architects and constructed by local artisans, it shows a remarkable fusion of Ottoman, Arab, and European Renaissance styles.

The standard visit takes around two hours. Highlights include the **Dar al-Wasat** middle courtyard with its fluted columns; the **hammam** with three chambers and a star-vaulted dome; the **harem quarters** with their intricately painted ceilings and stained-glass windows; the **grand reception hall** lined in mother-of-pearl; and the **Byzantine mosaics** museum housed in what were the palace stables, displaying floor mosaics rescued from Lebanese coastal churches.

The palace also contains the **tomb of Sit Shams**, Bashir's beloved wife. Since 1943 it has served as the official summer residence of the President of the Republic, so during state functions parts of the complex may close.
