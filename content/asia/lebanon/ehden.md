Ehden is a mountain village in the northern Lebanon range, sitting at around 1,500 metres on the southwestern slopes of Mount Makmal, about 120 km northeast of [Beirut](/asia/lebanon/beirut). It is one of Lebanon's finest mountain villages, and the combination of a intact Maronite village centre, an exceptional nature reserve, and proximity to the Qadisha Valley makes it worth a dedicated trip rather than a passing stop.

The main draw is the **[Horsh Ehden Nature Reserve](/asia/lebanon/ehden/horsh_ehden)**, which protects one of the last patches of native mixed forest in Lebanon — cedar, Cilician fir, juniper, and wild apple growing together as they have for millennia. Wildlife in the reserve includes wolves, wild boar, and over a hundred bird species. The reserve is closed in winter and best visited in the early morning during the summer months.

In the village, the **[Church of Mar Sarkis and Bakhos](/asia/lebanon/ehden/mar_sarkis_and_bakhos)** is the most significant historic site, a Byzantine-rooted Maronite church that has been at the centre of the community for over a thousand years. The **[village centre](/asia/lebanon/ehden/village_centre)** itself is well-preserved, with traditional stone architecture and a lively summer character when Beirut families move up to escape the coast heat.

Ehden is also close to the **Qadisha Valley** (Wadi Kadisha), a UNESCO World Heritage site whose cliff-side monasteries and dramatic gorge scenery make it one of the best hiking areas in the Middle East. The **[Monastery of Saint Anthony of Qozhaya](/asia/lebanon/ehden/monastery_of_qozhaya)**, a few kilometres down the valley, is one of the great Maronite monastic sites and includes the cave where Lebanon's first printing press was housed in 1610. The valley stretches for about 50 km and is within easy reach of [Bcharre](/asia/lebanon/bcharre) to the east.
