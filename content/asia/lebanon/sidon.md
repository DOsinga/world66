Sidon (Saida in Arabic) is the capital of South Lebanon and one of the great ancient Phoenician cities. It sits on the coast about 40 km south of [Beirut](/asia/lebanon/beirut) and is worth visiting for its atmospheric old town, its sea castle and its legendary sweets.

The [Sea Castle](/asia/lebanon/sidon/sea_castle), built by Crusaders in the 13th century on a small island connected to the shore by a causeway, is Sidon's most recognisable landmark. The [old town](/asia/lebanon/sidon/old_souks) behind it is a warren of narrow lanes, vaulted souks and Ottoman-era khans (caravansaries). The [Khan el-Franj](/asia/lebanon/sidon/khan_el_franj) (Inn of the Foreigners) is a beautifully restored 17th-century trading house. Just north of the city, the [Temple of Eshmun](/asia/lebanon/sidon/temple_of_eshmun) is the only substantially preserved Phoenician temple in Lebanon.

Sidon is famous throughout Lebanon for its sweet shops. If you have a taste for baklava, knafeh or other Middle Eastern pastries, [Al-Baba](/asia/lebanon/sidon/al_baba_sweets) and [Kanaan](/asia/lebanon/sidon/kanaan_sweets) are the two names to know — both have legions of devoted fans across the country.

The city is predominantly Sunni Muslim and more conservative than [Beirut](/asia/lebanon/beirut). It is a working city rather than a tourist town, which gives it an authenticity that can be refreshing. Sidon can be visited as a day trip from Beirut or as a stop on the way to [Tyre](/asia/lebanon/tyre) further south.
