  Traditional hammams (Turkish baths) in Tripoli were as much social institutions as hygiene facilities — in a city without private bathrooms until the twentieth century, the hammam was where you went to wash, socialise, discuss business, and mark life events. Weddings, circumcisions, and births were all celebrated in the hammam. The social function gradually declined as private bathrooms became standard, but Tripoli still has several functioning hammams where the traditional rituals are still practised.

The Hammam Abd al-Wahid is one of the oldest functioning hammams in Tripoli, a Mamluk-era bathhouse in the old city that has been in continuous operation for centuries. The building follows the traditional hammam plan: a cold room (bayt al-awwal), a warm intermediate room (bayt al-wastani), and the hot steam room (bayt al-harara), with a central dome pierced by glass oculi that dapple the interior with shafts of coloured light.

The hammam operates on a gender-segregated schedule, with separate sessions for men and women. A visit includes the traditional scrub (kese) and soap massage. The experience is genuinely relaxing and gives a sense of one of Tripoli's oldest surviving daily practices. The neighbourhood around the hammam has several other Mamluk buildings nearby.
