The Rachid Karameh International Fair is a large, unfinished modernist complex on the outskirts of Tripoli, designed by Oscar Niemeyer in the 1960s as an ambitious international exhibition centre. The scale is extraordinary: a domed oval conference hall, sweeping curved pavilion buildings, a helipad, and a network of elevated walkways spread across a large site. Construction stopped with the civil war and has never resumed.

The complex is now accessible to visitors, who come to see one of the most atmospheric examples of unfinished modernist architecture in the world. The concrete forms — Niemeyer's signature curves and sweeping lines — are intact but overgrown. The helipad sits abandoned in weeds. The juxtaposition of ambitious 1960s optimism with decades of neglect is striking. It has become a significant destination for architecture and photography enthusiasts. Check current access arrangements before visiting as the site has periodic security or administrative restrictions.
