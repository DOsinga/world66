The Taynal Mosque is one of the finest examples of Mamluk religious architecture in Lebanon and a UNESCO-listed monument. Built in 1336 by the Mamluk emir Sayf al-Din Taynal, the mosque is notable for its striped stonework (ablaq masonry) in alternating black and white, its elegant courtyard with ablution fountain, and its pointed Mamluk-style minaret.

The prayer hall contains columns taken from an earlier Crusader church that occupied the site, creating an unusual mix of Gothic structural elements within an Islamic context. Non-Muslim visitors are generally welcome outside prayer times; dress modestly and remove shoes. The neighbourhood around the mosque still has a working, neighbourhood character far removed from tourist Tripoli.
