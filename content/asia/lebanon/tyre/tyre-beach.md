Tyre Beach runs south from the city along the edge of the Tyre Coast Nature Reserve — a long, broad strip of pale sand with clear water and no beach clubs charging for the right to put your feet in the sea. The beach is cleaner than most on the Lebanese coast, partly because the nature reserve designation limits development, and the water quality is generally good. Facilities are basic: a few informal vendors during peak season, but no sun-lounger rentals or admission gates.

The beach sits within the nature reserve and borders sea turtle nesting habitat, so avoid disturbing the upper beach area above the waterline if you see nesting activity. Loggerhead and green turtles nest here between May and September. The combination of ancient ruins, a working old town, and a genuinely good free beach makes Tyre one of the most complete day-trip destinations from Beirut — when security conditions in the south permit travel. Verify current conditions before visiting.
