Malaysian Borneo is the wild counterweight to peninsular Malaysia: rainforest, limestone caves, orangutans, proboscis monkeys, coral islands, oil towns, longhouse rivers, and mountains that rise straight out of the tropics. It covers [Sabah](/asia/malaysia/sabah) in the north-east, [Sarawak](/asia/malaysia/sarawak) along the north-west coast, and the federal island of Labuan.

Most travellers start in [Kota Kinabalu](/asia/malaysia/kotakinabalu) or [Kuching](/asia/malaysia/sarawak/kuching). Kota Kinabalu is the gateway to [Mount Kinabalu](/asia/malaysia/borneo/mount_kinabalu), islands, and Sabah wildlife trips; Kuching is the softer landing, with riverfront food, museums, [Bako National Park](/asia/malaysia/borneo/bako_national_park), and orangutan centres nearby. [Miri](/asia/malaysia/miri) is the practical jump-off for [Gunung Mulu National Park](/asia/malaysia/borneo/gunung_mulu_national_park) and northern Sarawak.

Borneo is not untouched wilderness, and it is better to be honest about that. Logging, plantations, and road building have changed huge areas. But the protected places that remain are extraordinary: caves big enough to swallow cathedrals, canopy so dense it seems to breathe, rivers where hornbills and macaques cross above the boat, and reefs that still justify the long journey. Come for nature, build in travel time, and do not try to see Sabah and Sarawak as if they were neighbouring districts of one city.

## Destinations in Malaysian Borneo

- [Sabah](/asia/malaysia/sabah) — mountains, islands, orangutans, and the strongest adventure-travel infrastructure
- [Sarawak](/asia/malaysia/sarawak) — caves, longhouse rivers, Kuching, and deep rainforest parks
- [Kota Kinabalu](/asia/malaysia/kotakinabalu) — Sabah's main gateway and the base for Kinabalu trips
- [Kuching](/asia/malaysia/sarawak/kuching) — Sarawak's riverfront capital, good food, and easy wildlife day trips
- [Miri](/asia/malaysia/miri) — oil city and staging point for Mulu and Niah
