The Cameron Highlands rise from Malaysia's central mountain spine, a tableland of tea plantations, strawberry farms, and cool highland air sitting roughly 1,500 metres above the heat of the peninsula below. For Malaysians the highlands are the classic escape from the lowland heat — a place to breathe cooler air, eat steamboat hotpot in the evenings, and pick strawberries. For foreign visitors it can come as a surprise: the views across the rolling green rows of tea bushes are genuinely beautiful, and the walks into the surrounding mossy forest go places that feel far removed from the busy market towns in the valleys.

Tea is the defining feature. The [Boh](/asia/malaysia/cameronhighlands/bharatteaestate) and Bharat estates dominate the hillsides, and their tea factories are open to visitors who want to understand how the leaf goes from bush to cup. The tea here is robust and unremarkable by global standards but it is drunk locally with dedication. The highland strawberries have an intensity that flat-grown fruit rarely matches.

The three main towns — Ringlet, Tanah Rata, and Brinchang — form a chain along the single main road. Tanah Rata is the backpacker hub and the most useful base, with the widest range of guesthouses and restaurants. Brinchang is higher and quieter, good for the [mossy forest walk around Gunung Brinchang](/asia/malaysia/cameronhighlands/gunungbrinchang), which tops out near a telecommunications tower but the forest on the way up is genuinely eerie and strange. The [Butterfly Garden](/asia/malaysia/cameronhighlands/butterflygarden) near Brinchang is a pleasant hour if you have children in tow.

The highlands are cooler than the coast but still humid; mists roll in most afternoons. Come prepared for rain and pack a layer for the evenings.
