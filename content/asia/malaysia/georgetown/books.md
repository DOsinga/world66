**The Garden of Evening Mists** by Tan Twan Eng (2011). Set in the Cameron Highlands but resonating with the broader history of Malayan Chinese and the Japanese occupation, Tan Twan Eng's novel — about a former judge and her relationship with a Japanese gardener after the war — is the most lyrical recent fiction about Malaysia. His earlier novel **The Gift of Rain** is set squarely in Penang during the Second World War and is the better starting point for a traveller to Georgetown.

**A History of Malaysia** by Barbara and Leonard Andaya. The standard modern history, balanced and readable, covering the peninsula from the Malacca Sultanate to the present. Good context for understanding the layered communities of Penang — Malay, Chinese, Indian, Eurasian — and the way they fit into the wider story.

**This Endless Noise: Travels in Malaysia** by Brian Yapp. A wry recent travel narrative by a Malaysian-Australian writer returning to his ancestral country, including extensive sections on Penang and the food culture.

**The Penang Heritage Trust's walking guides**. Available from their shop on Lebuh Pantai and the Georgetown World Heritage Office, these slim trail guides are the best companions for a self-guided walking tour of the heritage zone — small, accurate, well written, with proper street maps.
