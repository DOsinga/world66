  The Rafflesia, which grows here in the Crocker Range foothills, is the largest
  individual flower in the world — a single bloom can reach 1 metre across and
  weigh up to 10 kg. It has no leaves, no stem, and no roots visible above ground;
  it exists as a parasite inside the tissues of a Tetrastigma vine, bursting into
  flower only for four to five days before rotting away. Finding one in bloom requires
  timing, luck, and a reliable local guide.

The forest reserve around Crocker Range, accessible from Keningau, is one of the best places in Sabah to see the Rafflesia, the world's largest flower. These extraordinary parasitic blooms appear unpredictably through the year — the reserve's rangers and local guides track their development and can direct you to a blooming specimen when one is open.

To see a Rafflesia, hire a guide through your guesthouse or the Tourism Malaysia office in Keningau. The trek to a bloom is usually 1–2 hours through secondary rainforest and is not technically difficult, but the terrain is muddy after rain. The flower itself is unmistakable — a mottled red-and-cream disc the size of a car tyre, with a smell that has earned it the local nickname "corpse flower."
