Kota Bharu is the most Malay city in Malaysia. The capital of Kelantan, tucked into the northeast corner of the peninsula near the Thai border, it runs on Islamic principles and old-fashioned hospitality. There are no bars, no nightclubs, and no pretense. What you get instead is one of the most culturally authentic towns in the country.

The city's heart is its market life. The central market, [Pasar Siti Khadijah](/asia/malaysia/kotabharu/pasarsitikhadijah), is a four-storey landmark where women vendors sell everything from fresh rambutans and durians to dried fish, spices, and batik. The [Medan Selera Night Market](/asia/malaysia/kotabharu/medanseleranightmarket) on Jalan Pintu Pong fills the streets with the smell of ayam percik and nasi kerabu. Eating well and eating cheaply are the same thing here.

Kelantan calls itself the "Cradle of Malay Culture" and backs it up. The [Gelanggang Seni](/asia/malaysia/kotabharu/gelanggang_seni) stages traditional shadow puppet shows, giant drum competitions, and kite-making — living crafts, not museum pieces. Around Padang Merdeka, the carved wooden [Istana Jahar](/asia/malaysia/kotabharu/istanajahar) and the royal [Istana Balai Besar](/asia/malaysia/kotabharu/istana_balai_besar) tell the story of the Kelantanese sultanate. The [Kampung Laut Mosque](/asia/malaysia/kotabharu/kampung_laut_mosque), built without a nail, is one of the oldest in Malaysia. And [Wat Photiwihan](/asia/malaysia/kotabharu/watphotiwihan), with its 40-metre reclining Buddha, is a reminder that Kelantan has a centuries-old Thai-Buddhist minority.

Beyond the city, the surroundings reward exploration. River cruises on the Kelantan River, cave adventures at Gua Ikan, waterfalls deep in the jungle, and the powdery [Pantai Cahaya Bulan](/asia/malaysia/kotabharu/pantai_cahaya_bulan) are all within easy reach. Kota Bharu is not a one-night stop. Give it time and it will surprise you.
