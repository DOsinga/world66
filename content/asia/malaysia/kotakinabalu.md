Kota Kinabalu sits on the edge of the South China Sea with [Mount Kinabalu](/asia/malaysia/kotakinabalu/mount_kinabalu) — Southeast Asia's highest peak — looming on the horizon. The city is Sabah's capital and the gateway to Borneo's most spectacular nature: coral islands a short boat ride from the waterfront, orangutans and pygmy elephants at [Lok Kawi Wildlife Park](/asia/malaysia/kotakinabalu/lok_kawi_wildlife_park), and the legendary mountain itself drawing climbers from around the world.

The city center is compact and walkable, built around a busy waterfront lined with seafood restaurants and a sunset strip that draws locals and travelers every evening. The [Gaya Street Sunday Market](/asia/malaysia/kotakinabalu/gaya_street) is a genuine weekly event — arrive early for the best produce, fresh-roasted coffee, and local snacks. For history, the [Atkinson Clock Tower](/asia/malaysia/kotakinabalu/atkinson_clock_tower) is the oldest surviving structure in the city, and the [Sabah State Museum](/asia/malaysia/kotakinabalu/sabah_state_museum) covers the full sweep of Borneo's cultures and natural history. The [Kota Kinabalu City Mosque](/asia/malaysia/kotakinabalu/kota_kinabalu_city_mosque), built over a lagoon, appears to float on the water at high tide.

KK is an easy city to like: the food is excellent (the [Filipino Market](/asia/malaysia/kotakinabalu/filipino_market) is not to be missed for grilled seafood), the people are welcoming, and it punches well above its size as a base for exploring one of the world's great natural environments.
