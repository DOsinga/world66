The Abidin Mosque (Masjid Abidin) stands near the centre of Kuala Terengganu's old town, a short walk from the waterfront. It is the city's state royal mosque and a functioning place of worship, though non-Muslim visitors are welcome outside prayer times. The architecture reflects Terengganu's connections with Java rather than the Mughal tradition — multiple stepped roofs in a style that was once common across maritime Southeast Asia.

The interior is relatively plain compared to more ornate Malaysian mosques, but the setting is pleasant, with a large ablution pool and mature trees in the compound. The mosque sits on a slight rise above the surrounding streets, giving the entrance a modest grandeur. Friday prayers draw the largest crowds; other times are quieter and more suitable for a look around. Women should bring a headscarf; sarongs are available at the entrance gate if needed.
