  Istana Maziah was built in 1884 using French-Baroque architectural elements, which is surprising in a conservative Malay sultanate. The story goes that the design was influenced by European palaces visited by members of the royal household, and the result — a yellow-painted confection with arched windows and a tiled roof — stands out dramatically against its surroundings. The palace has been the site of royal ceremonies for over a century and remains the official palace of the Sultan of Terengganu.

Istana Maziah is the official palace of the Sultan of Terengganu, a distinctive yellow building set in formal grounds above the waterfront. The palace itself is closed to the public — it is a working royal residence and ceremonial venue — but the exterior is the main draw for most visitors, and it is easily photographed from the road and the surrounding area. The main gate carries the Terengganu royal crest and is flanked by guardhouses.

The palace grounds are used for royal investitures, state ceremonies, and major Islamic celebrations. When the sultan is in residence, the grounds are more restricted, but the gates and façade can always be seen from the street. The palace sits in an elevated position above the old town and river, and the surrounding area includes a few other colonial-era buildings that make for a pleasant short walk.
