---
title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

KL is one of the great eating cities of Asia, with three distinct culinary traditions — Malay, Chinese, and Indian — overlapping in the same neighbourhoods and often the same restaurants. Eat at hawker centres, follow the queues, and don't worry too much about décor. The food is the point.

For a first night, head to **Jalan Alor** in Bukit Bintang, where a string of open-air seafood and Chinese-Malay stalls run late under strings of red lanterns. **Brickfields** (KL's Little India) is the place for South Indian banana-leaf rice and chai. **Lot 10 Hutong** in the basement of the Lot 10 mall gathers a curated selection of KL's most famous hawker classics — old shophouse names like Soong Kee beef noodles and Hon Kee porridge under one air-conditioned roof.

Try **nasi lemak** for breakfast (coconut rice, fried anchovies, peanuts, sambal, hard-boiled egg), **char kuey teow** (smoky stir-fried noodles) for lunch, **satay** or roast duck for dinner, and **ais kacang** or **cendol** to cool off afterwards. Service charge of 10% and SST of 6% are added to restaurant bills; hawker stalls take cash and add nothing.
