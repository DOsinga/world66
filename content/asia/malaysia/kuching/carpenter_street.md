Carpenter Street (Jalan Carpenter) and the Main Bazaar running parallel to the waterfront form the commercial heart of Kuching's Chinese quarter. The shophouses lining these streets date mostly from the late 19th and early 20th centuries and house antique dealers, handicraft shops, traditional coffee houses, and the odd repair workshop operating on principles that haven't changed in fifty years.

The Chinese History Museum at the corner of Carpenter Street and the Main Bazaar covers the history of Kuching's Chinese communities — the Hakka, Hokkien, Cantonese, and Teochew migrants who built the city's commercial economy and still dominate the waterfront trade. The Hong San Si Temple on Carpenter Street is one of the oldest in Sarawak, with an elaborate interior and a characteristic mix of Taoist and Buddhist elements. The street is at its most atmospheric on weekday mornings before the tourist shops open.
