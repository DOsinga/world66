  The museum was established in 1993 when the city government decided to lean fully into the cat connection. Nobody is certain why Kuching is called Kuching — one theory says it derives from the Malay word for cat, another that it's a corruption of a Chinese trader's name. The museum takes the cat theory and runs with it: 2,000 objects from 30 countries, including a mummified Egyptian cat, Andy Warhol cat prints, and a life-size replica of a white cat float from a Taiwanese parade.

The Cat Museum occupies a building in the Kuching North City Hall (Dewan Bandaraya Kuching Utara) complex, about 4 km from the city centre. Kuching means "cat" in Malay, and this museum takes the city's feline identity with appropriate seriousness — or at least appropriate thoroughness. The collection of roughly 2,000 objects spans cat artefacts from ancient Egypt to the present, including ceramics, paintings, folklore items, and an inexhaustible supply of cat-shaped kitsch.

It is, unmistakably, a very peculiar place. The enthusiasm with which it was assembled — the mummified Egyptian cat, the Andy Warhol print, the traditional Southeast Asian cat figurines — has a charm that more sober museums lack. Worth half an hour.
