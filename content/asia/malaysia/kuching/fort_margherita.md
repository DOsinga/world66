Fort Margherita stands on the north bank of the Sarawak River, directly across from the Kuching Waterfront. Built by the second White Rajah, Charles Brooke, in 1879 and named for his wife Ranee Margaret, the fort was designed to protect the river approach to the city from pirates. The crenellated white walls and tower give it the air of a minor English castle transplanted to the tropics — which is more or less what it is.

The fort is now a museum of policing and security in Sarawak. The interior is less compelling than the exterior and the views, but the battlements are accessible and the perspective across the river to the waterfront esplanade is excellent. Cross the river by bumboat from the Kuching Waterfront — the fare is minimal and the ride takes about two minutes.
