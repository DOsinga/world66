The oldest functioning Chinese temple in Malaysia, established in 1646 by Lee Wei King, a minister who accompanied Zheng He's famous voyages. The temple compound contains three prayer halls dedicated to different deities, a main hall of exceptional woodcarving, and a courtyard used for festivals and performances.

Every detail of the architecture — the roof with its elaborate ridge decorations, the carved gilded panels, the ceramic figurines — was produced in China and shipped over. The interior is dense with incense smoke, gold lacquer, and devotional objects accumulated over nearly four centuries. This is not a museum piece but an active place of worship used daily by Melaka's Chinese community.
