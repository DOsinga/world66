Muar is an old river town on Johor's west coast, close enough to [Melaka](/asia/malaysia/melaka) to be overlooked and different enough to deserve its own stop. It is one of Johor's royal towns, with a broad river mouth, pre-war shophouses, a handsome mosque, and a food culture that makes Malaysians talk about breakfast before they mention sightseeing.

The town is compact. [Sultan Ibrahim Jamek Mosque](/asia/malaysia/muar/sultan_ibrahim_jamek_mosque) stands near the river mouth with a style that mixes local, colonial, and Middle Eastern cues. [Tanjung Emas](/asia/malaysia/muar/tanjung_emas) is the evening stroll, where families gather by the water and the river opens toward the Strait of Malacca. Around Jalan Ali and the older commercial streets, murals, kopitiams, and shophouses give Muar a lived-in texture.

Muar is not a monument city. It works best as a slow day: mosque, riverfront, murals, coffee, otak-otak, and a walk through streets that have not been polished into a museum.
