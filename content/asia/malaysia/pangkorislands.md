Pangkor is a small island about 90 minutes by ferry from Lumut on the west coast of Malaysia, just south of [Ipoh](/asia/malaysia/ipoh). It sits in the sheltered Strait of Malacca, which has historically made it a significant waypoint for everyone from Dutch spice traders to pirates to holidaying Malaysians. Today it is best known for good beaches, fresh seafood, and a pace of life considerably slower than the mainland.

The best beaches are on the western side of the island, particularly Pasir Bogak and the quieter Teluk Nipah further north. The eastern shore is where the fishing villages and ferries are — less beautiful but more authentic, with boats unloading at the jetty and fish drying on racks in the sun. Scattered around the island are Chinese temples, the ruins of a Dutch fort, and enough winding roads to fill a day on a rented motorbike.

Pangkor Laut, a private island just offshore, is home to one of Malaysia's most celebrated resorts — but for those on a normal budget, the main island has sufficient accommodation at every price level. The island is best visited during the dry season (November to March). In the monsoon months the sea can be rough and the west-coast beaches windswept.

The [Dutch Fort Kota Belanda](/asia/malaysia/pangkorislands/kota_belanda) and the [Fu Ling Kung Temple](/asia/malaysia/pangkorislands/fu_ling_kung_temple) are the main historical sights. The beaches of [Pasir Bogak](/asia/malaysia/pangkorislands/pasir_bogak) and [Teluk Nipah](/asia/malaysia/pangkorislands/teluk_nipah) are the main reasons people come.
