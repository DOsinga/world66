**Anthony Burgess, *The Malayan Trilogy* (1956–59).** Burgess taught at Malay College Kuala Kangsar in the early 1950s, and the experience formed the basis for *Time for a Tiger* — the first novel of his Malayan trilogy and one of the best evocations of late-colonial Malaysia in English. Read it before visiting Kuala Kangsar.

**Tash Aw, *The Harmony Silk Factory* (2005).** Aw's first novel is set in 1940s Kinta Valley and centres on a Perak tin and textile magnate; the landscape — limestone karst, rubber estates, the long shadow of the Japanese occupation — is unmistakably this state. A Booker-longlisted novel that gives a strong sense of the place.

**Anthony Burgess, *Little Wilson and Big God* (1987).** The first volume of Burgess's autobiography includes his Kuala Kangsar chapters, with portraits of the Malay College that are as fond and acerbic as the novel.

**Eric Hobsbawm, *Industry and Empire* (1968).** Not specifically about Perak, but contains the best short account in English of the late 19th-century **tin rush** that turned the Kinta Valley into one of the most economically important places on earth — and made Ipoh and Taiping into the cities they are today.

**Jonathan Kennedy, *Pathogenesis* (2023).** Recent global history of disease that includes a vivid passage on the *plasmodium*-driven economy of British Malaya, with detail on the malarial Kinta Valley and the labour conditions of the tin mines.
