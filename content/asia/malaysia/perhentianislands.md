The Perhentian Islands — Pulau Perhentian Besar (Big Island) and Pulau Perhentian Kecil (Small Island) — lie roughly 20 kilometres off the northeastern coast of the Malay Peninsula, in Terengganu state. They are among the finest diving and snorkelling destinations in Malaysia, with gin-clear water, abundant marine life, and a relaxed, unmanicured atmosphere that sets them apart from the more developed Langkawi. The name means "stopping point" in Malay — the islands were historically a rest stop for boats carrying goods between Thailand and Singapore.

The two islands offer different experiences. Besar is quieter and has more upscale resorts; it draws couples and divers who want comfort with their coral. Kecil is cheaper, louder, and more popular with backpackers — Long Beach on the island's eastern side has a strip of beach bars and budget bungalows that gets lively on weekend evenings. Both islands have excellent snorkelling directly off the beach and good diving at reefs within a short boat ride. Turtles are common year-round at several sites, particularly around the green turtle nesting beaches.

The islands are closed from November to February when the northeast monsoon makes the sea rough and dangerous. The season runs from March to October, with April through August generally offering the clearest water. Everything here is very seasonal: most resorts close entirely in the off-season and the beaches return to empty stretches of sand visited only by the turtles that nest on them.

Getting here requires a ferry from Kuala Besut on the mainland, a small jetty town about 45 minutes from Kota Bharu airport or accessible by bus from Kuala Lumpur (roughly nine hours). The ferry crossing takes under an hour. Bring enough cash — the islands have limited ATMs and most transactions are cash-only.
