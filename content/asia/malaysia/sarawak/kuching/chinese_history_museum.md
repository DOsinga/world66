The Chinese History Museum tells the story of the Chinese communities of Sarawak — how they came, what they built, how they maintained culture and language across generations in a far corner of Borneo. The Hokkien, Teochew, Hakka, Cantonese, and Foochow communities all arrived at different times and for different reasons, and the museum traces each with photographs, artefacts, and documents.

The building itself is significant: it served as the Chinese Court under Brooke administration, the formal venue for dispute resolution within the Chinese community. It sits right on the waterfront at the end of Main Bazaar, making it an easy addition to a waterfront walk. Entry is free or very low cost, and the exhibition is thoughtfully presented. Particularly strong on the Sarawak pepper trade and the role of Chinese merchants in the colonial economy.
