A vast limestone cave system in the lowland forest near Miri, protected as a national park and one of the most important archaeological sites in Southeast Asia. The Great Cave is enormous — a single chamber over 60 metres tall — with the additional draw of red ochre rock paintings of human figures and boats in the so-called Painted Cave, dated to around 1,000 BCE.

A 3 km plankwalk through lowland rainforest connects the park headquarters to the cave mouth; the walk takes about an hour each way. Bring a torch and a hat (bat guano), and pace yourself in the heat. Day-trippable from Miri (1.5 hours by road); the simple park lodges allow an overnight if you want to catch the bat exit at dusk.
