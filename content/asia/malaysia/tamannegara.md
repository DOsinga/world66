Taman Negara is [Malaysia](/asia/malaysia)'s oldest and largest national park, covering over 4,000 square kilometres of rainforest that is estimated to be 130 million years old — making it one of the oldest tropical forests on earth. The park straddles three states in the interior of Peninsular Malaysia, about four hours northeast of [Kuala Lumpur](/asia/malaysia/kualalumpur).

The main entry point is the riverside settlement of [Kuala Tahan](/asia/malaysia/tamannegara/kuala_tahan_village), where most accommodation and the park headquarters are located. From here, trails lead into the forest to a [canopy walkway](/asia/malaysia/tamannegara/canopy_walkway) (one of the longest in the world at 530 metres), [rapids for swimming](/asia/malaysia/tamannegara/lata_berkoh), and [wildlife hides](/asia/malaysia/tamannegara/bumbun_hides) where you can spend the night watching for tapir, deer and elephants at salt licks. Longer treks to [Gunung Tahan](/asia/malaysia/tamannegara/gunung_tahan), the highest peak in Peninsular Malaysia at 2,187 metres, take about a week and require a guide.

The traditional way to arrive is by boat: drive to Jerantut, then take a three-hour longboat ride upstream on the [Tembeling River](/asia/malaysia/tamannegara/tembeling_river) through the forest — a journey that is an attraction in itself. Road access to Kuala Tahan is also possible for those short on time. Within the park, communities of [Orang Asli](/asia/malaysia/tamannegara/orang_asli_village) people maintain a way of life that has continued in these forests for millennia.
