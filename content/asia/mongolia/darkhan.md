Darkhan is Mongolia's third-largest city, a Soviet-built industrial centre about 200 km northwest of [Ulaanbaatar](/asia/mongolia/ulaanbaatar), connected to the capital by daily buses and frequent express trains on the Trans-Mongolian line. The city itself is not a prime attraction — its wide Soviet-era streets and industrial districts have more in common with Siberian towns than the nomadic Mongolia of the tourist imagination — but it is an honest, functional place with a few cultural highlights and, more importantly, good access to two of the most rewarding monasteries in the country.

The [Morin Khuur Complex](/asia/mongolia/darkhan/morin_khuur_complex), dedicated to Mongolia's iconic horsehead fiddle, and the [Museum of Darkhan-Uul Province](/asia/mongolia/darkhan/museum_of_darkhan_uul_province) give a sense of local culture and history. But the real reason to come to Darkhan is to arrange a jeep and driver for Amarbayasgalant Monastery, about 100 km east — one of the most complete surviving Buddhist monastery complexes in Mongolia, built in the 1720s and dedicated to the lama Zanabazar. The road passes through rivers and forest and can take two to three hours each way depending on conditions. The smaller Haragiin Khid is closer and easier to reach as a half-day excursion.
