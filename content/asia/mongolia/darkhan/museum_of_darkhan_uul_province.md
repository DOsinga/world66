  Darkhan-Uul Province takes its name from the Mongolian word for "blacksmith" or "artisan" — an echo of the nomadic tradition of itinerant craftspeople 
  who served the steppe communities. The Soviet-era city of Darkhan was built from scratch in the 1960s as an industrial counterweight to Ulaanbaatar, 
  and the museum documents both the ancient nomadic culture of the region and the more recent Soviet industrial history that reshaped it.

The Museum of Darkhan-Uul Province, also known as the Traditional Museum of Folk Art, holds a collection of local history exhibits covering the nomadic culture of the region, traditional Mongolian crafts, Buddhist artefacts from the nearby monasteries, and displays on the Soviet-era development of Darkhan as an industrial city.

The folk art collection is the highlight — examples of the Mongolian craft traditions that the city's name commemorates, including metalwork, embroidery, and traditional garments. The museum is a good introduction to the region before setting out to visit Amarbayasgalant or the surrounding steppe.
