Khovd (also Hovd) is the largest city in western [Mongolia](/asia/mongolia), a dusty administrative capital on the Buyant River with the snow-capped Altai Mountains rising to the south and west. It is the main gateway for travellers heading into the Mongolian Altai — one of the most remote and spectacular mountain ranges in Central Asia.

The city itself is functional rather than beautiful: a Soviet-era grid of apartment blocks and government buildings, a lively market, and the small [Khovd Aimag Museum](/asia/mongolia/khovd/khovd_museum). The most distinctive sight in town is the ruined Qing-dynasty fortress of [Sangiin Kherem](/asia/mongolia/khovd/sangiin_kherem), burned in the 1912 Mongolian uprising. The surroundings are extraordinary: [Kazakh eagle hunters](/asia/mongolia/khovd/eagle_hunters) practise their ancient art in the valleys to the north, and the [Altai Tavan Bogd National Park](/asia/mongolia/khovd/altai_tavan_bogd) — with Mongolia's highest peak — is reachable on a multi-day expedition. Closer to hand, [Khar Us Lake](/asia/mongolia/khovd/khar_us_lake) is a vast bird sanctuary on the Central Asian migratory flyway.

Getting to Khovd involves either a long flight from [Ulaanbaatar](/asia/mongolia/ulaanbaatar) or an even longer drive across the steppe. Once here, you are genuinely remote — and that is the point.
