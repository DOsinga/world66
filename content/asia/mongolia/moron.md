Mörön is the capital of Khövsgöl province in northern Mongolia, a small, dusty town that serves as the gateway to Lake Khövsgöl — one of the most beautiful lakes in Asia. The town itself has little to detain you: a market, a few ger camps on the outskirts, and the usual Mongolian provincial-town mix of Soviet apartment blocks and wooden fences. But it is the last significant settlement before the lake, and a good place to organise transport, guides, and supplies. Spare an afternoon for the [Museum of Khövsgöl Province](/asia/mongolia/moron/museum_of_khovsgol) on the central square and the small [Danzandarjaa Monastery](/asia/mongolia/moron/danzandarjaa_monastery) on the western edge of town — both will frame everything you see in the days that follow.

Lake Khövsgöl, about 100 km north of Mörön, is Mongolia's largest freshwater lake and holds nearly 2% of the world's fresh surface water. It sits in a forested mountain basin near the Russian border, surrounded by larch-covered hills that are home to the Tsaatan, the reindeer herders of the taiga. The lake is pristine, cold, and extraordinary. In winter it freezes solid enough to drive across; in summer the shoreline is perfect for horse trekking and camping. Twenty kilometres west of Mörön on a steppe track stand the Bronze Age [Uushgiin Uver deer stones](/asia/mongolia/moron/uushgiin_uver_deer_stones) — worth the detour on the way to the lake.

Mörön has a small airport with flights from Ulaanbaatar, or you can take the long overland drive (about 12 hours on unpaved roads).
