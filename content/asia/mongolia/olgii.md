Ölgii sits far out in western Mongolia, close to the borders with China, Russia, and Kazakhstan. It is not a polished city, and that is part of the point: the streets feel practical and wind-scoured, the mountains sit close, and Kazakh culture is more visible here than almost anywhere else in Mongolia.

Most travellers use Ölgii as the base for the Altai, but it is worth a day before or after the hard travel. The provincial museum explains the mix of Kazakh, Mongol, Tuvan, and Uriankhai cultures in Bayan-Ölgii. The central mosque and market make the town feel distinct from Buddhist and Soviet-shaped central Mongolia, while eagle hunters and embroiderers connect it to the wider steppe.

The strongest reason to plan around Ölgii is the Golden Eagle Festival, usually held in early October. Outside festival time, the city is still the place to find drivers, guides, permits, and local families for trips toward Altai Tavan Bogd and Tsambagarav.
