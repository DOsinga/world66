On the outskirts of Bago, the Hmwe Paya — known to travellers as the Snake Monastery — is a small pagoda complex built around a very large and very old Burmese python. The python lives in an enclosure within the shrine, adorned with a gold necklace and small jewelled ornaments, and is regarded by the local community as the reincarnation of a revered abbot who once presided over the monastery. The monks care for it, worshippers leave offerings, and the animal has become the spiritual centre of the place.

The python is genuinely enormous — one of the larger specimens you will encounter outside a zoo — and apparently entirely docile, which only adds to its otherworldly quality. Visitors can approach the enclosure, and on some days the monks will allow respectful interaction. Whatever your feelings about the theological premises, the place has an atmosphere unlike anything else in the area: flowers, incense, gold-leaf offerings, and a very large snake receiving the devotion of the faithful. It is not on every itinerary but it should be.
