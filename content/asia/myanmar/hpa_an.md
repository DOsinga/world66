Hpa-an is the capital of Kayin (Karen) State in southeastern Myanmar, a small, unhurried town on the Thanlwin River with a landscape of limestone karst hills that has made it one of the most visually striking destinations in the country. The surrounding countryside — rice paddies, cave pagodas, dramatic rock formations, and the sacred mountain of Zwegabin — is what people come for, and it delivers at every turn.

The setting is extraordinary: Mount Zwegabin rises 723 metres directly south of town, its distinctive profile visible from most of Hpa-an's streets, with the rice plain spreading around its base. The karst is riddled with caves, many of them sacred and filled with thousands of Buddha images — [Saddar Cave](/asia/myanmar/hpa_an/saddar_cave), [Kawgun Cave](/asia/myanmar/hpa_an/kawgun_cave), and [Yathaypyan Cave](/asia/myanmar/hpa_an/yathaypyan_cave) are the most remarkable, each with its own character and atmosphere. A circular cycling route takes you past most of the key sites in a long day.

Hpa-an is close to [Mawlamyaing](/asia/myanmar/mawlamyaing) (about 2 hours by road) and can be reached from Yangon by overnight bus in about 7 hours. The town itself is small and navigable on foot; the surrounding countryside requires a bicycle, motorbike, or arranged transport. Kayin State was long restricted for foreign travel, and Hpa-an retains a less-touristed character than Bagan or Inle Lake — the guesthouses are simple, the restaurants local, and the experience correspondingly authentic.

The Karen people are the dominant ethnic group here, and the festivals and religious life reflect a mix of Buddhism and animist traditions that differs subtly from the Mon and Bamar areas further north.
