Kengtung (Chiang Tung in Shan) is a highland town in eastern Shan State, set in a broad valley surrounded by hills and accessible overland from the Thai border crossing at Tachilek. For years it was closed to foreigners, and it retains a feel of isolation — this is far from the main tourist circuits of Myanmar.

The town is a treasure trove of traditional Shan architecture, with old wooden houses featuring intricately carved balconies lining quiet lanes. The most sacred site is the [Maha Myat Muni Temple](/asia/myanmar/kengtung/maha_myat_muni_temple), housing a Buddha image revered across the region. The 15th-century [Wat Zom Khum](/asia/myanmar/kengtung/wat_zom_khum) is one of the finest examples of Tai Khun temple architecture in Myanmar. The ruined site of [Kengtung Palace](/asia/myanmar/kengtung/kengtung_palace) — demolished by the military in 1991 — is a sobering reminder of the region's troubled recent history. [Naung Tung Lake](/asia/myanmar/kengtung/naung_tung_lake) on the western edge of the old town is a peaceful morning spot. The [central market](/asia/myanmar/kengtung/kengtung_market) draws Akha, Lahu, and Ann hill tribe vendors from the surrounding mountains and is one of the most colourful in the Shan State.

Kengtung is a base for [hill tribe trekking](/asia/myanmar/kengtung/hill_tribe_trekking) to Akha, Lahu, and Ann villages in the surrounding hills, though a licensed guide is required. The town is connected to Tachilek by a winding mountain road that takes several hours.
