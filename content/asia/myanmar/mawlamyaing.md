Mawlamyaing (formerly Moulmein) is the capital of Mon State and Myanmar's fourth-largest city, spread along the eastern bank of the Thanlwin River where it widens toward the Andaman Sea. Kipling wrote about it — "By the old Moulmein Pagoda, lookin' lazy at the sea" — and the ridge of gilded pagodas along the hilltop above town still catches the light exactly as he described.

The city has a languid, provincial charm that Yangon has largely lost. The [Thanlwin waterfront](/asia/myanmar/mawlamyaing/thanlwin_waterfront) is lined with colonial-era buildings in various states of repair, and the central market is busy and unhurried. [Kyaikthanlan Pagoda](/asia/myanmar/mawlamyaing/kyaikthanlan_pagoda), the one Kipling likely meant, sits at the highest point of the ridge and offers views across the river to the limestone hills of Kayin State. Several other pagodas including [Uzina](/asia/myanmar/mawlamyaing/uzina_pagoda) line the same ridge, making the hilltop walk one of the most pleasant strolls in Myanmar.

Nearby excursions include [Bilu Island](/asia/myanmar/mawlamyaing/bilu_island) (Ogre Island), accessible by ferry or road across the new bridge, where villages specialise in different crafts — pipes, rubber bands, walking sticks — and [Win Sein Taw Ya](/asia/myanmar/mawlamyaing/win_sein_taw_ya), home to one of the world's largest reclining Buddha statues, 24 km to the south. Mawlamyaing is well connected by bus and train to Yangon and makes a good stop on a journey south toward the beaches at Dawei.
