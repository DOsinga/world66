Pyin Oo Lwin (still widely known as Maymyo, its colonial name) is a hill station on a plateau at 1,000 metres in the Shan Hills, about two hours northeast of [Mandalay](/asia/myanmar/mandalay). The British established it as a summer retreat — Colonel May, after whom the town was named, was stationed here in the 1880s — and it still feels markedly different from the lowland cities.

The [colonial architecture](/asia/myanmar/maymyo/colonial_architecture) is one of the main draws: brick and timber houses with English-style gables, turrets, and chimneys line the streets of the old town. [Horse-drawn carriages](/asia/myanmar/maymyo/horse_carriage) are still a common way to get around. The [National Kandawgyi Botanical Gardens](/asia/myanmar/maymyo/national_kandawgyi_botanical_gardens), laid out by the former Governor Sir Harcourt Butler, are the main attraction — 177 hectares of well-maintained grounds with plants labelled in Latin and Burmese, and a good orchid collection.

The cooler climate means the area produces strawberries, coffee, and a range of vegetables that do not grow well in the plains. The [Pwe Kauk Falls](/asia/myanmar/maymyo/pwe_kauk_falls) and [Anisakan Falls](/asia/myanmar/maymyo/anisakan_falls), both within easy reach of town, are popular excursions. Anisakan in particular involves a steep hike down through forest to a dramatic cascade.
