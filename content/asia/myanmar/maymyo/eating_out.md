---
title: Eating Out
type: section
---

Pyin Oo Lwin has a distinctive food scene shaped by its cool climate and Shan proximity. Strawberries appear on menus from November onwards — in milkshakes, jams, and as fresh fruit at the market. The town produces its own coffee (look for Myanmar Shan highland coffee), along with vegetables that don't grow in the plains: lettuce, broccoli, and a range of European cultivars that thrive at altitude.

The central market area has local tea shops serving Burmese breakfasts of mohinga (fish noodle soup), fried samosas, and sweet milk tea. For something more substantial, Shan noodle dishes — thin rice noodles with a light broth and toppings — are the regional staple. There is a small cluster of restaurants catering to visitors near the botanical gardens entrance.
