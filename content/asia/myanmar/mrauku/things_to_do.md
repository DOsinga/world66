title: "Things to Do"
type: section
---

The remains of Mrauk-U lie scattered across a few square kilometres of green hill country at the edge of the present-day town. The temples are mostly Buddhist, built between the 15th and 17th centuries when Mrauk-U was capital of the Rakhine kingdom. They differ sharply from the slimmer-spired temples of Bagan: Mrauk-U's are dark, massive, and fortress-like, often half-submerged in vegetation.

The essential cluster around the central hill includes the **Shite-thaung** ("Temple of 80,000 Buddhas"), the **Htukkanthein** with its labyrinthine corridor, the elegant five-spired **Andaw-thein**, and the **Ratanabon Pagoda**. To the east, the **Koe-thaung Temple** is the largest of all. Most temples are reached on foot or by motorbike taxi from the central market.

The town also has a small **Mrauk-U Archaeological Museum** with stelae, coins, ceramics and Rakhine bronzes; the surrounding **Chin villages** (with the now-elderly Chin tattooed-face women) are a controversial half-day boat trip from Mrauk-U.
