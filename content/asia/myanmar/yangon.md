Yangon is Myanmar's largest city and commercial capital, a sprawling place of lakes, pagodas, and crumbling colonial buildings set against a backdrop of street vendors and trishaws. It is not as frantic as Bangkok or as clean as Singapore, but it has a character all its own — slow-moving and atmospheric in a way that most of Southeast Asia no longer is.

The [Shwedagon Pagoda](/asia/myanmar/yangon/shwedagonpagoda) dominates the skyline and is the undisputed centrepiece of any visit. The glistening gold-covered stupa, reputedly 2,500 years old, towers over Singuttara Hill and draws pilgrims from across Myanmar. Allow a few hours here, particularly at dusk when the light is at its best. The [Chaukhtatgyi Buddha Temple](/asia/myanmar/yangon/chaukhtatgyi_pagoda) north of the Shwedagon houses one of the largest reclining Buddhas in the country.

[Downtown Yangon](/asia/myanmar/yangon/downtown_yangon) preserves one of Asia's most intact grids of British colonial architecture. The grand but decaying buildings along Strand Road and the streets surrounding [Bogyoke Market](/asia/myanmar/yangon/bogyoke_market) were built in the early 20th century and remain largely unchanged. The [Secretariat](/asia/myanmar/yangon/secretariat) — where independence leader Aung San was assassinated in 1947 — is now partly open to visitors. The [Strand Hotel](/asia/myanmar/yangon/strand_hotel), though expensive, is worth visiting for a drink at the bar.

The [Sule Pagoda](/asia/myanmar/yangon/sule_pagoda) sits at the centre of a downtown roundabout, a gilded stupa surrounded by colonial government buildings. At the riverfront end of downtown, the [Botahtaung Pagoda](/asia/myanmar/yangon/botahtaung_pagoda) is uniquely hollow — visitors can walk through its mirrored interior. For an unhurried cross-section of city life, the [Circular Railway](/asia/myanmar/yangon/circular_train) loops around the outer suburbs for three hours, cheap and revealing.
