Ghandruk (1,940m) is the main Gurung village in the Annapurna foothills and one of the most attractive settlements on any of the regional treks. The village sits on a south-facing hillside with direct views of Annapurna South and Machhapuchhre, its stone-flagged lanes and slate-roofed houses spreading up the slope in tiers. It is a natural stop on both the Annapurna Base Camp trek and the loop from Nayapul via Poon Hill.

The **Ghandruk Eco-Museum** at the upper end of the village documents the history and culture of the Gurung people — their festivals, their dress, their traditions, and their long relationship with the British Gurkha regiments. It is a well-designed small museum worth an hour. The village also has a strong teahouse and guesthouse culture; spending a night here and watching the sun set behind Machhapuchhre is one of the simple pleasures of the trek.

The trail to the Annapurna Sanctuary passes through Ghandruk, continuing to Chhomrong (2,170m) — the last significant village before the gorge and the upper sanctuary. The descent from Ghandruk to Nayapul via Birethanti is a pleasant three to four hours through terraced fields and the gorge of the Kimrong Khola.
