The cave at Manang is one of several in Nepal associated with his meditation retreats. Monks from the local gompa maintain it as an active pilgrimage site.

The Milarepa Cave sits at approximately 4,100m just outside Manang on the Annapurna Circuit trail, a 30-minute walk above the main village. The cave is a small rock shelter associated with the 11th-century Tibetan Buddhist saint Milarepa, who is said to have meditated here. A small shrine and prayer flags mark the entrance; inside there are butter lamps, carved stones, and an atmosphere of genuine devotion.

The walk to the cave makes an excellent acclimatisation half-day from Manang. The trail climbs steeply through scrubby juniper and rock, with good views back down the valley to the Gangapurna glacier. A few trekkers continue above the cave to the Gangapurna glacier viewpoint or turn the acclimatisation walk into a longer loop taking in the monastery at Braga.

The cave requires no special permit to visit, though removing shoes is expected and a small donation to the monks who maintain it is appropriate. It is a quiet, genuinely sacred place — one of the few spots on the Annapurna Circuit where you can be certain that the spiritual life of the valley predates the trekking trade.
