Upper Mustang is among the most spectacular and culturally preserved regions accessible to trekkers in the Himalayas. Until 1992 it was a restricted kingdom, and it still requires a special permit — US$500 for ten days — that keeps visitor numbers low and the trails uncrowded. The landscape is nothing like the rest of Nepal: an eroded red and ochre canyon country of crumbling cliffs, cave dwellings, and ancient monasteries that looks more like the American Southwest than the Himalayas.

The main destination is **Lo Mantang** (3,840m), the walled capital of the former Lo Kingdom, a day's walk north of Kagbeni. The town's white-walled buildings, four-storey palace, and Jampa Lhakhang monastery have changed little in five centuries. The gompa contains 15th-century wall paintings and large clay statues in excellent condition. Monks still conduct daily prayers here; the monastery is active, not a museum.

The trek to Lo Mantang takes four to five days from Jomsom, through a succession of extraordinary villages — Chele, Syangboche, Ghemi, Charang — each with its own ancient gompa and eroded landscape. The return trip can be varied by taking a different route. The permit cost funds conservation and keeps the area from being overrun; the experience of walking in a Tibetan Buddhist kingdom that the modern world largely bypassed is worth every rupee.
