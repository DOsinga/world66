Butwal is a busy commercial city in the western Terai, the flat lowland strip along Nepal's southern border with India. It serves as the main transport hub for western Nepal and is the closest significant city to [Lumbini](/asia/nepal/lumbini), the birthplace of the Buddha, about 22 km to the southwest.

The city itself is functional rather than attractive — a transit point where the roads from the hills meet the east-west highway. But the [Siddha Baba temple](/asia/nepal/butwal/siddha_baba_temple) on the Tinau River gorge at the northern edge of town is a dramatic spot: a white pilgrimage temple wedged between steep forested hills where the river emerges from the Siwaliks onto the plains. The [Jit Gadhi fort](/asia/nepal/butwal/jit_gadhi) on the western ridge marks where fighting took place during the Anglo-Nepal War of 1814, and [Manimukunda Sen Park](/asia/nepal/butwal/manimukunda_sen_park) provides a central green space connected to the area's pre-Gorkha history.

Most travellers pass through Butwal on the way to Lumbini, Pokhara (via the Siddhartha Highway), or Palpa (Tansen) in the hills above. But it is a perfectly comfortable place to spend a night, with decent food, affordable guesthouses, and a lively bazaar that reflects its role as a regional commercial centre for western Nepal.
