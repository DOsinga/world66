Ghorepani is a trekking village in the Annapurna foothills of Nepal, at roughly 2,860 metres, sitting on a ridge above terraced farmland and rhododendron forest. It is the main base for the classic short trek to Poon Hill, the viewpoint at 3,210 metres that offers some of the finest panoramic views of the Himalaya accessible without technical climbing.

The Poon Hill sunrise is genuinely spectacular. From the summit you can see more than a dozen major peaks — Dhaulagiri, the Annapurna massif, Machhapuchhre (Fishtail Mountain), and Manaslu — arranged across the northern horizon. The combination of accessibility (the trek from Nayapul takes four to five days return) and the quality of the views has made this one of Nepal's most popular treks, and teahouses along the route are well established. Expect company, especially in the main trekking seasons of October-November and March-April.

The usual circuit links Nayapul, Tikhedhunga, Ulleri, Ghorepani, Poon Hill, Tadapani, and Ghandruk, passing through villages of Magar and Gurung people and descending through subtropical forest to the Pokhara valley. Nayapul is about an hour and a half by road from Pokhara, which is itself connected to Kathmandu by frequent buses and flights.
