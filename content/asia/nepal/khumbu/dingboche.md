Dingboche (4,410m) is the main acclimatisation stop above Namche on the approach to Everest Base Camp and Island Peak, a small village of stone-walled teahouses set in a wide, flat-bottomed valley enclosed by stone field walls originally built to protect crops from yaks. At this altitude the sky is impossibly blue, the air thin, and the mountains overwhelming: Ama Dablam (6,812m) rises in a perfect pyramid directly above the village, one of the most beautiful mountain profiles in the Himalayas.

Most standard EBC itineraries include two nights here — one rest day for acclimatisation, which is typically spent on a high day walk to Nagarjun Hill (5,083m) or the Chukung Valley. From Nagarjun the views encompass Makalu (8,485m) and Baruntse (7,129m) alongside the closer Ama Dablam and Lhotse. The effort is significant at this altitude — a 600m climb — but the panorama from the top is exceptional.

From Dingboche the trail to base camp continues through Lobuche (4,940m) and Gorak Shep (5,140m), the last permanent teahouse stop before base camp. The valley above Dingboche becomes increasingly barren and glaciated; the settlements grow smaller and the moraines larger.
