  Tenzing-Hillary Airport at Lukla is routinely described as the world's most dangerous airport. The runway is 527 metres long, slopes uphill at 12 degrees to help aircraft brake, and ends at a drop into a valley at one end and a mountain at the other. Weather cancellations are frequent; flights pile up and trekkers can be stuck for days waiting for a weather window. The airport's design was largely improvised by Sir Edmund Hillary, who helped build it in 1964 as a development project for the Sherpa community.

Lukla (2,860m) is where the Khumbu begins for almost every trekker — a small mountain town built around the famous Tenzing-Hillary Airport, gateway to the Everest region. The airport's short, sloping runway and dramatic valley setting make the approach one of the most exciting (and anxiety-inducing) short flights in aviation. The 25-minute hop from Kathmandu, in a small turboprop threading between cloud-covered ridges, is an experience in itself.

The town itself is functional but atmospheric. A single street of guesthouses, bakeries, gear shops, and porter agencies leads uphill from the airstrip. At dawn on departure day, the airport fills with trekkers and porters, the air is cold, the chai stalls are busy, and the mountains loom in the early light. On arrival, the smell of cooking fires, juniper smoke, and altitude-thin air is an immediate signal that you are somewhere genuinely different.

From Lukla, the trail descends to Phakding and then climbs to Namche Bazaar (one to two days). Helicopter evacuations for altitude sickness or injuries also land at Lukla before transferring patients to Kathmandu. In high season (October), flight delays can strand trekkers for days; build flexibility into your schedule when flying back.
