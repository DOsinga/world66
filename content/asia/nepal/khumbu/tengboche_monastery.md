Tengboche Monastery (3,867m) is the spiritual heart of the Khumbu and the most photographed monastery in Nepal, its white walls and crimson-and-gold roofline set against the soaring peak of Ama Dablam (6,812m). It is the largest Buddhist monastery in the Khumbu and the religious centre for the Sherpa community. The head lama — the Rinpoche of Tengboche — oversees the religious life of the entire valley from here.

The monastery is an active religious community, not a museum. Monks conduct twice-daily prayer sessions that visitors may observe from the back of the assembly hall. The interior is richly decorated with thangkas, butter lamps, and giant statues of the Buddha. The courtyard and the ridge above provide the classic view of the monastery with Everest and Nuptse in the background — one of the iconic Himalayan images.

The Mani Rimdu festival in October/November, when masked monks perform sacred dances in the monastery courtyard, draws both local Sherpa pilgrims and trekkers. If your timing coincides, it is worth planning around. The attached retreat centre offers shorter meditation courses for serious practitioners. The walk from Namche to Tengboche takes three to four hours through birch and rhododendron forest.
