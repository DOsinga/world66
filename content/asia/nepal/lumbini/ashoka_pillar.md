  The Brahmi inscription on the pillar reads: "King Piyadasi (Ashoka), beloved of the gods, in the twentieth year of the coronation, himself made a royal visit. **Buddha Shakyamuni having been born here**, a stone railing was built and a stone pillar erected. The Lord having been born here, Lumbini village was taxed at one-eighth (the usual rate)." It is the oldest surviving written reference to the Buddha's life.

A six-metre polished sandstone column, erected on the orders of Emperor **Ashoka of the Mauryan Empire** in 249 BC after his pilgrimage to Lumbini. It is one of the most important inscriptions in Buddhist archaeology — the inscription confirms that this is the Buddha's birthplace and that Ashoka knew the site as such.

The pillar stands a few metres west of the Mayadevi Temple inside the Sacred Garden. The lion-shaped capital that once topped it has been lost; the surviving shaft was rediscovered by archaeologists in 1896. Pilgrims tie prayer flags to the metal fence around it. Read the inscription panel before approaching — the Brahmi script itself is invisible to the untrained eye but the translation gives the pillar its meaning.
