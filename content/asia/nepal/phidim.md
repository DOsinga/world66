Phidim is a small hill town in eastern Nepal, perched in the foothills below Kanchenjunga — the world's third highest mountain. It's the district headquarters of Panchthar, a practical stop for trekkers heading toward the Kanchenjunga Conservation Area, and a place that rewards a slower look.

Two rivers — the Phewa and the Hewa — run through the town, which spreads across terraced hills in the usual Nepali mix of tin-roofed houses and concrete blocks. The setting is genuinely lovely: green valleys, cloud-draped ridges, and on clear days the [white mass of Kanchenjunga](/asia/nepal/phidim/kanchenjunga_views) rising to the north.

The population is ethnically mixed — Limbu, Magar, Rai, Tamang, Brahmin, Chhetri — making it one of the more diverse towns in the region. That diversity shows in the [Panchthar Bazaar](/asia/nepal/phidim/panchthar_bazaar) and festivals if you're passing through at the right time.

Getting here means a six-hour drive from Charaali in Jhapa on the [Mechi Highway](/asia/nepal/phidim/mechi_highway_drive), winding up through dense forest and terraced farmland. The road trip itself is one of the better ones in eastern Nepal — slow, winding, and worth it.
