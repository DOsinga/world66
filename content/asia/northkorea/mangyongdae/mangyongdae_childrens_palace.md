The Mangyongdae School Children's Palace is a large extracurricular facility where selected children from across the Mangyongdae district — and sometimes beyond — come after school to study music, dance, arts, computing, and sports. The building is enormous, a Soviet-era structure decked with mosaics and propaganda murals, its corridors lined with practice rooms.

Tour groups are typically shown a performance by the students at the end of the visit: a polished, highly choreographed show that can run 30 to 45 minutes, featuring acrobatics, folk dancing, accordion ensembles, and classical music. The skill level is genuinely impressive. Whatever you make of the political context, the performances are a vivid window into how North Korea invests in collective discipline and artistic achievement.
