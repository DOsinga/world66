  The funfair was opened in 1982 to mark Kim Il-sung's 70th birthday and has changed little since. Visitors sometimes describe the experience as unsettling — a working amusement park with the aesthetic of a theme park frozen in time, its rides running cheerfully while uniformed attendants keep an eye on proceedings.

The Mangyongdae Funfair is one of the few places in [Pyongyang](/asia/northkorea/pyongyang) where you can watch families simply having a good time. Roller coasters, a log flume, carousels, and bumper cars operate alongside shooting galleries and carnival games, all in a setting of Soviet-influenced architecture and painted murals. Admission is cheap by any standard, and on weekends it draws genuine crowds of Pyongyang residents rather than just tour groups.

For Western visitors, the funfair is striking precisely because it looks so normal — and so different from everything else on the itinerary. Children queue for rides, couples eat ice cream, and parents push strollers, much as they would anywhere else. It is one of the more human glimpses available on a North Korea tour.
