The West Sea Barrage (also called Nampo Dam, or the West Sea Lock Gate) is an eight-kilometre tidal dam and lock system that closes off the mouth of the Taedong River where it meets the Yellow Sea, about fifteen kilometres west of central Nampo. It contains three large ship locks, dozens of sluice gates, a roadway, and a railway. Built between 1981 and 1986 and opened on 24 June 1986, it is one of the largest civil engineering projects ever completed in North Korea.

In practical terms it converts the saline tidal estuary into a freshwater reservoir for irrigation of the Pyongyang plains and prevents the salt intrusion that was eating into the country's agriculture. In tour terms it is the headline stop on the Pyongyang–Nampo road trip: a viewing platform, a propagandistic narrative film in the visitor centre, and a long walk out along the dam wall to look at boats locking through. It is genuinely impressive on the ground, and one of the few engineering sites you will see in North Korea where the scale matches the official rhetoric.
