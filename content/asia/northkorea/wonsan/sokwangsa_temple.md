  Sokwangsa Temple was founded in the Goryeo dynasty and rebuilt extensively in the Joseon period — more than 50 large and small buildings were constructed on the site over several centuries. The temple survived the Korean War, which destroyed much of Wonsan city, relatively intact. It is one of the few Buddhist institutions in North Korea that still functions as a working temple, albeit under strict state supervision.
---

Sokwangsa Temple, dating to 1386, is one of the most historically significant Buddhist sites accessible on Wonsan tours. The complex contains more than 50 structures spread across a mountain valley setting north of the city, with wooden halls, pagodas, and garden areas typical of Joseon-dynasty Buddhist architecture. The setting — mountains, forested slopes, a stream — is the most genuinely peaceful environment most visitors encounter in North Korea.

The temple is sometimes included in Wonsan itineraries and sometimes not, depending on the specific tour programme. When it is accessible, it is worth the detour: the architecture is authentic, the grounds are well maintained, and the contrast between the tranquility of the site and the intensity of urban North Korea is striking.
