  Omani bullfighting has nothing to do with the Spanish variety. There is no matador, no blood, and no death. Two bulls are led to face each other and push, horn to horn, until one backs down or breaks away. The contest is about strength and stubbornness, not spectacle. The bulls are valued animals — well fed and carefully trained — and their owners take the outcome extremely seriously. The bouts are accompanied by much shouting, side betting, and commentary from the crowd.

Barka is one of the few places in Oman where you can witness traditional Omani bullfighting, held on Friday mornings during the cooler months from roughly October through April. The event draws a largely local audience — men gathering after Friday prayers, many of them the bull owners themselves or serious enthusiasts. Visitors are welcome but should observe respectfully and not interfere with the proceedings.

The bouts take place in an open area near the coast. The timing is informal — things get started when enough people and bulls have arrived, usually mid-morning. Check locally or ask at the fort or your accommodation for the current schedule, as it varies.
