  The fort played a role in the Imamate rebellions of the mid-20th century, when Ibri was contested between the Sultan of Muscat and the elected Imam of Oman. Control of the town changed hands several times between the 1950s and 1959, when Sultan Said bin Taimur's forces finally consolidated control of the interior. The fort's location at the junction of routes to the coast, the mountains, and the Empty Quarter made it strategically important long before oil changed Oman's economic calculus.

Ibri Fort is a large, well-restored Omani fort in the centre of town, built in the traditional mud-brick and stone style of the Hajar Mountain region. The fort has multiple towers of varying heights, interior courtyards, and the labyrinthine room arrangements typical of Omani military architecture — designed to confuse attackers and create defensive chokepoints at every turn.

Entry is free and the fort is open during standard Omani government hours (typically mornings and a few hours in the afternoon, closed Friday mornings). The views from the towers over the town and surrounding date palm groves are worth the climb. Like most Omani forts, this one has been thoughtfully restored using original materials and techniques — it looks maintained without looking fake.
