Mirbat sits on the Dhofar coast east of [Salalah](/asia/oman/salalah), quieter and rougher-edged than the main city. It was once tied to the frankincense trade and Arabian horse breeding; today it is a fishing town with a small fort, old merchant houses, and empty beaches running out toward the Arabian Sea.

The town's strongest story is modern and violent. In 1972, during the Dhofar Rebellion, a small Omani and British SAS force held Mirbat against a much larger insurgent attack. That battle gives the fort and old quarter a sharper edge than their modest size suggests.

Come as a half-day trip from Salalah if you care about Dhofari history, coastal architecture, or diving. Mirbat is not polished, but it has a real sense of place.
