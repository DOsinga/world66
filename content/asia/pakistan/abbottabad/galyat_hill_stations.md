The hour-long drive south of Abbottabad climbs steeply through a chain of small British-era hill resorts strung along the ridge: Changla Gali, Khaira Gali, Dunga Gali, Nathia Gali, Ayubia. "Gali" means "lane" or "pass" in the local language. The combined area is the Galyat.

**Nathia Gali**, the largest, sits at 2,500 metres in pine, oak, walnut, and maple forest. The views over the Kunhar valley are excellent and the cool air is the immediate reward. **Ayubia** has Pakistan's only chairlift, which lifts visitors above the forest for a short ride and a view. **Dunga Gali** is the quieter alternative — a small village on the slopes of Mukshpuri Hill (2,376m), from which a footpath leads to the summit and on to the higher Miranjani peak (2,990m).

The whole circuit is the standard summer outing from Abbottabad. Day trips are easy; for a slower visit, several hotels operate in Nathia Gali. Roads are open year-round but can be snowed in for a few days in January and February.
