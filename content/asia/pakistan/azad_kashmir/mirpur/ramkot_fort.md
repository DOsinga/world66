  Ramkot Fort sits on a peninsula that was always surrounded by the Jhelum River on three sides — making it naturally defensible. When the Mangla Dam flooded the valley in 1967, the water rose around its base, transforming the peninsula into an island. The fort, which survived the Mughal conquest, the Sikh empire, and British rule, was not abandoned for any of those — it was only water that finally made it inaccessible by land.
---

Ramkot Fort occupies a rocky peninsula in Mangla Lake, accessible only by boat from the Army Water Sports Club at Sukhian — a crossing of roughly 45 minutes to an hour depending on conditions. The fort was built on the foundations of a Hindu temple and refortified under Mughal and Sikh rule, giving it a layered history that the crumbling walls and bastions only hint at.

The climb from the water's edge is about 350 steps to the top of the fortifications. From the highest point, the views over Mangla Lake and the surrounding bare hills are exceptional, and on clear days the distant mountains of Kashmir are visible. The journey itself is part of the attraction — the boat ride across the flat, still lake in the early morning, surrounded by brown hills, has a particular quietness to it.
