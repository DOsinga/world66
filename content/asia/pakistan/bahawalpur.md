Bahawalpur is a historic city in southern Punjab, Pakistan, on the edge of the Cholistan Desert near the Indian border. It was the capital of a princely state ruled by the Nawabs of Bahawalpur until Pakistan's independence, and their legacy is visible in the city's ornate palaces and public buildings — several built in an Indo-Saracenic style in the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

The surrounding Cholistan Desert, stretching some 26,000 square kilometres towards the Thar, gives the region a distinctive character. [Derawar Fort](/asia/pakistan/bahawalpur/derawar_fort), a massive mud-brick fortification rising from the desert 100km south, is one of the most dramatic historical monuments in Pakistan. [Lal Suhanra National Park](/asia/pakistan/bahawalpur/lal_suhanra_national_park), 35km east of the city, protects desert and wetland habitat with blackbuck, chinkara gazelle, and migratory birds.

The city's palaces — [Noor Mahal](/asia/pakistan/bahawalpur/noor_mahal), [Gulzar Mahal](/asia/pakistan/bahawalpur/gulzar_mahal), and the Darbar Mahal complex — reflect the architectural ambition of the nawabs who ruled this rich cotton-growing state until 1947. The ancient Sufi town of [Uch Sharif](/asia/pakistan/bahawalpur/uch_sharif), 75km west, adds a deeper historical dimension. Bahawalpur is a commercial and educational centre for the region, with a university and busy bazaars around [Fareed Gate](/asia/pakistan/bahawalpur/fareed_gate) trading in cotton textiles, carpets, and the distinctive crafts of the Cholistan — hand-woven camel-hair goods, embroidered cloth, and date-palm basketwork.
