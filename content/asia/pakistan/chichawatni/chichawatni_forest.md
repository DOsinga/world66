  The Chichawatni Forest is the second-largest planted forest in Pakistan, after Changa Manga. Both are products of the colonial-era Public Works Department programme that planted trees for railway fuel — the early steam locomotives consumed enormous quantities of wood, and the Punjab's forests were deliberately engineered to supply it. The railway fuel need is long gone but the forests remain, now serving as lungs and weekend retreats for the surrounding plains.

The Chichawatni Forest covers around 9,000 acres on the outskirts of town, making it the second-largest planted forest in Pakistan after Changa Manga near Lahore. It's a quiet place for a walk or a drive away from the dust of the Grand Trunk Road — something genuinely unusual in this intensively farmed agricultural landscape where trees are otherwise sparse.

The forest is composed mainly of eucalyptus, shisham, and poplar trees, planted in orderly rows in the colonial tradition. There are no formal hiking trails or visitor facilities, but locals come here for shade, picnics, and a measure of quiet. It is at its most pleasant in winter and spring, when the heat is manageable. In summer the forest offers shade but the temperatures are punishing.
