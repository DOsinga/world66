title: "Books"

---

Chitral has attracted a steady trickle of travel writers and a serious anthropological literature on the Kalash. The following are worth seeking out:

**Where Three Empires Meet** by E. F. Knight (1893). A despatch from the Great Game era, including the Chitral campaign of 1895 — the siege of the fort and the relief column over the passes.

**Wild Wales of the East** is the wrong subtitle; you want **The Gilgit Game** by John Keay (1979), still the best single-volume history of British, Russian and Chinese moves in the western Karakoram and Hindu Kush. The Chitral chapters are excellent.

**Caravans** by James A. Michener (1963). A novel set in Afghanistan but with extended detours into nomadic life across the porous Chitral border. Better as a sense of place than as anthropology.

**The Kalasha** by Wynne Maggi (2001). A clear-eyed ethnographic account of the Kalash women of Rumbur valley; one of the best modern books on the people most travellers come to see.
