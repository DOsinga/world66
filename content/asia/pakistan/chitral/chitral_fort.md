The fort sits low on the right bank of the Chitral River with the bulk of Tirich Mir behind it on clear days. Built in the early 18th century and rebuilt several times, it served as the residence of the Mehtars — the hereditary rulers of Chitral — until the kingdom was absorbed into Pakistan in 1969. The current Mehtar's family still uses parts of the complex.

Visiting is informal: ask at the gate. The polo ground next door — one of the highest natural grass polo grounds in the world — is the venue for big matches against the rival valley of Mastuj. The famous summer Shandur tournament against the Gilgit side draws thousands of spectators to a 3,700-metre pass.
