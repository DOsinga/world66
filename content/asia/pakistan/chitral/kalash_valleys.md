Three side-valleys south of Chitral — Bumburet, Rumbur and Birir — are home to the Kalash, a community of around 3,000 people who follow an ancient animist religion and speak their own Indo-Aryan language. The villages cling to the steep slopes: timber-frame houses stacked on stone terraces, walnut and mulberry trees, women in heavy black wool dresses with elaborately beaded headdresses.

Bumburet is the largest valley and the easiest to reach — about 40 km from Chitral by rough jeep road, three to four hours each way. Rumbur and Birir are closer in distance but harder going. Three big festivals draw visitors: **Joshi** in May (welcoming spring), **Uchau** in August or September (harvest), and **Chowmos** in December (winter solstice). Festivals are open to visitors but treat them as religious events, not performances; ask before photographing people, and stay clear of the bashali (the women's seclusion houses).
