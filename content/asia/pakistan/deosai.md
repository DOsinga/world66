The Deosai Plains spread across a vast high-altitude basin south of Skardu, a treeless plateau sitting at an average elevation of around 4,100 metres that ranks among the highest in the world. The name is often translated as "the land of giants," and the scale of the place earns it: a rolling, seemingly empty expanse rimmed by snow peaks, cut by clear streams, and silent except for wind and birdsong. For much of the year it lies under deep snow and is completely cut off; the jeep tracks across it are open only from late June to early October.

The park was established in 1993 specifically to protect the Himalayan brown bear, which had been reduced to around twenty animals in the area; numbers have since recovered to over fifty. Marmots whistle from burrows beside the track, golden marmots and ibex graze the slopes, and golden eagles and lammergeiers work the ridgelines. In July and August the plateau erupts into one of the great wildflower displays of the Himalaya, a carpet of yellows, blues and pinks across ground that is frozen solid for the rest of the year.

Most visitors cross Deosai as a long day-trip from Skardu, climbing the jeep track from Sadpara, but the plateau rewards more time. An overnight at the simple camps near Sheosar Lake, the deep blue tarn at the southern edge of the plateau, lets you see the early-morning light when bears are most active. A popular two- or three-day route continues out via the Astore valley to the Karakoram Highway.

Conditions are basic and the environment is unforgiving: the road is rough, the altitude takes its toll, and the weather can turn from sun to sleet within an hour even in midsummer. Come with a guide, warm and waterproof clothing, and time in hand. There is no fuel, no reliable signal, and little shelter once you are on the plateau.
