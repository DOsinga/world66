Dera Ismail Khan — commonly abbreviated to D.I. Khan — is a city on the west bank of the Indus River in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, [Pakistan](/asia/pakistan). Founded in the late 15th century by the Baloch adventurer Ismail Khan, it sits at the edge of the tribal areas where the southern Punjab plains meet the mountains, about 200 kilometres west of Lahore.

The city is a commercial centre for the surrounding agricultural region, known especially for the Dhakki Date, a prized variety grown in nearby villages and exported to the Middle East, Europe, and the United States. Sohan Halwa — a dense sweet made from wheat sprouts and ghee — is the city's most famous delicacy and sold by sweet shops throughout the bazaars.

Tourist attractions are modest. The [Old Bazaars](/asia/pakistan/dera_ismail_khan/old_bazaars) — Topanwala, Bhatiya, Muslim, and Kalan — converge at the Chowaglla intersection and are worth exploring for local crafts including lacquered woodwork, glass and ivory ware, and woven mats. A historic Sikh shrine in the old bazaar marks the spot where Guru Nanak is said to have stayed. The pre-Islamic [Bilot Fort](/asia/pakistan/dera_ismail_khan/bilot_fort), on a hill near the Chashma highway, and the archaeological ruins at Handeray, about 45 kilometres along the Indus Highway, are the main historical sites in the area.

D.I. Khan is connected by road to Bannu and Peshawar via Kohat, and to Mianwali and Punjab via the Chashma Barrage bridge. The airport is 10 kilometres from the city centre.
