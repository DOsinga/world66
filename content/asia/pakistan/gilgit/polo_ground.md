  Polo in the Gilgit-Baltistan highlands is played in a form that locals claim is the 
  sport's original version: no rules, unlimited players per side, and music played 
  throughout by a band of drummers and flute players who follow the action up and down 
  the ground. This free-form game — sometimes called "tent-pegging polo" or simply 
  "mountain polo" — is entirely different in character from the structured sport played 
  in Argentina or England.

The polo ground at the edge of Gilgit town is the social centre of the city. Matches are played on weekends throughout the season, and the games are free to watch — spectators crowd along the side of the dusty pitch while musicians play throughout the match. Gilgit polo is fast and rough, with players leaning from their horses at full gallop in a way that looks genuinely dangerous.

The game's connection to the region runs deep. The Shandur Polo Festival, held each July at the 3,700-metre Shandur Pass between Gilgit and Chitral, is one of the highest-altitude polo tournaments in the world and draws visitors from across Pakistan. If you are in Gilgit when a match is scheduled, watching is one of the better free experiences the town offers.
