Wah Gardens is a Mughal garden complex dating to the reign of Emperor Akbar, located at the village of Wah just south of Hassan Abdal. It is one of the better-preserved Mughal gardens in Pakistan outside of Lahore, though it receives a fraction of the visitors that the Lahore gardens attract.

The formal garden layout follows the classical charbagh (four-garden) plan divided by water channels, with a central pavilion. At its height under Jahangir the complex included bathhouses, a mosque, and multiple terraced garden enclosures. The ruins of the bathhouse and the mosque foundations are still visible. The setting, against a low ridge with views over the surrounding plain, remains attractive.

Wah Gardens is managed by the Archaeology Department and charges a small entrance fee. It is about 3 km south of the main gurdwara.
