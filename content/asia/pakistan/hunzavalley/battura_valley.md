Battura Valley — also written Batura — cuts north from the Karakoram Highway near Passu in upper Hunza, and is dominated by the Batura Glacier, one of the longest glaciers outside the polar regions. The glacier stretches for nearly 60 kilometres through the mountains and draws serious trekkers and mountaineers willing to make the journey.

The valley's name derives from Brushuski — the indigenous language of central Hunza — where "bat" means skin and "tur" means horns, a reference to the ibex hunted here and whose skins and horns were found around the glacier. The pastures along the valley carry names in both Brushuski and Wakhi, the other main language of the upper Hunza region, reflecting the layered history of this remote corridor.

Travel up the Battura Valley is for those who have already done Hunza and want to go further. The landscape is extreme — glaciers pouring off walls of rock, moraine ridges, and the constant sound of ice and water. The nearest base is [Passu](/asia/pakistan/hunzavalley/gojal_valley), which has basic guesthouses and sits where the Batura and Ghulkin glaciers converge. From Passu you can walk to the Batura Glacier's snout in a few hours, and experienced trekkers do multi-day trips through the valley's high pastures.
