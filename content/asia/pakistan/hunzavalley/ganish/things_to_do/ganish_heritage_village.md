---
title: "Ganish Heritage Village"
type: poi
---

Ganish Heritage Village — also written Ganesh — is considered the oldest surviving settlement in the Hunza Valley, with a historic core that predates both Baltit and Altit forts. UNESCO has recognised the cultural importance of the site, and some conservation work has been carried out on its most significant structures. The oldest quarter, known as Ganish Khun, preserves a street plan that has changed little over centuries, with a distinctive S-shaped lane threading between stone houses, stables, and communal spaces.

Walking into Ganish Khun feels like stepping back several hundred years. The lanes are barely wide enough for two people to pass, the walls of rough-cut stone rise two or three storeys, and small wooden balconies jut out overhead. Scattered around the perimeter of the village are ancient carved rocks bearing Buddhist carvings and early inscriptions, evidence of the valley's pre-Islamic history when it lay along a major Silk Road route connecting Central Asia with the Indian subcontinent.

Entry to the heritage area is controlled by a small community office near the main entrance. A local guide — available for a modest fee — is well worth hiring, as many of the most significant structures are unmarked and easy to miss. The best light for photography falls on the village in the morning hours.
