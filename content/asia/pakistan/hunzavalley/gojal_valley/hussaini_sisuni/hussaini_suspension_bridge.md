A long pedestrian bridge of spaced wooden planks strung on a pair of steel cables, crossing the Hunza River below where it widens out toward Borit Lake. From a distance it looks like a length of wire suspended over a brown gorge; up close it sways and creaks underfoot, and the gaps between planks are easily wide enough to see the river through.

It is a working footbridge for villagers on the western bank, not a tourist installation. There is no safety harness, no admission fee, and no railings beyond a single hand cable. Most visitors walk a few metres out, take a photograph, and walk back; a few cross the full length to the small farms on the other side. Wind picks up in the afternoon and the bridge moves noticeably — go in the morning if the swaying bothers you.
