The fort known as Pacco Qillo (the Solid Fort) was built in 1768 by Ghulam Shah Kalhoro and sits on a limestone hill above the old city. For most of its history it dominated the riverfront, but the Indus has moved east over the centuries and the fort now overlooks the modern city rather than the water. The fortifications contain a mosque, several graves of the Kalhora rulers, and sections of the original ramparts.

Access is straightforward and the fort is one of Hyderabad's most frequently visited historical sites. The views from the battlements over the city and across to the Indus plain are the main reward. The structure has been incompletely restored, and some sections are in poor condition, but the overall sense of the place — its setting, scale, and historical weight — comes through. The old city's badgir-topped rooftops spread out below.
