A short reading list to get a feel for Islamabad and Pakistan before you arrive.

**Moth Smoke**, Mohsin Hamid. The novelist's first book, set in Lahore but instantly recognisable to anyone who knows the urban Pakistani upper middle class. Sharp, dark, very readable.

**A Case of Exploding Mangoes**, Mohammed Hanif. A blackly comic novel about the 1988 assassination of General Zia ul-Haq in a C-130 crash — the event that ended Pakistan's longest military dictatorship and indirectly created the political setting Islamabad still operates in.

**Pakistan: A Hard Country**, Anatol Lieven. The most clear-eyed single-volume account of contemporary Pakistan written by an outsider — a British academic who travelled widely and listened carefully. Good background for understanding the politics you will hear discussed every evening in the city.

**The Reluctant Fundamentalist**, Mohsin Hamid. A short, taut novel about a young Pakistani's disillusion with America after 9/11. Set mostly in New York, but Islamabad and Lahore feature in the closing chapters.

**Behind the Beautiful Forevers**, Katherine Boo. Set in Mumbai not Islamabad, but the closest English-language account of the way modern South Asian cities really work — useful context for understanding the social geography of any Pakistani capital.
