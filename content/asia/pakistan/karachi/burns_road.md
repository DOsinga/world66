Burns Road is Karachi's most celebrated food street, a dense concentration of restaurants and street stalls in the Kharadar area that has been feeding the city for generations. The specialities are the slow-cooked dishes of old Delhi and Mughal-influenced cooking — haleem (a thick stew of lentils, wheat, and meat), nihari (braised beef or lamb in rich spiced broth), biryani in the Karachi style, and the various kebabs and breads that together constitute the cuisine of northern South Asian Muslim communities. Many of the restaurants have been run by the same families since the 1950s.

The street comes fully alive after 9 pm and continues until 2 or 3 am — eating late is standard practice in Karachi, particularly in summer when the heat makes earlier outdoor dining uncomfortable. The atmosphere is unpretentious and very local: plastic chairs, strip lighting, proprietors who have been doing this for decades and have no interest in explaining the menu in English. Most dishes are ordered by pointing at what someone else is eating. The stretch between the main junction and Bohri Bazaar is the densest part of the food street; the nihari shops toward the northern end are particularly well regarded.
