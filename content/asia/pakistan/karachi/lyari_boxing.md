Lyari is a densely populated neighbourhood west of the old city, one of the most economically deprived areas of Karachi and also one of its most culturally distinct. The neighbourhood has a long history as a home for workers from Balochistan and the Makran coast, and it has developed its own cultural identity — including a disproportionate contribution to Pakistani boxing, music, and football. Several amateur boxing clubs operate here, and the tradition of serious boxing training in Lyari has produced professional fighters who have competed at the Commonwealth and Asian Games level.

Visiting Lyari as a foreign tourist is not straightforward — the neighbourhood has experienced serious security problems in the past, and independent exploration is not recommended without local guidance. Karachi-based NGOs and journalists regularly work in the area, and arranging a visit through a local contact gives access to a community that offers a completely different perspective on the city from the Clifton or DHA experience. The contrast between Lyari's boxing gyms, its murals, its music culture, and its extreme poverty is one of the most striking things about a city that specialises in extreme contrasts.
