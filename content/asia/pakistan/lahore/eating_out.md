---
title: Eating Out
type: section
---

Lahore has one of the great food cultures of the subcontinent — a city where eating well is a serious civic activity, and where what you order matters more than where you sit. The Punjabi tradition leans heavily on grilled and slow-cooked meats: **siri paya** (slow-cooked head and trotters, eaten at dawn), **nihari** (a slow-stewed beef curry), **karahi** (chicken or mutton in a wok with tomato and ginger), and the seekh kabab.

For traditional food, the hubs are **Lakshmi Chowk** (karahi central), the **Food Street at Heera Mandi** (rooftop dining with views of the Badshahi Mosque), and **Gawalmandi Food Street** in the Walled City. For more cosmopolitan modern cooking — Chinese, Italian, fusion — head to **M.M. Alam Road** in Gulberg.

Lahori breakfast (halwa puri, paya, lassi) is famous; tea-houses on the Mall still serve cardamom chai with parathas. Vegetarian options exist but are limited — Punjabi food is unapologetically meat-heavy.
