Satpara Lake is a natural glacial lake about 8 kilometres south of Skardu at 2,636 metres elevation, set among arid mountains with the blue-green water characteristic of high-altitude Karakoram lakes. The Satpara Dam, completed in 2011, has raised the water level somewhat from the natural baseline, creating a larger reservoir while preserving much of the scenic character.

The lake road passes through a landscape that becomes increasingly dramatic as you climb from the Skardu valley floor, with views back over the wide Indus valley. The lake itself sits in a bowl of eroded granite and scree, with the surrounding mountains reflecting in the still water on calm mornings. Boating is available; local boats can be hired for short trips across the lake.

Satpara is the main source of drinking water for Skardu and a popular escape for locals on weekends. The road continues beyond the lake towards Deosai National Park (accessible in summer), one of the world's highest plateaus and a habitat for Himalayan brown bears and snow leopards.

**Getting there:** The paved road from Skardu takes about 20 minutes; jeeps and local transport serve the route regularly.
