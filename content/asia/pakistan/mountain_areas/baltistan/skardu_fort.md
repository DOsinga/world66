Skardu Fort, known locally as Kharpocho (meaning "king of forts" in Balti), sits on a dramatic 120-metre granite rock above the town of Skardu, overlooking the wide Indus valley. The fort was built in the 16th century and served as the seat of the rulers of Baltistan through the Mughal period and into the Sikh conquest of 1840. The ruins include several towers, walls, and a small mosque; much of the stonework has survived in reasonable condition despite centuries without systematic maintenance.

The climb to the fort from the base takes about 30–45 minutes on steep rocky paths and requires reasonable fitness. The reward is one of the best panoramic viewpoints in Baltistan: from the top you can see the Skardu town and valley spread below, the Indus bending west, the Shigar River joining it from the north, and the distant Karakoram peaks when weather permits. The fort is most atmospheric in late afternoon light.

There is no entrance fee; local boys may offer to guide you up for a small tip. Allow 2 hours for the round trip including time at the top.
