---
title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

Multani food is heavy, sweet and very spicy. The local specialities are the famous **sohan halwa** — a dense, sticky semolina-and-ghee fudge studded with nuts and edible silver, sold by the kilo in tins — and **Multani sweets** generally: gajar halwa, gulab jamun, kheer. For savoury food, the city is best known for **chicken karahi** cooked in great open pans over coal fires, for **paya** (slow-cooked trotters served at breakfast), and for the **fish karahi** along the Chenab south of town. Cheap, plentiful **chaat** and **gol gappay** carts work the bazaars from late afternoon.

The Cantonment area has a row of more polished restaurants along **Bosan Road** and **Abdali Road**, mostly serving Punjabi standards plus the inevitable Chinese-Pakistani fusion. The walled city is where the interesting eating happens — especially around Chowk Bazaar and the Hussain Agahi food street, which gets going after sunset and stays loud well past midnight.
