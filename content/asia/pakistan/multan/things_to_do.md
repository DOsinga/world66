---
title: "Things to Do"
type: section
---

Multan is a city of shrines, mosques and bazaars, and almost everything worth seeing is concentrated in the small area in and around the old **Multan Fort**. The two great Suhrawardiyya shrines — **Shah Rukn-e-Alam** and **Bahauddin Zakariya** — sit side by side on the raised fort mound, their tiled domes visible from miles across the Punjab plain. Half a kilometre east, beyond the city wall, is the shrine of **Shah Shams Tabrez**. A walking circuit through these three, with stops at the half-buried **Sawi Mosque**, the abandoned **Prahladpuri Hindu Temple**, and the British-built **Clock Tower**, fills an absorbing day.

Beyond the fort, the **walled city** is entered through six surviving gates — Bohar, Pak, Haram, Delhi, Lohari and the now-vanished Daulat — and dissolves into a maze of bazaars: pottery and **Multani khussa** leather slippers in Chowk Bazaar, brass and copper in Hussain Agahi, dried fruit and **sohan halwa** sweets along the lane to the Phool Hatta Mosque. Multan is searingly hot from April through September; the rest of the year is the time to walk it.
