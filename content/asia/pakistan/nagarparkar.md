Nagarparkar sits at the far south-eastern edge of Sindh, where the Thar desert meets the pink granite of the Karoonjhar Hills and the flats of the Rann of Kutch. It is remote, dry, and not a polished sightseeing town. That is part of the point.

The reason to come is the cultural landscape around the town. Jain temples, Hindu shrines, a marble mosque, old water tanks, and hill paths are scattered through the granite country. The best of them are not grand in the way Lahore or Thatta are grand, but they feel startling here: carved domes and frescoed ceilings rising from a desert borderland that was once connected to Jain trade and pilgrimage routes.

Travel here takes planning. Distances are long, public transport is thin, and the heat is serious outside the cooler months. With a local driver or guide, though, Nagarparkar makes one of Sindh's more unusual heritage days: [Gori Temple](/asia/pakistan/nagarparkar/gori_temple), the [Bhodesar monuments](/asia/pakistan/nagarparkar/bhodesar_mosque_and_temples), the [bazaar Jain temple](/asia/pakistan/nagarparkar/nagarparkar_bazaar_temple), and the [Karoonjhar Hills](/asia/pakistan/nagarparkar/karoonjhar_hills) together give it enough weight to justify the journey.
