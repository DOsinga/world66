Sehwan, usually called Sehwan Sharif, is one of Sindh's great pilgrimage towns. It sits west of the Indus on the road between Hyderabad and northern Sindh, and its life turns around the shrine of Lal Shahbaz Qalandar, the 13th-century Sufi saint whose name draws pilgrims, musicians, malangs, families, and curious travellers from across Pakistan.

The town is old, hot, dusty, and not especially polished. That is part of the point. Around the [Shrine of Lal Shahbaz Qalandar](/asia/pakistan/sehwan/shrine_of_lal_shahbaz_qalandar), stalls sell garlands, sweets, incense, and green chadars through much of the day, while the Thursday dhamal turns the shrine precinct into a dense, drummed, devotional scene.

Sehwan rewards travellers who are interested in living religious culture more than tidy monuments. The [Sehwan Fort](/asia/pakistan/sehwan/sehwan_fort) is rough and eroded, the [Sehwan Museum](/asia/pakistan/sehwan/sehwan_museum) is modest, and the old bazaar is a working lane rather than a curated attraction. Together they give the town enough depth for a spare day, especially if you add [Manchar Lake](/asia/pakistan/sehwan/manchar_lake), a vast freshwater lake just outside town.
