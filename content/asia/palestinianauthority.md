The Palestinian Authority administers most of the West Bank, a landlocked territory between Israel and Jordan that holds some of the oldest and most layered cities in the world. For travellers willing to navigate the logistics — Israeli-controlled checkpoints, variable road access, and an uncertain security situation — this is a remarkably rewarding destination. The history is deep, the hospitality is genuine, and the food is excellent.

The main population centres are [Ramallah](/asia/palestinianauthority/ramallah), the de facto administrative capital; [Nablus](/asia/palestinianauthority/nablus), the commercial heart of the northern West Bank, famous for its old-city souq, knafeh pastry, and olive oil soap; [Jenin](/asia/palestinianauthority/jenin) in the fertile north, with its old Crusader church at Burqin; [Jericho](/asia/palestinianauthority/jerico), the lowest city on earth and one of the oldest, with its Neolithic tower and Umayyad palace; [Bethlehem](/asia/palestinianauthority/bethlehem), 10 km south of Jerusalem, drawing Christian pilgrims to the Church of the Nativity; and [Hebron](/asia/palestinianauthority/hebron), one of the most politically charged cities in the region, home to the Cave of the Patriarchs. The [Gaza Strip](/asia/palestinianauthority/gazastrip), technically separate from the West Bank Authority, lies on the Mediterranean coast; independent travel there is not currently feasible.

Most travellers enter via Israel — through Ben Gurion airport near Tel Aviv, or overland from Jordan via the Allenby Bridge crossing. A car or regular service taxis (sheruts) are the most practical way to move between Palestinian towns. Israeli rental cars are not permitted to enter Palestinian-controlled areas, so you'll either need to hire a Palestinian car and driver or use public transport. Jerusalem, from which most of these cities are accessible as day trips, makes a practical base.
