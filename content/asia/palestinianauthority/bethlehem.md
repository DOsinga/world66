Bethlehem is one of the most visited places in the Palestinian territories, drawing pilgrims and tourists who come to see the Church of the Nativity — built over the traditional site of Jesus's birth and one of the oldest continuously operating churches in the world. The church, with its fourth-century mosaics and the silver star marking the spot in the grotto below, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Beyond the Nativity Church, the town has the [Milk Grotto Chapel](/asia/palestinianauthority/bethlehem/milk_grotto_chapel), the Shepherd's Field, and a lively old market along [Star Street](/asia/palestinianauthority/bethlehem/star_street) in the centre. [Manger Square](/asia/palestinianauthority/bethlehem/manger_square), the main plaza in front of the church, is the focal point. [Banksy's Walled Off Hotel](/asia/palestinianauthority/bethlehem/walled_off_hotel) — a hotel, gallery, and political statement facing the separation wall — has become a major draw in its own right, as has the [street art on the wall](/asia/palestinianauthority/bethlehem/separation_wall_art) itself. The [Aida Refugee Camp](/asia/palestinianauthority/bethlehem/aida_camp) at the northern edge of town offers community tours that put the city's situation in broader context.

Bethlehem lies just 10 km south of [Jerusalem](/asia/israel/jerusalem) and is an easy day trip, though crossing the checkpoint at the separation wall can involve queues. It can also be visited from [Hebron](/asia/palestinianauthority/hebron) to the south.
