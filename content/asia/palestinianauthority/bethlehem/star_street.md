  Star Street follows the traditional route by which Christian pilgrims entered Bethlehem from Jerusalem, and its name refers to the star said to have led the Magi. The street was a working commercial thoroughfare for centuries; a major restoration project in the 2000s cleaned the stonework and converted several historic houses into cultural venues, though it remains an active residential neighbourhood rather than a tourist corridor.
---

Running from the north entrance of the old town down towards Manger Square, Star Street (Tariq an-Najma) is the main artery of Bethlehem's historic centre. The street is lined with Ottoman-era stone houses, churches, and small workshops — it gives a better sense of what the old town actually looks like day-to-day than the tourist-heavy square around the Nativity Church.

The Al-Badd Museum of Olive Oil Production on the street occupies a restored 14th-century building with the original olive press still in place. Several traditional craft workshops sell carved olive wood — the main local product and the souvenir most worth buying here.
