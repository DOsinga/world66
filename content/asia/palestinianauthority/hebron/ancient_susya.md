Ancient Susya is an archaeological site on the southern edge of the West Bank, with remains from a town occupied by Jewish and Muslim communities from the 3rd to the 14th centuries. Its best-known feature is a Byzantine-era synagogue with mosaic floors, partly reconstructed walls, and signs explaining the settlement's long history.

The site is easiest to visit by car. Public transport is limited, and the walk from the nearest road junction is long and exposed.
