The Russian Museum and Sycamore Tree site in Jericho brings together pilgrimage history, archaeology, and one of the city's most repeated biblical associations. The museum compound reflects the long Russian Orthodox presence in the Holy Land, while the sycamore tradition connects the area to the story of Zacchaeus.

It is a compact but layered stop, especially for visitors already seeing Tel es-Sultan, Hisham's Palace, and the monasteries above Wadi Qelt. The appeal is not spectacle; it is the way Jericho's religious, imperial, and archaeological histories overlap in a small area.
