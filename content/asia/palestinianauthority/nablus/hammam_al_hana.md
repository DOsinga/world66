Hammam al-Hana is one of the old bathhouses that help explain why Nablus feels so rooted in Ottoman and Levantine urban traditions. The city's hammams were not just places to wash; they were social spaces tied to markets, weddings, family life, and the rhythm of the old city.

Check current access locally, since historic hammams can change function or opening patterns. Even if you only see the exterior, it belongs in a walk through Nablus's souq lanes, alongside soap factories, sweet shops, mosques, and stone houses.
