Bougainville is the largest island in the Solomon Islands chain, though politically it belongs to [Papua New Guinea](/asia/papuanewguinea) as an autonomous region. It is a lush, mountainous island with dense tropical rainforest, active volcanoes, and a history as complex and painful as anywhere in the Pacific.

The island was devastated by a civil war from 1988 to 1998, triggered by disputes over the [Panguna copper mine](/asia/papuanewguinea/bougainville/panguna_mine) — one of the largest open-pit mines in the world, which poisoned the island's river systems and paid almost nothing to local landowners. The mine remains closed; the island is still recovering. Bougainville voted 98% in favour of independence in a 2019 referendum, and the political future continues to unfold.

Visiting Bougainville requires planning. Infrastructure is basic, accommodation limited, and travel is mostly by small boat or on rough roads. But for those who make the effort, the island offers pristine reefs, WWII wreck sites including the [Yamamoto crash site](/asia/papuanewguinea/bougainville/yamamoto_crash_site) near Buin, and a glimpse of a society rebuilding itself on its own terms. [Buka](/asia/papuanewguinea/bougainville/buka_town), the main town on the smaller northern island, is the usual arrival point by air from Port Moresby.
