Goroka is the capital of Papua New Guinea's Eastern Highlands, sitting around 1,600 metres above sea level in coffee country. The climate is cooler than the coast, the mountains press close, and the town is one of the more manageable bases for travellers trying to understand the highlands without going immediately remote.

Its name travels far because of the [Goroka Show](/asia/papuanewguinea/goroka/goroka_show), the great September sing-sing where groups from across Papua New Guinea gather in feathers, paint, shells, drums, and masks. If you can time a trip for the show, Goroka becomes one of the strongest cultural destinations in the Pacific. Outside festival week, the town is quieter but still useful: the [J.K. McCarthy Museum](/asia/papuanewguinea/goroka/jk_mccarthy_museum) gives context for highland culture and wartime history, while guided visits to the [Asaro Mudmen](/asia/papuanewguinea/goroka/asaro_mudmen) villages explain one of PNG's most recognisable images.

Goroka is not an easy independent-travel town in the European sense. Security, transport, and village etiquette all require care, and many worthwhile outings are best arranged through a local guide or your accommodation. That is part of the point: this is a place where you need introductions, patience, and respect, and where the reward is a much clearer sense of the highlands than you get from a quick airport transfer.
