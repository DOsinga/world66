A protected area covering much of inland Malay and the neighbouring municipality of Buruanga, the Northwest Panay Peninsula Natural Park is one of the most important pieces of remaining lowland rainforest in the Western Visayas. It supports unusually high levels of endemism, including the critically endangered **Visayan warty pig** (Sus cebifrons) and the **rufous-headed hornbill** (Rhabdotorrhinus waldeni).

Visiting requires effort — the park has no developed tourist infrastructure, and serious birdwatchers usually book a guide through the **Philippines Biodiversity Conservation Foundation**, which runs research and conservation projects in the area. Day visits to the forest edge from Caticlan are easier, with sightings of common forest birds and butterflies almost guaranteed.
