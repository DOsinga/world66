Alaminos City in [Pangasinan](/asia/philippines/pangasinan) province is the gateway to the [Hundred Islands National Park](/asia/philippines/alaminos/hundred_islands_national_park), a scattering of 123 limestone islands and islets in the Lingayen Gulf. Most visitors come for the island-hopping — you hire a bangka outrigger boat from [Lucap Wharf](/asia/philippines/alaminos/lucap_wharf) and spend the day snorkelling, kayaking and scrambling up to viewpoints over the surrounding gulf.

The three main islands — [Governor Island](/asia/philippines/alaminos/governor_island), [Quezon Island](/asia/philippines/alaminos/quezon_island) and Children's Island — have basic facilities and can get crowded on Philippine school holidays. The real appeal is pushing past them to the quieter islands where you can have a beach entirely to yourself. The park was established in 1940, one of the first national parks in the Philippines, and the mushroom-shaped karst formations are genuinely dramatic.

The city itself is a practical base rather than a destination. The [Lucap seafood market](/asia/philippines/alaminos/lucap_seafood_market) near the wharf serves excellent fresh fish at low prices — stop here before or after your island trip. The [Alaminos Cathedral](/asia/philippines/alaminos/alaminos_cathedral) at the city centre is worth a brief look if you have time. Otherwise, Alaminos exists to get you to the water.
