The Pamintuan Mansion is one of the oldest surviving structures in Angeles City, built by the Pamintuan family in the 1880s during the Spanish colonial era. The house represents the architecture of the principalia, the wealthy Filipino landed class who occupied a privileged position between the Spanish colonial administration and the general population. Its solid stone construction and generous proportions speak to the family's status in Pampanga society.

The mansion has witnessed several chapters of Philippine history, from the Philippine Revolution against Spain to the subsequent American colonial period. It stands today as a reminder of the city's roots as a prosperous market town long before the American air base arrived and transformed it into something else entirely. The building is located in the older part of the city, near the historic center, and can be visited on foot from the main plaza.
