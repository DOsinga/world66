Mayon Volcano is the dominant fact of life in Legazpi. At 2,462 metres, it rises from near sea level with almost mathematical regularity to a near-perfect cone, and it is visible — on clear mornings especially — from anywhere in the city and from aircraft approaching from Manila. It is the most active volcano in the Philippines and one of the most active in the world, with a persistent plume and intermittent eruptions that have shaped the landscape and culture of Albay province for centuries.

The best views are from the Legazpi Boulevard waterfront at dawn, from Lignon Hill west of the city, and from the open rice paddies on the approach roads from Daraga. ATV tours operate from the lava fields at the volcano's base, taking riders across hardened lava flows to viewpoints within a few kilometres of the summit. During periods of low volcanic activity, trekking to the crater is possible with a registered guide — check current alert levels, as eruptions are frequent enough that the permit system exists for real reasons.
