Divisoria Market runs along the streets adjacent to the plaza and into a covered wet market section where the serious buying happens. The produce section carries the full range of Northern Mindanao agriculture: durian from Davao, pineapples from Bukidnon, a wide variety of root vegetables, and fresh fish landed at the bay. Prices are lower here than anywhere else in the city. The market is at its most crowded in the early morning before eight, when the fresh deliveries come in.

The street food is the main reason to come. Vendors along the outer perimeter sell grilled corn, caramelized banana-cue, barbecued pork and chicken on sticks, and steaming bowls of lugaw. There are also stalls doing kinilaw — raw fish cured in vinegar and ginger, the local version of ceviche — which is among the freshest and cheapest you'll find in the city. The covered interior gets hot by midday, so morning is the better time to visit. Navigation requires some patience; the layout is organic rather than logical, but the food rewards the effort.
