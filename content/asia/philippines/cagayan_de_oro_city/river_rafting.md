The Cagayan River drops out of the Bukidnon highlands and runs through the city before emptying into Macajalar Bay. The stretch used for rafting covers roughly 14 kilometers and takes between two and three hours depending on water levels. The upper section has the most technical rapids — Class 3 and 4 when the water is high after rain — while the lower section calms down to Class 1–2 and is suitable for families and first-timers. Most outfitters offer a combined package that runs both sections in a single day.

Outfitters cluster around the Cagayan de Oro Rafting Association grounds near Barangay Mambuaya, upstream from the city center. Gear — helmets, life jackets, paddles, and a safety kayaker — is included in the standard fee, which runs around 800–1,200 pesos per person. The best time to raft is during the wetter months from July to October when water levels push the rapids higher; in the dry months the lower section becomes fairly tame. The sport is well-organized by Philippine standards, with licensed guides and mandatory safety briefings.
