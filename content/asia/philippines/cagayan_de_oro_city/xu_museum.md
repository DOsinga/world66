The Xavier University Museum sits inside the campus of Xavier University, a Jesuit institution that has been in CDO since 1933. The museum is small by national standards but well-curated, covering the ethnographic diversity of Mindanao in more depth than you'd expect from a university collection. The highlights are the indigenous artifacts from the Higaonon, Talaandig, and other Lumad groups of the region: woven cloth, brass work, agricultural tools, and ceremonial objects that document cultures rarely represented in mainstream Philippine museums.

The historical sections cover the pre-colonial period, the Spanish era, and the American colonial period with a reasonable level of detail. There's a particular emphasis on Northern Mindanao's role as a meeting point between Islamized lowland communities, indigenous highland peoples, and later Christian settler populations — a complexity that the museum handles with more nuance than the usual Philippine historical narrative. Entry fees are modest. The campus itself is pleasant to walk through, with older Spanish-era buildings alongside the modern facilities. Check opening hours in advance as the museum sometimes closes during semester breaks.
