In 1871 a volcanic eruption caused a coastal cemetery near Catarman to sink beneath the sea. A large white cross erected in 1982 now marks the site above water. At low tide coral-encrusted tombstones are visible about six metres down, and the site is one of Camiguin's most popular dive spots — the combination of marine life and submerged headstones is genuinely eerie.

Snorkellers can see the upper graves; scuba divers descend to the deeper sections where the most intact structures remain. The reef that has grown over the cemetery is rich, and the juxtaposition of bright coral fish weaving through carved stone crosses gives the site an atmosphere unlike any other dive in the Philippines. Bancas from nearby resorts make the short crossing; the site is marked by a surface buoy.
