Mandaue is a highly urbanised city in the province of Cebu, forming part of Metro Cebu — the second-largest conurbation in the Philippines. It occupies a coastal strip on the eastern side of Cebu Island, facing Mactan Island across the Mactan Channel. Two bridges connect the cities: the older Mactan–Mandaue Bridge and the newer [Marcelo Fernan Bridge](/asia/philippines/cebu/mandaue_city/marcelo_fernan_bridge), an extradosed cable-stayed structure opened in 1999.

The city is predominantly industrial and commercial — furniture manufacturing, rattan goods, and food processing are its economic backbone — and it lacks the tourist infrastructure of nearby Cebu City or Lapu-Lapu. For travellers, the main reason to stop is the old town centre, where the [Mandaue Presidencia](/asia/philippines/cebu/mandaue_city/mandaue_presidencia) (a well-preserved American-period civic building) and the [Mandaue Church](/asia/philippines/cebu/mandaue_city/mandaue_church) (a National Shrine founded in 1599 by the Augustinians) stand side by side on the historic plaza.

Mandaue shares a land border with Cebu City to the south and west, and with Consolacion to the north. Mactan-Cebu International Airport, on Mactan Island, is about 10 minutes by car across the Marcelo Fernan Bridge.
