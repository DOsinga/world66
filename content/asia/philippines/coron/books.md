title: Books
type: section
---

There's surprisingly little written specifically about the Calamian Islands, but a few books help fill in the context.

**Ghost Fleet: The Sinking of the Imperial Japanese Navy** by Andrew McKenzie is one of the few detailed accounts of the September 1944 raid that produced the wrecks Coron is now famous for, drawing on US Navy after-action reports and Japanese survivors' accounts. Essential reading if you plan to dive the wrecks.

**Playing with Water: Passion and Solitude on a Philippine Island** by James Hamilton-Paterson is one of the finest pieces of contemporary writing about the Philippines — a memoir of a year spent on a small uninhabited island off the coast of Marinduque. Not Coron, but the landscape and rhythms it describes will be instantly familiar.

**Among the Mangroves** by Charlie Walker is a short, beautifully written travelogue across Palawan including time spent on Coron and Culion. It gives a good sense of how rapidly the area is changing under tourism.

**In Our Image: America's Empire in the Philippines** by Stanley Karnow is the standard history of the American period — the era during which the leper colony at Culion (a short boat ride from Coron) became the largest in the world.
