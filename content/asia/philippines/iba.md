Iba is the quiet capital of Zambales province, a small coastal town about 200 km northwest of Manila along the South China Sea. It is not a famous destination, but Filipinos know it for its 12 km of clean, undeveloped grey-sand beaches, its clear, unpolluted waters, and the prized **Dinamulag mangoes** that ripen in April and May — some of the sweetest in the country.

The town centre clusters around the colonial-era Saint Augustine Cathedral and the Zambales Provincial Capitol, a handsome building that anchors what little civic grandeur Iba possesses. Beyond the government buildings and a handful of markets, the real draw is the coast: a string of low-key beach resorts stretching along Barangay Bangantalinga, where the South China Sea laps at grey sand with barely a building higher than a coconut palm. The Zambales mountain range rises steeply to the east, and on clear days the silhouette of the Sierra Madre closes the inland view.

Iba has a relaxed, unhurried pace even by Philippine provincial standards. There are no big malls, no traffic jams, and no tourist hordes. The traveller who comes here is usually after one of three things: a quiet stretch of beach within day-trip range of Manila, fresh mango and seafood, or a jumping-off point for the wilder beaches of northern Zambales. All three are easily satisfied. Come in the dry season (November to May) for the beaches, and aim for the week-long **Paynauen Festival** in late April if you want street dancing, sand-castle contests, and the mango harvest at its peak.
