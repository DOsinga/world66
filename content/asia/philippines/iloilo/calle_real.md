story: Calle Real was the main commercial street of colonial Iloilo, and the row of grand buildings along it — built by sugar and rice merchants between the 1880s and 1920s — represents the most intact collection of American colonial-era commercial architecture in the Philippines. The buildings were constructed when Iloilo was the second-largest city in the Philippines and one of the wealthiest in Southeast Asia, with more sugar and rice exports passing through its port than any other Philippine city.
---

Calle Real — now officially General Luna Street — runs through the old Iloilo City commercial centre and retains a remarkable row of early 20th-century commercial buildings from the American colonial period. The two- and three-storey structures have ornate facades in Neoclassical and Art Nouveau styles, their arched windows and decorative stonework still largely intact despite decades of neglect. Several have been restored and house restaurants, boutiques, and cultural spaces.

The street is the core of the effort to position Iloilo as a heritage tourism destination, and ongoing restoration work has made it significantly more presentable than it was a decade ago. Walking the full length of the remaining heritage block takes about 20 minutes and gives a vivid sense of how prosperous the city was in its sugar export heyday. The area connects naturally to the Iloilo River esplanade a few streets away.
