  Calle Real — now officially General Luna Street — was the commercial spine of colonial Iloilo during its sugar export heyday in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. At its peak, Iloilo was the second largest city in the Philippines and one of the wealthiest ports in Southeast Asia, processing more rice and sugar than any other Philippine city. The buildings along Calle Real were financed by that wealth and represent the ambitions of an era that ended abruptly with the shift of trade to Manila.

Calle Real runs through the old commercial centre of Iloilo City and retains a remarkable stretch of early 20th-century commercial buildings from the American colonial period. The two- and three-storey structures, built by sugar and rice merchants between the 1880s and 1920s, feature ornate Neoclassical and Art Nouveau facades with arched windows, decorative stonework, and ironwork balconies.

Several buildings have been restored and now house restaurants, boutiques, and cultural spaces; others remain faded but intact, conveying the original scale of the street's ambition. Walking the full heritage block takes about 20 minutes. The area sits a few streets from the Iloilo River esplanade, making it easy to combine both into a morning's walk through the historic centre.
