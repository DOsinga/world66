title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

Kalinga food is rice-based and meat-heavy. The defining dish is **pinuneg** — a black blood sausage made with rice, ginger, and pork, sliced and pan-fried. **Pinikpikan** (literally "the beaten one") is the local chicken stew, controversial elsewhere because the bird is beaten with a stick before slaughter to bring blood to the surface and darken the meat; it is the centrepiece of feast occasions. The simpler everyday version is **inasin**, salted and grilled pork or freshwater fish from the Chico.

For drinks, **tapuy** (rice wine) and **basi** (sugarcane wine) are made in most villages. The mountain coffee — small-batch arabica from the surrounding hills — is increasingly good and worth seeking out at the cafés in Tabuk.

In the small villages, food comes with whatever accommodation you arrange — homestays normally include three substantial meals.
