Koronadal City — known to nearly everyone as Marbel — is the capital of South Cotabato province in the Soccsksargen region of Mindanao, southern Philippines. It sits at the foothills of the Roxas and Quezon mountain ranges, roughly 50 kilometres north of General Santos City, at an elevation that keeps it a few degrees cooler than the coast. The name Koronadal comes from the B'laan language and means "place of cogon grass" — a reminder that this fertile valley was once open grassland before it was settled.

The city is primarily a regional administrative and commercial hub, but that underestimates its appeal. Marbel Public Market pulses with produce from the surrounding farms — pineapples, asparagus, and palm oil are the big export crops — and the food stalls around it are some of the liveliest in Mindanao. The city has a notably diverse population: descendants of Visayan settlers brought in under the National Land Settlement Administration programme of the 1940s and 50s live alongside B'laan and T'boli indigenous communities, and the resulting food and culture reflects that mix.

The annual Tinalak Festival each July is the city's biggest event, celebrating the t'nalak cloth woven by the B'laan people — a sacred fabric made from abaca fibre, with geometric patterns that each weaver receives in dreams. It is one of the most visually striking indigenous textiles in the Philippines. Beyond the festival, Koronadal is best understood as a gateway: Lake Sebu and its Seven Falls are an hour's drive into the highlands, and General Santos City is easily reached for onward connections.

The city is typhoon-free, which sets it apart from much of the Philippines and makes it a reliable destination year-round.
