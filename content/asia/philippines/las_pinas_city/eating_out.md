Las Piñas's eating options are typical of Metro Manila's southern cities — a mix of Filipino home cooking restaurants, fast-food chains, and the inevitable mall food courts. There is no specific Las Piñas cuisine, but Filipino food is generally good value and the city has plenty of neighbourhood eateries serving the full range of national dishes: adobo, sinigang, kare-kare, and lechon.

The SM City Southmall on the Alabang–Zapote Road has the widest concentration of restaurants for visitors in one place, from Filipino chains like Jollibee and Max's Restaurant to Chinese dim sum and fast food. The BF Resort Village area, a planned residential district in the eastern part of the city, has a cluster of more neighbourhood-oriented restaurants and cafes.

For a genuinely local experience, the Las Piñas Public Market has cooked food stalls alongside the wet market, serving cheap Filipino plates at breakfast and lunch. Turo-turo (point-point) canteens — where you point at the dishes you want — are the most affordable and authentic way to eat in this part of Manila.
