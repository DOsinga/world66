Lechon is the undisputed centrepiece of Filipino festive cooking: a whole pig roasted slowly on a bamboo spit over charcoal until the skin crackles and the meat falls away from the bone. Every fiesta, every birthday party, every significant occasion in the Philippines involves lechon, and the sight of it being carried shoulder-high by a cook through a restaurant is an unmistakable signal that something is being celebrated.

In Manila, the best lechon comes from hole-in-the-wall shops in areas like Tondo and Cavite that specialize in it. La Lola Manila in BGC and Pepita's Kitchen in various locations serve upscale versions; Lydia's Lechon is a reliable chain. The Cebu style (herb-stuffed, no sauce needed) is considered the gold standard; the Manila version typically comes with liver sauce. If you are in Manila for a week and don't eat lechon at least once, you have missed something important.
