The jeepney is the most distinctively Filipino thing you will see in Manila. Descended from American military jeeps left behind after World War II, they were lengthened, decorated with chrome horses and patron saints, painted in vivid colours, and turned into shared minibuses that have run Manila's streets ever since. Each jeepney is individually owned and decorated — no two are exactly alike — and they function as both transport and expression of Filipino popular visual culture.

Riding one costs almost nothing, the routes are painted on the sides (though memorising them takes local knowledge), and the experience — squeezed in with twelve other people, listening to the radio, watching Manila go past the open sides — is one of the most authentic ways to experience the city. The traditional gasoline jeepney is being slowly phased out in favour of modern electric versions as of 2023, so the classic version is worth seeking while it still exists. Routes serving the historic centre are most useful for visitors.
