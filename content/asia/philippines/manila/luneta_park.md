Luneta, officially Rizal Park, is the symbolic centre of the Philippines. The vast open park at the edge of Intramuros and Manila Bay was the site of José Rizal's execution in 1896, and the monument to him — a tall column topped with his statue, flanked by bronze lions — marks the spot. The park is where Manila celebrates national holidays, gathers for events, and simply hangs out on weekends.

The 58-hectare park contains multiple formal garden sections, an orchidarium, a Chinese garden, a Japanese garden, and a geological survey of the Philippines done in a relief map that shows the archipelago's topography. The oceanarium and National Museum complex border the park. Weekend mornings bring out joggers, tai chi practitioners, kite flyers, and families — a cross-section of Manila life in a way few other public spaces offer. The park is safe during daylight hours.
