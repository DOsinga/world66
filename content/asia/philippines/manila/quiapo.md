Quiapo is the commercial and religious heart of old Manila, centred on the Minor Basilica of the Black Nazarene on Plaza Miranda. The church houses the most venerated religious image in the Philippines — a dark wood statue brought from Mexico in 1606 — and draws constant pilgrims. The plaza and surrounding streets are packed at almost all hours with vendors, jeepneys, and commerce of every variety.

The neighbourhood is an experience in Manila's full intensity: the herbal medicine vendors in front of the church selling folk remedies alongside candles and religious figurines; the crowded Quiapo market stretching north with fabrics, electronics, and everything else; the smell of incense and frying food; the omnipresent chaos of traffic. [San Sebastian Church](/asia/philippines/manila/san_sebastian_church) — the steel church — is a short walk away. Visit in the daytime; the area quiets down and becomes less safe after dark.
