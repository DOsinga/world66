José Rizal remains the most important figure in Philippine history — the polymath doctor, novelist, sculptor, and ophthalmologist whose two novels are credited with igniting Filipino nationalism against Spanish rule. His life and death are commemorated in a trail that connects the key sites in Manila: the Rizal Shrine in Fort Santiago, where he spent his last night and wrote his final poem "Mi Último Adiós" (My Last Farewell); the route he walked at dawn on December 30, 1896; and the Luneta execution site marked by the monument and the bronze footprints embedded in the pavement.

A visit to Fort Santiago and Rizal Park together takes half a day if done properly. The Rizal Shrine within Fort Santiago holds Rizal's personal effects, letters, and the shoes he wore at his execution. The monument in Luneta contains his remains. A modest, well-curated biography of Rizal makes the historical weight of these sites legible; the more you know about him, the more moving the locations become.
