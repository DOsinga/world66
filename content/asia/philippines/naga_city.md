Naga City sits in the Bicol Region of Luzon, about 440 km south of Manila, and is the cultural and commercial heart of the Camarines Sur province. It's a lively mid-sized city with a strong university presence and a distinct Bicolano identity — you'll notice the local cuisine here, hotter than most Filipino food, built on coconut milk and chillies.

The city's defining event is the **Peñafrancia Festival** every September, one of the largest Marian celebrations in Asia. A massive fluvial procession carries the image of Our Lady of Peñafrancia along the [Naga River](/asia/philippines/naga_city/naga_river), with hundreds of boats and crowds estimated in the millions lining the banks. The [Peñafrancia Basilica](/asia/philippines/naga_city/penafrancia_basilica) is the image's home for most of the year; it stays at the [Naga Metropolitan Cathedral](/asia/philippines/naga_city/naga_cathedral) for the nine-day novena before the fluvial return. The [Porta Mariae](/asia/philippines/naga_city/porta_mariae) arch near the cathedral marks the traditional festival route.

Outside of festival season, Naga is a comfortable base for the Bicol region. The Caramoan Peninsula, known for limestone islands and beaches made famous by various *Survivor* productions, is the most popular day trip; Mayon Volcano is two hours south. Bicol food — Bicol Express, laing, pinangat — deserves serious attention: it's the most distinctive regional cuisine in the Philippines.
