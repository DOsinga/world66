Silay City sits 14 kilometres north of Bacolod on the island of Negros, and is the best-preserved sugar-baron town in the Visayas. Known as the "Paris of Negros" in its heyday, the city retains more than thirty heritage houses from the late nineteenth and early twentieth century — grand wooden and stone mansions built on sugar wealth, several of which are now open to visitors as museums.

The [Balay Negrense](/asia/philippines/negrosisland/silay/balay_negrense) is the centrepiece — a 1897 mansion with capiz-shell windows and period furniture that gives the most vivid picture of how the sugar barons actually lived. The [Bernardino Jalandoni Museum](/asia/philippines/negrosisland/silay/bernardino_jalandoni_museum) is a quieter alternative, with a literary collection alongside the historic furnishings. For art, the [Hofileña Ancestral House](/asia/philippines/negrosisland/silay/hofilena_ancestral_house) holds an extraordinary private collection of nineteenth-century Philippine painting — works by Luna, Amorsolo, and Hidalgo that would be at home in a national museum. The [San Diego Pro-Cathedral](/asia/philippines/negrosisland/silay/san_diego_pro_cathedral) anchors the town plaza and has been the spiritual centre of the community since the sixteenth century.

A walk along Rizal Street ties these together. Stop at the [Cesar Lacson Locsin Ancestral House](/asia/philippines/negrosisland/silay/cesar_lacson_locsin_ancestral_house) for pastries from El Ideal Bakery before you set out — this century-old institution is as much a part of the Silay experience as the museums. The town has a quiet, old-world atmosphere that makes a pleasant contrast to Bacolod's bustle, and it is close to the Silay-Talisay airport that serves the wider Negros Occidental region.
