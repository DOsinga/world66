The Balay Negrense is the finest ancestral house museum in Silay and one of the best-preserved examples of late nineteenth-century Filipino domestic architecture in the Visayas. Built in 1897 for sugar baron Victor Fernandez Gaston, the two-storey house combines native hardwoods (molave, narra) with capiz shell windows that diffuse the tropical light in a warm, amber glow.

The interior has been restored to its original state, complete with period furniture, capiz-shell chandeliers, and photographs of the Gaston family at the height of the sugar boom. The kitchen, in particular, gives a vivid sense of the scale of a prosperous nineteenth-century Negrense household. Entry includes a guided tour from staff who are knowledgeable about the family history. The house sits in a garden with heritage trees; allow an hour for a proper visit.
