title: Heritage Cafes Along Rizal Street
type: poi

A handful of small cafes have opened along and near Rizal Street in recent years, tucked into the ground floors of heritage buildings or in gardens behind ancestral houses. They cater to the day-trip crowd from Bacolod and serve the standard Filipino cafe menu — brewed coffee, fruit shakes, light rice meals, and an obligatory selection of local pastries. The setting, more than the coffee itself, is the draw: ceiling fans, wooden floors, and the relative quiet of a city that has not been overrun by tourism.

The best time to settle in is mid-morning, after the museum heat sets in and before the tricycle back to Bacolod. Barako coffee (the native Philippine robusta variety, stronger and more bitter than commercial blends) is sometimes available and worth ordering if you see it on the menu. Pair it with a napoleones from the next-door pastry counter and you have a better mid-morning break than anything on offer in Bacolod's malls.

Specific cafe names and hours shift year to year as businesses open and close, so ask your accommodation in Bacolod for current recommendations before setting out.
