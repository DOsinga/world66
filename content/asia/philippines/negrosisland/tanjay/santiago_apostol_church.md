The Santiago Apóstol Church, dedicated to Saint James the Greater, stands on the north side of the central plaza and is the reason most people pause in Tanjay. The thick stone walls and low, fortress-like proportions are typical of Spanish-era churches in the central Visayas, designed to double as refuges during the long centuries of coastal raiding. The yellow-painted facade and bell tower are the result of nineteenth- and twentieth-century renovations on top of the older colonial structure.

The interior is modest — whitewashed walls, a simple retablo, wooden pews — but the building's presence on the square is what matters. In the early evening locals gather on the steps and across the plaza, and the bells still ring the hours for a town that has grown up around the church for nearly five hundred years.
