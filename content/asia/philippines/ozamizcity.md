Ozamiz City sits at the western tip of Misamis Occidental province on the shores of Panguil Bay in northern Mindanao, where the bay narrows to a strait separating Misamis from Lanao del Norte. The city grew from the Spanish settlement of Misamis, established around 1756 and important enough in the 18th and early 19th centuries to be called the most significant town in northern Mindanao. That history is still visible: the [Fuerte de la Concepción y del Triunfo](/asia/philippines/ozamizcity/fuerte_de_la_concepcion), a coral-and-sandstone fort built that year to repel Moro pirates, stands largely intact on the southern waterfront.

Modern Ozamiz is the most populous city in Misamis Occidental, with around 144,000 people, and it functions as a regional hub for commerce, education, and the Catholic church — it is the seat of an archdiocese whose [Metropolitan Cathedral](/asia/philippines/ozamizcity/metropolitan_cathedral) was designed by Leandro Locsin, the National Artist for Architecture. The cathedral houses the only pipe organ in Mindanao, which tells you something about the city's quiet ambitions.

The pace here is provincial, in the best sense. The [city market](/asia/philippines/ozamizcity/ozamiz_city_market) runs on seafood from Panguil Bay, tricycles outnumber cars in many streets, and the [bayfront promenade](/asia/philippines/ozamizcity/panguil_bay_promenade) is where people go to watch the evening light drop behind the mountains of Lanao del Norte. The [Port of Ozamiz](/asia/philippines/ozamizcity/port_of_ozamiz) at the southern end of the waterfront has been moving people and goods around the southern Philippines for centuries, and still does.

The city changed significantly in September 2024 when the Panguil Bay Bridge opened — a 3.77-kilometre crossing that reduced the journey to Lanao del Norte from a two-and-a-half-hour ferry trip to a short drive. For the traveller, Ozamiz now makes more sense as a base for exploring both sides of the bay.
