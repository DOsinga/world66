---
title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

Pagadian cooks Cebuano-Filipino food with a coastal slant — grilled tuna belly, *kinilaw* (raw fish cured in vinegar and calamansi), and *sinigang na isda* (sour fish soup) are everywhere. The cheapest and best meals are at the *carinderia* stalls in and around the [public market](/asia/philippines/pagadiancity/public_market). For a sit-down dinner, head to the strip of restaurants along Rizal Avenue and around the rotonda, where mid-range Filipino chains share space with local seafood houses.

Don't leave without trying *kakanin* — sticky rice cakes wrapped in banana leaves — sold at little stands on every corner in the morning. *Suman* and *puto* are reliable picks, and a cup of strong sweet coffee from the same stall goes well with them.
