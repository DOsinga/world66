---
title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

Palawan's food scene is dominated by **seafood** — the daily catch is excellent and very fresh — and by the usual Filipino staples: **adobo**, **sinigang** (a sour tamarind soup), **kinilaw** (the local ceviche, often with tuna), and grilled chicken inasal. The signature regional dish is **tamilok**, the woodworm of the local mangroves, served raw with vinegar — a delicacy in the strict sense (genuinely good if you can get past the idea) which you will be repeatedly offered in any half-decent Puerto Princesa restaurant.

In El Nido and Coron, expect the menus to broaden in proportion to the tourist crowds: more pasta, more pizza, the inevitable banana pancake, but also a surprising number of genuinely good international restaurants opened by long-term expats. Most places open from about 11 a.m. through to 10 p.m.; smaller carinderias close in the early afternoon.
