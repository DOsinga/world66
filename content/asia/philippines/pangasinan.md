Pangasinan is one of Luzon's most rewarding provinces if you like your beach trips mixed with old churches, fishing towns, and long provincial roads. The famous draw is [Alaminos](/asia/philippines/alaminos), where bangka boats fan out into Hundred Islands National Park, but the province is bigger and more varied than that one postcard view.

The west is the rougher, prettier side: Bolinao has [Patar Beach](/asia/philippines/pangasinan/patar_beach), caves, waterfalls, and the [Cape Bolinao Lighthouse](/asia/philippines/pangasinan/cape_bolinao_lighthouse) watching over the South China Sea. Around Lingayen the mood changes to broad gulf beaches, war history, and the grand [Pangasinan Provincial Capitol](/asia/philippines/pangasinan/pangasinan_provincial_capitol). Inland, [Manaoag Church](/asia/philippines/pangasinan/manaoag_church) is one of the Philippines' busiest Catholic pilgrimage sites.

Pangasinan is not slick. Public transport can be slow, towns sprawl, and the best beaches usually take patience to reach. That is part of the point. Come for the islands, stay for grilled bangus, salt farms, quiet coves, and a province that still feels more local than packaged.
