---
title: "Cultural Center of the Philippines"
type: poi
---

The Cultural Center of the Philippines (CCP) was built during the Marcos era and opened in 1969, designed by National Artist Leandro Locsin in a modernist brutalist style that remains striking today. The complex sits on reclaimed land along the Manila Bay waterfront and serves as the country's premier venue for the performing arts — the main theatre hosts opera, ballet, symphony concerts, and theatrical productions by the country's top companies.

The CCP grounds are expansive and pleasant to walk through, especially in the late afternoon when the light hits the bay. Adjacent to the main building is the Folk Arts Theater, and nearby stands the Coconut Palace, a Marcos-era guest house built largely from coconut products and intended for a 1981 papal visit that Pope John Paul II declined to use. Today the Coconut Palace operates as a museum and event venue, and can be visited on a guided tour.

The entire CCP complex area, including the bayside promenade, is one of the calmest and most atmospheric parts of Pasay — a contrast to the busier Mall of Asia area a short distance up the bay. Performance schedules are listed on the CCP's website, and tickets for major productions sell out well in advance.
