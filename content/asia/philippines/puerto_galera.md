Puerto Galera sits on the northeastern tip of Mindoro Island, tucked into one of the finest natural harbours in the Philippines. The bay is genuinely beautiful — deep, calm, and ringed by forested hills — and it was recognised as a Man and Biosphere Reserve by UNESCO in the 1970s. Most visitors arrive by ferry from Batangas on the Luzon mainland, a crossing of roughly ninety minutes, and the town's Muelle Port is where the boats dock. The name "Puerto Galera" translates roughly as "Port of Galleons," a nod to the Spanish colonial ships that sheltered here centuries ago.

The area most visitors know is not really a single place but a cluster of coastal settlements spread along the shoreline east and west of town. [Sabang](/asia/philippines/puerto_galera/sabang_beach), about fifteen minutes by road from the pier, is the diving hub — a compact, lively strip of beach with dive shops, bars, and restaurants that stay busy well into the evening. [White Beach](/asia/philippines/puerto_galera/white_beach), west of town in the other direction, has a longer stretch of sand, calmer energy, and a more family-oriented crowd.

The main reason serious divers travel to Puerto Galera is the [Verde Island Passage](/asia/philippines/puerto_galera/verde_island_passage), the strait between Mindoro and the Luzon mainland. Scientists have identified it as one of the most biodiverse marine environments on earth — the "centre of the centre of marine shorefish biodiversity," in the words of one landmark study. The diving ranges from gentle coral gardens suitable for beginners to dramatic walls, strong drift dives, and pelagic encounters for the more experienced. Verde Island itself, accessible by banca boat in 30–40 minutes from Sabang, has a famous drop-off that ranks among the best dives in Southeast Asia.

The [town itself](/asia/philippines/puerto_galera/puerto_galera_town) is easy to overlook but worth an hour of your time. The waterfront around Muelle Port has a quiet, working character — fishing boats, a local market, a colonial-era church on the hill above the bay — that is a welcome contrast to the busier beach scenes nearby. Puerto Galera is popular year-round with Manila residents looking for a quick escape, so it never entirely empties out, but outside the main holiday weekends the pace is genuinely relaxed.
