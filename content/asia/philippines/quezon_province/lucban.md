Lucban is a small town in Quezon Province, tucked into the foothills of [Mount Banahaw](/asia/philippines/quezon_province/lucban/mount_banahaw) on the island of Luzon. It is best known for the Pahiyas Festival, held every 15 May in honour of San Isidro Labrador, when houses along the main street are decorated with colourful rice wafers called kiping and displays of fruit, vegetables, and handicrafts. The festival, which centres on [Lucban Church](/asia/philippines/quezon_province/lucban/lucban_church), draws visitors from across the Philippines and is one of the most visually spectacular fiestas in the country.

Outside festival season, Lucban is a quiet agricultural town with good longganisa (local sausage) and a pleasant old town centre. The hillside shrine of [Kamay ni Hesus](/asia/philippines/quezon_province/lucban/kamay_ni_hesus), with its 50-metre statue of the risen Christ, draws weekly bus-loads of pilgrims, and Mount Banahaw — considered a sacred mountain — offers hiking trails for those with a permit.
