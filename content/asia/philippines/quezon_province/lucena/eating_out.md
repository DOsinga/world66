---
title: Eating Out
type: section
---

Lucena's food scene is anchored in provincial pride. The city claims two dishes you won't find quite the same anywhere else: pancit habhab, egg noodles stir-fried with vegetables and pork and served on a banana leaf to be eaten without utensils, and longganisa Lucena, a sweet, garlicky fresh sausage that appears at breakfast stalls all over town. Both are available from street vendors near the public markets and at the cluster of carinderias (casual canteens) around the Grand Central Terminal.

The central public market is the best place to graze — stalls inside sell grilled seafood caught that morning in Tayabas Bay, alongside fresh coconut products and vinegar-pickled vegetables. For a sit-down meal, the commercial strip along Enverga Avenue has a range of Filipino-Chinese restaurants serving the kind of hearty rice-and-ulam meals that fuel truck drivers and market traders alike.
