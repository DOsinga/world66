San Pablo City in Laguna province, around 100km south of Manila, is known as the "City of Seven Lakes" — seven volcanic crater lakes lie within and around the city, the largest being [Sampaloc Lake](/asia/philippines/sanpablocity/sampaloc_lake) at 104 hectares. Bamboo rafting on the twin lakes of [Pandin and Yambo](/asia/philippines/sanpablocity/pandin_yambo_lakes), cycling around Sampaloc, and visiting lakeside restaurants built over the water are the main draws.

The city is an agricultural centre for the wider Laguna region, with coconut — the dominant crop — at the heart of its economy and food culture. [Villa Escudero](/asia/philippines/sanpablocity/villa_escudero), a large coconut plantation and heritage museum south of the city, preserves material from the Philippine struggle for independence and attracts domestic tourists. The [AERA Memorial Museum](/asia/philippines/sanpablocity/aera_memorial_museum) has a substantial collection of religious artefacts, wartime exhibits, and natural history. The [San Pablo Cathedral](/asia/philippines/sanpablocity/san_pablo_cathedral) anchors the city plaza, surrounded by street food vendors and a lively evening scene.

San Pablo is a practical base for exploring southern Luzon: Mount Banahaw, an active volcano and popular pilgrimage site, is to the southeast; Anilao on the Batangas coast, one of the Philippines' best diving destinations, is around 55km to the south.
