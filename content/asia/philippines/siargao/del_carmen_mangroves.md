Del Carmen contains one of the largest and most intact mangrove forests in the Philippines, covering over 6,000 hectares in the northwestern part of Siargao. The municipality has protected and replanted its mangroves for decades, and the result is a dense, labyrinthine ecosystem that provides habitat for hundreds of species of birds, fish, crustaceans, and reptiles.

The easiest way to explore is by kayak or outrigger boat along the mangrove channels. The same boats that take visitors to Sugba Lagoon pass through sections of the forest. Firefly watching after dark is a memorable experience — the mangroves fill with synchronised flashing lights that are genuinely beautiful. Early morning birdwatching is equally rewarding, with kingfishers, herons, and various wading birds common. Del Carmen is about 25 km northwest of General Luna.
