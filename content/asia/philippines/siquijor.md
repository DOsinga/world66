Siquijor is the third-smallest province in the Philippines, an island of around 340 square kilometres in the Visayas region, encircled by coral reefs and largely carpeted in forest. For centuries it was known to Filipinos as "the island of fire" — early Spanish sailors reportedly saw it glowing with bioluminescent plankton in the sea around it — and its reputation for sorcery, faith healers, and traditional healing rituals persists, giving the island an atmosphere distinct from the beach-resort routine of its neighbours.

In practice, Siquijor is a small, quiet island with good diving and snorkelling, a few fine beaches, and an unhurried pace that many travellers prefer to the more developed islands nearby. The dive sites around the island range from shallow reef gardens to steep walls with good visibility; the most popular spots are around San Juan on the west coast and the reefs off Paliton Beach. The Cantabon Cave, a short bike ride from Loquon, requires a short swim through a river running underground and is one of the more unusual cave experiences in the Visayas.

The island's roads form a rough circuit of about 70 kilometres, easily covered by motorbike in a day. The interior is hilly and forested, with old balete trees draped in Spanish moss and the small waterfall at Cambugahay providing a popular swimming stop. The towns are quiet — the capital Siquijor town, the ferry port of Larena, and the laid-back beach municipality of San Juan — and the general absence of mass tourism makes the whole island feel like the Philippines of 20 years ago.

Siquijor is best reached by ferry from Dumaguete (Negros island) in about 45 minutes, or from Bohol or Cebu. Given how small it is, two to three days is enough to see most of the island; a week passes very pleasantly for those who come to dive or simply decompress.
