Siquijor's reputation as an island of sorcery and healing is both genuine and sometimes overdone. The island does have a living tradition of folk healers — called mananambal or mangkukulam — who use herbal remedies, prayers, and ritual to treat ailments ranging from physical illness to bad luck and lovesickness. This tradition has deep roots in pre-colonial animist practices that survived the Spanish conversion period by merging with Catholic forms.

The most significant gathering happens at Easter, when healers from across the island (and from neighbouring islands) converge at the Lazi convent to prepare their annual stock of remedies and exchange knowledge. For the rest of the year, individual healers see patients by appointment in their homes, often in the island's interior villages. Seeking out a healer is a personal decision; they are not difficult to find with the help of guesthouse owners, and the encounter is invariably courteous and unhurried. Expect a blend of herbal knowledge, prayer, and practical advice.
