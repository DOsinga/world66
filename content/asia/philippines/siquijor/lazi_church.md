The Church of San Isidro Labrador in Lazi, along with its massive adjacent convent, is the most architecturally significant historic building on Siquijor. The church was built in the late 18th and early 19th centuries by Augustinian friars from coral stone quarried on the island; the result is a solid, thick-walled structure with a belltower and a facade that has survived earthquakes and typhoons for over two centuries. The convent alongside is one of the largest in Asia — a sprawling complex of arched corridors and heavy timber-floored rooms that now functions as a heritage museum.

The complex is most striking at dawn or dusk, when the coral stone takes on a warm ochre colour. Lazi town itself is worth a slow wander: old Spanish-era houses line the streets near the church, and the town retains more historical atmosphere than the more tourist-oriented west coast. The nearby Lazi Convent Museum (inside the convent building) displays religious artefacts, old photographs, and traditional textiles collected from the surrounding municipalities.
