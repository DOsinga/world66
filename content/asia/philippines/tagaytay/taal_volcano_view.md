The view from the Tagaytay Ridge of Taal Volcano is one of the most dramatic in Southeast Asia. Below the highway the land falls steeply to the shores of Taal Lake, a vast crater lake occupying the remains of an ancient volcanic caldera. In the centre of the lake rises Volcano Island, its flanks still scarred from the eruption of January 2020, and on the island sits a second crater lake — a geological stacking of lake on island on lake on island that is genuinely unusual.

The view is best early in the morning, before clouds build up over the water. Most viewpoints along the Tagaytay Ridge highway offer roughly the same panorama; the area near the People's Park in the Sky and the various cliffside restaurants give the most unobstructed views. Sunrise visits are worth the alarm call — the combination of morning mist over the lake and the silhouette of the volcano against pink sky is one of those images that actually matches the postcard.

Since the 2020 eruption, access to Volcano Island itself has been restricted; check current PHIVOLCS advisories before planning any descent to the lakeshore at Talisay for a boat crossing. Even from the ridge, the spectacle is complete.
