title: Things to Do
type: section
---

Tarlac City itself is short on big sights, but the surrounding province makes for rewarding day trips. The [Capas National Shrine](/asia/philippines/tarlac/capas_national_shrine), half an hour north, is the biggest single attraction — a hilltop memorial to the tens of thousands of Filipino and American soldiers who died at the end of the Bataan Death March in 1942. Closer to town, the [Aquino Center](/asia/philippines/tarlac/aquino_center) in Hacienda Luisita is a small museum dedicated to Ninoy and Cory Aquino, founded on the family estate where Cory grew up. The [Museo ng Tarlac](/asia/philippines/tarlac/museo_de_tarlac) on Romulo Boulevard covers the province's full history from pre-colonial Aeta culture through the American period — free admission, well worth an hour.

The [Monasterio de Tarlac](/asia/philippines/tarlac/monasterio_de_tarlac) on Mount Resurrection at San Jose is the other set-piece — a Catholic shrine with a giant statue of the Risen Christ overlooking the plain, reached by a winding mountain road. [San Sebastian Cathedral](/asia/philippines/tarlac/tarlac_cathedral) anchors the [Plaza Maniago](/asia/philippines/tarlac/plaza_maniago) in the town centre. Tarlac's Spanish-era churches in the smaller towns of Camiling and Paniqui are also worth a stop if you are driving through.
