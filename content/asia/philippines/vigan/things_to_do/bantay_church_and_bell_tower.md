---
title: "Bantay Church and Bell Tower"
type: poi
---

The Saint Augustine Church at Bantay is one of the oldest churches in the Philippines, founded by Augustinian missionaries in 1590. It stands on a low hill just north of central Vigan — a massive, fortress-like structure of brick and coral stone, its thick walls and narrow windows a reminder that colonial churches in the Philippines were built to serve as refuges as much as places of worship.

Across the road, separated from the church itself, is the Bantay Bell Tower — a four-storey watchtower that was used to raise alarms when Dutch and Chinese raiders threatened the town from the sea. The tower is open to climb, and the view from the top takes in the rooftops of Vigan, the Abra River, and on clear days the mountains of the Cordillera to the east. It is one of the better vantage points in the area and well worth the short walk from the heritage zone.

The combination of church and detached bell tower is a feature found at several Ilocos churches — the towers were positioned to give better sightlines for coastal defence. Bantay is the most accessible example near Vigan, and the surrounding neighbourhood retains a quiet, working-town feel that contrasts pleasantly with the tourist activity around Calle Crisologo.
