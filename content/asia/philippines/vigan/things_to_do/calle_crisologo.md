---
title: "Calle Crisologo"
type: poi
---

Calle Crisologo is the cobblestone spine of Vigan's UNESCO World Heritage zone — a narrow street of Spanish colonial mansions stretching several hundred metres through the old quarter. The two-storey bahay na bato houses that line it were built by Spanish colonists and Ilocano ilustrado families in the 18th and 19th centuries: their lower floors are stone and brick, their upper floors timber, and their windows are fitted with sliding panels of translucent capiz shell that diffuse the light into a warm amber glow.

The street is closed to motor vehicles for much of the day, and horse-drawn kalesas fill the vacuum, ferrying tourists between the mansions at a pace that matches the colonial-era atmosphere. Many of the ground-floor shops sell local crafts — abel Ilocos cloth, burnay pottery, vinegar and baguio beans — and some of the houses have been converted into heritage inns and restaurants.

Come in the morning if you can. The light is better, the crowds are thinner, and the smell of Vigan longganisa frying at the breakfast stalls along the side streets is reason enough to be up early. At night the whole street is lit by lanterns, and the atmosphere shifts from picturesque to genuinely romantic.
