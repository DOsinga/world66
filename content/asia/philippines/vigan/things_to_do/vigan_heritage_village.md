---
title: "Vigan Heritage Village"
type: poi
---

The UNESCO World Heritage Site at Vigan is not just Calle Crisologo but the broader historic town centre — a planned Spanish colonial grid of streets that has survived four centuries of earthquakes, typhoons, and war largely intact. The heritage zone covers several blocks of bahay na bato mansions, churches, government buildings, and plazas, making it the most complete surviving example of a Spanish colonial town in Asia.

What makes Vigan unusual is the hybridisation of architectural styles. The buildings are not purely Spanish but a fusion: Spanish floor plans and arcaded ground floors combined with Chinese construction techniques (the wealthy Fukien merchants who built many of them brought their own methods) and Filipino adaptations to the tropical climate. The result is something found nowhere else — a coherent urban streetscape that is identifiably colonial but distinctly its own thing.

Wandering beyond Calle Crisologo into the quieter streets of the heritage zone is worthwhile. The side lanes are less polished, some of the houses are in various states of romantic decay, and you get a better sense of the town as a living community rather than a theme park. The area around Plaza Burgos and the cathedral is particularly good in the late afternoon.
