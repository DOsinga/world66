Anawangin Cove is the classic first-timer's destination in Zambales — accessible only by a 30-minute boat ride from the village of Pundaquit in San Antonio municipality, it delivers one of the most striking beach scenes in the Philippines. The crescent of dark volcanic ash-grey sand is backed by a forest of agoho (ironwood) pine trees, their needles casting thin shadows, with steep green hills rising behind them. There is no resort, no permanent structure — just tents on the beach and a freshwater stream that feeds a small lagoon behind the treeline.

The boat ride from Pundaquit takes around 20–30 minutes and passes the volcanic coast heading north. The cove has no electricity and no running water beyond the stream. Most visitors come for a one or two-night camp, carrying in their own food and water, sleeping to the sound of the South China Sea. The best time to visit is between October and June — July through September the open sea can turn rough.
