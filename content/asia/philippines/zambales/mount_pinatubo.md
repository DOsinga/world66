  When Pinatubo erupted on 15 June 1991, it was the second-largest volcanic eruption of the 20th century. The blast ejected 10 cubic kilometres of magma, lowered global temperatures by 0.5°C for two years, and killed over 700 people. The mountain itself collapsed by around 260 metres. The crater that formed has since filled with turquoise lake water, one of the most spectacular examples of post-eruption transformation anywhere on earth.

The hike to Mount Pinatubo's crater lake is arguably the best day trek in the Philippines. The trail starts from the village of Santa Juliana in Capas, Tarlac — just east of Zambales province — and follows a 4WD jeep track through a vast lahar plain, a grey-white moonscape of volcanic deposits that still stretches across the landscape more than three decades after the 1991 eruption. The lahar field is disorienting in its scale: you are walking through material that once buried entire towns.

The lake itself — Lake Pinatubo — sits at the summit in the collapsed caldera, its water a vivid turquoise-green that changes colour slightly depending on the light. The hike takes 2–3 hours each way on foot from the jump-off point, or about 45 minutes by 4WD jeep to the trailhead and then an hour on foot to the crater rim. The trek requires a permit and a registered guide arranged through the Capas Eco-Tourism Office. The recommended months are November through May — the trail is dangerous and sometimes closed during typhoon season.
