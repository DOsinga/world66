Al-Ahsa (also Al-Hasa) in the Eastern Province is the world's largest oasis — a sprawling complex of date palm gardens, ancient forts, and historic towns fed by natural springs in the middle of the desert. It was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2018, a recognition of both its agricultural landscape and its 7,000 years of continuous human settlement.

The historic heart is Hofuf, the main city, where [Qasr Ibrahim (Ibrahim Palace)](/asia/saudiarabia/alhasa/qasr_ibrahim) and the [Souq Al Qaisariya](/asia/saudiarabia/alhasa/souq_al_qaisariya) define the Ottoman-era old town. Al-Ahsa produces some of the finest dates in Saudi Arabia — the Khalas variety in particular is prized across the Gulf — and the market is the best place to sample the range.

Beyond the city, the [Jabal Qarah Caves](/asia/saudiarabia/alhasa/jabal_qarah) are a sandstone mountain riddled with natural passages that stay cool year-round in defiance of the desert heat. [Jawatha Mosque](/asia/saudiarabia/alhasa/jawatha_mosque), 12 km north of Hofuf, is among the oldest mosques in Islam, dating to the early 7th century CE. Al-Asfar Lake, a glimmering freshwater body about 40 minutes northeast of the city, attracts migratory birds and offers desert scenery of a different kind. Al-Ahsa is an easy drive from [Dammam](/asia/saudiarabia/dammam) or [Khobar](/asia/saudiarabia/khobar), about 80 km to the southwest.
