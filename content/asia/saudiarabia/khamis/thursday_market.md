The Thursday market remains one of the most authentic traditional markets in Saudi Arabia. Unlike the souqs of Riyadh or Jeddah that have become largely tourist attractions, Khamis operates as a genuine commercial gathering where farmers from the surrounding mountains sell fresh produce alongside vendors of traditional Asiri goods.

The market sprawls across a large area and divides roughly into sections: livestock in one corner, fresh fruit and vegetables in another, with spices, honey, textiles, silver jewellery, and traditional implements distributed through the remainder. Asiri honey — produced from the wild thyme and other mountain flowers in the surrounding hills — is among the best in Arabia and commands high prices both locally and when exported.

Silver jewellery is another highlight: Asiri craftsmen produce heavy silver pieces in regional styles distinct from jewellery made elsewhere in the country. Old pieces with traditional designs are increasingly scarce as demand from collectors has grown, but new work in traditional patterns is still made and sold here. The market is most active from mid-morning until early afternoon on Thursdays, and winds down by late afternoon.
