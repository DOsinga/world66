The Abraj al-Bait complex (officially the Makkah Royal Clock Tower Hotel) is a group of seven skyscraper hotels immediately adjacent to the Grand Mosque, built between 2004 and 2012 as part of the Saudi government's ongoing expansion of Mecca's pilgrimage infrastructure. The central tower, at 601 metres, is topped by the largest clock faces in the world; the towers house thousands of hotel rooms and serve as landmarks orienting pilgrims to the Kaaba.

The complex is controversial for several reasons: its demolition of the Ajyad Fortress and the neighbourhood that stood here, its visual dominance over the Grand Mosque's skyline, and what critics see as the commercial homogenisation of the area around Islam's holiest site. The hotels charge premium rates during Hajj and are largely patronised by wealthy pilgrims. For most pilgrims staying in government-assigned accommodation, the towers are visible landmarks that help with orientation in an otherwise disorienting city.
