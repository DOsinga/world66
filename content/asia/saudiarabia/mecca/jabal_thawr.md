Jabal Thawr is a hill about 4 kilometres south of Mecca where, according to Islamic tradition, the Prophet Muhammad and his companion Abu Bakr hid in a cave for three days during the Hijra — the migration from Mecca to Medina in 622 CE — while Qurayshi fighters searched for them. The cave at the summit is known as the Cave of Thawr and is visited by pilgrims, though it is less frequented than the Cave of Hira on Jabal al-Nour.

The climb to the summit is steep and takes around 45 minutes. The cave is small and the view from the top extends over the southern outskirts of Mecca. The traditional account holds that a spider spun a web over the cave entrance and pigeons nested there, which persuaded the searchers that no one could be hiding inside. The site has historical significance as the beginning of the Islamic calendar: the Hijra marks year 1 of the Muslim calendar.
