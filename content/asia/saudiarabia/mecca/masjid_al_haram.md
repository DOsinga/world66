Masjid al-Haram — the Grand Mosque — is the largest mosque in the world and the holiest site in Islam. It surrounds the Kaaba, the cube-shaped structure that Muslims face during prayer worldwide and which is the symbolic centre of the Islamic faith. The mosque grounds are open around the clock, every day of the year, and accommodate millions of worshippers during the Hajj season.

The mosque as it exists today is the result of centuries of expansion, with the largest phases occurring under the Saudi dynasty. The structure encompasses the Kaaba, the Zamzam Well, the Maqam Ibrahim (Station of Abraham), and the hills of Safa and Marwa between which pilgrims perform the ritual sa'i. The white marble floor and the air-conditioning systems — a recent addition to handle the enormous crowds — are part of the continuous modernisation of the site.

Access is restricted to Muslims. This is enforced by checkpoints on every approach road and is a legal requirement under Saudi law.
