Mount Arafat, known also as the Mount of Mercy (Jabal ar-Rahmah), is a granite hill about 20 kilometres east of the Grand Mosque in Mecca. It stands approximately 70 metres above the surrounding plain of Arafat, which accommodates the pilgrims who gather here during Hajj.

The plain of Arafat is the site of the central ritual of Hajj: the wuquf, or Standing, on the 9th day of Dhul Hijjah. Millions of pilgrims gather here from noon to sunset in prayer. The atmosphere during wuquf is described by those who have experienced it as one of extraordinary collective spiritual intensity — a moment when pilgrims feel the boundaries between themselves and the millions gathered around them dissolve. After sunset, pilgrims move to Muzdalifah for overnight prayers before returning toward Mecca for the remaining rituals.

The hill itself has a small mosque at its summit and steps leading up from the plain. It is accessible year-round, not only during Hajj, though the area is busiest during the pilgrimage season.
