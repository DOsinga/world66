  The Zamzam Well is described in Islamic tradition as the well that appeared when Hajar (Hagar), wife of Ibrahim (Abraham), desperately searched for water for her infant son Ishmael in the desert. The baby's heel struck the ground and water sprang forth. The well is approximately 30 metres deep and draws from an aquifer fed by a combination of local rainfall and water percolating through the surrounding rock. Scientific analysis has found the water has elevated mineral content compared to standard groundwater. Saudi authorities distribute Zamzam water to pilgrims and export it worldwide.
---

The Zamzam Well is located within the Masjid al-Haram, a few metres from the Kaaba. It has been in continuous use for thousands of years and is considered sacred by Muslims. Pilgrims drink Zamzam water during both Hajj and Umrah, and it is dispensed at water stations throughout the mosque precincts. Many pilgrims carry containers of Zamzam water home to family and friends.

The well was originally open and accessible; it is now enclosed within a basement facility fitted with modern pumps and filtration systems that supply the mosque's extensive network of water stations. The original head of the well can be seen through a glass window in the basement area. The taste of Zamzam water is slightly mineral — distinctive enough that regular pilgrims recognise it immediately.
