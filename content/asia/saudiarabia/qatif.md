Qatif is one of the oldest continuously inhabited places in the Eastern Province, with a history of settlement going back several thousand years. It sits on the Arabian Gulf coast north of [Khobar](/asia/saudiarabia/khobar) and has historically been a centre of pearl diving and trade. The mound on which the old fortifications stood has been inhabited since the Dilmun civilisation, and the city remained a key port through the Portuguese, Ottoman, and Saudi eras.

The city is predominantly Shia Muslim, which makes it culturally distinct from much of the rest of Saudi Arabia — a distinction with a long and sometimes turbulent political history. It is a fairly low-key destination, not much visited by tourists, but worth a stop if you are exploring the Eastern Province. The main sights are [Tarout Castle](/asia/saudiarabia/qatif/tarout_castle) on the nearby island, the old market area in the city centre, and the evening [Qatif Corniche](/asia/saudiarabia/qatif/qatif_corniche) along the Arabian Gulf waterfront.
