story: >
  Souq al-Zal translates roughly as "the moving market" — a reference to its origins as a travelling auction where items passed rapidly from hand to hand. Traditionally it was the place where families sold household goods during hard times and buyers came to find bargains. The items for sale today still follow this logic: antique coffee pots, old silver jewellery, Bedouin daggers, embroidered robes, and an assortment of things that defy easy categorisation.
---

The most interesting traditional market in old Riyadh, a warren of stalls selling antiques, silver jewellery, Bedouin weapons, old coins, traditional textiles, and miscellaneous curios. Unlike the more tourist-oriented souqs, Souq al-Zal operates as a genuine auction and second-hand market used by Saudis, and prices are negotiated in Arabic. It helps to have some Arabic or a Saudi friend along, but even without either, it is worth walking through.

The market is liveliest in the mornings and on Thursday evenings. The surrounding streets of the Qasr al-Hukm District hold other traditional shops — gold dealers, spice merchants, cloth sellers — in a compact area that can be explored on foot in a couple of hours.
