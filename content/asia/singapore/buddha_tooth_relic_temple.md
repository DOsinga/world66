The Buddha Tooth Relic Temple anchors the southern end of Chinatown with a five-storey hall built in the style of the Tang dynasty and completed in 2007. Despite its traditional appearance it is a recent building, but its scale and decoration are genuine: the main hall holds a hundred Buddha statues, and the whole complex is richly gilded and carved.

The relic that gives the temple its name — said to be a tooth of the Buddha — is housed in a two-tonne gold stupa on the fourth floor, viewable through a window. The rooftop holds a peaceful garden with a large prayer wheel, and the basement has a small museum and a vegetarian canteen. Visit during the morning chanting for the fullest sense of it as a working temple rather than a sight.
