The coral lagoon at Pulau Hantu is the reason people make the effort to get out here. Snorkeling conditions are never tropical-postcard perfect — the Strait of Singapore is turbid, and visibility typically runs between two and five metres — but what you find once you're in is genuinely surprising. Brain corals, table corals, and staghorn corals crowd the shallower sections, and the fish life is richer than you'd expect this close to one of the world's busiest shipping lanes. Look for schools of damselfish, batfish hanging motionless in mid-water, and sea anemones hosting clownfish that seem entirely unbothered by the industrialised cityscape looming on the horizon.

The best time to visit is between November and March, during the northeast monsoon, when reduced swell and better water clarity make for the most rewarding snorkeling. Low tide on a calm morning is ideal: the drop in water level concentrates fish around the coral heads, and you can peer into rock pools along the seawall that reveal sea urchins, sea cucumbers, and small octopus tucked into crevices. April to October can be murkier due to boat traffic and monsoon runoff, though dedicated snorkelers still get their fix. Bring your own mask and fins — there is no equipment for hire on the island.

The seawall between the two islands is the focal point of most snorkeling. Work the western side first, where the lagoon is calmer and shallower, then drop down the eastern face if conditions allow. The coral is patchier there but the fish are bigger, and the deeper water sometimes turns up something unexpected: a sleeping nurse shark, a passing eagle ray, or a hawksbill turtle that has wandered in from the open strait.
