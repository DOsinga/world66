Fort Siloso occupies the western tip of Sentosa and preserves the last surviving coastal gun battery in Singapore. Built by the British in the 1880s to guard the western approaches to Keppel Harbour, the fort saw action in February 1942 when Japanese forces invaded from the north through Malaya — a direction the fixed guns could not cover. The battery surrendered without firing a shot seaward.

The site includes restored underground tunnels, original 15-inch guns, and a walk-through diorama depicting the fall of Singapore. It is managed as part of the Sentosa nature and heritage trail system and is one of the few genuinely historical sites on an island otherwise dominated by modern entertainment. Admission is free.

story: "The fort's big guns faced out to sea, but the Japanese came from the north. On 15 February 1942, Lieutenant-General Arthur Percival surrendered Singapore to General Yamashita at the Ford Factory in Bukit Timah — the largest surrender of British-led forces in history. Over 80,000 troops became prisoners of war."
