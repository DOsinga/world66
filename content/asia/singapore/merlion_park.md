The Merlion — a creature with a lion's head and a fish's body — is Singapore's national personification, invented by the tourism board in 1964 and now the most photographed object in the country. The eight-metre statue stands at the mouth of the Singapore River where it meets Marina Bay, spouting a continuous jet of water into the harbour.

The park around it is small but superbly placed, looking straight across the bay at Marina Bay Sands and the ArtScience Museum. It is almost always crowded with visitors lining up the perspective shot where they appear to catch the water in their hands or balance the statue on a finger. Come at dusk, when the towers across the water light up and the whole bay turns gold.
