Sri Mariamman is Singapore's oldest Hindu temple, founded in 1827 and standing — improbably and wonderfully — in the middle of Chinatown, its towering gopuram covered in brightly painted figures of gods and mythological creatures rising over the surrounding shophouses. It is dedicated to the goddess Mariamman, known for her power to cure disease.

The temple is a national monument and a working place of worship, busiest during the Theemithi firewalking festival each autumn, when devotees walk across a pit of burning coals in the temple courtyard. Inside, the ceilings and shrines are densely decorated, and the contrast of an active South Indian temple amid the Chinese quarter captures Singapore's religious layering better than almost anywhere else. Remove your shoes at the entrance.
