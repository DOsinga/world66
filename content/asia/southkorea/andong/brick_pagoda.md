The Seven-Story Brick Pagoda at the Beopheungsa Temple Site is the largest and oldest brick pagoda in Korea, dating to the Unified Silla period (roughly 8th century CE). It rises 17 metres in seven tapering tiers, built entirely from small handmade bricks in a style influenced by Tang Dynasty Chinese architecture. The pagoda is designated a Korean National Treasure.

The temple that once surrounded it has completely vanished, and the pagoda now stands in a small urban park, a striking anachronism among modern apartment buildings. Up close, you can see the individual bricks and the slightly different-coloured replacements installed during restoration. The site is a short walk or taxi ride from central Andong. A small information board explains the pagoda's construction and history.
