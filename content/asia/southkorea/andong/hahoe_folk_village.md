Hahoe Folk Village is a living testament to Korea's Joseon aristocratic culture, a UNESCO World Heritage Site where the Ryu clan has lived continuously since the 14th century. The village occupies a stunning natural setting — a peninsula formed by a sweeping loop of the Nakdong River, with forested Buyongdae Cliff on the opposite bank. About 170 traditional houses remain inhabited, including the grand tile-roofed mansions of the aristocratic Ryu family and the humbler thatched cottages of the commoners who once served them.

Walking through the village is an experience unlike any other heritage site in Korea. The residents actually live here, growing vegetables in garden plots between the old houses, and the layout follows centuries-old feng shui principles. The Buin-dang pavilion perched at the water's edge is particularly beautiful. Across the river at Buyongdae Cliff, you can see the entire village from above — take the short ferry crossing and climb the path for the view. Traditional mask dance performances (Hahoe Byeolsingut Talnori) are staged at the village on weekends.
