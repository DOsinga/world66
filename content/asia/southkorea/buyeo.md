Buyeo is the quietest of Korea's old capitals, and that is a large part of its charm. Known as Sabi when it served as the Baekje capital from 538 until the kingdom fell in 660, it has none of [Seoul](/asia/southkorea/seoul)'s scale or [Gyeongju](/asia/southkorea/gyeongju)'s tourist machinery. What it does have is a compact set of Baekje ruins, museums, tombs, and river walks that still feel connected to the landscape.

Several of Buyeo's core sites belong to the UNESCO-listed Baekje Historic Areas. [Busosanseong Fortress](/asia/southkorea/buyeo/busosanseong_fortress) covers the wooded hill above the Baengma River, with pavilions, shrines, and the cliff-edge Nakhwaam legend attached to the kingdom's final days. [Jeongnimsa Temple Site](/asia/southkorea/buyeo/jeongnimsa_temple_site), in town, preserves one of Korea's great early stone pagodas.

The [Buyeo National Museum](/asia/southkorea/buyeo/buyeo_national_museum) is essential, especially for the extraordinary gilt-bronze Baekje incense burner found nearby in 1993. [Gungnamji Pond](/asia/southkorea/buyeo/gungnamji_pond) adds a softer finish: lotus beds, paths, and a pavilion reflected in the water. Buyeo is not loud, but it is coherent, walkable, and unusually good at turning one spare day into a clear picture of Baekje Korea.
