Jeju Island (Jejudo) is South Korea's largest island, a volcanic landmass about 100 km off the southwest coast of the mainland. It is the country's most popular domestic holiday destination — particularly for honeymooners — and was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site for its volcanic landscape. The island has a unique history, dialect, architecture, and tradition that set it apart from the mainland.

[Hallasan](/asia/southkorea/chejuisland/hallasan), the extinct volcano at the centre of the island, rises to 1,950 metres and is South Korea's highest peak. The slopes pass through subtropical, temperate, and near-alpine vegetation zones as you climb. At the summit, the crater lake Baengnokdam (White Deer Lake) is surrounded by more than 300 smaller volcanic craters scattered across the island. The [Manjanggul lava tube](/asia/southkorea/chejuisland/manjanggul_cave) on the northeast coast is another geological highlight — one of the longest lava tubes in the world.

The [dol hareubang](/asia/southkorea/chejuisland/dol_hareubang) stone statues, the [haenyeo diving women](/asia/southkorea/chejuisland/haenyeo), and the [Jeju Olle Trail](/asia/southkorea/chejuisland/olle_trail) walking network are the cultural anchors. [Seongsan Ilchulbong](/asia/southkorea/chejuisland/seongsan_ilchulbong) on the east coast — a tuff cone rising from the sea — is the most dramatic viewpoint. [Seogwipo](/asia/southkorea/chejuisland/seogwipo), the island's second city on the south coast, is a calmer base and the starting point for the best coastal walks.

Jeju is an hour's flight south of [Seoul](/asia/southkorea/seoul) or an overnight ferry from [Busan](/asia/southkorea/pusan). A rental car is the most practical way to explore — the island is large and public transport between sights is slow.
