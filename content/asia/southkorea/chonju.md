Chonju (Jeonju) is one of South Korea's most rewarding cities for travelers — the birthplace of bibimbap, the country's most famous rice dish, and home to a beautifully preserved [Hanok Village](/asia/southkorea/chonju/jeonju_hanok_village) where hundreds of traditional tile-roofed houses have survived intact.

The [Hanok Village](/asia/southkorea/chonju/jeonju_hanok_village) sits in the heart of the old city, a rare pocket of pre-modern Korea where you can stay in a traditional guesthouse, eat well, and wander stone alleyways without feeling like you're in a theme park. The village is genuinely lived-in, which is what makes it work. Within the village, [Gyeonggijeon Shrine](/asia/southkorea/chonju/gyeonggijeon_shrine) houses the portrait of the dynasty's founder, and the red-brick [Jeondong Cathedral](/asia/southkorea/chonju/jeondong_cathedral) stands just across from the hanok rooftops in one of Korea's most striking architectural contrasts. Up the hill above the village, [Jaman Mural Village](/asia/southkorea/chonju/jaman_mural_village) covers an entire hillside neighbourhood in paintings.

Beyond the city itself, the region rewards exploration. About an hour east, Mt. Maisan is known for its twin rocky peaks and the extraordinary stone pagodas built in its valleys — especially striking in spring and autumn. Kumsansa (Gold Mountain) Temple, dating to the 6th century and housing a 12-metre-high golden Buddha, is one of the largest temple complexes in the country. Deogyu-san National Park and the ancient Mireuksa stone pagoda are both within reach for day trips. The [Jeonju National Museum](/asia/southkorea/chonju/jeonju_national_museum) holds the region's finest historical artefacts, with a strong collection of Goryeo celadon and Joseon-era royal objects.
