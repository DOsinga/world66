Gunsan is a west-coast port in North Jeolla, about an hour from [Jeonju](/asia/southkorea/chonju). It is not pretty in the usual Korean city way, but it has one of the country's clearest collections of early-20th-century colonial buildings: old banks, warehouses, a Japanese-style temple, and streets that still carry the mood of a trading port.

The city was important under Japanese rule and came through the Korean War with much of that fabric intact. That makes Gunsan a good stop if you want to understand Korea's modern history outside the big museums of Seoul. The modern history quarter around Jangmi-dong is compact enough to walk, with the museum, art spaces, and bakery culture all close together.

Gunsan also has a second identity offshore. The Gogunsan islands and Seonyudo give the city a summer beach and cycling escape, linked by bridges and backed by low hills. Come for the modern-history walk, then add the islands if the weather is kind.
