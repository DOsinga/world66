Cheomseongdae is a squat stone cylinder rising from a field near the city centre, and at first it does not look like much. Then you realise it has stood here since 632 CE, making it one of the oldest surviving astronomical observatories in Asia. Queen Seondeok of Silla — the first female monarch in Korean history — had it built to track the movements of the stars and calibrate the agricultural calendar.

The structure is compact, about 9 metres tall, but its survival is remarkable. It is made entirely of precisely fitted granite blocks with no mortar, yet it has withstood fourteen centuries of earthquakes, floods, and invasions intact. You cannot enter — the interior was originally accessed by ladder through a square opening in the middle section — but walking around the outside and considering the precision of its construction against the timeline is rewarding. The surrounding field, planted with flowers in season, gives the site a pleasant park-like atmosphere. Cheomseongdae is a five-minute walk from the main tumuli park at Daereungwon.
