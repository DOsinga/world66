story: "The pond was known as Anapji ('wild goose and duck pond') from medieval times until 1980, when excavations of the pond bed revealed thousands of Silla-era artefacts — ceramics, roof tiles, Buddhist figurines, games pieces, and a wooden document recording palace maintenance expenses from AD 674. The find confirmed that the site had been the Silla court's pleasure garden and banqueting venue, and the pond was renamed Wolji, 'moon pond', based on a Silla poem."

The most spectacular night-time sight in Gyeongju: a restored Silla palace garden with three ornamental islands in an artificial pond, the surviving pavilions reflected in the still water under lights. Originally built in AD 674 as an East Palace garden for banquets and royal recreation, the site was abandoned after the fall of Silla in 935 and gradually submerged until excavation and restoration in the 1970s and 1980s.

The park is good during the day but truly comes alive after dark, when the illuminated pavilions and their reflections in the pond create one of the most photogenic scenes in Korea. Admission around 3,000 won; open until 10pm.
