Girimsa is the most secluded of Gyeongju's major temples, sitting in a narrow valley on the western slopes of Hamwolsan Mountain about 15 kilometres from the city centre. It was founded in the seventh century and expanded into a large complex in 643 CE by the monk Wonhyo. Today it houses about 80 resident monks and functions as a serious meditation monastery.

The main hall, Daejeokgwangjeon, is an imposing Joseon-period structure with a painted ceiling and three large gilt Buddha figures. The surrounding buildings include a drum tower, a bell pavilion, and several smaller shrines scattered up the hillside. In autumn the maple trees that cover the valley slopes turn brilliant orange and red, and the temple is a favourite destination for Koreans escaping the city. The journey there — bus from central Gyeongju or taxi along a winding mountain road — is part of the experience.
