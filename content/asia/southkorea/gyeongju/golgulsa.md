Golgulsa is carved directly into a granite cliff at Hamwolsan Mountain, making it one of the most unusual temples in Korea. The main religious images are rock-cut rather than housed in buildings: twelve Buddhist shrines are cut into the cliff face and connected by stone paths, with a large relief carving of the Amitabha Buddha presiding over the whole complex from the highest point.

The temple also functions as the home of sunabi, a Korean martial art that draws on Buddhist meditation practices, acrobatic dance, and military techniques. Morning demonstrations take place in the courtyard in front of the cliff, and the temple offers sunabi practice programmes for overnight temple-stay visitors. Golgulsa draws a mix of pilgrims, martial arts practitioners, and visitors simply curious about its singular setting. It is about 20 kilometres from central Gyeongju — accessible by local bus or taxi.
