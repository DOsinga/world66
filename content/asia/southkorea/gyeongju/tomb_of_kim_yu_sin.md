The tomb of Kim Yu-sin, the Silla general who masterminded the military campaigns that unified the Korean peninsula in 668 AD and is revered as the greatest warrior in Korean history. The burial mound, decorated with carved stone figures of the twelve zodiac animals in warrior costume, is one of the finest examples of Silla tomb sculpture still in situ.

The tomb sits on a hillside northwest of the city centre with views across the Gyeongju basin. The carved stone figures — rat, ox, tiger, rabbit, and the rest — are unusually detailed and expressive. The site is free to enter and uncrowded.
