Jindo is an island county off the far southwest of [South Korea](/asia/southkorea), surrounded by tidal channels and smaller islets. It is best known for the [Jindo Sea Parting](/asia/southkorea/jindo/jindo_sea_parting), when the sea briefly retreats enough to expose a walkable route toward Modo Island. That event can feel half natural phenomenon, half village festival, and it gives Jindo a stronger identity than many quiet rural islands.

Outside festival dates, Jindo is a slow coastal place of ridges, fishing villages, and pockets of culture. [Ullimsanbang](/asia/southkorea/jindo/ullimsanbang) preserves the mountain studio of a Joseon-era painter, while the west coast road ends in island-filled sunset views at [Sebang Sunset Observatory](/asia/southkorea/jindo/sebang_sunset_observatory). Near the bridge to the mainland, [Jindo Tower](/asia/southkorea/jindo/jindo_tower) adds a clear view over the strait where Admiral Yi Sun-sin fought the Battle of Myeongnyang.

Jindo is not a quick add-on from Seoul. It makes most sense if you are already travelling through Jeollanam-do, Mokpo, or the south coast. With a car and a spare day, though, it has enough landscape, history, and local character to reward the detour.
