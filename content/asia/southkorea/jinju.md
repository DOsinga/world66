Jinju sits on the Namgang River in western South Gyeongsang, far enough from Seoul and Busan that many travellers miss it. That is a pity if you care about Korean history. The city is remembered above all for the sieges of Jinju during the Imjin War, when Korean defenders held off a much larger Japanese force in 1592 before the fortress fell the following year.

The old fortress is still the heart of the visit. [Jinju Fortress](/asia/southkorea/jinju/jinju_fortress) contains the riverside walls, [Chokseongnu Pavilion](/asia/southkorea/jinju/chokseongnu_pavilion), shrines, gates, and the [Jinju National Museum](/asia/southkorea/jinju/jinju_national_museum), whose collection focuses on the war and Korea-Japan contact. The river below gives the whole site a strong setting, especially after dark.

If you can time the trip for October, Jinju becomes much more than a historical stop. The [Namgang Yudeung Festival](/asia/southkorea/jinju/namgang_yudeung_festival) fills the river and fortress area with lanterns, a modern festival tied to wartime signal lanterns and one of the city's best reasons to detour.
