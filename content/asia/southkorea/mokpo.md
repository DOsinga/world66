Mokpo sits at the southwest corner of mainland [South Korea](/asia/southkorea), where the hills drop into ferry terminals, fishing boats, and the broken island country of the Dadohae. It is a working port first and a tourist town second, which is part of its appeal: the city feels salty, compact, and a little worn in the best way.

The easiest way to understand Mokpo is to start on [Yudalsan](/asia/southkorea/mokpo/yudalsan), the low mountain above the old port. From the rocks near the top you can see the harbour, the bridge, and the islands that explain why Mokpo has always looked outward to the sea. The [Mokpo Marine Cable Car](/asia/southkorea/mokpo/mokpo_marine_cable_car) turns that geography into a ride, crossing the mountain and water toward Gohado.

Down below, Mokpo's older streets still carry traces of the Japanese colonial period. The [Mokpo Modern History Museum](/asia/southkorea/mokpo/mokpo_modern_history_museum) occupies the former Japanese consulate, while [Gatbawi](/asia/southkorea/mokpo/gatbawi) and the museum district show a softer, more local side of the city. Mokpo is a good base before a ferry to the islands, but it deserves a day of its own before you leave the mainland.
