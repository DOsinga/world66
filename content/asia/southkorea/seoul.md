Seoul is one of the biggest, densest, and most relentlessly modern cities in the world. Home to roughly half of South Korea's population in its wider metropolitan area, it is a place of endless neon, twenty-four-hour street food, and 5G on the subway — and yet, tucked between the office towers, it still contains five Joseon-era palaces, a city wall, and the shrines and hanok villages that have been there for six centuries.

The city fills a valley on the Han River, ringed by mountains ([Bukhansan](/asia/southkorea/seoul/bukhansan) to the north is a national park right at the city limits). North of the river, Jongno and the old downtown contain [Gyeongbokgung](/asia/southkorea/seoul/kyongbokpalace) and [Changdeokgung](/asia/southkorea/seoul/changdokpalace) palaces, the craft-and-tea alleys of [Insadong](/asia/southkorea/seoul/insadong), the hanok lanes of [Bukchon](/asia/southkorea/seoul/bukchon_hanok), and the market chaos of [Gwangjang](/asia/southkorea/seoul/gwangjang_market) and [Namdaemun](/asia/southkorea/seoul/namdaemun_market). [Myeongdong](/asia/southkorea/seoul/myeongdong) is the tourist shopping heart, brash and crowded. South of the river, [Gangnam](/asia/southkorea/seoul/gangnam) is the money — glass skyscrapers, plastic-surgery clinics, and the Lotte World Tower. [Itaewon](/asia/southkorea/seoul/itaewon) is the old foreign quarter, [Hongdae](/asia/southkorea/seoul/hongdae) the student and indie-music district, and [Seongsu](/asia/southkorea/seoul/seongsu) the current hipster favourite for third-wave coffee and craft beer.

Food is a reason in itself to come. [Korean barbecue and Korean fried chicken](/asia/southkorea/seoul/eating_out) are obvious pleasures; the deeper ones are the late-night [pojangmacha tents](/asia/southkorea/seoul/pojangmacha), the 24-hour seolleongtang joints, the temple-cuisine restaurants, the dakgalbi dens, and whatever new fried-rice-cake combination has caught on this month. A day trip north to [Panmunjom and the DMZ](/asia/southkorea/seoul/dmz_day_trip) is the strangest excursion any capital can offer — you can look directly into North Korea from a few kilometres inside the Republic of Korea.
