The National Museum of Korean Contemporary History covers South Korea's turbulent modern story, from liberation from Japanese rule through war, dictatorship, protest movements, rapid industrial growth, and democratization. Its location near Gwanghwamun makes it easy to combine with Gyeongbokgung, Cheonggyecheon, and central Seoul's main civic spaces.

The exhibits are most useful for visitors who want context for the Korea they see today: division, reconstruction, student protest, economic change, and the country's place in the wider Cold War. Allow about an hour, longer if you read the historical material closely.
