Suwon is the largest city in Gyeonggi province, immediately south of Seoul and easily reached by subway — yet it feels like a proper city rather than a capital suburb. Its reason to visit is Hwaseong, an 18th-century fortress wall that loops nearly six kilometres around the old city centre, and which was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1997.

**Hwaseong Fortress** was built between 1794 and 1796 by King Jeongjo, partly as a monument to his father — who died after being locked in a rice chest by court rivals — and partly as a planned city that combined military defence with commerce. The design is unusually sophisticated, blending Korean, Chinese, and European military architecture of the period, and the original construction records were so detailed that the post-Korean War reconstruction is considered remarkably faithful. Walking the full parapet circuit takes about two hours; the restored Janganmun gate in the north and the Paldalmun gate in the south are the most impressive sections, and the views over the modern city from the walls are striking.

The area inside the walls retains some older streetscapes and the Hwaseong Haenggung, a temporary palace used by the king during visits. Suwon is an easy day trip from Seoul, though the fortress warrants an overnight if you want to attend the evening lighting or the archery demonstrations at the northern stretch.
