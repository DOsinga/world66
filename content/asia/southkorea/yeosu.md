Yeosu is a south-coast port spread across a peninsula and a scatter of islands. It has enough city around the harbour to feel busy, but the best parts look outward: bridges lit over black water, ferry docks, camellia paths, seafood markets, and Buddhist steps climbing toward sea cliffs.

The strongest reason to come is the mix of maritime history and coastal scenery. [Hyangiram Hermitage](/asia/southkorea/yeosu/hyangiram_hermitage) sits on the edge of Dolsan Island, reached by steep paths and narrow rock passages with views across the islands. Back in town, [Jinnamgwan](/asia/southkorea/yeosu/jinnamgwan) and the [Turtle Ship Replica](/asia/southkorea/yeosu/turtle_ship_replica) connect Yeosu to Admiral Yi Sun-sin and the naval wars of the late 16th century.

Yeosu also works as an easy day of movement. Walk [Odongdo](/asia/southkorea/yeosu/odongdo), cross toward Dolsan Bridge after dark for the lights, then eat seafood near the harbour. It is not as polished as Busan or as famous as Jeju, but that is part of the appeal: Yeosu still feels like a working Korean port that happens to have excellent views.
