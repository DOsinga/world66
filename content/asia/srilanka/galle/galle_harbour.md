The natural harbour at Galle has been used for centuries — Arab, Persian, Chinese and later Portuguese, Dutch and British ships all anchored here on the spice route. Until the British developed Colombo in the late nineteenth century, Galle was the main port of Ceylon.

Today the harbour is a working commercial port with limited public access, but the view across the bay from the eastern ramparts of the fort, especially in the early morning, is one of the most evocative in Sri Lanka. A few yacht charter operators run short cruises around the harbour and along the coast.
