Haththikuchchi is an ancient site in the Kurunegala district, near the town of Galgamuwa. The [Haththikuchchi Viharaya](/asia/srilanka/haththikuchchi/haththikuchchi_viharaya) is one of the oldest and largest monastery complexes in Sri Lanka, established in the 2nd century BCE and occupied continuously for over a thousand years. According to tradition, this is where King Siri Sangabo — revered in Sri Lanka as a bodhisattva king — gave his head to a poor man, fulfilling a vow of sacrifice. The story is disputed; some place the event at Aththanagalla near Nittambuwa. From the top of the elephant-shaped rock that gives the site its name (hatthi = elephant, kuchchi = belly), the pinnacles of the Ruwanwelisaya stupa at [Anuradhapura](/asia/srilanka/anuradhapura) and the hill of Mihintale are said to be visible on a clear day.

The site is largely unexcavated and receives few foreign visitors — an advantage for those who find the more famous archaeological parks in the North Central Province overcrowded. It is a working place of Buddhist pilgrimage as well as an archaeological site.
