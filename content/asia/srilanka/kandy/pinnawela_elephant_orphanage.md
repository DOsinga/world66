  to over ninety elephants, making it the largest captive elephant herd in the world. The
  elephants that were born at Pinnawela have never lived in the wild and could not survive
  there — a fact that raises complex questions about the long-term purpose of the facility,
  which has evolved from rescue operation into what is effectively an elephant breeding
  programme.

The Pinnawela Elephant Orphanage, about 40 km northwest of Kandy, houses a large herd of elephants including orphaned calves, injured adults, and a substantial population of captive-born animals. Twice daily the entire herd walks down to the Maha Oya river for a bath — a remarkable spectacle best seen from the riverbank restaurants that line the opposite shore.

The ethics of Pinnawela are debated: some animal welfare organisations have concerns about riding, chaining, and breeding practices. The daily river bath, however, is genuinely impressive and not staged — the elephants walk down on their own schedule and the bathing is real. Most visitors combine it with a stop at one of the riverside restaurants. The drive from Kandy takes about an hour; shared taxis or organised tours are the easiest options.
