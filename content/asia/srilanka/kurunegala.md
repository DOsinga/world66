Kurunegala is a mid-sized city in the North Western Province of Sri Lanka, about 100 kilometres north of Colombo. It was a medieval capital of the Sinhalese kingdom in the 13th and 14th centuries, and the large rocky outcrops that surround the town were given the names of animals — [Elephant Rock](/asia/srilanka/kurunegala/ethagala), [Tortoise Rock](/asia/srilanka/kurunegala/ibbagala) — that still serve as local landmarks. A large white Buddha statue now crowns the highest summit.

The town sits at a crossroads between Colombo and the Cultural Triangle (Kandy, Sigiriya, Anuradhapura), and most travellers pass through rather than stay. For those who do stop, both rocks can be climbed for views over the [lake](/asia/srilanka/kurunegala/kurunegala_lake) and the surrounding plains, and the area north of town holds some of the Cultural Triangle's least-visited ancient sites: the rock fortress of Yapahuwa, the forest hermitage of Arankele, and the beautiful [Ridi Viharaya](/asia/srilanka/kurunegala/ridi_vihara) (Silver Temple), renowned for its Kandyan frescoes.

The surrounding district is productive agricultural land — coconut plantations and paddy fields stretch in most directions — and the town itself is a busy commercial hub with good transport connections to the main cultural sites of the interior.
