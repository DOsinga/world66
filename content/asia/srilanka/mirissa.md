Mirissa is a small beach town on Sri Lanka's south coast that has become one of the island's most popular destinations for good reason. The bay curves beautifully between two headlands, the sea is warm and swimmable for most of the year, and the pace of life is unhurried enough to make you stay longer than you planned. It sits about 150 km south of Colombo, just west of [Matara](/asia/srilanka/matara).

The main draw is simply being here — on the sand, in the water, watching the fishing boats head out at dawn. The [Mirissa beach](/asia/srilanka/mirissa/mirissa_beach) itself runs for about a kilometre, with a rocky headland at the western end where [Coconut Tree Hill](/asia/srilanka/mirissa/coconut_tree_hill) gives one of the south coast's best viewpoints. At the eastern end a smaller cove called Secret Beach offers relative quiet even when the main beach is busy.

Between November and April, Mirissa is one of the best places in the world for [blue whale watching](/asia/srilanka/mirissa/whale_watching). The waters off the south coast see the largest animals on earth year-round, and local operators run morning trips that have a high success rate. Sperm whales, dolphins, and spinner dolphins are common too. This is the genuine article — not a guarantee, but better odds than almost anywhere else.

The beach road is lined with places to eat and drink. The catch-of-the-day seafood is excellent and cheap if you pick the right spots. Getting here is straightforward: take the train from Colombo or Galle to [Weligama](/asia/srilanka/weligama) and then a tuk-tuk, or come by bus from [Matara](/asia/srilanka/matara). Weligama, a few kilometres to the west, is worth a stop for the stilt fishermen and [Taprobane Island](/asia/srilanka/mirissa/taprobane_island).
