The old sea walls that ring the western edge of the island are among the most evocative parts of Arwad. Built in layers over millennia — Phoenician stone foundations, Hellenistic additions, Crusader rebuilds — they now serve as a walkway with views out to the open Mediterranean. In the afternoon light, when the stones warm and the sea turns blue-green, the setting is memorable.

Behind the walls, the island is a dense tangle of narrow alleyways between tightly packed stone houses. There is no plan to the street layout — it evolved organically over three thousand years. Getting slightly lost here is the point. The residential alleys are quiet and few tourists venture far from the harbour, so even a short walk inward brings a sense of the island's compressed, ancient ordinariness.
