A short reading list for understanding Damascus and Syria:

**Damascus: Taste of a City** by Marie Fadel and Rafik Schami. A literary tribute by two Syrian exiles, structured as a series of letters about Damascene food, smells and street life. Read this before a visit and the Old City will feel familiar.

**The Dark Side of Love** by Rafik Schami. A sprawling Damascene family epic spanning a century, from the Ottoman period through to the 1960s. Long but immersive; gives a strong sense of how Damascus's communities — Christian, Muslim, Druze, Alawite — lived alongside each other and against each other.

**Cities of Salt** by Abdelrahman Munif. Munif was born in Amman, lived in Damascus, and wrote what is widely considered the great Arabic novel of the 20th century — a quintet about an unnamed Gulf state transformed by the discovery of oil. Damascus is the editorial heart of the modern Arabic literary world, and you cannot understand its self-image without Munif.

**Damascus Nights** by Rafik Schami. Set in a Damascus coffee house in the 1950s, where an old storyteller has lost his voice and friends try to bring it back with tales of their own. Short, warm, formally inventive.

**The Hakawati** by Rabih Alameddine. A Lebanese-American novel that draws heavily on Damascene oral tradition — strongly recommended for the same reasons.
