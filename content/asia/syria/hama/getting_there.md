---
title: "Getting There"
type: section
---

Hama sits on the main Damascus–Aleppo corridor and was well connected to both cities before the conflict. Regular Pullman (express) buses linked Hama to Damascus in roughly 3.5 hours and to Aleppo in about 2 hours; Homs, 45 km south, was a 45-minute trip and served as the main interchange point for services to Tartus, Latakia, and the coast. The Karnak and Al-Ahliah bus companies operated the major intercity routes from depots on the edge of the city.

Hama also has a railway station on the main Damascus–Aleppo line, which passes through Homs. Train services were slower than the buses but comfortable, and the station is close to the city centre. The journey to Damascus by rail took around four hours; to Aleppo, roughly two and a half hours.

Since the outbreak of the Syrian civil war in 2011, travel to Hama has been extremely difficult and dangerous for foreigners. The security situation has changed repeatedly and the practical information above reflects pre-war conditions. Anyone considering travel to Syria should consult current advisories from their government and specialist sources; most Western governments have had travel bans to Syria in place for years.
