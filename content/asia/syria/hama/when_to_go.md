---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

Hama has the climate of Syria's interior: hot, dry summers and cool winters, with spring and autumn offering the most comfortable conditions. The city sits at around 300 metres above sea level, giving it slightly milder summers than the coastal cities but more pronounced winter cold.

**Spring (March–May)** is the best season. Temperatures are warm but not oppressive, typically 18–25°C, and the Orontes valley is green from winter rains. The riverside gardens are at their best, and the countryside around Hama — used extensively for day trips to Apamea and the surrounding sites — is pleasant to travel through.

**Summer (June–September)** is hot and dry. July and August regularly reach 38–42°C in the afternoon. The heat is dry rather than humid, which makes it more bearable than the coast, but extended sightseeing in the midday hours is uncomfortable. Mornings and evenings are manageable, and Syrians traditionally structure outdoor life around the cooler parts of the day.

**Autumn (October–November)** is the second-best season, warm and settled, with temperatures similar to spring. The landscape is drier and browner than in spring, but the light in autumn has a particular quality that suits the stone-built old city.

**Winter (December–February)** is mild by European standards — daytime temperatures around 10–15°C — but cold enough at night to make unheated buildings uncomfortable. Rain is possible throughout the winter months. Snow is rare but not unknown.
