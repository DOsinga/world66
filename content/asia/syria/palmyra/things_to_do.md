Palmyra's ruins are among the most significant archaeological sites in the world. Before the Syrian Civil War, the site covered several square kilometres of well-preserved Roman-era structures: the Great Colonnade, the Temple of Bel, the Arch of Triumph, the theatre, the Valley of the Tombs, and the hilltop Palmyra Castle.

ISIS caused severe damage to the site between 2015 and 2017: the Temple of Bel, the Temple of Baalshamin, the Arch of Triumph, and many tower tombs were deliberately destroyed, and the site's chief archaeologist Khaled al-Asaad was publicly executed. The ruins that survive — the colonnaded street, the theatre, the Valley of the Tombs, portions of the Temple of Bel's precinct, and the castle above the town — remain remarkable. This section records what was here and what remains, as a historical record.
