---
title: "Alishan Forest Railway"
type: poi
---

The Alishan Forest Railway is one of the world's great mountain railways — a narrow-gauge line that has been climbing from Chiayi city to the high mountain station since 1912. Originally built by the Japanese colonial administration to haul timber down from the old-growth forests, it long outlasted its logging purpose and is now a heritage attraction listed alongside the Darjeeling Himalayan Railway and the Swiss mountain lines as one of the notable steep-gradient railways anywhere.

The main line runs 71 kilometres from Chiayi (30 metres above sea level) to Alishan Station (2,216 metres), gaining nearly the full elevation in a journey of about three hours. The route passes through tropical lowland forest, subtropical tea and bamboo country, and dense temperate cypress forest — three distinct climate zones visible through the carriage windows. The engineering is remarkable: 49 tunnels, 77 bridges, and a series of switchbacks where the train reverses direction on steep sections to gain height.

Within the Alishan scenic area itself, a network of smaller internal lines connects the main station to Zhushan, Shenmu, and Chaoping. These are the trains most visitors use day-to-day — for the pre-dawn Zhushan sunrise run especially. Demand for seats on the main Chiayi line is high during holidays and cherry blossom season; book through the Taiwan Railways website well in advance if you plan to travel up or down by train.
