---
title: "Ancient Cypress Forests"
type: poi
---

The old-growth red and hinoki cypress trees scattered across Alishan's forest trails are among the most extraordinary living things in Taiwan. Many are over a thousand years old; the largest specimens — some with trunk circumferences of more than ten metres — are survivors of the intensive Japanese-era logging that cleared most of the original forest between 1910 and 1945. What remains is a remnant, but it is still deeply impressive.

The Giant Trees trail near Shenmu Station is the main route for seeing the oldest specimens. The path is well-maintained and passes a sequence of named trees, each with a placard giving their estimated age and height. Allow a couple of hours to walk it without rushing. The forest here is quiet, cool, and frequently misty, with shafts of light filtering through the high canopy — a very different atmosphere from the crowded sunrise platforms at Zhushan.

The Alishan Sacred Tree — a red cypress estimated at around 2,300 years old — was for decades the centrepiece of the area until it toppled in a storm in 1997. A section of its trunk is still displayed near Shenmu Station as a monument. The trees that remain standing nearby are nearly as old and arguably more evocative for being alive: the scale of them becomes apparent when you stand at the base and look up.
