---
title: "Sunrise at Zhushan"
type: poi
---

Watching the sun rise above the cloud sea from Zhushan peak is one of Taiwan's signature experiences — and, unusually for something so hyped, it tends to deliver. The scene unfolds in stages: first the sky lightens from black to deep violet, then the highest peaks emerge above a flat white ocean of cloud, and finally the sun breaks the horizon and floods everything in amber light. On a clear morning it is genuinely spectacular.

To get there, take the pre-dawn forest train from Alishan Station to Zhushan. Departure time varies by season (roughly 90 minutes before sunrise); check the board at the station the evening before and buy tickets then if you can. The train is small and fills up fast, so arrive at the platform early. Alternatively, you can walk up in about 45 minutes from Alishan village if you prefer — the path is well-marked.

The viewing platform at the summit gets crowded on weekends and during spring cherry blossom season. Arriving a little before the rush and finding a spot toward the edges of the platform gives you more room. Bring warm layers regardless of the season; it is cold at altitude before dawn, and wind at the summit makes it feel colder still.
