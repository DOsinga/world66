Keelung's old WWII bomb shelter is a practical piece of living history: a tunnel network built during the Japanese colonial period for civilian protection during Allied bombing raids, now repurposed as a pedestrian passageway cutting through the city centre. The passage is genuinely old — the brickwork and original structure are largely intact — and it sees daily foot traffic from locals using it as a shortcut between streets.

The tunnel is low and somewhat atmospheric, with the rough texture of wartime construction and occasional displays about its history. It is not a curated museum attraction; people walk through it to get places, and that ordinariness is part of what makes it interesting. The site serves as a reminder that Keelung was heavily bombed during the final years of WWII due to its strategic importance as a port. Finding it requires some navigation — ask at the tourist office near the train station or look for the entrance signs in the old downtown area near the harbour. The visit takes ten to fifteen minutes and costs nothing.
