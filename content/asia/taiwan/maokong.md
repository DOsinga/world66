Maokong is a tea-growing hillside in the very south of the Taipei municipality, planted with bushes of the local **Tieguanyin** oolong since the 19th century. The slopes are gentle and green, the views back across the city are wide, and the whole area is dotted with old-style **teahouses** where you brew your own tea on a charcoal stove and order small dishes — duck breast, tea-smoked eggs, Hakka cooking — to go with it. It makes one of the easiest and most relaxing half-day escapes from the centre of Taipei.

Getting up to Maokong is half the point. The **Maokong Gondola**, opened in 2007, climbs from Taipei Zoo MRT station to the top of the hill in a 25-minute ride, with some cabins fitted with glass floors that look straight down on the forest canopy. The walking trails around the gondola's upper station are short and pleasant; the better teahouses are a kilometre or two further along the ridge, reachable on foot or by the local minibus.

Come in the late afternoon and stay into the evening: the teahouses are at their best after dark, when the lights of Taipei spread out below and a pot of oolong stretches comfortably across a couple of hours. Weekends are busy and the gondola queues can be long, so a weekday visit is calmer. Bring a light layer — the ridge is noticeably cooler than the city below.
