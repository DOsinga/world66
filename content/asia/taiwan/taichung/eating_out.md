---
title: 'Eating Out'
type: section
---

Taichung's food scene runs across the full spread of Taiwanese cuisine — Hakka, Hokkien, Japanese-influenced, mainland regional, and a strong line in coffee, tea, and Western-style cafés. Sun cake (taiyangbing), a flaky pastry filled with malt sugar, was invented in the city and remains the standard local sweet to take home.

The cheapest eating is at the night markets — Fengjia is the biggest, Yizhong and Zhongxiao are easier to navigate. For a sit-down meal, the small streets around Wuquan Park have the best concentration of independent restaurants, with several with one or two Michelin Bib Gourmand listings.

**JL Studio**, Section 1, Wuquan West Road, is the city's two-Michelin-star Singaporean-Taiwanese kitchen and one of the best restaurants in Taiwan. Tasting menu only, book months ahead.

**Lung Mei Beef Noodles** at the corner of Sanmin Road and Yixin Street is the city standard for the dish — large bowls, hand-pulled noodles, queue at lunch. The original branch of Chun Shui Tang, on Siwei Street, is where bubble tea was reputedly invented in 1983.
