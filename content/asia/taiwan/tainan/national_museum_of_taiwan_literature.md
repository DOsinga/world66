The National Museum of Taiwan Literature occupies a handsome 1916 Japanese colonial-era building in the centre of old Tainan, and the building itself is as interesting as the collection it houses. The museum traces Taiwanese literature from the earliest indigenous oral traditions through the Dutch colonial period, the Qing era, Japanese occupation, and the complex post-war decades — a literary history that maps closely onto Taiwan's turbulent political history.

The permanent collection includes manuscripts, first editions, and literary artifacts from across three centuries, with English-language explanations that make the exhibits accessible to non-Chinese readers. The museum hosts regular temporary exhibitions on individual authors and literary movements, and the bookshop is one of the better places in Tainan to find literature in translation. The building's colonial architecture — arched colonnades, a red-brick facade, and a landscaped garden — makes it worth visiting even for those with limited interest in the literary content.
