Tainan Wu Garden is a small classical Chinese garden from the 1830s, tucked alongside the National Museum of Taiwan Literature in the heart of the old city. The garden was built by a wealthy Qing-era salt merchant as a private retreat for literary and scholarly gatherings — a miniature landscape of an artificial hill, a pond with a zigzag bridge, bamboo groves, and traditional pavilions, arranged to give the impression of a landscape considerably larger than it actually is.

The garden is compact and can be walked through in twenty minutes, but it rewards slower movement. The design follows the classical southern Chinese principle of arranged framing: each pavilion frames a view of something else in the garden, so the space unfolds rather than simply presenting itself all at once. It is quieter than most of the city's main attractions and makes a natural complement to the adjacent literature museum. Entry is usually included with the museum ticket or has a nominal separate charge.
