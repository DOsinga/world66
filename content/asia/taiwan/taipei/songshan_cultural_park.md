The 1937 Songshan Tobacco Factory was one of the largest industrial complexes in Japanese-era Taiwan, producing cigarettes for the colonial monopoly. The factory closed in 1998 and the buildings — broad single-storey ferro-concrete halls with double-pitched tiled roofs, set around a square of lawns and a long ornamental pond — have been adapted into a design and exhibition park.

The complex houses the **Taiwan Design Museum**, the well-curated **Not Just Library**, several galleries, a hotel, and a permanent line-up of design and craft shops. The 2014 **Taipei Cultural and Creative Park building** at the back, a curving pale-clad pavilion by Toyo Ito, is one of the city's most striking pieces of contemporary architecture. Free entry to the grounds; some exhibitions ticketed.
