Zhongshan Hall, on the edge of Ximending, is one of the most historically significant buildings in Taipei — a grand civic auditorium completed in 1936 during the Japanese colonial era, originally named the Taipei Public Auditorium. It was here, in 1945, that the Japanese formally surrendered control of Taiwan to the Republic of China, making it the stage for one of the defining moments in the island's modern history.

The building is a fine example of late-colonial public architecture, its restrained Spanish-influenced facade enclosing a large concert and assembly hall that still hosts performances and exhibitions. Set in a small plaza just north of the Ximen pedestrian streets, it is an easy and rewarding stop — a piece of monumental history a few steps from the neon and youth culture of Ximending. The upper-floor cafe and the surrounding square are pleasant places to pause.
