Yilan is the county seat of Taiwan's rainy northeast, close enough to Taipei for a day trip but with a softer, lower-rise feel. The city is not as dramatic as the mountains, hot springs, and coast around it, but it gives the Lanyang Plain a useful cultural centre: old Japanese-era buildings, temples, small museums, night-market food, and local industries that have turned into visitor stops.

The best short visit stays near the centre. [Yilan Distillery Chia Chi Lan Liquor Museum](/asia/taiwan/yilan/yilan_distillery_chia_chi_lan_liquor_museum) explains the city's old wine-making industry, while the [Yilan Literary Museum](/asia/taiwan/yilan/yilan_literary_museum) and [Yilan Museum of Art](/asia/taiwan/yilan/yilan_museum_of_art) make good use of Japanese-era buildings. [Taiwan Theater Museum](/asia/taiwan/yilan/taiwan_theater_museum) is the most distinctive museum if you want a local angle on opera, puppetry, and performance.

Yilan is also a practical base. Jiaoxi hot springs, Luodong's night market, Wujie's traditional arts centre, and the coast are all close, but the city itself earns a few hours before you rush outward. Come hungry and unhurried; rain is part of the atmosphere here.
