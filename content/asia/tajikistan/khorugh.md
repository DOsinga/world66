Khorugh is the Pamir town where the long road east starts to feel properly high and remote. It sits in a steep valley at the meeting of the Gunt and Panj rivers, with Afghanistan just across the water and bare mountains rising fast above the streets.

Most travellers come through on the Pamir Highway, but Khorugh is more than a fuel-and-shared-taxi stop. The town is the capital of Gorno-Badakhshan, the centre of Pamiri Ismaili life in Tajikistan, and a useful place to slow down before or after the harsher roads to Murghab, Ishkashim, or the Wakhan.

The best spare day starts above town at the [Pamir Botanical Garden](/asia/tajikistan/khorugh/pamir_botanical_garden), where paths and terraces look back over the valley. In the centre, the [Pamir Museum](/asia/tajikistan/khorugh/pamir_museum) gives some context for the region's languages, dress, archaeology, and Soviet history. The [Ismaili Centre](/asia/tajikistan/khorugh/ismaili_centre) is the cleanest modern architectural statement in town, while [Khorugh City Park](/asia/tajikistan/khorugh/khorugh_city_park) is the place to watch the town go by.

Khorugh is not slick. Sundays can be quiet, transport depends on full vehicles, and the political atmosphere around Pamiri identity can be sensitive. Come with patience and it becomes one of the more memorable pauses in Central Asia: a small, humane town pressed between river, border, and mountain.
