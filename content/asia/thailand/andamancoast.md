The Andaman Coast is Thailand's other sea, facing the Indian Ocean along the western side of the southern peninsula — generally clearer water, more dramatic scenery, and a longer high season than the Gulf side, at the cost of a rougher monsoon from May to October.

[Phuket](/asia/thailand/andamancoast/phuket) is the region's hub and Thailand's largest island, an international airport surrounded by a string of beach towns of very different characters — brash [Patong](/asia/thailand/andamancoast/patong), calmer [Karon](/asia/thailand/andamancoast/karon) and [Kata](/asia/thailand/andamancoast/kata), upmarket [Surin](/asia/thailand/andamancoast/surin), and the Sino-Portuguese streets of [Phuket City](/asia/thailand/andamancoast/phuket_city) itself. [Phang Nga Bay](/asia/thailand/andamancoast/phangngabay), between Phuket and Krabi, is the limestone-karst seascape of James Bond Island fame.

[Krabi](/asia/thailand/andamancoast/krabi) has the same dramatic karst scenery on the mainland side, centred on the resort strip of [Ao Nang](/asia/thailand/andamancoast/aonang) and the boat-only cliffs of Railay. Offshore, [Koh Phi Phi](/asia/thailand/andamancoast/kohphiphi) and [Koh Lanta](/asia/thailand/andamancoast/kohlanta) are the best-known islands, while [Khao Lak](/asia/thailand/andamancoast/khao_lak) further north is the main departure point for diving the Similan Islands.

The far south is quieter and less developed: [Trang](/asia/thailand/andamancoast/trang) and its offshore islands, [Koh Phayam](/asia/thailand/andamancoast/koh_phayam) and [Ranong](/asia/thailand/andamancoast/ranong) near the Myanmar border, and [Tarutao National Park](/asia/thailand/andamancoast/tarutao) and [Satun](/asia/thailand/andamancoast/satun) close to Malaysia — some of the least-crowded island scenery left on this coast.
