Patong is [Phuket](/asia/thailand/andamancoast/phuket)'s busiest and most developed beach resort, a long crescent of sand facing the Andaman Sea backed by a wall of hotels, restaurants, and shops. It is loud, commercial, and unapologetically geared toward mass tourism — and for many visitors, that is exactly the point.

The beach itself is broad and swimmable, with parasailing, jet skis, and longtail boats available along its length. Behind the beach, [Bangla Road](/asia/thailand/andamancoast/patong/bangla_road) is the epicentre of Phuket's nightlife: neon-lit bars, go-go clubs, live music, and street performers spill onto the road every evening. By day, the same streets are calmer — good for browsing market stalls, getting a Thai massage, or finding cheap pad thai at a street cart. The [Jungceylon](/asia/thailand/andamancoast/patong/jungceylon) shopping mall offers air-conditioned escape from the midday heat, and [Simon Cabaret](/asia/thailand/andamancoast/patong/simon_cabaret) hosts the island's most famous ladyboy revue.

Patong works best as a base if you want easy access to everything: diving and snorkelling day trips depart from the southern end of the beach, and it is well connected by songthaew and taxi to quieter beaches like [Kata](/asia/thailand/andamancoast/kata) and [Karon](/asia/thailand/andamancoast/karon) to the south. Tucked-away coves like [Freedom Beach](/asia/thailand/andamancoast/patong/freedom_beach) and [Tri Trang Beach](/asia/thailand/andamancoast/patong/tri_trang_beach) offer a quieter alternative within a few kilometres.
