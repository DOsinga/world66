Bang Saphan is a quiet beach district on the Gulf of Thailand coast, some 450 kilometres south of Bangkok, where the tourist infrastructure of the more famous resorts gives way to something simpler: long empty beaches, fishing villages, and rice paddies running to the sea. The main beach areas — Bang Saphan proper and the nearby village of Ban Krut to the north — have a handful of small resorts and guesthouses but no bars, nightclubs, or jet-ski operators. People come here specifically to escape those things.

The beach at Ban Krut is the more developed of the two, with a well-maintained strip of sand backed by casuarina trees and a few good seafood restaurants. Bang Saphan itself is more local in character: working fishing boats share the coast with a modest tourist scene. Offshore, the small island of Koh Talu has a single simple resort and good snorkelling on its reef. Day trips to the island are easily arranged from Bang Saphan.

Getting here takes some commitment — it is a five-hour drive from Bangkok, or about the same by train. That distance is part of what keeps it quiet. The highway and railway both run along the coast, making it easy to hop between Bang Saphan, Ban Krut, and the nearby beach towns to the north such as Pranburi and Hua Hin if you want to explore the whole Gulf coast stretch.
