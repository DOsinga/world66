Ari is the leafy, low-rise pocket of northern Bangkok that locals reach for when they want the city's café-and-design culture without Sukhumvit's crowds. A residential enclave of mid-century shophouses and tucked-away sois off Phahonyothin Road, it became, over the 2010s, the neighbourhood where Bangkok's creative class opened the kind of small, particular places — third-wave coffee bars, single-table tasting rooms, plant-filled bistros — that reward wandering rather than ticking off sights.

The action threads through the numbered Ari sois north and south of the BTS station, where converted houses and garden compounds hold coffee roasters, independent boutiques, wine bars, and some of the city's most talked-about restaurants. La Villa mall anchors the main road, but the neighbourhood's character lives in the back lanes — Soi Ari 1 and 4 in particular — and in the morning markets and old-school Thai-Chinese coffee shops that have outlasted the hipster wave around them.

Come to eat and drink: graze a long brunch, hop between roasters, and settle into a soi-side bistro as the heat drops. Ari is compact, walkable, and easy to reach on the Sukhumvit BTS line, which makes it one of the most rewarding half-days in Bangkok for travellers who like their cities at street level.
