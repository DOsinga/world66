Garland's 1996 novel about a young backpacker who discovers a secret beach community in southern Thailand put Khao San Road on the map for a generation of travellers and made Ko Samui fashionable. The Bangkok sections — the guesthouses, the traveller circuit, the particular mix of freedom and squalor — are still accurate. The novel is sharper and darker than the film, and its central insight about the tourism industry devouring what it seeks still holds.
