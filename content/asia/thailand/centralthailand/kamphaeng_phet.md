Kamphaeng Phet is the Sukhothai-era city that most travellers miss. It sits on the Ping River in lower northern Thailand, midway between the better-known historic sites of [Sukhothai](/asia/thailand/centralthailand/sukhothai) and central Thailand, and its old name, Chakangrao, belonged to a fortified frontier town of the Sukhothai kingdom.

The draw is **[Kamphaeng Phet Historical Park](/asia/thailand/centralthailand/kamphaeng_phet/kamphaeng_phet_historical_park)**, part of the UNESCO-listed "Historic Town of Sukhothai and Associated Historic Towns." It does not have Sukhothai's broad lawns and postcard symmetry. Its character is rougher and more solitary: laterite walls, forest temples, elephant-ringed chedis, and Buddha images scattered through a park that often feels almost empty.

That quiet is exactly why Kamphaeng Phet works. You can cycle between **[Wat Phra Kaeo](/asia/thailand/centralthailand/kamphaeng_phet/wat_phra_kaeo)**, **[Wat Chang Rop](/asia/thailand/centralthailand/kamphaeng_phet/wat_chang_rop)**, and the archaeological museum without feeling pulled through a fixed tourist circuit. It is not a substitute for Sukhothai, but it is a strong second chapter for travellers who want to see how the kingdom's defensive and religious landscape continued beyond the old capital.
