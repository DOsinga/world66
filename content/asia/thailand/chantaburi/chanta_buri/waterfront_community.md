The narrow street that runs along the west bank of the Chanthaburi River is the most evocative part of the city. It is lined with about three hundred wooden shophouses, mostly from the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, built by Chinese and Vietnamese traders. The river was the city's lifeline and these houses had their merchandise unloaded straight from boats at the back door.

Today many of the buildings have been carefully restored. Cafés and galleries occupy some, families still live in others, and the street is quietest in the morning before tourists arrive from Bangkok. A small community heritage centre at house number 69 explains the history. Walk both sides of the river by the wooden footbridges and you have seen the best of Chantaburi.
