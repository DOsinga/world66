Chiang Saen is an ancient walled city on the Mekong, about 60km north of Chiang Rai, and one of the oldest urban sites in northern Thailand. It was the capital of the Chiang Saen Kingdom before becoming part of the Lanna realm, and significant stretches of the original earthen walls survive, along with ruined chedis and temple compounds scattered through and around the town. The most impressive, Wat Chedi Luang, has a large octagonal chedi still standing to considerable height. The Chiang Saen National Museum is small but has a worthwhile collection of Lanna-era artefacts, Buddha images, and ceramics recovered from the surrounding ruins.

The town itself is quiet and pleasant, set right on the Mekong with views across to Laos. Chinese cargo boats dock at the riverside pier, and there is a small market in the mornings. It makes a natural stop on the way to or from the Golden Triangle, which is another 9km upriver. Combine the two into a full day from Chiang Rai and you will have seen some of the most historically layered countryside in the province.
