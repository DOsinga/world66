The Deep South is culturally and religiously distinct from the rest of Thailand — Muslim-majority, Malay-speaking in parts, and closer in feel to northern Malaysia than to Bangkok. The three southernmost border provinces (Pattani, Yala, and Narathiwat) have experienced a long-running separatist insurgency, and most governments advise against non-essential travel there; check current travel advisories before planning a visit.

[Hat Yai](/asia/thailand/deepsouth/hatyai) is the region's commercial hub and its most visited city, popular with Malaysian and Singaporean shoppers for its markets and street food rather than with the international backpacker circuit. [Songkhla](/asia/thailand/deepsouth/songkhla), on the coast nearby, is a quieter old trading town with a well-preserved historic quarter. [Pattani](/asia/thailand/deepsouth/pattani) itself has a deep Malay-Muslim history worth understanding even for travellers who give the wider province a wide berth.
