The Eastern Seaboard runs from Bangkok's outer edge to the Cambodian border, a mix of Thailand's busiest industrial ports and some of its most accessible island getaways. It's a region of contrasts — refineries and container terminals give way, within an hour's drive, to jungle-covered islands and quiet fruit-growing provinces.

[Pattaya](/asia/thailand/easternseaboard/pattaya) is the region's best-known destination, Thailand's original purpose-built beach resort and still its brashest. Just south, [Jomtien Beach](/asia/thailand/easternseaboard/jomtien_beach) offers a calmer version of the same coastline. [Koh Samet](/asia/thailand/easternseaboard/kosamet), the closest proper island beach to Bangkok, gets busy on weekends but is easy to reach for a quick escape.

Further east, [Chanthaburi](/asia/thailand/easternseaboard/chantaburi) is a gem-trading town with a well-preserved riverside quarter, and [Trat](/asia/thailand/easternseaboard/trat) is the gateway to [Koh Chang](/asia/thailand/easternseaboard/kohchang), Thailand's third-largest island and increasingly the region's main draw — jungled, mountainous, and still rougher around the edges than Phuket or Samui. The smaller, quieter islands of [Koh Kood](/asia/thailand/easternseaboard/koh_kood) and [Koh Mak](/asia/thailand/easternseaboard/koh_mak) sit further offshore for those willing to make the extra crossing.
