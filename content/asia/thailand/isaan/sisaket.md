Sisaket is a provincial capital in the Isan region of northeastern Thailand, sitting on the Khmer-influenced plateau near the Cambodian border. It is not a heavily visited city, but it serves as a base for reaching some interesting Khmer ruins and, most notably, Khao Phra Viharn National Park.

The park, on the Thai-Cambodian border, is best known for its connection to the Preah Vihear temple complex — a 10th–12th century Khmer sanctuary that is one of the most dramatically sited ruins in Southeast Asia, perched atop a cliff escarpment with sheer drops on three sides. Access has been intermittently complicated by a long-running border dispute between Thailand and Cambodia, so check the current situation before going. The national park itself has good forest wildlife and the Mor E-Dang cliff viewpoint gives panoramic views over the Cambodian plains below.

In the city itself, Somdet Sri Nagarin Park is a pleasant public space that fills with the fragrance of lamduan blossoms in March, and the city marks the season with the Dok Lamduan Festival — a cultural fair celebrating the four ethnic groups of the region: Khmer, Suay, Lao, and Yer. Sisaket's June fruit festival, centred on rambutan and durian from the province's orchards, is another worthwhile reason to time a visit carefully.
