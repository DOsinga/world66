---
title: "Day Trips in Sisaket"
type: section
---

The main reason to base yourself in Sisaket is Khao Phra Viharn National Park, on the Thai-Cambodian border about two hours south of the city. The park sits below the clifftop Preah Vihear temple, which lies just across the border in Cambodia, and its Thai side is built around the escarpment itself rather than the ruins. The Mor E-Dang cliff viewpoint gives one of the best views in northeastern Thailand, looking out over the Cambodian plains from a red sandstone bluff; nearby, a bas-relief carved into the same stone depicts three Hindu deities and is thought to have been a practice piece by the Khmer stonemasons who went on to carve Preah Vihear itself.

Other sites in the park can fill out a full day. Two sandstone stupas known locally as the Double Stupas sit west of the cliff; the ruined Don Tuan Khmer complex, dating to the tenth or eleventh century, lies close to the border near Ban Phum Sarol; and a stream called Sra Trao, believed to have supplied water for the construction of Preah Vihear, runs through a rock-walled tunnel past a small waterfall and cave. Access to the park has been intermittently closed by the long-running Thai-Cambodian border dispute, so check the current situation before setting out, and carry your passport in case of checks near the border itself.
