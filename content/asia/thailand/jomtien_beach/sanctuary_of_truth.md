story: "Construction began in 1981 and is still going on — every plank, beam and ornament is hand-carved teak with no metal nails, which means termites and rot are constantly being replaced. The complex was the obsession of Lek Viriyaphant, a Thai-Chinese tycoon who wanted to leave a permanent monument to Buddhist, Hindu and Khmer cosmology. Lek died in 2000, but the work continues under his foundation and is not expected to finish before 2050."

About fifteen minutes' drive north of Jomtien, on a small headland at the edge of Naklua, stands one of the strangest religious buildings in the world — an enormous wooden temple, all spires and carvings, dedicated to the universal religious philosophies of Asia. Every surface, inside and out, is covered with intricate teak relief sculpture: gods, demons, planets, signs of the zodiac.

It is, technically, not finished. Visitors are given hard hats and led through a working site where craftsmen are still cutting wood and replacing decayed sections. Entrance is around 500 baht, which includes a guided tour and the option to ride a wooden boat round the bay. Surprisingly affecting — go.
