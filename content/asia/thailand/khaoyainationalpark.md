Khao Yai is Thailand's oldest national park and one of its best. It covers 2,168 square kilometres of forest and grassland across four provinces about 200 kilometres northeast of Bangkok, straddling an important watershed in the Dangrek mountain range. In 2005, it was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site as part of the larger Dong Phayayen–Khao Yai Forest Complex.

The park is exceptional for wildlife. Elephants, gibbons, macaques, gaur, and sambar deer are all present in reasonably good numbers, and the birding is outstanding — more than 300 species have been recorded, including four kinds of hornbill. Leopards and tigers are officially resident too, though sightings are rare. Night safaris along the park roads give good chances of seeing civets, porcupines, and other nocturnal animals caught in the spotlights.

Trails vary from easy riverside walks to full-day jungle hikes. The waterfalls — Haew Narok is the largest — are impressive in the wet season and worth the longer walks to reach them. The park is busy with Bangkok weekenders at weekends; come midweek if you want quieter trails.
