Krabi Town is the provincial capital of Krabi, sitting where the Krabi River flows into Phang Nga Bay on Thailand's Andaman coast. Most travellers pass through on their way to the beaches and islands — [Ao Nang](/asia/thailand/krabi/aonang), Railay, and the ferry piers are all nearby — but the town itself is worth a couple of days. It has a genuine character that the resort strips lack: a busy morning market, a walkable riverside promenade, Buddhist temples, and a lively night market.

The setting is spectacular. Limestone karst peaks rise straight out of the flat coastal plain in every direction, and the river mouth is lined with mangroves. Longtail boats tie up at the pier where fishermen unload their catches alongside tour operators selling island trips. The pace is relaxed and the prices are much lower than at the beaches.

Wat Kaew Korawaram, the town's main temple, gleams white on a hill above the centre and is worth climbing for the views. The Krabi Fossil Shell Beach (Susa Hoi) — a 75-million-year-old shellbed that looks like a rocky walkway — lies about 17 kilometres west of town and is one of only three such formations in the world. Tiger Cave Temple (Wat Tham Suea), with its 1,237-step climb to a hilltop viewpoint, is the most dramatic excursion in the area.

For the beaches: Ao Nang is the nearest resort beach, about 4 kilometres west by road. The islands — Ko Phi Phi, Ko Lanta, Ko Jum — are reached by ferry from the pier. Songthaews (shared trucks) and tuk-tuks cover most local routes. Long-distance buses and minivans depart from the bus terminal on Uttarakit Road.
