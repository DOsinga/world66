Lampang is a provincial city in northern Thailand, slower and less visited than nearby Chiang Mai, which makes it worth the detour. The old town retains some of its teak-era character, with wooden shophouses and horse-drawn carriages that persist as a local curiosity rather than a tourist gimmick.

The city and its surroundings are known for Burmese-influenced temple architecture, reflecting the period when Burmese teak traders and craftsmen settled here. **Wat Phrathat Lampang Luang**, about 20 kilometres south of the city, is one of the finest examples of northern Thai religious architecture — a cluster of chedis and carved wooden viharns set within a walled compound. **Wat Phra Kaeo Don Tao**, on the right bank of the Wang River, has a Burmese-style chapel with particularly refined decoration, probably dating from the late 18th century. **Wat Chedi Sao** (Temple of Twenty Chedis) sits in an open rice field north of town, its row of Burmese bell-shaped spires distinctive against the flat landscape.

Lampang is easy to reach from Chiang Mai by train or bus — about two hours — and makes a comfortable base for exploring the temples of the region.
