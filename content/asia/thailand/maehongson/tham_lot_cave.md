  Tham Lot is one of the largest cave systems in Thailand and contains one of the most extraordinary natural spectacles in Southeast Asia: at dusk, several million wrinkle-lipped bats emerge from the cave exit in a continuous stream that can last 30-45 minutes. The column of bats spirals upward and disperses across the surrounding forest. Archaeologists have also found wooden coffins inside the cave dating back approximately 1,200 years — placed in inaccessible locations by an unknown culture using techniques that remain unclear.

Tham Lot is a massive limestone cave 75 km northeast of Mae Hong Son, near the market village of Soppong (also called Pang Mapha). A river runs through the cave, and the standard visit involves a bamboo raft with a lantern-carrying guide through three interconnected chambers before emerging at the far end. The cave is large enough that sections of it require wading waist-deep; wear clothes you don't mind getting wet.

The cave is also notable for prehistoric wooden coffins placed in elevated positions by an ancient culture — some are still visible on ledges above the main chambers. The cave closes at 5pm; the bat emergence at dusk happens outside. Most visitors from Mae Hong Son combine the cave with a night in Soppong. Guides are mandatory and local; reasonable fee at the entrance.
