Northern Thailand was the independent Lanna kingdom until the late 19th century, and it still feels culturally distinct from the rest of the country — cooler hills, a different dialect, and an architectural style all its own. It's Thailand's trekking and mountain-scenery region, wedged between Myanmar and Laos.

[Chiang Mai](/asia/thailand/northernthailand/chiangmai) is the region's capital in every sense: walkable, temple-filled, and the base for almost every trip further north. [Chiang Rai](/asia/thailand/northernthailand/chiangrai) and the border town of [Chiang Saen](/asia/thailand/northernthailand/chiang_saen) sit at the edge of the Golden Triangle, where Thailand, Myanmar, and Laos meet. [Mae Hong Son](/asia/thailand/northernthailand/maehongson) and the loop through [Pai](/asia/thailand/northernthailand/pai) and [Soppong](/asia/thailand/northernthailand/soppong) are the classic mountain circuit — winding roads, mist-covered valleys, and hill-tribe villages.

Quieter still are [Nan](/asia/thailand/northernthailand/nan), tucked into its own remote valley with some of the region's finest temple murals, and [Mae Sariang](/asia/thailand/northernthailand/mae_sariang) and [Mae Sot](/asia/thailand/northernthailand/mae_sot) along the Myanmar border, where Burmese and Karen influence is everywhere. [Lampang](/asia/thailand/northernthailand/lampang) keeps a slower, more traditional pace than Chiang Mai, horse-drawn carriages included.
