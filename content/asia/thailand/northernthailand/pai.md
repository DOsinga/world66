Pai is a small town in Mae Hong Son Province, tucked into a mountain valley about 130 kilometres northwest of Chiang Mai on the winding Highway 1095. Once a backwater market town where hill tribe and Shan farmers came to trade, it discovered tourism in the 1990s and has settled into an identity as one of Thailand's most popular alternative escapes: a combination of hot springs, waterfalls, dramatic canyon landscapes, and a distinctly laid-back atmosphere that keeps visitors longer than they planned.

The town sits on the Pai River at around 400 metres elevation, which gives it cooler temperatures than the Thai lowlands and morning mist in the cool season. The surrounding mountains, mostly forested, are home to Karen, Lahu, and other hill tribe communities, and several villages are within motorbike range. The valley floor beyond the town has striking views of green rice fields against steep forested ridges.

What brings most visitors, besides the general ambience, is the collection of easy day trips within 15 kilometres: [Pai Canyon](/asia/thailand/northernthailand/pai/pai_canyon) for sunset views over the valley, [Tha Pai Hot Springs](/asia/thailand/northernthailand/pai/tha_pai_hot_springs) for a warm soak in forested pools, the [WWII Memorial Bridge](/asia/thailand/northernthailand/pai/wwii_memorial_bridge) over the Pai River, and [Mo Paeng Waterfall](/asia/thailand/northernthailand/pai/mo_paeng_waterfall) with its natural rock slides. The [Santichon Chinese Village](/asia/thailand/northernthailand/pai/santichon_village) on a hilltop to the west is kitsch but draws crowds for the views.

Pai has a clear traveller infrastructure: guesthouses lining the river, a walking street, cafes, yoga studios, and tour agencies renting motorbikes. It can feel overrun during Thai holidays and December–January high season, but outside those peaks it remains genuinely relaxed. The 762 curves on the road from Chiang Mai are memorable whether you drive yourself or take the bus — many arrive by minivan and leave on a rented motorbike.
