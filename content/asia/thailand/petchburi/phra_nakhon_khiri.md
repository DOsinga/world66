Phra Nakhon Khiri -- known locally as Khao Wang, "Palace Hill" -- is the city's defining landmark. King Rama IV built his royal summer retreat here in 1860, spreading the compound across three linked hilltops. The result is an extraordinary mix of Thai, Western neoclassical, and Chinese architectural styles, with royal audience halls, Buddhist temples, a white chedi visible from across the city, and a private observatory.

A cable car runs from the base to the summit, or you can walk up through forested paths shared with troops of macaques -- entertaining, but guard your food and sunglasses. The views over Phetchaburi and the surrounding plains from the top are some of the best in the region. The museum in the main palace building holds Rama IV's personal belongings and royal regalia.

Allow two hours, more if you want to explore all three peaks properly. Entry is 150 baht; the cable car costs extra.
