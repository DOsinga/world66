Wat Chalong is the largest and most important Buddhist temple on Phuket and one of the most visited religious sites in southern Thailand. The main complex includes a series of halls and chedis in Thai, Burmese, and Chinese architectural styles, the whole ensemble painted in orange and cream and set in well-maintained gardens. The most sacred building holds relics believed to be fragments of the Buddha's bones.

The temple is associated with two nineteenth-century monks — Luang Pho Chaem and Luang Pho Chuang — who helped suppress a rebellion of Chinese tin miners in 1876. Their life-size wax figures sit in glass cases in the main hall and are venerated by local worshippers who bring offerings of flowers, incense, and gold leaf. The atmosphere is genuinely religious rather than touristic, which makes it more interesting than many similar sites. Located about eight kilometres from Karon, easily reached by taxi or rented motorbike.
