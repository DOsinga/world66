Khao Yai is Thailand's oldest national park and one of its best. Together with Thap Lan, Pang Sida, and Ta Phraya parks, it forms the Dong Phayayen–Khao Yai Forest Complex, inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2005 for its exceptional biodiversity. The park covers 2,168 square kilometres of tropical and subtropical forest across the Sankamphaeng mountain range, with peaks rising to over 1,300 metres.

The wildlife here is extraordinary. Elephants, gaurs, tigers, leopards, and more than 300 bird species — including all four of Thailand's hornbill species — roam the forest. Wild elephants are regularly spotted along the park roads at dawn and dusk. Gibbons call at first light. The park has five major waterfalls; Haew Narok, a 150-metre double-drop cascade in the southern section, is the most dramatic.

Most visitors approach Khao Yai from the north via Pak Chong in Nakhon Ratchasima, but the park's eastern side is accessed through Prachinburi province — specifically via Pang Sida National Park and Route 3077. This eastern approach sees far fewer visitors and offers a wilder experience. Birdwatching and night safaris are best arranged with a guide based in the park area. Entrance fee is 400 baht for foreigners; accommodation inside the park can be booked through the Thai national parks website.
