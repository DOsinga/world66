The Royal Coast is the stretch of Gulf of Thailand shoreline running south from Bangkok toward the Isthmus of Kra — named for the Thai royal family's long association with the area, dating back to King Rama VI building a summer palace at Hua Hin in the 1920s. It's the easiest beach escape from the capital, reachable by car or train in a few hours, and correspondingly popular with Thai weekenders rather than the international backpacker circuit.

[Cha-am](/asia/thailand/royalcoast/chaam) and [Hua Hin](/asia/thailand/royalcoast/huahin) are the anchor towns, a long low-key beach stretching between them and past [Pranburi](/asia/thailand/royalcoast/pranburi) to the south. [Prachuap Khiri Khan](/asia/thailand/royalcoast/prachuap_khiri_khan) is quieter still, backed by dramatic limestone hills, and [Kui Buri National Park](/asia/thailand/royalcoast/kui_buri/kui_buri_national_park) inland is one of the most reliable places in Thailand to see wild elephants.

Further south, the coast turns rural and increasingly overlooked — [Bang Saphan](/asia/thailand/royalcoast/bang_saphan) and the offshore island of [Koh Talu](/asia/thailand/royalcoast/koh_talu_island) reward the extra travel time with empty beaches, before [Chumphon](/asia/thailand/royalcoast/chumphon) marks the practical gateway to the Gulf islands further south.
