Soppong (also called Pang Mapha) is a small traditional market village on the road between Pai and Mae Hong Son in northern Thailand. It sits at 750 metres elevation on a small river, surrounded by forested karst hills. Residents are mostly Shan and hilltribe people, and it sees far fewer tourists than nearby Pai while offering more natural attractions.

The main draw is [Tham Lot](/asia/thailand/soppong/tham_lot), one of the largest river caves in Thailand — a 1,666-metre cavern with three huge chambers, ancient teak coffins, and massive stalactite formations, navigated partly by bamboo raft. The nightly spectacle of millions of cave swifts returning to roost while bats simultaneously pour out to feed is alone worth the trip. Further into the hills are [Spirit Cave](/asia/thailand/soppong/spirit_cave), an important archaeological site, and [Tham Mae Lana](/asia/thailand/soppong/tham_mae_lana), one of Southeast Asia's longest cave systems for those wanting a real adventure.

The village market on Tuesdays draws [hilltribe people](/asia/thailand/soppong/hilltribe_villages) from the surrounding hills. Several guesthouses along the river organize village treks and caving trips. The Soppong River Inn has been running responsible tourism here for years. The area is genuinely off the beaten track compared to Mae Hong Son — a good base for a few days of outdoor exploration.
