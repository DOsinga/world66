Trat is the last proper town in eastern Thailand before the Cambodian border and the island ferries. Most travellers know it only as the place to sleep before boats to [Ko Chang](/asia/thailand/kohchang), [Ko Mak](/asia/thailand/kohchang/koh_mak), or Ko Kood, but that is selling it a little short.

The centre is small, low-rise, and easy to like: old wooden shop-houses, a good evening market, Chinese-Thai shrines, and a slower pace than the islands offshore. [Wat Buppharam](/asia/thailand/trat/wat_buppharam) is the standout historic temple, while [Wat Yothanimit](/asia/thailand/trat/wat_yothanimit) and the streets around the city pillar give you a compact sense of the town.

Trat also makes sense if you want to see the mainland coast before boarding a ferry. [Ban Nam Chiao](/asia/thailand/trat/ban_nam_chiao), southwest of town, is a working fishing and ecotourism community where Buddhist and Muslim families share the same estuary. [Laem Ngop Pier](/asia/thailand/trat/laem_ngop_pier) is the classic ferry-side waypoint.

Do not plan Trat as a beach destination; plan it as a humane pause between long bus rides and island boats. One night is enough for most travellers, but it is a pleasant one.
