---
title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

Adana takes its food very seriously, and the most serious food of all is the Adana kebab — coarsely hand-chopped lamb mixed with hot pepper and tail fat, pressed onto a flat sword-like skewer and grilled over charcoal. It is registered with the EU as a protected geographical indication and the rules are strict: the meat must be hand-chopped (not minced), the seasoning must be salt and red pepper alone, and the skewer must be a particular width. Eat it on flatbread, with grilled tomatoes, raw onion macerated in sumac, and a glass of cold *şalgam* — a fermented turnip-and-purple-carrot juice that is sharp, salty and the perfect counterbalance to the chilli heat.

The classic kebab strip is Kazancılar Çarşısı near the [Ulu Camii](/asia/turkey/adana/great_mosque), but most locals will tell you that the best version in town is at Kebapçı Yüzevler in Reşatbey or at Onbaşılar near the river. Open-air courtyard restaurants up on Tepebağ serve the kebab alongside the city's other essentials: *bici bici* (rosewater shaved-ice dessert), *analı kızlı* (stuffed meatball soup in tomato broth) and *şırdan* (a delicately spiced sheep-stomach dish that is more interesting than it sounds).

For breakfast, try a *kahvaltı salonu* on Atatürk Caddesi — long tables of cheeses, olives, jams, eggs, fresh bread and pots of tea.
