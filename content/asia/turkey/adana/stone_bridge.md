The Taşköprü is a 300-metre stone bridge of 14 arches spanning the Seyhan, just east of the old town. It is the most striking single piece of heritage in Adana and one of the oldest still-functioning bridges anywhere. Although heavily restored, the lower courses of the central piers and the springers of the main arches are demonstrably second-century Roman work; the later repairs followed the original profile carefully enough that the bridge reads as a unified composition.

Now closed to cars, it makes a pleasant evening crossing — locals come here to watch the river light up, and there are usually a few simmit sellers and tea vendors at either end. The view of the [Sabancı Central Mosque](/asia/turkey/adana/sabanci_merkez_mosque) upstream is the postcard shot.
