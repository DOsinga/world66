The Bayezid II Mosque complex is the finest Ottoman monument in Amasya, and one of the more accomplished examples of 15th-century Ottoman architecture anywhere in Anatolia. The mosque itself is elegantly proportioned, with a single large dome and a graceful portico of pointed arches. The stonework alternates between light and dark bands — a technique borrowed from Seljuk builders and adapted into the Ottoman idiom.

The adjacent Darüşşifa (hospital) is what makes this complex historically significant. Built at the same time as the mosque, it functioned as a medical and psychiatric hospital well into the 19th century. The vaulted chambers and the acoustics of the central courtyard were used for music therapy — a method of treating mental illness that Anatolian physicians had refined over several centuries. The space is now open for visitors and worth a look.
