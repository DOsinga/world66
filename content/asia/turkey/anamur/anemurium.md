Anemurium lies at the southwestern edge of Anamur, near the cape, and covers a substantial area between the mountains and the sea. The city was founded by the Phoenicians, came under Roman control, and flourished through the Byzantine period before the Arab raids of the 7th century emptied it for good. Because it was abandoned rather than absorbed into a later settlement, the ruins are unusually complete: streets are traceable, public buildings identifiable, and private houses still show their room divisions.

The necropolis alone is worth the visit — hundreds of tomb-houses clustered on the hillside, some with painted interiors. The two bath complexes demonstrate Roman engineering clearly, with the underfloor heating channels intact. The site is open to visitors with minimal infrastructure, so bring water and sun protection; the exposed ground gets hot in summer. Allow at least two hours if you want to cover the main areas properly.
