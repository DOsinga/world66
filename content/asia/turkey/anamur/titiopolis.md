It was sufficiently important to have its own bishop in the early Christian era, appearing in ecclesiastical records from the 4th century onward. Titiopolis is an ancient Cilician town site a few kilometres north of Anamur, identifiable on the ground but not formally developed for visitors. The location places it in the foothills above the coastal plain, which fits the pattern of Cilician settlements that used elevated positions for security before the Roman pacification of the region made coastal sites more viable.

The site is genuinely obscure — it rarely appears in guidebooks, and there are no signs directing visitors to it from the town. For travellers with an interest in the density of ancient settlement along this coast, it is worth knowing that Anamur district contained multiple layered sites beyond the showpieces of Anemurium and Mamure. Titiopolis represents the more typical pattern of secondary Cilician towns: significant enough to be named and bishoped, modest enough to leave little above ground. Visit Anemurium first; consider Titiopolis a supplementary stop if you have your own transport and genuine interest in the texture of ancient settlement.
