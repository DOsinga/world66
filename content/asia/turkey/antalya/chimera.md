story: >
  The Chimaera flames have burned continuously for at least 2,500 years — the ancient Greeks attributed them to the breath of a monster with a lion's head, a goat's body, and a serpent's tail. The fires are real: methane and hydrogen seep through fissures in the Lycian rock and ignite on contact with air. They cannot be permanently extinguished. Sailors once used them as a navigation beacon.

The Chimaera at Yanartaş, near Çıralı, is a hillside scattered with dozens of eternal flames burning from natural gas seeps in the rock. The effect is genuinely eerie, particularly at night, when the flames glow across the hillside among the ruins of a Byzantine church that was presumably built here precisely to contain whatever power the ancients attributed to the spot.

The walk up to the flames takes about 30 minutes from the car park. Go in the evening — the flames are more visible and the heat less brutal. It is about 80 km from Antalya (roughly 90 minutes by car). Combine with a night in Çıralı, which has a good pebble beach and some of the most relaxed accommodation on the Turkish Mediterranean coast.
