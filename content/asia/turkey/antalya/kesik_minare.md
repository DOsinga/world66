  The Kesik Minare has been repurposed more times than almost any building in Turkey: it was built as a Roman temple, converted to a Byzantine church, converted to a mosque by the Seljuks (when the minaret was added), converted back to a church by the Crusaders, then back to a mosque by the Ottomans. A fire in the 19th century destroyed the wooden roof and the top of the minaret, leaving the truncated tower that gives it its current name.

The Kesik Minare (literally "Broken Minaret") is one of the most historically layered monuments in Kaleiçi. The ruin is not much to look at from outside — a fire-damaged hulk of stonework in an overgrown yard — but the accumulation of architectural history it contains is remarkable. Roman column drums are visible in the walls; Byzantine stone carvings mix with Seljuk tilework; Ottoman additions sit above the whole.

The site is usually accessible during daylight hours. It requires a short detour from the main tourist lanes of Kaleiçi but is worth it for anyone interested in the palimpsest of religions and rulers that characterises this coast.
