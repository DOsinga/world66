story: >
  Nicholas of Myra, the real-life bishop who became the template for Santa Claus, was born in Patara around 270 CE and served as bishop of Myra until his death around 343 CE. His bones were stolen from Myra by Venetian merchants in 1087 and taken to Bari in southern Italy, where they remain in the Basilica di San Nicola. The church in Myra is the place of his original burial.
---

Myra, near the coastal town of Demre (about 160 km west of Antalya on the Lycian coast), is one of the most remarkable sites in Turkey. The city has an extraordinary rock-cut Lycian necropolis — hundreds of elaborate tombs carved directly into a cliff face, some with carved facades that imitate the fronts of wooden houses, others with painted interiors still faintly visible. Below the cliff, a well-preserved Roman theatre.

The Church of St Nicholas in Demre town, a Byzantine building on the site of the saint's original church, draws pilgrims as well as tourists. The combination of pagan Lycian tombs, Christian history, and the Santa Claus connection makes for an unusual day trip. Combine with Olympos and Phaselis to the east for a full coastal drive.
