  Selge was one of the most remote and independent cities in the ancient Mediterranean world — a Pisidian mountain city that maintained its independence from both the Persians and Alexander the Great by virtue of its position in the mountains above the Köprülü Canyon. When Alexander passed through Pamphylia, Selge supplied him with guides and mercenaries rather than submitting.

Selge, a ruined Pisidian city high in the mountains 80 km northeast of Antalya, combines one of the most dramatic settings of any ancient site in Turkey with excellent rafting and hiking in the Köprülü Canyon below. The remains include a theatre, a stadium, a temple of Zeus, and extensive city walls, all perched at 950 metres with views of the mountains and the canyon.

The Köprülü Canyon National Park, in the valley below, has two remarkably preserved Roman bridges still spanning the river, and white-water rafting on the Köprüçay River from spring to autumn. The full excursion — canyon, Roman bridges, rafting, and Selge — makes one of the best day trips from Antalya.
