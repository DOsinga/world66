  Side's name is not Turkish — it comes from the local pre-Greek language and appears in inscriptions that scholars still cannot fully decipher. The city may have been founded by Aeolian Greeks as early as the 7th century BCE, but unlike most of the other cities of Pamphylia, it maintained a distinct local identity and language long after Greek colonisation.
---

Side, 75 km east of Antalya, is the most dramatic of the coastal ancient cities — a former Roman town of 60,000 inhabitants whose ruins sit right inside a modern resort village. The theatre, the temples of Apollo and Athena, and the agora are surrounded by hotels, restaurants, and souvenir shops, which sounds bad but is somehow charming. The temple of Apollo, with its five standing columns at the sea's edge, is the most photographed image on the Turquoise Coast.

The beaches on either side of the promontory are sandy and good. The Site Museum, housed in the Roman baths, has a strong collection of local finds. Side can be reached by dolmuş from Antalya's main bus station in about 90 minutes.
