  When the Persian general Harpagus besieged Xanthos in 546 BCE, the Xanthian defenders — seeing defeat was inevitable — gathered their women, children, and valuables into the acropolis, set it on fire, and then sallied out to die fighting. The entire population perished. The city was repopulated by 80 families who happened to be away at the time. The same thing happened again when Brutus besieged the city in 42 BCE.

Xanthos, in the Xanthos River valley about 230 km west of Antalya, was the capital of ancient Lycia and the site of some of the most dramatic events in Lycian history. The ruins include a Lycian acropolis, a Roman agora, several extraordinary Lycian pillar tombs, and the remarkable Nereid Monument — or rather, its base, since the monument itself was removed to the British Museum in the 1840s.

Xanthos and Letoon (the Lycian federation's sacred sanctuary, 5 km away) are a UNESCO World Heritage Site together. They are remote and rarely crowded. The drive from Antalya along the Lycian coast road is part of the experience.
