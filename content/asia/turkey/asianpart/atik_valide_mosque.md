The Atik Valide is one of the great imperial mosques of Istanbul, commissioned by the chief architect Mimar Sinan in 1570 for Nurbanu Sultan, wife of Sultan Selim II. It crowns a hill above Üsküdar with commanding views across the Bosphorus to the European shore. The mosque complex includes a medrese (theological school), a hospice, a caravanserai, and a hamam — a complete Ottoman institution in miniature.

The interior is spacious and well-lit, with Iznik tile panels of exceptional quality. This is one of Sinan's later works, built after his masterpieces at Süleymaniye and Edirne, and the handling of light and space is assured. The mosque is less visited than the great European-side mosques, which makes it all the more worth coming for.
