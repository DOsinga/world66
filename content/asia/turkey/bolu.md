Bolu sits in the green, damp mountains between Istanbul and Ankara, a city that many people only see from the highway. That is a pity. The town itself is modest, but it makes a good base for some of western Turkey's most appealing lake and forest country.

The main draw is nature. [Lake Abant National Park](/asia/turkey/bolu/lake_abant_national_park) has a broad mountain lake ringed by forest and walking paths, while [Yedigoller National Park](/asia/turkey/bolu/yedigoller_national_park) is the wilder autumn classic, with seven small lakes tucked among beech, oak, and hornbeam. Closer to town, [Golcuk Nature Park](/asia/turkey/bolu/golcuk_nature_park) is the easy half-day choice, especially if you want a lake walk without a long drive.

Bolu also has a food reputation in Turkey. The nearby town of Mengen is famous for training chefs, and local menus lean into mountain cooking: soups, stuffed greens, rice dishes, cheese, and hearty bread. It is not a place for grand monuments, though [Bolu Museum](/asia/turkey/bolu/bolu_museum) gives the city a useful historical anchor, with finds from the ancient Bithynian and Roman layers of the region.

Come for a spare day if you are crossing between Istanbul and Ankara and want forest air instead of another motorway stop. Stay longer if you want to use Bolu as a base for Abant, Yedigoller, and the wider Black Sea foothills.
