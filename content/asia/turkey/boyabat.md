Boyabat sits inland from Sinop in the Gokirmak valley, a farm town watched over by a fortress on a sheer rock. It is not a polished tourist stop, but it has a strong sense of place: rice fields in the valley, brick and tile workshops, old houses below the castle, and market streets that still serve the villages around it.

The [castle](/asia/turkey/boyabat/boyabat_castle) is the reason most travellers stop. Its walls follow the cliff above town, and tunnels and chambers under the rock hint at a much older defensive site. In town, the [living cultural heritage museum](/asia/turkey/boyabat/town_museum) gives the old houses some context.

Boyabat's appeal is compact and a little rough-edged. Add the [Salar rock tomb](/asia/turkey/boyabat/salar_rock_tomb) west of town and a Monday market lunch of local kebab or rice dishes, and it becomes a worthwhile inland detour rather than just a road stop to the Black Sea.
