Burdur is a quiet market town in Turkey's Lakes District, about 130 kilometres inland from Antalya. It has never been a showpiece city. Earthquakes removed much of the older fabric, the castle has vanished, and the centre is practical rather than pretty. What makes Burdur worth considering is the landscape and archaeology around it.

The [Burdur Museum](/asia/turkey/burdur/burdur_museum) is the essential stop, especially for its finds from Sagalassos, one of the great ruined cities of Pisidia. Around the centre, the [Grand Mosque and Clocktower](/asia/turkey/burdur/grand_mosque_and_clocktower) give a small historic focus. South of town, [İnsuyu Cave](/asia/turkey/burdur/insuyu_cave) cuts into the limestone, while [Lake Burdur](/asia/turkey/burdur/lake_burdur) stretches west as a salty, alkaline bird lake.

Burdur is not a rival to Antalya, Pamukkale, or Cappadocia. It is a one-day stop for travellers with wheels or archaeology interests, and it works best as part of a Lakes District route rather than a destination on its own.
