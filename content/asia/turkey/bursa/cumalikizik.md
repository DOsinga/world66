Cumalıkızık is the village that makes Bursa's Ottoman story feel domestic rather than imperial. Its cobbled lanes, timber-and-stone houses, courtyards, and breakfast tables sit at the foot of Uludağ, showing the rural side of the early Ottoman capital that the mosques and tombs in central Bursa cannot.

It is popular, especially on weekends, and the village can feel more like a breakfast excursion than an open-air museum. Go early if you want the lanes before the crowds, then stay long enough to notice the uneven houses, small market stalls, and mountain backdrop that explain why UNESCO included it with Bursa's founding monuments.
