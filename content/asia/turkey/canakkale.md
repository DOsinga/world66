Canakkale sits on the narrowest part of the Dardanelles — just over a kilometre across — the strait that separates Europe from Asia and links the Aegean Sea to the south with the Sea of Marmara to the north.

Somewhere near here Alexander the Great crossed the straits on his campaign against the Persian Emperor. According to legend, he ordered his men to beat the waves to show them their master was coming to cross the sea.

Many travellers — including many from Australia and New Zealand — come to Canakkale to visit the World War One [Gallipoli battlefields](/asia/turkey/canakkale/gallipoli_battlefields). In 1915, the German commander Liman von Sanders and Mustafa Kemal Pasha, later known as Atatürk, defended the coast against the Allied forces. More than 250,000 soldiers lost their lives during the campaign. Many are buried in the cemeteries of the surrounding area.

Nearby is the ancient city of [Troy](/asia/turkey/canakkale/troy), where people have lived continuously since 3000 BC. The city itself has the Ottoman fortress and naval museum at [Çimenlik Castle](/asia/turkey/canakkale/cimenlik_castle), its European-shore twin at [Kilitbahir](/asia/turkey/canakkale/kilitbahir_castle), and on the seafront the famously incongruous [wooden horse](/asia/turkey/canakkale/trojan_horse) from the 2004 Hollywood film.
