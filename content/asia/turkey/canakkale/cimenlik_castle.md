Çimenlik Castle (Kale-i Sultaniye) was built by Mehmed II in 1462 — the same year as Kilitbahir Castle directly across the water on the European shore — to control shipping through the narrowest point of the Dardanelles. The two fortresses together gave Çanakkale its name: çanak (pot) and kale (castle), after the local pottery that supplied the garrison.

Today the castle houses the **Çanakkale Naval Museum**, with displays on the 1915 Battle of the Dardanelles and a parade of exhibits and outdoor artillery pieces. The replica of the minelayer **Nusret** — whose mines laid in March 1915 sank the Allied battleships Bouvet, Ocean, and Irresistible and saved the Dardanelles for the Ottomans — is the highlight. Closed on Mondays.
