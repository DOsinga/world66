Eceabat sits on the European shore of the Dardanelles, directly across the water from [Çanakkale](/asia/turkey/canakkale). For many travellers it is less a town to linger in than a practical base: ferries cross the strait from here, and the roads west and south lead into the Gallipoli battlefield landscape.

That practical role is still useful, even after the 1915 Çanakkale Bridge pulled much of the through traffic away. Eceabat is small, modern, and easy to understand, with ferries, cafes, tour offices, and guesthouses clustered near the waterfront. It is a good place to sleep if you want an early start for Anzac Cove, Lone Pine, Chunuk Bair, Cape Helles, or the Turkish memorials spread across the peninsula.

The strongest sights are not in the town centre. [Kilitbahir Castle](/asia/turkey/canakkale/kilitbahir_castle), a short way south, guards the narrowest point of the strait, while the [Gallipoli battlefields](/asia/turkey/canakkale/gallipoli_battlefields) need a car, a guide, or a patient taxi driver. If you are coming from Çanakkale, the ferry crossing itself is part of the appeal: Asia falls away behind you and the peninsula opens ahead.
