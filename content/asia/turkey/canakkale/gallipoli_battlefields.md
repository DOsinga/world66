The Gallipoli peninsula, across the Dardanelles from Çanakkale, was the site of one of the great Allied failures of the First World War — and one of the foundational events of modern Australian, New Zealand, and Turkish national identity. The 1915 campaign cost over 250,000 lives on each side; the trenches, cemeteries, and memorials are preserved across a long stretch of coast and ridge as the Gallipoli Peninsula Historical National Park.

The main sites — **ANZAC Cove**, **Lone Pine** and **Chunuk Bair**, the **Çanakkale Şehitler Anıtı** (Turkish Martyrs' Memorial), and the **Helles Memorial** at the southern tip — are spread over a long line and very hard to do without transport. Most travellers either join a small-group tour from Çanakkale or Eceabat (around 8 hours, lunch usually included) or hire a car. **Anzac Day, 25 April**, sees thousands of antipodean pilgrims and an overnight dawn service; book months ahead if you want to come then.
