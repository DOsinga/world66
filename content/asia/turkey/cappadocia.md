Cappadocia is one of Turkey's most extraordinary landscapes — a high volcanic plateau in central Anatolia where millions of years of eruption, erosion, and human ingenuity have produced something unlike anywhere else on earth. The famous *peri bacaları* — fairy chimneys — are columns of soft volcanic tuff capped with harder basalt, carved by wind and rain into cones, spires, mushrooms, and stranger shapes. What makes Cappadocia uniquely compelling is that people have lived inside this rock for thousands of years: early Christians carved entire church complexes into the hillsides, Byzantine monks painted frescoes in underground chapels, and whole populations retreated into elaborate underground cities when invaders swept across the plateau.

The beating heart of the region is [Göreme](/asia/turkey/cappadocia/goreme), a small town encircled by valleys of fairy chimneys, whose [Open-Air Museum](/asia/turkey/cappadocia/goreme/goreme_open_air_museum) preserves thirty rock-cut Byzantine churches from the 10th to 13th centuries. [Ürgüp](/asia/turkey/cappadocia/urgup) offers a slightly more polished base, with boutique cave hotels and Cappadocia's own wine industry. [Avanos](/asia/turkey/cappadocia/avanos) is the pottery town, sitting on the red banks of the Kızılırmak river. South of these three, the underground city of [Derinkuyu](/asia/turkey/cappadocia/derinkuyu) descends eight storeys into the earth — one of hundreds of such cities in the region, capable of sheltering tens of thousands of people.

The [Ihlara Valley](/asia/turkey/cappadocia/ihlaravalley) in the southwest is a deeply cut gorge of the Melendiz river, with more than forty rock churches carved into its walls — less visited than Göreme and more rewarding for it. [Zelve](/asia/turkey/cappadocia/zelve) is an abandoned troglodyte village where people lived in the carved cones until the 1950s. [Uçhisar](/asia/turkey/cappadocia/uchisar) crowns the highest natural rock castle in the region, with panoramic views across the valleys. And [Hacıbektaş](/asia/turkey/cappadocia/hacibektas) is a pilgrimage town for followers of the Bektashi order, whose great 13th-century saint is buried there.

For most visitors, the dawn hot-air balloon flights — rising above the chimneys as the sun picks out every fold in the landscape — are the single most memorable experience in Turkey. Book in advance and allow for cancellation by wind. Cappadocia rewards slow travel: the valleys are best walked, the light changes dramatically through the day, and the wine at sunset in Ürgüp needs no justification.

## Destinations in Cappadocia

- [Göreme](/asia/turkey/cappadocia/goreme) — the main base, with the Open-Air Museum and the best valley walks
- [Ürgüp](/asia/turkey/cappadocia/urgup) — upmarket cave hotels, wine bars, and easy access to Ortahisar
- [Uçhisar](/asia/turkey/cappadocia/uchisar) — the rock castle town with the best views in the region
- [Avanos](/asia/turkey/cappadocia/avanos) — pottery workshops on the red river
- [Çavuşin](/asia/turkey/cappadocia/cavusin) — a ruined village with an important early church; good hiking base
- [Zelve](/asia/turkey/cappadocia/zelve) — abandoned troglodyte valley, eerily beautiful
- [Derinkuyu](/asia/turkey/cappadocia/derinkuyu) — the deepest underground city open to visitors
- [Ihlara Valley](/asia/turkey/cappadocia/ihlaravalley) — the gorge with rock churches, best walked end to end
- [Nevşehir](/asia/turkey/cappadocia/nevsehir) — the regional capital and transport hub
- [Hacıbektaş](/asia/turkey/cappadocia/hacibektas) — Bektashi pilgrimage town
