Göreme sits in the heart of [Cappadocia](/asia/turkey/cappadocia), surrounded by a moonscape of volcanic tuff carved by millions of years of wind and water into cones, pillars, and the tall phallic columns that Turks politely call peri bacaları — fairy chimneys. The town itself is small and easy to navigate, with most guesthouses and tour agencies clustered along a few streets above the bus station, but the landscape that rings it on every side is extraordinary.

The Göreme Open-Air Museum, a short walk east of town, is the anchor attraction. A UNESCO World Heritage Site, it preserves a compact Byzantine monastic settlement where some thirty rock-cut churches, chapels, and refectories still bear their painted frescoes. The Karanlık Kilise (Dark Church) has the most vivid colours, protected for centuries by its almost lightless interior — it costs extra but is worth it. Outside the museum boundaries, the valleys fan out in every direction: Rose Valley and Red Valley glow amber and pink in the late afternoon, Pigeon Valley links Göreme to [Uçhisar](/asia/turkey/cappadocia/uchisar) along a well-marked trail, and Love Valley to the north offers some of the most surreal rock formations in the region.

Göreme is also the launch point for Cappadocia's hot-air balloon flights, which take off before dawn and drift over the chimneys as the sun rises. Prices are not cheap but the experience is, for most visitors, the single most memorable thing they do in Turkey. Book a day or two in advance; flights are cancelled in high wind.

The town has filled up with cave hotels, rooftop restaurants, and tour operators since the 1990s, but it has managed to keep some of its village character. Wander up past the main street and you will still find rock-cut homes where families hang laundry between the chimneys and keep pigeons in the carved dovecotes.
