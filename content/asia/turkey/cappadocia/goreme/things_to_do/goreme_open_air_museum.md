A UNESCO World Heritage Site and the single must-see attraction in Cappadocia. The open-air museum is a compact monastic settlement carved into volcanic tuff, preserving around thirty rock-cut churches, chapels, and refectories from the 10th to 13th centuries. Most are decorated with Byzantine frescoes in varying states of preservation.

The Karanlık Kilise (Dark Church) is the jewel of the complex — its near-total darkness preserved vivid blue and ochre frescoes of Christ Pantocrator, the Nativity, and the Crucifixion. Entry costs extra but the colours are incomparably better than any other church here. The Tokalı Kilise (Buckle Church), just outside the main gate, is included in the standard ticket and is actually the largest church in Göreme, with an impressive painted barrel vault.

The museum is about 1 km east of town centre on the main road — an easy walk or a short taxi ride. Go early; the site fills up with tour groups by mid-morning.

  The Dark Church earned its name from the single small window that lets in almost no daylight. When UNESCO-funded restorers reached it in the 1990s, they found frescoes so vivid they looked freshly painted — 900 years of darkness had kept the pigments intact while every other church in the region had faded.
