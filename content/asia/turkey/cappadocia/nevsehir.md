Nevşehir is the provincial capital and the main transport hub for [Cappadocia](/asia/turkey/cappadocia), the extraordinary landscape of volcanic rock formations, underground cities, and cave churches that draws visitors from across the world. The city itself is functional rather than picturesque — most travellers pass through quickly on their way to [Göreme](/asia/turkey/cappadocia/goreme), [Ürgüp](/asia/turkey/cappadocia/urgup), or [Avanos](/asia/turkey/cappadocia/avanos) — but it deserves more than a cursory transit stop.

The city was transformed in the 18th century by Damat İbrahim Paşa, a local who rose to become Ottoman grand vizier. His [mosque complex](/asia/turkey/cappadocia/nevsehir/damat_ibrahim_pasha_kulliyesi) remains the finest building in the city, set in the old quarter below the [hilltop castle](/asia/turkey/cappadocia/nevsehir/nevsehir_castle). The [Nevşehir Museum](/asia/turkey/cappadocia/nevsehir/nevsehir_museum) holds a surprisingly strong collection of Hittite, Roman, and Byzantine finds from across the province. The [bazaar](/asia/turkey/cappadocia/nevsehir/nevsehir_bazaar) operates at local rather than tourist prices.

Buses from Ankara, Istanbul, and other Turkish cities terminate here, making Nevşehir the practical entry point for the region. Dolmuş minibuses run from the city centre to the main Cappadocian villages throughout the day.
