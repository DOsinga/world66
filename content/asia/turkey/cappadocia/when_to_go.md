---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

Cappadocia sits on a high plateau — around 1,000 to 1,400 metres — and the climate reflects it. Summers are hot (30°C and above in July and August) and winters are genuinely cold, with heavy snow from December through February. Spring and autumn are the best times to visit.

**April to June** brings mild temperatures, green hillsides, and good balloon-flying conditions. April can still be cool in the evenings. **September and October** are arguably the finest months: the heat has broken, the summer crowds have thinned, and the light is excellent for photography. The grape harvest in the Ürgüp vineyards happens in September.

**July and August** are peak season — high prices, tour buses at every site, and balloon queues. If you go in summer, be at the Open-Air Museum before 9am and finish the main valleys by noon. **Winter** (December to March) is quiet, often beautiful when there is snow on the chimneys, and significantly cheaper. Hot-air balloon flights are more likely to be cancelled by wind and cold, and some smaller guesthouses close entirely.

The **Hacıbektaş Festival**, held in mid-August, draws thousands of pilgrims and visitors to the Bektashi shrine town and is worth timing a visit around if you are interested in Alevi-Bektashi culture.
