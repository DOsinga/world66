Zelve is a rock-cut monastery complex and open-air museum about 10 kilometres from [Göreme](/asia/turkey/cappadocia/goreme) on the road towards [Avanos](/asia/turkey/cappadocia/avanos), set in a series of narrow valleys carved by erosion into the volcanic tuff of Cappadocia. The site was continuously inhabited from the Byzantine period until 1952, when the last residents were evacuated because the soft rock was becoming dangerously unstable. Today it stands as the most completely inhabited rock-cut village you can visit in the region — a place where domestic and religious life were entirely cut into the cliff faces.

The [Zelve Open Air Museum](/asia/turkey/cappadocia/zelve/open_air_museum) encompasses three interconnected valleys. Unlike the more famous Göreme Open Air Museum, Zelve has fewer painted churches but more archaeological depth — the landscape is denser with carved rooms, tunnels, cisterns, mills, and dovecotes that give a picture of how a working community actually lived here. The [Fish Church](/asia/turkey/cappadocia/zelve/fish_church) and [Grape Church](/asia/turkey/cappadocia/zelve/grape_church) are the main decorated interiors, with simple but striking carvings and inscriptions.

The exploration here is genuinely physical: you will climb, duck, and squeeze through tunnels, and the site rewards those who wander off the main paths to investigate less visited corners. A torch is useful. The valleys can be explored in two to three hours at an unhurried pace.

The site is managed as part of the Göreme Historical National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. An entrance fee applies and a combined ticket with the Göreme Open Air Museum is available.
