Dalaman is a small farming town on Turkey's southwestern Mediterranean coast, known almost entirely for its international airport. Charter flights from across Europe land here daily, funneling visitors toward the spectacular destinations nearby — [Fethiye](/asia/turkey/fethiye), [Dalyan](/asia/turkey/dalyan), and Marmaris among them.

The town itself is unremarkable, and few visitors linger longer than it takes to catch a bus or pick up a rental car. But the surrounding region is genuinely beautiful: a stretch of coast where ancient Lycian ruins meet turquoise water, loggerhead turtles nest on protected beaches, and the Dalaman River cuts through forested hills ideal for [white-water rafting](/asia/turkey/dalaman/dalaman_river_rafting). The [Kaunos rock tombs](/asia/turkey/dalaman/kaunos_rock_tombs) carved into the cliffs above the Dalyan River are one of the great sights of the Turkish coast. [Iztuzu Beach](/asia/turkey/dalaman/iztuzu_beach), a protected loggerhead nesting ground accessible by boat from Dalyan, is one of the most beautiful and undeveloped beaches in Turkey. The [Sultaniye thermal springs](/asia/turkey/dalaman/sultaniye_thermal_springs) on Lake Köyceğiz — mud baths at 40°C — are accessible on a boat trip from Dalyan.

If you do find yourself with time in Dalaman, there are a handful of decent restaurants and cafes near the centre of town. The real draws, though, are all within easy reach — and that is precisely the point of being here.
