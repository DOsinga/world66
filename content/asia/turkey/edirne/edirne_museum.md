The Edirne Archaeology and Ethnography Museum sits close to the Selimiye Mosque complex and holds collections covering the long human history of Thrace — a region that has been settled, fought over, and traded through for millennia. The archaeological section includes finds from the Bronze Age through the Hellenistic, Roman, Byzantine, and Ottoman periods: ceramics, coins, jewellery, weapons, and architectural fragments. The ethnographic galleries cover Ottoman and local Thracian folk culture, with embroidery, costumes, household items, and documentation of traditional crafts.

The museum is modest in scale but useful for grounding the city's monuments in context. After walking through mosques and bazaars, seeing the objects that filled them — the lamps, the metalwork, the everyday domestic goods — adds texture to what might otherwise remain abstract architectural history. The building itself, constructed in the early Republican period, is well-kept and the displays are adequately labelled. A small entry fee applies.
