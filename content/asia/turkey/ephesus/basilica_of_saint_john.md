The ruined Basilica of Saint John on Ayasuluk Hill in Selçuk was one of the greatest churches of the Byzantine world, built by Emperor Justinian I in the 6th century over the traditional tomb of the Apostle John. At its height it had six domes, a nave nearly 120 metres long, and marble columns brought from across the empire. What remains today — substantial walls, re-erected columns, the outline of the floor plan — gives a strong impression of the scale. A cruciform tomb at the crossing marks the spot where John was believed to be buried.

The hill commands excellent views over Selçuk and the surrounding plain. The İsa Bey Mosque — a 14th-century masterpiece of Anatolian Seljuk architecture — sits immediately below the basilica and is worth including in the same visit. Both are within walking distance of each other and of the Temple of Artemis column, making the whole area around Selçuk worth a half-day even if you have already visited the main Ephesus site. Tickets are sold at the basilica entrance.
