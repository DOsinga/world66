A small stone structure on Mount Koressos, about 7 km from the main Ephesus site, traditionally identified as the house where the Virgin Mary lived out her final years after leaving Jerusalem with the Apostle John. The tradition is based partly on the early Christian belief that John brought Mary to Ephesus, and partly on a series of visions described by a German nun in the early nineteenth century whose descriptions matched the site remarkably closely. Three popes have visited; Catholic, Orthodox, and Muslim pilgrims all come here.

The setting is the main draw regardless of historical views: a wooded hillside, genuinely peaceful and cool even in summer, with a small chapel and a wall of prayer requests where visitors tie cloth and paper wishes. The site is 7 km from the upper (south) gate of Ephesus and is most easily reached by taxi or as part of a guided tour. A small museum at the entrance covers the history of the site and the Emmerich story.
