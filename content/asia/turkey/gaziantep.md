*Asia > Turkey > Gaziantep*

Gaziantep is Turkey's food capital — a city whose culinary reputation extends far beyond its borders and whose residents will tell you with complete seriousness that everything you know about baklava is wrong until you have eaten it here. The city has built an entire museum around its cuisine, and the local tradition of food knowledge — which restaurants opened when, whose grandmother invented which recipe — runs through daily life with the intensity of an oral history project.

The gastronomic identity is grounded in Gaziantep's position at the edge of the Fertile Crescent, where Anatolian, Arab, and Kurdish culinary traditions converge. But the city has other claims to attention: the [Zeugma Mosaic Museum](/asia/turkey/gaziantep/zeugma_mosaic_museum) houses one of the world's great collections of Roman mosaic art, salvaged from an ancient city submerged when the Birecik Dam was built. The [Gaziantep Castle](/asia/turkey/gaziantep/gaziantep_castle) dominates the centre from a basalt mound that has been fortified since at least the second century CE. And the old copper bazaar and historic caravanserais preserve a tangible sense of the Silk Road city this once was.

Gaziantep sits 185 kilometres east of Adana near the Syrian border, and the proximity is reflected in the population — the city has absorbed hundreds of thousands of Syrian refugees since 2011, and Syrian Arabic is now widely spoken in some neighbourhoods. The old city is compact and walkable; the surrounding streets of restored Ottoman-era houses are among the most attractive in eastern Turkey.

The city is best known internationally for its pistachio baklava, its kebabs (especially the local spicy variety called beyran), and its olive-oil soap. All three are worth seeking out. Come hungry.
