Gebze is easy to dismiss from the train window: factories, logistics yards, and the eastern edge of the Istanbul commuter belt. Stay a little longer and it becomes more interesting. This is where the Gulf of İzmit narrows, where Byzantine walls looked over the water, and where Osman Hamdi Bey, one of the great figures of Ottoman painting and archaeology, kept his summer house.

The best day in Gebze starts in Eskihisar, the old seaside village below the town. [Eskihisar Castle](/asia/turkey/gebze/eskihisar_castle) stands above the ferry port, while the nearby [Osman Hamdi Bey Museum](/asia/turkey/gebze/osman_hamdi_bey_museum) gives the place a more intimate cultural weight than most industrial suburbs can claim. East along the coast, [Hereke Silk Weaving and Carpet Factory](/asia/turkey/gebze/hereke_silk_weaving_and_carpet_factory) connects the district to the palace carpets of late Ottoman Istanbul.

The surprise is inland. [Ballıkayalar Nature Park](/asia/turkey/gebze/ballikayalar_nature_park) cuts a limestone valley through the countryside north of Gebze, with hiking, picnic spots, waterfalls after rain, and some of the Marmara region's best-known sport climbing. Gebze is not a romantic base, but if you are already crossing the eastern Marmara, it has enough texture for a solid spare day.
