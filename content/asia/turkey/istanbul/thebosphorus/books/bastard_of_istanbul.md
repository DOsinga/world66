Two families — one Turkish, one Armenian-American — converge in modern Istanbul, and the city itself becomes the main subject: its cafes, its political tensions, its complicated relationship with the Armenian genocide. Shafak writes from inside Istanbul's social fabric in a way outsiders cannot, and the novel captures the particular texture of the city's liberal, secular middle class. Controversial in Turkey when published in 2006.
