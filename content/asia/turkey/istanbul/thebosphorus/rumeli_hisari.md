The greatest of Istanbul's Bosphorus fortresses stands on the European shore at the strait's narrowest point, built in 1452 by Mehmed II in preparation for his conquest of Constantinople. Three large towers and a curtain wall enclose a broad area on the hillside, with superb views across the water to the Asian shore and the older Anadolu Hisari. After the conquest the fortress became a customs post and later a prison for foreign prisoners of war. Now a museum with an open-air theatre inside the walls.
