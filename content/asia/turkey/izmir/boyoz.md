story: >
  Boyoz was brought to İzmir by Sephardic Jewish immigrants from Spain after the expulsion of 1492. The pastry — made only with flour, water, oil, and a small amount of tahini, with no butter and no yeast — is one of the few genuinely kosher pastries in Turkish cooking. It became so thoroughly associated with İzmir that it now has protected geographical status as an İzmir product, and İzmiris regard anyone who claims to make a proper boyoz elsewhere with gentle scepticism.
---

Boyoz is a flaky, oily, unleavened pastry sold in bakeries and street stalls across İzmir from early morning. It is eaten for breakfast, usually with a hard-boiled egg alongside, and has no close equivalent anywhere else in Turkey. The texture is something between a croissant and a layered flatbread — light, slightly greasy, and extraordinarily satisfying.

The best boyoz in İzmir is a matter of intense local debate. The traditional neighbourhood to find the best versions is Alsancak, where several old bakeries have been making them to the same recipe for generations. Buy one warm from the oven and eat it standing at the counter for the full experience.
