  The Temple of Artemis at Ephesus was one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World — larger than the Parthenon, with 127 columns 18 metres tall. It was burned down by a man named Herostratus in 356 BCE, who reportedly confessed that he had done it purely to achieve immortality through infamy. The Temple of Artemis at Ephesus, one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World, stands today as a single reconstructed column rising from a marshy field on the edge of Selçuk. It is one of the most dramatic anti-climaxes in classical tourism — and one of the most thought-provoking, because the contrast between the lone column and the temple's documented glory makes the point about time and loss more powerfully than any complete monument could.

Selçuk is a 75-minute train ride from İzmir, and the temple site is about 15 minutes' walk from the station. It combines naturally with a visit to Ephesus (5 km away by taxi or dolmuş) and the Selçuk Museum, which holds some of the finest finds from both sites.
