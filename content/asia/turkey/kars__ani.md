Kars is a frontier city in northeastern Turkey, cold and austere, perched on a plateau at 1,800 metres. It spent decades as a Russian garrison town — the grid-plan streets and crumbling Baltic-style mansions still show it — and Orhan Pamuk set his novel *Snow* here, capturing the city's melancholy and isolation perfectly.

The real draw is [Ani](/asia/turkey/kars__ani/ani_ruins), the ruined medieval Armenian capital about 45 kilometres east on the border with modern Armenia. Once a city of 100,000 that rivalled Constantinople, Ani is now an eerie field of churches, walls, and broken palaces on a windswept plateau above the Akhurian River gorge. The [Church of the Redeemer](/asia/turkey/kars__ani/church_of_the_redeemer), split clean in half by lightning, is one of the most striking ruins in Turkey. The [Cathedral](/asia/turkey/kars__ani/cathedral_of_ani), the Church of St Gregory, and the remains of the citadel are all worth the trip. The site is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and rarely crowded.

Back in Kars, the [castle](/asia/turkey/kars__ani/castle_of_kars) above town offers good views, and the local speciality is [kars gravyer](/asia/turkey/kars__ani/kars_gravyer_cheese) — a hard cheese modelled on Swiss Gruyère, a legacy of the dairy traditions brought by Russian settlers. The city is also a starting point for the restored [Eastern Express](/asia/turkey/kars__ani/eastern_express_train) train route from Ankara, which has become popular in recent years.
