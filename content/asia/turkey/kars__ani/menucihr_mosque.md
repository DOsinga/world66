The Menucihr Mosque stands at the western edge of the Ani plateau on the lip of the gorge above the Akhurian River. Built by the Seljuk governor Menucihr after the conquest of 1064 AD, it is regarded as the oldest surviving mosque in Anatolia and represents the first major Islamic construction at the newly captured city.

The building is a small mosque by any standard — Ani's Armenian population vastly outnumbered the new Seljuk administration — but its position above the gorge gives it an outsized presence in the landscape. The octagonal minaret is largely intact and offers, at certain angles, one of the most photographed views at the site: a reminder that Ani's history is a layered one, with Armenian, Seljuk, Georgian, Mongol, and Ottoman presences overlapping across the centuries.
