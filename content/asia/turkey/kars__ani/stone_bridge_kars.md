The Stone Bridge over the Kars River is the oldest surviving structure in the modern city and a reminder that Kars existed as a significant settlement long before the Russians remade it in the 19th century. The bridge dates from approximately the 14th century and is built in the pointed-arch style common to medieval Anatolian bridge construction.

It sits in the lower part of the city near the river, a short walk from the city centre. The bridge is still used by pedestrians and provides a good vantage point over the river gorge below the castle. In winter, when the Kars River freezes and snow lies on the parapets, it becomes one of the more atmospheric spots in the city — the kind of scene that would not look out of place as an illustration in Pamuk's *Snow*.
