Kayseri Ethnographic Museum is housed in Güpgüpoğlu Mansion, one of the city’s notable surviving Ottoman domestic buildings. The mansion is the real exhibit: courtyards, carved details, timber ceilings, and room arrangements that show how a wealthy Kayseri household separated public, private, and family life.

The ethnographic displays add costumes, household objects, and regional material culture, but architecture-minded visitors will get the most from it. It pairs well with Kayseri Castle and the old centre, giving the city a more intimate scale after its Seljuk monuments and busy commercial streets.
