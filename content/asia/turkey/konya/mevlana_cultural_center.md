The Mevlana Cultural Centre is the modern auditorium on the eastern edge of the city where the **sema** ceremony — the whirling dervish ritual — is performed weekly for visitors. The hall is purpose-built around a circular performance floor, with the audience ranged in rising tiers; the acoustics are good, the lighting subdued, and photography is permitted only at the very beginning and end.

The ceremony lasts about an hour and follows the traditional sequence: chanted Quranic recitation, the rhythmic music of the *ney* (reed flute) and *kudüm* (drums), and four cycles of slow rotation by the white-skirted dervishes around the master. It is not a dance performance in the casual sense — the rotation is a form of prayer, and the audience is expected to keep silent throughout.

Performances take place every Saturday evening; the schedule is denser during the **Şeb-i Arus** festival in mid-December, marking the anniversary of Rumi's death. Booking ahead is wise outside the very hot summer months. Modest dress is required.
