The Sahib Ata Külliyesi is a Seljuk religious complex commissioned in the 1270s by Sahib Ata Fahreddin Ali, the same vizier who built the İnce Minareli Medrese. The complex originally comprised a mosque, a dervish lodge (hanikah), a mausoleum, and a public bath, of which the bath and parts of the lodge survive.

The **mosque** has been heavily rebuilt, but its monumental brick portal, with elaborate stone and faïence tile decoration, is a fine example of late-thirteenth-century Seljuk style — the period when stonework was loosening from rigid geometry into something more fluid. The **türbe** beside it holds Sahib Ata's own tomb. The **dervish lodge**, behind the mosque, was one of the early centres of the Mevlevi order. The associated **Sahip Ata Museum** displays the lodge interior and a collection of artefacts from the period.
