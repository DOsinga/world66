Manisa sits just inland from [Izmir](/asia/turkey/izmir), pressed against the lower slopes of Mount Sipylus. It is industrial now, and nobody should pretend otherwise, but the old centre still has enough Ottoman stonework and Anatolian myth to make a spare day here feel well spent.

The city was once Magnesia ad Sipylum, a Greek and Roman town built over much older Anatolian settlement. Under the Ottomans it became a training ground for princes, which explains the unusually rich set of mosques, medreses, and charitable complexes in a city that otherwise feels workaday. The [Sultan Mosque](/asia/turkey/manisa/sultan_mosque), [Hatuniye Mosque](/asia/turkey/manisa/hatuniye_mosque), and the restored [Manisa Mevlevihanesi](/asia/turkey/manisa/manisa_mevlevihanesi) give the centre its best texture.

The real reason Manisa lingers in the mind is the mountain. [Mount Sipylus](/asia/turkey/manisa/mount_sipylus) rises immediately south of town with forests, wild tulips, snow in winter, and a view back over the plain. Around its lower slopes are the [Weeping Rock](/asia/turkey/manisa/weeping_rock), tied to the myth of Niobe, and the weathered Hittite [Cybele relief](/asia/turkey/manisa/cybele_relief), one of those ancient things that looks half natural, half summoned.

Manisa works best as a day trip or overnight from Izmir. Come for the mosques in the morning, climb or drive towards the mountain in the afternoon, and do not expect a polished museum town. Its charm is rougher than that.
