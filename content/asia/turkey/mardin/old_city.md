Mardin's old city is the entire reason to come here. Built on a ridge at 1,070 metres, the medieval and Ottoman urban fabric climbs from the lower bazaar streets to the citadel at the summit, connected by a series of narrow alleys, stairways, and tunnels that burrow through the hillside. The uniformity of the honey-coloured limestone — every building, wall, and pavement cut from the same local stone — gives the city a visual coherence rare in the region.

The main road through the old city, called Cumhuriyet Caddesi, is lined with 19th-century mansions, hans, and churches whose carved facades compete for attention. Turning off into the side lanes takes you into an older, quieter fabric of lower dwellings, workshops, and occasional open courtyards where families sit in the shade. The city is best explored without a fixed itinerary: the pleasures are in the light on a carved doorway, a Syriac inscription on a church lintel, a view that opens suddenly between buildings across the plain below.
