Niğde sits south of Cappadocia in the drier, more workaday heart of Central Anatolia. It does not have the fairy-chimney drama of Göreme or the easy polish of Ürgüp, but it has a good old centre, several serious medieval monuments, and the feeling of a Turkish city that still belongs mostly to itself.

The compact historic core is the reason to pause. [Niğde Castle](/asia/turkey/nigde/nigde_castle) crowns the hill, while [Alaaddin Mosque](/asia/turkey/nigde/alaaddin_mosque), [Sungur Bey Mosque](/asia/turkey/nigde/sungur_bey_mosque), and the [Niğde Museum](/asia/turkey/nigde/nigde_museum) put Seljuk, Ilkhanid, and local archaeology within a walk of each other.

The exceptional sight is outside town: [Gümüşler Monastery](/asia/turkey/nigde/gumusler_monastery), a rock-cut Byzantine complex with frescoes, cut into the soft stone of a village hill. Add the high outline of the Aladağlar Mountains to the south and Niğde becomes a worthwhile one-day stop for travellers already moving through central Turkey by car or bus.
