The cluster of "treehouse" pensions on the road into the Olympos ruins is the place's calling
card — a backpacker tradition since the 1990s, when a few small camps built simple wooden
bungalows on stilts among the trees and started serving cheap dinners on long communal tables.

Many of the original camps still operate (Kadir's, Bayrams, Saban). The standard is rustic: thin
walls, paper-flat mattresses, communal showers, three meals included for one rate. Don't expect
romance or quiet — it's loud, social, and built for groups of 20-somethings. Go for the
atmosphere, not the comfort, and stay in Çıralı if either of those is a deal-breaker.
