**Pamukkale** — literally "Cotton Castle" in Turkish — is one of the most distinctive natural sites in the world: a 2,700-metre-long white limestone escarpment cascading down a hillside in south-western Turkey, with hot mineral-rich spring water flowing over a series of gleaming [travertine terraces](/asia/turkey/pamukale/travertine_terraces) and shallow pools. From a distance the whole thing looks exactly like a frozen waterfall or, as the name suggests, a huge fluffy cotton castle. It has been a tourist destination for at least 2,000 years — the Romans built a spa city on top of it — and is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

On the ridge immediately above the terraces sit the ruins of [Hierapolis](/asia/turkey/pamukale/hierapolis), the Graeco-Roman spa city founded in the 2nd century BCE. The site is enormous and surprisingly well preserved: the [Roman theatre](/asia/turkey/pamukale/hierapolis_theatre) carved into the hillside is one of the finest in Turkey, the colonnaded main street and the extensive [necropolis](/asia/turkey/pamukale/hierapolis_necropolis) stretch for kilometres, and the [Hierapolis Archaeological Museum](/asia/turkey/pamukale/hierapolis_museum) is housed in the restored Roman baths. The [Antique Pool](/asia/turkey/pamukale/antique_pool) — also called Cleopatra's Pool — is a thermal swimming pool inside the site where you can paddle among genuine fallen Roman columns at 35°C. And the [Ploutonion](/asia/turkey/pamukale/ploutonion) — the Gate of Pluto — is a Roman shrine built over a natural CO2 vent the ancients understood as a literal entrance to the underworld, and which still kills birds that get too close.

The travertines must be walked **barefoot** — a strict rule to protect the fragile calcium carbonate surface — and you should stay within the marked paths. In dry months some upper terraces can be dry, which disappoints first-time visitors; spring visits after winter rain offer the fullest and whitest pools. Most travellers visit as a day trip from [Denizli](/asia/turkey/denizli) (20 km south) or from the Aegean coast — Kuşadası is about 2.5 hours away. The village of Pamukkale, immediately below the site, has dozens of cheap guesthouses for those who stay overnight.
