The Hierapolis Archaeological Museum occupies the restored Roman baths at the centre of the Hierapolis site, and holds finds from excavations going back to the 19th century. The collection is strong on Roman-period sculpture — portrait heads, a well-preserved sarcophagus frieze, architectural decoration from the theatre stage building — and also covers the prehistoric and Hellenistic periods of the site.

The museum is included in the main Hierapolis-Pamukkale ticket and is worth an hour, both for the objects and for the building itself. The bath complex's proportions give a good sense of the civic scale of Roman Hierapolis. The museum is in the centre of the plateau, a short walk from the Antique Pool and roughly equidistant between the theatre and the North Gate.
