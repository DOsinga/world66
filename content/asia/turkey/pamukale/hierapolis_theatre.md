The Roman theatre at Hierapolis is the finest monument on the upper plateau and one of the best-preserved Roman theatres in Turkey. Built in the second century AD under Hadrian and enlarged under Septimius Severus, it held around 12,000 spectators in its cavea carved into the natural hillside. The stage building — the scaenae frons — survives three storeys high, decorated with reliefs depicting the myths of Apollo and Artemis, the Gigantomachy, and scenes from Dionysus's life.

Climb to the upper tiers for a panoramic view across the plateau, over the travertine terraces, and down to the plain stretching toward Denizli. On a clear day you can see the full extent of the necropolis along the road to the north. The theatre is occasionally used for concerts and cultural performances in summer. Access is included in the main Hierapolis-Pamukkale site ticket.
