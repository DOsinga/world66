The travertine terraces of Pamukkale are among the most arresting natural landscapes in Turkey: a cascade of brilliant white limestone shelves down a 200-metre hillside, filled with shallow pools of warm, milky-blue mineral water. The formations were created over millennia as calcium carbonate-rich thermal springs cooled on contact with the air, depositing layer after layer of white limestone. The pools vary in depth from a few centimetres to about half a metre, and the water temperature is around 35°C.

Walking down the terraces is permitted on a marked barefoot path — shoes are deposited at the entrance and collected at the exit. The path takes 30 to 45 minutes at a gentle pace. Water flow varies by season and by which sections the park authority is currently allowing to regenerate; spring visits (March to May) after the winter rains typically offer the fullest and whitest pools. Midday in July and August is both hot and crowded; late afternoon light turns the terraces golden and the crowds thin somewhat. The view from the top plateau, looking west over the broad Çürüksu plain toward the Aegean hills, is exceptional at any time.
