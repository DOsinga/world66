Hidirlik Hill rises immediately above the old town of Safranbolu and provides the best overview of the UNESCO-listed historic district: red tile roofs descending in layers into the narrow valley, domes and minarets punctuating the skyline, and the wooded hillsides beyond. The climb takes about fifteen minutes from the old bazaar via a path through the residential neighbourhood.

At the summit there is a small park with a café, a few trees, and the characteristic haze of any viewpoint over a compact Ottoman town. The angle from here reveals exactly why Safranbolu's layout is distinctive: houses fit into the valley topography rather than levelling it, following the ridges and slopes in ways that a flat-town planner would never design. The view at sunset, when the light catches the tile roofs and the call to prayer echoes up from the mosques below, is as good as Turkey gets.
