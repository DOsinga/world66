The İzzet Mehmet Pasha Mosque is the largest and most prominent mosque in Safranbolu's old bazaar, built in 1796 by the Ottoman Grand Vizier who took its name. The single-domed structure is an elegant example of late Ottoman mosque architecture, with a portico of five arches and a slender minaret rising beside it. The proportions are carefully judged — it is large enough to anchor the bazaar square without dominating it.

The interior is well-preserved and painted in the light, airy style of 18th-century Ottoman decoration, with calligraphic inscriptions in the lunettes and a carved wooden mimber (pulpit). The mosque is active and open to non-Muslim visitors outside prayer times; remove shoes and dress conservatively before entering.
