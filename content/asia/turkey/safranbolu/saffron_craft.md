The old bazaar of Safranbolu is the obvious place to buy the town's signature products, and saffron is the most important of them. The town has grown saffron since at least the Ottoman period — the name Safranbolu essentially means "saffron city" — and while modern commercial saffron production in Turkey has declined, small local producers still sell genuine Safranbolu saffron in the market. It is expensive even by saffron standards, but the quality is excellent and buying it here means it is almost certainly authentic.

Other crafts worth looking for include traditional Turkish delight (lokum), made locally in several flavours, copper goods from the metalworkers in the covered bazaar, and the yemeniler shoes — flat, pointed leather slippers in vivid colours that Safranbolu was once famous for exporting across the Ottoman Empire. The shoe-making tradition has declined but a few workshops still produce them by hand. Prices are reasonable by Turkish tourist-town standards, and the quality of the traditional goods is generally higher than in more heavily touristed places.
