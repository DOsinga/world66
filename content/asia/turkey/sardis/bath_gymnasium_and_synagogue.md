The Bath-Gymnasium is the most legible part of Roman Sardis: a restored facade, courtyards, marble surfaces, and the scale of a provincial city that once mattered. It is impressive, but the adjoining synagogue gives the complex its deeper charge.

The synagogue was built into part of the bath-gymnasium and decorated with mosaics, inscriptions, and marble revetment. Its discovery changed how scholars thought about Jewish life in late Roman Asia Minor, and for travellers it makes Sardis feel less like a generic ruin and more like a real city with layered communities.
