The Temple of Artemis was one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World — a vast marble temple, four times the size of the Parthenon, that was rebuilt three times on the same site between the 8th and 4th centuries BC. Today the wonder is reduced to a single column, reassembled by archaeologists from fragments, standing in a marshy field on the edge of town. A stork's nest usually sits on top.

There is little here to look at, and yet there is something specifically humbling about the gap between what was and what remains — a column in a flooded paddock, a flock of geese, and the Isa Bey Mosque and the Basilica of St John visible on the hill above. The site is free, takes ten minutes, and should be combined with the museum and the basilica.
