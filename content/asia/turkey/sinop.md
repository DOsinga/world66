Sinop sits on a headland of Turkey's Black Sea coast, a harbour city with just enough distance from the main inland routes to feel self-contained. It has been Greek, Byzantine, Seljuk, Genoese, Ottoman, and republican, but the story is easiest to read in the walls around the old harbour.

The [Sinop Fortress Prison](/asia/turkey/sinop/sinop_fortress_prison) is the reason most travellers should make time for the city. It is grim, memorable, and deeply tied to modern Turkish literature and political history. Around it, the [old city walls and harbour](/asia/turkey/sinop/city_walls_and_harbour), [Alaaddin Mosque](/asia/turkey/sinop/alaaddin_mosque), and museums make a compact walking day.

Sinop also has the weathered charm of a Black Sea port: fishing boats, tea gardens, summer beach crowds from inland Turkey, and a sea horizon that feels more northern than Mediterranean. It is not polished, and that is part of the point.
