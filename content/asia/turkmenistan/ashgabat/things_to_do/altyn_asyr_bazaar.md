---
title: "Altyn Asyr Bazaar (Tolkuchka)"
type: poi
---

The original Tolkuchka Bazaar on the northern edge of the city was once Central Asia's largest outdoor market — a sprawling weekend gathering of carpet dealers, livestock traders, food vendors, and ordinary Turkmen from across the country. The original site was demolished and the market relocated and partly formalised as the Altyn Asyr (Golden Age) Bazaar, further from the centre on the northern ring road. Weekend mornings, particularly Saturday and Sunday, are still when the market is most alive.

What you find here is categorically different from anything on the marble boulevards. The bazaar is a genuine cross-section of Turkmen life: women in bright silk dresses and heavy silver jewellery haggling over carpets; men in tall white telpek sheepskin hats trading livestock in the outer yards; stalls selling spices, dried fruit, flatbread, and the small domestic goods of ordinary households. The carpet section is particularly worth time — Turkmen tribal carpets, with their characteristic red grounds and geometric guls (medallions), are sold new and old, and the quality range is wide enough to be instructive.

Bargaining is expected and good-natured. Your guide may want to steer you toward approved souvenir shops afterward; resist if you can. The bazaar is the antidote to the marble city — chaotic, human-scaled, and entirely unrehearsed. Bring cash (manat), go early, and allow at least two hours.
