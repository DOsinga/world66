---
title: "The White Marble City"
type: poi
---

Ashgabat holds a Guinness World Record for having the highest density of white marble-clad buildings in the world — over 543 buildings at last count, though the number grows as construction continues. The record itself is the point: no city on earth has been so deliberately engineered as an architectural statement of state power. Walking or driving the main boulevards, you pass monumental presidential palaces, government ministries, hotels, and cultural institutions, every surface faced in the same brilliant white Carrara-style marble, gleaming in the desert sun.

The effect is most extraordinary after dark. The city has invested heavily in illumination — fountains are lit in multicolour, ministry buildings glow with internal LED arrays, and the golden domes of state buildings catch floodlights in ways that make the whole centre feel like a fairground built at government scale. The boulevards are immaculate and largely empty; the effect is of a capital city built for the photographs rather than for the citizens. That emptiness is itself a kind of spectacle.

President Niyazov (Turkmenbashi) began the transformation in the early 1990s, pouring oil and gas revenues into the capital's reinvention. His successor, Gurbanguly Berdimuhamedow — who styles himself "Arkadag" (the Protector) — has continued and extended the project, adding his own monuments, statues, and superlative claims. Walking these avenues, you are moving through a live political document. The marble is not decoration; it is the argument.
