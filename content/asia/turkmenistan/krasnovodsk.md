Krasnovodsk — officially renamed Türkmenbaşy in 1993 — is a Caspian Sea port city on the western edge of [Turkmenistan](/asia/turkmenistan). The name change was personal: President Saparmurat Niyazov bestowed his own honorific title on the city, "Türkmenbaşy" meaning Leader of the Turkmens. The old name persists in international usage and among older travellers, and the city still goes by Krasnovodsk on many maps.

Founded in 1869 as a Russian military outpost, the city became the western terminus of the Trans-Caspian Railway and the main Caspian Sea port for Central Asia. Troops, cotton, and oil all moved through here during the Soviet period. The railway still runs east through [Ashgabat](/asia/turkmenistan/ashgabat) and on toward Uzbekistan, and ferries still cross the Caspian to [Baku](/europe/azerbaijan/baku) in Azerbaijan — a 12 to 15 hour crossing with no fixed schedule.

Today Krasnovodsk is a transit city. Turkmenistan is one of the world's most closed countries, and few travellers come here for the sights. Those who do arrive are typically crossing between Central Asia and the Caucasus by the Caspian ferry route, or passing through on overland journeys toward Iran or Russia. The city has an oil refinery and a natural gas terminal; it smells of industry and salt water. Russian colonial architecture survives in the older quarters near the harbour. The mountains behind the city and the open Caspian coastline offer a measure of relief from the heat and the dust.
