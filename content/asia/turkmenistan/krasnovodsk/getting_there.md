title: Getting There
type: section
---

**By ferry.** The Türkmenbaşy International Seaport runs ferries across the Caspian to [Baku](/europe/azerbaijan/baku) in Azerbaijan. There is no published timetable — boats depart when they have enough cargo and passengers, which can mean waiting anywhere from a few hours to several days. The crossing takes roughly 12 to 15 hours. This is one of the classic overland Central Asia routes: dusty, unpredictable, and genuinely memorable. Bring food, water, and reading material.

**By rail.** Türkmenbaşy railway station (IATA reference: the Trans-Caspian Railway) is the western terminus of the line running east through [Ashgabat](/asia/turkmenistan/ashgabat) and on to Uzbekistan. The train to Ashgabat takes around six hours. From Ashgabat you can continue east to [Mary](/asia/turkmenistan/mary), to Tashkent in roughly 24 hours, or by a separate route toward Iran. A rail link opened in 1996 connects to the Iranian network, making a theoretical all-rail journey to Istanbul possible.

**By air.** Türkmenbaşy International Airport (IATA: KRW) has domestic connections to Ashgabat and occasional international flights. Most travellers use Ashgabat's main airport for international arrivals.

**Visas.** Turkmenistan is one of the world's most difficult countries to enter. Most nationalities require a visa supported by a letter of invitation from a Turkmen citizen or a registered tour operator. Transit visas for the ferry crossing are sometimes available but the rules change. Check current requirements carefully before attempting this route.
