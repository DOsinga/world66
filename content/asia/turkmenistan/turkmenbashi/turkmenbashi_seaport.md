  The Caspian Sea is geographically a lake — the largest in the world — and has no legal connection to international maritime law. The borders of its waters were disputed for decades after Soviet collapse. The ferry crossing from Turkmenbashi to Baku crosses this legal grey zone; there are no standardised departure times, and the boats leave when they are full. Waiting in the port for a ferry is a rite of passage for Central Asian travellers.

The Türkmenbaşy International Seaport is the city's reason for existing. Ferries run across the Caspian to [Baku](/europe/azerbaijan/baku) in Azerbaijan — a crossing of roughly 12 to 15 hours in reasonable conditions — and less regularly to Astrakhan in Russia. The port handles both cargo and passengers. There is no fixed timetable; boats depart when they have enough cargo and passengers to justify the crossing, which means you may wait anywhere from a few hours to several days.

The port itself is a Soviet-era complex that has been partially modernised. The waiting area is basic. Bring food, water, and patience. The crossing is an authentic slice of Central Asian travel — fellow passengers range from long-distance truck drivers to traders moving goods between the Caspian nations to the occasional adventurer completing an overland journey.
