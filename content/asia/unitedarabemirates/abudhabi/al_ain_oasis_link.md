The largest of the six UNESCO-listed oases of Al Ain, a 90-minute drive east of Abu Dhabi at the foot of the Jebel Hafit mountain. The cool palm groves cover 1,200 hectares and are still irrigated by the ancient *falaj* system — narrow stone channels carrying water from underground aquifers.

Walking paths cut through the date plantations between the falaj walls, with information panels at intervals. Free entry, plus a small ecocentre at the entrance. Combine with the rest of Al Ain — the Al Ain Palace Museum, the Al Jahili Fort, and the drive up Jebel Hafit at sunset — for a full day out.
