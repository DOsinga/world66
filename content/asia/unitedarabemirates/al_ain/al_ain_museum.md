The Al Ain Museum is the oldest museum in the UAE, established in 1969 — two years before the country itself existed. That longevity shows in the collection, which was built with scholarly rigour rather than the showy ambitions of some of the region's newer cultural institutions. The focus is on the archaeology and traditional life of the Al Ain region, drawing on excavations at Hili and the other Bronze Age and Iron Age sites scattered around the city's outskirts.

The ethnographic galleries cover the material culture of the pre-oil Gulf: tools, weapons, jewellery, household objects, and a detailed reconstruction of a traditional Emirati interior. The archaeological section has some genuinely impressive finds — ceramics, bronze objects, and inscribed pieces from the Umm an-Nar period (roughly 2600–2000 BC), when Al Ain was already an important node in the trade network connecting Mesopotamia with the Indus Valley.

The museum sits immediately adjacent to Al Jahili Fort and can be visited on the same trip without any additional travel. Allow 45 minutes to an hour. Modest entry fee; closed Monday.
