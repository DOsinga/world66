The Al Ain Oasis sits in the heart of the city and is easily the most important single place to visit in Al Ain. It is one of six oases inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2011, recognised for the exceptional preservation of its traditional *falaj* irrigation system. The falaj — a network of underground and surface channels that tap into the water table and distribute water by gravity — is one of the oldest agricultural technologies in the Arabian Peninsula, and Al Ain is where you can see it working at its most impressive scale.

Enter through the main gate on Zayed bin Sultan Street and follow any of the shaded paths inward. The palms are enormous and densely planted, the ground underfoot still slightly damp from the channels that run alongside the paths, and the noise of the city disappears almost immediately. A restored traditional building near the centre explains the falaj system with displays and a short film — worth ten minutes of your time before you wander further.

Come early morning or late afternoon for the best light and the coolest temperatures. The oasis covers roughly 1,200 hectares, so you could walk for an hour without retracing your steps. The date harvest in September and October brings the whole place to life, with workers moving through the groves collecting the heavy bunches. Free entry; open daily.
