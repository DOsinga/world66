Al Jahili Fort is the largest fort in Al Ain and one of the best-preserved examples of traditional Gulf military architecture anywhere in the UAE. The main structure dates from around 1891, though it was substantially expanded in the early 20th century. The name means "place of ignorance" in Arabic — a reference to the pre-Islamic era, suggesting the site may have had earlier significance before the fort was built.

The restoration, completed in the mid-2000s, was done with unusual care: the mud-brick walls were repaired using traditional methods and materials, and the internal layout was left largely intact. You can walk the perimeter walls, climb the round watchtower for views across the palm groves toward Jebel Hafeet, and visit the permanent Wilfred Thesiger exhibition in the tower's lower rooms. Thesiger's photographs are genuinely exceptional documents of a world that no longer exists.

The fort opens onto a pleasant public park — a good place to sit and recover from sightseeing before moving on to the nearby Al Ain Museum. Plan an hour for a thorough visit. Free entry; closed Monday.
