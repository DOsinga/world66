The Sheikh Zayed Palace Museum occupies the original Al Ain residence of Sheikh Zayed, the man who founded the United Arab Emirates in 1971 and remained its president until 2004. The building itself predates the UAE entirely — this is where Zayed grew up, governed, and raised his family when Al Ain was a small oasis settlement and not yet the prosperous city it became under his rule.

The rooms have been preserved and in some cases restored to reflect how they looked during the mid-20th century, with original furnishings, personal effects, and photographic displays explaining Zayed's life and the political history of the Emirates. The tone is reverential but the content is genuinely interesting: the transition from this mud-brick compound to the glass towers of Abu Dhabi happened in about 40 years, and the museum makes that trajectory visible.

The adjacent grounds include a garage of vintage cars once owned by Zayed and a traditional Emirati garden. Allow an hour for a careful visit. The museum sits just east of the Al Ain Oasis, making it a natural pairing on the same morning. Free entry.
