**Dubai: The Story of the World's Fastest City** by Jim Krane (2009) — The most comprehensive English-language account of how a fishing and pearling village became a global megacity in 40 years. Krane has journalist's access and a sharp eye for the absurdities of Dubai's development. Essential reading before a first visit.

**City of Gold: Dubai and the Dream of Capitalism** by Jim Krane — an alternative title for the same book in some markets, occasionally confused with later works.

**Sandstorm: Libya in the Time of Revolution** — not relevant. Instead:

**The Pearl** by Douglas Lyne — a short novel set among the pearl-diving communities of the old Trucial Coast, capturing the world that existed before oil and the transformation that followed. Fiction but rooted in deep research.

**Expats** by Janice Y.K. Lee — a novel set in 2010s Dubai (and Hong Kong) exploring the lives of privileged expatriates; it captures the disorientation and moral murkiness of expat Gulf life better than most non-fiction accounts.

**On Saudi Arabia** by Karen Elliott House — technically about Saudi Arabia but indispensable for understanding the religious and cultural landscape of the entire Gulf region, which explains much of what seems inexplicable about Dubai's contradictions.
