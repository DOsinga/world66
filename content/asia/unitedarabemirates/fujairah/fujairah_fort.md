Fujairah Fort is one of the oldest and largest forts in the UAE, perched on a rocky hill about two kilometres north of the city centre. The mud-brick structure dates to around the 16th century and served as the seat of the ruling Al Sharqi family for hundreds of years. From the hilltop, you can see across the date palm gardens of the old town toward the Gulf of Oman and the Hajar Mountains beyond — a view that makes it obvious why this particular hill was chosen.

The fort is well-maintained and open to visitors. The interior chambers, watchtowers, and ramparts give a clear sense of the defensive architecture's logic: multiple concentric walls, angled towers designed to cover each other's blind spots, and a main tower that dominates the surrounding landscape. Information panels explain the fort's history and the broader context of the trucial sheikhdoms that preceded the UAE. The adjacent heritage village reconstructs traditional Fujairah life, with old weapons, household objects, and craft demonstrations.

Entry is free and the fort is generally open daily except Fridays. Allow at least an hour for a thorough visit. Combine it with the Fujairah Museum, which stands a short walk away and holds archaeological finds from across the emirate.
