The Sharjah Museum of Islamic Civilization is among the most comprehensive displays of Islamic heritage in the world, occupying an entire converted souq building on the Corniche. Six main galleries trace the development of Islamic art, science, cartography, astronomy, and everyday life from the seventh century to the present, with objects drawn from across the Muslim world.

The collection is genuinely impressive in scope: there are Quranic manuscripts, scientific instruments, astrolabes, ceramics, textiles, jewellery, and armour from Persia, India, Spain, Egypt, and the Ottoman Empire. The gallery documenting Islamic contributions to mathematics and science is particularly strong — it challenges the assumption, common in Western education, that these fields developed exclusively in Europe. The museum is an essential stop for anyone interested in Islamic history and far better than its relatively low international profile would suggest.
