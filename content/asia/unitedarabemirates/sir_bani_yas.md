**Sir Bani Yas** is a 90 km² desert island off the western coast of the emirate of Abu Dhabi, about three hours' drive from the city plus a short ferry crossing. It was the personal nature reserve of **Sheikh Zayed**, the founding father of the [UAE](/asia/unitedarabemirates), who from the 1970s introduced Arabian oryx, gazelles, giraffes, cheetahs, and more than 40 other species to the island — a Noah's Ark experiment that has grown into one of the largest wildlife reserves in Arabia.

The **Arabian Wildlife Park** covers most of the island and supports the largest free-roaming herd of Arabian oryx in the world, along with cheetahs, giraffes, hyenas, and Arabian gazelle. You explore it on guided game drives, nature walks, mountain-bike rides, and kayak trips that thread through inland salt-water creeks lined with mangroves. The animals roam freely rather than being penned, so sightings depend on patience and luck — which is part of the appeal.

The island also holds a remarkable piece of history. Excavations have uncovered a **7th-century Christian monastery**, the only known pre-Islamic monastery in the UAE, which you can visit on a tour. It is a reminder that the Gulf was a crossroads of faiths long before oil.

There are three Anantara hotels on the island, and while excursions are possible as a day trip from Abu Dhabi, the long drive and ferry really merit an overnight stay. Plan ahead: access is managed, and most activities and the ferry need to be booked in advance.
