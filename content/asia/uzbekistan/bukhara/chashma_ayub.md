  According to local legend, the prophet Job (Ayub in Arabic) visited this spot and struck his staff on the ground, whereupon a spring of pure water burst forth. The spring still exists beneath the mausoleum and is considered holy — the water is sold in bottles outside the entrance. Whether or not Job came to Bukhara, the spring is real, and the 14th-century mausoleum built around it has an interior that seems genuinely ancient and cool regardless of the season.

Chashma-Ayub — "Spring of Job" — is a mausoleum and sacred spring located in the park next to the Samanid Mausoleum, just outside the old city walls. The current structure dates mostly to the reign of Timur in the late 14th century, though parts are older, and its unusual conical dome — different in style from anything else in Bukhara — reflects the influence of Khorezm architects brought in by Timur.

Inside, the building houses a small water museum alongside the shrine. The spring is accessible through an opening in the floor, and the water that flows from it is strikingly cold and sweet-tasting even in summer. Most visitors to Bukhara combine a visit here with the adjacent Samanid Mausoleum; the two monuments are a short walk from the Ark and make a logical morning excursion from the Lab-i-Hauz area.
