Namozgoh Mosque sits away from Bukhara's densest old-city circuit, which is part of its interest. It was designed as a festival prayer ground, a place where crowds could gather for Eid prayers rather than a neighbourhood mosque folded into the lanes.

The architecture is plainer than Bukhara's famous madrasahs, but the scale and setting explain a different side of the city's religious life. It is worth adding if you have already seen the central monuments and want to understand how public worship spread beyond the trading domes and minarets.
