Sitorai Mokhi-Khosa — "Palace of Moon-Like Stars" — was the summer retreat of Bukhara's last emir, Alim Khan, and it sits about five kilometres north of the old city in a large walled garden. The complex blends Russian imperial architecture with traditional Uzbek ornament in a way that reflects the peculiar hybrid culture of the late Bukharan emirate: carved wooden columns support Russian-style crystal chandeliers, and the throne room mixes Islamic tilework with European furniture.

The harem building is particularly interesting — a two-storey whitewashed structure with carved wooden balconies that looks more like a guesthouse than a place of confinement. The gardens include a small pool where the emir supposedly held beauty contests, choosing his wives by having candidates walk across stepping stones: those who slipped were rejected. The story may be apocryphal but the palace is real, and the combination of lush gardens, interesting architecture, and a well-curated applied arts museum makes it worth the short taxi ride from the centre.
