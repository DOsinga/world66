The Armenian Church of the Holy Mother of God is a small but telling stop in Samarkand, a city usually approached through Timurid monuments and Islamic architecture. Its presence points to the Armenian merchants, craftsmen, and families who formed part of the city's wider Eurasian mix.

It is not a rival to Registan or Shah-i-Zinda, and should not be judged that way. Visit if you are interested in Samarkand as a lived, multi-community city rather than only as a sequence of blue-tiled masterpieces.
