Fayaz Tepe is a Buddhist monastic complex from the first to third centuries AD, located about 15 kilometres west of Termez off the M39 highway. It is one of the best-preserved early Buddhist sites in Central Asia and a compelling place even for travellers without a specialist interest in archaeology.

The ruins include the remains of a stupa, monks' cells, a refectory, and halls used for teaching and prayer. Many of the original clay sculptures and wall paintings found here are now displayed in the Termez Archaeological Museum, but the site itself gives a strong sense of the monastic community that once lived and practised here — far closer to Gandhara and the Indian Buddhist heartland than to China or Southeast Asia.

Access requires a short drive from the city followed by a walk across open ground. Hire a local guide or a car for the day, as public transport doesn't run to the site.
