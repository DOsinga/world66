[Ba Be National Park](/asia/vietnam/ba_be/ba_be_national_park) sits in Bac Kan province, about 250 km north of Hanoi, and is built around one of the most striking landscapes in northern Vietnam. At its heart is [Ba Be Lake](/asia/vietnam/ba_be/ba_be_lake) — the name means "three lakes", though the lake is a single continuous body of water 8 km long and up to 800 m wide. At 178 m above sea level, it is Vietnam's only significant natural mountain lake, set among limestone karst peaks that rise above 1,000 m and drop sharply to the water's edge.

The park covers 7,610 hectares of limestone karst and lowland evergreen forest, with over 550 plant species recorded and a freshwater ecosystem that supports endemic fish found nowhere else. Among the mammals: Francois's Langur, Owston's Civet, slow lorises, and bears. The place is comparably beautiful to Ha Long Bay or Ninh Binh — and far less visited.

The standard itinerary is a day on the water, with a wooden motorboat tracing the Nang River from the lake into [Puong Cave](/asia/vietnam/ba_be/puong_cave) — a river cave where tens of thousands of bats roost overhead — and on to [Dau Dang Waterfall](/asia/vietnam/ba_be/dau_dang_waterfall) at the southern end. Trekking trails lead into the forest and to the villages of the Tay, Dao, and H'Mong minority peoples who live in and around the park.

Most visitors stay in homestays in [Pac Ngoi village](/asia/vietnam/ba_be/pac_ngoi_village), a Tay minority settlement on the lakeshore where wooden stilt houses open directly onto the water. It is the right choice over Cho Ra town. Ba Be is a full-day journey from Hanoi by bus, or five to six hours by private vehicle.
