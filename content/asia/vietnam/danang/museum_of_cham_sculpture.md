story: "The Cham kingdom ruled the central Vietnamese coast for over a thousand years, building great Hindu temple complexes in brick and sandstone. When French scholars began systematically studying the ruins in the late 19th century, they needed somewhere to bring the carved stonework — torsos of dancing apsaras, intricate kala-heads, linga, and processional statuary — that had been scattered across jungle sites from Quảng Bình to Bình Thuận. The purpose-built museum opened in 1919. Almost nothing has been looted since then; the collection has only grown."
---

The finest collection of Cham Hindu sculpture in the world, housed in a graceful French colonial building on the bank of the Han River. The galleries are arranged by the major Cham temple sites — Mỹ Sơn, Trà Kiệu, Đồng Dương — so the stylistic evolution from 7th-century austerity to the elaborate 10th-century style is visible as you walk through.

The star pieces are the **Trà Kiệu altar** (an exquisitely detailed narrative frieze of the Ramayana carved in sandstone) and the enormous dancing Shiva from Đồng Dương. Allow at least two hours; the collections reward unhurried looking. The museum is small enough that a single visit covers everything but large enough that the detail repays repeat trips.
