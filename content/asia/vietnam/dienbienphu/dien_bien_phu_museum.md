story: >
  The decisive engagement at Dien Bien Phu lasted 57 days, from March 13 to May 7, 1954. General Vo Nguyen Giap's forces moved artillery by hand — disassembled and carried piece by piece through the jungle — to positions on the surrounding hills overlooking the French garrison, a feat the French commanders had considered impossible. When the guns opened fire from above, the tactical situation that French General Navarre had designed to lure the Viet Minh into a set-piece battle became an encirclement.
---

The main museum documents the battle of Dien Bien Phu in considerable detail, covering the military planning on both sides, the logistics of moving Viet Minh artillery through jungle-covered mountain terrain, and the human cost of the 57-day siege. Exhibits include captured French equipment, maps of the French defensive positions (nicknamed "Beatrice," "Gabrielle," "Anne-Marie," and others), weapons from both armies, and photographs.

The museum has been expanded and updated over the years and is now one of the more professionally presented provincial military museums in Vietnam. The signage is in Vietnamese and French (fitting given the history), with some English captions. Allow an hour for the main hall; an additional room covers the political aftermath and the Geneva Accords of 1954.
