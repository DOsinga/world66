Ha Giang is Vietnam's northernmost province and, for most travelers, its wildest frontier. The province pushes hard against the Chinese border through a landscape of sheer limestone karsts, plunging river valleys, and high plateaux that feel nothing like the rest of Southeast Asia. The Ha Giang Loop — a 200-kilometer circuit through the Dong Van Karst Plateau — has become one of the most celebrated motorbike routes in the country, and the reason most visitors come here at all.

The [Dong Van Karst Plateau Geopark](/asia/vietnam/hagiang/dong_van_karst_plateau) is the visual centrepiece: a UNESCO-recognized geological wonder of ancient limestone formations that jut from the earth like shattered teeth, dotted with terraced fields carved by H'mong, Lo Lo, and Dao minority communities over centuries. The [Dong Van](/asia/vietnam/hagiang/dong_van_old_quarter) old town is the most atmospheric settlement in the province, a cluster of French colonial buildings and traditional stone houses pressed against a cliff. From there, the Ma Pi Leng Pass drops in dizzy switchbacks toward [Meo Vac](/asia/vietnam/hagiang/meo_vac), with views that inspire the same giddy speechlessness in almost everyone who crests the ridge.

This is not easy travel. The roads are narrow, the drops are serious, and the weather changes fast. Many visitors hire a local Easy Rider guide in Ha Giang city for the Loop — the guides are worth it, both for safety and for introductions to villages that are otherwise hard to approach as an outsider. The minority communities here — particularly the Hmong in the high plateau — have maintained their customs and dress more completely than in more touristed northern towns like Sapa.

Ha Giang city, the provincial capital on the Lo River, is a serviceable but unremarkable base. The real province begins an hour north, once the road climbs into the mountains and the flat valley gives way to the karsts. Come between September and November for the buckwheat flowers — pink and white blooms covering the plateau — or in January when the valley mists roll in and the mountains look like Chinese ink paintings.
