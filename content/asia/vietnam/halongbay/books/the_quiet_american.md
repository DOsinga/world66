Greene's 1955 novel, set in Saigon during the final years of the French colonial war in Indochina, is one of the great political novels of the 20th century. The narrator — a cynical British journalist — watches as a naive American idealist blunders through a country he thinks he understands, with catastrophic consequences. Written a decade before American involvement escalated, it reads like prophecy. Set mostly in Saigon, but the North Vietnam chapters include the colonial port of Haiphong, the gateway city to Ha Long Bay.
