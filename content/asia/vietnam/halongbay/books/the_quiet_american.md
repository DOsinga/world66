Greene's 1955 novel follows a British war correspondent in Saigon during the final years of French colonial rule, watching a young, idealistic American arrive with dangerous certainty about what Vietnam needs. It is a novel about innocence and intervention — written before the American involvement became a full-scale war, and prescient about the disaster that would follow. The Saigon Greene describes, with its cafes, its violence, its cynicism and beauty, is gone, but the political argument of the novel has never dated. The quietest, most devastating indictment of American foreign policy ever written.
