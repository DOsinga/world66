Ha Long Bay has a small permanent population living on floating villages — clusters of houses, schools, and fish farms moored among the limestone islands. Cua Van, the largest, has been there since the mid-twentieth century, though most residents have been relocated to shore in recent years. Visits to the floating villages by kayak or small boat are part of most cruise itineraries, and getting close to the fish farm cages — where grouper and other fish are raised in net enclosures — gives a sense of the economic life of the bay that the standard karst scenery does not. The villages are being gradually depopulated as the Vietnamese government resettles residents on land; what remains is increasingly a cultural heritage site rather than a working community.
