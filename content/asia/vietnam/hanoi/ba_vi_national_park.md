Ba Vi mountain rises about 60 kilometres west of Hanoi, a forested massif that the French colonial administration turned into a hill station to escape the summer heat of the capital. The roads up through the national park pass cloud forest inhabited by macaques, birds, and a remarkable variety of orchids. At around 1,100 metres, the ruins of the French-era villas, churches, and government buildings emerge from the jungle, their walls consumed by roots and moss in the way that only decades of tropical growth can manage.

The mountain is accessible as a half-day or full-day trip from Hanoi by motorbike, hired car, or occasionally organized tour. The national park entrance is at the mountain's base; the road winds steeply up through several climate zones. Most visitors go to the summit area to see the ruins and the views over the surrounding landscape — on clear days you can see across the Red River delta to the east. The park also has hiking trails, a botanical garden, and temples dedicated to the mountain god Tan Vien, who is among the most important figures in Vietnamese religious tradition. Go on a weekday to avoid the Hanoian weekend crowds.
