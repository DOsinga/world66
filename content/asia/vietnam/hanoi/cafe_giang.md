story: "Nguyen Van Giang was working as a bartender at the Sofitel Metropole during the French colonial period when the war against France created shortages of almost everything, including dairy. Fresh milk for coffee became impossible to obtain. Giang experimented with chicken egg yolks as a substitute, beating them with sugar and condensed milk into a thick, sweet foam that he then poured over robusta espresso. The drink was a hit at the hotel; when he left to open his own cafe in 1946, he took the recipe with him. The cafe on Nguyen Huu Huan Street has been in the Giang family for three generations, the recipe unchanged."

Cafe Giang is down a narrow alley off Nguyen Huu Huan Street in the Old Quarter — easy to miss if you don't know to look for it. Inside, the small rooms have the accumulated patina of a family business that has been in continuous operation for eighty years: dark wood furniture, old photographs, the smell of coffee and sweet egg. The egg coffee (ca phe trung) is the reason to come: a small cup of strong Vietnamese robusta espresso, topped with a thick, warm, slightly custardy foam of beaten egg yolk and condensed milk. It tastes more like coffee-flavored dessert than coffee, which is not a criticism.

Drink it as intended: use a small spoon to fold a little of the foam down into the coffee with each sip, working through the layers. The hot version is served in a cup nested inside a bowl of warm water to keep it at temperature. There is also an iced version, and a coconut coffee. The cafe is always busy, particularly on weekends; you may need to wait for a table. The family has opened additional branches around Hanoi, but this original location on the alley is the right place to come.
