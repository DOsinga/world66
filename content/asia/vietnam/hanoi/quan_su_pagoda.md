Quan Su Pagoda — the Ambassador's Pagoda — takes its name from its original purpose: it was built in the 15th century to house Buddhist delegations visiting the Vietnamese court from neighboring kingdoms. A guest house for diplomatic monks, in other words. Today it is the seat of the Vietnam Buddhist Sangha and the most important active Buddhist pagoda in Hanoi, which means it is always busy, always full of incense smoke, and always populated by worshippers rather than just visitors.

The pagoda is on Quan Su Street in the French Quarter, unimposing from the outside but worth pushing through the entrance gate. The main hall is hung with lanterns and thick with offerings; the sounds of chanting, bells, and the steady murmur of prayer fill the courtyard throughout the day. Weekend mornings are particularly intense — the place is packed with ordinary Hanoians praying, making merit, and consulting monks, giving you a window into the living practice of Vietnamese Mahayana Buddhism that the better-known but more touristy pagodas around the city don't provide.
