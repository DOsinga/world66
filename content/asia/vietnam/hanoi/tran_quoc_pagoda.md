The 11-storey lotus-shaped stupa in the courtyard was built in 1998 but follows the form of much older Vietnamese religious architecture."

Tran Quoc Pagoda sits on a small peninsula connected to the southern shore of West Lake by a causeway, its red-walled buildings reflected in the water on three sides. The complex has been modified and rebuilt many times over its 1,500 years, but the setting — water surrounding the trees and towers, the city skyline visible across the lake — makes it one of the most serene places in Hanoi.

The pagoda is an active place of worship rather than a museum piece. Come on a weekday morning rather than a weekend, when it can be crowded with tourists and tour groups. The lotus pond inside the courtyard, the bodhi tree grown from a cutting supposedly taken from the original tree in Bodh Gaya, India, and the stupa with its niches holding small golden Buddha figures reward a slow walk around the grounds. Photography is welcome in the exterior areas but show discretion near worshippers.
