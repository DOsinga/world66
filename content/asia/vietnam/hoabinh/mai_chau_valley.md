Mai Chau is the most celebrated destination in Hoa Binh province — a wide, flat-bottomed valley about 60 km southwest of the city, surrounded by dramatic limestone ridges and filled with White Thai villages, terraced rice paddies, and water buffalo. It draws a steady stream of visitors from Hanoi, many of them on overnight packages, and it has become polished enough that the nightly dance performances and batik textiles for sale are transparently tourist-oriented. None of that makes the valley less beautiful.

Ban Lac, the most-visited village, has several hundred-year-old stilt houses and genuinely skilled weavers selling brocade cloth and bags at reasonable prices. Ban Pom Coong next door is slightly quieter. Homestays are widely available and represent the best way to experience the valley — sleeping on raised bamboo platforms, eating meals prepared by the host family, and waking to the sound of the rice fields. The valley is also good cycling country: roads between villages are flat, the scenery is continuous, and you can cover a full circuit in a morning without a guide.

story: >
  The White Thai of Mai Chau trace their arrival in the valley to at least the 13th century, when they migrated from what is now Yunnan province in southern China. They brought a tradition of silk weaving that survives today in the backstrap looms still used in village houses — the geometric patterns of each clan's fabric were once a form of identification, legible to anyone who knew the code.
