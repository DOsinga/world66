Da Kao (often written Dakao) is the northern ward of District 1, an older residential quarter that holds some of the city's most atmospheric temples and quietest colonial streets. Less polished than the downtown core and less chaotic than the backpacker zone, it is where Saigon's layered history — French villas, Chinese pagodas, and Vietnamese shophouses — sits closest together.

The neighbourhood's anchor is the Jade Emperor Pagoda, one of the most important and atmospheric Taoist temples in the city, thick with incense smoke and crowded statues of gods and demons. Nearby, the Tomb of Lê Văn Duyệt (Lăng Ông) honours the Nguyen-dynasty general and remains a busy place of worship. The streets around Dien Bien Phu and Dinh Tien Hoang hold long-running local restaurants and the kind of pavement coffee culture that defines the city.

Da Kao rewards wandering more than ticking off sights. The architecture is a mix of grand old villas and dense vernacular housing, and because it sees fewer tourists than the streets to the south, it gives a clearer sense of how central Saigon lives day to day.
