District 7, and specifically its planned township of Phu My Hung, is the most un-Saigon part of Saigon — a master-planned new town of wide boulevards, landscaped canals, gleaming towers, and international schools built on former marshland south of the centre since the 1990s. It is where a large share of the city's expatriate families and affluent Vietnamese have settled, and it functions almost as a self-contained suburb.

The contrast with the dense, organic centre is total: orderly grids, parks, and lakeside promenades replace the tangle of motorbikes and shophouses. The neighbourhood is dominated by malls — Crescent Mall on the artificial lake is the social centre — and by a dense, internationally minded dining scene, including the city's biggest concentration of Korean and Japanese restaurants around the Korean district near Pham Van Nghi.

There are no historic sights here, and most travellers never visit. But District 7 is worth understanding as a glimpse of where the city is heading, and the lakeside Crescent area, lit up in the evening, is a pleasant and unexpected counterpoint to the chaos of District 1.
