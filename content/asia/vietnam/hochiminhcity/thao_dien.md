Thao Dien is the leafy expatriate enclave across the Saigon River in what was District 2 (now part of Thu Duc City), a riverside neighbourhood that feels like a different city from the dense centre. Wide, tree-shaded lanes run down to the water, and the area has the highest concentration of international restaurants, specialty coffee roasters, wine bars, and boutiques in Ho Chi Minh City.

The neighbourhood grew as foreign residents and a moneyed Vietnamese class moved into the villas and riverside apartments here, and the commerce followed: brunch cafes, craft-beer taprooms, yoga studios, organic grocers, and some of the best non-Vietnamese dining in the country. The streets around Thao Dien Road and Xuan Thuy are the densest, and the riverside spots with views back toward the downtown skyline are the prize.

It is not where you come for old Saigon — there is little history and few sights here — but it is where the city eats and drinks internationally, and a short Grab ride or a trip on the new metro line connects it to the centre. For a relaxed afternoon away from the traffic and noise of District 1, Thao Dien is the antidote.
