Vietnamese coffee is among the strongest in the world — robusta beans, dark-roasted, brewed through a small metal drip filter (phin) directly over a glass of ice and condensed milk. The result is cà phê sữa đá: thick, sweet, intensely caffeinated, and drunk through a straw. It is the fuel that runs Saigon.

The coffee culture is visible on every pavement: plastic stools, small tables, people reading the newspaper or staring at phones while the coffee drips. The best street cafés charge 15,000–25,000 đồng (under $1.50) for a glass. The upmarket end of the coffee scene has developed rapidly — there are now excellent specialty coffee roasters in Ho Chi Minh City serving high-quality arabica from the Central Highlands. Trung Nguyên Legend Café on Nguyen Hue is the chain most associated with Vietnamese coffee, but the side-street plastic-stool establishments are better.
