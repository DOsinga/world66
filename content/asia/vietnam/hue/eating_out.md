---
title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

Hue is Vietnam's most distinctive food city. The imperial court developed a tradition of elaborate, beautifully presented cuisine — multiple small dishes, each prepared with meticulous care — and that legacy survives in the street food more than anywhere else. Many dishes are unique to Hue and unavailable anywhere else in the country with the same quality.

The non-negotiable order of business: a bowl of **bún bò Huế**, the spicy beef and lemongrass noodle soup that is Hue's claim to national food fame. Look for hole-in-the-wall places with plastic stools and constant turnover. Bún Bò Huế Bà Rớt near the citadel and stalls around Đặng Tất Street are reliably good. A full bowl costs 35,000–55,000 VND.

**Bánh khoái** — crispy rice pancake stuffed with shrimp and bean sprouts, dipped in peanut sauce — is another essential. Lac Thien restaurant at 6 Dinh Tien Hoang Street has been serving it for decades and is the most visited, though the neighbouring stalls are comparable in quality and less crowded. **Bánh bèo**, **bánh nậm**, and **bánh ướt** — various steamed rice-cake dishes — are best tried as a set at [Dong Ba Market](/asia/vietnam/hue/dong_ba_market) or at small restaurants on Nguyen Binh Khiem Street.

For sit-down meals, **Không Gian Xưa** (1 Pham Hong Thai) does excellent traditional Hue cuisine in a courtyard setting. **La Residence** hotel's restaurant is the smartest option in the city, serving a modern take on Vietnamese and French cuisine from a restored 1930s art-deco mansion right on the river.

**Cơm hến** — clam rice with chilli, peanuts, sesame, and crackling — is the classic cheap breakfast, sold from carts and simple shops on Con Hen islet and around the citadel markets.
