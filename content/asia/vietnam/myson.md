My Son is the most important Cham archaeological site in Vietnam — a cluster of red-brick temple towers set in a narrow valley surrounded by jungle-covered mountains, about 40 kilometres southwest of [Hoi An](/asia/vietnam/hoian). Built between the 4th and 13th centuries as the religious and political capital of the Champa kingdom, My Son was the equivalent of [Angkor](/asia/cambodia/angkorwat) for the Cham civilisation. UNESCO listed it as a World Heritage Site in 1999.

The towers and sanctuaries are Hindu temples, mostly dedicated to Shiva, and the stone carvings that survive are remarkably detailed despite centuries of weather and war. [Groups B, C, and D](/asia/vietnam/myson/group_b_c_d) are the most intact — you can still see carved lintels, mythological reliefs, and the distinctive Cham brick tower silhouette, built without mortar using techniques that scholars have still not fully explained. [Group A](/asia/vietnam/myson/group_a), once the grandest complex, was largely destroyed by American bombing in August 1969, and the craters and rubble remain as a sobering reminder of what was lost. [Groups E and F](/asia/vietnam/myson/group_e_f) show earlier 8th-century construction, with a different architectural vocabulary. A small [in-situ museum](/asia/vietnam/myson/in_situ_museum) in the Group D mandapas displays carvings and context panels; the finest pieces are now at the Museum of Cham Sculpture in Da Nang.

Most visitors come on a half-day trip from Hoi An. The site opens at 6:00 a.m., and the first hours of the morning, before the tour buses arrive, are the best time to explore. The drive through the countryside and the Thu Bon river valley is part of the experience.
