Groups B, C, and D form the central cluster of My Son — the most intact section of the site and the most visited. Group B contains several substantial kalan (sanctuary towers) and the building known as B5, a remarkable "storehouse" or kosagrha with a distinctive saddle-shaped roof that has no close parallel in Southeast Asian religious architecture. The brick carvings on the exterior walls include mythological figures, floral patterns, and scenes from Hindu scripture.

Group C sits adjacent and is in a similar style, with a well-preserved kalan and surrounding subsidiary structures. Group D has two mandapas (entry halls) that have been partially roofed by conservators to protect the interior — these contain an in-situ display of Cham sculpture fragments and replica pieces, with the originals now in the Museum of Cham Sculpture in Da Nang. Together these three groups give the best sense of Cham temple architecture at its peak: red brick towers with intricate carved decoration, built without mortar, surviving from the 9th to 12th centuries.
