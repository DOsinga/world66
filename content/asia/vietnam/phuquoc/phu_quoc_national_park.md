Phu Quoc National Park covers roughly 31,400 hectares in the island's northern half, protecting tropical lowland forest, mangroves, and coral reefs in the waters offshore. The interior is dense jungle of dipterocarp forest with streams running off the granite hills to the coast. Several trails enter the park from the road on the western edge, and the two main summits — Chua Mountain (603 m) and Ham Ninh Mountain (365 m) — are accessible on foot.

The marine section of the park protects coral reefs around the northern beaches and some of the offshore islets. The snorkelling here is less developed than at the An Thoi archipelago in the south but far less crowded. Wildlife in the park includes macaques, slow lorises, sea eagles, hornbills, and — occasionally, deep in the interior — clouded leopards. The biodiversity is high and the park well worth exploring if you want to get beyond the beach resorts.
