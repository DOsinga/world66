The Phu Quoc Prison, also known as the Coconut Tree Prison or Camp Phu Quoc, operated from 1967 to 1973 and became notorious for its brutal treatment of North Vietnamese and Viet Cong prisoners. The site is now a museum with detailed reconstructions of the original prison compound, including the tiger cages — cramped concrete enclosures designed to hold prisoners doubled over — and the interrogation rooms.

The exhibits include photographs, documents, and wax figure reconstructions of prisoners being subjected to various forms of torture. It is confronting material and deliberately so. Outside the main compound, a memorial garden with statues commemorates those who died here. The museum stands in the southwest of the island near An Thoi town, about 25 km from Duong Dong. It is worth the trip for anyone interested in the Vietnam War and its impact on Vietnamese civilians and combatants.
