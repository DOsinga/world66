Qui Nhon is a mid-sized coastal city in Binh Dinh province, tucked between mountains and the sea on Vietnam's south-central coast. Long overshadowed by the tourist juggernaut of Hoi An to the north and Nha Trang to the south, it has stayed quieter and more genuinely Vietnamese than either — a working port and fishing town that happens to have excellent beaches.

The city sits on a peninsula jutting into Thi Nai Lagoon, with a long crescent of [sandy beach](/asia/vietnam/qui_nhon/qui_nhon_beach) stretching along its eastern edge. The coastline is clean and rarely crowded. West of the city, the mountains rise quickly, and the highway south climbs to a pass with panoramic views back over the bay — one of the finer viewpoints on the whole coastal route. The secluded [Ky Co beach](/asia/vietnam/qui_nhon/ky_co_beach), reached by boat from a nearby fishing village, is among the finest spots on the entire south-central coast.

Qui Nhon has cultural depth beyond its beaches. The region was once the heartland of the Cham kingdom, and the [Twin Cham Towers](/asia/vietnam/qui_nhon/twin_cham_towers) stand right in the city centre, while the more dramatic [Banh It towers](/asia/vietnam/qui_nhon/banh_it_cham_towers) rise from a hillock 20 kilometers to the northwest. The city is also associated with Han Mac Tu, one of Vietnam's most celebrated early 20th-century poets, who spent his final years at a French leprosarium here and is buried on the hillside above town. The [memorial and cemetery](/asia/vietnam/qui_nhon/han_mac_tu_memorial) have an unexpectedly moving quality.

Travellers on a longer journey down the coast find Qui Nhon a good place to slow down for a day or two: good [seafood](/asia/vietnam/qui_nhon/bbq_seafood_beach), uncrowded beaches, interesting day trips into the hinterland, and a pace of life that hasn't been reshaped around tourism.
