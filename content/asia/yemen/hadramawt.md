Hadramawt is a vast wadi region in eastern Yemen, stretching about 560 kilometres from the Arabian Sea to the Saudi border. It is one of the most spectacular landscapes in the Arabian Peninsula — a deep canyon cut through a limestone plateau, with ancient mud-brick tower cities perched along its walls.

The most remarkable of these is [Shibam](/asia/yemen/shibam), a 16th-century walled city of skyscraper-like mud towers that has been called the "Manhattan of the desert." [Seiyun](/asia/yemen/hadramawt/seiyun) has a white-washed [sultan's palace](/asia/yemen/hadramawt/seiyun_palace) that rises incongruously from the flat valley floor, now a museum. [Tarim](/asia/yemen/hadramawt/tarim) is known for its hundreds of mosques — including the [Al-Muhdhar Mosque](/asia/yemen/hadramawt/al_muhdhar_mosque) with its 50-metre mud-brick minaret — and its traditional Hadrami merchant architecture. Given the ongoing conflict in Yemen, travel to this region requires careful assessment of current security conditions.
