Shibam is known as the "Manhattan of the Desert" — and when you first see it rising from the floor of the [Hadramawt](/asia/yemen/hadramawt) valley, the nickname makes sense. Hundreds of mud-brick tower houses, some up to eight storeys tall, are packed tightly together within a defensive wall, their brown facades glowing in the desert light. Manhattan is taller, but it was not built a thousand years ago.

The tower houses of the [walled city](/asia/yemen/shibam/walled_city) date from the 16th century onward, though the city itself is far older, and UNESCO declared it a World Heritage Site in 1982. Walk the narrow streets between the towers, stroll through the [palm groves](/asia/yemen/shibam/wadi_palm_groves) that line the wadi below, and gaze out at the sand dunes beyond. The [Friday Mosque](/asia/yemen/shibam/friday_mosque) dates from the early Islamic period. End the day on the [low ridge across the wadi](/asia/yemen/shibam/sunset_viewpoint) for the silhouette of the whole city against the canyon walls — and if time permits, drive a few hours deeper into the Hadramawt to see the cliff-perched town of Hajarayn, equally dramatic in a different way.
