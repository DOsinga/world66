The Australian Capital Territory is a small enclave carved out of New South Wales in 1911 as a compromise between Sydney and Melbourne, neither of which could agree to host the national capital. [Canberra](/australiaandpacific/australia/australiancapitalterritory/canberra) is the result — a planned city built from scratch on sheep-farming land, designed by the American architects Walter Burley Griffin and Marion Mahony Griffin after a competition in 1912.

Canberra houses the national institutions: Parliament House, the Australian War Memorial, the National Gallery, the National Museum, and the National Library. It's a city of roundabouts, embassies, and wide avenues that Australians from the big coastal cities like to mock, but the collections in the galleries and museums are world-class and worth a dedicated trip.

The Territory is otherwise small — most of it is Namadgi National Park, an extension of the alpine country that runs along the border with New South Wales.
