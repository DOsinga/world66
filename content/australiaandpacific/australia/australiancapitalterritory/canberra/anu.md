The Australian National University, in the suburb of Acton on the northern lakeshore, is the country's most research-intensive university and was founded by an act of Federal Parliament in 1946 to do work that no individual state university could afford. The campus is leafy and pleasant — older 1950s sandstone buildings around University Avenue, contemporary architecture filling in the gaps — and most of it is open to walk through.

Highlights include the Drill Hall Gallery (a small contemporary art gallery in a former military building), the Sidney Nolan Ned Kelly mural in the Coombs Building, the School of Music recitals (most are free or cheap), and the cafe and bookshop scene around Childers Street. The campus is also one of the easiest places in Canberra to spot kangaroos at dawn or dusk.
