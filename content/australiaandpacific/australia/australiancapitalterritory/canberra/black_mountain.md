Black Mountain rises directly behind the Australian National University and the Botanic Gardens, west of the city centre, and is crowned by the 195-metre Telstra Tower — a concrete telecommunications spire built in 1980 and visible from almost everywhere in the city. The tower has been closed to the public for several years for refurbishment and as of writing remains shut.

The mountain itself is well worth climbing even without tower access. A network of walking tracks runs through the open eucalyptus forest on its slopes; the summit road takes you up by car; and from the top there are 360-degree views over Canberra, Lake Burley Griffin, the Brindabella Ranges to the west, and the rolling sheep country of the Southern Tablelands. Look for kangaroos, eastern grey wallabies and a substantial bird list.
