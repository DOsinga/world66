The Captain Cook Memorial Jet, in the centre of Lake Burley Griffin, shoots a column of water 147 metres into the air — high enough to be visible from most of central Canberra. It was commissioned in 1970 to mark the bicentenary of Cook's arrival on the east coast of Australia and operates on a fixed daily schedule (10am-noon and 2-4pm, weather permitting).

The jet is best viewed from the southern lakeshore at Commonwealth Place or from the steps of the National Library; the wind direction usually means one side of the lake gets wet spray and the other doesn't. The jet shuts down in high winds, so on a bad day you may turn up to find nothing happening — try checking the lake camera before walking over.
