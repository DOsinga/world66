About 35 kilometres southwest of Canberra in Tidbinbilla, the NASA Deep Space Communication Complex is one of three giant antenna farms (the other two are in Spain and California) that maintain continuous communication with NASA's deep-space spacecraft. The four enormous dishes here — including the 70-metre DSS-43, the largest steerable dish in the southern hemisphere — talk to the Voyager probes, the Mars rovers, the New Horizons spacecraft and most other interplanetary missions.

The on-site visitor centre is small but excellent — a working operations centre and a free museum with genuine Apollo-era hardware, real Moon rock, a section of the original Parkes Honeysuckle Creek-era console, and a thoughtful set of exhibits on the science. Allow half a day if you combine it with the surrounding Tidbinbilla Nature Reserve.
