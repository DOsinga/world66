Lake Burley Griffin is the centrepiece of Canberra's plan: an 11-kilometre-long ornamental lake created by damming the Molonglo River in 1963, with all the major national institutions arranged around its edges. It is large enough for genuine open-water sailing (the Canberra Yacht Club uses it) and shaped to provide the dramatic axial views that Walter Burley Griffin's original 1912 design required.

Walking or cycling the full lakeside loop takes about three hours and is the single best introduction to Canberra. The Captain Cook Memorial Jet on the southern shore shoots a 147-metre water column into the sky on a fixed schedule (10am-noon, 2-4pm); the National Carillon on Aspen Island has 55 bronze bells playing regular recitals; and there is a regular passenger ferry that connects the major waterfront sights.
