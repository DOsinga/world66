The National Gallery of Australia, on the south shore of Lake Burley Griffin, holds the country's principal collection of Australian, Indigenous and international art — over 155,000 works, from Aboriginal bark paintings and ancestral sculpture to colonial-era oils, modernist Australian work, and a substantial international holding. The signature paintings are Jackson Pollock's Blue Poles and a strong Sidney Nolan Ned Kelly series; the highlight for many visitors is the Aboriginal Memorial, an installation of 200 painted bark poles by Yolngu artists.

The building itself, by Colin Madigan, is a piece of unapologetic 1980s brutalism — controversial when new, much loved now — set in a sculpture garden with major works by Bert Flugelman, Auguste Rodin and Clement Meadmore. Free admission to the permanent collection; ticketed touring exhibitions.
