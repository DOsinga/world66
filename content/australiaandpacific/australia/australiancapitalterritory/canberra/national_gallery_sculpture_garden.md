The Sculpture Garden, free to enter at any hour, wraps around the southern and eastern sides of the National Gallery building. The collection mixes large-scale Australian sculpture (Bert Flugelman's mirrored cones, Clement Meadmore's looping steel forms, Robert Klippel) with major international pieces including Auguste Rodin's Burghers of Calais and Henry Moore. The James Turrell Skyspace installation at the southern end is the obvious highlight — book a dusk viewing if you can.

The garden also includes pleasing native plantings, a pond, and a misted "Fog Sculpture" by Fujiko Nakaya that triggers on a timed cycle. Pack a picnic; tables are scattered through the garden, and the lake is a few steps further south.
