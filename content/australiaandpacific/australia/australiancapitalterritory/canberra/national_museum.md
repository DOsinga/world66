The National Museum of Australia, on Acton Peninsula on the northern shore of Lake Burley Griffin, opened in 2001 to a wave of architectural controversy. The Ashton Raggatt McDougall building is a deliberately fragmented, looping, brightly coloured composition meant to express the multiplicity of Australian identity — and depending on the visitor, it either thrills or annoys. The collections are more uncontroversially excellent.

Permanent galleries cover Australian environmental history, indigenous Australia, settler history and national stories. Star objects include Phar Lap's heart, the original Holden prototype, the Kelly Gang armour, the Australian Honey Bee collection, and a substantial range of Aboriginal bark paintings and contemporary art. The museum is free, with a paid ticket for some touring shows.
