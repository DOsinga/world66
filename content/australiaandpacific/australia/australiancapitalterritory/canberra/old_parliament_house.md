The original federal Parliament House — known officially as Old Parliament House and now reincarnated as the Museum of Australian Democracy (MoAD) — is a long low Beaux-Arts building of 1927 that hosted Australian parliamentary politics for sixty years before the move uphill to the new building. The conservation work was unusually thorough: the senators' lounges, prime ministers' offices and press gallery rooms are largely as they were when Parliament adjourned for the last time in 1988.

The museum focuses on Australian democracy as both a system and a lived experience — exhibits on suffrage, indigenous citizenship, prime ministers, the dismissal of the Whitlam government in 1975 — together with regular touring exhibitions. The Aboriginal Tent Embassy still occupies the lawn out front, where it has been since 1972. Allow at least two hours.
