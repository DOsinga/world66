Albury-Wodonga is a twin city on the Murray River, with Albury in New South Wales and Wodonga in Victoria. For travellers it works as one place: a border crossing, rail town, river city, and useful base for the wineries, gold towns, and alpine country nearby.

It is easy to dismiss Albury-Wodonga as a Sydney-Melbourne stopover, but that sells it short. The state border gives the city a real story: customs posts, railway gauges, river trade, immigration camps, and grand 20th-century plans to build a national growth centre inland. The best day here mixes the river and botanic gardens with the [Murray Art Museum Albury](/australiaandpacific/australia/newsouthwales/albury_wodonga/murray_art_museum_albury), [Monument Hill](/australiaandpacific/australia/newsouthwales/albury_wodonga/monument_hill), and the sobering [Bonegilla Migrant Experience](/australiaandpacific/australia/newsouthwales/albury_wodonga/bonegilla_migrant_experience).

The city also makes a practical break in a long overland trip. Trains, coaches, and the Hume Highway all meet here, but the town is most rewarding if you stop long enough to understand why the Murray mattered.
