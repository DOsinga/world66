Bendemeer is a small New England hamlet on the Macdonald River, north-east of Tamworth where the New England and Oxley highways meet. It is not a town of big sights. The appeal is slower and more rural: river gums, old road houses, a country pub, and the sense of a place built to serve sheep and cattle country.

The village began as MacDonald River in the 19th century and later took the name Bendemeer from Thomas Moore's poem *Lalla Rookh*. Bushranger stories cling to the district too; Captain Thunderbolt robbed the northern mail coach here in 1864. Today most travellers stop for fuel, lunch, or a break from the highway, but Bendemeer is worth a little more time if you like small-town New South Wales without much polish.

The best moment is down by the river. On a warm day you can picnic, fish, or sit under the trees while trucks hum across the highway bridge. If you are around in rodeo season, the showground gives the village its loudest weekend of the year.
