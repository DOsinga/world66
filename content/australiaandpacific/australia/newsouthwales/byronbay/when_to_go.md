---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

Byron Bay's subtropical climate means there is never a bad season, but there are better and worse times to visit. **Autumn (March–May)** is the sweet spot: water still warm from summer, crowds thinning out, and temperatures in the mid-20s. **Spring (September–November)** is similar — warm enough for the beach, not yet peak season. These are the times to come if you want space.

**Summer (December–February)** is peak season. School holidays bring enormous crowds to the town, accommodation prices spike, and the streets become genuinely difficult. The weather is hot (often 28–32°C) and humid, with occasional afternoon storms. Worth avoiding unless you book months ahead.

**Winter (June–August)** is mild by any Australian standards — daytime temperatures of 18–22°C are typical, and the sea drops to around 19°C. It is quieter and cheaper, and this is prime whale-watching season: humpback whales pass offshore during their northward migration from June through November, with the peak in July and August.

**Byron Bay Bluesfest** is held every Easter long weekend at a farm north of town. It is one of the best music festivals in Australia and draws large crowds; book accommodation well in advance if visiting at Easter.
