Cobar is a red-dust mining town in the middle of western New South Wales, the kind of place where the highway, the pub verandahs, and the open-cut mines all feel part of the same story. It began with copper in the 19th century and still looks outward to the mines and the dry country around them.

The town is worth more than a fuel stop if you like outback history. The [Great Cobar Museum](great_cobar_museum) fills the old mine administration building with mining, pastoral, and local material, while [Fort Bourke Hill Lookout](fort_bourke_hill_lookout) gives a blunt view into the modern open cut. The [Cobar Sound Chapel](cobar_sound_chapel), a contemporary sound installation tied to architect Glenn Murcutt and composer Georges Lentz, adds a strange and memorable cultural note.

The strongest reason to stay longer is [Mount Grenfell Historic Site](mount_grenfell_historic_site), northwest of town. Its Wongaibon rock art, scarred trees, ochre pits, and waterhole give Cobar a deeper story than mining alone. Treat it respectfully and check road conditions before heading out.
