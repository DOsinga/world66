  The Watagans were a stronghold of the timber industry in the 19th and early 20th centuries. Vast red cedar forests were logged until nearly none remained. What you walk through today is regrowth — but tall and dense enough that you'd never know from the trail.

The Watagan State Forest rises steeply into the hills west of Cooranbong and offers the best bushwalking in the area. The Watagan Mountain loop is the most popular route — about 10km, taking in gum forest, ferny creek crossings, and decent views from the ridge. The forest is home to lyrebirds, which you're more likely to hear than see; their mimicry is startling if you're not expecting it.

Mountain bikers use the forest extensively: the Ourimbah Creek and Watagan trails are well-marked and technically varied. Families with children tend to stick to the Mambo Wetlands, a flat boardwalk through lakeside wetlands at the forest's edge. There are basic camping areas with fire pits and pit toilets — no booking required and no fee.
