Cowra is a quiet Central West town with one of Australia's strongest stories of wartime memory turning into reconciliation. The 1944 Cowra Breakout, when more than a thousand Japanese prisoners of war tried to escape from the local POW camp, could have left the town with only a grim footnote. Instead, Cowra has built a whole peace precinct around the event, with gardens, cemeteries, memorials, and a bell that make the story feel human rather than abstract.

The [Cowra Japanese Garden](/australiaandpacific/australia/newsouthwales/cowra/cowra_japanese_garden) is the obvious first stop. It is large, carefully made, and especially good in spring when the cherry blossom softens the dry inland light. From there, follow Sakura Avenue towards the [Cowra POW Camp](/australiaandpacific/australia/newsouthwales/cowra/cowra_pow_camp), where a walking trail and interpretive panels trace the camp layout and the night of the breakout.

In town, [Australia's World Peace Bell](/australiaandpacific/australia/newsouthwales/cowra/australias_world_peace_bell) sits in Civic Square, close to the library, gallery, and everyday business of Cowra. That ordinariness is part of the appeal: this is not a polished capital-city monument but a small agricultural town that chose to remember generously. Add the [Cowra Regional Art Gallery](/australiaandpacific/australia/newsouthwales/cowra/cowra_regional_art_gallery), nearby vineyards, and the Lachlan River country, and Cowra is worth a deliberate detour rather than a quick fuel stop.
