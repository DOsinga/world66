Currarong is a small South Coast village on the northern side of Jervis Bay, about 30 kilometres east of Nowra. It is not a big resort town, and that is part of the appeal: a strip of houses, a creek, rock pools, fishing boats, and low-key holiday shacks facing clear water.

The reason to come is the edge of the Beecroft Peninsula. From the village you can walk straight into [Abrahams Bosom Reserve](/australiaandpacific/australia/newsouthwales/currarong/abrahams_bosom_reserve), where bush tracks lead to coves, sandstone shelves, the wreck of the *Merimbula*, and the strange crawl-through opening of [Gosangs Tunnel](/australiaandpacific/australia/newsouthwales/currarong/gosangs_tunnel). It is one of the better short walking areas on the Shoalhaven coast, especially if you like your beaches with a little rock-scrambling and sea-cliff drama.

South of town, the Defence-managed part of the peninsula opens to visitors when the Beecroft Weapons Range is not in use. That is where you find [Point Perpendicular Lighthouse](/australiaandpacific/australia/newsouthwales/currarong/point_perpendicular_lighthouse), set above the northern entrance to Jervis Bay. Check access before you drive out; the headland is worth the detour, but the gate is not always open.
