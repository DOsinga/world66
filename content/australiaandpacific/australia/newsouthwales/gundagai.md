Gundagai is a small historic town on the Murrumbidgee River, about halfway between Sydney and Melbourne on the Hume Highway. It sits at the foot of Mount Parnassus, 387 km south-west of Sydney, and has been a stopping point for travelers since the days of bullock teams hauling wool across the continent.

The town is best known to Australians through folklore — the [Dog on the Tuckerbox](/australiaandpacific/australia/newsouthwales/gundagai/dog_on_the_tuckerbox) monument, five miles out of town, is one of those peculiarly Australian icons that every schoolchild knows. Beyond the legend, Gundagai has genuine historic character: the [historic courthouse](/australiaandpacific/australia/newsouthwales/gundagai/historic_courthouse) is a handsome Greek Revival sandstone building still in use today. The [Great Rescue of 1852 sculpture](/australiaandpacific/australia/newsouthwales/gundagai/yarri_sculpture) on Gundagai Common commemorates the Wiradjuri men Yarri and Jacky Jacky, who saved 69 lives when catastrophic floods swallowed the original township. And [Rusconi's Marble Masterpiece](/australiaandpacific/australia/newsouthwales/gundagai/rusconis_marble_masterpiece) — a hand-carved miniature cathedral assembled from 20,948 pieces of local marble — is an act of obsessive craftsmanship that rewards anyone who takes the time to look closely.

It's a natural stop if you're driving between Sydney and Melbourne and want somewhere with more soul than a highway service centre.
