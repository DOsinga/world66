Gunnedah sits in the Namoi Valley of northern New South Wales, where wheat, cotton, coal, and grazing country meet. It is a practical country town rather than a polished weekend escape, but it has enough texture for a good day if you are already travelling between Tamworth, Narrabri, and Coonabarabran.

The strongest stops are close to the centre. [Cumbo Gunnerah Keeping Place](/australiaandpacific/australia/newsouthwales/gunnedah/cumbo_gunnerah_keeping_place) gives the town an Aboriginal cultural anchor, while the [Dorothea Mackellar Memorial Statue](/australiaandpacific/australia/newsouthwales/gunnedah/dorothea_mackellar_memorial_statue) ties Gunnedah to one of Australia's best-known poems. [Porcupine Lookout](/australiaandpacific/australia/newsouthwales/gunnedah/porcupine_lookout) and [Pensioners Hill Lookout](/australiaandpacific/australia/newsouthwales/gunnedah/pensioners_hill_lookout) are easy ways to see the river flats, silos, and hills that shape the town.

It works best as a slow inland break: a gallery or keeping-place visit, a short lookout drive, and a walk along Conadilly Street before moving on.
