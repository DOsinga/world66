Lockhart is a small Riverina town southwest of Wagga Wagga, known locally as the "Verandah Town" for the shaded shopfronts along Green Street. It is not a big-ticket destination, but it clears the day-trip test because its identity is unusually clear: pastoral history, recycled-farm-machinery sculpture, wool art, and a tidy old main street that still feels lived in.

The best introduction is the [Lockhart Sculpture and Heritage Trail](/australiaandpacific/australia/newsouthwales/lockhart/lockhart_sculpture_and_heritage_trail), where farm scrap has been turned into bulls, dragons, and other larger-than-life figures. [Green's Gunyah Museum](/australiaandpacific/australia/newsouthwales/lockhart/greens_gunyah_museum) adds the serious local history, including Brookong Station and the 1888 shearers' strike. The [Water Tower Mural](/australiaandpacific/australia/newsouthwales/lockhart/lockhart_water_tower_mural) gives the town a modern landmark visible from the surrounding flat country.

Come with a car and a spare half-day. Lockhart works best as a slow stop in the Riverina: walk the main street, call into the museum, follow the sculpture trail, and leave with a sharper feel for inland New South Wales than you would get from a highway roadhouse.
