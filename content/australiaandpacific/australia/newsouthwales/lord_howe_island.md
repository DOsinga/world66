Lord Howe Island is a small volcanic island in the Tasman Sea, far enough from mainland New South Wales that arrival already feels like a commitment. The island is only about 10 kilometres long, but it has an astonishing range of scenery: a sheltered coral lagoon, white beaches, rainforest, sea cliffs, and the twin southern peaks of Mount Lidgbird and Mount Gower.

This is one of Australia's best nature islands, and the limit of 400 visitors at a time keeps it from feeling like a resort strip. Most people get around by bicycle. Days fall naturally into a rhythm of snorkelling at Ned's Beach, walking the northern ridges, watching seabirds wheel over the cliffs, and saving enough energy for the guided climb of Mount Gower.

Lord Howe is not cheap or casual to reach, but it is not a place built around luxury either. Its appeal is the tight relationship between island life and protected nature: residents, visitors, reef, forest, and seabirds all sharing a very small piece of rock in a very large ocean.
