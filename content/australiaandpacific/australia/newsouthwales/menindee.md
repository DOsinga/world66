Menindee is a small outback town on the Darling River, southeast of Broken Hill, where river red gums, lake water, dry plains, and colonial history meet. It can look almost too quiet at first, but the country around it is one of the better reasons to leave the highway in far western New South Wales.

The main draw is [Kinchega National Park](/australiaandpacific/australia/newsouthwales/menindee/kinchega_national_park), where the Darling bends past old pastoral ruins, river camps, bird habitat, and wide lake country. The [Menindee Lakes](/australiaandpacific/australia/newsouthwales/menindee/menindee_lakes) can be full and silver or low and haunting, depending on water levels. Either way, they explain why this place mattered to Aboriginal communities, river steamers, graziers, and explorers.

Menindee is worth a day if you are already in Broken Hill or crossing the outback with time to spare. Bring water, fuel, and a plan; the distances are real, the roads can change after rain, and the best stops sit outside the town itself.
