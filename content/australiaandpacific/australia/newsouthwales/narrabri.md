Narrabri sits on the Newell Highway in north-western New South Wales, where cotton country, dry woodland, and old volcanic ranges meet. The town itself is practical and low-key, but the country around it is unusually good: big basalt cliffs, a serious radio astronomy site, and the dark forests of the Pilliga within reach by car.

The best reason to stop is [Mount Kaputar National Park](/australiaandpacific/australia/newsouthwales/narrabri/mount_kaputar_national_park), an eroded volcanic landscape east of town. [Sawn Rocks](/australiaandpacific/australia/newsouthwales/narrabri/sawn_rocks) is the easy showpiece, a wall of columnar basalt that looks like a giant organ pipe. West of town, the [Australia Telescope Compact Array](/australiaandpacific/australia/newsouthwales/narrabri/australia_telescope_compact_array) gives Narrabri a different kind of drama: six movable radio dishes on a rail line, listening to deep space from flat farmland.

Narrabri is not a polished resort town. It works best for travellers already crossing inland NSW who want a spare day with real landscape and science rather than another highway meal.
