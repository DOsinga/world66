The Entrance sits on a narrow strip of land between Tuggerah Lake and the Pacific Ocean on the New South Wales Central Coast, about an hour and a half north of Sydney. It is a low-key beach town that makes a good alternative to the crowded city beaches -- the sand is just as good and you will have far more of it to yourself.

The main beach at The Entrance is patrolled in summer, but the real finds are further along the coast. [Blue Bay](/australiaandpacific/australia/newsouthwales/the_entrance/blue_bay_beach), North Shelly, and Crackneck Point are popular surf breaks, while Bateau Bay and South Shelly offer calmer water for swimming. The [pelican feeding](/australiaandpacific/australia/newsouthwales/the_entrance/pelican_feeding) at The Entrance waterfront, held daily at 3:30pm, has become a local institution.

The town itself is unpretentious — fish and chip shops, a few cafes, and a pleasant lakeside promenade. [Tuggerah Lake](/australiaandpacific/australia/newsouthwales/the_entrance/tuggerah_lake) behind the town is good for kayaking and stand-up paddleboarding. You will need a car to get the most out of the area, as public transport on the Central Coast is limited.
