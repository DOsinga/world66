The great central plug of the Tweed Volcano rises 1,156 metres from the surrounding cane fields, its silhouette visible for 50 kilometres in every direction. To the Bundjalung people it is Wollumbin — Cloud Catcher — a sacred site that gives the surrounding national park its official dual name: Wollumbin–Mount Warning National Park. The summit is the first point on the Australian mainland to catch each day's sunrise, a fact that drew crowds of predawn climbers for generations. The traditional custodians request that visitors respect the site's sacred status and refrain from ascending to the peak, and Parks NSW supports this position. The real reward lies in the lower trails anyway: subtropical and temperate rainforest cloaks the slopes, and the wildlife — Albert's lyrebirds, paradise riflebirds, Boyd's forest dragons — is extraordinary for anyone who moves quietly.
