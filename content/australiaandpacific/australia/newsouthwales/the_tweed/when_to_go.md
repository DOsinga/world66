---
title: When to Go
type: section
---

The Tweed has a subtropical climate: hot, humid summers (December–February) with afternoon thunderstorms; mild, dry winters (June–August) that are the best time to visit. Water temperatures stay above 20°C year-round, and the hinterland rainforest is accessible in all seasons — though summer can bring leech activity on the forest trails after rain.

**Winter (June–August)** is ideal. Days are warm and sunny (18–24°C), nights are cool, humidity drops, and the rainforest trails are at their best. This is also when water visibility at Cook Island peaks. The Tyalgum Music Festival runs in July.

**Spring (September–November)** brings warmer seas and reliable surf swells. Wollumbin is at its most photogenic in the clear post-winter air.

**Summer (December–February)** is hot and sticky, with daily maxima often above 30°C and afternoon storms rolling in from the hinterland. The beaches are at their busiest. Hinterland walks are best done in the early morning.

**Autumn (March–May)** offers warm water, declining crowds, and pleasant temperatures. The Murwillumbah Show (April) is a traditional country agricultural show worth catching.

Avoid the Gold Coast school holidays (particularly the Queensland September–October break and Christmas–January) if you want quieter beaches — the Tweed's coastal towns fill with families crossing the border.
