Wisemans Ferry is a small village on the Hawkesbury River, about an hour north of [Sydney](/australiaandpacific/australia/newsouthwales/sydney). It is not a place of grand monuments or busy streets. Its appeal is quieter: slow river crossings, steep sandstone country, and one of the best-preserved stretches of colonial convict road in Australia.

The [Old Great North Road](/australiaandpacific/australia/newsouthwales/wisemans_ferry/old_great_north_road_world_heritage_walk) is the real reason to come. Convicts built the road through brutal bush country in the 1820s and 1830s, and the Devines Hill section above town still has stone retaining walls, culverts, quarry marks, and views back over the Hawkesbury. The walk sits inside [Dharug National Park](/australiaandpacific/australia/newsouthwales/wisemans_ferry/dharug_national_park), so the history comes with eucalypt forest, birds, and hot exposed ridges.

The village itself works best as a slow day out. Cross the river on the free car ferry, picnic at [Wisemans Ferry Park](/australiaandpacific/australia/newsouthwales/wisemans_ferry/wisemans_ferry_park), and leave time for the road down to the water. This is a good choice when you want something more grounded than another Sydney beach day.
