Wollongong — known affectionately as "the Gong" — is the third-largest city in New South Wales, sitting on the coast 80 kilometres south of Sydney with the dramatic [Illawarra Escarpment](/australiaandpacific/australia/newsouthwales/wollongong/illawarra_escarpment) rising steeply behind it. The name is generally said to mean "sound of the sea" in the local Aboriginal language, which feels right: this is a place defined by its coastline.

The city has a long industrial history — coal mines, steelworks, a working port — but it has increasingly reinvented itself around its university and its natural setting. The University of Wollongong draws a large international student population, giving the city a younger, more cosmopolitan feel than you might expect.

The beaches are excellent and far less crowded than Sydney's. [Wollongong City Beach](/australiaandpacific/australia/newsouthwales/wollongong/wollongong_city_beach) is the most central, with [Flagstaff Point lighthouse](/australiaandpacific/australia/newsouthwales/wollongong/wollongong_lighthouse) at its southern end. [Nan Tien Temple](/australiaandpacific/australia/newsouthwales/wollongong/nan_tien_temple), the largest Buddhist temple in the Southern Hemisphere, is worth a visit for its scale and the serenity of its gardens. The [Sea Cliff Bridge](/australiaandpacific/australia/newsouthwales/wollongong/sea_cliff_bridge), a cantilevered coastal road north of town, is one of the most scenic drives in Australia.

Getting here from Sydney is easy: the train follows a scenic coastal route that takes about 90 minutes, or you can drive the Grand Pacific Drive, one of the finest coastal roads in Australia.
