The Northern Territory is Australia at its most elemental: red desert, monsoon-flooded floodplains, ancient Aboriginal culture, and an outback so vast it resists comprehension. With a population of around 250,000 spread across 1.35 million square kilometres, it's one of the least-densely populated places on earth.

[Darwin](/australiaandpacific/australia/northernterritory/darwin) is the capital — a frontier city rebuilt after Cyclone Tracy levelled it on Christmas Day 1974. It's relaxed, multicultural, and serves as the gateway to Kakadu National Park, the largest national park in Australia, where ancient Aboriginal rock art mingles with saltwater crocodiles and seasonal wetlands. The jumping crocodile cruises on the Adelaide River are a reliable spectacle.

[Alice Springs](/australiaandpacific/australia/northernterritory/alicesprings) sits in the Red Centre, surrounded by the MacDonnell Ranges. It's the base for visiting [Uluru-Kata Tjuta National Park](/australiaandpacific/australia/northernterritory/ulurukata_tjuta_n), where the great sandstone monolith and the domed rock formation of the Olgas dominate the flat desert. Uluru is sacred to the Anangu people; climbing it was banned in 2019.

The Territory has two seasons: the Wet (November to April), when the north floods and access to some parks closes; and the Dry (May to October), when temperatures are bearable and wildlife concentrates around waterholes.
