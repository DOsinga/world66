Uluru-Kata Tjuta National Park is the spiritual heart of Australia's Red Centre, a UNESCO World Heritage site in the southern Northern Territory. The park is jointly managed by its Anangu traditional owners and Parks Australia — Uluru is a deeply sacred site, and visiting it respectfully is part of the experience.

[Uluru](/australiaandpacific/australia/northernterritory/ulurukata_tjuta_n/uluru) itself needs no introduction: a single enormous sandstone monolith rising 348 metres above the flat desert plain, measuring 3.6 by 2.4 km at the surface, with a circumference of 9.4 km. The rock changes colour dramatically through the day — deep orange at sunrise, glowing red at sunset, grey-purple after rain. The base walk (10 km, flat) takes you past waterholes such as [Mutitjulu](/australiaandpacific/australia/northernterritory/ulurukata_tjuta_n/mutitjulu_waterhole), caves with ancient rock art, and patches of surprising green. Climbing Uluru is permanently closed, at the request of the Anangu people.

[Kata Tjuta](/australiaandpacific/australia/northernterritory/ulurukata_tjuta_n/kata_tjuta), 36 km to the west, is a collection of 36 rock domes that many visitors find equally impressive. The [Valley of the Winds](/australiaandpacific/australia/northernterritory/ulurukata_tjuta_n/valley_of_the_winds) walk (7.4 km loop) weaves between the domes and is one of the best hikes in central Australia. Geologists believe these domes may once have formed a single monolith far larger than Uluru.

The nearest town is [Yulara](/australiaandpacific/australia/northernterritory/ulurukata_tjuta_n/yulara) (Ayers Rock Resort), purpose-built to serve the park. Connellan Airport has direct flights from Sydney, Melbourne, and Cairns. The park is about 450 km by road from [Alice Springs](/australiaandpacific/australia/northernterritory/alicesprings).
