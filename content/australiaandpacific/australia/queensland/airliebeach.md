Airlie Beach is the gateway to the Whitsunday Islands, a small coastal town in tropical North Queensland that exists almost entirely to get people onto boats. The main strip is a short stretch of bars, tour booking offices, backpacker hostels, and dive shops, with a man-made [lagoon pool](/australiaandpacific/australia/queensland/airliebeach/airlie_beach_lagoon) on the foreshore that compensates for the stinger-season swimming restrictions.

The town itself is not the attraction — the Whitsundays are. Day trips and overnight sailing tours depart daily to [Whitehaven Beach](/australiaandpacific/australia/queensland/airliebeach/whitehaven_beach) (consistently rated among the world's best), the fringing reefs of the [Great Barrier Reef Marine Park](/australiaandpacific/australia/queensland/airliebeach/great_barrier_reef), and the 74 islands of the archipelago. Most visitors spend a night or two in Airlie Beach before and after their island time.

The atmosphere is young, relaxed, and firmly backpacker-oriented, though the marina development at [Abel Point](/australiaandpacific/australia/queensland/airliebeach/abel_point_marina) has brought some upmarket dining and accommodation. The surrounding area offers bushwalks through [Conway National Park](/australiaandpacific/australia/queensland/airliebeach/conway_national_park) and views over the Coral Sea from the hilltops above town.
