The basalt walls of **Lamington National Park** rise immediately south-east of Beaudesert and form the northern half of the McPherson Range, the rim of the long-extinct Tweed shield volcano. The park's 200 km of walking tracks pass through subtropical rainforest, palm groves, and the higher-altitude Antarctic beech forests of the plateau, with dozens of waterfalls dropping into the rainforest below. It is part of the **Gondwana Rainforests of Australia** World Heritage area.

There are two main access points, both reached via Beaudesert. **Binna Burra** to the east has the higher tracks and the famous Daves Creek and Coomera Falls walks; **O'Reilly's** to the west is built around a long-established guesthouse and has the popular Border Track and the **Tree Top Walk** suspension bridges. Both sections have campsites, eating houses, and ranger-led birdwatching — the park is famous for satin bowerbirds, Albert's lyrebirds and pademelons.

The mountain roads up to both ends are narrow, winding, and well worth the effort. Allow at least a full day; an overnight stay lets you reach the back of the plateau.
