About 45 minutes' drive south-west of Beaudesert, **Mount Barney National Park** is the most rugged of the Scenic Rim parks. It centres on a tight cluster of peaks — Barney itself (1,359 m), Lindesay, Maroon and the smaller Ballow — formed by a Cenozoic volcano whose softer cone eroded away to leave a knot of granite stocks. The result is a landscape of steep grassy ridges, sandstone bluffs and dense rainforest gullies that draws serious bushwalkers and climbers from Brisbane.

The most popular outing is the **South Ridge Track** to Mount Barney's summit (around 17 km, 9–11 hours, no marked path above the saddle) and the easier walks to **Lower Portals** and **Barney Falls**, both of which end at swimming holes in granite gorges. There is no road to the summit; the carpark at Yellow Pinch is the standard trailhead. Camping is permitted with a permit.

Even if you do not plan to climb, the drive south along the Mount Lindesay Highway is one of the most scenic in the region.
