Birdsville is one of those Australian places whose name is bigger than the town. It sits in the far south-west corner of Queensland, near the South Australian border, where the Birdsville Track meets the eastern edge of the Simpson Desert and the flood country of the Diamantina.

The town itself is tiny, dusty, and practical: fuel, food, repairs, a pub, a bakery, and a visitor centre for people who have driven a very long way to get here. The reason to come is the outback setting. From town you can watch the water and birdlife at the [Birdsville Billabong](/australiaandpacific/australia/queensland/birdsville/birdsville_billabong), drive west to [Big Red](/australiaandpacific/australia/queensland/birdsville/big_red), or stand by the [Burke and Wills marked tree](/australiaandpacific/australia/queensland/birdsville/burke_and_wills_marked_tree) and think about how unforgiving this country was for early explorers.

Birdsville is not a casual detour. Roads close after rain, summer heat is dangerous, and distances are enormous. But if you are already crossing the Channel Country or the Simpson Desert, it has the feel of a true frontier stop rather than a service town pretending to be one.
