Blackall is a small Outback Queensland town on the Matilda Way, a long way from the coast and proud of it. Its claim is not scenery so much as identity: wool, artesian water, sheep-shearing records, and the old phrase "beyond the Black Stump" all gather here in a way that makes the town more than another fuel stop.

The strongest reason to pause is the [Blackall Historical Woolscour](/australiaandpacific/australia/queensland/blackall/blackall_historical_woolscour), an intact steam-era wool washing plant just outside town. It is rare industrial heritage, and the guided tours make the old machinery and shed life easier to picture.

In town, the [Black Stump](/australiaandpacific/australia/queensland/blackall/black_stump) and [Jack Howe Statue](/australiaandpacific/australia/queensland/blackall/jack_howe_statue) are quick stops, but they tell you why Blackall matters in Australian folklore. End the day in the [Blackall Artesian Aquatic Centre](/australiaandpacific/australia/queensland/blackall/blackall_artesian_aquatic_centre), soaking in warm basin water after a long drive.
