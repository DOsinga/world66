---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

Cairns has two seasons: the wet (November to April) and the dry (May to October). The distinction matters.

The **dry season** is the classic time to visit. Temperatures sit between 18°C and 26°C, humidity is manageable, and the weather is reliably clear. This is when reef conditions are best, trails are accessible, and Cairns fills up with travellers — book accommodation in advance, especially in July and August.

The **wet season** brings heavy rain, high humidity, and occasional cyclones. Some roads north of the Daintree River become impassable for days at a time. But the wet season also brings life: waterfalls run full, the rainforest is intensely green, and inland swimming holes are at their best. Prices drop significantly and the crowds thin out. The wet is not a reason to avoid Cairns if you are flexible, but it requires different plans.

**Stinger season** (October to May) means marine stingers — primarily box jellyfish — make swimming in the open sea dangerous without a stinger suit. Stinger nets protect some beaches north of Cairns (Port Douglas, Palm Cove), but most reef operators provide stinger suits for in-water activities. The Esplanade Lagoon is unaffected year-round.
