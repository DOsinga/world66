Caloundra sits at the southern end of Queensland's Sunshine Coast, where the [Pumicestone Passage](/australiaandpacific/australia/queensland/caloundra/pumicestone_passage) separates the mainland from Bribie Island. It is a relaxed seaside town with a string of beaches — from the calm swimming waters of Bulcock Beach in the town centre to the surf breaks at [Kings Beach](/australiaandpacific/australia/queensland/caloundra/kings_beach), Dicky Beach, and [Moffat Beach](/australiaandpacific/australia/queensland/caloundra/moffat_beach) further north.

The town is popular with families from Brisbane, about an hour's drive south, and has a quieter, less developed feel than the resort strips further up the coast at Mooloolaba and Noosa. The coastal boardwalk connecting Kings Beach to the headland — where a pair of [historic lighthouses](/australiaandpacific/australia/queensland/caloundra/caloundra_lighthouses) stand side by side — is one of the best short walks in southeast Queensland. Humpback whale sightings are common during the migration season from June to November, and the [Pumicestone Passage](/australiaandpacific/australia/queensland/caloundra/pumicestone_passage) is home to dugongs, dolphins, and a remarkable range of migratory wading birds.

The [Queensland Air Museum](/australiaandpacific/australia/queensland/caloundra/queensland_air_museum) at Caloundra Airport holds over 65 vintage aircraft and is a worthwhile stop for anyone interested in aviation history.
