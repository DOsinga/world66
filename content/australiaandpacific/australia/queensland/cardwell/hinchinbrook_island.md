Hinchinbrook Island rises dramatically from the Coral Sea, its granite peaks draped in rainforest and its western shore separated from the mainland by a shallow mangrove channel. At 393 km² it is the largest island national park in the world, entirely free of permanent settlement and development, accessible only by boat from Cardwell or Lucinda.

The island has two distinct personalities: the western shore, facing the mainland, is sheltered mangrove and calm water — good for kayaking and estuarine fishing; the eastern shore, facing the open Coral Sea, is wild beach, boulder headlands, and crashing surf. The [Thorsborne Trail](/australiaandpacific/australia/queensland/cardwell/thorsborne_trail) runs along the eastern coast.

Day trips to the island are available from Cardwell by water taxi. The most popular are to Ramsay Bay at the northern tip — a long beach with good snorkelling — and to the mangrove waterways on the western side. Kayak hire is available in Cardwell.
