---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

Springbrook can be visited year-round, but the experience varies considerably by season. The plateau sits at around 900 metres, which means it operates on a different climate to the Gold Coast coast just 45 minutes away: cooler temperatures, higher rainfall, and frequent cloud and mist, particularly in summer.

Summer (December to February) is warm and wet. Afternoon thunderstorms are common, and the escarpment catches cloud that rolls in from the coast. Waterfalls are at their most dramatic — Purling Brook Falls can be spectacular after heavy rain — but tracks can be muddy and the humidity is high. The glow-worm cave at Natural Bridge is atmospheric any time of year, but rain doesn't improve the drive.

Autumn and spring are arguably the best seasons: mild temperatures, clearer skies, and enough moisture to keep the rainforest looking its best. Winter (June to August) is cool and often crisp on the plateau — morning temperatures can drop toward single digits — but also brings the clearest views from the lookouts. The Antarctic beech forest looks particularly beautiful in the cool, still air of a winter morning. Bring a layer; the temperature contrast with the coast is marked.

School holidays and public holiday weekends see the car parks fill early. Outside these periods, Springbrook is relatively uncrowded even in peak season.
