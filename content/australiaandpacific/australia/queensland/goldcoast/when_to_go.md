---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

The Gold Coast has a subtropical climate with warm, humid summers and mild, dry winters. The best time to visit for most travellers is **April to October**: temperatures are comfortable (18–25°C), humidity is low, and rainfall minimal. June through September are the driest months, and the clear skies and consistent surf make this ideal for outdoor activities. Winter cold is genuinely rare — frost is virtually unknown on the coast.

**Summer** (December to February) is hot and humid, with temperatures regularly reaching 30–33°C and high humidity. Afternoon thunderstorms are common but usually brief. The surf can be excellent after tropical systems pass offshore. Christmas and January school holidays bring the Australian domestic crowds to their peak — prices spike, beaches become very busy, and Surfers Paradise is at its most chaotic.

**Surfing**: The banks at Coolangatta and Burleigh Heads work best from autumn to winter, when swells from the south push up quality waves. The northern beaches get more consistent sand banks from summer beach building.

**The hinterland** (Springbrook, Lamington) is accessible year-round but is most spectacular in spring (September–November) when the wildflowers and bird life are at their best, and in the glow-worm season which is essentially year-round in the rainforest gullies.

Avoid the school holiday periods (January, Easter, and July) if crowds and prices are concerns. Outside school holidays, the Gold Coast is remarkably easy to navigate even in peak season.
