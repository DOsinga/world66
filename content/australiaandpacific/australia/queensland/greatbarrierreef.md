The Great Barrier Reef is the largest coral reef system on earth, stretching over 2,300 kilometres along the Queensland coast from Cape York in the north to Bundaberg in the south. It consists of some 2,900 individual reefs and 900 islands, and is visible from space. The whole system has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1981.

The reef is accessed from a string of Queensland coastal towns, each offering a slightly different experience. [Cairns](/australiaandpacific/australia/queensland/cairns) is the main hub, with dozens of dive and snorkel operators running day trips to the outer reef. Port Douglas, about an hour north, reaches quieter sections. The Whitsundays, further south, are the classic sailing ground — 74 islands in a sheltered passage with long white beaches and reliable winds. Whitehaven Beach on Whitsunday Island is frequently cited among the most beautiful beaches in the world.

The reef faces serious pressure from climate change — mass bleaching events have damaged large sections, particularly in the northern reaches. The parts of the reef most accessible from Cairns and Port Douglas remain in reasonable condition, but the long-term picture is uncertain. Snorkelling and diving here is still spectacular; the diversity of coral, fish, and marine life is unlike anything most people have seen. Come now rather than later.
