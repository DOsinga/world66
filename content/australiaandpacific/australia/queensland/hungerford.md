Hungerford is a tiny outback town in southwestern [Queensland](/australiaandpacific/australia/queensland), hard against the New South Wales border. It is the sort of place where the journey is most of the point: long gravel roads, flat country, the Paroo River nearby, and the famous Dingo Fence running just south of the main street.

The town has a strong literary footnote. Henry Lawson wrote about Hungerford, and the place still has the spare, dry-border feeling that suits his stories. The Royal Mail Hotel is the practical centre of town, part pub, part general store, and part outback meeting point.

Do not treat Hungerford like a normal road stop. Flooding on the Paroo can cut access even when the town itself is dry, and distances are serious: Bourke, Eulo, and Thargomindah are all a long way off on gravel roads. If you come prepared, the reward is a sharp little taste of the Channel Country edge.
