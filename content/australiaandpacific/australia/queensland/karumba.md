Karumba is the end-of-the-road Gulf town at the mouth of the Norman River, a place of prawning boats, tidal flats, fishing talk, and wide sunsets over the Gulf of Carpentaria. It is small and remote, but it has a clear reason to exist for travellers crossing the Gulf Country: this is where the sealed road finally reaches the sea.

The town has two parts. Karumba proper sits by the river and the barramundi centre; Karumba Point faces the gulf and is where visitors gather in the late afternoon for fish and chips, cold drinks, and the red ball of the sun dropping into flat water. Between them, the low country is all saltpan, mangrove, paperbark, birds, and wallabies.

Karumba is not a polished resort. It is hot, seasonal, and practical, with a dry-season rhythm built around fishing charters, river cruises, and caravans. Come for the Gulf edge: barramundi at the [Les Wilson Barramundi Discovery Centre](/australiaandpacific/australia/queensland/karumba/les_wilson_barramundi_discovery_centre), birds along the [Karumba Heritage Walk](/australiaandpacific/australia/queensland/karumba/karumba_heritage_walk), and sunset at [Karumba Point](/australiaandpacific/australia/queensland/karumba/karumba_point).
