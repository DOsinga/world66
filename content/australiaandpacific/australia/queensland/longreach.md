Longreach sits in the red dust of outback Queensland, about 1,200 km west of the coast. It is a quintessential Australian bush town — wide streets, low buildings, enormous skies — and the birthplace of Qantas, which started here in 1920 as a two-plane mail service before becoming Australia's national airline.

The [Australian Stockman's Hall of Fame](/australiaandpacific/australia/queensland/longreach/australian_stockmans_hall_of_fame) is the town's main attraction, a museum celebrating the drovers, shearers, and station workers who shaped the outback. It is well done and worth several hours. Nearby, the [Qantas Founders Museum](/australiaandpacific/australia/queensland/longreach/qantas_founders_museum) has a retired 747 you can walk through, plus the original 1922 Qantas hangar and a Boeing 707 in passenger configuration — the only one in the world.

Longreach is also a departure point for the [Thomson River sunset cruises](/australiaandpacific/australia/queensland/longreach/thomson_river_cruise), where you drift past river red gums while watching the outback sky turn orange, followed by a camp oven dinner. The heritage-listed [Longreach Railway Station](/australiaandpacific/australia/queensland/longreach/longreach_railway_station), still served by the weekly Spirit of the Outback train from Brisbane, is worth visiting even for non-rail travellers. The town makes a good overnight stop on the long drive between the coast and the Channel Country further west.
