Normanton is a small Gulf Country town with a larger-than-expected sense of theatre. It began as a river port for cattle and mining country, then became the western end of the railway to Croydon's goldfields. Today it is dusty, practical, and memorable in the way outback towns often are: a big sky, a few strong stories, and one or two sights that could not be anywhere else.

The [Gulflander](/australiaandpacific/australia/queensland/normanton/gulflander_and_normanton_railway_station) is the headline. The little heritage train still runs from Normanton across the Gulf Savannah, and the station museum gives the town a proper historical anchor. Nearby, [Krys the Crocodile](/australiaandpacific/australia/queensland/normanton/krys_the_crocodile) is pure roadside theatre, but it also points to the Norman River country that made such stories possible.

Normanton is not polished. That is the point. Walk the heritage streets, look for the old [Burns Philp Building](/australiaandpacific/australia/queensland/normanton/burns_philp_building), and drive out toward the [Mutton Hole Wetlands](/australiaandpacific/australia/queensland/normanton/mutton_hole_wetlands) if birds and floodplain country are part of your trip.
