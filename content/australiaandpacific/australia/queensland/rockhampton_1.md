Rockhampton sits on the Fitzroy River in central Queensland, roughly halfway up the Queensland coast between Brisbane and Cairns. It lies almost exactly on the Tropic of Capricorn — a fact marked by several roadside signs on the highway — and the climate has the tropical warmth of the north without quite reaching its humidity.

The city is the commercial centre of central Queensland's beef cattle industry, and it leans into this: the streets are lined with statues of Brahman bulls and the region produces a large share of Australia's beef. The [Quay Street heritage precinct](/australiaandpacific/australia/queensland/rockhampton_1/quay_street) contains some of the finest 19th-century commercial architecture in Queensland, and the [Rockhampton Museum of Art](/australiaandpacific/australia/queensland/rockhampton_1/rockhampton_museum_of_art) is a genuine regional gallery worth an hour's visit.

The [Capricorn Caves](/australiaandpacific/australia/queensland/rockhampton_1/capricorn_caves) north of the city are the main natural attraction — limestone cave systems with cathedral-like chambers that host concerts and a remarkable solstice light show. [Great Keppel Island](/australiaandpacific/australia/queensland/rockhampton_1/great_keppel_island), accessible by ferry from Rosslyn Bay, has white-sand beaches and snorkelling over fringing reefs, and is quieter than better-known Queensland island resorts.
