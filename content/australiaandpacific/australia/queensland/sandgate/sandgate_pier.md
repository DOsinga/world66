  The original Sandgate jetty was built in the 1860s when the town was a seaside resort for Brisbane families arriving by ferry. 
  The current timber pier is one of the longest surviving wooden piers in Queensland and has been in continuous use for recreational fishing, 
  walking, and swimming since the Victorian era — a remarkable survivor in a state where most coastal infrastructure has been rebuilt in concrete.

The Sandgate Pier extends into Bramble Bay at the foot of the foreshore park, a long timber structure that is one of the suburb's defining landmarks. At low tide the mudflats around the pier are good for wading birds — ibis, herons, and the occasional spoonbill feed along the water's edge — while at high tide the pier is popular with local fishers chasing bream and flathead. Dolphins occasionally enter the bay and can be seen from the pier at dusk.

The walk along the pier gives views back across the foreshore to the row of old shopfronts and the hills behind Sandgate — a view that has changed remarkably little since the Victorian period. The foreshore park below the pier has picnic tables and a playground; on weekends it fills with families from across northern Brisbane.
