Stanthorpe is the main town of the Granite Belt, a cool, high-country pocket of southern Queensland where vineyards, apple orchards, and granite outcrops replace the coastal heat people often expect from the state. It sits on the New England Highway, close to the New South Wales border, and makes a useful base for exploring wineries and national parks spread across the surrounding countryside.

The town itself is small and practical, with cafes, pubs, farm-produce shops, a local museum, and a good regional art gallery. The bigger appeal is the landscape around it. Drive south and the country opens into vineyards and boulder fields, with [Girraween National Park](/australiaandpacific/australia/queensland/stanthorpe/girraween_national_park) as the standout: granite arches, balancing rocks, spring wildflowers, and walking tracks that feel unlike most of Queensland.

Stanthorpe is best with a car. Spend a morning at Girraween or [Donnellys Castle](/australiaandpacific/australia/queensland/stanthorpe/donnellys_castle), then use the afternoon for a cellar door, the heritage museum, or the lookout above town. It is not a big-ticket city, but as a one-day Granite Belt stop it has a clear identity and enough real anchors to justify the detour.
