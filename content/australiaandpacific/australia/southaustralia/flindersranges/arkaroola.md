Arkaroola is the wild northern end of the [Flinders Ranges](/australiaandpacific/australia/southaustralia/flindersranges): a private wilderness sanctuary of ragged red ridges, dry creekbeds, hot springs, and hard desert light. It is not a town in the usual sense. It is a remote base for people who have come for geology, 4WD tracks, bushwalking, wallabies, and night skies.

The sanctuary was created by geologist Reg Sprigg in 1968 and now covers about 610 square kilometres. That scientific streak still gives Arkaroola its character. The rocks are ancient and strange, the ridgelines expose tilted seabeds and mineral country, and the landscape feels less like a scenic backdrop than an open-air geology lesson.

The classic experience is the [Ridgetop Tour](/australiaandpacific/australia/southaustralia/flindersranges/arkaroola/ridgetop_tour), a rough guided 4WD climb to Sillers Lookout. Walkers can take on creek and gorge routes such as [Bararranna Gorge](/australiaandpacific/australia/southaustralia/flindersranges/arkaroola/bararranna_gorge_walk), while clear nights belong to the sanctuary's [observatories](/australiaandpacific/australia/southaustralia/flindersranges/arkaroola/arkaroola_observatories).

Come prepared. Arkaroola is several hundred kilometres from Adelaide, with long unsealed approaches, little shade, and weather that can make roads difficult fast. That remoteness is exactly why it is worth the effort.
