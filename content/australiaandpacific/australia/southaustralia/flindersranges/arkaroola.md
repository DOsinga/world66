  Arkaroola was established as a private wildlife sanctuary in 1968 by geologist Reg Sprigg,
  who had discovered the Ediacaran fossil fauna — among the oldest complex multicellular
  life forms known — in the hills of the northern Flinders. His goal was to protect both
  the geological heritage and the native fauna from grazing pressure. The sanctuary is
  now one of Australia's best examples of private conservation at scale.

Arkaroola Wilderness Sanctuary sits in the remote northern Flinders Ranges, a private reserve covering 610 square kilometres of rugged ridgelines, gorges, and springs. It is four to five hours by car from Adelaide on partly unsealed roads, and that distance keeps visitor numbers low and the feeling of wilderness intact.

The signature experience is the Ridgetop Tour — a four-wheel-drive trip along an exposed mountain track with 360-degree views over the northern ranges and the gibber plains beyond. The stargazing at Arkaroola is also exceptional: with no town nearby, the night sky is as dark as the outback gets, and the sanctuary runs an observatory. Thermal springs feed a swimming pool of naturally warm water.
