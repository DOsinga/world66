Blinman is the highest town in South Australia, perched in the northern Flinders at about 610 metres above sea level. It has a population of fewer than fifty people, a historic pub, and the Blinman Copper Mine — which operated from the 1860s and can be toured today. The mine produced copper during the Victorian-era mining boom that briefly made the northern Flinders look like a frontier of progress.

The Old Blinman Mine Heritage Site offers self-guided and guided tours through the restored tunnels and surface workings. The surrounding country is excellent for wildlife watching: emus, kangaroos, and yellow-footed rock-wallabies are regularly seen on the road between Blinman and Wilpena.
