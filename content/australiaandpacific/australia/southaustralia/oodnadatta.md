Oodnadatta is a tiny town on the Oodnadatta Track, a long way from almost everywhere and all the better for it. It was once an important stop on the old Ghan railway and on older outback routes; today it is a roadhouse, museum, Aboriginal community, and practical pause in a huge desert landscape.

The town's identity is stronger than its size. The [Pink Roadhouse](/australiaandpacific/australia/southaustralia/oodnadatta/pink_roadhouse) is both service station and outback landmark, while the [Railway Tjukurpa Museum](/australiaandpacific/australia/southaustralia/oodnadatta/railway_tjukurpa_museum) gives the old railway story a local home. South of town, [Algebuckina Bridge](/australiaandpacific/australia/southaustralia/oodnadatta/algebuckina_bridge) is the kind of lonely engineering relic that makes the track memorable.

Oodnadatta is not a casual detour from Adelaide. Roads are unsealed, rain changes everything, and distances are serious. If you are already on the track, though, it is more than a fuel stop: it is one of the places where the route's railway, pastoral, Arabana, and road-trip stories come together.
