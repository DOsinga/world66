Port Germein is a tiny Spencer Gulf town with one outsized reason to stop: its long wooden jetty, built for shallow water and wheat ships, still pushes far out across the tidal flats. At low tide the sea can feel almost absent; at high tide the same foreshore becomes a fishing, crabbing, and sunset place.

This was once a working grain port tied to the country east of the Southern Flinders Ranges. The railway and bigger ports ended that role, but the town kept enough pieces to make the story readable: [Port Germein Jetty](/australiaandpacific/australia/southaustralia/port_germein/port_germein_jetty), the old [tide clock](/australiaandpacific/australia/southaustralia/port_germein/port_germein_tide_clock), the foreshore, and the road inland through [Port Germein Gorge](/australiaandpacific/australia/southaustralia/port_germein/port_germein_gorge).

Port Germein is not a resort and should not be sold as one. Come if you are driving between Adelaide, Port Pirie, and the Flinders Ranges and want a slow coastal stop with a real local shape: weathered timber, big sky, shallow water, and a town that still faces its jetty.
