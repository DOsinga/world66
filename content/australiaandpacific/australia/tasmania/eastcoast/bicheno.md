Bicheno is a small fishing and holiday town on Tasmania's east coast, between [St Helens](/australiaandpacific/australia/tasmania/eastcoast/st_helens) and the Freycinet Peninsula. It is less polished than Coles Bay and quieter than the big-ticket national parks, which is part of the appeal. You come for granite, cold clear water, seafood, and little penguins returning after dark.

The town's sights are close together. The [Bicheno Blowhole](/australiaandpacific/australia/tasmania/eastcoast/bicheno/bicheno_blowhole) is an easy walk from the centre, and the coastal rocks around it are worth lingering over when the sea is up. At dusk, [Bicheno Penguin Tours](/australiaandpacific/australia/tasmania/eastcoast/bicheno/bicheno_penguin_tours) takes visitors to see little penguins come ashore without trampling the colony.

Bicheno also has a stronger wildlife and underwater side than many east-coast towns. [East Coast Natureworld](/australiaandpacific/australia/tasmania/eastcoast/bicheno/east_coast_natureworld) is useful if you want Tasmanian devils and native wildlife in one place, while [Governor Island Marine Reserve](/australiaandpacific/australia/tasmania/eastcoast/bicheno/governor_island_marine_reserve) is one of Tasmania's best-known temperate dive sites.

It works well as a slow overnight stop on the east coast drive. With a spare day, you can mix the blowhole, a beach walk, seafood, and either penguins or a dive without spending the whole day in the car.
