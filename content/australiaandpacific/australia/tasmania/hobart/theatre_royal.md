story: >
  The Theatre Royal opened in 1837 and has staged continuous productions ever since, making it the oldest working theatre in Australia. Noël Coward called it "a dream of a theatre"; Laurence Olivier added that "this little Georgian jewel must be preserved." It nearly wasn't — in 1984 the building was gutted by fire and only the original Georgian stage box, the cast-iron pillars, and a small portion of the auditorium survived. The painstaking restoration, completed in 1986, is the version visitors see today.

A small Georgian theatre tucked into a narrow lane in the CBD, with a four-tier auditorium, gilded plasterwork, and a painted dress-circle ceiling. The intimacy is the thing — only 700 seats, and from the balcony you can almost reach the stage. The contemporary programme, run in partnership with the next-door **Hedberg** rehearsal and performance complex (opened 2019), is a mix of Tasmanian Symphony Orchestra concerts, touring theatre, comedy, and the Tasmania State Opera's annual season.

Guided tours run on Wednesdays at 11:00 and are worth it for the building alone — the guide will point out the secret tunnel that was used to ferry actors to the next-door **Shipwrights' Arms Hotel** during long intervals. Performance schedule on theatreroyal.com.au.
