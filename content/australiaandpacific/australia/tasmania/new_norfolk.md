New Norfolk sits on a broad bend of the Derwent River, about 35 minutes northwest of [Hobart](/australiaandpacific/australia/tasmania/hobart). It is one of Tasmania's older European settlements, with a country-town centre, deciduous trees, and a river setting that feels more English than much of Australia.

The town is worth more than a fuel stop if you like layered colonial history. [Willow Court](/australiaandpacific/australia/tasmania/new_norfolk/willow_court) began as part of a 19th-century asylum complex and has become the town's most distinctive precinct, mixing heavy history with antiques, food, and slow restoration. Nearby, [St Matthew's Anglican Church](/australiaandpacific/australia/tasmania/new_norfolk/st_matthews_anglican_church) gives New Norfolk one of Tasmania's cleanest early church landmarks.

New Norfolk also has a strong rummaging streak. Its antique shops are the kind where you can lose an hour without meaning to, and they give the town a clear identity beyond being the gateway to the upper Derwent Valley. If you want the whole scene in one glance, drive up to [Pulpit Rock Lookout](/australiaandpacific/australia/tasmania/new_norfolk/pulpit_rock_lookout) for the river bend, hills, and town below.

This is a spare-day trip from Hobart rather than a major base. Come for heritage, antiques, autumn colour, and the Derwent Valley mood; keep driving if you only want big-ticket wilderness.
