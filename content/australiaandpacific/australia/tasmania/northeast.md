The north-eastern corner of Tasmania is one of the less visited parts of the state, but that is more a reflection of its sparse population than a lack of things to do. The area takes in the [**Tamar Valley**](/australiaandpacific/australia/tasmania/northeast/tamar_valley) wine region, [**Ben Lomond National Park**](/australiaandpacific/australia/tasmania/northeast/ben_lomond_national_park) (Tasmania's main skiing destination), and a rugged coastline running from [St Helens](/australiaandpacific/australia/tasmania/northeast/st_helens) to Georgetown.

Offshore, [**Flinders Island**](/australiaandpacific/australia/tasmania/northeast/flinders_island) and **Cape Barren Island** are rich in both history and wilderness — Flinders in particular is worth the trip for its empty beaches, bushwalking, and the remains of the Wybalenna settlement where Aboriginal Tasmanians were confined in the 1830s.

The north-east can be approached from several directions: up the east coast from Hobart via St Helens, inland from Launceston towards Ben Lomond, or north from Launceston to Georgetown and the coast.
