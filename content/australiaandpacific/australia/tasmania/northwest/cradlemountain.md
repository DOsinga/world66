Cradle Mountain is Tasmania's postcard wilderness, but it is not tame scenery. The mountain rises above glacial lakes, button grass moorland, pencil-pine forest, and weather that can switch from sun to sleet in a single walk. It is the northern gateway to Cradle Mountain-Lake St Clair National Park and the start of the Overland Track, one of Australia's classic multi-day hikes.

The easiest introduction is [Dove Lake](/australiaandpacific/australia/tasmania/northwest/cradlemountain/dove_lake), where the mountain's jagged profile reflects in dark water on still mornings. Shorter walks such as the [Enchanted Walk](/australiaandpacific/australia/tasmania/northwest/cradlemountain/enchanted_walk) make the area accessible even if you are not a serious hiker, while the climb to [Marions Lookout](/australiaandpacific/australia/tasmania/northwest/cradlemountain/marions_lookout) gives the big view without committing to the full Overland Track.

Come prepared even for short walks. Boardwalks and shuttle buses make the front country feel organised, but this is still alpine Tasmania: waterproof layers, warm clothing, and a realistic sense of daylight matter. Wildlife is a consolation prize on bad-weather days; wombats, wallabies, echidnas, and currawongs are often easier to find than clear skies.
