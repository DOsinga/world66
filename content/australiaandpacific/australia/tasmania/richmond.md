Richmond is a small Georgian village in the Coal River Valley, about 25 kilometres northeast of [Hobart](/australiaandpacific/australia/tasmania/hobart). It is one of the best-preserved colonial settlements in Australia, and its main street of sandstone buildings looks much as it did in the 1830s.

The [Richmond Bridge](/australiaandpacific/australia/tasmania/richmond/richmond_bridge), built by convict labour in 1823, is the oldest stone span bridge in Australia and the centrepiece of the village. The [Richmond Gaol](/australiaandpacific/australia/tasmania/richmond/richmond_gaol), which predates Port Arthur by five years, is worth a visit for the cramped cells and the flogging yard — it gives a more intimate and less theatrical sense of convict punishment than the larger site down the peninsula. [St John's Catholic Church](/australiaandpacific/australia/tasmania/richmond/st_johns_church), finished in 1837, is the oldest Catholic church in Australia.

Beyond the history, the [Coal River Valley](/australiaandpacific/australia/tasmania/richmond/coal_river_valley_wine) has become one of Tasmania's wine regions, with cellar doors producing cool-climate pinot noir and sparkling wine. Richmond makes an easy half-day trip from Hobart, or a pleasant stop on the way to the east coast.
