Rosebery is a small mining town in western Tasmania, tucked below Mount Black and surrounded by wet forest, steep roads, and industrial history. The mine still matters here, so the town feels lived-in rather than dressed up for visitors.

The reason to stop is the landscape around it. [Montezuma Falls](/australiaandpacific/australia/tasmania/rosebery/montezuma_falls), reached from nearby Williamsford, is one of Tasmania's great short walks. [Stitt Falls](/australiaandpacific/australia/tasmania/rosebery/stitt_falls) is almost inside town, and [Mount Murchison](/australiaandpacific/australia/tasmania/rosebery/mount_murchison) gives strong walkers a hard, high view over the West Coast Range.

Rosebery is not a soft base. The weather is wet, the roads can be black-ice country in winter, and the town's charm is practical rather than pretty. But if you are already crossing western Tasmania, it is worth more than a fuel stop.
