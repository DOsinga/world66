Ross is a tiny heritage village in the Tasmanian Midlands, on the old road between [Hobart](/australiaandpacific/australia/tasmania/hobart) and [Launceston](/australiaandpacific/australia/tasmania/launceston). It is not a big destination, but it is one of the cleanest snapshots of early colonial Tasmania: sandstone cottages, a convict-built bridge, a church spire, and a main street that still feels scaled for horses and carts.

The [Ross Bridge](/australiaandpacific/australia/tasmania/ross/ross_bridge) is the reason to stop first. Built by convict labour in 1836, it crosses the Macquarie River with carved stone panels that are far more playful than you expect from colonial public works. Nearby, the [Ross Female Factory](/australiaandpacific/australia/tasmania/ross/ross_female_factory) gives the village a darker edge, marking one of the Tasmanian sites where transported women were imprisoned, punished, and hired out as labour.

Ross works best as a slow half-day rather than a rushed photo stop. Walk the grid of Georgian cottages, visit the [Tasmanian Wool Centre](/australiaandpacific/australia/tasmania/ross/tasmanian_wool_centre), and leave time for the bakery culture that has made the village oddly famous with Japanese visitors. If you are already driving the Midlands Highway, it is a much better break than another anonymous service station.
