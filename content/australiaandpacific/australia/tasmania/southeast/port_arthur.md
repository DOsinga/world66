Port Arthur is a small settlement on the Tasman Peninsula, almost 100 kilometres southeast of [Hobart](/australiaandpacific/australia/tasmania/hobart), but the name carries much more weight than the town's size suggests. This was one of the British Empire's harshest convict stations, chosen for its isolation and surrounded by water, forest, and cliffs that made escape nearly hopeless.

The [Port Arthur Historic Site](/australiaandpacific/australia/tasmania/southeast/port_arthur/port_arthur_historic_site) is the reason to come: a large World Heritage-listed landscape of ruins, restored buildings, gardens, museum displays, and harbour views. It is not a quick plaque-and-photo stop. The entry ticket encourages a slower visit, and the site makes more sense if you allow time for the talks, the harbour cruise, and the walk between the prison, church, commandant's house, and separate prison.

The wider peninsula adds a second layer. The road from Hobart passes Eaglehawk Neck and the coastal rock formations of Tasman National Park, and the [Coal Mines Historic Site](/australiaandpacific/australia/tasmania/southeast/port_arthur/coal_mines_historic_site) gives a quieter view of the same convict system. Port Arthur works as a long day trip from Hobart, but it is better if you stay late enough for the crowds to thin and the ruins to feel lonely again.
