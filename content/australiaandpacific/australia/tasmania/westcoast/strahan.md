Strahan is the small harbour town where Tasmania's west coast becomes accessible without becoming tame. It sits on Macquarie Harbour, a huge sheet of dark water cut off from the Southern Ocean by the narrow, dangerous entrance of Hells Gates.

The town began as a port for mining and timber, but it now works as the main base for the region's wilderness trips. [Gordon River cruises](/australiaandpacific/australia/tasmania/westcoast/strahan/gordon_river_cruises) cross the harbour, pass the old convict station on [Sarah Island](/australiaandpacific/australia/tasmania/westcoast/strahan/sarah_island), and nose into temperate rainforest. The [West Coast Wilderness Railway](/australiaandpacific/australia/tasmania/westcoast/strahan/west_coast_wilderness_railway) leaves from Regatta Point for the old mining line toward Queenstown.

Strahan is not a polished resort. It is wet, far away, and often moody, which is part of the point. Walk to [Hogarth Falls](/australiaandpacific/australia/tasmania/westcoast/strahan/hogarth_falls) through rainforest, then drive out to [Ocean Beach](/australiaandpacific/australia/tasmania/westcoast/strahan/ocean_beach) to watch surf roll in from the open Southern Ocean. If you have one spare day on the west coast, this is the place to spend it.
