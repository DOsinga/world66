Bendigo is one of Victoria's great gold rush towns, about two hours northwest of [Melbourne](/australiaandpacific/australia/victoria/melbourne) by car or a comfortable train ride on the V/Line. Like [Ballarat](/australiaandpacific/australia/victoria/ballarat), it boomed in the 1850s when gold was found at Bendigo Creek, and the wealth still shows in the elaborate Victorian architecture lining Pall Mall and View Street.

The [Central Deborah Gold Mine](/australiaandpacific/australia/victoria/bendigo/central_deborah_gold_mine) lets you descend 60 metres underground into the tunnels where miners once worked by candlelight. The [Golden Dragon Museum](/australiaandpacific/australia/victoria/bendigo/golden_dragon_museum) tells the story of the Chinese miners who shaped the town's character and houses Sun Loong, one of the world's longest imperial dragons. Bendigo's [Easter Festival](/australiaandpacific/australia/victoria/bendigo/easter_festival), running since 1871, is one of Australia's oldest and features the dragon in a parade through the centre of town.

Beyond the gold rush heritage, Bendigo has reinvented itself as a regional arts centre. The [Bendigo Art Gallery](/australiaandpacific/australia/victoria/bendigo/bendigo_art_gallery) is one of the oldest and largest in regional Australia, regularly hosting major touring exhibitions. The town's food and wine scene draws on the surrounding Goldfields region, with local producers supplying a growing number of good restaurants and cafes along [Pall Mall and View Street](/australiaandpacific/australia/victoria/bendigo/pall_mall_and_view_street).
