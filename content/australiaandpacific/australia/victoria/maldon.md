Maldon is a small goldfields town in central [Victoria](/australiaandpacific/australia/victoria), about 16km from Castlemaine and 40km from [Bendigo](/australiaandpacific/australia/victoria/bendigo). It is not a big place, but it has one of the most intact 19th-century townscapes in regional Australia: verandah-fronted shops, stone gutters, old hotels, and mining relics sitting close enough together to make the story easy to read on foot.

The town grew after gold was found around Mount Tarrengower in the 1850s. Unlike busier gold rush centres, Maldon kept its scale, and in the 1960s the National Trust named it Australia's first "notable town" for the way its historic fabric had survived. That can sound like museum language, but Maldon still works as a lived-in country town, with cafes, galleries, pubs, and antique shops using the old buildings rather than embalming them.

The best visit mixes the centre with the bush just outside it. Walk High Street and Main Street, ride the [Victorian Goldfields Railway](/australiaandpacific/australia/victoria/maldon/victorian_goldfields_railway) to Castlemaine, then head for [Beehive Mine and Chimney](/australiaandpacific/australia/victoria/maldon/beehive_mine_and_chimney), [Carman's Tunnel](/australiaandpacific/australia/victoria/maldon/carmans_tunnel), or [Mount Tarrengower Lookout](/australiaandpacific/australia/victoria/maldon/mount_tarrengower_lookout). Maldon is strongest as a slow day in the Goldfields, not as a checklist stop.
