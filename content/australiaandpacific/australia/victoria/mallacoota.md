Mallacoota is Victoria's far-eastern coastal town, tucked between the ocean, Mallacoota Inlet, and the forests of Croajingolong National Park. It feels remote because it is remote: the Princes Highway passes inland, and the final road into town winds down through quiet bush with the sense that you are leaving the busier coast behind.

The appeal is water and wilderness rather than urban polish. You can swim at ocean beaches, walk to headlands, paddle or cruise the inlet, watch birds in the heath and wetlands, and use the town as a base for Croajingolong's long, lightly settled coast.

Mallacoota is worth the detour if you like slow coastal places. It is not a resort strip, and that is the point. Bring supplies, respect fire and weather warnings, and give yourself enough time for the inlet and the national park rather than treating it as a quick stop.
