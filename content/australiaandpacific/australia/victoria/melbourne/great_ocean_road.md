story: >
  The Great Ocean Road was built between 1919 and 1932 by returned soldiers from World War I as a memorial to their fallen comrades, making it the world's largest war memorial. The work was extraordinarily difficult — workers blasted and scraped a road from cliff faces using hand tools and primitive machinery, living in tents along the way. The road was also a depression-era employment project, and the tension between commemoration and necessity is part of its history.

The Great Ocean Road is one of Australia's most spectacular coastal drives, running 243 kilometres along the southwest Victorian coast from Torquay to Allansford. The most visited section, from Lorne to Port Campbell, takes in the Twelve Apostles — the limestone sea stacks rising from the Southern Ocean that are the most photographed landform in Australia. Allow at least a full day for the round trip; two days with an overnight stop is better.

From Melbourne, head southwest through Geelong to Torquay, the surf capital of Victoria, then follow the coast road west. Lorne is a good lunch stop with excellent restaurants overlooking the beach. The Otway Ranges rise steeply behind the coast and contain temperate rainforest with Californian redwoods and giant tree ferns accessible on short walks. The Twelve Apostles are best at sunrise or sunset when the light is warm and the tour buses have not yet arrived or have already left.
