  When the Tote was forced to close briefly in 2010 over liquor-licensing conditions blamed on live music, the response was extraordinary: a crowd estimated at 20,000 marched through central Melbourne in the SLAM (Save Live Australian Music) rally, one of the largest cultural protests in the city's history. The Tote is one of the most important small live music venues in Australia, a grungy corner pub on Johnston Street that has been a launching pad and proving ground for Melbourne's rock, punk, and independent bands for decades. The front bar is all sticky carpet and band stickers; the band room out the back is where the noise happens, most nights of the week.

It is not pretty, and that is exactly the point — this is a working music pub, not a heritage display. The booking is heavy on local and touring guitar bands, with cheap beer and a beer garden for the gaps between sets. For anyone wanting to understand why Melbourne calls itself a live music city, a night at the Tote is the single best lesson.
