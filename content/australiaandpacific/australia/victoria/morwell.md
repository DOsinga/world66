Morwell sits in the Latrobe Valley in Gippsland, the heart of Victoria's brown coal country. It is not a tourist destination in the conventional sense — the open-cut mines and power stations that dominate the valley are a stark reminder of industrial Australia — but the town has an unexpected natural asset on its doorstep.

[Morwell National Park](/australiaandpacific/australia/victoria/morwell/morwell_national_park), on the town's southern edge, protects a stretch of tall eucalypt forest and is home to a resident koala colony. The Billys Creek walk winds through the forest past fern gullies and a waterfall, and it is one of the few places in Gippsland where you can reliably spot koalas in the wild without a guide. The [Latrobe Regional Gallery](/australiaandpacific/australia/victoria/morwell/latrobe_regional_gallery) on Commercial Road is the cultural anchor of the region, with a solid collection of Australian art and a changing program of exhibitions from major institutions.

For those interested in industrial history, the [Hazelwood Open Cut Mine Lookout](/australiaandpacific/australia/victoria/morwell/hazelwood_open_cut) offers a vertiginous view over one of the world's largest former coal mines — a scar in the landscape that powered Victoria for most of the 20th century. If you are passing through the Latrobe Valley on your way to the Gippsland Lakes or Wilsons Promontory, the national park and gallery make worthwhile stops.
