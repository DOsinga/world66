A former coal-mining town in South Gippsland, Wonthaggi is the main service centre for the Bass Coast region of [Victoria](/australiaandpacific/australia/victoria). It was founded in 1909 when black coal was discovered here, and the mining heritage still defines the town's character. The [State Coal Mine](/australiaandpacific/australia/victoria/wonthaggi/state_coal_mine), now a heritage site, offers underground tours that take you into the old workings with a guide who explains the harsh conditions the miners endured — it is one of the most interesting industrial heritage experiences in Victoria.

The town itself is a practical, no-frills place, but its location makes it a useful base. Phillip Island and its famous penguin parade are about 30 minutes to the west, and [Cape Paterson](/australiaandpacific/australia/victoria/wonthaggi/cape_paterson), a quiet stretch of coastline with rock pools and good surf, is just 10 minutes south. The [Wonthaggi Wind Farm](/australiaandpacific/australia/victoria/wonthaggi/wonthaggi_wind_farm) sits on the hills west of town — a striking counterpoint to the coal mine just down the road — and the desalination plant on the coast is one of the largest in the southern hemisphere.

Wonthaggi is about two hours southeast of [Melbourne](/australiaandpacific/australia/victoria/melbourne). If you are visiting Phillip Island and want to avoid the resort prices of Cowes, Wonthaggi offers more affordable options and a genuine country-town atmosphere.
