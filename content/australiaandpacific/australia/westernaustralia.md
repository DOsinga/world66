Western Australia is vast — one-third of the Australian continent, larger than Western Europe — but almost entirely empty. The population clusters in the southwest corner around [Perth](/australiaandpacific/australia/westernaustralia/perth) and the port city of [Fremantle](/australiaandpacific/australia/westernaustralia/fremantle); beyond that, the state sprawls into red desert, ancient outback, and thousands of kilometres of Indian Ocean coastline.

Perth is one of the world's most isolated cities, closer to Singapore than to Sydney. It's also one of the sunniest: the Swan Valley wineries, Kings Park above the river, and the white-sand beaches of Cottesloe and Scarborough are integral to the lifestyle. Fremantle, the old port town, has kept more of its colonial character than Perth and has a good food and market scene.

To the north, the Kimberley region is spectacular and remote — gorges, ancient Aboriginal rock art, and the extraordinary Bungle Bungle Range at Purnululu. [Broome](/australiaandpacific/australia/westernaustralia/broome) is the gateway, known for its pearl luggers and cable beach sunsets. Further south, [Esperance](/australiaandpacific/australia/westernaustralia/esperance) has some of the finest beaches in Australia. [Margaret River](/australiaandpacific/australia/westernaustralia/margaretriver) in the southwest corner is excellent wine country with caves and surf.
