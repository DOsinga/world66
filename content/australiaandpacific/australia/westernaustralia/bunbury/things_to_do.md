Bunbury's best-known attraction is also its most unusual. Up to 90 bottlenose dolphins have made Koombana Bay their home, and they regularly come into the shallows to interact with people. The [Dolphin Discovery Centre](/australiaandpacific/australia/westernaustralia/bunbury/dolphin_discovery_centre) on the foreshore is the hub for this: you can wade in and hope for an encounter, or join a boat cruise out into the bay.

The old city has some worthwhile heritage buildings. [St Patrick's Cathedral](/australiaandpacific/australia/westernaustralia/bunbury/st_patricks_cathedral) has an unusual history — built in 1921, elevated to cathedral status by Pope Pius XII in 1954, then damaged by a tornado in 2005 and rebuilt. The [Old Bunbury Railway Station](/australiaandpacific/australia/westernaustralia/bunbury/old_bunbury_railway_station), a brick building dating from 1905, now serves as the bus interchange and visitor centre.

For nature, the [Leschenault Estuary](/australiaandpacific/australia/westernaustralia/bunbury/leschenault_estuary) lies just north of the city — a 25 km lagoon with ancient mangroves, up to 62 waterbird species, and boardwalk access through the mangrove colony. South of Bunbury, the [Tuart Forest](/australiaandpacific/australia/westernaustralia/bunbury/tuart_forest) preserves the largest remaining stand of pure tuart woodland in the world, with a scenic drive and spotlighting trail for nocturnal possums.
