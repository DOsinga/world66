Carnarvon sits where the Gascoyne River reaches the Indian Ocean, about halfway up the long road between [Perth](/australiaandpacific/australia/westernaustralia/perth) and the Ningaloo coast. Many travellers treat it as a fuel-and-groceries stop, but that undersells the place. Carnarvon has enough odd history, fresh produce, and harsh coastline to make a spare day worthwhile.

The town's two best stories could hardly be more different. At Babbage Island, the [One Mile Jetty](/australiaandpacific/australia/westernaustralia/carnarvon/one_mile_jetty) recalls the wool and livestock port that once tied the Gascoyne to the sea. East of town, the [Carnarvon Space and Technology Centre](/australiaandpacific/australia/westernaustralia/carnarvon/carnarvon_space_and_technology_centre) explains how this remote coast helped track Apollo missions and satellite communications.

Add [Gwoonwardu Mia](/australiaandpacific/australia/westernaustralia/carnarvon/gwoonwardu_mia) for Aboriginal culture, the plantation roads for bananas and mangoes, and the [Quobba Blowholes](/australiaandpacific/australia/westernaustralia/carnarvon/quobba_blowholes) for a properly wild edge. Carnarvon is not polished, but it has a real Gascoyne identity: river country, salt air, space dishes, and road-trip practicality.
