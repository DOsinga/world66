Donnybrook is a small country town in the south-west of [Western Australia](/australiaandpacific/australia/westernaustralia), about 35 kilometres inland from [Bunbury](/australiaandpacific/australia/westernaustralia/bunbury) and 210 kilometres south of [Perth](/australiaandpacific/australia/westernaustralia/perth). It is best known as the **apple capital of WA** — orchards line the Preston River valley, and the harvest from February through May draws backpackers looking for fruit-picking work.

There is a year-round farmgate trade in apples, pears, and stone fruit, and a couple of small cider makers worth seeking out. The town itself is quiet: a single main street with a few cafes, a heritage stretch of nineteenth-century buildings cut from the local sandstone (the same Donnybrook stone you see in many old Perth buildings), and the giant fibreglass apple that is required of any self-respecting Australian fruit town.

The other reason to stop is the Apple Fun Park in the centre of town — one of the largest free playgrounds in the southern hemisphere and a near-mandatory break for families on the long drive south. Beyond that, Donnybrook makes a workable, cheap base for the [Margaret River](/australiaandpacific/australia/westernaustralia/margaretriver) wine country and the tall-tree forests further south.
