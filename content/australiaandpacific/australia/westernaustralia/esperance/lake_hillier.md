story: >
  Lake Hillier was first recorded by Matthew Flinders in 1802, who noted its "rose-coloured" water in his journal. For over a century nobody could explain why it was pink. We now know the colour comes from a combination of Dunaliella salina algae, which produces red-orange carotenoid pigments, and halophilic bacteria that thrive in the ultra-saline water. Unlike some other pink lakes, Hillier retains its colour year-round and remains pink even when water is removed and held in a container.
---

Lake Hillier sits on Middle Island, the largest island in the Recherche Archipelago about 130km east of Esperance, and it is one of the most photogenic natural phenomena in Australia: a vivid bubblegum-pink lake separated from the deep blue Southern Ocean by only a thin strip of white sand and eucalyptus forest. The colour is not a trick of light — it is real, consistent, and visible from the ground as well as the air.

Middle Island is not accessible by public transport. The practical options are a scenic flight over the lake from Esperance Airport (several operators run these, lasting 1–2 hours) or a boat tour from the town that includes a landing on the island. The scenic flight gives the better visual experience — the contrast between the pink lake, white sand barrier, and turquoise sea is best appreciated from above. Ground-level access on the island is limited, partly to protect the lake's ecosystem, which includes research into the microorganisms that cause the colouration.
