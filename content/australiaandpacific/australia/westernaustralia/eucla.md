Eucla is a tiny settlement on the Western Australian edge of the Nullarbor, only a few minutes from the South Australian border. Most travellers meet it as fuel, food, and sleep on a very long road, but it has a stranger identity than that suggests: the old town was half-swallowed by dunes after the telegraph station closed and the sand began to move.

That buried history is the reason to stop. South of the highway, the [Old Telegraph Station](/australiaandpacific/australia/westernaustralia/eucla/old_telegraph_station) stands in the white sand like a desert ruin, with the Southern Ocean just beyond the dunes. Nearby paths lead to an empty [Eucla Beach](/australiaandpacific/australia/westernaustralia/eucla/eucla_beach), while the [Traveller's Cross and Eyre Memorial](/australiaandpacific/australia/westernaustralia/eucla/travellers_cross_and_eyre_memorial) give a broad view from the escarpment.

Eucla is not a town to linger in for days. It works best as one of the great small pauses on the Nullarbor drive: a place where the road, the telegraph line, the sea cliffs, and the abandoned settlement all meet in one lonely, memorable corner.
