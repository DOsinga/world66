The Dingo Flour mill on the northern shore of Fremantle Harbour carries a vast painted dingo — head down, tail up, in profile — on the side of its silo, the work of commercial artist Les Nash in 1940 and one of the longest-lived pieces of large-scale advertising in Australia. The sign is visible from much of central Fremantle and from every ship that has ever entered the harbour.

The mill is still in production and the sign is regularly repainted in the original brick-red ochre. There is no formal viewpoint; the easiest view is from the train carriage on the northbound line a minute out of Fremantle station, or from the southern footpath of the Stirling Bridge. A piece of Fremantle vernacular history that is impossible to miss once it has been pointed out.
