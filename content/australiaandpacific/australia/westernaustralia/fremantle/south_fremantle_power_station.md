The South Fremantle Power Station, on the coast at North Coogee five kilometres south of central Fremantle, ran from 1951 to 1985 and has stood empty ever since. The vast Art Deco hall — pale brick, arched windows, six tall stacks — is one of the most spectacular industrial ruins in Australia.

The site is privately owned and not officially open, but it has become a magnet for graffiti artists; the walls inside are covered in some of the most ambitious street art in the city. The exterior and the surrounding open ground are accessible and the building photographs particularly well at sunset, when the western façade is lit up against the Indian Ocean behind. The future is uncertain — proposals for residential conversion have been circling for years.
