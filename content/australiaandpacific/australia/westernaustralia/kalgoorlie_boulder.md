Kalgoorlie-Boulder is the capital of the Western Australian goldfields, a desert city built on a fortune that never quite stopped being dug out of the ground. It is rough-edged, practical, and unusually vivid: wide streets, old hotels, mining headframes, red dust, and the enormous Super Pit at the edge of town.

The first stop should be the [Super Pit Lookout](/australiaandpacific/australia/westernaustralia/kalgoorlie_boulder/super_pit_lookout), because it explains the scale of everything else. From there, the [Museum of the Goldfields](/australiaandpacific/australia/westernaustralia/kalgoorlie_boulder/museum_of_the_goldfields) gives the rush, the money, and the mining culture more depth, while [Hannan Street](/australiaandpacific/australia/westernaustralia/kalgoorlie_boulder/hannan_street) shows how much Victorian ambition was dragged into the desert.

This is not a resort town, and that is the point. Kalgoorlie-Boulder is most rewarding if you are curious about work, wealth, isolation, and the way a boomtown keeps reinventing itself when the hole in the ground keeps paying.
