Windjana Gorge cuts through the Napier Range — a low limestone escarpment that runs east from Derby and is, improbably, the remnant of a Devonian-era coral reef. The gorge itself is about 3.5 km long, with walls rising 90 metres above the narrow valley floor, and the Lennard River flows through it during the wet season, leaving a chain of waterholes in the dry. The walk along the gorge floor is one of the more accessible in the Kimberley — manageable for anyone fit enough to walk on uneven ground.

The main attraction beyond the geology is the freshwater crocodiles. Dozens of them bask along the waterhole banks — quiet, scaly, and completely indifferent to walkers passing a few metres away. They pose essentially no threat at normal distances, but the sheer concentration of them, sunning on the pale gravel bars with the orange limestone walls behind them, makes this one of the better wildlife spectacles in the region without requiring a full-scale expedition. Windjana is about 145 km east of Derby and is accessible in a conventional vehicle in dry weather.
