Margaret River is the best known name in Western Australia's South West: a small inland town, a serious wine region, and a rugged surf coast rolled into one. The town itself sits among eucalypt forest about three hours south of [Perth](/australiaandpacific/australia/westernaustralia/perth), but the real appeal spreads west to the Indian Ocean and along the Cape Naturaliste-Cape Leeuwin coast.

The region works because the parts are so close together. You can taste chardonnay at [Voyager Estate](/australiaandpacific/australia/westernaustralia/margaretriver/voyager_estate), walk below suspended limestone at [Lake Cave](/australiaandpacific/australia/westernaustralia/margaretriver/lake_cave), and be standing above the surf at [Surfers Point](/australiaandpacific/australia/westernaustralia/margaretriver/surfers_point) before sunset. The wineries are not a decorative extra here; Margaret River produces some of Australia's finest cabernet sauvignon and chardonnay, and the better cellar doors treat food just as seriously as wine.

The coast gives the place its edge. [Canal Rocks](/australiaandpacific/australia/westernaustralia/margaretriver/canal_rocks), [Sugarloaf Rock](/australiaandpacific/australia/westernaustralia/margaretriver/sugarloaf_rock), [Hamelin Bay](/australiaandpacific/australia/westernaustralia/margaretriver/hamelin_bay), and the two capes make this much more than a wine weekend. Inland, the karri trees of [Boranup Forest](/australiaandpacific/australia/westernaustralia/margaretriver/boranup_forest) and the show caves of the Leeuwin-Naturaliste ridge add a cooler, greener side to the trip.

You need a car. Public transport can get you to the town, but the wineries, caves, beaches, and lighthouses are spread along country roads. Two nights is the minimum that makes sense; three or four lets you slow down enough to enjoy the region rather than turning it into a tasting-room checklist.
