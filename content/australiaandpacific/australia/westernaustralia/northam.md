Northam is a Wheatbelt town on the Avon River, about 100 kilometres east of Perth. It is not as polished as [Toodyay](/australiaandpacific/australia/westernaustralia/toodyay) or as postcard-pretty as York, but it has a real day-trip identity: sunrise ballooning over the Avon Valley, a riverfront cultural centre, old civic buildings, and enough dry-country space to feel clear of the city.

The best reason to come is seasonal and early. [Hot air ballooning](/australiaandpacific/australia/westernaustralia/northam/avon_valley_ballooning) runs over the Avon Valley in the cooler months, when still morning air and low light make the farms, river bends, and hills look far better from above than they do from the highway. It is not cheap, but it is the one experience here that can justify the trip on its own.

Back on the ground, [Bilya Koort Boodja](/australiaandpacific/australia/westernaustralia/northam/bilya_koort_boodja) gives the town a stronger cultural anchor than many Wheatbelt stops. The [Avon River Suspension Bridge](/australiaandpacific/australia/westernaustralia/northam/avon_river_suspension_bridge) and riverside paths make an easy walk, especially if you are already in town for breakfast after a flight.

Northam is a low-pass destination under a strict bar: worthwhile if you are nearby, especially from Perth, but not a place to cross Western Australia for unless ballooning is the point.
