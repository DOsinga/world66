---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

Baker Island is an uninhabited atoll in the Pacific Remote Islands Marine National Monument, administered by the US Fish & Wildlife Service. It has had no permanent population since WWII and is closed to general visitation; only researchers with explicit permits may land.

For the few who go, the climate is hot, dry, and equatorial. Temperatures hold steady around 28–32°C year-round, the trade winds blow constantly out of the east, and rainfall is genuinely scarce — the island is in a permanent equatorial dry pocket.

**Drier and calmer months (December–March)** offer the most pleasant conditions for the brief landings researchers undertake. Seabird colonies — frigatebirds, boobies, terns — are active year-round.

**Tropical cyclone risk** is essentially zero this close to the equator, but seas can be heavy during the wetter months (July–October), making landings difficult or impossible.

There is no infrastructure on the island and no fresh water. For practical travel, treat Baker as inaccessible to the public.
