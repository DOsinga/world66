The Cook Islands Christian Church in Arutanga is one of the oldest churches in the Cook Islands, established in 1828 by missionaries of the London Missionary Society. The building itself is a solid coral-stone structure that has survived cyclones and nearly two centuries of island life with quiet dignity. It sits near the waterfront in Arutanga village, surrounded by tropical gardens, and is still the active centre of religious life for the community.

Sunday morning service is the main reason visitors come. The choir is exceptional — unaccompanied four-part harmonies that rise and fill the church with a sound that is distinctly Pacific and genuinely moving even for non-religious visitors. Locals dress formally: white is the traditional colour for churchgoers on Sunday, and the congregation in their whites against the coral stone and tropical green outside makes for an arresting scene. If you attend, dress conservatively and arrive a few minutes early. Visitors are welcomed but this is an active service, not a performance — behave accordingly and you'll be treated warmly.
