Mangaia is the southernmost and oldest island in the Cook Islands — geologically, one of the oldest islands in the Pacific. Like Atiu, it is ringed by a dramatic belt of makatea (raised coral limestone) that gives the coastline a jagged, fortress-like appearance. Inside the makatea is a central plateau with a small population spread across several villages.

The island is rarely visited, which is both its appeal and its limitation. There are a handful of guesthouses, no restaurants, and limited infrastructure. Those who make the effort — via [Air Rarotonga](/australiaandpacific/cookislands/mangaia/mangaia_airport) from Rarotonga — find [cave systems](/australiaandpacific/cookislands/mangaia/teruarere_cave), a quiet interior, and an island largely unchanged by tourism. [Te Rua Rere](/australiaandpacific/cookislands/mangaia/teruarere_cave) cave is one of the largest in the Cook Islands.

Mangaia produces good [taro](/australiaandpacific/cookislands/mangaia/taro_plantations) and has a tradition of intricate woodcarving. If you are looking for an outer island experience without the birdwatching reputation of Atiu or the fame of Aitutaki, Mangaia rewards patience.
