Manihiki is a remote atoll in the northern Cook Islands, about 1,200km north of Rarotonga. It consists of roughly 40 small islets ringing a large central [lagoon](/australiaandpacific/cookislands/manihiki/manihiki_lagoon), and the population of a few hundred lives in two villages: [Tukao](/australiaandpacific/cookislands/manihiki/tukao_village) on the northern rim and [Tauhunu](/australiaandpacific/cookislands/manihiki/tauhunu_village) on the southern side.

The atoll is famous for its [black pearls](/australiaandpacific/cookislands/manihiki/black_pearl_farms) — Manihiki produces some of the finest cultured black pearls in the world and accounts for more than 90 percent of Cook Islands pearl exports. Pearl farming is the main industry and the defining feature of life here. The lagoon's protected waters and the skills of the local divers have made this a significant economic resource for an island otherwise very remote from the mainstream. Cyclone Martin in 1997 swept across the low islets and devastated the farms; the community rebuilt slowly, but the atoll never returned to pre-cyclone production levels.

Getting to Manihiki is difficult. Air Rarotonga operates infrequent flights, and cargo ships come irregularly. There is minimal tourist infrastructure. Those who make it find a traditional atoll community, extraordinary [diving and snorkelling](/australiaandpacific/cookislands/manihiki/lagoon_diving), and a place almost entirely removed from the modern tourist circuit.
