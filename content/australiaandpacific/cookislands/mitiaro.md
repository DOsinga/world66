Mitiaro is a small, low-lying island sitting between Atiu and Mauke in the southern Cook Islands. Like its neighbours, it is encircled by makatea (raised coral limestone), and the interior consists of a fertile taro swamp plateau. The population of a few hundred lives in a single village, Mangarei.

The island has two freshwater lakes, [Te Rotonui and Te Rotoiti](/australiaandpacific/cookislands/mitiaro/te_rotonui), which together cover much of the island's land area and attract migratory birds. The cave system is the island's highlight: [Vai Nauri](/australiaandpacific/cookislands/mitiaro/vai_nauri) is a deep freshwater pool in a limestone cavern where small eels swim peacefully alongside bathers, and [Vai Marere](/australiaandpacific/cookislands/mitiaro/vai_marere) is the only sulphur cave pool in the Cook Islands. On the makatea, [Te Pare Fort](/australiaandpacific/cookislands/mitiaro/te_pare_fort) — the only known pre-European fortification in the Cook Islands — stands as a reminder of the island's history of conflict with Atiu.

Mitiaro receives very few visitors. Getting there means a small Air Rarotonga flight from Rarotonga, and accommodation is limited to basic guesthouses. It is the kind of place where a stranger's arrival is a notable event and hospitality is accordingly generous. If you are looking for a genuinely remote outer-island experience in the Cook Islands, Mitiaro delivers it.
