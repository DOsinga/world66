  Bouma was established in 1990 not by government decree but by the island's landowning clans, who collectively decided to set aside the forest rather than accept logging contracts. The park now covers roughly a third of the island — one of the larger community-owned protected areas in the Pacific — and the decision has been credited with preserving Taveuni's biodiversity and its appeal as an ecotourism destination.

Bouma National Heritage Park covers roughly one third of Taveuni's land area and contains some of the best-preserved native tropical forest in Fiji. The park is managed by the local landowning communities and protects habitat for a remarkable range of endemic and rare species, including orange dove, silktail, and collared lory.

The main visitor access is through the Tavoro Waterfalls trail, which follows a clear forest river up to a series of three waterfalls, each with a swimming pool below. The first waterfall is an easy 20-minute walk; the second requires about an hour; the third, the largest and most dramatic, takes two to three hours each way. The trail is well-maintained and the forest is dense and beautiful throughout. The park also provides access to Des Voeux Peak and the Lavena Coastal Walk.
