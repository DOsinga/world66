Melville's semi-autobiographical sequel to Typee, following his adventures in Tahiti and Moorea after the crew of his whaling ship mutinied and were briefly imprisoned by French colonial authorities. Written in 1847, it is the most vivid early account of Polynesian life by an American writer — frank about the destruction wrought by missionaries and traders, and full of the author's genuine delight in the landscape and people of the Society Islands. Lighter and funnier than Melville's later work, and a genuinely useful companion to French Polynesia travel.
