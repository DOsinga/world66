Maugham's fictional portrait of Paul Gauguin — a London stockbroker who abandons his family and career to become a painter, eventually settling in Tahiti. The novel is only loosely biographical and is more interested in the psychology of artistic obsession than in factual accuracy, but it captures something real about what the Pacific represented to Europeans of the era: an escape from bourgeois civilization into a more vivid and dangerous way of being. Maugham himself visited Tahiti after writing it, and reported that he had got it right.
