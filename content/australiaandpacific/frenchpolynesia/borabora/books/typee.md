Melville's first novel, published in 1846, is based on his own four weeks living among the Typee people of the Marquesas Islands after jumping ship. The book made him famous — it reads as adventure, ethnography, and veiled critique of Western civilisation all at once. Melville is alternately fascinated and horrified by Polynesian culture, and honest about the contradictions. The islands he describes are not Bora Bora, but the South Pacific he encounters — its beauty, its peoples, and the missionaries and merchants already transforming it — is the same Pacific you visit today. The beginning of a literature.
