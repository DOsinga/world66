---
title: "Getting Around"
type: section
---

The main island is encircled by a single road, approximately 32 km in total length. Most of the island's restaurants, guesthouses, and shops are in and around Vaitape on the western shore.

**Le Truck:** The local bus — an open truck with bench seating — circles the island irregularly and cheaply. It is the authentic way to travel but runs on an unpredictable schedule. Ask locals at the Vaitape quay about current departure times.

**Scooter rental:** The most practical way to see the main island. Rental shops in Vaitape hire 50cc scooters by the half-day or full day. The road is flat and the traffic light — a circumnavigation of the island takes about two hours without stops. Bicycles are also available for hire, though the equatorial heat makes cycling more demanding than it sounds.

**Boat transport:** Getting between the main island and the motu (reef islets) where most hotels are located requires a boat. Resorts run their own shuttle boats. For independent travellers, water taxis and boat rentals are available at Vaitape quay. Renting a small outboard boat to explore the lagoon independently is one of the great underrated pleasures of Bora Bora.
