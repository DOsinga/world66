The Opunohu Valley, climbing from the bay up toward the Belvedere, contains the densest cluster of pre-European Polynesian ceremonial sites on Moorea — and one of the most accessible in French Polynesia. Marae Titiroa, just below the Belvedere, is a large rectangular *ahu* (altar platform) of dressed basalt with carved upright slabs marking the priest's seats. A short walk on a marked forest path connects it to several smaller marae, an archery platform, and the foundations of council houses, all under tall *māpe* chestnut trees with their fluted trunks. The site is in active use for cultural ceremonies and was a major centre of authority for the western Society Islands in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries. Free to visit; allow an hour for the loop.
