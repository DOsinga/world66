Tahiti is the largest island in French Polynesia, covering 1,042 km², and is divided into a larger northwestern lobe (Tahiti Nui) and a smaller southeastern peninsula (Tahiti Iti), joined by an isthmus. Most of the population lives on the northwest coast around the capital [Papeete](/australiaandpacific/frenchpolynesia/tahiti/papeete). The island's interior is dominated by dramatic volcanic peaks, and the coastal road — 114 km around the main island — passes waterfalls, blowhole, lava tubes, and archaeological sites.

The island is more than just a transit point for the outer atolls. The [Arahoho Blowhole](/australiaandpacific/frenchpolynesia/tahiti/arahoho_blowhole) and [Faarumai Waterfalls](/australiaandpacific/frenchpolynesia/tahiti/faarumai_waterfalls) are on the north coast. [Point Venus](/australiaandpacific/frenchpolynesia/tahiti/point_venus_tahiti), where Captain Cook observed the transit of Venus in 1769, is a historically significant black-sand beach. [Marae Arahuruhu](/australiaandpacific/frenchpolynesia/tahiti/marae_arahuruhu) near Paea preserves the island's best-restored ancient Polynesian temple. In the mountainous interior, the Fautaua Valley trail leads to a large waterfall on a half-day walk.

On the southeast peninsula, [Teahupo'o](/australiaandpacific/frenchpolynesia/tahiti/teahupoo) is one of the most famous — and feared — big-wave surf breaks in the world, and hosted the 2024 Olympics surfing competition. The currency is the Pacific Franc (XPF/CFP), fixed to the Euro at 119 CFP per euro.
