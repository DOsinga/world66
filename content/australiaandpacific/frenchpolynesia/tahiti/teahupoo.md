Teahupo'o (pronounced roughly "chop-oo") sits at the end of Tahiti Iti, the smaller southeastern peninsula of the island, accessible by road about 70 km from Papeete. The wave breaks on a shallow coral reef directly offshore from the village and is, by widespread agreement among professional surfers, the most terrifying wave in the world.

The geometry is unusual: the Pacific swell travels for thousands of miles across open ocean before hitting this particular reef configuration, which causes the wave to lurch forward and form a thick, round tunnel — a tube — before collapsing over the dry reef. At modest size (2–3 metres) it is a spectacular but manageable wave. When swell exceeds 3 metres, it becomes one of the most exclusive and dangerous waves on the circuit; when it's 5 metres or more, only a handful of people in the world can surf it.

Visitors can watch from the beach at the end of the road or hire a boat to position out by the reef during swells. The village itself is tiny and traditional, with outrigger canoes drawn up on the shore. The **Billabong Pro Tahiti** competition, when it runs here, draws the world's best surfers. The 2024 Paris Olympics held its surfing events here — an extraordinary venue for international competition.

Even without swell, the lagoon around Teahupo'o is beautiful — crystal clear, sheltered, and ringed by dense forest and mountains.
