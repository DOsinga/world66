---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

Tahiti has a tropical climate with two distinct seasons. The **dry season** (May to October) brings cooler temperatures (23–27°C), lower humidity, and minimal rainfall. This is the recommended time for most visitors — conditions are comfortable, the roads are passable, and hiking trails are not waterlogged. July is the peak month: the **Heiva i Tahiti** festival brings weeks of traditional Polynesian dance competitions, outrigger canoe races, javelin throwing, and cultural celebrations throughout Papeete and the island.

The **wet season** (November to April) is hot, humid, and prone to heavy rain and occasional cyclones. Temperatures reach 30–33°C with high humidity. Cyclones are rare but possible; when they hit, they can be destructive. That said, many visitors come in the wet season without problems — the rains are often predictable afternoon storms rather than day-long downpours. The landscape is lush and spectacularly green.

**Surf at Teahupo'o** is best from May to August when consistent south and southwest swells arrive from the Southern Ocean. The **Billabong Pro** professional surfing competition typically runs in August.

**Diving and snorkelling** in the lagoon is year-round. The clearest visibility tends to be in the dry season. Whale-watching season for humpbacks is July to November, when mothers and calves are regularly seen in the lagoon.

French Polynesia's school holidays (synchronised roughly with the French school calendar) bring a surge of domestic tourism in July and August. International tourist numbers peak at the same time. Outside July–August, the island is noticeably quieter.
