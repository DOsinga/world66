Asan is a small village on Guam's western coast, just west of [Hagatna](/australiaandpacific/guam/agana), and one of the most important places on the island for understanding the 1944 Battle of Guam. The broad grass and beach between Asan Point and Adelup Point look peaceful now, but this was the northern landing area where American forces came ashore to retake Guam from Japanese occupation.

The village is closely wrapped by the War in the Pacific National Historical Park. [Asan Beach](/australiaandpacific/guam/asan/asan_beach) holds the landing monuments and beachfront paths, while [Asan Bay Overlook](/australiaandpacific/guam/asan/asan_bay_overlook) gives the clearest view of the terrain the troops faced. Inland, the ridges and valleys around [Fonte Plateau](/australiaandpacific/guam/asan/fonte_plateau) and [Matgue River Valley](/australiaandpacific/guam/asan/matgue_river_valley) explain why the fight did not end at the sand.

Asan is not a resort village. Come with a car, water, and a little time to read the signs. The reward is a compact, sobering set of sites that makes Guam's wartime history much easier to grasp than a museum display alone.
