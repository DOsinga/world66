Asan Beach is the central coastal unit of the War in the Pacific National Historical Park, stretching across a broad lawn and beach where US Marines came ashore in July 1944. Today it is open, green, and often quiet, with coconut palms, walking paths, monuments, and interpretive panels facing the reef.

The contrast is the point. Stand near the shore and look back toward the inland ridges: the beach was only the first obstacle. Pieces of wartime infrastructure and memorials sit around the park, but the most powerful thing here is the geography itself.
