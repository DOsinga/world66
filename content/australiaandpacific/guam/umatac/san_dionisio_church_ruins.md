The ruins of the San Dionisio Church stand near the centre of Umatac village — three walls and a partial facade of coral-stone masonry, open to the sky, with a modest modern church operating beside them. The ruins are compact and take only a few minutes to examine, but they carry a concentrated layer of history: the site has been a place of worship since the Spanish colonial period, and the destruction of the building records a series of disasters — earthquakes, typhoons, and the violence of World War II — that shaped the island.

The contrast between the pitted coral-block walls and the neat new church next door is itself worth noticing. Guam's Catholic faith survived all the damage; the building did not. The ruins are unfenced and can be explored freely.
