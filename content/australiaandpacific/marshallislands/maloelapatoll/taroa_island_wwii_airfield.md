Taroa Island is the main reason most history-minded travelers put Maloelap on a Marshall Islands map. During World War II it was developed as a Japanese air base, with runways, revetments, buildings, and defensive positions packed onto a narrow strip of coral land.

What remains today is not a museum. Concrete edges, airfield traces, rusting fragments, and vegetation all blend together, and the tropical climate is slowly taking the site back. That roughness is part of the impact: you are looking at a Pacific battlefield that was never tidied up for visitors.
