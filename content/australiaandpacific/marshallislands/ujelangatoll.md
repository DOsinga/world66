Ujelang Atoll is one of the most remote places in the Marshall Islands, far out in the western Ralik Chain. It is quiet now, but its modern history is heavy: after the United States began nuclear testing at Enewetak, many Enewetak people were relocated here and lived on Ujelang for decades before return became possible.

That history makes Ujelang more than an empty reef on a map. The old settlement traces around [Eimnlapp](/australiaandpacific/marshallislands/ujelangatoll/eimnlapp_settlement_site) and the wide [lagoon](/australiaandpacific/marshallislands/ujelangatoll/ujelang_lagoon) tell a story of displacement, endurance, and a Pacific community asked to carry the cost of Cold War decisions made elsewhere.

Travel here is extremely difficult. There is no normal tourist infrastructure and no reason to attempt it without permission and serious local coordination. For most travelers, Ujelang is best understood as part of the Marshall Islands' nuclear legacy rather than as a practical destination.
