---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

The Federated States of Micronesia — Yap, Chuuk, Pohnpei, Kosrae — stretch across 2,700 km of the western Pacific just north of the equator. The climate is tropical and humid, with temperatures consistently around 27–30°C year-round; the difference between months is mostly about rain and typhoon risk.

**Drier season (January–March)** is the best window for most visitors. Rainfall is lower, seas are calmer, and dive visibility is at its best — particularly important on Chuuk, where the WWII Japanese fleet wrecks in the lagoon are the country's main draw.

**Shoulder (April–June)** remains good for diving and surfacing weather is still mostly fine.

**Wet season (July–November)** brings the rains. They are heavy but typically short, and dive operators continue running. **Typhoon risk** runs roughly August–December, with most storms tracking toward the Philippines but occasionally crossing the FSM.

The **Yap Day cultural festival** in early March, with traditional dancing, stone-money exhibitions, and canoe demonstrations, is the country's biggest cultural event.

International access is mostly via the Island Hopper (United Airlines route from Honolulu via Majuro, Kosrae, Pohnpei, Chuuk, Guam) — bookable as separate stops, the only way to see the whole country in one trip.
