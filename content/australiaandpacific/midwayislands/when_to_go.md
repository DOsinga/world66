---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

Midway is a US National Wildlife Refuge run by the Fish & Wildlife Service. There is no general tourism — public access has been closed since 2013 — but the question of "when" is still meaningful for the small number of authorised visitors (researchers, volunteers, and the occasional sanctioned tour).

The climate is subtropical maritime, moderated year-round by the surrounding ocean. Temperatures sit between 18°C and 27°C most of the year.

**Laysan albatross nesting season (November–July)** is the visual peak. By the height of the season, hundreds of thousands of nesting birds cover the atoll's two main islands, with chicks fledging in June and July. This is also when most authorised visits are scheduled.

**Quiet months (August–October)** see the albatross gone and the trade winds at their strongest.

**Hurricane / tropical cyclone risk** is low but non-zero; storms occasionally swing this far north from the central Pacific basin.

For tourism, the practical reality is: you almost certainly cannot go. Check the Pacific Remote Islands Marine National Monument page on the FWS site for current access policy.
