The Museum of New Caledonia occupies a handsome colonial building near Place des Cocotiers and holds the most important collection of Kanak material culture in the world. Galleries move through the islands of the Pacific as well as New Caledonia specifically, with carved house-post ends, ceremonial masks, woven baskets, and jade pendants that demonstrate the sophistication and variety of Kanak art before French colonisation.

The Kanak section is the centrepiece — the wooden architecture, the weaponry, and the ceremonial objects are beautifully displayed and well labelled in both French and English. The museum also covers the wider Melanesian and Polynesian Pacific, allowing comparison across island cultures. Entry is free and the building is worth a look in its own right. Allow an hour to an hour and a half.
