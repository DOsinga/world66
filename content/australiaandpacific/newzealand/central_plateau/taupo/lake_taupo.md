Lake Taupo is so large — 616 square kilometres — that it takes the eye a moment to register it as a lake rather than a sea. On calm days the southern shore reveals the snow-capped cones of Ruapehu, Ngauruhoe, and Tongariro reflected in the water, a view that is one of New Zealand's most iconic.

The lakefront in town is well set up for visitors: a promenade runs along the shore, and a shallow bay provides sheltered swimming. Boat trips leave from the marina for Mine Bay, where massive Māori rock carvings — carved in the 1970s by master carver Matahi Whakataka-Brightwell — are visible on the cliffs above the waterline and can only be approached from the water. Sailing, kayaking, and waterskiing are all available from the town beach.
