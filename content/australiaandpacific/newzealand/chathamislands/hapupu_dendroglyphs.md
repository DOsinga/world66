The J.M. Barker (Hapupu) National Historic Reserve protects one of the most
remarkable indigenous art sites in the South Pacific: a grove of kopi trees
whose smooth trunks were carved by Moriori with stylised human and animal
figures, some hundreds of years old.

The site sits in a small reserve in the northeast of Chatham Island. A short
boardwalk leads through the bush to the surviving trees, with interpretation
panels explaining the carvings and the broader Moriori cultural context. Walk
slowly and quietly — this is a sacred place to local Moriori and the carvings
are fragile.
