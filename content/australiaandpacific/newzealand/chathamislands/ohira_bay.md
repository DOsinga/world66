The columnar basalt formations at Ohira Bay on the south coast of Chatham
Island are the islands' most dramatic geological feature — hexagonal cliffs of
black volcanic rock that look almost machined, rising directly out of the
Southern Ocean. The Chathams are themselves the eroded remnants of an ancient
volcanic chain, and Ohira preserves the clearest evidence of that origin.

Reaching the bay requires permission to cross private farmland and a short
walk across rough country. Ask at the visitor information centre in Waitangi
for current access arrangements. Allow half a day, and bring waterproofs and
sturdy footwear — there is no shelter and the weather changes fast.
