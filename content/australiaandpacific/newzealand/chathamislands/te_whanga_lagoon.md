Te Whanga Lagoon dominates the geography of Chatham Island, spreading across the island's interior as a shallow, brackish sheet of water. It is not a conventional beach attraction, but it is central to the way the Chathams look and feel.

Travellers usually encounter it as part of a drive or guided outing rather than as a single fixed viewpoint. Its scale, birdlife, and changing light help explain why the Chatham Islands feel so separate from mainland New Zealand.
