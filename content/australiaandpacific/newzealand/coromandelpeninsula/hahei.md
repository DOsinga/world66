Hahei is a small seaside settlement on Mercury Bay, on the eastern side of the [Coromandel Peninsula](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/coromandelpeninsula). It is tiny most of the year, then swells in summer when Aucklanders and overseas travellers come for the beach, the marine reserve, and the walk or kayak route to [Cathedral Cove](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/coromandelpeninsula/hahei/cathedral_cove).

The village has a simple rhythm: a long pale beach, a few cafes and tour desks, pohutukawa trees above the sand, and offshore islands that make the water feel sheltered. [Hahei Beach](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/coromandelpeninsula/hahei/hahei_beach) is good enough for a lazy day by itself, especially outside the Christmas rush.

The real reason to stop is the coast north and south of town. To the north, Te Whanganui-o-Hei Marine Reserve protects reefs, clear water, and the famous cove with its natural rock arch. To the south, [Te Pare Point](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/coromandelpeninsula/hahei/te_pare_point_historic_reserve) gives you a short walk to old pa sites and wide views back along the bay.

Hahei is not a place for a long checklist. Come for one strong coastal day: swim, walk the headlands, and if the weather is calm, go by kayak or boat into the marine reserve.
