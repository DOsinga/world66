Wellington is the capital of New Zealand and, at the southernmost tip of the North Island, the windiest major city in the world. That wind is a constant presence — locals accept it as part of the city's character — but it doesn't diminish Wellington's appeal as one of the most liveable and culturally rich cities in the southern hemisphere.

The central city is compact enough to walk, built around a harbour with hills rising steeply on all sides. Te Papa Tongarewa, the national museum on the waterfront, is one of the best museums in the Pacific. The Cuba Street precinct has the city's best cafes, bars, and independent shops. The cable car from Lambton Quay to Kelburn offers a quick escape to the Botanic Garden and views back over the harbour. Oriental Bay is the city beach — fifteen minutes' walk from the centre, with a good promenade and, on warm summer days, actual swimming.

Wellington is the seat of government and also the creative hub of New Zealand's film and arts industries. Peter Jackson's Weta Workshop is based here, and the city has a vibrant theatre scene and a serious coffee culture. Food is taken seriously too: the farmers' market in Newtown on Sunday mornings draws most of the city. The ferry across Cook Strait to Picton and the South Island is one of the world's great short sea voyages — dramatic enough to justify arriving by boat on purpose.
