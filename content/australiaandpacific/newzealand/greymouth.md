Greymouth is the largest town on New Zealand's [West Coast](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/south_island/west_coast) — about 13,000 people strung along the mouth of the Grey River, at the foot of the Paparoa Range. It is a working town, built on coal and gold and now leaning on tourism, with the wide grey Tasman Sea on one side and a wall of bush-covered hills on the other. The weather is famously wet; "the Coast" has its own rainfall figures that bear no relation to the rest of the country.

The town's main role for travellers is as a transport hub. The [TranzAlpine](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/greymouth/tranzalpine_railway) — widely regarded as one of the great train journeys of the world — terminates here after a four-hour run across the Southern Alps from Christchurch. The West Coast Wilderness Trail, a 132-kilometre cycle route, starts at the railway station and runs south through bush, wetland, and old gold-mining country to Ross. Greymouth is also the northern gateway to [Hokitika](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/south_island/west_coast/hokitika) and the [glacier country](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/south_island/west_coast/franzjosef) further south.

Spend an afternoon walking the [floodwall](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/greymouth/floodwall_walk) along the riverfront — built after the 1988 flood that put the central business district underwater — and the small but well-curated [History House Museum](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/greymouth/history_house_museum) on Gresson Street. Mawhera Quay, the original wharf street, has a couple of decent cafes; Monteith's [Brewery Tour](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/greymouth/monteiths_brewery) is the standard wet-weather indoor activity. The town does not detain most visitors for long, but it is the natural overnight stop between Christchurch and the glaciers.
