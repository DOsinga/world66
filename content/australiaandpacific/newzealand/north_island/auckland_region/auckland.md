Auckland sprawls across a narrow isthmus between two harbours, built on and around some fifty volcanic cones. It is New Zealand's largest city by far, home to roughly a third of the country's population, and the place where most international visitors first set foot in the country. The Sky Tower pierces the skyline from almost anywhere in the city, a useful compass point and the most dramatic viewpoint in the southern hemisphere.

The city's character comes from the water. The Waitemata Harbour opens north to the Hauraki Gulf, dotted with islands and ringed by beaches. Auckland calls itself the City of Sails, and on any given weekend the harbour is thick with yachts. The waterfront Viaduct Basin, once a working wharf district, now holds restaurants and bars with views across to Devonport and Rangitoto Island. The Wynyard Quarter, further west along the waterfront, has been redeveloped into a lively precinct of seafood restaurants, craft breweries, and weekend markets.

Away from the water, the neighbourhoods have distinct personalities. Ponsonby Road is lined with boutiques, cafes, and preserved Victorian villas — the kind of street where brunch is taken very seriously. Karangahape Road (K'Road) is grittier and more interesting, with independent bars, vintage shops, and some of the best live music in the country. Parnell, heading east from the CBD, is leafier and more refined, with heritage buildings housing galleries and good restaurants along its ridge.

The volcanic cones are the city's secret weapon. Mount Eden and One Tree Hill rise above the suburban streets and give panoramic views across both harbours that no skyscraper can match. You can walk up either in twenty minutes. The Auckland Domain, a sprawling park at the base of a dormant volcanic field, holds the Auckland Museum — a war memorial building whose Maori and Pacific collections are among the finest in the world. For something wilder, take the ferry to Rangitoto Island, a young volcanic island covered in pohutukawa forest, and hike to the summit crater.
