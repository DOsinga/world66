**The Bone People** by Keri Hulme (1984) — Set on the South Island's West Coast but essential reading for understanding New Zealand: its Maori-Pakeha tensions, its coastal isolation, its violence and tenderness. It won the Booker Prize and remains the most internationally recognised New Zealand novel.

**Mister Pip** by Lloyd Jones (2006) — A Bougainville Islands story by a New Zealand writer, but it captures something about the Pacific world that Auckland is part of. The novel was shortlisted for the Booker and is accessible and moving.

**The Penguin History of New Zealand** by Michael King (2003) — Not a novel, but if you want to understand what you're looking at — the volcanic landscape, the Maori place names, the colonial architecture — this is the clearest single account of how New Zealand became what it is. King was one of New Zealand's finest historians before his death in 2004.

**Once Were Warriors** by Alan Duff (1990) — A brutal and unflinching novel set in South Auckland's state housing estates. The film adaptation (1994) brought international attention; the book is harder but more complete. It is uncomfortable reading, which is rather the point.
