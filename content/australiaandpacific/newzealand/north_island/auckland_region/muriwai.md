Muriwai is Auckland's wilder west-coast beach: black iron sand, hard Tasman surf, wind-bent grass, and cliffs full of nesting gannets. It is close enough to [Auckland](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/north_island/auckland_region/auckland) for a half-day escape, but it feels much less tame than the city beaches on the harbour.

The main beach runs north for kilometres, with surf patrols in season and plenty of room once you walk away from the car park. Swimming here is for conditions when lifeguards are on duty; the rips are serious, and the sea deserves respect. For many visitors the best plan is to watch the surfers, walk the sand at low tide, and save the close-up nature moment for the cliffs.

At the southern end, [Ōtakamiro Point](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/north_island/auckland_region/muriwai/otakamiro_point_gannet_colony) is the reason Muriwai is more than just another Auckland beach. Gannets nest on the point and nearby islands during the breeding season, close enough that you can see courtship, chicks, and the awkward precision of birds landing in a crowded colony.

South of the point, [Maukatia Bay](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/north_island/auckland_region/muriwai/maukatia_bay) gives Muriwai a second mood: smaller, rockier, and geologically odd, with pillow lava cliffs now recognised for their global geological value. Check local access before setting out, especially after storms, but if the tracks are open this is one of the easiest wild-feeling day trips from Auckland.
