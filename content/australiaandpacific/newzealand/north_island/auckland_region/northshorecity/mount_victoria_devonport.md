story: "The summit of Takarunga was a significant pā (fortified village) for the local Māori people well before European settlement. When the British arrived in the 1840s, they renamed it Mount Victoria and added a flagstaff — a British flag on the highest point was a colonial statement made in harbours across the Pacific. The Māori name was formally restored in recent decades, and both names now appear on official maps."
---

The higher of Devonport's two volcanic cones, Takarunga rises steeply from the village to give 360-degree views that are genuinely extraordinary on a clear day. From the summit you can see the Auckland CBD skyline, Rangitoto Island, the Waitemata Harbour, the Hauraki Gulf, and out to the Coromandel Peninsula on a good day. The walk from the village takes about 15 minutes — steep in places but well-maintained.

The summit has some old military earthworks from the late nineteenth century when New Zealand was concerned about Russian naval incursions. There is no entry fee; the reserve is open at all times. Go in the morning for the best light on the city, or at dusk when the harbour turns gold.
