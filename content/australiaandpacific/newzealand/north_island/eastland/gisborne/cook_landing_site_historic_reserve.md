Cook Landing Site Historic Reserve marks a difficult and important moment in New Zealand history: the 1769 arrival of James Cook's expedition and its first encounters with local Maori. The site is not grand, but its historical weight is substantial.

Visit it as a place for context rather than spectacle. The reserve works best when paired with Gisborne's waterfront and local museums, because the story is about contact, misunderstanding, violence, and memory as much as navigation.
