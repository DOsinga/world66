Te Poho-o-Rawiri is one of the largest Maori meeting houses in New Zealand, built in 1925 and named after Rawiri Paratene, a paramount chief of the Ngati Porou people. It stands on the flat land near the estuary on the Maori side of the river, and remains an active centre of community life.

The interior is the reason to visit: carved pou (posts) depicting ancestral figures, elaborate tukutuku panels woven in traditional geometric patterns, and painted kowhaiwhai scroll designs on the rafters combine to make one of the most visually powerful wharenui interiors in the country. Guided visits can be arranged — ring ahead rather than simply turning up.
