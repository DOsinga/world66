Raumati Beach is a small seaside suburb on the Kapiti Coast, sitting between Paraparaumu to the north and Paekakariki to the south. It has a long [sandy beach](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/north_island/greater_wellington/kapiti_coast/raumati_beach/raumati_beach_itself) facing west across the Tasman Sea, with Kapiti Island sitting just offshore — close enough to look enormous at sunset.

The village centre is modest: a few cafes, a dairy, and not much else. That's mostly the point. People come here to slow down, walk the beach, and watch the light change over the island. The [Raumati Marine Gardens](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/north_island/greater_wellington/kapiti_coast/raumati_beach/raumati_marine_gardens) at the beach's northern end give easy access to the sand, and the [Wharemauku Stream Walk](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/north_island/greater_wellington/kapiti_coast/raumati_beach/wharemauku_stream_walk) follows the stream inland for a flat, easy cycle or stroll.

Raumati is effectively suburban — many residents commute to Wellington on the train, which stops nearby at Paraparaumu. For access to Kapiti Island itself, boats depart from Paraparaumu Beach; a permit is required and should be booked well ahead. The island is one of New Zealand's best predator-free wildlife reserves and the standout reason to make this stretch of coast a destination rather than just a stopover.
