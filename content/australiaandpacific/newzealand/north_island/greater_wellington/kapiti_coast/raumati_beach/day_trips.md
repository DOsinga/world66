The single most important day trip from Raumati Beach is Kapiti Island, visible from the shore and one of the best wildlife reserves in New Zealand. The Southward Car Museum near Paraparaumu is worth half a day if you have any interest in vintage vehicles.

## Kapiti Island

Kapiti Island is a predator-free nature reserve six kilometres offshore, managed by the Department of Conservation. It was cleared of all introduced predators — rats, stoats, possums — in the 1990s, and the results are remarkable. Birds that are nearly silent elsewhere in New Zealand are abundant and fearless here: kaka shriek overhead, weka walk up to your boots, and if you stay overnight you will almost certainly hear little spotted kiwi calling in the dark.

Day visits are strictly limited by permit. Book well in advance through the DOC website — places sell out months ahead in summer. Boat transfers depart from Paraparaumu Beach, about 3 km north of Raumati. The crossing takes around 15 minutes. Visitors must be off the island by 4pm unless they have an overnight permit and are staying at the DOC hut.

The walking tracks are well-marked and range from short beach strolls to a steep climb to the island's highest point at Tuteremoana (521m), with views back to the mainland and north toward the rest of the Kapiti Coast.

## Southward Car Museum

The [Southward Car Museum](https://southward.org.nz/) at Otaihanga, about 12 km north of Raumati, holds one of the largest private collections of vintage and classic vehicles in the Southern Hemisphere. Over 400 cars, motorcycles, and carriages are on display, ranging from a 1895 Benz to a collection of gangster-era American cars. The Bugatti and Rolls-Royce collections are particularly strong. It is an unexpectedly serious institution for the Kapiti Coast and well worth the entry fee.
