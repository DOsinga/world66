  The Dowse opened in 1971, named after Lower Hutt's long-serving mayor Walter
  Mervyn Dowse. It was one of the first public galleries in New Zealand to collect
  contemporary New Zealand craft and design as art in its own right — not as
  craft or applied art, but as a serious collecting discipline. Today its collection
  of jewellery, ceramics, and decorative objects is among the strongest of its
  kind in the country.

The Dowse Art Museum is Lower Hutt's most significant cultural institution — a modern gallery with a strong collection of New Zealand contemporary art, craft, and design. The building itself is compact and well-organised, with galleries rotating through the permanent collection and a programme of temporary exhibitions that tends to be more experimental than the city-centre galleries in Wellington.

The Dowse is particularly known for its craft and design collection: jewellery, ceramics, glass, and textile work by New Zealand makers from the 1970s to the present. Entry to the permanent collection is free. The museum has a café and a well-stocked gallery shop. It is in the civic centre of Lower Hutt, a five-minute walk from the train station.
