  When the 1931 earthquake struck, Napier burned for two days — the fire broke out from ruptured gas mains and the fire brigade was overwhelmed. By the time it was out, most of the brick buildings in the central city had collapsed or burned. What makes Napier unique is not the earthquake but what came after: a decision to rebuild immediately, in the style of the moment, with a coherence of vision that has never been replicated elsewhere at this scale.

Napier's central city is an almost intact collection of 1930s Art Deco buildings, built after the 1931 earthquake flattened the old town. The main streets — Emerson Street, Dalton Street, Tennyson Street — are lined with buildings in Spanish Mission and Art Deco styles: clean geometric lines, sunburst motifs, decorative friezes, and distinctive pastel facades. No other city in the world has such a concentrated and coherent collection.

The Art Deco Centre on Tennyson Street provides self-guided walk maps and runs guided walking tours that add context about the earthquake and the rebuilding effort. The self-guided walk takes about 90 minutes and is well marked. The guided tours are worth the extra cost if you want the stories behind individual buildings.
