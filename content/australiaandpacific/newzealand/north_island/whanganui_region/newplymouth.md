New Plymouth sits on the west coast of New Zealand's North Island, in the shadow of [Mount Taranaki](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/north_island/whanganui_region/newplymouth/mount_taranaki) — a symmetrical volcanic cone that dominates the skyline from almost everywhere in the city. The mountain is the main reason to come: its national park offers everything from short bush walks to a serious multi-day circuit, the Pouakai Crossing, and in winter there is skiing on its slopes.

The city itself is small but has more going on than you might expect. The [Govett-Brewster Art Gallery and Len Lye Centre](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/north_island/whanganui_region/newplymouth/govett_brewster_art_gallery) house one of the country's best contemporary art collections. The [Coastal Walkway](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/north_island/whanganui_region/newplymouth/coastal_walkway) runs for thirteen kilometres along the waterfront, past Len Lye's striking [Wind Wand](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/north_island/whanganui_region/newplymouth/wind_wand) sculpture and out to the harbour. [Pukekura Park](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/north_island/whanganui_region/newplymouth/pukekura_park) is a lush botanical garden in the centre of town with a cricket ground, lakes, and a fernery. [Puke Ariki](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/north_island/whanganui_region/newplymouth/puke_ariki), the combined museum and library on the waterfront hill, has a strong Māori and natural history collection.

New Plymouth has a strong café culture and a growing food scene, fuelled by the surrounding dairy country. The surf beaches along the coast are good, if often windswept. It is a quieter, less-visited part of New Zealand that rewards a few days of exploration.
