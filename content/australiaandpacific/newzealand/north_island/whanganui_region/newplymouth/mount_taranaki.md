Mount Taranaki is an almost perfect volcanic cone that dominates the skyline of New Plymouth and the entire Taranaki region. At 2518 metres it is the second-highest peak in the North Island, and its symmetry is striking enough that it has been used as a film stand-in for Mount Fuji. The summit is a serious alpine undertaking — a full-day climb requiring experience and good weather — but the national park offers a wide range of walks for all abilities.

The Pouakai Crossing is the best day walk: a 19-kilometre route that passes the Pouakai Tarns, two reflective alpine pools that, on a still day, perfectly mirror the cone above them. It requires transport at both ends or a return trip. The Around the Mountain Circuit is a multi-day walk that circles the entire mountain. In winter, skiing is available at Manganui ski field on the mountain's eastern slope. The park visitor centre at North Egmont is the starting point for most routes from the New Plymouth side.
