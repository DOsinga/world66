story: >
  The Durie Hill elevator was built in 1919 by tunnelling 213 metres through the hill — so that residents of the new suburb on top could get to work in the city centre without climbing the steep face. At its peak it carried over 200,000 passengers per year. The tunnel is lit by a single line of bulbs and takes about 90 seconds to traverse; it remains the only public elevator tunnel in New Zealand.
---

The Durie Hill Elevator is a short tunnel and lift system that connects the city centre to the hilltop suburb above, bored through the volcanic rock of the hill in 1919. You walk through a dimly lit tunnel, step into a small cage lift, and emerge on the hilltop 65 metres above the city.

From the top, the Memorial Tower (a concrete war memorial at the summit) adds another 34 metres of views over the city, the river mouth, and on a clear day, Mount Taranaki and Mount Ruapehu. The elevator runs during business hours for a small fee. It is charming, slightly creaky, and completely worth doing.
