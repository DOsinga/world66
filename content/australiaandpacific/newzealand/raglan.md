Raglan sits on the west coast of New Zealand's North Island, about 45 km from Hamilton, where a small harbour town opens onto black-sand beaches and the Tasman Sea. Its Maori name is Whaingaroa, and the place still feels more like a coastal community than a resort: surf vans, artists' studios, fish and chips, and a lot of people who would rather be outside.

The surf is the reason Raglan is known far beyond Waikato. [Manu Bay](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/raglan/manu_bay), Whale Bay, and Indicators form a chain of left-hand point breaks along Whaanga Road, while [Ngarunui Beach](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/raglan/ngarunui_beach) is the easier place to swim or learn. On a good swell the coastline earns its reputation; on a flat day the black sand, the harbour, and Mount Karioi still make the trip worthwhile.

Raglan also has a gentle creative streak. The [Raglan and District Museum](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/raglan/raglan_and_district_museum) tells the local story, the Old School Arts Centre hosts markets and exhibitions, and the cafes around Bow Street fill up quickly after the beach. [Waireinga / Bridal Veil Falls](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/raglan/bridal_veil_falls) is the easy inland side trip: a short bush walk to a 55-metre drop into a green amphitheatre.
