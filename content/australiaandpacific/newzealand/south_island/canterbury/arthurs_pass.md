**Arthur's Pass National Park** is the most accessible alpine wilderness in Canterbury, straddling the Main Divide of the Southern Alps at an elevation of 920 metres. The pass itself connects Christchurch (about 155 km east) to Greymouth on the West Coast via State Highway 73, and the TranzAlpine scenic train makes the crossing daily. The national park covers 114,500 hectares of dramatic terrain: deep gorges, beech forest, sub-alpine scrub, and high rocky peaks, with rainfall that can change from sunshine to heavy snow within hours.

Walks range from short valley-floor routes (the 20-minute Devils Punchbowl waterfall track is popular with day-trippers) to serious multi-day alpine routes. The **Avalanche Peak** track, which climbs steeply from the village to a 1,833-metre summit, is one of the best short alpine climbs in the South Island: a full day return, with outstanding views on clear days across the Main Divide and towards the Canterbury Plains. The small village at the pass has a DOC visitor centre, a cafe, and basic accommodation. Weather changes fast and altitude brings genuine alpine hazard — check conditions with DOC before any summit attempt.
