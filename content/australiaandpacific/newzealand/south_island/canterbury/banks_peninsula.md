**Banks Peninsula** is a volcanic outlier attached to the Canterbury coast, rising sharply from the flat plains to hills of 900 metres. Two large harbours — Lyttelton Harbour (Christchurch's port) and Akaroa Harbour (the French-heritage town) — are the drowned craters of the two ancient volcanoes that built the peninsula. The hills between them are covered in a mixture of farmland and regenerating native bush, connected by a dramatic Summit Road that runs the length of the peninsula's spine with views in both directions.

The peninsula is home to Hector's dolphins, the world's smallest and rarest dolphin, which are found in good numbers in Akaroa Harbour and along the outer coast. Little blue penguins nest in several locations along the coast. The [Akaroa](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/south_island/canterbury/akaroa) township is the peninsula's main visitor destination, but the back roads through the hill farms and down to the numerous small bays give a much more private experience of the landscape. The Summit Road drive from Christchurch to Akaroa, with stops at viewpoints, takes most of a day and is one of the better day-trips in Canterbury.
