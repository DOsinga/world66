**Lake Tekapo** is a turquoise glacial lake in the Mackenzie Basin, 225 kilometres southwest of Christchurch via State Highway 8. The water's vivid colour comes from rock flour — particles of finely ground rock suspended in the meltwater from the Southern Alps glaciers. On clear days the lake mirrors the mountains behind it, and the small **Church of the Good Shepherd**, a stone church built in 1935 right on the lakeshore, is one of the most photographed buildings in New Zealand.

Beyond the lake itself, Tekapo is the jumping-off point for the Mackenzie Country — a high-country basin of sheep stations, lupins (which turn the lakeshores purple in November and December), and wide open spaces that feel quite different from the rest of New Zealand. In winter it becomes a ski destination, with the Round Hill Ski Area on the hills above. The hot pools at **Tekapo Springs** on the lake edge are excellent in cold weather. From Tekapo, the road south towards Mount Cook passes Lake Pukaki, with the ice-clad summit of Aoraki visible at the far end — the most dramatic mountain approach road in the country.
