**Mount John University Observatory** sits on a ridge above Lake Tekapo at 1,029 metres, operated by the University of Canterbury since 1965. It is one of New Zealand's main astronomical research facilities and the reason that the surrounding Aoraki Mackenzie International Dark Sky Reserve was established — the reserve exists partly to protect the observatory's observing conditions by limiting artificial light pollution across 4,300 square kilometres. On clear nights, which are frequent in the dry Mackenzie Basin, the Milky Way is visible with extraordinary clarity and the southern sky constellations are sharp to the naked eye.

The observatory offers guided stargazing tours through the **Earth and Sky** tourism operation, running nightly sessions with telescopes and laser guides to the southern constellations. Tours need booking in advance, particularly in winter when the nights are longest and most sought-after. The daytime view from the summit — lake and mountains in three directions — is also exceptional and the short walk to the top is worthwhile regardless of the night programme.
