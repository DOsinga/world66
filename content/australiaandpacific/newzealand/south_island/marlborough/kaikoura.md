Kaikoura sits on a narrow strip of coast where the Seaward Kaikoura Range drops almost straight into the Pacific. The town is small, but the setting is not: snowy mountains behind you, deep ocean in front, and marine life unusually close to shore.

The reason is Kaikoura Canyon, a deep submarine canyon just off the peninsula. It brings rich feeding grounds close enough that sperm whales, dusky dolphins, fur seals, and albatrosses can all be seen without mounting a major expedition. [Whale Watch Kaikoura](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/south_island/marlborough/kaikoura/whale_watch_kaikoura) is the headline trip, but the coast has plenty for travellers who prefer to keep their feet on land.

The [Kaikoura Peninsula Walkway](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/south_island/marlborough/kaikoura/kaikoura_peninsula_walkway) is the best free outing, with clifftop views, seabirds, old whaling sites, and the seal colony around [Point Kean](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/south_island/marlborough/kaikoura/point_kean_viewpoint). Inland, [Mt Fyffe](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/south_island/marlborough/kaikoura/mt_fyffe_track) turns the whole coast into a map.

Kaikoura also has a clear food identity. The name means "meal of crayfish", and roadside seafood shacks north of town still make that more than a slogan. It is a strong stop even if you only have one day between Christchurch, Picton, and Marlborough wine country.
