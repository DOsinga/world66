Arrowtown is the prettiest of the old Central Otago gold towns, tucked beside the Arrow River twenty minutes from [Queenstown](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/south_island/otago/queenstown). It began as a rough mining camp after gold was found here in 1862, then settled into a small stone-and-timber town that somehow escaped the worst of modern resort sprawl.

The main street, [Buckingham Street](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/south_island/otago/arrowtown/buckingham_street), is the reason most people come: low verandas, old cottages, cafes, galleries, and enough restored gold-rush architecture to make the place feel lived-in rather than themed. The best counterpoint is the [Arrowtown Chinese Settlement](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/south_island/otago/arrowtown/arrowtown_chinese_settlement), a modest cluster of huts that tells the harder story of the Chinese miners who worked the river after the first rush had moved on.

Arrowtown works well as a half-day trip from Queenstown, but it rewards a slower stop. Walk the [Arrow River Bridges Trail](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/south_island/otago/arrowtown/arrow_river_bridges_trail), climb [Tobin's Track](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/south_island/otago/arrowtown/tobins_track) for views over the Wakatipu Basin, or detour to [Lake Hayes](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/south_island/otago/arrowtown/lake_hayes) when the water is calm. In April the poplars and willows turn gold and the town briefly becomes one of the most photographed places in Otago.
