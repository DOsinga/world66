The Dunedin Museum of Natural Mystery is deliberately odd: part natural-history cabinet, part art project, part private collection. Instead of the polished scale of Otago Museum, it offers a close-up encounter with bones, shells, ethnographic objects, drawings, and the personality of the collector behind them.

That intimacy is the reason to go. It is best for travellers who like idiosyncratic museums and do not mind a small, appointment-like experience. If your Dunedin itinerary already includes the railway station, Olveston, and the university museums, this adds a stranger and more personal note.
