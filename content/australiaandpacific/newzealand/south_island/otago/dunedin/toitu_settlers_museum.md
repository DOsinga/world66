The Settlers Museum tells the story of Otago from its Maori roots through the gold rush that turned Dunedin into New Zealand's wealthiest city in the 1860s, and on through the Scottish Presbyterian settlement that gave the city its character. The displays are well-designed and genuinely informative — less dusty than most regional history museums.

The highlight is the restored transport collection in the front hall, which includes original coaches, early cars, and a horse tram. The gold rush galleries explain why Dunedin was briefly the largest and richest city in New Zealand, which surprises most visitors who assume Auckland or Wellington always held that role. Free entry, central location near the railway station.
