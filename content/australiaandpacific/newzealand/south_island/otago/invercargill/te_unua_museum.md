Te Unua Museum of Southland (previously the Southland Museum and Art Gallery) sits at the entrance to Queens Park inside a distinctive white pyramid that has become one of the city's landmarks. The museum covers the natural and human history of Southland — the geology, the Māori settlement, the Scottish and European colonisation, and the environment of Foveaux Strait and the sub-Antarctic islands.

The living tuatara vivarium is the most visited part of the museum. These ancient reptiles — unchanged for 220 million years — move slowly through the enclosure, basking under heat lamps, occasionally opening one eye. They can live for over a century, and some of the individuals on display are elderly by any measure. The art gallery component has a solid collection of New Zealand work, particularly from Southland artists.
