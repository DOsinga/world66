Invercargill punches above its weight for a city of its size. The museums are genuinely good — Te Unua Museum of Southland houses one of New Zealand's most significant tuatara populations, and the transport collections at Bill Richardson Transport World and E. Hayes & Sons are remarkable by any standard.

[Te Unua Museum of Southland](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/south_island/otago/invercargill/te_unua_museum) is the cultural anchor of the city, set in a striking pyramid structure in Queens Park. [Queens Park](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/south_island/otago/invercargill/queens_park) itself is 80 hectares of gardens, wildlife pens, and sports grounds — free to enter and worth an afternoon.

[Bill Richardson Transport World](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/south_island/otago/invercargill/bill_richardson_transport_world) claims to be the largest private automotive museum in the world, and it may be right. The adjacent [Classic Motorcycle Mecca](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/south_island/otago/invercargill/classic_motorcycle_mecca) adds the two-wheeled dimension. Both are connected, and a combined ticket covers the day.

[E. Hayes & Sons](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/south_island/otago/invercargill/e_hayes_and_sons) is the city's most beloved institution — a hardware store that expanded into a museum of Burt Munro memorabilia, including the World's Fastest Indian motorcycle. The [Invercargill Water Tower](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/south_island/otago/invercargill/invercargill_water_tower), built in 1889, is the most photographed building in the city.
