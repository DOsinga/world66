Gabriel's Gully, about 5 km south of Lawrence, is where Gabriel Read made the gold discovery in May 1861 that transformed Otago and, indirectly, all of New Zealand. The population explosion that followed — Lawrence reached 11,500 people within a year — funded the development of Dunedin and provided the capital base for much of Otago's subsequent infrastructure.

Today the gully is a quiet, scenic reserve with a walking track, information panels, and a few preserved workings from the gold-rush era. Gold panning is still possible here; equipment can be hired in Lawrence. The creek that Gabriel Read worked is still recognisable, though the scale of what once happened here — thousands of men with picks, shovels, and sluices — is hard to imagine in the present peaceful setting. The reserve is free to enter and open year-round.
