story: "Mount Aspiring — Tititea in Māori, meaning 'glistening peak' — was not climbed until 1909, by the surveyor Major Bernard Head, who reached the summit via the Northwest Ridge. At 3,033 metres it is New Zealand's highest peak outside the Aoraki/Mount Cook area. Its isolation and elegant pyramidal shape have led to comparisons with the Matterhorn, a comparison that irritates serious alpinists and delights everyone else."

Mount Aspiring National Park stretches from the outskirts of Wanaka to the West Coast, encompassing some of the most remote and spectacular mountain scenery in New Zealand. The park is accessible at its eastern end directly from Wanaka — the Matukituki Valley walk, starting 54 km from town, is one of the finest day walks in the South Island, leading through beech forest and open river flats to views of glaciers and the rocky pyramid of Mount Aspiring itself.

The Routeburn Track, one of New Zealand's Great Walks, starts at the park's northern boundary. Multi-day alpine routes, glacier approaches, and technical climbs are all available for those with the experience. The Department of Conservation visitor centre in Wanaka (in the town centre) has maps, track conditions, and hut booking services.
