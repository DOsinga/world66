  Captain Cook named it Doubtful Harbour in 1770 because he was uncertain whether the winds would allow him to sail out again once he had sailed in. He never entered it. It remained one of the last explored fiords in New Zealand well into the 20th century.

Doubtful Sound is three times longer and ten times less visited than Milford Sound, and many people who've seen both prefer it. The approach alone sets it apart: a cruise across Lake Manapouri, a bus over the Wilmot Pass, and then a boat into the fiord — a journey that takes most of a day. The extra effort filters out casual visitors, and the silence and wildlife reflect that.

Bottlenose dolphins inhabit the sound year-round, and New Zealand fur seals haul out on the rocks near the entrance. Fiordland crested penguins are visible in winter near the entrance to the fiord. Real Journeys operates the standard day trips from Te Anau (including the Manapouri crossing) and overnight cruises with accommodation on the boat. Overnight trips are expensive but extraordinary — the sound at night, away from all light pollution, is unforgettable.
