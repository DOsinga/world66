  Milford Sound is technically a fiord, not a sound — formed by glacial action, not river erosion. Rudyard Kipling called it the eighth wonder of the world. Locals often say it's best seen in the rain, when waterfalls appear on every cliff face and the mist swirls through the peaks.

Milford Sound is the single most dramatic landscape in New Zealand, and possibly the most famous. The fiord reaches 15 km inland, framed by sheer cliffs rising to 1,692 m at Mitre Peak — arguably New Zealand's most photographed mountain. Day cruises run year-round and take about 2 hours to reach the mouth of the fiord and return. Kayaking tours are also available for those who want to get close to the walls.

The drive from Te Anau is 120 km along State Highway 94 — itself one of New Zealand's great scenic routes, passing through the Homer Tunnel blasted through solid rock at 945 m. Allow 2 hours each way and more time in winter when snow can cause delays. Do not expect to fill up with petrol between Te Anau and Milford — there are no services on the road.
