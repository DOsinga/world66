Granity is a small settlement on the northern [West Coast](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/south_island/west_coast), squeezed between the Tasman Sea and the steep coal hills above State Highway 67. It is not a polished destination. That is exactly why it works: weathered timber buildings, mining relics, sea air, and the feeling that the coast still belongs to people who live there.

The town is best understood as a cluster of old coal communities: Granity itself, Ngakawau and Hector along the coast, and Millerton up on the hill. The [Northern Buller Museum](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/south_island/west_coast/granity/northern_buller_museum) gives the area some grounding, while [Millerton Mine Heritage Area](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/south_island/west_coast/granity/millerton_mine_heritage_area) shows why hauling coal down to the coast required serious engineering.

The real outdoor prize is [Charming Creek Walkway](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/south_island/west_coast/granity/charming_creek_walkway), a bush tramway route through gorge, tunnels, relics, and waterfalls. Granity is not a place to detour across the island for, but if you are north of Westport it gives the coast a rough, memorable edge.
