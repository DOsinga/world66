Hokitika is a small West Coast town that was once one of New Zealand's busiest ports during the 1860s gold rush. Today it is a quiet place known for its [pounamu (jade) workshops](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/south_island/west_coast/hokitika/pounamu_greenstone) — you can watch carvers at work and buy greenstone jewellery at better prices than in the tourist shops elsewhere.

The [driftwood-strewn beach](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/south_island/west_coast/hokitika/hokitika_beach) is dramatic, especially on a grey West Coast day, and [Hokitika Gorge](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/south_island/west_coast/hokitika/hokitika_gorge), a short drive inland, has striking turquoise water. The town also hosts the annual [Wildfoods Festival](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/south_island/west_coast/hokitika/wildfoods_festival), where adventurous eaters can try huhu grubs, whitebait patties, and other bush delicacies. It is a good stopping point on the drive between Greymouth and the glaciers further south.
