Reefton is a small inland town on the [West Coast](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/south_island/west_coast) of New Zealand's South Island, set in the Inangahua River valley between forested hills. It made its money from quartz-reef gold mining, and it still wears that history well: old commercial buildings, mining relics, a School of Mines, and a local habit of telling stories properly.

Its best nickname is the Town of Light. In 1888 Reefton became the first town in the Southern Hemisphere with a public electricity supply, powered by a small hydro scheme whose remains can still be reached on the [Bottled Lightning Powerhouse Walk](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/south_island/west_coast/reefton/bottled_lightning_powerhouse_walk). That single fact gives the town more character than many larger West Coast stops.

Reefton is worth a spare day if you like places where engineering, mining, and bush country overlap. Start with the heritage buildings, visit the [Reefton School of Mines](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/south_island/west_coast/reefton/reefton_school_of_mines), then head to [Blacks Point Museum](/australiaandpacific/newzealand/south_island/west_coast/reefton/blacks_point_museum) or into Victoria Forest Park. It is quieter than Greymouth and less dramatic than the glaciers, but it has a strong identity of its own.
