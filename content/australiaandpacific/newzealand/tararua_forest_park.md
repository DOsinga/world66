The Tararua Forest Park protects the bulk of the Tararua Ranges, the steep, bush-clad mountains that run down the spine of the lower North Island between the Kapiti Coast and the Wairarapa. Rising sharply east of Ōtaki, the park is reached most easily from the Ōtaki Forks area, about 18 kilometres up Ōtaki Forks Road from the town, where several walking tracks begin where the bush meets the river flats.

The Tararuas are known among New Zealand trampers as genuinely serious country. They are a compact but unpredictable range where weather can change violently, and several people have died over the decades from exposure. The track conditions and sudden climate shifts that make the Tararuas notorious are not separate from the experience but central to it: these are mountains that demand respect rather than simply reward presence. The full crossing of the ranges is a multi-day undertaking requiring proper preparation, navigation skills, and equipment, and conditions on the tops can turn alpine in any season.

For most visitors the appeal is gentler. The easier tracks around Ōtaki Forks pass through beech forest along the Ōtaki River — a genuine New Zealand bush experience without the commitment of a full alpine crossing. Day walks into the foothills are manageable and pleasant, with good birdlife and the clarity of the river making even the shorter tracks worthwhile. Swimming holes and picnic spots near the Forks make it a fine half-day outing from Ōtaki or the wider Kapiti Coast.

If you plan to go beyond the foothills, check the forecast carefully, carry warm layers and wet-weather gear, and sign in with your intentions. The park is also accessible from the eastern side near Masterton and from Levin to the north, so the range threads together a good slice of the lower North Island for anyone serious about tramping.
