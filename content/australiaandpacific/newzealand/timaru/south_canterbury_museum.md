The South Canterbury Museum is a well-organised regional museum covering the natural and human history of the area, but most visitors come for one exhibit: Richard Pearse. Pearse was a farmer and self-taught engineer from Waitohi near Temuka, about 25 kilometres north of Timaru. Working largely alone, without knowledge of the Wright Brothers' research, he built a powered monoplane and — according to credible witness accounts — flew it in the spring of 1903, months before the famous flight at Kitty Hawk.

The evidence is genuinely compelling: multiple eyewitness accounts, his own surviving letters, and physical remnants of his aircraft. The date of 31 March 1903 is well-supported, though Pearse himself was characteristically modest about it and never sought recognition. His aircraft design was in some respects more advanced than the Wrights' — it used a tractor propeller configuration, which became the standard in aviation, while the Wright Flyer used a pusher design. The museum displays a replica of the aircraft alongside the documentary evidence. It's one of those stories that, once you know it, you wonder why it isn't more widely told. Admission is free.
