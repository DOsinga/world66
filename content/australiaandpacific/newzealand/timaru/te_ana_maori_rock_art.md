Te Ana Māori Rock Art is the place in Timaru to understand the charcoal and ochre drawings scattered through the limestone shelters of South Canterbury. The centre is run with Ngāi Tahu involvement, and that matters: the art is presented as living cultural heritage, not as isolated archaeological curiosities behind glass.

The town-centre exhibition is interactive and easy to fit into a Timaru visit, while guided tours can take you out to protected rock-art sites in the surrounding landscape. Go if you are interested in early travel, hunting, and storytelling in Te Waipounamu, the South Island. It adds a much older layer to a town that visitors too often treat as just a stop on State Highway 1.
