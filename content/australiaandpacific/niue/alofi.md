Alofi is Niue's capital and only real town, with a population of under 1,000. It sits on the west coast and looks out over a wide bay. The cliffs here are lower than on much of the island, making access to the water easier, though there is no beach — just a rocky shoreline and a concrete pier.

The town is small enough to walk around in half an hour. There are a few shops, a bank, the government buildings, the tourism office, and most of the island's restaurants and cafes. The Fale Fono (parliament) has a display of traditional tapa cloth worth a quick look, and the [Tāoga Niue Museum](/australiaandpacific/niue/alofi/taoga_niue_museum) in the centre has one of the Pacific's best collections of Niuean cultural artefacts.

The Niue Tourism Visitor Information Centre is useful for maps, activity booking, and advice. The market next door runs Tuesday, Wednesday, and Friday mornings — the place to buy local produce and, if you're lucky, coconut crab.

Outside Alofi, Niue's north coast is home to some of the Pacific's most striking natural sites. The [Matapa Chasm](/australiaandpacific/niue/alofi/matapa_chasm) and [Talava Arches](/australiaandpacific/niue/alofi/talava_arches) are carved into the island's coral limestone cliffs; the [Limu Pools](/australiaandpacific/niue/alofi/limu_pools) offer world-class snorkelling with resident sea snakes, and the sacred [Avaiki Cave](/australiaandpacific/niue/alofi/avaiki_cave) connects the island to the deepest stratum of Polynesian mythology. Alofi is where most practical life on Niue happens — you will pass through repeatedly even if you are staying elsewhere.
