The Northern Mariana Islands are a chain of fifteen islands in the western Pacific, a US commonwealth that stretches 750 kilometres from just north of Guam to the vicinity of Japan. The three inhabited islands — Saipan, Tinian, and Rota — each have a distinct character, though all share the warm water, coral reefs, and heavy World War II history that define the archipelago.

[Saipan](/australiaandpacific/northernmarianaislands/saipan) is the capital and the hub, with the main airport and most of the infrastructure. The Battle of Saipan in 1944 was one of the bloodiest Pacific engagements; the cliffs at the north of the island, now called Suicide Cliff and Banzai Cliff, are sobering memorials. [Tinian](/australiaandpacific/northernmarianaislands/tinian) is smaller and quieter, but historically just as significant: the bomb pits from which the Enola Gay took off in August 1945 are preserved as a National Historic Landmark. Rota, the southernmost inhabited island, is the least developed and the most serene, with excellent diving and a slow pace of life that the other islands have largely lost.

Getting between the islands means short flights or, occasionally, ferry. A car is essential on each island — public transport is minimal — but roads are manageable and distances short. The Chamorro people are the indigenous inhabitants; their culture, language, and food survive alongside a significant Filipino and Korean diaspora that has shaped the islands' character over the past decades.
