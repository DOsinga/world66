The Northern Mariana Islands Museum of History and Culture is in central Garapan and serves as the main repository for the islands' cultural and historical record. The permanent collection covers the indigenous Chamorro people and their settlement of the Marianas, the Carolinian community that migrated to Saipan from the Caroline Islands in the early 19th century, the succession of Spanish, German, and Japanese colonial administrations, and the 1944 battle. Artifacts include traditional navigation tools, household items from the Japanese colonial period, recovered military equipment, and oral history recordings.

The museum is a useful grounding in local history before heading to the battlefield sites — understanding who actually lived on the island before and during the war changes how the memorial sites read. The Chamorro and Carolinian cultural sections are genuinely informative and get less attention than the WWII material, but are worth the time. The museum is small enough to cover in under two hours. Entry fees are modest by American standards.
