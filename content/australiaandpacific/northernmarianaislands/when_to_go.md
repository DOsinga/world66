---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

The Northern Mariana Islands — Saipan, Tinian, Rota — sit just north of Guam in the western Pacific. The climate is tropical and humid, with temperatures consistently between 24°C and 31°C year-round.

**Dry season (December–June)** is the best time to visit. Lower humidity, calmer seas, reliably sunny days, and excellent dive visibility on the wreck dives off Saipan. This is also the busiest tourist window, especially around the Christmas/New Year and Easter holidays.

**Wet season (July–November)** brings shorter days of heavy rain and increasing humidity. Dive operators usually continue running, but the underwater visibility drops.

**Typhoon season (August–December)** is the more serious planning concern. The islands are squarely in the western Pacific typhoon belt, and 2018's Super Typhoon Yutu badly damaged Saipan and Tinian. Most storms pass without hitting, but the risk is real.

**Liberation Day** on 4 July is the biggest annual event on Saipan, commemorating the end of the WWII occupation. The **Flame Tree Arts Festival** in late April/early May fills American Memorial Park with music and crafts.

Visitors with at least minimum dive certification should plan around the **Banzai Cliff, Grotto, and Lau Lau Beach** dives off Saipan.
