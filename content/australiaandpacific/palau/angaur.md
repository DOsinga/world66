Angaur is the southernmost inhabited island of Palau, a small flat island of about 8 square kilometres sitting apart from the main Palauan island chain. It is one of Palau's least-visited destinations, with a small resident population and no significant tourist infrastructure.

The island has historical significance from World War II — US forces landed here in September 1944 in a battle that lasted several weeks. Some [remnants of the fighting](/australiaandpacific/palau/angaur/wwii_battlefield) are still visible in the jungle. Angaur is also known for its small population of [feral macaque monkeys](/australiaandpacific/palau/angaur/macaque_monkeys), a legacy of Japanese occupation when the monkeys were brought to the island. The reefs around Angaur offer [good diving](/australiaandpacific/palau/angaur/diving_angaur) with strong currents and abundant pelagic fish. Access is by light aircraft or boat from Koror.
