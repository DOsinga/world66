Babeldaob is the largest island in [Palau](/australiaandpacific/palau) but far less developed than neighbouring [Koror](/australiaandpacific/palau/koror), connected to it by a bridge. Most of the island is covered in rainforest, with scattered villages along the coast and a new capital complex at Melekeok on the east side.

The main attractions are spread around the island and a car is essential. In Melekeok you will find ancient carved stone heads, a beautifully maintained [bai](/australiaandpacific/palau/babeldoab/melekeok_bai) (a thatched-roof traditional meeting house for chiefs and elders), and the hilltop [Capitol of Palau](/australiaandpacific/palau/babeldoab/capitol_of_palau) at [Ngerulmud](/australiaandpacific/palau/babeldoab/ngerulmud). [Lake Ngardok](/australiaandpacific/palau/babeldoab/lake_ngardok), Micronesia's largest freshwater lake, lies just inland. The best beaches stretch northward from Melekeok to the northern tip. In the north, [Taki Falls](/australiaandpacific/palau/babeldoab/taki_falls) drops 25 metres into a jungle pool and the stone monoliths of [Badrulchau](/australiaandpacific/palau/babeldoab/badrulchau) — 37 enormous basalt columns arranged in rows on a hillside overlooking the coast — are one of the Pacific's more mysterious archaeological sites. On the western side, [Mount Ngerchelchuus](/australiaandpacific/palau/babeldoab/mount_ngerchelchuus) is Palau's highest point and the climb is short but real.

Japanese wartime ruins, interesting caves, and remote villages round out the picture. Babeldaob rewards a day or two of exploration if you can arrange transport.
