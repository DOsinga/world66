Jellyfish Lake, on the uninhabited island of Eil Malk in the southern Rock Islands, is one of those places that lives up to the hype. Snorkelling among millions of palm-sized golden jellyfish that drift around you without stinging is genuinely strange and beautiful — there is nothing quite like it elsewhere in the world.

Access is by boat from Koror as part of an organised Rock Islands tour, and the lake is closed periodically when populations dip. Scuba diving is forbidden — the deeper layers of the lake are anoxic and full of hydrogen sulphide that can be absorbed through the skin — so it is snorkel only. Bring a permit, included in the standard Rock Islands fee, and respect the no-sunscreen rule that helps protect the jellies.
