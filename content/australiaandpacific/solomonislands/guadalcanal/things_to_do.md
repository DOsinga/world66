title: "Things to Do"
type: section
---

Guadalcanal's attractions are mostly the legacy of the six-month campaign fought here in 1942–43. The island's northern coastal plain, sandwiched between the airport and the Honiara waterfront, is dense with battlefields, memorials, and rusting hardware — a sprawling open-air war museum if you have a guide who can point things out. Offshore, Iron Bottom Sound holds one of the world's great concentrations of wartime wrecks; reachable dives include destroyers, transport ships, and aircraft scattered across a stretch of seabed barely twenty kilometres long.

Beyond the battlefields the island is wild. Mountainous and thickly forested, the interior rises to Mount Popomanaseu and is crossed by only a handful of tracks. The Weather Coast on the south side is even more remote — long days of hard walking between villages where outboard engines and a Sunday hymn are the only signs that the twentieth century happened at all. None of this is set up for casual tourism. Expect to hire a guide through your guesthouse in Honiara.
