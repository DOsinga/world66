---
title: "Things to Do"
type: section
---

Malaita's appeal is cultural rather than resort-style. The island has no dive operators or organised tour infrastructure to speak of, but it offers something rarer: access to living traditions — artificial island villages, shell money production, and kastom communities — that are still genuinely intact. Most experiences require patience, goodwill, and a willingness to negotiate access respectfully through local guides or guesthouse owners in Auki.

The west coast and the lagoons off it are the most accessible area for visitors. Auki itself is worth a half-day of exploration before heading out to the lagoon settlements or further into the island's interior. Note that photography in some kastom villages requires permission, and some areas have restrictions on outsiders entering certain ceremonial spaces.
