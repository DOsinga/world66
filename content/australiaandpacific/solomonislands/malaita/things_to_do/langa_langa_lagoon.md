---
title: "Langa Langa Lagoon"
type: poi
---

West of Auki, the Langa Langa Lagoon holds one of the Pacific's most extraordinary human achievements: dozens of artificial islands built entirely by hand over many centuries. The Langa Langa people constructed these platforms by hauling coral rocks and shells into the shallows, layer by layer, until they stood above the waterline — large enough to support entire communities. The islands were originally built to escape the raids and disease of the mainland; today they remain inhabited, their residents still moving between island and reef by dugout canoe.

Approaching by canoe from Auki, the low shapes of the islands emerge gradually from the lagoon — clusters of thatched houses and coconut palms sitting improbably on the water. Some islands are barely larger than a basketball court; others support populations of several hundred. The contrast between the handmade land beneath your feet and the surrounding open water is disorienting in the best way.

Local guesthouses in Auki can arrange canoe transport and introductions to island communities. Visits work best when arranged through someone with existing relationships in the lagoon — turn-up tourism is not the norm here, and a degree of formality in making contact goes a long way.
