title: "Traditional Kastom Villages"
type: poi
---

Malaita's interior was the last part of the Solomon Islands to come under effective colonial administration, and the resistance was not merely passive. The Kwaio people of the mountains killed a British district officer and his party in 1927; the punitive expedition that followed — in which colonial forces killed dozens of villagers and looted sacred valuables — remains a source of deep historical grievance, and some Kwaio communities have refused Christian conversion or government interference to this day. The interior is not a tourist destination in any conventional sense, but it is one of the most culturally intact corners of Melanesia.

For visitors prepared to approach slowly and respectfully, some communities in the hills above the coast will accept guests, particularly those who come with an introduction from a trusted Malaitan intermediary. The divisions between "saltwater people" (the coastal and lagoon communities) and "bush people" (the interior) remain socially meaningful — they are not simply geographic descriptions but reflect distinct cultural traditions, exchange networks, and social identities.

Any visit to interior kastom villages should be arranged through local connections in Auki well in advance, with clear agreement about expectations and protocols before travelling. This is not a place where improvisation is appropriate.
