Nuku'alofa is the capital of Tonga and the only real town in the kingdom. It sits on the north shore of Tongatapu, the main island, and is small enough to walk across in half an hour — but it is the centre of everything: government, commerce, churches, and the waterfront.

The white Victorian [Royal Palace](/australiaandpacific/tonga/tongatapu/nukualofa/royal_palace), set in manicured grounds behind iron railings, is the most recognisable building in Tonga. You cannot go inside, but when the Royal Standard is flying the king is in residence. Nearby, the [Royal Tombs](/australiaandpacific/tonga/tongatapu/nukualofa/royaltombs) — burial site of Tongan royalty since 1893 — sit in a fenced enclosure near the [Basilica of St. Anthony of Padua](/australiaandpacific/tonga/tongatapu/nukualofa/basilica_st_anthony), whose tent-shaped concrete roof is the city's other landmark. On Sunday morning the king and queen often attend service at the [Centenary Chapel](/australiaandpacific/tonga/tongatapu/nukualofa/centenary_chapel), where the unaccompanied four-part Tongan harmony is some of the best free music in the Pacific.

The main street runs parallel to the waterfront and holds most of the shops, restaurants, and the lively vegetable market where you can find local handicrafts, bone carvings, and woven baskets alongside the produce. Nuku'alofa is not a sophisticated city, but it has a relaxed, friendly character that makes it easy to spend a day or two before heading out to the outer islands.
