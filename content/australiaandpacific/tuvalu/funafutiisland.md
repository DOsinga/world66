Funafuti is the capital atoll of [Tuvalu](/australiaandpacific/tuvalu) and the only place in the country with anything resembling infrastructure. It is a thin ribbon of coral and palm strung around a wide turquoise lagoon — roughly 25 kilometres long, with thirty-three named islets, of which [Fongafale](/australiaandpacific/tuvalu/funafutiisland/fongafale) is by far the largest and the only one with permanent settlement. Around 6,000 people live here, more than half of Tuvalu's total population, and almost everything that happens in the country — government, banking, the single high school, the one paved road — happens on this strip of land.

Fongafale is a thin sliver, in places only a couple of hundred metres wide between the lagoon and the open Pacific. The international airport runs down its spine, and outside flight hours the [airstrip](/australiaandpacific/tuvalu/funafutiisland/funafuti_international_airport) becomes the de facto town square: kids play football on it, families walk in the cool of the evening, and motorbikes race the length. When a plane is due, a siren sounds and people clear off. [Vaiaku](/australiaandpacific/tuvalu/funafutiisland/vaiaku), the administrative village, holds the government buildings, the [National Bank of Tuvalu](/australiaandpacific/tuvalu/funafutiisland/national_bank_of_tuvalu), the post office (a small but reliable source of Tuvaluan stamps), and the [Funafuti Lagoon Hotel](/australiaandpacific/tuvalu/funafutiisland/funafuti_lagoon_hotel) — the country's only proper hotel.

The lagoon itself is the main reason to come. The [Funafuti Conservation Area](/australiaandpacific/tuvalu/funafutiisland/funafuti_conservation_area) covers six uninhabited islets and a large chunk of reef on the western side, with excellent snorkelling, sea turtles, and water clear enough to see the bottom at depth. Boat trips out to islets like [Tepuka](/australiaandpacific/tuvalu/funafutiisland/tepuka) or [Fualopa](/australiaandpacific/tuvalu/funafutiisland/fualopa) can be arranged through guesthouses for negotiable rates. The Second World War left its mark here too — Funafuti was a major Allied staging base for the central Pacific campaign, and old aircraft parts, gun emplacements, and the remains of [Naval Base Funafuti](/australiaandpacific/tuvalu/funafutiisland/naval_base_funafuti) are scattered along the islets.

You will not find restaurants in the Western sense, or bars, or shops with much in them. What you will find is a small Polynesian community living at sea level, on a strip of land that may not exist in a hundred years, with a lagoon that is one of the loveliest in the Pacific. Stay a few nights, walk the runway at dusk, eat what the guesthouse cooks, and let the pace of the place slow you down — there is no other speed available here.
