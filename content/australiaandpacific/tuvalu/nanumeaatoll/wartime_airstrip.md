The Americans built a coral-surfaced airstrip on the northeastern side of Nanumea in late 1943 to support the Gilbert Islands campaign. It was the most northerly of the three forward strips the Marines laid down in Tuvalu, and for a few months it was one of the busiest places in the central Pacific.

The strip itself has not been used since 1945 and is mostly returning to bush, but the alignment is unmistakable and the coral surface remains firm in long stretches. Walk it from end to end with a local guide and you will find old fuel drums, anti-aircraft positions in the scrub, and the foundations of the camp buildings. It is a quiet, unmarked place — no signs, no fence, no museum.
