Vaitupu is the largest island of [Tuvalu](/australiaandpacific/tuvalu) by land area and one of the more densely populated. It is a classic reef island rather than a true atoll: a roughly oval lump of coral and palm forest about five and a half kilometres long, enclosing two small shallow lagoons in its interior. Around 1,600 people live here, almost all of them in the village of [Asau](/australiaandpacific/tuvalu/vaitupuisland/asau) on the western side.

Vaitupu's distinction within the country is educational rather than scenic. [Motufoua Secondary School](/australiaandpacific/tuvalu/vaitupuisland/motufoua_secondary_school), at the southern end of the island, has been Tuvalu's only senior secondary school for decades, and almost every Tuvaluan teacher, civil servant, and politician of the post-independence era passed through it. The school was the site of a tragedy in 2000 — a [dormitory fire](/australiaandpacific/tuvalu/vaitupuisland/motufoua_dormitory_fire) killed nineteen girls and one teacher, the worst single disaster in the country's recent history — and a memorial on the school grounds commemorates them.

The island has a more complicated colonial past than most of its neighbours. In 1947 a group of Vaitupuans, frustrated with land pressure at home, purchased the island of Kioa in northern Fiji from a planter and resettled around fifty families there. Kioa remains a Tuvaluan-speaking community in Fiji to this day, and the connection between the two islands runs deep — a quiet act of Polynesian self-determination decades before independence.

Outside the school and the village there is not a great deal to see in the touristic sense — a couple of small interior ponds, the [Catholic church](/australiaandpacific/tuvalu/vaitupuisland/asau_church) in Asau, a wide reef-flat on the lagoon side that is good for low-tide reef walking, and a great many coconut palms. There is no formal tourist accommodation. If you have made the trip out on the inter-island vessel from Funafuti you will have a host arranged through Tuvaluan contacts, and you will be welcomed accordingly.
