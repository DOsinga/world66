Vaitupu has two small, shallow brackish ponds in its interior, ringed by pulaka pits and pandanus. They are not impressive in any conventional sense — neither is more than a few hundred metres across — but they are the only freshwater-ish bodies of water in the country apart from the similar ponds on Nanumanga and Niutao.

The villagers fish a particular kind of small mullet from them, and the ponds are surrounded by the gardens that produce most of the island's pulaka, the giant swamp taro that is the traditional staple of Tuvaluan cooking. A walk to and around them is one of the more reliable ways to spend a quiet afternoon on Vaitupu.
