---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

Wake Island is an unincorporated US territory administered by the Air Force as a strategic airfield and emergency landing site. There is no civilian tourism — access is restricted to military personnel, contractors, and the occasional sanctioned researcher.

For the small number of authorised visitors, the climate is tropical maritime: temperatures consistently 25–30°C, humidity high year-round.

**Drier window (January–March)** is the most comfortable, with steady trade winds and lower rainfall.

**Wet season (July–November)** is humid with frequent showers, and the **typhoon season (August–October)** poses the main planning risk — Wake has been struck several times, most destructively by Super Typhoon Ioke in 2006.

WWII history is the island's defining significance — the December 1941 Japanese assault, the brutal occupation, and the post-war US recovery. A small memorial complex commemorates the defenders and the civilian construction workers who died in captivity. For practical travel, treat Wake as effectively inaccessible to the general public.
