Upolu is the busier and more visited of Samoa's two main islands. It holds the capital, Apia, the international airport, most government offices, and the easiest circuit of beaches, waterfalls, villages, and inland forest. That makes it the practical starting point for almost every trip to Samoa.

The north coast is the lived-in side of the island: Apia, markets, churches, traffic, and the slopes of [Mount Vaea](/australiaandpacific/westernsamoa/upolu/mount_vaea) rising just behind town. Inland, the road climbs quickly into damp volcanic hills where [Falemauga Caves](/australiaandpacific/westernsamoa/upolu/falemauga_caves), [Lake Lanoto'o](/australiaandpacific/westernsamoa/upolu/lake_lanotoo), and the high forest of [O Le Pupu-Pu'e National Park](/australiaandpacific/westernsamoa/upolu/o_le_pupu_pue_national_park) show the island's wilder side.

The south coast is the part that most visitors remember: surf breaking on the reef, steep green cliffs, village fales facing the lagoon, and swimming holes such as [To Sua Ocean Trench](/australiaandpacific/westernsamoa/upolu/to_sua_ocean_trench). Upolu is small enough to circle in a long day by car, but it rewards slowing down. The best moments here are not always the big sights; sometimes they are a church choir drifting across a village green, a roadside umu, or the sudden drop from rainforest to blue Pacific.
