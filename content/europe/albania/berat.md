Berat is one of the most beautiful towns in Albania and, with [Gjirokastër](/europe/albania/gjirokaster), is a UNESCO World Heritage Site for its remarkably well-preserved Ottoman architecture. The nickname "town of a thousand windows" refers to the distinctive Ottoman-era houses of the [Mangalem](/europe/albania/berat/mangalem) quarter, stacked up the hillside in tight rows, each with rows of large windows looking out over the Osum river valley.

The town divides into three parts: Mangalem (the Muslim lower quarter), [Gorica](/europe/albania/berat/gorica) (the Christian quarter across the river, connected by the old [Gorica Bridge](/europe/albania/berat/gorica_bridge)), and Kala (the hilltop citadel). [Berat Castle](/europe/albania/berat/berat_castle) walls enclose an inhabited village — people have lived here continuously since antiquity — with Byzantine churches, old houses, and a small museum. The [Onufri Iconographic Museum](/europe/albania/berat/onufri_museum), in the Church of the Dormition of St Mary within the castle, houses an outstanding collection of 16th-century Albanian iconography. Onufri was one of the great Byzantine painters of his era, known for a distinctive vivid red pigment that has never faded.

The walk up through the old cobbled streets past the [Lead Mosque](/europe/albania/berat/lead_mosque) and [Holy Trinity Church](/europe/albania/berat/holy_trinity_church) to the castle is the main experience. Allow a full day.

Berat is a useful base for the surrounding region. The Monastery of [Ardenica](/europe/albania/ardenica) is around 30 km away, and the Greek-era ruins of [Apolonia](/europe/albania/apolonia) can be visited as a day trip.
