Butrint is Albania's most impressive archaeological site and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. It occupies a small forested peninsula between the Straits of Corfu and Lake Butrint, about 18 km south of [Saranda](/europe/albania/saranda).

The site has been inhabited since prehistoric times and has served, in succession, as an Illyrian settlement, a Greek colony, a Roman city, a Byzantine bishopric, and a Venetian outpost. Each era left something behind, and the density of ruins in a compact area is remarkable. A [Greek theatre](/europe/albania/butrint/greek_theatre), [Roman baths](/europe/albania/butrint/roman_forum), an [early Christian baptistery](/europe/albania/butrint/baptistery) with one of the most complete mosaic floors in the Balkans, Byzantine basilicas, and a [Venetian castle](/europe/albania/butrint/venetian_castle) are all within a short walk of each other.

The setting adds to the atmosphere: tall trees shade the paths, the lake is visible through the vegetation, and in the early morning or late afternoon the site feels genuinely remote and ancient. It is less crowded than comparable sites in Greece or Italy.

A ferry crossing connects the main ruins to the [Venetian triangular castle](/europe/albania/butrint/venetian_castle) on the opposite bank — worth doing. The [Lion Gate](/europe/albania/butrint/lion_gate) in the inner walls and the [site museum](/europe/albania/butrint/butrint_museum) in the Ottoman tower near the entrance are both easy to miss if you're moving quickly.

Butrint is easily visited as a day trip from Saranda. A taxi or bus gets you to the gate; most people spend two to three hours on site. The [national park](/europe/albania/butrint/butrint_national_park) surrounding the ruins protects wetlands of significant ecological value and rewards birdwatchers and anyone who prefers a quieter walk beyond the main site.
