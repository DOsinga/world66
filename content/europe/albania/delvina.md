Delvina is a small town in southern Albania, set on a hillside between [Saranda](/europe/albania/saranda) and [Gjirokastër](/europe/albania/gjirokaster). It is not a destination in its own right but a convenient stop on the road between the two major southern cities, with enough interesting buildings nearby to justify half a day.

The ruins of the ancient Greek city of [Phoenice (Phoinike)](/europe/albania/delvina/phoenice), capital of the Chaonians of Epirus, are six kilometres south on a defensible hill — one of the largest ancient sites in the country and far less visited than Butrint. Closer to town, the 17th-century [Xhemahallë Complex](/europe/albania/delvina/xhemahalle_complex) is the best preserved early-Ottoman ensemble in the area, and the [Gjin Aleksi Mosque](/europe/albania/delvina/gjin_aleksi_mosque) in nearby Rusan is famous for its acoustics. The medieval [St Nicholas Monastery at Mesopotam](/europe/albania/delvina/mesopotam_monastery), with its unique carved animal reliefs, is another short detour. A Greek minority community has traditionally lived in this area (the region was known as "Northern Epirus" in Greek nationalist terminology).

The old [castle above the town](/europe/albania/delvina/delvina_castle) is worth a brief look for the views.
