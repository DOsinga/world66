  King Zog I commissioned the villa in 1927 as the most prestigious architectural project of his short-lived monarchy, at a cost of 1.5 million gold francs. He used it as a summer residence from its completion in 1937 until 1939, when the Italian invasion forced him into exile. He never returned to Albania. The villa passed through Italian and German occupation, became a communist state guesthouse, was ransacked during the 1997 civil unrest, and is now — nearly a century after it was built — still seeking its future.

The Royal Villa of Durrës was built for King Zog I in the late 1920s and early 1930s in a style of Italian Rationalism with monumental elements — a central belvedere tower flanked by receding wings, an austere stone facade, and a pyramidal roofline visible from across the bay. The interiors were lavish: imported marble, gilt finishes, neoclassical detailing. The setting on the hill above the city gives sweeping views over Durrës and the Adriatic.

The villa is not fully open to the public, but the exterior and grounds can be seen and the building is clearly visible from the waterfront. Plans have been announced for restoration and eventual opening as a tourism and cultural facility, but these have been in various stages of discussion for years. Even in its current unrestored state, it is worth seeing for the dramatic architecture and the view.
