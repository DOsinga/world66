---
title: "Skenduli House"
type: poi
---

The Skenduli House is one of the finest kulla (tower houses) in Gjirokastër open to visitors, and arguably the best introduction to the domestic architecture that defines the city. Built in the 18th century, it is a classic example of the local style: a stone-walled lower floor used for storage, a vaulted middle floor for livestock and provisions during siege, and elaborate upper floors with carved wooden ceilings, built-in divans, and large windows designed to catch the mountain light. The Skenduli family still owns the property and typically conducts tours themselves, which gives the visit an unusual personal quality.

The interiors have been carefully maintained rather than museum-ified — the textiles, metalwork, and household objects are original family possessions rather than assembled collections. The carved wooden ceilings are exceptional examples of the Gjirokastër craft tradition that reached its peak in the 19th century. The top-floor reception room, designed for receiving male guests, offers a clear view of why the Ottoman-era merchant families of the city competed in architectural elaboration.

The house is on the steep slopes of the old town below the castle, reachable on foot from the bazaar via a cobbled lane. Small entrance fee; opening hours can vary, so check locally.
