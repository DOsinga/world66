Pogradec sits on the western shore of Lake Ohrid, one of the oldest and deepest lakes in Europe, shared between [Albania](/europe/albania) and [North Macedonia](/europe/macedonia). The town is backed by the lofty mountains of Kamja and Guri i Topit, giving it an almost alpine setting.

The [lakeside promenade and beach](/europe/albania/pogradec/lake_ohrid_beach) are the main draws — Pogradec has one of the best bathing beaches in Albania, and the water is remarkably clear. Above the town, the [ruins of Pogradec Castle](/europe/albania/pogradec/pogradec_castle) sit on the hilltop, offering views over the lake. The [Buchimas archaeological site](/europe/albania/pogradec/buchimas_museum) a few kilometres south holds Illyrian and prehistoric finds.

Pogradec is a common stop for travellers heading between [Korce](/europe/albania/korce) and [Ohrid](/europe/macedonia/ohrid) across the North Macedonian border. The koran trout — an endemic species found only in Lake Ohrid — is the local speciality and reason enough to linger over dinner by the water.
