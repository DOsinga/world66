Rozafa is the castle that defines Shkodër — a long, narrow citadel on a 130-metre limestone hill at the confluence of the Drin, Buna, and Kir rivers. Illyrian foundations from the 4th century BC, Venetian walls from the 14th, Ottoman gates from the 15th: the layered fabric of the place is a thousand years of Balkan history compressed into one site.

The walk up takes 20 minutes from the foot of the hill. Allow at least two hours at the top: there are three concentric wards, a small museum in the restored 19th-century barracks, the ruined Church of St Stephen at the highest point, and four separate viewpoints over the rivers, the lake, and the mountains beyond. Open daily; tickets are inexpensive. Bring water in summer — there is no shade.
