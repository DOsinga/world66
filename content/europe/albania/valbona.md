The Valbona Valley is Albania's most spectacular mountain landscape: a long, glacier-carved trough in the Albanian Alps (the Accursed Mountains) that cuts between walls of grey limestone several hundred metres high. The valley floor runs along the turquoise Valbona River, flanked by beech and pine forest, with permanent snow visible on the peaks above until well into summer. It is, by any measure, extraordinary — and until recently almost entirely unknown outside the Balkans.

The valley has become one of the great hiking destinations of the Balkans, largely because of the **Valbona to Theth trail** — a full-day crossing over the mountain pass between two remote valleys that became famous through travel writing in the 2000s. The trail crosses the [Valbona Pass](/europe/albania/valbona/valbona_pass) at around 1,800 metres, drops through forest to the traditional stone villages of Theth, and involves some of the finest mountain walking in Europe. It can be done in either direction but is typically walked Valbona to Theth (the approach from Fierza by ferry being dramatically scenic).

The village of Valbonë itself is a loosely scattered settlement of guesthouses at the valley entrance, where the river is widest and the views up to [Maja e Jezercës](/europe/albania/valbona/jezerce), the highest peak in the Dinaric Alps, are clear. The whole valley is protected as [Valbona Valley National Park](/europe/albania/valbona/valbona_valley_national_park). Infrastructure is basic — no ATMs, electricity from solar panels or generators in many guesthouses — but the hospitality of the local Gegë families who run the lodges is genuine and considerable.

Getting here requires a ferry from Fierza (reached by bus from Shkodër or Bajram Curri) across the Koman Lake reservoir, one of the most unexpectedly beautiful journeys in Europe. The ferry runs once daily; if you miss it, you wait.
