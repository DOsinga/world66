The Accursed Mountains — *Bjeshkët e Namuna* in Albanian, *Prokletije* in Serbian — are the highest and wildest part of the Dinaric Alps, straddling the borders of Albania, Kosovo, and Montenegro. From the valley floor at Valbonë, the range looms immediately to the west and north: a saw-toothed limestone skyline that changes character entirely depending on light and cloud.

The range is the setting for some of the most important fiction in Albanian literature — Ismail Kadare set much of his early work in these mountains — and for the highland culture of the Geg Albanians, including the *Kanun of Lekë Dukagjini*, the medieval customary law code that governed blood feuds and hospitality alike. The hospitality traditions in particular remain strong: a genuine welcome to strangers is still practised by most guesthouse-running families. Do not mistake friendliness for naivety — these are proud people with a long memory.
