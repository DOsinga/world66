**Ismail Kadare: *Broken April*** — Kadare's most celebrated novel, set in the mountains of northern Albania where the blood feud law (*kanun*) demands an endless cycle of killing. A traveller and his new wife arrive from Tirana and encounter a world utterly unlike the capital. The landscape is recognisably this one; the customs are still partly remembered.

**Edith Durham: *High Albania*** (1909) — Durham was a British traveller who made several journeys into the Albanian highlands at the turn of the 20th century, when almost no outsider had been here. Her account is vivid, sympathetic, and remarkably specific about the highlands and their customs. Still the best primary source for understanding the world these mountains shaped.

**Robert Carver: *The Accursed Mountains: Journeys in Albania*** — an account of travelling through Albania in the 1990s, shortly after the end of communism. Dark, funny, and unsparing about both the country and himself. The mountains section is essential reading.
