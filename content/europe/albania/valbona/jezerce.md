Maja e Jezercës (2,694 m) is the highest point of the Dinaric Alps and visible from much of the Valbona Valley on clear days — a massive limestone massif with pale grey cliffs dropping from its upper ridges. It sits at the junction of Albania, Kosovo, and Montenegro, making it politically as well as physically prominent.

The summit is a serious mountain objective: the standard routes involve long approaches over rough terrain, route-finding on bare karst, and some scrambling. Most climbers base in Valbonë and hire a local guide for the 2-day round trip. The views from the top encompass an enormous area of the Balkans on clear days. The peak is technically non-technical — no ropes or equipment required — but isolated enough that being caught out in bad weather is genuinely dangerous.
