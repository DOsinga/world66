Valbona Valley National Park was established in 1996 and covers about 8,000 hectares of the Albanian Alps. The park's core is the Valbona River valley, a glacially formed trough whose sheer limestone walls and beech forests give it the character of an Albanian Yosemite. Above the treeline, bare karst ridges and permanent snowfields stretch toward the highest peaks in the Dinaric chain.

Wildlife in the park includes brown bear, wolf, and lynx — all rarely seen but definitely present. The river has clean, fast-running water that supports brown trout. The combination of Mediterranean-influenced valley floor with alpine peaks creates an unusual variety of plant communities: more than 350 plant species have been recorded, including rare alpine flowers that bloom briefly in July and August. The park is managed on a minimal-infrastructure basis; marked trails are limited and some route-finding skill is needed for longer walks.
