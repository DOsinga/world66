Ordino is a small parish in the northern part of [Andorra](/europe/andorra), quieter and less developed than the commercial centres closer to the Spanish border. It sits in a valley of the Valira del Nord river at around 1,300 metres, backed by forested slopes that rise toward some of the highest peaks in the Pyrenees. The pace here is noticeably slower than in Andorra la Vella — there are no duty-free hypermarkets, just a handsome stone village, a scattering of traditional Andorran farmhouses, and a cluster of small museums that make it worth a half-day.

Ordino's museums are an unlikely collection. The Museu Iconogràfic presents Eastern Orthodox religious art — audiovisuals, historical documents, and a hundred-odd icons, the result of a private collection donated to the parish. Next door (or within a short walk) the Museu de la Miniatura houses microminiatures by Ukrainian artist Nikolai Siadristyi, claimed to be the world's finest practitioner of the art — tiny paintings visible only under magnification. The [Museu Postal](/europe/andorra/ordino/museupostal) traces Andorra's unusual postal history: the country runs parallel Spanish and French postal systems, which has made its philatelic output disproportionately varied for such a small place.

Beyond the museums, Ordino is a good base for walking in summer and cross-country skiing in winter. The Tristaina lakes, a short hike above the valley, are among the prettiest high-altitude lakes in Andorra. The village has a few cafes and restaurants serving Catalan-influenced mountain food, and it is close enough to the main Andorran road to be easily combined with a visit to the capital or the ski resort at Ordino Arcalís.
