The Church of the Holy Redeemer is one of Ani's most haunting ruins: a circular Armenian church split almost exactly in half, leaving its interior and outer shell exposed to the plateau. The broken form makes the building instantly recognisable among the medieval city's scattered monuments.

Its survival is part of the drama. Built in the 11th century and later damaged by collapse, weather, and neglect, the church now reads as both architecture and fragment. It is a key stop on any walk through Ani, especially for understanding the variety and ambition of the city's Armenian church building.
