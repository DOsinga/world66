Artashat is the administrative capital of Ararat province and a small market town of around 20,000 people on the main road south from Yerevan toward Iran. Founded by the Armenian king Artaxias I in the early second century BC on the advice (allegedly) of Hannibal Barca, the ancient city was for centuries the royal seat — but the modern town has very little to do with that history; the ruins of ancient Artashat are nearby but technically at the foot of Khor Virap monastery, several kilometres to the south.

The contemporary town is the usual Armenian provincial mix of Soviet apartment blocks, a market, a few cafes and a church. It is unlikely to detain you long, but it is a reasonable place to stop for lunch, buy fruit, or fix problems with the rental car en route between Yerevan, Khor Virap and the Iranian border.
