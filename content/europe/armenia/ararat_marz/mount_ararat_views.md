The 5,137-metre peak of Mount Ararat, although now in Turkey, is the defining landmark of southern Armenia and an essential symbol of national identity. The slopes of the volcano fill the western horizon across most of Ararat province, and on clear days the snow line is so close-looking that travellers are routinely shocked to learn they cannot reach it on foot.

The best viewpoints from Ararat province are the platform at Khor Virap monastery (5 km from the border), the upper road to the Khosrov reserve, and the small hill above the village of Lusarat. Early mornings in autumn give the cleanest views; the mountain often disappears into haze and cloud through the long summer. A pair of binoculars or a long lens picks out the lower summit (Little Ararat, 3,896 m) clearly to the south.
