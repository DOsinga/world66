The main reason to come to Garni is the [Temple of Garni](/europe/armenia/garni/garni_temple), the only standing Greco-Roman colonnaded building in Armenia. Built around 77 AD by King Tiridates I and dedicated to the sun god Mihr, it stands on the edge of a gorge with a drop of 300 metres to the Azat river below — a spectacular setting. Next to the temple are the remains of a Roman bath with a mosaic floor.

The [Garni Gorge](/europe/armenia/garni/garni_gorge) below is worth walking down to see the "Symphony of Stones" — a long wall of hexagonal basalt columns rising from the riverbank like the pipes of an enormous organ. A path leads steeply down from the temple site to the valley floor and the clear, cold Azat river. In the village itself, the small [Mashtots Hayrapet Church](/europe/armenia/garni/mashtots_hayrapet_church) is dedicated to the monk who created the Armenian alphabet. Across the gorge, visible from the temple, the ruined [Havuts Tar Monastery](/europe/armenia/garni/havuts_tar) is worth the 90-minute detour for those with time.

Garni is usually visited as a day trip from [Yerevan](/europe/armenia/yerevan), combined with the cave monastery of [Geghard](/europe/armenia/geghard) a few kilometres further up the valley.
