Haghpat (Հաղպատ, meaning "a huge wall") is a village in northern Armenia built around one of the country's finest medieval monasteries. It sits high above the [Debed canyon](/europe/armenia/haghpat/debed_canyon_viewpoint) about 10 km from [Alaverdi](/europe/armenia/alaverdi). To get there you will need your own transport, negotiate with a local to give you a lift, or take a long walk. Take the main road northwards (towards the Georgian border) and soon after you leave town, turn right onto a steep climbing road that takes you to Haghpat village.

The 10th-century [Haghpat Monastery](/europe/armenia/haghpat/haghpat_monastery) is beautiful and has fantastic views. One of the most striking Armenian khachkars sits in the gavit of the library.

In 1996 Haghpat and neighbouring [Sanahin](/europe/armenia/sanahin_1) were the first Armenian monuments inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage list. The ruined fortress of [Kayan Berd](/europe/armenia/haghpat/kayan_berd) a short walk west of the village is worth the scramble for the view alone.

Apart from a parking fee (about 100 AMD) there is no entrance fee — just buy some candles (40 AMD a piece) as is the custom at Armenian churches.
