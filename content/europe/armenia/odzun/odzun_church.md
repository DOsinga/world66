  The church was built as a tribute to Hovhannes Odznetsi — John of Odzun — who served as Supreme Katholikos of the Armenian Apostolic Church from 717 to 728. He was born in this village, rose to lead the Armenian church, and convened a council that decisively outlawed Chalcedonian doctrine, cementing Armenian Christianity's distinct identity for centuries. The basilica that bears his legacy was raised over the ruins of an earlier 6th-century church on the same spot.

The Odzun basilica is one of the finest examples of early medieval Armenian church architecture, and it sees far fewer visitors than the more famous monasteries at [Haghpat](/europe/armenia/haghpat_3) and [Sanahin](/europe/armenia/sanahin_1), just across the Debed gorge. Built around the 8th century on the foundations of a 6th-century church, it takes the form of a pink felsite basilica with three naves, the two side naves notably narrow. The façades are covered in intricate carvings depicting Christ, angels, and scenes from the New Testament.

The arcaded cloister running along the southern side is particularly graceful, and the western wall — a blind facade with a single arched entrance — gives the building an unusually austere dignity. Surrounding the basilica is a cemetery dense with khachkars and tombstones spanning from the 13th to the 20th centuries, many of the older ones beautifully carved. There is no entry fee: tradition holds that you buy and light a candle. If the church is locked, the caretaker is likely tending the grounds nearby.
