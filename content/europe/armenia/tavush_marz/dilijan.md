Dilijan sits in a forest bowl in the hills of Tavush Marz, a small town that has served as a retreat for artists, composers, and cinematographers since Soviet times. The Cinematographers House — a grand sanatorium-style building where the most celebrated directors of the Soviet era came to think and work — is still standing. The town itself has a restored nineteenth-century quarter of wooden houses on [Sharambeyan Street](/europe/armenia/tavush_marz/dilijan/old_quarter), where artisans sell handicrafts and musical instruments from their ground-floor workshops.

Armenians love Dilijan. It is their go-to weekend escape, prized for clean air, green hills, and a pace of life that is a world away from Yerevan. [Dilijan National Park](/europe/armenia/tavush_marz/dilijan/dilijan_national_park) wraps around the town with beech and oak forest that turns gold in autumn. In summer the guesthouses fill up with families; trails lead into the park from the edge of town.

The two monasteries near Dilijan are among the finest in Armenia. [Haghartsin](/europe/armenia/tavush_marz/dilijan/haghartsin_monastery), a few kilometres north along a beautiful forest road, is a complex of three churches with a 13th-century royal dining hall and extraordinary khachkars. [Goshavank](/europe/armenia/tavush_marz/dilijan/goshavank), a little further, was founded by the twelfth-century scholar Mkhitar Gosh and includes what many consider the finest khachkar in existence. Most visitors see both in a single half-day loop. [Parz Lake](/europe/armenia/tavush_marz/dilijan/parz_lake), a clear mountain lake in the national park, is a pleasant afternoon excursion.
