Armenia's third-largest city sits in a valley where three rivers meet, surrounded by the forested mountains of Lori province. Vanadzor itself is a quiet Soviet-era city without major sights of its own, but it is a terrific base for exploring one of Armenia's most beautiful regions — pristine forests, deep gorges, and long-forgotten Bronze Age, Byzantine, and medieval ruins scattered through the hills.

The train from [Gyumri](/europe/armenia/shirak_marz/gyumri) or from the Georgian border passes through some of the most dramatic terrain in the country. From Vanadzor you can reach the forest monasteries of the Debed Canyon — [Haghpat](/europe/armenia/haghpat), [Sanahin](/europe/armenia/sanahin_1), and [Odzun](/europe/armenia/odzun) — via [Alaverdi](/europe/armenia/alaverdi). Head east into Tavush province for the most pristine wilderness in northern Armenia, a region rarely visited by tourists, with good bases at Ijevan and [Dilijan](/europe/armenia/tavush_marz/dilijan). Or go north to Stepanavan, where the last wild population of Armenian water lilies survives in a small lake.

In the city itself, start with [Hayk Square](/europe/armenia/vanadzor/hayk_square) and the colonnaded [Hovhannes Tumanyan State Drama Theatre](/europe/armenia/vanadzor/hovhannes_tumanyan_drama_theatre), then walk over to the 19th-century [Church of the Holy Mother of God](/europe/armenia/vanadzor/church_of_the_holy_mother_of_god) and the small [Vanadzor Fine Arts Museum](/europe/armenia/vanadzor/vanadzor_fine_arts_museum). The hilltop [Battle of Karakilisa Monument](/europe/armenia/vanadzor/battle_of_karakilisa_monument) and the [Black River Gorge](/europe/armenia/vanadzor/black_river_gorge) east of town are both worth an hour or two on foot.
