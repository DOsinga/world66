Yeghegnadzor is the capital of [Vayots Dzor](/europe/armenia/vayots_dzor_marz), Armenia's least populated province, sitting in a sun-baked valley along the main highway that runs south from Yerevan toward the Iranian border. The town itself is modest — Soviet-era apartment blocks, a central park, a few shops and cafes — but its location makes it the natural base for exploring one of Armenia's most rewarding regions.

The real attractions are all within day-trip range. [Noravank monastery](/europe/armenia/vayots_dzor_marz/yeghegnadzor/noravank_monastery), about 25 minutes south, sits at the end of a dramatic red-rock gorge and is one of the most photogenic buildings in the country. The Areni wine region is just down the road, and the [Areni-1 cave complex](/europe/armenia/vayots_dzor_marz/yeghegnadzor/areni_cave) nearby yielded the world's oldest known leather shoe, dating back over 5,500 years, along with evidence of the earliest known winemaking — a discovery that gave Armenian winemakers considerable bragging rights.

Yeghegnadzor has a few simple restaurants serving khorovats and other Armenian staples, and the town makes a good overnight stop if you are driving between Yerevan and the south. The [Selim Caravanserai](/europe/armenia/vayots_dzor_marz/yeghegnadzor/selim_caravanserai), a beautifully preserved 14th-century Silk Road waystation on the mountain pass to the north, is another worthwhile stop. The [Yeghegnadzor Regional Museum](/europe/armenia/vayots_dzor_marz/yeghegnadzor/regional_museum) in town provides context on the archaeological finds of the region. The [Gladzor University site](/europe/armenia/vayots_dzor_marz/yeghegnadzor/gladzor_university_site), just north of town, marks where one of medieval Armenia's great centres of learning operated from 1280 to 1340.
