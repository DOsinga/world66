story: The University of Gladzor operated from around 1280 to 1340 in the village of Gladzor, just north of Yeghegnadzor, and was one of the two great centres of medieval Armenian learning. It produced manuscripts, trained clergy, and served as a centre for Armenian philosophy and theological debate at a time when the Armenian church was under sustained pressure from Mongol rule. The manuscripts produced here are among the finest examples of medieval Armenian illumination.
---

The site of the University of Gladzor is in the village of the same name, a short drive north of Yeghegnadzor through the hills. The university itself no longer stands, but the village retains the monastery where it was based, and the area gives a sense of the wooded, defensive terrain that sheltered this scholarly community during a turbulent period.

Gladzor's most lasting legacy is the manuscripts its scholars produced and the students it trained. Several of the leading figures in 14th-century Armenian intellectual life were educated here. The associated museum in the village displays reproductions of the manuscripts and explains the university's role in Armenian cultural survival during the Mongol period. The site is less visually dramatic than Noravank or the Selim Caravanserai, but for visitors interested in the depth of Armenian cultural history, it adds an important layer.
