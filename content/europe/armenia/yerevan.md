Yerevan is the capital of Armenia and one of the world's oldest continuously inhabited cities — older than Rome, founded in 782 BC as the Urartian fortress of [Erebuni](/europe/armenia/yerevan/erebuni_fortress). For much of its history it was a provincial outpost of larger empires (Persian, Russian, Soviet); its modern face as a capital was largely invented in the 1920s by the architect Alexander Tamanian, who replanned the city around the pink-tufa-faced [Republic Square](/europe/armenia/yerevan/republic_square), radiating boulevards, and the gigantic stairway of [the Cascade](/europe/armenia/yerevan/cascade).

The city is compact and walkable, with most of what visitors want to see concentrated in the [Kentron](/europe/armenia/yerevan/kentron) district. Cultural life is unusually rich for the city's size — the [Matenadaran](/europe/armenia/yerevan/matenadaran) holds one of the great manuscript collections of the world, the [Cafesjian Center](/europe/armenia/yerevan/cascade) turns the Cascade into a sculpture museum, and small house-museums dedicated to [Sergei Parajanov](/europe/armenia/yerevan/parajanov_museum), [Martiros Saryan](/europe/armenia/yerevan/saryanmuseum), and [Aram Khachaturian](/europe/armenia/yerevan/aram_khachaturian_house) preserve the studios of the country's artistic giants. The [Armenian Genocide Museum](/europe/armenia/yerevan/genocidemuseum) on the western ridge is the emotional counterweight to all of it.

Cafes and bars are at the centre of daily life. The Opera district fills with outdoor tables in summer; [Saryan Street](/europe/armenia/yerevan/martiros_saryan_park) has become Yerevan's wine bar district; and the third-wave coffee revolution has long since arrived. The food has improved sharply over the past decade as well, both at the high end ([Dolmama](/europe/armenia/yerevan/dolmama)) and in cheerful mid-range places like [Lavash](/europe/armenia/yerevan/lavash_restaurant) and [Tavern Yerevan](/europe/armenia/yerevan/tavern_yerevan).

With Yerevan as your base you can easily visit [Echmiadzin](/europe/armenia/echmiadzin), the Holy See of the Armenian Church; the Hellenistic temple of [Garni](/europe/armenia/garni); and the rock-cut monastery of [Geghard](/europe/armenia/geghard) — all comfortable day trips. On a clear morning, look up: Mount Ararat, on the Turkish side of a closed border, dominates the southern horizon, a reminder of the much larger Armenian world that this small republic carries on its shoulders.
