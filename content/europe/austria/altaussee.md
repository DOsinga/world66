Altaussee is a small lakeside village in the Salzkammergut, tucked into a high valley between steep wooded mountains in Styria. The [lake](/europe/austria/altaussee/lake_altaussee) — brilliant green-blue and surrounded by meadows — is one of the most beautiful in the region, and the village itself is quiet and uncommercialised compared to the more-visited spots nearby. Artists and writers have been coming here since the 19th century; the place has always had a contemplative quality that the bigger resort towns lack.

The village is also famous for a remarkable wartime story: the nearby [salt mines](/europe/austria/altaussee/altaussee_salt_mine), which had been used to store thousands of art treasures looted by the Nazis, were saved from destruction in the final days of the war by local miners who packed the entrances with explosives specifically designed to collapse without damaging the art inside. The salt mine can be visited, and the story it holds is one of the stranger chapters of the war.

Above the village rises the [Loser plateau](/europe/austria/altaussee/loser_plateau), a moonscape of bare limestone reached by a panoramic road or a summer chairlift — prime hiking country in summer, a quiet little ski area in winter.
