  The waterfall was not always this dramatic. In 1899, a catastrophic flood
  sent a wall of water through the valley, destroying much of the lower town
  and reshaping the river's course through the centre. The rebuilt town used
  the disaster as an opportunity: the rebuilt bridges and promenades were
  designed specifically to frame and celebrate the falls, turning what had
  been a hazard into the town's defining architectural feature.

The waterfall that defines Bad Gastein — an 85-metre cascade that plunges in three leaps through the very centre of town, framed on both sides by Belle Époque hotels. There are viewing platforms at several levels, and the footbridge across the top gives a dizzying close-up view. At night it is floodlit. Completely free, utterly unmissable.
