The Convent of St. Peter is a Capuchin convent that has stood on the southern edge of Bludenz since 1661. It is one of the oldest continuously inhabited religious buildings in Vorarlberg, and the small attached church is open to visitors during daylight hours. The exterior is modest — Capuchin architecture tends to avoid ornamentation — but the interior has a genuine quiet to it, and the altar paintings are worth pausing for.

The convent sits slightly apart from the main pedestrian circuit, which means most visitors walk past rather than in. That's a shame, because the surrounding garden and courtyard give a sense of how the town's religious and civic life were once intertwined. The building is still occupied by a small community, so the usual respectful visiting conventions apply.
