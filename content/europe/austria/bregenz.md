Bregenz is the capital of Vorarlberg, Austria's westernmost province, sitting on the eastern shore of Lake Constance (Bodensee) where the lake meets the alpine foothills. It is a compact, attractive town, and the lake gives it an almost coastal feel — unusual for landlocked Austria.

The town is best known for the [Bregenzer Festspiele](/europe/austria/bregenz/bregenzer_festspiele), held each summer on a spectacular floating stage on the lake. The operatic productions staged here use the lake's surface and the sky as a backdrop; the stage sets are engineered on a scale matched nowhere else. It is one of the most unusual performance venues in Europe.

Above the modern lakeside town, the [Oberstadt](/europe/austria/bregenz/oberstadt) has medieval fortifications, the [Church of St Martin](/europe/austria/bregenz/church_of_st_martin) with 14th-century frescoes, and good views over the lake. The [Martinsturm](/europe/austria/bregenz/martinsturm) — a medieval tower capped with an enormous baroque onion dome — is the most distinctive landmark. A cable car rises to the [Pfänder](/europe/austria/bregenz/pfander) mountain, 1,064 m, with a panorama across the lake to Germany and Switzerland and a wildlife park near the summit.

The [Kunsthaus Bregenz](/europe/austria/bregenz/kunsthaus_bregenz) and the [Vorarlberg Museum](/europe/austria/bregenz/vorarlberg_museum) sit side by side on the lakeside, forming a cultural cluster that would not embarrass a city five times the size. The [lake promenade](/europe/austria/bregenz/lake_constance_promenade) is the place to swim, cycle, or simply watch the Alps catch the evening light across the water.
