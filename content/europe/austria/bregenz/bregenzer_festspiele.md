The Bregenz Festival, held every July and August, is one of the most unusual performing arts events in Europe. Productions are staged on a vast floating platform anchored in Lake Constance, with the audience seated on the lakeside. The spectacle of singers and orchestra performing against a backdrop of water, mountains, and evening sky is genuinely extraordinary.

The festival was founded in 1946 and has grown into a major international event attracting around 200,000 visitors a season. Works by Verdi, Puccini, and Mozart have all been staged here, along with more adventurous choices. The outdoor stage productions run for two consecutive summers with the same set — which means the stage designers plan for immense scale and lasting visual impact. A companion programme of opera and concerts runs in the Festival Hall (Festspielhaus) alongside the lakeside productions.

Tickets sell out well in advance; book as early as possible if you want the main lakeside performance. The Festspielhaus sits right at the lake edge and is also worth seeing as a piece of contemporary architecture.
