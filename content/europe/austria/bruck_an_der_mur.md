Bruck an der Mur is a compact Styrian town where the Mur and Murz valleys meet. Many travellers only notice it as a rail junction between Vienna, Graz, and the alpine routes west, but the centre has enough medieval texture and river scenery to reward a slow stop.

The main square is the heart of the visit. [Hauptplatz and Kornmesserhaus](/europe/austria/bruck_an_der_mur/hauptplatz_and_kornmesserhaus) show Bruck's old trading-town confidence, with a broad square, late-Gothic house fronts, and the iron fountain at the centre. A short climb brings you to [Schlossberg](/europe/austria/bruck_an_der_mur/schlossberg), where the clock tower and Landskron ruins look over the meeting of valleys.

Bruck is not a substitute for Graz, and it does not pretend to be. It works best as a half-day town: arrive by train, walk the old centre, climb for the view, and, if you have more time, follow the paths into [Weitental](/europe/austria/bruck_an_der_mur/weitental) above the city.
