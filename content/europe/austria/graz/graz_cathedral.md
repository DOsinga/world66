  On the outer south wall of the cathedral hangs a large fresco from around 1485, showing the three plagues that afflicted Graz: the locust plague, the Turkish invasion, and the bubonic plague. It was painted as a votive offering, and it remains one of the most direct depictions of medieval catastrophe in Austria — three disasters laid out side by side in fading paint, still visible on the wall.

The Cathedral of Graz (Grazer Dom) was built in the 1460s under Emperor Friedrich III, who used it as his court church. The Gothic exterior is relatively plain; the interior is the reason to go in. The choir stalls are 15th-century Gothic, the reliquary cabinet in the sacristy still holds the personal relics Friedrich kept nearby, and the overall atmosphere is one of authentic medieval piety rather than tourist-polished grandeur.

Next to the cathedral stands the Mausoleum of Ferdinand II, and together they form a dense cluster of Habsburg religious architecture in the narrow streets east of the Hauptplatz. Worth 30 minutes of your time. Free entry.

Burgring/Kaiserstrasse area, directly south of the old town centre.
