*[Europe](/europe) > [Austria](/europe/austria) > Hochpustertal*

Hochpustertal is a high alpine valley in East Tirol, pressed against the Italian border and the jagged wall of the Sextner Dolomites to the south. The valley villages — [Sillian](/europe/austria/hochpustertal/sillian), Heinfels, Strassen, Abfaltersbach, Anras, Außervillgraten, Kartitsch, Ober- and Untertilliach — sit between 950 and 1,400 metres and run east to west along the upper Drava river. The Pustertaler Höhenstraße links them; it is one of the prettiest mountain roads in Austria.

In summer this is hiking and mountain biking country. The [Karnischer Höhenweg](/europe/austria/hochpustertal/karnischer_hohenweg) ridge trail traces the old Italian-Austrian front line and is one of the great long-distance paths in the Alps. Paragliders launch from the slopes above Sillian, and the [Drau Radweg](/europe/austria/hochpustertal/drau_radweg) cycle route runs the full length of the valley. In winter the [Hochpustertal ski area](/europe/austria/hochpustertal/hochpustertal_ski_area) above Sillian links to Italian pistes around Sexten, giving small-town atmosphere with international terrain.

The Hochpustertal feels quieter and more traditional than the resorts further north. Wooden farmhouses, hayfields, processions on saints' days, hand-made dumplings in the inns. The biggest landmark is [Heinfels Castle](/europe/austria/hochpustertal/heinfels_castle), recently restored and visible from much of the valley. The cross-border feeling is strong: most people speak both German and a smattering of Italian, and the Italian Pustertal is half an hour away on the train.
