Kitzbühel is one of the most famous names in skiing, thanks to the [Hahnenkamm](/europe/austria/kitzbuehel/hahnenkamm) downhill — the most feared race on the World Cup circuit, held every January on [the Streif course](/europe/austria/kitzbuehel/streif_course) so steep and icy that it has no real equivalent anywhere. During race week the medieval town fills with athletes, celebrities, and fans, and the atmosphere is electric.

The rest of the season, Kitzbühel is a pleasant if not spectacular ski resort. The town is charming — a compact medieval centre with painted facades, church towers, and a pedestrianised main street. The [Kitzbüheler Horn](/europe/austria/kitzbuehel/kitzbueheler_horn) on the northeast side of the valley offers alpine flowers and quieter skiing. [St Catherine's Church](/europe/austria/kitzbuehel/st_catherines_church) in the old town dates from 1360 and has the oldest frescoes in Tyrol. The [Museum Kitzbühel](/europe/austria/kitzbuehel/museum_kitzbuehel) is worth visiting for its large collection of works by Tyrolean painter Alfons Walde, who defined the visual identity of the Austrian ski resort between the wars.

Kitzbühel is not the exclusive resort its reputation suggests. Alongside the four-star hotels are plenty of affordable pensions and guesthouses, and the town attracts a mix of budgets. It is 80 kilometres from Innsbruck and 120 from Munich, well connected by train, and you do not need a car — free ski buses serve the resort. In summer, the [Schwarzsee](/europe/austria/kitzbuehel/schwarzsee) lake two kilometres west of town warms up for swimming, and the mountains are excellent for hiking and mountain biking.
