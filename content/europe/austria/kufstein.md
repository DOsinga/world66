Kufstein sits at the point where the Inn River cuts through a dramatic limestone gorge on the Austrian-German border, and the [medieval fortress](/europe/austria/kufstein/kufstein_fortress) perched on the rock above the old town is visible for kilometres in every direction. It is one of the best-preserved fortresses in Tyrol and the natural centrepiece of a town that otherwise feels pleasantly unhurried — good cafes, a handful of wine bars, and streets lined with painted facades in the style typical of this corner of Austria.

The town has long been a crossing point between Bavaria and Tyrol, and the fortress reflects that strategic past: its walls were contested by the Habsburgs and Bavarian dukes for centuries before becoming definitively Austrian in 1504. Today visitors can walk the ramparts and visit the impressive [Heroes' Organ](/europe/austria/kufstein/heroes_organ), a massive outdoor pipe organ installed in 1931 that plays a short daily recital audible across the valley. You reach the fortress by the [Festungsbahn funicular](/europe/austria/kufstein/festungsbahn) from the old town square.

Kufstein is an excellent base for the surrounding Kaiserwinkl region: the [Wilder Kaiser mountains](/europe/austria/kufstein/wilder_kaiser) rise dramatically to the south, offering hiking in summer and skiing in winter, and the [Inn valley promenade](/europe/austria/kufstein/inn_promenade) and cycling route pass through town. The [Kaiserbad](/europe/austria/kufstein/kaiserbad) spa complex on the eastern edge of town is popular with families, and the [Kaisertal valley](/europe/austria/kufstein/kaisertal) to the east leads up into the mountains past the [Tischofer Cave](/europe/austria/kufstein/tischofer_cave), one of the most important Ice Age sites in the Alps. It is easily reached from Innsbruck (45 minutes by train) or Salzburg (one hour), and sits just a few kilometres from the German border crossing into Rosenheim.
