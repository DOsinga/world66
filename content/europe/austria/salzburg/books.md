title: Books
type: section
---

**Thomas Bernhard, *Gathering Evidence***. The Austrian novelist's brutal childhood memoir is set largely in wartime and postwar Salzburg, and skewers the city's self-image as a place of music and beauty. A useful corrective to any Sound of Music sentimentality.

**Stefan Zweig, *The World of Yesterday***. Zweig — Austria's most translated writer in the 1920s — lived for years on the Kapuzinerberg and wrote some of his best work there. His memoir captures the lost cultural world from which the Salzburg Festival emerged.

**Maria von Trapp, *The Story of the Trapp Family Singers***. The real memoir behind The Sound of Music — more interesting than the film, with much more about the family's musical training and considerably less about romance.

**Wolfgang Hildesheimer, *Mozart***. A serious, sometimes provocative critical biography that strips away the cherubic-genius mythology built up around Mozart in his birthplace. Best read after a day at the Geburtshaus.

**Friedrich Torberg, *Tante Jolesch***. Not specifically about Salzburg, but a comic portrait of the vanished Austrian-Jewish coffeehouse culture that puts the city's pre-war musical world in context.
