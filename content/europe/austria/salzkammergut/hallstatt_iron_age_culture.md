The name Hallstatt refers not just to the village but to an entire epoch of European prehistory: the Hallstatt Period, from roughly 800 to 500 BCE, the earliest phase of the Iron Age in central Europe. The cemetery above the village yielded such a wealth of grave goods — bronze vessels, iron weapons, amber jewellery, silk from China — that archaeologists named the whole culture after the find site.

The [Hallstatt Museum](/europe/austria/hallstatt/hallstatt_museum) in the village market square holds the best of the excavated material, including reconstructed burial goods, sections of preserved wooden mining equipment, and explanations of how the salt trade connected Hallstatt to the Mediterranean world. The UNESCO World Heritage designation covers not just the village but the entire cultural landscape of the Hallstatt-Dachstein Salzkammergut, recognising the prehistoric, Habsburg, and industrial layers that make this one of the most densely historical landscapes in central Europe.
