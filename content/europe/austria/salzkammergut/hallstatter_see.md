The Hallstätter See is the most dramatic of the Salzkammergut lakes: narrow, mountain-walled, with the village of Hallstatt pressed improbably between cliff and water at its southwestern corner. The lake is cold and very deep — reaching 125 metres — and the colour ranges from deep blue-black in the shadows under the Dachstein wall to brilliant aquamarine in the shallower northern sections.

Swimming is possible at the northern end, where the lake is shallower and a degree or two warmer. Boat trips run from Hallstatt around the lake and can be combined with a visit to the salt mine above the village. The view of the village from the lake — or from the viewpoint on the opposite shore, reached by a short walk from the ferry station — is the most reproduced image in Austria. Come at dawn or dusk if you want it without the crowds and the selfie sticks.
