There are two railways that go by the name Salzkammergutbahn, and tourists in the lake district encounter both names interchangeably. The original was the **Salzkammergut-Lokalbahn**, a 760 mm narrow-gauge line that ran from Salzburg through St. Gilgen, along the [Wolfgangsee](/europe/austria/salzkammergut/wolfgangsee) and over the Pötschen Pass to [Bad Ischl](/europe/austria/badischl). Opened in 1890, it was the lifeline of the region for nearly seventy years — moving salt, timber, and the imperial court — before being shut down in 1957 and replaced by buses. A small museum in Bad Ischl preserves stations, signs, and a couple of locomotives, and short stretches of the trackbed have been rebuilt as cycling paths.

The name now usually refers to the standard-gauge **Salzkammergutbahn** (ÖBB line 156), which runs from Attnang-Puchheim on the main Vienna–Salzburg line south through [Gmunden](/europe/austria/salzkammergut/gmunden), Bad Ischl, [Hallstatt](/europe/austria/salzkammergut/hallstatt), and Bad Aussee, ending at Stainach-Irdning. It is one of Austria's scenic lines: the train hugs the eastern shore of the [Traunsee](/europe/austria/salzkammergut/traunsee), threads the gorge below the Dachstein, and arrives at Hallstatt on the opposite side of the lake from the village — passengers walk down to a small ferry that crosses to the famous viewpoint. Regional trains run hourly in season and a few times a day in winter; an unreserved seat costs little, and the route makes a perfect lake-hopping day even if you never get off the train.
