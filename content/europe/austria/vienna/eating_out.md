---
title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

Vienna's cooking is a hybrid of Austrian, Bohemian, Hungarian, and northern Italian traditions — the Habsburg empire on a plate. The standards are well known: **Wiener Schnitzel** (veal pounded thin, breaded, fried in clarified butter), **Tafelspitz** (boiled beef in broth), **Gulasch**, **Backhendl** (fried chicken), and a stream of pastries — Apfelstrudel, Topfenstrudel, Sachertorte, Kaiserschmarrn.

The traditional venue for everyday eating is the **Beisl**, an intimate neighbourhood place between restaurant and pub, serving home cooking and good local beer or wine. The best concentrations are in the 6th, 7th, and 8th districts. The **Würstelstand**, the city's beloved sausage stand, is the standard quick lunch; look for the classic Käsekrainer or a Leberkäs Semmel.

For market eating, the **Naschmarkt** and **Karmelitermarkt** both have excellent stalls. Vienna's contemporary food scene — modern Austrian cooking, natural wine, and a strong Israeli/Middle Eastern strand — clusters around the 2nd and 7th districts.

Specific restaurants worth seeking out: **Figlmüller** for the city's most famous Schnitzel, **Plachutta** for Tafelspitz, **Steirereck** for the high-end tasting menu, **Zum Schwarzen Kameel** for stand-up sandwiches and wine, **Skopik & Lohn** for inventive contemporary Austrian.
