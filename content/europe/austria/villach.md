Villach is Austria's seventh-largest city and the relaxed southern counterpart to [Klagenfurt](/europe/austria/klagenfurt). Sitting where the Drau river meets the Gail valley, it has been a crossroads since Roman times — Italy and Slovenia are both less than an hour away, and you can feel the Mediterranean influence in the architecture, the food, and the pace of life.

The compact old town centres on the [Hauptplatz](/europe/austria/villach/hauptplatz), a broad square ringed by pastel facades and cafe terraces. The [Stadtpfarrkirche St. Jakob](/europe/austria/villach/stadtpfarrkirche_st_jakob) perches above the square with panoramic views from its tower. South of town, [Warmbad Villach](/europe/austria/villach/warmbad_villach) has drawn visitors since antiquity and remains one of the best thermal bathing spots in Carinthia.

Villach makes an excellent base for exploring the surrounding lakes. [Faaker See](/europe/austria/faakersee) and [Ossiacher See](/europe/austria/ossiachersee) are both within easy reach, and in summer the whole region turns into a landscape of warm swimming lakes, cycling paths, and mountain trails. The [Villacher Alpenstrasse](/europe/austria/villach/villacher_alpenstrasse) climbs from the edge of town to the [Dobratsch](/europe/austria/villach/dobratsch) summit at 2,166 metres, offering one of the finest panoramic roads in the Alps. To the northeast, [Landskron Castle](/europe/austria/villach/landskron_castle) sits on a wooded hill with eagles circling above the ruins.

Every summer the city hosts the [Villacher Kirchtag](/europe/austria/villach/villacher_kirchtag), Austria's largest traditional folk festival — a week of music, costumes, and open-air revelry that draws visitors from across the country. In winter, the [Villacher Fasching](/europe/austria/villach/villacher_fasching) carnival brings a different kind of crowd, with elaborate processions and a distinctly southern exuberance that reflects Villach's proximity to Italy and Slovenia.
