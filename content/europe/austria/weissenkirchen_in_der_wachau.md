Weißenkirchen in der Wachau is a small wine village on the north bank of the Danube, between Spitz and Dürnstein. The setting is the whole argument for coming: terraced vineyards climb behind the houses, the river runs broad below, and the white fortified church rises above the roofs like a watchtower.

This is still the Wachau covered from [Melk](/europe/austria/melk), so Weißenkirchen has to earn its own stop. It does. The [fortified parish church](/europe/austria/weissenkirchen_in_der_wachau/fortified_parish_church) and Teisenhoferhof form one of the valley's best preserved defensive church ensembles, while the [Wachau Museum](/europe/austria/weissenkirchen_in_der_wachau/wachau_museum) gives the village a cultural centre beyond pretty facades.

The other reason to come is wine. The slopes around Weißenkirchen include some of the Wachau's serious Riesling and Grüner Veltliner vineyards, especially [Ried Achleiten](/europe/austria/weissenkirchen_in_der_wachau/ried_achleiten), whose dry-stone terraces face the Danube. A slow walk through the lanes and vineyards, followed by a heuriger meal, is exactly the kind of half-day that makes the Wachau more than a river cruise postcard.
