The Agdam Juma Mosque is the one significant building that survived the decades of abandonment following the 1993 evacuation. Built in 1868, the mosque is a two-storey structure with two minarets and a modest but dignified Azerbaijani Islamic architectural style. During the period of Armenian control, the mosque was not destroyed but was reportedly used as a cattle shelter, which preserved its walls while stripping it of religious fittings.

Since Azerbaijan recaptured Agdam in 2020, the mosque has been a focus of restoration efforts. It has been cleaned, repaired, and reopened for religious use. Standing among the ruins of the surrounding city, it is a striking sight — a functioning place of worship surrounded by decades of desolation now slowly being cleared. The mosque is open to visitors; dress appropriately and be respectful of any worshippers present.
