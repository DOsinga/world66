Baku is the capital and largest city of [Azerbaijan](/europe/azerbaijan), a sprawling metropolis on the western shore of the Caspian Sea that has reinvented itself dramatically since the oil boom of the 2000s. The skyline now features the [Flame Towers](/europe/azerbaijan/baku/flame_towers) — three glass skyscrapers shaped like tongues of fire that have become the city's most recognizable landmark — alongside Soviet-era blocks, Belle Époque mansions from the first oil boom of the 1890s, and the medieval walls of the Old City.

The [Icherisheher](/europe/azerbaijan/baku/icherisheher), or Old City, is the historic heart of Baku and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Its narrow, winding lanes are packed with carpet shops, tea houses, and caravanserais, and the [Maiden's Tower](/europe/azerbaijan/baku/maidenstower) and the [Palace of the Shirvanshahs](/europe/azerbaijan/baku/palace_of_the_shirvanshahs) are the standout monuments. Just outside the walls, [Fountain Square](/europe/azerbaijan/baku/fountain_square) and the pedestrian [Nizami Street](/europe/azerbaijan/baku/nizami_street) form the modern center, lined with cafes and boutiques. The [Heydar Aliyev Center](/europe/azerbaijan/baku/heydar_aliyev_center), designed by Zaha Hadid, is a striking piece of architecture worth visiting for the building alone.

Baku is a city of contrasts. The wealth from oil is visible in the new buildings and wide boulevards, but step a few streets back from the waterfront promenade and the city feels more like a post-Soviet capital working through its growing pains. The food is excellent — try plov (rice pilaf with lamb and dried fruits), kutabs (stuffed flatbreads), and the region's distinctive pomegranate-laced cuisine. The surrounding Absheron Peninsula has [fire temples](/europe/azerbaijan/baku/ateshgah) and [mud volcanoes](/europe/azerbaijan/baku/mud_volcanoes) that speak to Azerbaijan's ancient association with fire and petroleum.
