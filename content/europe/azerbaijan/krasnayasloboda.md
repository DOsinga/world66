Krasnaya Sloboda sits on the northern bank of the Kudyal River gorge, directly facing [Quba](/europe/azerbaijan/quba) — you can walk between the two towns across the [footbridge](/europe/azerbaijan/krasnayasloboda/footbridge_to_quba). The town is remarkable as one of the last surviving settlements of the Mountain Jews, a community that has lived in the eastern Caucasus for centuries.

Although emigration to [Israel](/asia/israel) has reduced the population considerably, the Jewish heritage remains visible. The [Six Dome Synagogue](/europe/azerbaijan/krasnayasloboda/six_dome_synagogue) is the most striking building in town, and the [Museum of Mountain Jews](/europe/azerbaijan/krasnayasloboda/museum_of_mountain_jews) tells the story of the Juhuro people. The [Jewish cemeteries](/europe/azerbaijan/krasnayasloboda/jewish_cemetery) spread up the hillside with headstones that span centuries. Houses throughout the town show signs of prosperity from remittances sent by family abroad. The overall atmosphere is quiet and very similar to Quba across the gorge.

Not far from town, [Khinalig village](/europe/azerbaijan/krasnayasloboda/khinalig_village) is home to an ethnic group who have preserved their own distinct language, customs, and traditions — one of the most isolated communities in the Caucasus and well worth the rough drive up into the mountains.
