Sharur sits on the western edge of the Nakhchivan autonomous republic, on the road between Nakhchivan city and the Turkish border. The town itself is a recent one, grown up around a Soviet-built railway station; the closure of the line northwest into Armenia in the early 1990s was a severe blow to the local economy.

The centre has a double-minaret mosque, an oddly shaped [regional museum](/europe/azerbaijan/naxcivan/sharur/regional_museum), and a [martyrs' memorial](/europe/azerbaijan/naxcivan/sharur/martyrs_memorial) in the park near the stadium. There are a couple of basic hotels — an old Soviet block in the centre and a newer place on the northern exit of town.

The real interest in the area lies in the archaeology. The hilltop Iron Age citadel of [Oğlanqala](/europe/azerbaijan/naxcivan/sharur/oglanqala) and the prehistoric settlement of [Ovçular Tepesi](/europe/azerbaijan/naxcivan/sharur/ovcular_tepesi), both within a few kilometres of town, are among the most important pre-classical sites in the southern Caucasus. The older town of [Yengijar](/europe/azerbaijan/naxcivan/sharur/yengijar), 2 km south across the railway, is also worth a visit for its old mosques, abandoned bathhouse, and small bazaar.

On a clear day Mount Ararat / Ağrı dominates the horizon to the west. Around Sharur and Yengijar lie vast stretches of fertile agricultural land along the Arpa river.

Sharur is 460 km southwest of Baku.
