Shusha (also written Şuşa) is a historic city in the Karabakh highlands of Azerbaijan, perched at around 1,400 metres above sea level. It is culturally significant to both Azerbaijanis and Armenians — both communities consider it central to their heritage — which made it one of the most contested prizes of the Nagorno-Karabakh conflict.

The city was controlled by Armenian forces from 1992 until November 2020, when Azerbaijani forces retook it during the Second Nagorno-Karabakh War. It has since been declared a "cultural capital" of Azerbaijan and reconstruction work is underway, though access for independent travellers remains restricted.

Shusha was historically renowned as a centre of Azerbaijani music and poetry — many prominent composers and mugham (classical music) performers were born here, earning it the title "cradle of Azerbaijani culture." The Şuşa State Historical Reserve encompasses numerous historic buildings from the eighteenth-century founding period, including the twin minarets of the [Yukhari Govhar Agha Mosque](/europe/azerbaijan/naxcivan/shusha/yukhari_govhar_agha_mosque), the [House of Khurshidbanu Natavan](/europe/azerbaijan/naxcivan/shusha/house_of_khurshidbanu_natavan), and the Armenian [Ghazanchetsots Cathedral](/europe/azerbaijan/naxcivan/shusha/ghazanchetsots_cathedral). For the best views of the surrounding mountains, walk out to the cliff edge above [Jidir Plain](/europe/azerbaijan/naxcivan/shusha/jidir_plain).
