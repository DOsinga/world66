Qax is a pleasant, tranquil provincial town in northwestern Azerbaijan, at the base of an appendix of Azerbaijani territory that juts into Georgia and Dagestan. It sits northwest of [Sheki](/europe/azerbaijan/saki) and about 400 km from Baku.

Social life centres on the chaikhana, an interesting building with a metallic dome located near the Baku bus stop on a park with a monument to Mammad Mammadov. The main bus station is in the south of town, near [St George's Church](/europe/azerbaijan/qax/st_georges_church), an attractive Georgian-style church that is the only active Georgian Orthodox church in Azerbaijan. Near the village of Tangyt, you can find the [tomb of Haji Murat](/europe/azerbaijan/qax/haji_murat_tomb), the Avar resistance fighter made famous by Tolstoy's novella *Hadji Murad*.

The nearby village of [Ilisu](/europe/azerbaijan/qax/ilisu_village), once the capital of an independent sultanate, is worth the short detour. There is only one street, but two castle towers — including the four-storey [Sumug fortress](/europe/azerbaijan/qax/sumug_fortress) — a mosque, and an 18th-century bridge survive, along with a twenty-minute trail down to a [beautiful waterfall](/europe/azerbaijan/qax/ilisu_waterfall) in the forest below.
