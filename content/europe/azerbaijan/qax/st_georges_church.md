On the southern edge of Qax, near the main bus station, a Georgian-style stone church stands quietly in a walled compound on the left bank of the Kakistskali river. This is St George's Church, a Georgian Orthodox building that dates in its current form to the early 18th century, built to replace an earlier church levelled during Shah Abbas I's invasion of Georgia in the early 17th century.

The church is still active and serves the local Ingilo community — Georgians who settled in this area centuries ago and whose descendants remain today. It is the only active Georgian Orthodox church in Azerbaijan, a fact that gives it a significance well beyond its modest size. Visitors are generally welcome outside service times. The setting, between the river and the forested hills, is attractive in itself.
