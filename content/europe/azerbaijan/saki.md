Sheki (historically known as Nukha, and still sometimes spelled Shaki) sits in the foothills of the Greater Caucasus in northwestern Azerbaijan, surrounded by chestnut forests and mountain streams. It was a major stop on the ancient Silk Road and served as the capital of the Sheki Khanate from the 17th through 19th centuries — a history you can see in the remarkable buildings that survive from that era.

The [Xan Saray](/europe/azerbaijan/saki/sights) (Khan's Palace) is the centrepiece: a 18th-century summer palace whose interior walls are covered floor to ceiling in intricate geometric paintings made with no adhesive — the pigments mixed with egg and pressed through stained glass to create patterns that have lasted three centuries without restoration. The Sheki Caravansarai, a huge two-story stone inn from the same era built to house Silk Road traders, still operates as a guesthouse and restaurant, making it probably the most atmospheric place to stay or eat in the South Caucasus.

The town bazaar remains a working market rather than a tourist attraction, selling silk, dried fruits, and the famous Sheki halva — a layered sweet made from rice flour and butter that is pressed with hazelnuts and available in every shop in town. The [silk factory](/europe/azerbaijan/saki/sights) still operates on the edge of town and sells directly.

A short drive or taxi ride brings you to Kish, a village with an Albanian Christian church dating to the 1st century AD — one of the oldest continuously used places of worship in the Caucasus. Getting to Sheki from Baku involves a five-hour train or four-hour bus journey northwest through the Kura valley.
