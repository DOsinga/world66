Baranovichi is a mid-sized city of about 180,000 in western Belarus, roughly halfway between [Minsk](/europe/belarus/minsk) and [Brest](/europe/belarus/brest) on the main road and rail line. It is a railway junction town at heart — it grew up where two imperial rail lines crossed in the 1870s — and most travellers pass through rather than stop.

Those who do stop will find a tidy Soviet-era city with a handful of churches, a [local history museum](/europe/belarus/baranovichi/baranovichi_local_history_museum), and a few pleasant parks. The [Great Patriotic War Memorial Complex](/europe/belarus/baranovichi/wwii_memorial_complex) reflects the enormous suffering Belarus endured during the Second World War. The city was almost entirely destroyed during the German occupation and the subsequent Soviet reconquest, which makes any surviving pre-war structure worth attention — including the [Church of Saints Peter and Paul](/europe/belarus/baranovichi/peter_and_paul_church). Near the station, a retired [locomotive on a plinth](/europe/belarus/baranovichi/railway_workers_memorial) pays honest tribute to the city's founding industry.

The main interest for travellers is as a base for visiting the Belovezhskaya Pushcha (Białowieża Forest), Europe's last lowland primeval forest, which begins about 100 km to the south. Day trips are feasible; the forest straddles the Polish border and is home to the largest free-roaming herd of European bison on the planet.
