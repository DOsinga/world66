The Museum of Confiscated Art in Brest is one of the most unusual museums in Eastern Europe — a collection built entirely from objects seized by border control at the Brest customs crossing with Poland. The items on display include icons, paintings, coins, jewellery, and decorative objects that smugglers were attempting to move out of Belarus illegally when they were caught.

The collection is eclectic and unexpected: you might find a 17th-century icon next to a collection of Soviet-era medals, or a piece of folk embroidery next to a carved amber figure. The museum makes no attempt to impose a curatorial narrative because there is none — these are objects that happened to be confiscated in the same place over the same period. That randomness is part of what makes the collection interesting.

The museum is small and easy to miss, but it rewards a visit for anyone interested in the texture of the Belarus-Poland border zone and the strange historical flows of objects across it.
