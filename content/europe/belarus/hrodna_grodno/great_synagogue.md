The Choral Synagogue on Vialikaja Trajeckaja Street is the main surviving Jewish building in Hrodna, a substantial Orthodox-Hasidic synagogue that has been restored and is open to visitors. It stands as a monument to the once-large Jewish community of the city, which was one of the most significant in the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth and later the Russian Empire. Jewish merchants, craftsmen, and scholars played a central role in Hrodna's commercial and intellectual life for centuries.

The building is not architecturally exceptional but is historically significant and worth a visit for anyone interested in the Jewish heritage of Eastern Europe. There is a small exhibition inside about the history of the community. The nearby Zaniomanskaja Synagogue, further south on the other side of the Nioman, is another surviving fragment of a community that once numbered in the tens of thousands.
