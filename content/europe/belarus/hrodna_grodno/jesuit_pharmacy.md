The Jesuit Pharmacy on Savieckaja Square was built between 1700 and 1709 and is one of the most unusual historic buildings in Hrodna. The ground floor still functions as a pharmacy today; the upper level houses a small museum of the history of pharmacy in the region, with period equipment and exhibits on medicinal plants and the history of the Jesuit medical tradition in the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth.

The building itself — a well-preserved example of early 18th-century Baroque architecture — is worth looking at from the outside even if you don't go in. The corner position on the main square gives it good visibility, and the pharmacy sign is a modest indicator of an institution that has served the city for over 300 years.
