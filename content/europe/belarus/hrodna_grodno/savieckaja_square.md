  The Jesuit Pharmacy that stands on the corner of the square was built between 1700 and 1709 and is one of the oldest continuously operating pharmacies in Europe. When the Jesuits were expelled from the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth in the 1770s, the pharmacy passed through various owners but never closed. Today it still functions as a pharmacy, though it also contains a small museum of historical pharmaceutical equipment.

The central square of Hrodna is a wide, pleasantly proportioned space ringed with pastel-coloured buildings of various periods. The Jesuit Pharmacy on the corner dates from around 1700 and is an unusually well-preserved example of 18th-century urban architecture in Belarus; most of its contemporaries were destroyed in the wars of the 20th century. The square itself is the focal point of the pedestrian area and a good place to start exploring the old town.

Soviet-era additions give the square its current name, but the underlying urban fabric is older. The streets radiating from it — particularly Savieckaja Street (formerly Sovetskaya), the main pedestrian axis — have a good concentration of cafes, shops, and small restaurants. Evenings here are livelier than you might expect for a Belarusian city this far from Minsk.
