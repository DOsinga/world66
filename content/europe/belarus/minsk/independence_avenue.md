Independence Avenue — Praspiekt Niezaliezhnastsi in Belarusian, or simply "the prospect" to locals — is one of the most complete examples of Stalinist urban planning anywhere in the world. Stretching six kilometres from the main railway station to the Academy of Sciences, it was laid out during the postwar reconstruction of Minsk (the city was 80% destroyed in World War II) and lined with monumental neoclassical buildings of a scale intended to inspire awe.

Walking the length of the avenue takes about 90 minutes and takes you past October Square, the Government House, the KGB building (hard to miss), the Church of St Simon and Helena, and numerous ornate apartment buildings whose ground floors are occupied by shops. The avenue was designated part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2023, recognising its exceptional architectural integrity as an example of postwar Soviet urban planning.

The best time to walk it is in the late afternoon when the long light catches the pale stone facades. At night, the main buildings are lit up and the effect is dramatic, if slightly surreal.
