story: >
  Construction of the Palace of the Republic began in the Soviet era but stalled repeatedly due to changing plans, money shortages, and the collapse of the USSR. For years the site was an embarrassing hole in the ground in the middle of October Square — locals called it "the pit." Alexander Lukashenko, who came to power in 1994, ordered its completion as a prestige project. It opened in 2001.
---

The Palace of the Republic dominates the south side of October Square with a facade of granite and glass that aims for grandeur and mostly achieves it, in the way that Soviet architecture always aimed for grandeur. The building is a major events venue: rock concerts, government ceremonies, and international conferences all take place here. It holds 2,700 people in its main hall.

The Palace is primarily of interest to visitors as a piece of urban scenery rather than a venue to visit. The scale of the building and its placement on October Square give you the full effect of Minsk's postwar urban design ambitions. From this vantage point you can see the city as it was intended to be seen: the long avenues, the heroic proportions, the deliberate dwarfing of the individual.
