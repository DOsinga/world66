Troitsky Predmestye — Trinity Suburb — is the closest thing Minsk has to an old town. A compact cluster of 19th-century buildings along the west bank of the Svislach River, it was one of the few areas of Minsk spared from wartime destruction (or reconstructed with enough care to feel authentic) and now houses cafes, galleries, and cultural institutions.

The area is small — you can walk through it in twenty minutes — but it provides a crucial human scale after the monumental Stalinist avenues that dominate the city centre. The riverfront is pleasant for a stroll, particularly in the evening when the buildings are lit up and Minsk's young professionals congregate at the outdoor terrace bars and cafes that line the embankment.

The Old Town connects easily to the Isle of Tears memorial to the north and the pedestrianised Vulitsa Lenina to the east. It's the natural starting point for exploring the human-scaled Minsk that exists in the gaps between the grand Soviet set pieces.
