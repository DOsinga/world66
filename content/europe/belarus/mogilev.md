Mogilev, also written Mahilyow, is Belarus's big eastern city on the Dnieper. It is less polished than [Minsk](/europe/belarus/minsk) and less romantic than [Hrodna](/europe/belarus/hrodna_grodno), but it has real weight: old trade routes, Polish-Lithuanian town rights, Russian imperial layers, Soviet industry, and a wartime history that still presses hard on the city.

The compact historic centre is the place to start. The reconstructed [Mogilev Town Hall](/europe/belarus/mogilev/mogilev_town_hall) gives the city back its old civic symbol, while the [Co-Cathedral of St Stanislaus](/europe/belarus/mogilev/co_cathedral_st_stanislaus) and [St Nicholas Monastery](/europe/belarus/mogilev/st_nicholas_monastery) preserve the stronger religious architecture. Mogilev rewards slow walking more than quick ticking-off: Leninskaya Street, the Dnieper slopes, and the red-brick [Drama Theatre](/europe/belarus/mogilev/drama_theatre) make a coherent city centre.

The darker layer sits just outside the centre. The [Cold Synagogue site](/europe/belarus/mogilev/cold_synagogue_site) recalls a Jewish community largely destroyed in the Holocaust, while [Buinichi Field](/europe/belarus/mogilev/buinichi_field) remembers the 1941 defence of the city. Mogilev is not an easy sell as a holiday destination, but it is one of the better Belarusian cities for travellers who care about how history survives rebuilding.
