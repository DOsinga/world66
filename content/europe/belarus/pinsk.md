Pinsk sits in the flat, watery country of Polesia, where the Pina River threads toward the Pripyat marshes. It is one of Belarus's more rewarding smaller cities: old enough to have a real street plan, quiet enough to walk without hurry, and still marked by the Catholic, Orthodox, Polish, Jewish, and Belarusian layers that shaped this borderland.

The centre is compact. Start around Lenin Square, where the massive [Jesuit Collegium](/europe/belarus/pinsk/jesuit_collegium) faces the river and the [Cathedral Basilica of the Assumption](/europe/belarus/pinsk/assumption_cathedral) keeps the city's best Baroque interior. Nearby, [Butrimovich Palace](/europe/belarus/pinsk/butrimovich_palace) is a reminder that Pinsk was not just a provincial backwater but a prosperous Polesian town with merchants, noble families, and river trade.

Pinsk also asks for a sober stop. Before the Second World War it had one of the most important Jewish communities in the region; the [Pinsk Ghetto memorial](/europe/belarus/pinsk/pinsk_ghetto_memorial) records a history that is easy to miss if you only look at the restored facades. Give the city half a day if you are travelling between [Brest](/europe/belarus/brest) and the Pripyat wetlands, longer if you like small cities that reveal themselves slowly.
