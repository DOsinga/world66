The Sluch River bends through southern Slutsk and provides the town's most pleasant walking ground. A riverside promenade follows the bank through a park belt, past Soviet-era monuments, benches, and a landscape that opens up the view across the water — a relief from the enclosed apartment blocks of the main streets. The setting is typical of Belarusian provincial towns: calm, a little melancholy, and entirely unhurried.

The river was always the lifeblood of the city — Slutsk grew on its banks, the old castle stood above it, and the medieval town extended along the higher ground beside the water. Nothing of that survives, but the physical geography remains: the slight escarpment, the flood meadows on the far bank, the sense of the town turning toward the water. On summer evenings the promenade fills with families and the local fishing crowd.
