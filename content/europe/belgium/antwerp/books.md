**A Dog of Flanders** by Ouida. The Victorian children's novel set in Antwerp — Nello and his loyal dog Patrasche, the boy's longing to see the Rubens paintings in the Cathedral. Largely forgotten in the West, but a cultural touchstone in Japan and Korea; both produced animated TV versions that have made millions of pilgrimages from Asia to the Cathedral.

**Conscience: the Lion of Flanders** by Hendrik Conscience. The nineteenth-century historical novel that more or less invented the modern Flemish identity, telling the story of the Battle of the Golden Spurs (1302). Available in English translation; the Hendrik Conscience Heritage Library on the square named for him preserves the original manuscripts.

**The Sorrow of Belgium** by Hugo Claus. Claus's autobiographical novel covers the Flemish experience of the Second World War, much of it set in Antwerp. One of the great Belgian novels of the twentieth century.

**War and Turpentine** by Stefan Hertmans. A grandson's reconstruction of his grandfather's life — the boy from Ghent who fought in the First World War and ended his days in Antwerp. Quiet, lyrical, and beautifully translated.

**Antwerp** by Roberto Bolaño. The short, fragmentary first novel by the Chilean novelist — set partly in the city, partly in the head. Difficult, slim, recommended for fans of his later work more than for first-time readers.
