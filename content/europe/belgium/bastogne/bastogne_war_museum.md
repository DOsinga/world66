The Bastogne War Museum is one of the finest military history museums in Western Europe, opened in 2014 on the site of the old Historical Center. The building itself is architecturally striking — a star-shaped structure embedded in the hillside near the Mardasson Memorial — and the interior makes full use of immersive design. Rather than a conventional display of vehicles and artifacts, the museum builds its narrative through four parallel stories: a Belgian civilian, an American soldier, a German soldier, and a Luxembourg refugee, each experiencing the Battle of the Bulge from their own vantage point.

The collection includes original uniforms, weapons, documents, and personal effects from both sides of the battle, set within reconstructed environments ranging from a bombed Ardennes farmhouse to a winter forest foxhole. Audio and film elements are integrated throughout, and the museum does not flinch from the scale of suffering on all sides. Budget at least two to three hours; rushing through would be a waste. The museum is particularly meaningful for visitors with family connections to the American units that fought here — the 101st Airborne, the 10th Armored Division, and many others are documented in detail.
