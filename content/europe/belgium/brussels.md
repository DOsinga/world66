Brussels wears two hats. Politically, it is the de facto capital of the European Union — home to the European Commission, the Parliament, the Council, and a vast functional neighbourhood of glass-and-steel around Schuman that lives off EU business. Culturally, it is the bilingual capital of a country that is itself oddly constructed — every sign in French and Flemish, every official bureaucracy duplicated, a layer of dry self-aware humour about the whole arrangement.

This second Brussels is what most visitors come for. The [**Grand-Place**](/europe/belgium/brussels/grand_place) is one of the great squares in Europe, a UNESCO World Heritage Site of gilded guild houses and a Gothic town hall. Behind it winds a warren of medieval streets with [**Manneken Pis**](/europe/belgium/brussels/mannekenpis) at the dead-end of one of them, and the beautiful glass-roofed [**Galeries Saint-Hubert**](/europe/belgium/brussels/galeries_saint_hubert) opening off the main street. Uphill, the [**Royal District**](/europe/belgium/brussels/royaldistrict) holds the [Magritte Museum](/europe/belgium/brussels/magritte_museum), the [Musical Instruments Museum](/europe/belgium/brussels/musical_instruments_museum), the Royal Fine Arts, and the formal 18th-century city that the Habsburgs built. Downhill, the antiques district of [**Sablon**](/europe/belgium/brussels/zaveldistrict) and the scruffy, wonderful working-class **Marolles** are where Brussels is most itself. The [**Belgian Centre for Comic Strip Art**](/europe/belgium/brussels/comic_strip_museum) north of the centre is worth a detour even if you don't know Tintin.

Brussels is also the world capital of **Art Nouveau**. Victor Horta's houses in Saint-Gilles and [**Ixelles**](/europe/belgium/brussels/ixelles), four of which are UNESCO-listed, are the set pieces, but the style is all over the city if you know what to look for — around Square Ambiorix, Avenue Louise, Place Brugmann. The [**Atomium**](/europe/belgium/brussels/atomium) further out is the great modernist monument, a 102-metre stainless steel rendering of an iron crystal left over from the 1958 World's Fair. Out east, the [**Parc du Cinquantenaire**](/europe/belgium/brussels/cinquantenaire) combines a triumphal arch with three major museums and is a good half-day from the centre.

The food is very good. Brussels rivals Paris for density of serious restaurants, and is the world capital of two things — **beer** and **chocolate** — and a strong claimant for **fries** and **waffles**. Eating here is cheaper and less formal than Paris, and the culture of drawing out drinks and snacks into a whole evening is part of the local rhythm.

A warning: Brussels has a reputation for street crime higher than most Western European capitals. The main stations (Midi/Zuid and Nord) and the arc of streets between them are genuinely sketchy after dark. Stick to the historic centre, Ixelles, Saint-Gilles, and the EU district at night, and watch your bag on the metro.

Two nights is the minimum; three or four is better, especially if a festival is on. Brussels is also well placed as a base for [Bruges](/europe/belgium/bruges), [Ghent](/europe/belgium/gent), and [Antwerp](/europe/belgium/antwerp), all within an hour by train.
