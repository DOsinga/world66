East of the centre, Brussels turns into the capital of Europe: a district of glass institutions where the European Commission, the Council, and — a little further on — the Parliament cluster around the Schuman roundabout. It is sleek and a touch soulless during the week, and dead at weekends, but it holds real draws for visitors. The **Parlamentarium** and the **House of European History** explain the EU project, for free and surprisingly well.

The district's green showpiece is the **Parc du Cinquantenaire**, laid out for the 1880 jubilee of Belgian independence and dominated by a vast triple triumphal arch. Around it sit three big museums — the Art & History Museum, the military museum, and **Autoworld**'s collection of vintage cars. Round off a visit with a paper cone of *frites* from the celebrated stand on nearby Place Jourdan.
