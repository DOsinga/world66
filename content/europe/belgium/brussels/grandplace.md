The Grand-Place is the obvious place to begin in Brussels, and the dense lattice of pedestrian lanes around it — the *Îlot Sacré* — is the city's tourist heart. The square itself, ringed by gilded guild houses and the lace-like spire of the Town Hall, is one of the most theatrical in Europe; UNESCO-listed, it is at its best early in the morning or late at night, when the floodlights catch the gold. Every two years in August it disappears under a vast carpet of begonias.

A few minutes' walk in any direction brings you to the rest of the centre's set pieces: the small bronze **Manneken Pis**, the glass-roofed **Royal Galleries of Saint-Hubert** — Europe's oldest shopping arcade — and a tangle of streets, Rue des Bouchers above all, packed with restaurants, *frites* stands, chocolate shops, and some of the city's oldest beer cafés. It is touristy, unapologetically so, but the beauty is real.

This is also the easiest base for a first visit: walkable, central, and a short stroll from the Sablon, Sainte-Catherine, and the Royal District.
