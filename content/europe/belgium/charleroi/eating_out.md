---
title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

Charleroi's food scene is unpretentious Belgian: solid brasseries, friteries (chip shops), and a sprinkling of Italian and North African restaurants reflecting the city's immigrant communities. The **Ville-Haute** around Place Charles II has the most concentrated restaurant options.

Belgian standards apply: mussels and chips, steak with Liège sauce, boudin noir (black pudding) with apple, carbonnade flamande (beef braised in beer). The local **Côte à l'os** (bone-in rib steak) is a specialty. Beer selection in any reasonable brasserie will run to dozens of Belgian labels.

The **Place du Manège** area in the lower town has several affordable options. For a quick lunch, the **friterie** near the Central station is a reliable Charleroi institution — the chips here are fried in beef fat in the traditional Belgian manner and are genuinely good.
