Dinant sits in a dramatic setting on the Meuse River, squeezed between limestone cliffs and the water. The town is dominated by its [citadel](/europe/belgium/dinant/citadel), perched a hundred metres above the distinctive onion-domed [Collegiate Church of Notre-Dame](/europe/belgium/dinant/collegiate_church), and makes a fine base for exploring the Belgian Ardennes.

South of Dinant the Meuse cuts a deep trench through the Ardennes plateau. The most spectacular stretch is at Freyr, six kilometres downstream, where the sheer [Rocks of Freÿr](/europe/belgium/dinant/rocks_at_freyr) on one bank face a [Renaissance castle](/europe/belgium/dinant/freyr_castle) and classical gardens on the other. [Boat trips](/europe/belgium/dinant/meuse_boat_trip) along the river run throughout the summer, and [kayaking](/europe/belgium/dinant/lesse_kayaking) down the River Lesse from Houyet to Anseremme — past the dramatically perched [Walzin Castle](/europe/belgium/dinant/walzin_castle) — is the area's most popular outdoor activity.

Dinant is also the birthplace of [Adolphe Sax](/europe/belgium/dinant/maison_de_monsieur_sax), inventor of the saxophone — a fact the town celebrates with saxophone-shaped benches, a small museum, and the [Charles de Gaulle bridge](/europe/belgium/dinant/charles_de_gaulle_bridge) lined with 28 painted saxophones honouring each EU member state. The surrounding countryside offers cycling, walking, and caves; the show cave of [La Merveilleuse](/europe/belgium/dinant/la_merveilleuse), 500 metres from the railway station, has impressive stalactites and underground waterfalls.

For day trips, the [water gardens at Annevoie](/europe/belgium/dinant/annevoie_gardens) and the storybook [Château de Vêves](/europe/belgium/dinant/chateau_de_veves) at Celles are within 15 km, and Namur — Wallonia's capital — is a 30-minute train ride north. Don't leave without trying a **[couque de Dinant](/europe/belgium/dinant/couque_de_dinant)**, the local honey biscuit said to have been invented during the 1466 siege.
