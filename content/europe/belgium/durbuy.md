Durbuy claims to be the smallest city in the world — it holds a formal charter of city status while having a population of only a few hundred in the historic core. Whether or not the claim holds up to scrutiny, the medieval centre is genuinely lovely: cobblestone streets, an old stone bridge over the Ourthe, and a tight cluster of historic buildings presided over by a 17th-century castle.

Cross the old bridge and you face the [Castle of Ursel](/europe/belgium/durbuy/castle_of_ursel), rebuilt in the 17th century on the ruins of an 11th-century fortification, with the [Saint-Nicolas Church](/europe/belgium/durbuy/saint_nicolas_church) and the [Corn Hall](/europe/belgium/durbuy/corn_hall) — a half-timbered civic building documented since 1380 — completing the ensemble. The whole historic core can be walked in under an hour, which is part of its appeal.

The surrounding Ardennes countryside is the other draw. [Kayaking the Ourthe](/europe/belgium/durbuy/kayaking_ourthe) is the quintessential summer activity, with outfitters in Barvaux-sur-Ourthe, 5 kilometres away, providing equipment and logistics for river trips. Rock climbing, mountain biking, and hiking trails fan out into the forested hills above the valley. A quirky [topiary park](/europe/belgium/durbuy/topiary_park) near the edge of town offers something different for families.

Durbuy fills up with day-trippers from Brussels and Liège on summer weekends. If you want the town to yourself, arrive on a weekday morning in spring or autumn.
