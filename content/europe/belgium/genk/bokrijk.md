Bokrijk is a 550-hectare domain east of Genk encompassing one of the largest open-air architecture museums in Europe. The site gathers historic buildings moved from across Belgium and Flanders — farmhouses, barns, windmills, a church, workshops, a town square with guild houses — and reassembles them in an intelligently organized landscape that demonstrates how Flemish communities lived from the medieval period to the early 20th century.

The buildings are not just preserved shells: craftspeople work in the workshops, demonstrating traditional trades, and the interiors are furnished and interpreted in detail. The ambition is to show how people actually lived rather than just what buildings looked like. The result is one of those museums that rewards slow walking and genuine curiosity rather than a quick circuit.

The Bokrijk domain also includes a substantial nature reserve and arboretum, making it an attractive destination beyond the architecture collection. Allow at least half a day; a full day is easy to fill. Located about 5 km east of Genk center, accessible by bus and bicycle along marked cycling routes.
