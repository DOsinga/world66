C-Mine is what happened when Genk decided to deal honestly with its industrial past. The former Waterschei colliery complex — headframes, machine halls, underground galleries, and the concrete structures that once processed coal — has been converted into a cultural campus without erasing what it used to be. The industrial scale and texture remain visible; the rust and weight of the machinery are part of the design.

Today the complex houses a cinema, a design school, a theater, exhibition spaces, restaurants, and a rooftop viewing platform that looks out over the flat Kempen landscape. Temporary exhibitions vary through the year; the permanent installation in the underground galleries takes visitors through the mine's history in a way that's more documentary than theme-park.

C-Mine is the clearest expression of Genk's attitude toward its own history — the mining past is not prettified or airbrushed, but neither is it treated as something to escape. The complex is busy with students, families, and visitors throughout the week.
