---
title: When to Go
type: section
---

Ghent is a genuine year-round city and does not have a bad season. The architecture looks good in any light, and the bars and restaurants keep operating through the grey Belgian winters.

Spring (April–May) and early autumn (September–October) are the most comfortable for walking the historic centre — mild temperatures, reasonable crowd levels, and good light for the canal views. Summer (June–August) brings the Ghent Festivities (Gentse Feesten) in the third week of July — ten days of free outdoor concerts, street theatre, and general revelry that transforms the entire city centre. It is one of the largest free arts festivals in Europe and genuinely worth timing a visit around, though accommodation fills completely.

Winter is quiet but atmospheric. Ghent runs a Christmas market in the centre from late November through late December, and the guild houses on the Graslei are illuminated at night. The museums are never crowded.
