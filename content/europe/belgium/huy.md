Huy is a small Walloon town on the Meuse River, roughly halfway between [Namur](/europe/belgium/namur) and [Liege](/europe/belgium/liege). It has been a crossroads since Roman times, and the river, the bluffs, and the citadel above make it one of the more attractive stops in the Meuse Valley.

The town's landmark is the [Collegiate Church of Notre-Dame](/europe/belgium/huy/notre_dame_collegiate_church), a 14th-century Gothic church with an enormous rose window — the Li Rondia — that is considered one of the finest in Belgium. Inside, the treasury holds a collection of medieval metalwork from the Meuse Valley workshops that were famous across Europe.

Above the town, the [Citadel of Huy](/europe/belgium/huy/citadel) sits on a bluff overlooking the river. The current fort dates from the early 19th century and was used as an internment camp during both World Wars. It now houses a museum on the Nazi occupation, and the views from the ramparts are worth the climb. A cable car (télépherique) runs up from the town centre in summer.

The [Grand Place](/europe/belgium/huy/bassinia_fountain) is pleasant without being spectacular — a few cafes, the town hall, and the copper fountain called the Bassinia, one of four symbols of Huy that appear on the town's coat of arms: the bridge, the rose window, the citadel, and the fountain. Three kilometres south, the ruins of [Solières Abbey](/europe/belgium/huy/solieres_abbey) make a worthwhile excursion into the Hoyoux valley.

Huy is an easy stop on the train line between Namur and Liège, about 25 minutes from either city.
