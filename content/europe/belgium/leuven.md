Leuven is Belgium's ultimate university town. With more than 25,000 students at the 500-year-old KU Leuven — one of Europe's oldest universities — the city has an energy that belies its modest size. The campus is woven into the fabric of the town itself: medieval colleges stand alongside research labs, and the student population keeps the bars, restaurants, and cultural scene thriving year-round.

The [Oude Markt](/europe/belgium/leuven/oude_markt) is the social heart of the city, ringed by over 60 cafes and bars — locals call it the longest bar in the world, and on a warm evening it certainly feels like it. The [Grote Markt](/europe/belgium/leuven/grote_markt) is the architectural centrepiece, dominated by the extraordinarily ornate 15th-century [Stadhuis](/europe/belgium/leuven/stadhuis) (town hall) and the imposing [Sint-Pieterskerk](/europe/belgium/leuven/sint_pieterskerk) (Church of St Peter), which houses Dirk Bouts' masterpiece *The Last Supper*.

Beyond the centre, the [Groot Begijnhof](/europe/belgium/leuven/groot_begijnhof) is one of the most beautiful beguinages in Flanders — a traffic-free enclave of cobbled streets and brick houses along the river Dijle, now used as university housing. [Park Abbey](/europe/belgium/leuven/park_abbey) in Heverlee, just south of town, is a magnificent abbey complex with a baroque church and landscaped grounds, inhabited by the same community of monks for nearly 900 years. And on [Ladeuzeplein](/europe/belgium/leuven/ladeuzeplein), the [University Library](/europe/belgium/leuven/library) stands as a moving symbol of resilience, rebuilt twice after destruction in both World Wars.
