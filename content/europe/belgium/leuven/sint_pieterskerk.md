Sint-Pieterskerk stands on the south side of the Grote Markt, its broad Gothic nave and unfinished west towers giving it a slightly rugged look beside the lacework extravagance of the town hall opposite. The church was begun in the late 14th century in the Brabantine Gothic style and took over a century to complete; a planned central tower was abandoned after the foundations proved inadequate, leaving the silhouette we see today.

The interior is spacious and well-lit, but the main reason to visit is Dirk Bouts' triptych *The Last Supper* (1464–68), housed in the Treasury section of the church. This is one of the masterpieces of early Flemish painting, a monumental altarpiece commissioned by the Brotherhood of the Holy Sacrament. The central panel shows Christ at the moment of the institution of the Eucharist — calm, frontal, deeply serious — surrounded by apostles in a precisely rendered Flemish interior. The church also holds several other Bouts works and a collection of medieval ecclesiastical treasures. Entry to the Treasury requires a small admission fee.
