The Palace of the Prince-Bishops dominates the Place Saint-Lambert, the large square at the heart of Liège. For over a thousand years, from the tenth century until the French Revolution swept away the Prince-Bishopric in 1795, this was the seat of one of the most powerful ecclesiastical rulers in Europe. The bishops of Liège controlled a sovereign state, minted their own coins, raised armies, and built this palace to match their ambitions.

The building that survives today was ordered by Prince-Bishop Everard van der Marck in 1526, and its outer walls are Italian Renaissance while the magnificent inner courtyard uses the massive Gothic columns of the Mosan tradition. The palace now serves as the Palace of Justice, but you can walk into the courtyard during weekday opening hours to see the architecture at close range. The facade facing the square, added in 1737, is among the finest baroque frontages in Wallonia.
