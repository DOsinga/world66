---
title: "Beaches"
type: section
---

Nieuwpoort's beach is wide, flat, and made of pale fine sand — typical of this stretch of the Belgian coast. It runs along Nieuwpoort-Bad, the seaside extension of the town, lined behind by the usual Belgian-coast mix of mid-rise apartment buildings and a tidy seafront promenade.

The water is shallow for a long way out, which makes it good for children but cold even in August — the North Sea here rarely climbs above 19 °C. There are lifeguarded sections, beach bars in summer, and rental cabanas in the classic Belgian style. The beach is least crowded west of the harbour, towards Oostduinkerke, where the dunes are wider and the apartment blocks thin out.

Long-distance walks along the sand are a real draw: low tide exposes hundreds of metres of firm sand and you can walk for kilometres in either direction with shellfish and stranded jellyfish for company.
