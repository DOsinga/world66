story: >
  Oudenaarde tapestries were renowned for their verdures — tapestries depicting forests, meadows, and wildlife rather than figures or narrative scenes. By the late 16th century, Oudenaarde weavers had cornered the European market for these richly foliated textiles. The craft was so specialised that the town had a Tapestry Hall for quality inspection as early as 1531, and exports reached palaces from Madrid to Stockholm.
---

The MOU museum occupies the upper floors of the Stadhuis and tells the twin stories of Oudenaarde's tapestry trade and its wider regional history. The tapestry collection is the main draw: pieces from the fifteenth through eighteenth centuries showing the distinctive verdure style — dense botanical compositions filled with birds and animals — for which the town was famous across Europe. The quality of the weaving is extraordinary, and many pieces remain vividly coloured despite their age.

Beyond the tapestries, the museum covers the history of the town and the surrounding Flemish Ardennes region, with collections relating to local guilds, religious life, and the landscape. The setting inside the Gothic town hall adds to the experience — the medieval council chamber and some of the original interior woodwork survive intact.
