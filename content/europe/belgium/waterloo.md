Waterloo is famous for a single day: 18 June 1815, when Napoleon's army met the Duke of Wellington's forces on the rolling farmland south of this small [Belgian](/europe/belgium) town. The battle ended Napoleon's rule and reshaped Europe. Today Waterloo is a prosperous commuter suburb of [Brussels](/europe/belgium/brussels), but the battlefield remains largely unchanged.

The [Lion's Mound](/europe/belgium/waterloo/lions_mound), a 40-metre artificial hill topped with a cast-iron lion, marks the spot where the Prince of Orange was wounded and offers a panoramic view of the entire battlefield. At its base, the [Memorial 1815](/europe/belgium/waterloo/memorial_1815) museum uses immersive displays to walk visitors through the events of the day. The [Panorama of the Battle of Waterloo](/europe/belgium/waterloo/panorama_of_waterloo), a vast circular painting from 1912, gives a vivid sense of the chaos. [Hougoumont Farm](/europe/belgium/waterloo/hougoumont) — where the battle's most critical engagement was fought — can be visited on a walking circuit south of the mound.

The battlefield is about 5km south of the town of Waterloo itself. The [Wellington Museum](/europe/belgium/waterloo/wellington_museum) in the town centre occupies the inn where Wellington had his headquarters. The [Church of Saint Joseph](/europe/belgium/waterloo/church_of_saint_joseph) next door contains a dense collection of memorial plaques to officers killed in the battle.
