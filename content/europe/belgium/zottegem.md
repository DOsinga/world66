Zottegem is a small town about 25 kilometres south of [Ghent](/europe/belgium/ghent) in East Flanders, at the northern edge of the Flemish Ardennes — the rolling hills that make this part of Belgium a favourite with road cyclists. The spring classics pass through here, and the surrounding lanes are excellent for riding year-round.

The town is historically associated with the Counts of Egmont. [Egmont Castle](/europe/belgium/zottegem/egmont_castle) (now used for municipal functions) sits in a park on the edge of the centre, and a small [Egmont Museum](/europe/belgium/zottegem/egmont_museum) in the old town hall on the main square covers the family's story. The bronze [Statue of Egmont](/europe/belgium/zottegem/statue_egmont) on that square is Zottegem's signature monument, and the rebel count is buried in the crypt of the [Onze-Lieve-Vrouw church](/europe/belgium/zottegem/onze_lieve_vrouw_church) a few steps away. The main square has a pleasant if quiet atmosphere, with a few cafes and a weekly market. Zottegem is not a destination in itself, but if you are cycling or driving through the Flemish Ardennes, it makes a good stopping point.
