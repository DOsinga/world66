About 150 km northwest of [Sarajevo](/europe/bosniaandherzegovina/sarajevo), Jajce is a medieval walled town built around a hilltop fortress where the Pliva River meets the Vrbas. It is best known for two things: the spectacular [Jajce Waterfall](/europe/bosniaandherzegovina/jajce/jajce_waterfall) that drops 21 metres right in the centre of town, and its role in Yugoslav history — the Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia was formally founded here in 1943 at the Second Session of AVNOJ.

The old town is compact and walkable. Narrow cobblestone streets climb to the [Jajce Citadel](/europe/bosniaandherzegovina/jajce/jajce_citadel), which offers wide views over the valley. Below the walls you will find the [Catacombs of Jajce](/europe/bosniaandherzegovina/jajce/catacombs_of_jajce), a Roman-era underground chapel carved into the rock, and the [Jajce Mithraeum](/europe/bosniaandherzegovina/jajce/jajce_mithraeum), a temple to the god Mithras. The [AVNOJ Museum](/europe/bosniaandherzegovina/jajce/avnoj_museum) documenting the wartime session is still open, though it saw damage during the 1990s conflict.

Outside the walls, the Pliva Lakes are the other highlight. The [Pliva Watermills](/europe/bosniaandherzegovina/jajce/pliva_watermills) — a string of small wooden mills on stilts between the two lakes — are one of the most photographed scenes in Bosnia. The lakes and surrounding forest make for easy half-day walks, and the whole area is dramatically green in summer.
