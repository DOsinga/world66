  On 29 November 1943, the Anti-Fascist Council of National Liberation of Yugoslavia (AVNOJ) held its second session in Jajce and formally proclaimed the foundations of socialist Yugoslavia. The date — 29 November — became Yugoslavia's national day, celebrated for decades until the country's dissolution. Jajce was chosen partly for its remoteness from German-controlled territory; Tito's partisans held the town briefly and used the occasion to establish political legitimacy.

The AVNOJ Museum occupies the building where the Second Session of the Anti-Fascist Council of National Liberation of Yugoslavia was held in November 1943 — the meeting at which the foundations of the Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia were formally laid. The session was convened by Josip Broz Tito while the Partisan resistance controlled this part of Bosnia, and it gave the Yugoslav communist movement its political legitimacy as a governing authority rather than merely a resistance force.

The museum suffered damage during the 1990s war and has been partially restored. Exhibits include photographs, documents, and period artefacts from the wartime session. For visitors interested in 20th-century Balkan history, this is a genuinely significant site — the room where one of Europe's largest socialist states was effectively founded. The building is in the lower old town, easy to find.
