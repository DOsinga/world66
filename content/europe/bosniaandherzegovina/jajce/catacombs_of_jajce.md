  The catacombs served as the mausoleum of the Bosnian dukes (vojvoda) of the Kosača family in the 15th century, just before the Ottoman conquest. The precise origins are debated — some scholars believe the space was adapted from an earlier Roman or early Christian structure. The Franciscans who maintained a presence in Jajce through the Ottoman period are thought to have kept the catacombs intact.

The Catacombs of Jajce are a small but remarkable underground chapel carved into the rock beneath the old town. Entered through an unassuming doorway near the main street, the catacombs consist of a narrow passage leading to a vaulted underground chamber with carved stone niches. The atmosphere is genuinely ancient and a little eerie — bare stone walls, low ceilings, and the sense of being somewhere people have prayed for six hundred years.

The catacombs are open to visitors and can be combined easily with the nearby Jajce Mithraeum. They are small — a visit takes fifteen minutes — but the historical density of the site justifies including them in any tour of the old town. A local guide can be arranged through the town's tourist office; the context they provide is worth the small cost.
