  Jajce was the last royal capital of the independent Bosnian Kingdom. In 1463, Ottoman forces besieged the town. After a fierce resistance, the citadel fell — marking the end of medieval Bosnia as a sovereign state. The Ottomans held Jajce for decades before eventually being pushed out by Hungarian forces, making it a contested prize throughout the late 15th century.

The Jajce Citadel crowns a steep pyramidal hill above the town, connected by approximately 1,300 metres of defensive walls that encircle the old city. The fortifications were built by the Bosnian nobility in the 14th and 15th centuries and strengthened during the Ottoman and later Austro-Hungarian periods. The walls, towers, and the citadel itself are in reasonable condition for a medieval fortification that has seen eight centuries of use.

Climbing to the citadel takes about 20 minutes on foot through narrow cobblestone streets. The view from the top is the reward: the Pliva and Vrbas valleys spread out below, the waterfall is visible in the town centre, and on clear days the surrounding mountains frame everything. The climb alone is worth the effort. Entry is charged through a small visitor office near the main gate.
