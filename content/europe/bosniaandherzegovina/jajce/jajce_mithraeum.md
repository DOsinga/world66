  Mithraism was a mystery religion popular among Roman soldiers from the 1st to 4th century AD. The Jajce Mithraeum is a Roman-era underground temple dedicated to the god Mithras, dating to roughly the 2nd or 3rd century AD. It lies beneath the old town and was used by Roman soldiers stationed in the area during the period of Roman control of Illyricum. The main feature is a carved relief of Mithras slaying a bull — the central image of the cult — along with the characteristic cave-like space in which Mithraic rites were conducted.

The mithraeum is modest in size but historically significant: Roman religious sites of this kind are rare in Bosnia. It sits close to the catacombs and can be visited in the same half-hour circuit of the old town's underground monuments. The site is accessed through a door below street level; hours and access arrangements should be confirmed with the local tourist office, as they occasionally coordinate joint entry with the catacombs.
