  There were once over twenty watermills on the wooden stilts between the two Pliva Lakes. The Pliva Watermills are a cluster of small wooden mills built on stilts over the narrow channel between the two Pliva Lakes, about two kilometres from the old town. The mills date from the Austro-Hungarian period and were used by local farmers to grind grain. The sight of a dozen wooden huts on stilts above still water, with the lake and forested hills behind them, is one of the most photographed scenes in Bosnia — and deservedly so. It's genuinely beautiful.

The mills are accessible on foot or by car from Jajce town. A short walking path leads around the channel and past the mills. The surrounding area is pleasant for a stroll: the Pliva Lakes are calm and green, the forest comes down to the water, and there are picnic spots along the bank. Allow about an hour for the round trip from town, more if you want to sit and enjoy the lakes.
