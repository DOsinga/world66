  The museum began as a book: journalist Jasmin Mujanović asked 100 Sarajevans born in 1980 to describe their war childhood in a single sentence. The resulting collection was so powerful that it was adapted into a museum. The original Sarajevo museum then spawned exhibitions in Ukraine, Israel, Palestine, and New York — the format traveling to wherever childhoods are currently being destroyed.
---

The War Childhood Museum occupies a compact space in the city centre and does something unusual: it presents the 1992–1995 siege through the recollections and objects of people who experienced it as children. A skipping rope. A cassette tape. A bottle of nail polish. Each object is accompanied by a testimony from its owner explaining what it meant to them during the siege.

The result is not a museum of atrocities — it is a museum of resilience and ordinary life under extraordinary conditions. Children played, fell in love, did homework, and grew up during the siege. The exhibits make clear that people are not defined entirely by the violence visited upon them. This makes it, paradoxically, one of the more quietly devastating museums in Sarajevo. Allow 90 minutes. The museum is small but the material requires time.
