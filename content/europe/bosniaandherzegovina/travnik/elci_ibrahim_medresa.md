The Elči Ibrahim-pašina Medresa, built in 1706, is one of the oldest and most important Islamic schools in Bosnia — a two-storey building in a distinctly Moorish-influenced architectural style, with arched colonnades, carved stone details, and a fountain courtyard. It stands beside the road to Plava Voda and is still operating as an educational institution today.

The architecture is striking for the region — more Andalusian than Balkan in character, reflecting the varied cultural influences that flowed through Travnik during its years as the seat of the Ottoman governors. Visitors can generally view the exterior; access to the courtyard depends on whether classes are in session.
