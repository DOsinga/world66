Zenica is Bosnia & Herzegovina's fourth-largest city, an industrial hub sitting in a broad valley along the Bosna River, about 70 km north of Sarajevo. It's not a typical tourist destination — the city's identity was shaped by its massive steelworks, which defined much of the 20th century here — but it has a pleasant old town centre, a handful of museums, and serves as a practical base for exploring central Bosnia.

The town's main square and pedestrian zone have been spruced up in recent years. The [Zenica City Museum](/europe/bosniaandherzegovina/zenica/zenica_city_museum) holds a decent collection covering local history, and the old clock tower ([Sahat Kula](/europe/bosniaandherzegovina/zenica/sahat_kula)) is worth a quick look. The [Bosna River Park](/europe/bosniaandherzegovina/zenica/bosna_river_park) is good for a walk. Zenica is also a gateway for reaching the nearby medieval fortress ruins at [Vranduk](/europe/bosniaandherzegovina/zenica/vranduk_fortress), about 12 km north.
