Belogradchik is a small town in northwest Bulgaria, best known for the extraordinary rock formations that surround it. The [Belogradchik Rocks](/europe/bulgaria/belogradchik/belogradchik_rocks) are sandstone and conglomerate formations rising up to 200 metres, carved by erosion into towers, pillars, and figures that local legend has given names — "The Madonna", "The Monks", "The Bear". The setting is genuinely dramatic.

Built into the rocks is the [Belogradchik Fortress](/europe/bulgaria/belogradchik/belogradchik_fortress), one of the best-preserved medieval fortresses in Bulgaria. The Romans laid the original foundations; the Bulgarians and then the Ottomans expanded it over centuries. The walls and towers integrate seamlessly with the rock faces — it's an impressive piece of military engineering. Combined with a walk among the rock formations, a visit takes a half-day.

The town itself is small and unhurried. There are a handful of guesthouses and restaurants. A short drive northeast brings you to [Kozarnika Cave](/europe/bulgaria/belogradchik/kozarnika_cave), one of Europe's most significant prehistoric sites. Most visitors come as a day trip from [Vidin](/europe/bulgaria/vidin) (about 50 km to the north) or as a stop on the way between Sofia and the Danube.
