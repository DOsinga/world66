The most important religious site in Bulgaria and one of the most visited in the Balkans. The Rila Monastery was founded in the 10th century by St. Ivan Rilski — a hermit who retreated to the Rila Mountains and gathered disciples — and has been rebuilt multiple times after fires, most recently and most spectacularly in the early 19th century. The current monastery complex, with its striped arches, painted frescoes, and central Hrelyo Tower (the only medieval structure to survive), is among the most beautiful architectural ensembles in the Balkans.

The monastery became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1983. During the Ottoman period it served as a centre of Bulgarian literacy and cultural preservation — manuscripts, school, and church tradition maintained when much else was suppressed. The frescoes in the Nativity Church are the centrepiece: hundreds of Biblical scenes painted between 1840 and 1848 by the master Zahari Zograf, covering every inch of the church's portico. The monastery is 45 km northeast of Blagoevgrad; a public bus runs from the city bus station in the morning and returns in the afternoon.

story: "The Rila Monastery houses the remains of St. Ivan Rilski in a reliquary in the main church. Ivan was a 10th-century Bulgarian nobleman who renounced his wealth and spent decades as a hermit in increasingly remote mountain caves. His tomb became a pilgrimage site within his lifetime; the monastery that grew around it has been continuously inhabited for more than a thousand years."
