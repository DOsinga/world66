Several of Bojentsi's finest Bulgarian Renaissance houses operate as small museums, and they are worth the modest entrance fee. The interiors are remarkably well-preserved, with original carved wooden ceilings, wooden floors, traditional furniture, and domestic objects of the 18th and 19th centuries. The carving is particularly fine — local craftsmen were famous across the Ottoman Balkans for their woodworking.

The most visited is the house attributed to a local merchant family, which retains its full complement of original fittings including the wooden icon corner, the carved niches for bedding, and the central hearth arrangement. A second house museum documents the lives of notable individuals born in Bojentsi — the village has produced a disproportionate number of Bulgarian scholars, artists, and public figures, a fact that locals note with justified pride. The museums are open daily in summer and on weekends in the off-season; hours vary and it is worth checking locally on arrival.
