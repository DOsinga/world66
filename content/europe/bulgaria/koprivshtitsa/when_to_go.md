---
title: When to Go
type: section
---

Late spring (May–June) and early autumn (September–October) are the best times to visit. The weather is mild, the crowds are manageable, and the wooded valley around the town is at its most attractive. Summer brings day-trippers from Sofia, and the narrow lanes can feel crowded on weekends.

The International Folk Festival is held every five years in August, drawing folk ensembles from across Bulgaria to perform on the hillsides outside town. It is one of the most significant gatherings of Bulgarian traditional music and dance, with tens of thousands of visitors attending over two days. The next edition is worth planning around if the timing aligns — check the Bulgarian Folk Festival schedule for the current year. When it happens, accommodation books out months in advance.

Winter is quiet and cold. The mountains around Koprivshtitsa get snow, which gives the painted houses a striking backdrop, but most visitors find the short days and closed museums less rewarding. The town doesn't shut down entirely, but several smaller museums reduce their hours.

April 20 — the anniversary of the 1876 uprising — draws commemorations each year, with wreath-laying and cultural events at the main monuments. A good day to visit if you want to see the town's pride in its history on open display.
