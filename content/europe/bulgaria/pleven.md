Pleven is a northern Bulgarian city whose name is tied to one of the decisive sieges of the Russo-Turkish War of 1877-78. That history is still the main reason to stop: the [Pleven Panorama](/europe/bulgaria/pleven/pleven_panorama), Skobelev Park, and the museum collections explain why this otherwise workaday city mattered far beyond Bulgaria.

The centre is easy to walk, with a long pedestrian street, fountains, and enough cafes to break up the museums. South of town, [Kaylaka Park](/europe/bulgaria/pleven/kaylaka_park) changes the mood completely: limestone cliffs, reservoirs, paths, and the remains of Storgosia fortress.

Pleven is not as pretty as Veliko Tarnovo or as lively as Plovdiv, but it has a strong identity and enough real anchors for a day. It is especially worthwhile if you are following Bulgarian liberation history or crossing the north between Sofia, Ruse, and the Danube.
