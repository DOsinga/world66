Nebet Tepe is the northern end of the Old Town and the reason there is a city here at all. It is a rocky outcrop with the ruins of successive fortifications on top — Thracian foundations, then Greek, Roman and Byzantine walls built on the same line, plus the remains of cisterns and a secret passage down to the river. The ruins are unfenced and you walk over them.

Come for the view, which is the best free one in Plovdiv: the Maritsa river to the north, the Rhodope foothills to the south, the tiled roofs of the Old Town immediately below, and Bunardzhik hill with the [Alyosha Monument](/europe/bulgaria/plovdiv/alyosha_monument) on the skyline. It is the standard sunset spot, so at that hour it is full of students with beer from the kiosk at the bottom.

It is a stiff ten-minute walk up cobbles from [Hisar Kapia](/europe/bulgaria/plovdiv/hisar_kapia), and there is nothing at the top but the ruins and the view. No entrance fee, no gate, open whenever.
