Smolyan is the largest town in the Rhodope Mountains — a long, narrow settlement stretched along the Arda River valley at around 1,000 metres altitude, with a centre that feels surprisingly substantial given its remote position. It was created in the 1960s by merging several older villages into a single administrative unit, which gives it an oddly elongated urban plan, but the town has a good museum, a cathedral, and serves as the practical base for exploring the central Rhodopes.

The **Smolyan Historical Museum** contains the finest collection of Rhodope artefacts in Bulgaria: traditional costumes, copper vessels, musical instruments, and prehistoric findings from the region's ancient Thracian and later Bulgarian settlements. The **Planetarium and Observatory**, the largest in Bulgaria, sits on the hill above the town and is worth a visit if you have children in tow. The **Smolyan Lakes**, a short distance east of the town centre, are a local walking destination, with a pleasant trail along the chain of small glacial lakes through beech forest.

The real reason to base yourself in Smolyan is what lies around it. [Pamporovo](/europe/bulgaria/rhodopemountains/smolyan/pamporovo) is 18 kilometres to the north; the [Trigrad Gorge](/europe/bulgaria/rhodopemountains/smolyan/trigrad_gorge) is 40 kilometres west. The village of [Shiroka Laka](/europe/bulgaria/rhodopemountains/smolyan/shiroka_laka) is 25 kilometres away. With a hired car, all of these are day trips from Smolyan, and the driving through the forested valleys is itself an attraction.
