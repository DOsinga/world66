  The Kaliopa House was built in 1864 for a wealthy Greek merchant family and takes its popular name from the daughter of the last owner, who died unmarried in the house and whose story became part of local legend. The Bulgarian name Kaliopa (after the Greek Muse Calliope) gave the building a romantic character that the city museum played up when it took over the property. The interior was restored to its appearance in the 1870s and 1880s — the peak of Ruse's prosperity as a Danube trading city.

The Kaliopa House, officially the Urban Lifestyle of Ruse Museum, is a mid-19th-century merchant townhouse preserved to show how the prosperous bourgeois families of Ruse lived during the city's most fashionable decades. The rooms are furnished with original period pieces: European furniture imported via the Danube, decorated porcelain, embroidered textiles, and the kind of Viennese clock that would have been the pride of any well-off household in 1870s Bulgaria.

The house is on a quiet street north of Freedom Square and is often overlooked by visitors who stick to the main sights. It is small — a visit takes 40 minutes — but gives the clearest picture of the Ruse that earned its European reputation. The garden has been restored with period plantings.
