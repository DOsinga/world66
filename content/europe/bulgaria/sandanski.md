Sandanski sits in the valley of the Sandanska Bistritsa river at the foot of the Pirin mountains in southwestern [Bulgaria](/europe/bulgaria). It has the warmest climate in the country — mild winters, long autumns, and over 2,400 hours of sunshine a year — and has been known as a health resort since antiquity.

The town's [mineral springs](/europe/bulgaria/sandanski/mineral_springs) are the main draw: hot, slightly mineralised water surfaces throughout two thermal zones, and several spa hotels and public baths make use of it. The climate and the clean mountain air have made Sandanski a centre for treating respiratory conditions since the Roman period.

Modern Sandanski was built directly over the ancient city of [Parthicopolis](/europe/bulgaria/sandanski/parthicopolis), and floor mosaics and foundations are visible in the central park. The [Sandanski Archaeological Museum](/europe/bulgaria/sandanski/archaeological_museum) holds the best finds from the excavations. The [city park](/europe/bulgaria/sandanski/city_park) is the hub of town life, with a large [statue of Spartacus](/europe/bulgaria/sandanski/spartacus_monument) at its heart — the town claims him as a local son, with some historical plausibility given that Spartacus was Thracian and this valley was Thracian territory.

For travellers, the town is a convenient stop on the road between [Sofia](/europe/bulgaria/sofia) and the Greek border at Kulata. The [Pirin mountains](/europe/bulgaria/sandanski/pirin_hiking) rise directly behind the town, and Melnik — Bulgaria's smallest town, famous for its wine and sandstone pyramids — is only 20 km south.
