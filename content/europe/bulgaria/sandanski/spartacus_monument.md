  Sandanski's claim to Spartacus is disputed but persistent: several ancient sources say the slave who led the great Roman slave revolt of 73–71 BCE was a Thracian, and some historians have placed his origin in the Struma valley near modern Sandanski. The town has embraced this connection enthusiastically — the town was itself named Sveti Vrach until 1947, when it was renamed after Yane Sandanski, a Macedonian revolutionary, but the Spartacus connection predates and outlasts the Communist era.

A large bronze statue of Spartacus stands in the centre of Sandanski, placed here partly for the plausible claim that the great slave rebel was from this area. Spartacus led the most significant slave revolt in Roman history from 73–71 BCE, eluding Roman armies for nearly two years before his final defeat. The monument is dramatic and well-executed.

Whether Spartacus was actually from Sandanski's immediate area is impossible to verify, but the Thracian origin of the historic Spartacus is well-established, and the Struma valley was Thracian territory. The monument has become a local pride point and a useful orientation landmark in the town centre.
