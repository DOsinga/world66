The Defenders of Stara Zagora Memorial Complex stands on a hill at the southern edge of the city centre, commemorating the Battle of Stara Zagora in July 1877. Russian forces and Bulgarian volunteer fighters held off a massive Ottoman army for several days, allowing civilians to flee before the city was overwhelmed and burned to the ground. The battle was a significant moment in the Russo-Turkish War that ultimately led to Bulgarian independence.

The monument itself is a substantial work of Soviet-era monumental architecture: a large stone plinth topped with a steel figure group, surrounded by reliefs depicting scenes from the battle. It is not subtle, but it is powerful — the scale and material are deliberately imposing. The surrounding park offers views over the rebuilt city grid. A small museum within the complex covers the war and the battle in more detail, with maps, weapons, and photographs of the city before and after.
