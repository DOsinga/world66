Tutrakan sits on the Bulgarian bank of the Danube, halfway between Ruse and Silistra. It is small, quiet, and not polished for international tourism, but it has a stronger story than many larger towns: Roman frontier walls, old fishermen's lanes, Danube boatbuilding, and the battlefield where one of Bulgaria's defining First World War victories was fought.

The riverside is the best place to start. Around Hristo Botev Park you can walk between the [Transmarisca ruins](/europe/bulgaria/tutrakan/transmarisca), the [History Museum](/europe/bulgaria/tutrakan/history_museum), and the [Museum of Danube Fishing and Boatbuilding](/europe/bulgaria/tutrakan/museum_of_danube_fishing_and_boatbuilding). The town still feels tied to the river, even if the old steamboat and fishing economy has faded.

The sharper edge of Tutrakan's history is south of town at the [Tutrakan Military Cemetery](/europe/bulgaria/tutrakan/tutrakan_military_cemetery). Bulgarian, German, Romanian, and Ottoman soldiers killed in the 1916 Battle of Turtucaia are buried there, with cannons, memorials, and a small museum on the old battlefield. If you are travelling the Danube road between Ruse and Silistra, Tutrakan is worth a deliberate half-day rather than a fuel stop.
