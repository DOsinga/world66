At the western end of the Valley of the Roses, about 65 km from Kazanlak, sits the town of Karlovo. It is the birthplace of Vasil Levski — the revolutionary who organised the Bulgarian struggle against the Ottomans in the 1860s and who is the closest thing Bulgaria has to a universally revered national hero. His childhood home is now a museum.

Karlovo is smaller and quieter than Kazanlak, and the old town is more atmospheric — cobbled streets, National Revival houses, the 19th-century Sucha Reka mosque, and a parish church with a fine iconostasis. It is also a centre of rose-oil distillation; the Karlovo cooperative is one of the country's largest producers. If you are working your way along the valley, Karlovo makes a logical lunch stop or an alternative overnight base.
