A Thracian burial mound near the town of Shipka, dating from the late 4th century BCE. The tomb chamber inside is unusual — it was carved from a single block of granite, with a ceiling painted in geometric panels and a portrait of a woman whose identity is unknown. The chamber's heavy granite cap weighs around 60 tonnes.

The mound sits within a wider necropolis sometimes called the **Valley of the Thracian Kings** — dozens of burial tumuli scattered across the plain between Shipka and Kazanlak. Several others, including the Golyama Kosmatka tomb where the gold mask of King Seuthes III was found, are accessible to visitors. A combined ticket from the Kazanlak museum covers several of the tombs.
