The Shipka Pass crosses the central Balkan range at 1,150 metres, the most famous of Bulgaria's mountain passes. In August 1877, during the Russo-Turkish War, a small force of Russian and Bulgarian volunteers held the pass against overwhelming Ottoman attacks — a battle that has become central to Bulgaria's national story of independence.

A stone tower memorial, the Monument of Freedom, stands at the top of the pass; you climb a long flight of steps to reach it, then a further set inside to a viewing platform with sweeping views down both sides of the mountains. At the foot of the climb is the Shipka Memorial Church with its golden onion domes, built in the 1880s in memory of the dead. The road from Kazanlak passes through the town of [Shipka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shipka_(town)) and on up; the Buzludzha turn-off is a few kilometres past the summit.
