The Roman Thermae of Varna are one of the largest surviving Roman structures in Bulgaria. Built in the 2nd–3rd century AD when the city was called Odessus, the bath complex covered around 7,000 square metres and incorporated the standard sequence of Roman bathing rooms: frigidarium (cold room), tepidarium (warm room), and caldarium (hot room), along with exercise areas and changing rooms. The scale makes clear that ancient Odessus was a prosperous city — this was an investment in public civic life on a considerable scale.

The ruins are well-presented and open to the public. You can walk through the surviving walls and vaulted chambers, with good interpretive signage in Bulgarian and English. The structure stands in a quiet residential area a short walk from the centre — it's not overwhelmed by tourist infrastructure, which makes for a more atmospheric visit than you might expect. Go early in the morning or late in the afternoon for the best light through the ruined arches.
