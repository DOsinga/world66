Veliko Turnovo (also spelled Tarnovo or Trnovo) was the capital of the Second Bulgarian Empire from the 12th to 14th centuries, and it still feels like it. The town is built on steep slopes above a horseshoe bend in the Yantra River, with old houses cascading down the hillsides and the [Tsarevets](/europe/bulgaria/velikoturnovo/tsarevets) fortress rising on a rocky promontory at the centre of it all.

[Tsarevets](/europe/bulgaria/velikoturnovo/tsarevets) is the main reason to come: the ruins of the medieval royal palace and the Patriarchal Cathedral, with reconstructed walls and towers, cover the entire hilltop. Across the river, [Trapezitsa](/europe/bulgaria/velikoturnovo/trapezitsa) — the nobility's fortress — is less visited but equally rewarding. The views over the Yantra gorge from either hill are excellent. In summer, a sound-and-light show is projected onto the Tsarevets walls in the evenings — touristy, but genuinely dramatic.

The old merchants' quarter of [Samovodska Charshia](/europe/bulgaria/velikoturnovo/samovodska_charshia) has been partially restored, with craft workshops selling pottery, woodcarving, and leatherwork. The [Church of the Holy Forty Martyrs](/europe/bulgaria/velikoturnovo/holy_forty_martyrs) in the Asenov quarter is a 13th-century church built to commemorate a Bulgarian military victory, with inscribed columns that are among the most important medieval texts in the Bulgarian language.

Veliko Turnovo is a university town, which keeps it lively out of season. It's a good base for the surrounding region — [Arbanasi](/europe/bulgaria/velikoturnovo/arbanasi) village 4km away has spectacular frescoed churches from the 17th century, and the monastery circuits around the Yantra valley are worth a day or two. About 240km from Sofia by road; 4–5 hours by bus or train.
