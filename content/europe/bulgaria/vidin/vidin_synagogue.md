A monumental 19th-century synagogue, the second largest in Bulgaria after the Sofia synagogue. The neo-Moorish facade with twin towers and a rose window still stands intact; the interior was a shell of broken plaster and rubble for decades. Long-delayed restoration is now underway, with the building being converted into a cultural centre.

Even half-restored, the building is one of the most striking sights in Vidin. It sits a few minutes from the river in what was once the Jewish quarter, near the [Krastata Kazarma](/europe/bulgaria/vidin/krastata_kazarma) and the old town. Check at the site whether the interior is currently accessible; access has been intermittent during the restoration.
