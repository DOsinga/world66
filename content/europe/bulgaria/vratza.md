Vratsa is a Bulgarian city of around 60,000 people tucked against the foothills of the Vratsa Balkan, a steep limestone range that rises dramatically behind the city centre. It sits in northwestern Bulgaria, about 110 kilometres north of Sofia on the road toward Vidin and the Danube. The mountain backdrop is not decorative — it is the reason most visitors come here.

The main draw is Vratsata, a narrow gorge that cuts through the cliffs immediately west of the city. The rock faces on either side rise 200 to 400 metres and have made Vratsa one of Bulgaria's premier climbing destinations, with more than 70 marked routes ranging from easy walls to serious overhangs. Each summer the gorge hosts national alpinism competitions. Paragliding launches from the upper ridges are also popular, and cycling and hiking trails connect the gorge to the Vrachanski Balkan Nature Park above.

In the city itself, the main cultural attraction is the Historical Museum, which holds an exceptional collection of Thracian treasures from the region — gold and silver objects, a golden laurel wreath, gilt greaves — alongside artefacts from the Neolithic through the Bulgarian Revival. The attached Ethnographic Complex, spread across five historic houses around a central courtyard, shows how a prosperous Bulgarian family lived in the 19th century. The cave of Ledenika, 16 kilometres west in the mountains, is one of Bulgaria's more dramatic show caves, with temperatures dropping below freezing in the entrance chamber in winter.
