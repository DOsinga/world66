St Peter Port is one of the most handsome harbour towns in the British Isles. The capital of Guernsey climbs steeply from a working waterfront lined with yachts and ferry terminals, its lanes of granite townhouses rising toward Castle Cornet, a medieval fortress that has guarded the harbour since the 13th century. Stand on the quayside on a clear day and the French coast is visible on the horizon.

The town has real character — Norman street names, a French-inflected café culture, and pubs that feel genuinely local rather than tourist-facing. Victor Hugo spent fourteen years in exile here, and [Hauteville House](/europe/channelislands/guernsey/st_peter_port/hauteville_house) is the town's most distinctive literary landmark, decorated by Hugo himself in an eccentric style entirely his own. He wrote *Les Misérables* here.

[Castle Cornet](/europe/channelislands/guernsey/st_peter_port/castle_cornet) juts into the harbour on its own island and contains four museums; its noon gun ceremony, fired by scarlet-clad keepers, has run for centuries. The [Guernsey Museum](/europe/channelislands/guernsey/st_peter_port/guernsey_museum) in [Candie Gardens](/europe/channelislands/guernsey/st_peter_port/candie_gardens) covers the island's history with particular attention to the German Occupation of 1940–45 — the closest Germany came to occupying British soil. The [La Valette Underground Military Museum](/europe/channelislands/guernsey/st_peter_port/la_valette_museum), in the actual German tunnels, goes further into that story.

The [covered market](/europe/channelislands/guernsey/st_peter_port/harbour_and_market) in the town centre is worth a visit for local produce: Guernsey butter, fresh crab, and the island's rich dairy products. Ferries to [Herm](/europe/channelislands/herm) and [Sark](/europe/channelislands/sark) depart from the harbour, making St Peter Port a natural base for exploring the smaller islands.
