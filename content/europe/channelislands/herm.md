Herm is tiny — a mile and a half long and half a mile wide — and entirely car-free. It sits three miles east of St Peter Port, Guernsey, and the fast catamaran crossing takes about 20 minutes. Around 60 people live here year-round; in summer day-trippers arrive to walk the [clifftop paths](/europe/channelislands/herm/cliff_path), swim off [Shell Beach](/europe/channelislands/herm/shell_beach) on the north end, and eat at the island's single pub, the [Mermaid Tavern](/europe/channelislands/herm/mermaid_tavern).

There are no crowds in any meaningful sense. The island is privately leased from the States of Guernsey and managed as a single estate. There is a small shop, a pub, and an 11th-century [chapel](/europe/channelislands/herm/st_tuguals_chapel). That is more or less everything.

Shell Beach itself is worth the trip — the beach is composed almost entirely of broken shells carried in by the Atlantic rather than sand, and the water in summer is clear and relatively warm. The [southern cliffs](/europe/channelislands/herm/cliff_path) offer views across to [Sark](/europe/channelislands/sark) and the private island of [Jethou](/europe/channelislands/herm/jethou). If you are based in [Guernsey](/europe/channelislands/guernsey) and looking for a quiet half-day, Herm is an easy and rewarding excursion.
