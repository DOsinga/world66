St Helier is the capital of [Jersey](/europe/channelislands/jersey) and home to about a third of the island's population. It sits on the south coast around a natural harbour, with [Elizabeth Castle](/europe/channelislands/jersey/sthelier/elizabeth_castle) standing on a tidal island at the entrance — accessible on foot at low tide or by amphibious ferry when the water comes in.

The town centre is compact and walkable, built around a pedestrianised high street and the covered [Central Market](/europe/channelislands/jersey/sthelier/central_market), a Victorian iron-and-glass hall filled with flower stalls, delis, and a good cafe. King Street and Queen Street have the usual chain shops, but the real finds are in the side streets — antique dealers, independent bookshops, and a few proper Jersey cider outlets.

Jersey sits outside the EU and VAT does not apply, which makes some goods noticeably cheaper. The harbour area has been redeveloped with restaurants and a waterfront promenade. For history, the [Jersey Museum](/europe/channelislands/jersey/sthelier/jersey_museum) on Pier Road covers everything from Neolithic Jersey to the German Occupation, and [16 New Street](/europe/channelislands/jersey/sthelier/16_new_street) preserves a Georgian merchant's townhouse almost exactly as it was left in the mid-1800s. [Royal Square](/europe/channelislands/jersey/sthelier/royal_square), where the island's parliament sits, is where Charles II was proclaimed King in 1649. Liberation Square, where the Jersey Museum stands, commemorates the island's liberation from Nazi occupation on 9 May 1945.
