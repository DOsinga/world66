Baška is a popular resort town on the southern tip of Krk, one of the largest islands in the Croatian Adriatic. It sits in a sheltered bay surrounded by vineyards, olive groves, and rocky mountains, with the islet of Prvić providing natural protection from the open sea.

The main draw is [Vela Plaža](/europe/croatia/baska/vela_plaza) (Great Beach), a 1,800-metre pebble beach right in the centre of town — one of the finest on the Adriatic and holder of the European Blue Flag since 1999. The beach is especially good for children and non-swimmers. Beyond the main beach, around thirty smaller pebble beaches can be reached on foot or by boat along the coastline.

Baška also has genuine cultural significance. The Baška Tablet, a stone inscription from around 1100 AD written in the oldest known form of Croatian Glagolitic script, was found in the nearby [Church of St. Lucy](/europe/croatia/baska/church_of_st_lucy) at Jurandvor. It is one of the most important documents in Croatian history and now resides in the Croatian Academy in Zagreb, though a replica is displayed locally.

Behind the town the karst rises steeply: short walks lead up to the pilgrimage [Shrine of Our Lady of Gorica](/europe/croatia/baska/shrine_of_our_lady_of_gorica) and longer ones up to [Obzova](/europe/croatia/baska/obzova), the highest point on Krk. Across the bay, uninhabited Prvić islet shields the harbour and is topped by the small [Stražica Lighthouse](/europe/croatia/baska/strazica_lighthouse).
