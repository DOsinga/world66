A deep, fjord-like bay on the north coast containing one of Brač's most beautiful harbour towns, and the home of the island's famous stone-carving school. Brački kamen — the fine-grained white limestone quarried on the island — has been used in Diocletian's Palace in Split, the White House in Washington, and the United Nations building in New York. The Pučišća school still trains stone masons in the old methods.

The town itself has a lovely waterfront with Renaissance and Baroque palaces reflected in the still bay. It is far less visited than Bol and retains an everyday working feel.
