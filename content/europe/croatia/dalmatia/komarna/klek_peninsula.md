The narrow Klek peninsula juts into the Adriatic just south of Komarna, technically belonging to Bosnia and Herzegovina as part of the tiny Neum corridor that splits Croatia in two. From the coast road above Komarna, several pullouts give a clear view across the bay to its low, scrubby ridge.

There is little to see on the peninsula itself; the interest is geopolitical and aesthetic. The line of demarcation cuts across the bay at an unusual angle that has been disputed for centuries, and the new Pelješac Bridge was built partly to allow Croatian traffic to bypass Klek and the Neum strip altogether. A glass of wine at sunset, with the Klek hills in silhouette, is the easiest way to take it in.
