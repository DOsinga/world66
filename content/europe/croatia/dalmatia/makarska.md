Makarska is the main town of the Makarska Riviera, on the Dalmatian coast about 60 kilometres southeast of [Split](/europe/croatia/split). The town sits at the foot of the Biokovo massif — a dramatic limestone range that rises sharply from the sea — giving it one of the most striking backdrops on the Croatian coast. The old town is centred on the Baroque [Kačić Square](/europe/croatia/dalmatia/makarska/kacic_square) with its [Church of St Mark](/europe/croatia/dalmatia/makarska/st_marks_church); just west the 16th-century [Franciscan monastery](/europe/croatia/dalmatia/makarska/franciscan_monastery) hides an unexpectedly serious seashell museum.

The Makarska Riviera extends roughly 50 kilometres, with a string of pebble and shingle beaches sheltered by the mountains. The [Biokovo Nature Park](/europe/croatia/dalmatia/makarska/biokovo_park) above the town has hiking trails and the [glass-floored Skywalk](/europe/croatia/dalmatia/makarska/biokovo_skywalk) cantilevered out over the cliff with views across to the islands of [Hvar](/europe/croatia/hvar) and Brač. Makarska is a lively resort town in summer, popular with both domestic and international visitors, with a good waterfront [Riva](/europe/croatia/dalmatia/makarska/riva), open-air bars, and seafood restaurants. Out of town the hidden [Nugal beach](/europe/croatia/dalmatia/makarska/nugal_beach) is the most dramatic swim on the coast.
