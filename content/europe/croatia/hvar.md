South of [Brač](/europe/croatia/brac) and easily reached from [Split](/europe/croatia/split), Hvar is the longest island in the Adriatic and one of the sunniest places in Croatia. The island is covered with vineyards, olive groves, and [lavender fields](/europe/croatia/hvar/lavender_fields) that scent the summer air — a crop that has shaped the island's economy and identity since the early 20th century. The local [Plavac Mali wine](/europe/croatia/hvar/hvar_wine) is among the best in Dalmatia.

[Hvar Town](/europe/croatia/hvar_town), at the western end, is the glamorous face of the island, with the liveliest restaurant and bar scene on the Dalmatian islands in summer. Quieter [Stari Grad](/europe/croatia/stari_grad), connected to the mainland by the main ferry, is the most architecturally significant town. Further east, [Vrboska](/europe/croatia/vrboska) and [Jelsa](/europe/croatia/jelsa) are quieter resort towns popular with families.

The [Pakleni Islands](/europe/croatia/hvar/pakleni_islands), just offshore from Hvar Town, offer secluded swimming, naturism, and good seafood konobas. The inland village of [Velo Grablje](/europe/croatia/hvar/velo_grablje), mostly abandoned but revived by a lavender festival each June, is worth a scooter trip.
