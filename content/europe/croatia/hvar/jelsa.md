Jelsa is the third-largest town on the island, on the north coast about halfway between Stari Grad and the eastern tip. Less glossy than Hvar Town and less self-consciously preserved than Stari Grad, it is the place to come if you want the island feel without the cruise-ship crowds.

The harbour is small and ringed with cafes; the parish church of St Mary, an unusual fortified Renaissance building, sits on a small hill behind the seafront. Walks lead out from the harbour to a handful of small bays — Mina Beach is the most popular, a 20-minute stroll east along a pine-shaded path. A water taxi from the harbour can take you across to Bol on Brač for a half-day excursion.
