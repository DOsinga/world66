Three kilometres northwest of Jelsa, Vrboska is a tiny village built along a narrow sea inlet so convoluted that the main street is itself a series of stone bridges. You can walk here in under an hour along the coastal path, or cycle it in twenty minutes. The village has two main attractions: the fortress church of St Mary, one of the most striking defensive churches on the Dalmatian coast, and the fishing museum in an old warehouse on the canal, which traces the island's sardine-canning history.

Vrboska sees far fewer visitors than either Jelsa or Stari Grad, which makes it worth the short trip. The harbour is small enough that you can take it all in from a single bench, and the bakery near the bridge produces fresh bread until noon. It is a very easy addition to a morning in Jelsa.
