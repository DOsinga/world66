  The Blue Cave's extraordinary colour effect depends on a precisely timed window. The sunlight enters through a submerged side entrance in the rock, refracts off the white limestone floor, and turns the water and everything in it an electric cobalt blue. This effect is at its strongest between 11am and noon from May to September; on cloudy days or outside those hours the cave is just a dark chamber.
---

The Blue Cave on the island of Biševo, about 5 km southwest of Komiža, is the most dramatic natural sight on Vis island and one of the finest sea caves in the Mediterranean. Excursion boats depart from Komiža harbour every morning in summer (typically from 8am), take visitors to the cave for a short swim-through in a small dinghy, and often include other stops around Biševo and the nearby islands.

The trips run from May through September and typically take four to five hours in total. Individual spots in the cave can only be reached by small dinghy in groups of three or four, which means queuing in the outer bay is common in peak season. Early morning trips are shorter on queues; the light is best between 10am and noon.
