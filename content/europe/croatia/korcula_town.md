Korčula town is a fortified medieval settlement jutting on a small peninsula into the Pelješac Channel, the main settlement on the island that shares its name. It is often described as a miniature Dubrovnik, and the comparison holds up: the same honey-coloured limestone walls, the same narrow lanes herringboned off a central axis, but far smaller and without the crowds.

Korčulans claim Marco Polo was born here, and [the house](/europe/croatia/korcula_town/marco_polos_house) traditionally identified as his birthplace is open to the public. The claim is contested, but the setting is compelling enough that you stop caring after a while.

[The Cathedral of St Mark](/europe/croatia/korcula_town/st_marks_cathedral_tower) dominates the old town. Next to it the Bishop's Treasury holds a surprisingly rich collection of Croatian and Italian Renaissance art, manuscripts, and ecclesiastical vestments. [The Town Museum](/europe/croatia/korcula_town/town_museum_korcula), installed in the 16th-century Palazzo Gabrielis, covers the island's long maritime history.
