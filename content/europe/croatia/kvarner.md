The Kvarner Gulf is the northernmost bay of the Croatian Adriatic, sheltered behind the islands of Krk, Cres, Lošinj, and Rab. It was the favoured seaside retreat of the Austro-Hungarian aristocracy in the late nineteenth century, and that history still shows in the grand villas and promenades of [Opatija](/europe/croatia/opatija), just around the headland from the working port city of [Rijeka](/europe/croatia/rijeka).

The gulf's islands are its main draw. [Krk](/europe/croatia/krkisland), the largest and only one linked to the mainland by bridge, has a walled medieval town, the cliffside wine village of Vrbnik, and the beach resort of Baška. [Rab](/europe/croatia/rabisland) has a perfectly preserved old town crowned by four bell towers and some of the only proper sandy beaches on the Croatian coast, concentrated around the village of Lopar. [Lošinj](/europe/croatia/losinjisland), linked by bridge to Cres, has been a health resort since the Habsburg era and today is known for its dolphin research and mild climate.

Away from the coast, the small ferry village of [Jablanac](/europe/croatia/jablanac) and the shipbuilding town of [Kraljevica](/europe/croatia/kraljevica) sit along the mainland shore, while inland [Gorski Kotar](/europe/croatia/gorski_kotar) rises into forested highland — a cool, green contrast to the coast, with karst caves and the Risnjak massif.
