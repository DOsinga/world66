The Narona Museum is unusual: instead of moving the finds to a town hall, the building was constructed directly over the archaeological pit, so visitors walk above the original temple floor and look down on sixteen Roman statues standing where they were placed in the 1st century AD. The collection includes Augustus, his wife Livia, several of their children, and a number of later imperial figures — one of the largest surviving groups of family statuary from any Roman provincial site.

The displays cover not only the Augusteum but the wider city of Narona, founded by Greek traders in the 4th century BC and developed into a substantial Roman port. Glass, ceramics, jewellery, and architectural fragments fill the upper galleries. The site is in the village of Vid, about 4 km north-west of Metković, and is open Tuesday to Sunday with shorter hours in winter.
