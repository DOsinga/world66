---
title: Books About Pag and Dalmatia
type: section
---

**Black Lamb and Grey Falcon** by Rebecca West is the essential book on the former Yugoslavia, written after a journey through the country in 1937. The sections on Dalmatia — including the islands — are among the finest travel writing of the 20th century: dense, opinionated, and alive to the layers of history that produced the landscape you see. It is a long book but no other account of the region comes close.

**Croatia: A History** by Ivo Goldstein is a useful single-volume account of Croatian history from antiquity to independence, providing context for everything from the medieval stone towns to the traumatic 1990s war. Not bedside reading, but a good reference for anyone wanting to understand the history behind the landscape.

**The Radetzky March** by Joseph Roth, set in the twilight of the Habsburg Empire, is fiction but gives an extraordinarily vivid sense of the cultural world that shaped Dalmatia before 1918 — when the coast was part of the Austro-Hungarian empire and places like Pag were administratively Italian-speaking. The novel is set elsewhere but the atmosphere it evokes is directly relevant to what you will see in the stone towns of the Dalmatian islands.
