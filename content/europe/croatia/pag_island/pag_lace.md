**Paška čipka** — Pag needle lace — was inscribed on UNESCO's List of Intangible Cultural Heritage in 2009 as one of Croatia's most distinctive craft traditions. The lace is made by a declining community of women in Pag Town using a technique unique to the island: a single needle builds up the intricate geometric and floral patterns without any foundation fabric, purely from thread. The finished pieces are extraordinarily fine and take months to complete.

The **Museum of Pag Lace** (*Muzej paške čipke*) on the main square of Pag Town displays historical and contemporary examples, explains the technique through photographs and tools, and occasionally has lacemakers working in the courtyard. Finished pieces are available for sale — price reflects the months of labour required, and genuine hand-made paška čipka is neither cheap nor a souvenir to impulse-buy, but it is an extraordinary craft object. The museum is small but well-run; admission is low.
