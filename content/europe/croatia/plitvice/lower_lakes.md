  The turquoise and emerald colours of the Plitvice lakes are entirely natural and change visibly throughout the day as cloud shadows move across the water. The pigmentation is caused by calcium carbonate and magnesium carbonate in the water — when light hits at certain angles, the water absorbs red light and reflects blue-green. In spring, algae bloom adds a green tint; in summer the lakes appear a more brilliant turquoise. Photographers who visit multiple times say no two visits look the same.
---

The Lower Lakes are the most dramatic part of the Plitvice system — a sequence of smaller lakes connected by tumbling waterfalls and cascades, all accessible from boardwalks that run along and sometimes over the water. The section between Lake Gavanovac and the Veliki Slap canyon offers the most intense scenery: the boardwalk passes within arm's reach of rushing water, with azure pools visible through the planking beneath your feet.

The Lower Lakes section is reached from Entrance 1, which puts you right into the thick of the action. The boat crossing across Lake Kozjak, which connects the Lower and Upper Lakes sections, is a highlight in its own right — the lake is the largest in the park and the crossing gives a sense of scale that the forest trails don't provide. Visiting on a weekday morning in May or September gives the best balance of good light and manageable crowds.
