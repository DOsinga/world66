The Plitvice Lakes were not immune to the violence of the 1990s Yugoslav breakup. On Easter Sunday 1991 — a date Croatians call "Krvavi Uskrs" (Bloody Easter) — armed clashes between Croatian police and Serbian paramilitaries at the park marked one of the first military engagements of the Croatian War of Independence.

The park was subsequently occupied and closed to visitors from 1991 to 1995, when Croatian forces retook the area in Operation Storm. The recovery of the park's infrastructure and its return to UNESCO status was a symbol of Croatian reconstruction. A small memorial plaque near the administration buildings marks the events of 1991. For most visitors the park's natural beauty is the overwhelming experience, but the recent history adds a layer of meaning — this extraordinarily peaceful place was a battleground within living memory.
