  Veliki Slap (Great Waterfall) stands 78 metres high — Croatia's tallest waterfall. It is made more spectacular by the fact that it can only be seen from across a canyon, with no path to its base. The water comes from the Plitvica stream, which empties into the gorge through an overhanging limestone cliff. In cold winters, the fall freezes entirely — a stunning spectacle that draws photographers from across Europe.
---

Veliki Slap is the centrepiece of the Lower Lakes section and the most photographed single feature in the park. The waterfall drops 78 metres over a sheer travertine cliff into the canyon below, with a permanent rainbow visible in clear morning light. The viewing platform across the canyon gives the best perspective on the full height and volume of the fall.

The walk from Entrance 1 to the Veliki Slap viewpoint takes about 30 minutes along well-maintained boardwalks and stone paths. The best light is in the morning, before the sun moves behind the gorge walls. The surrounding canyon is also home to the Sastavci falls — a collection of smaller cascades where several streams and lakes converge, often with kingfishers visible along the lower reaches.
