Porec sits halfway up the western coast of [Istria](/europe/croatia/istria), a small town built on a narrow peninsula that juts into the Adriatic. Its Roman street grid is still intact -- you can walk the original [Decumanus](/europe/croatia/porec/decumanus), the main east-west street, and Cardo Maximus crossing it at right angles, just as they were laid out two thousand years ago.

The town's great treasure is the [Euphrasian Basilica](/europe/croatia/porec/euphrasian_basilica), a 6th-century Byzantine church whose golden mosaics rival anything in Ravenna. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and reason enough to visit. But Porec is more than its basilica. The old town is a compact maze of Venetian and Romanesque buildings, with cafe terraces spilling onto medieval squares like [Marafor](/europe/croatia/porec/marafor_square) -- the old Roman forum -- and fishing boats bobbing in the harbour below. Medieval fortifications survive in the [Pentagonal Tower](/europe/croatia/porec/pentagonal_tower) and [Round Tower](/europe/croatia/porec/round_tower).

Beyond the historic centre, Porec is the heart of Istrian coastal tourism. The beaches stretch north and south, backed by pine forests and resort complexes; [Zelena Laguna](/europe/croatia/porec/zelena_laguna) is the most popular, and the small island of [Sveti Nikola](/europe/croatia/porec/sveti_nikola) is a short hop across the harbour. The surrounding countryside -- rolling hills dotted with hilltop villages, vineyards, and olive groves -- is some of the most beautiful in Croatia. The local wines, especially Malvazija, and the Istrian truffles are worth seeking out, and the [Parenzana cycling trail](/europe/croatia/porec/parenzana_trail) along an old Habsburg railway is the easiest way into the interior.
