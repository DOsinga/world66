Split is Croatia's second city and the capital of the Dalmatian coast — a working port that doubles, almost incidentally, as one of the best-preserved Roman sites in Europe. The city has grown up inside and around the walls of [Diocletian's Palace](/europe/croatia/split/diocletians_palace), an enormous 3rd-century imperial retirement complex that is still fully inhabited. Around 3,000 people live inside the palace walls today; the rest of the city has spread along the coast and up the hills behind.

The old town is the main draw: the [Cathedral of St Domnius](/europe/croatia/split/cathedral_st_domnius) — originally Diocletian's mausoleum — and the [underground cellars](/europe/croatia/split/diocletian_cellars) mirroring the palace floor plan, the [statue of Gregory of Nin](/europe/croatia/split/gregory_of_nin) at the Golden Gate, and the narrow lanes between Roman walls where bars and restaurants occupy 1,700-year-old doorways. West of the old town, [Marjan Hill](/europe/croatia/split/marjan_hill) rises above the city in pine forest, with the [Meštrović Gallery](/europe/croatia/split/mestrovic_gallery) — Croatia's finest collection of modern sculpture — on its lower slopes. The Riva waterfront promenade runs along the harbour edge and is the place to sit with a coffee and watch the ferries come and go.

Split is also the main ferry hub for the Dalmatian islands. Brač, Hvar, Vis, Šolta, Korčula, and the coastal town of Dubrovnik are all reachable from the main harbour. The water sports and island-hopping culture is an integral part of life here — the city faces outward to the sea as much as inward to its Roman past.
