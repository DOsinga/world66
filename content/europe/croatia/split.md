Split is Croatia's second city and the capital of the Dalmatian coast — a working port that doubles, almost incidentally, as one of the best-preserved Roman sites in Europe. The city has grown up inside and around the walls of [Diocletian's Palace](/europe/croatia/split/diocletians_palace), an enormous 3rd-century imperial retirement complex that is still fully inhabited. Around 3,000 people live inside the palace walls today; the rest of the city has spread along the coast and up the hills behind.

The palace itself is the main draw. The [Peristyle](/europe/croatia/split/peristyle), the ceremonial courtyard at its heart, has been the social centre of the city for 1,700 years; the [Cathedral of St Domnius](/europe/croatia/split/cathedral_st_domnius) — originally Diocletian's mausoleum — and the [Temple of Jupiter](/europe/croatia/split/temple_of_jupiter) occupy the western sector; the [underground cellars](/europe/croatia/split/diocletian_cellars) mirror the palace floor plan below; and the [Golden Gate](/europe/croatia/split/golden_gate) on the north side has Ivan Meštrović's enormous bronze statue of [Gregory of Nin](/europe/croatia/split/gregory_of_nin) standing in front of it, his toe polished gold by a thousand superstitious hands. West of the palace, the [Riva promenade](/europe/croatia/split/riva_promenade) runs along the harbour edge, and then [Marjan Hill](/europe/croatia/split/marjan_hill) rises into pine forest with the [Meštrović Gallery](/europe/croatia/split/mestrovic_gallery) on its lower slopes and [Kašjuni beach](/europe/croatia/split/kasjuni_beach) below.

The city's atmosphere comes partly from the ancient bones and partly from the life lived inside them. The [Prokurative](/europe/croatia/split/prokurative) and the old [Croatian National Theatre](/europe/croatia/split/croatian_national_theatre) speak to a civic confidence that goes back to the Austrian and Venetian periods. The fishing neighbourhood of [Varoš](/europe/croatia/split/varos_neighbourhood) immediately outside the western palace gate is the most local-feeling part of the centre. The [Bačvice beach](/europe/croatia/split/bacvice_beach), ten minutes' walk from the old town, is where picigin is played — a waterball game unique to Split. And the utterly inexplicable [Froggyland](/europe/croatia/split/froggyland), a collection of 507 taxidermied frogs in human poses, is one of the most cheerfully strange museums in Europe.

Split is also the main ferry hub for the Dalmatian islands. Brač, [Hvar](/europe/croatia/hvar), Vis, Šolta, Korčula, and [Dubrovnik](/europe/croatia/dubrovnik) by catamaran are all reachable from the main harbour. The Roman ruins of ancient [Salona](/europe/croatia/split/salona_ruins) are a short bus ride north; the UNESCO-listed medieval town of [Trogir](/europe/croatia/trogir) is 30 minutes west.
