Stari Grad — "old town" in Croatian — was founded as the Greek colony of Pharos in 384 BC, making it one of the oldest continuously inhabited towns in Europe. It is the architectural heart of Hvar island, a quiet stone town tucked at the end of a long, deep inlet on the north coast. The narrow lanes wind back from the harbour past Renaissance houses, small piazzas with lemon trees, and the heavy stone walls of [Tvrdalj Castle](/europe/croatia/stari_grad/tvrdalj_castle), the fortified summer residence of the poet Petar Hektorović.

Tvrdalj is the headline visit — Hektorović's sixteenth-century house arranged around a saltwater fish pond, with quotations from Latin and Croatian carved into the walls. The Dominican Monastery, just across the way, holds a small but startling Tintoretto and a collection of archaeological finds from the Greek period. Behind the town, the [Stari Grad Plain](/europe/croatia/stari_grad/stari_grad_plain) preserves the same rectangular field divisions the Greek settlers laid out in the fourth century BC, unchanged for over two millennia and inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage site. Allow half a day in town, or stay the night.
