  The Cathedral of St James in Šibenik is the only major Gothic-Renaissance cathedral in Europe built entirely of stone — no brick, no mortar. Juraj Dalmatinac began the project in 1431 and solved the structural problems of a stone dome and stone barrel vaults over a wide nave in ways that were not matched elsewhere for decades. The 71 portrait heads carved around the exterior apse represent the citizens of 15th-century Šibenik and give the building an unusual human quality for a Gothic cathedral.

Šibenik, 50 km north of Trogir on the central Dalmatian coast, has a dense and well-preserved old town that most visitors pass through on the way to Krka National Park but which rewards more time. The Cathedral of St James — a UNESCO World Heritage Site — is the centrepiece, but the medieval fortifications above the town (St Michael's Fortress and St Nicholas's Fortress) offer fine views over the city, the estuary, and the islands offshore.

The bus between Trogir and Šibenik takes about an hour. Combine Šibenik with a morning at Krka for a full day out: the park entrance at Skradin is a short bus or taxi ride from Šibenik's bus station.
