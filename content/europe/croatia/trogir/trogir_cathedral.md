  The west portal of the Cathedral of St. Lawrence, carved by the master sculptor Radovan and completed in 1240, is considered the finest example of Romanesque-Gothic sculpture in all of Croatia. The two lions at the base support columns that in turn support figures of Adam and Eve — both depicted with visible shame, their hands raised to their faces. Radovan signed his work on the lintel, one of the earliest known artist signatures in Croatian art.

The Cathedral of St. Lawrence is the centrepiece of Trogir's UNESCO-protected old town and one of the finest medieval churches on the Adriatic coast. Construction spanned four centuries, from the 13th to the 17th, and the result is a building that moves through Romanesque, Gothic, and Renaissance styles as you walk around it. The bell tower is a particularly fine piece of late Gothic work, with elaborate stone tracery and a Renaissance loggia at the base.

The interior houses the Chapel of St. John of Trogir, a Renaissance masterpiece by Nicola di Giovanni Fiorentino completed around 1468. The chapel's carved reliefs, vaulted ceiling, and stone altarpiece are extraordinary — and because Trogir is far less visited than Dubrovnik or Split, you can often stand in the chapel alone, which is a luxury that comparable spaces in more famous cities do not afford. Admission to the cathedral and the bell tower is charged separately; climbing the tower gives a fine view over the terracotta rooftops of the island town.
