Trpanj sits at the quiet north-western end of the [Pelješac peninsula](/europe/croatia/peljesac), and the whole peninsula opens up from here. The obvious excursion is inland into the wine country: the southern slopes around Potomje, Trstenik, and Janjina are the home of Dingač and Postup, Croatia's most celebrated reds, made from the Plavac Mali grape on near-vertical, sun-baked terraces above the sea.

A loop drive from Trpanj takes a half-day, threading through the Potomje tunnel to the seaward vineyards and stopping at a handful of family cellars — tastings are generally informal and possible without booking outside the busiest weeks of August. From the same road you can carry on down the spine of the peninsula toward [Orebić](/europe/croatia/peljesac/orebic) and the captains' houses, or catch the ferry across to Ploče on the mainland for a longer push toward the Neretva delta.
