The southern slope of the Pelješac peninsula, half an hour's drive inland from Trpanj, is the most famous red-wine territory in Croatia. The Dingač and Postup vineyards cling to almost-vertical south-facing slopes above the sea — a brutal terrain that produces concentrated, deep wines from the indigenous Plavac Mali grape (the parent of California Zinfandel).

A loop drive from Trpanj through Potomje (where the road tunnels through the ridge to the seaward vineyards) takes a half-day with stops at several family wineries — Bartulović, Madirazza, Skaramuča, Saints Hills. Tastings are generally informal and available without booking outside the busiest weeks of August.
