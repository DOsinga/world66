Zadar is a 3,000-year-old city on the Dalmatian coast, and for centuries it was the capital of the entire region. The old town sits on a narrow peninsula, still laid out on its ancient Roman street grid. A [Roman forum](/europe/croatia/zadar/roman_forum) — the largest on the eastern Adriatic — anchors the center, and the 9th-century [Church of St. Donatus](/europe/croatia/zadar/church_of_st_donatus) stands beside it, a round pre-Romanesque structure that has become the city's signature landmark.

What sets Zadar apart from other Croatian coastal cities is its willingness to mix old and new. The [Sea Organ](/europe/croatia/zadar/sea_organ), built into the waterfront steps in 2005, plays haunting chords as waves push air through underwater pipes. Nearby, the [Sun Salutation](/europe/croatia/zadar/sun_salutation) — a circle of glass solar panels — lights up in patterns after dark. These installations sit comfortably alongside medieval churches and the Venetian-era [Land Gate](/europe/croatia/zadar/land_gate).

Alfred Hitchcock reportedly said Zadar had the most beautiful sunset in the world, and the west-facing waterfront does put on a show. The city is less overrun than [Dubrovnik](/europe/croatia/dubrovnik) or [Split](/europe/croatia/split), with better prices and a more relaxed pace. It also makes a good base for visiting [Plitvice Lakes](/europe/croatia/plitvice) and the Kornati Islands.
