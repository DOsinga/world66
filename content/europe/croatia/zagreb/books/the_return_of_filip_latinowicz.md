Krleža is the giant of Croatian 20th-century literature — a Habsburg-era leftist, friend and then antagonist of Tito — and this 1932 novel is his most accessible to outside readers. Filip Latinowicz, a painter returning to Croatia after twenty years abroad, walks the streets of an unnamed but unmistakable Zagreb and grapples with provincial inertia, family secrets, and the cul-de-sac of Central European modernism. A more literary, demanding way into the city than guidebook prose.
