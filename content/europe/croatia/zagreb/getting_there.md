---
title: "Getting There"
type: section
---

**By air.** Franjo Tuđman Airport (ZAG) sits 17 kilometres southeast of the city, with direct flights from most major European hubs. A frequent shuttle bus runs to the central bus station (around half an hour); taxis cost roughly €25.

**By train.** Zagreb Glavni Kolodvor — the central station, at the foot of Tomislav Square — is the country's main railway hub. International trains run to Vienna, Budapest, Munich, Ljubljana, and Belgrade; domestic trains to Split, Rijeka, and Osijek. The interior of the station, with its high glass roof and 19th-century iron platforms, is itself worth a look.

**By bus.** The main bus station (Autobusni kolodvor), a kilometre southeast of the centre, runs a dense network of long-distance buses to every Croatian city as well as Sarajevo, Belgrade, Mostar, Vienna, and Munich. Buses are often faster than trains for Croatian destinations.

**By car.** Croatia's motorway network converges on Zagreb: the A1 south to Split, the A3 east toward Belgrade, the A4 north toward Hungary, the A2 west toward Slovenia. Tolls are payable.
