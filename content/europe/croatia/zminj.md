Žminj is a small hilltop town in central [Istria](/europe/croatia/istria), about 15 kilometres south of Pazin. First mentioned in 1177, it sits on the old road between Pazin and Pula and has the quiet, unhurried feel of inland Istria — stone houses, a parish church, and views over rolling countryside.

The historic core is built around the remains of a medieval [castle](/europe/croatia/zminj/zminj_castle) and the baroque [parish church of Saint Michael](/europe/croatia/zminj/parish_church_st_michael), with its older freestanding bell tower. The main draw nearby is the [Feštinsko Kraljevstvo](/europe/croatia/zminj/festinsko_kraljevstvo) cave, a stalactite-filled cavern discovered accidentally in 1930 when a vineyard owner's dog fell through a hole in the ground. The cave stayed hidden through the war — it sheltered locals from the German occupation — and was only opened to visitors much later.

Time your visit around 24 August for the [Bartolja](/europe/croatia/zminj/bartolic_folk_festival), one of the oldest folk festivals in Istria, when the main square fills with traditional roženice music and Teran wine.
