The Adršpach-Teplice Rocks (Adršpašsko-teplické skály) are an extraordinary labyrinth of sandstone pillars, gorges, and narrow defiles in the hills of northeastern [Bohemia](/europe/czechrepublic), about 40 km west of [Náchod](/europe/czechrepublic/nachod). Wind and water have carved the soft rock over millions of years into towers that rise a hundred metres and more, with names earned from their silhouettes — the Mayor, the Lovers, the Sugar Loaf. It is the largest rock town in central Europe and a protected nature reserve.

The area splits into two connected sections. The Adršpach Rocks, reached from the village and train halt of the same name, hold the most dramatic scenery: a marked circuit threads between sheer walls, past a small lake where you can take a short boat ride through a flooded gorge, and up to viewpoints over the stone forest. The Teplice Rocks, a few kilometres south, are quieter and wilder, with the deep, cool Wolf Gorge (Vlčí rokle) running between them. A long trail links the two, making a full and rewarding day on foot.

The rocks have drawn visitors since the Romantic era — Goethe came, and the place has stood in for fairy-tale wilderness in more than one film. Spring and autumn are the best times; summer weekends can be crowded, and the narrow passages bottleneck. Wear proper shoes, as the paths involve stairs, ladders, and damp stone, and bring a layer, because the gorges hold cold air even in high summer.

Both sections are easy to reach by train from Náchod and Trutnov, and there is parking and a cluster of refreshment stands at the Adršpach entrance. For walkers based in Náchod, this is the standout excursion in the region.
