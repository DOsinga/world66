Františkovy Lázně is the smallest and neatest corner of the West Bohemian Spa Triangle. Where Karlovy Vary is grand and theatrical, Františkovy Lázně is controlled, pale yellow, and almost geometric: a planned spa town of colonnades, pavilions, villas, and level walks through parks.

The town was founded in 1793 around mineral springs and later became part of the UNESCO-listed Great Spa Towns of Europe. That status is not just a badge. The appeal here is the whole spa landscape: the [Glauber Springs Hall](/europe/czechrepublic/frantiskovy_lazne/glauber_springs_hall), spring pavilions, gardens, and calm promenades designed for slow therapeutic walking.

Františkovy Lázně is not a place for a packed checklist. Come for a quiet day from Cheb or Mariánské Lázně, drink from the springs if you are curious, walk the parks, and let the town's slightly formal rhythm do its work.
