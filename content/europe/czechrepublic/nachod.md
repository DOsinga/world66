Náchod is a pleasant town in northeastern Bohemia right on the Polish border. The surroundings are beautiful — rolling hills, forests, and a frontier feel. It's not a major destination but makes a good base for exploring the region, particularly for those interested in Czech inter-war military history.

Above the town stands [Náchod Castle](/europe/czechrepublic/nachod/nachod_castle), a well-preserved Renaissance château that dominates the skyline. [Masaryk Square](/europe/czechrepublic/nachod/masaryk_square) below is picturesque and unhurried, lined with arcaded burgher houses.

The area around Náchod contains some of the most significant Czech border fortifications from the 1930s. The [Březinka](/europe/czechrepublic/nachod/brezinka_blockhouse) infantry blockhouse, built as part of Czechoslovakia's defensive line against potential German attack, has been enthusiastically restored by volunteers and opens daily in summer. The larger [Dobrošov Fortress](/europe/czechrepublic/nachod/dobrosov_fortress) is a few kilometres south, with extensive underground galleries to tour. Several monuments mark the [Battle of Náchod](/europe/czechrepublic/nachod/battle_of_nachod_monument) of 1866 on the ridge east of town.

Across the border in Poland, about 10 km away, is the spa town of Kudowa-Zdrój, worth a short excursion for its mineral springs, its park colonnades, and the unsettling [Skull Chapel](/europe/czechrepublic/nachod/skull_chapel) in the suburb of Czermna.
