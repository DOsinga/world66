Olomouc is one of the most underrated cities in Czechia. The historic center rivals Brno and even parts of Prague for architectural beauty, but it sees a fraction of the tourists. It was the capital of Moravia until the Thirty Years' War and retains a strong sense of its own history.

The town is known for its [Baroque fountains](/europe/czechrepublic/olomouc/olomouc_baroque_fountains) — six Baroque fountains and one art nouveau fountain, the most in the Czech Republic outside Prague. The [Holy Trinity Column](/europe/czechrepublic/olomouc/holy_trinity_column) on the [main square](/europe/czechrepublic/olomouc/horni_namesti) is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and the [Astronomical Clock](/europe/czechrepublic/olomouc/olomouc_astronomical_clock) set into the town hall has the unique distinction of being remade by socialist realists — saints replaced by workers and peasants.

Olomouc also has one of the oldest universities in Central Europe (founded 1573), which gives the city a lively student atmosphere and keeps it from feeling like a museum piece. Above the river, [Saint Wenceslas Cathedral](/europe/czechrepublic/olomouc/saint_wenceslas_cathedral) and the [castle complex](/europe/czechrepublic/olomouc/olomouc_castle) mark the spot where the last Přemyslid king was assassinated in 1306.

A local specialty: Olomoucké tvarůžky, a pungent fermented cheese with a very strong smell. It's an acquired taste, but locals are proud of it.
