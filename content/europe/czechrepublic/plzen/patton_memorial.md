  one of the few Czech cities liberated by the West rather than the Soviet Red Army. The Soviets

  liberation by American troops was simply not mentioned in Czech schools or public life. After 1989,
  Plzeň reclaimed the history, and Liberation Day (5 May) is now marked each year with a parade of
  period vehicles and American veterans, one of the largest such events in Europe.

The Patton Memorial Pilsen (Památník Patton Memorial Pilsen) is a small but well-curated museum dedicated to the American liberation of Western Bohemia in 1945. It occupies a converted building near the city centre and tells the story of General Patton's 3rd Army, the liberation of Plzeň on 6 May 1945, and the subsequent Communist-era suppression of that history.

The exhibition covers the full sweep of the Second World War in Western Bohemia — occupation, resistance, and liberation — through photographs, documents, personal testimonies, and military artefacts. There is a particular focus on the human dimension: stories of individual soldiers and civilians, and the bittersweet aftermath of liberation in a country that then fell behind the Iron Curtain within three years. The museum is more thoughtful and less triumphalist than many military museums, reflecting the complicated legacy of liberation that led to another form of occupation. Text is in Czech and English. The museum is closed Mondays.
