The twin Gothic spires of the Týn Church have loomed over Old Town Square since 1385, their dark, slightly menacing silhouette one of the defining images of Prague. They rise to 80 metres, slightly asymmetrical — legend says the taller one represents Adam, the shorter Eve. The church is entered from behind, through a passage beneath the arcaded building fronting the square.

Inside, the Gothic space is relatively plain compared to Prague's Baroque churches, which is part of its appeal: ribbed vaulting, old stone, a few good altarpieces. The tomb of Tycho Brahe, the Danish astronomer who served Rudolf II and died in Prague in 1601, is marked by a worn floor slab near the entrance. The church is active and not always open to visitors; check the current hours.
