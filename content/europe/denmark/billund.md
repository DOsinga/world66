Billund is a small town in central Jutland, Denmark, with a population of around 6,000. It is almost entirely defined by two things: Legoland (the original, opened 1968) and Billund Airport, which is Denmark's second-busiest airport and serves most of Jutland. The Lego Group has its global headquarters here and the town exists largely to service the park and its visitors.

[Legoland Billund](/europe/denmark/billund/legoland_billund) was the world's first Legoland when it opened in June 1968. The park features Miniland — a scale model of cities and landmarks built from millions of Lego bricks — alongside rides, shows, and themed areas for children up to around 12 years old. The adjacent [Lalandia](/europe/denmark/billund/lalandia_billund) resort (opened 2009) adds an indoor water park, bowling, and family accommodation. Together they make Billund a viable two- or three-day family destination.

The [Lego House](/europe/denmark/billund/lego_house), opened in 2017 and designed by Bjarke Ingels Group, is a separate attraction in the town centre aimed at older children and design enthusiasts. It is worth a visit even if you skip Legoland.

Outside the parks, Billund is unremarkable — a corporate headquarters town in flat Jutland. The interest is in the surrounding region: [Jelling](/europe/denmark/billund/jelling_stones) (15km north) has Viking rune stones and a UNESCO World Heritage church that are among the most important monuments in Scandinavia; Vejle (30km east) is a more conventionally attractive town with good restaurants and a fjord setting; [Givskud Zoo](/europe/denmark/billund/givskud_zoo) (20km west) is a large safari park. [Koldinghus](/europe/denmark/billund/koldinghus), a dramatic medieval castle in Kolding (50km east), is worth the drive if you have a car.
