The Church of Our Saviour in Christianshavn is famous for its golden corkscrew spire, which winds up 90 metres and is climbed on an external staircase open to visitors. The views from the top are among the best in Copenhagen — the harbour, the old city, the flat Danish landscape stretching in every direction.

The church itself, built in the late 17th century, is worth visiting for its baroque interior: a tiered wooden organ case, white plasterwork, and a massive ornamented altarpiece. But most people come for the spire. The external staircase is only open in summer and closes in bad weather — the last section, near the globe, is exposed and not for those uncomfortable with heights.
