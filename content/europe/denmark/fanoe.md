Fanø is a small island lying just off the coast of Esbjerg, reached by a twelve-minute ferry crossing from Esbjerg harbour. At 56 square kilometres it is one of the smaller Danish islands, but it has a character that belies its size — a combination of wide western beaches, a protected Wadden Sea coast, and two towns with very different personalities.

Nordby, near the ferry landing in the north, is the main town: compact, colourful, and well supplied with cafes and summer rental cottages. The beaches a short walk or cycle to the west are wide, flat, and Atlantic-facing — among the longest in Denmark, with low dunes and a horizon that stretches uninterrupted to Britain. Kitesurfers and windsurfers use the beach throughout the summer, and the tidal flats on the Wadden Sea side are part of the UNESCO-listed Wadden Sea National Park.

Sønderho, at the southern tip, is the more compelling destination. It is one of the best-preserved Wadden Sea villages in Denmark — a cluster of low, thatched houses on narrow lanes, with a distinctive local building style and almost no modern intrusion. The Sønderho Mill, the old inn, and the 18th-century church are the landmarks; the real experience is the lanes themselves, the gardens, and the quietness compared to the busier north.

Fanø also maintains one of Denmark's most distinctive regional folk costumes — an elaborate, originally seafaring community's dress worn at festivals. The Fannikerne, as the islanders are called, were historically fishermen and sailors; the costume reflects a community that had its own money and its own identity long before package tourism arrived.

The ferry from Esbjerg runs frequently through the day and takes twelve minutes. Bring bikes — the island is almost entirely flat and easy to cycle end to end in a day.
