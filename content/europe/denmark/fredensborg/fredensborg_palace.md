Fredensborg Palace was built in the 1720s for King Frederik IV and remains one of the Danish royal family's active residences. It is not a museum-palace in the usual sense; most of the year you see it from the forecourt and garden, which makes the place feel oddly alive. Guards, closed gates, and clipped avenues remind you that it still has a job.

Guided tours of parts of the palace and the private garden usually run in July, when the royal family is away. Outside that window, the best experience is the view across the forecourt and a walk through the public garden. It is a modest detour from Hillerød, but a rewarding one if you like royal Denmark without the heavy crowds.
