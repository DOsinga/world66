Frederiksberg is a municipality entirely surrounded by Copenhagen, though it is technically an independent municipality and not part of the city. From a visitor's perspective the distinction is invisible: Frederiksberg is a wealthy, gracious inner suburb that feels like a seamless part of central Copenhagen, with wide tree-lined avenues, elegant apartment buildings, and an excellent collection of parks and cultural institutions.

[Frederiksberg Gardens](/europe/denmark/frederiksberg/frederiksberg_gardens) (Frederiksberg Have) is one of the finest landscape gardens in Denmark, running up to the [Frederiksberg Palace](/europe/denmark/frederiksberg/frederiksberg_palace) — a Baroque royal summer residence now used as a military academy, with gardens open to the public. The [Copenhagen Zoo](/europe/denmark/frederiksberg/copenhagen_zoo) is at the southern edge of the park, and the two make a natural combination visit.

[Cisternerne](/europe/denmark/frederiksberg/cisternerne), a former Victorian water reservoir beneath the adjoining Søndermarken park, has been converted into one of Copenhagen's most unusual contemporary art venues — a vast, dimly lit underground space with water on the floor where visitors are given Wellington boots. The [Storm P. Museum](/europe/denmark/frederiksberg/storm_p_museum) is a charming small museum dedicated to Denmark's most beloved humorist. [Frederiksberg Allé](/europe/denmark/frederiksberg/frederiksberg_alle) is the best street to eat and drink in the municipality.

Frederiksberg is a natural base for visiting Copenhagen, with good metro connections and a slightly more residential, quieter atmosphere than the most central areas of the city.
