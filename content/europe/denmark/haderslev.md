Haderslev is a small port town in southern Jutland, sitting at the head of the Haderslev Fjord. Most travellers pass through on the train — it is a rail junction — but if you step off, the town rewards a few hours of wandering.

The [Haderslev Cathedral](/europe/denmark/haderslev/haderslev_domkirke) (Haderslev Domkirke) is the main draw, a Gothic brick church with origins in the 13th century. The [Haderslev Museum](/europe/denmark/haderslev/haderslev_museum) covers the archaeology of southern Jutland and the complex national question of this borderland — the region changed hands between Denmark and Germany three times between 1864 and 1945, and the town bears the marks of that history. The [ruins of Haderslevhus castle](/europe/denmark/haderslev/haderslevhus) sit on a hill above the [Haderslev Dam](/europe/denmark/haderslev/haderslev_dam), a medieval lake that runs through the centre of town and makes for pleasant walking.
