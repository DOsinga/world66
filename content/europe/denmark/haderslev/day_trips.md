---
title: "Day Trips"
type: section
---

Haderslev makes a good base for exploring the layered history of South Jutland, a region that changed hands between Denmark and Germany three times between 1864 and 1945.

Ribe, about 45 kilometres west, is one of the oldest towns in Scandinavia and well worth a day. Its Viking-age origins are documented in the excellent Ribe VikingeCenter open-air museum, and the medieval cathedral dominates the flat marsh landscape for miles around.

Kolding, 40 kilometres north, has Koldinghus — a partially ruined royal castle with a striking modern interior — and a pleasant historic centre. The Trapholt design museum, just outside town, is among the best of its kind in Denmark.

The Dybbøl battlefield, near Sønderborg about 40 kilometres south-east, is one of the most significant sites in Danish national memory: it was here in 1864 that Prussian and Austrian forces defeated the Danish army, leading to the loss of Schleswig-Holstein. A well-designed museum and the preserved mill make it a moving visit. Sønderborg itself has a fine castle on the fjord.

For nature, the Wadden Sea National Park begins west of Ribe and extends south along the coast to the German border — flat, stark, and extraordinary at low tide.
