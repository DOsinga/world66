---
title: "Haderslev Dam"
type: poi
---

Haderslev Dam is the long, narrow lake that curves through the centre of the town — one of the most distinctive features of any Danish market town. It was formed in the medieval period by damming the Haderslev stream, originally to power mills, and has shaped the layout of the town ever since. The lake stretches for roughly three kilometres and is bordered by a mix of parkland, old merchant houses, and wooded slopes.

A well-maintained footpath follows the lake shore for most of its length, making it the most popular walk in town. On summer evenings the paths are busy with locals cycling, jogging, and walking dogs. The far eastern end of the lake is quieter, shaded by mature trees, and good for birdwatching: ducks and coots are permanent residents and grey herons are often seen standing motionless at the water's edge. In winter the lake sometimes freezes, and when it does the town turns out to skate.

The dam itself — the actual weir at the western end, near the old town centre — is worth a look. The surrounding area has been landscaped and the views back towards the cathedral and the old rooflines above the water are among the best in town.
