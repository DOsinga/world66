Helsingør — known to English speakers as Elsinore — sits at Denmark's narrowest point, where the Sound separates it from Helsingborg in Sweden by just four kilometres of water. It's been a strategically vital crossing since the medieval period, and [Kronborg Castle](/europe/denmark/helsingor/kronborg_castle), which guards the strait, is one of the most impressive Renaissance fortresses in northern Europe. Shakespeare set Hamlet here, though he probably never visited — the play cemented the castle's fame far beyond Scandinavia.

[Kronborg](/europe/denmark/helsingor/kronborg_castle) (a UNESCO World Heritage Site) is the obvious draw, but the town itself rewards a few hours. The [old quarter around Stengade](/europe/denmark/helsingor/stengade) has some fine 16th-century merchant houses, and the [M/S Maritime Museum of Denmark](/europe/denmark/helsingor/ms_maritime_museum) — built underground inside the old dry dock next to the castle — is genuinely excellent. The [frequent ferry crossing to Helsingborg](/europe/denmark/helsingor/helsingborg_ferry) makes Helsingør an easy combined trip from Copenhagen, or a convenient gateway into Sweden.
