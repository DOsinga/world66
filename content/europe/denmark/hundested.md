Hundested sits at the far northwest tip of Zealand, where the Kattegat, Isefjord, and Roskilde Fjord squeeze the town into a small working port. It is not polished in the way some Danish seaside towns are, and that helps it: ferries still come and go, fishing boats still work, and the harbour has filled in around them with artists, glassmakers, food stalls, and summer events.

The strongest sight is [Knud Rasmussen's House](/europe/denmark/hundested/knud_rasmussens_house), the former work retreat of the polar explorer, set above the sea near [Spodsbjerg Lighthouse](/europe/denmark/hundested/spodsbjerg_lighthouse). Down at the harbour, [Hundested Havn](/europe/denmark/hundested/hundested_harbour) is the place to wander, eat fish, look into craft workshops, and catch the Rørvig ferry.

Hundested works best as a summer day from North Zealand or as a small stop on a slow coastal trip. The town has enough identity for a spare day: Arctic stories on the cliff, ferries in the harbour, and big temporary sand sculptures that are cheerfully more ambitious than you expect.
