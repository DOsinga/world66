Kolding sits at the head of Kolding Fjord in southeast Jutland, a mid-sized Danish city that most people drive past on the E45 motorway. That is a mistake. The city has one of Denmark's most striking buildings and a pleasant, walkable centre that rewards a half-day stop.

[Koldinghus](/europe/denmark/kolding/koldinghus), the medieval royal castle on the hill above town, dominates the skyline. Originally built in 1268, it was gutted by fire when Napoleon's Spanish troops were garrisoned here in 1808 — the soldiers reportedly lit fires to keep warm and lost control. The ruin stood open to the weather for nearly two centuries before a bold modernist restoration wrapped new spaces inside the old walls. The result is both a museum and an architectural conversation between medieval brick and raw concrete.

Below the castle, the compact old town has good cafes around [Akseltorv](/europe/denmark/kolding/akseltorv) square and along the pedestrianised streets. The [Trapholt](/europe/denmark/kolding/trapholt) museum of modern art and design, 5 km east of town on the fjord shore, is worth the detour for its furniture collection and sculpture park. The [Geografisk Have](/europe/denmark/kolding/geografisk_have) behind the castle holds 2,000 plant species from around the world, laid out by continent — a pleasant spot for a stroll.

Kolding is a natural stop on the way between [Copenhagen](/europe/denmark/copenhagen) and Jutland's west coast, and makes a practical base for visiting [Legoland](/europe/denmark/billund) in Billund, about 40 minutes northwest.
