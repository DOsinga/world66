Langeland — literally "long land" — is a narrow island extending 52 kilometres from north to south off the south coast of Funen. It is one of Denmark's least-altered agricultural landscapes: rolling fields, beech forests, village churches, and a coastline that feels genuinely remote in places.

The main town is [Rudkøbing](/europe/denmark/rudkoebing), a small port with a well-preserved old centre. It was the birthplace of Hans Christian Ørsted, the physicist who discovered electromagnetism, and has a small museum dedicated to him. At the northern tip, [Lohals](/europe/denmark/lohals) is a ferry port and quiet seaside village with a good beach. At the southern end, [Bagenkop](/europe/denmark/bagenkop) sits beside a small harbour used by fishing boats and visiting yachts.

The island is well set up for cycling — the distances are manageable, the terrain is gentle, and the coastal roads are quiet. [Tranekær Castle](/europe/denmark/langeland/trankaer_castle) in the north, one of Denmark's oldest inhabited castles, sits in a landscape park where [TICKON](/europe/denmark/langeland/tickon) has installed over 20 permanent land art works by international artists; both are free to visit. At the southern tip, [Ristinge Klint](/europe/denmark/langeland/ristinge_klint) has chalk cliffs and some of the best birdwatching in the region during migration season. Near [Bagenkop](/europe/denmark/bagenkop), [Langelandsfort](/europe/denmark/bagenkop/langelandsfort) is a superbly preserved Cold War NATO fort — four intact underground bunkers and a submarine on the lawn — that is one of the most compelling Cold War sites in the Baltic.
