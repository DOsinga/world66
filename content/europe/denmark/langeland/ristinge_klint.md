Ristinge Klint is a 2-kilometre stretch of coastal cliff on Langeland's southwestern shore, rising to 28 metres and designated a Geopark geosite of international significance. The exposed cliff face shows sediment layers representing four separate ice advances during the Weichselian glaciation, plus marine and lake deposits from the Eemian interglacial period 130,000–115,000 years ago — one of the clearest records of repeated glacial push structures in the world. Short coastal walks run along the clifftop with views over the Baltic. An important migration watchpoint in spring and autumn.
