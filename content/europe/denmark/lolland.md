Lolland is Denmark's fourth-largest island, lying south of Zealand across the Storstrøm and connected to the rest of the country by bridge. Flat, agricultural, and less visited than its neighbours, it rewards those who come looking for quiet countryside, medieval churches, and a coast that faces south across the Baltic.

The cultural heart is [Maribo](/europe/denmark/maribo), a small cathedral town set on the shores of Maribo Sø, one of Denmark's largest lakes. [Nakskov](/europe/denmark/nakskov) is the main port, once a significant industrial centre and still the base for the ferry to Langeland. At the island's eastern end, [Sakskøbing](/europe/denmark/sakskobing) is a pleasant market town known for its water tower and liquorice factory.

The island rewards slow exploration. [Nakskov Fjord](/europe/denmark/nakskov/nakskov_fjord) is one of Denmark's most scenic — a boat from the town harbour reaches the remote Albuen spit, a lighthouse, and empty beaches. In eastern Lolland, [Aalholm Slot](/europe/denmark/lolland/aalholm_slot) near Nysted claims to be Denmark's oldest continuously inhabited castle, and [Fuglsang Art Museum](/europe/denmark/lolland/fuglsang_art_museum) nearby is worth the detour.

In the late 2020s, Lolland will become the landfall for the Fehmarn Belt Fixed Link, a tunnel under the Baltic connecting Denmark to Germany — the longest immersed tunnel in the world. The construction site at [Rødby](/europe/denmark/rodby) is already visible from the coast road. Once open, the tunnel will bring Lolland into a new geography, linking it directly to Hamburg in under three hours.
