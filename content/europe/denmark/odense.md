Odense is Denmark's third-largest city and the birthplace of Hans Christian Andersen, a fact the city embraces wholeheartedly. The [Hans Christian Andersen Museum](/europe/denmark/odense/hc_andersen_museum), recently rebuilt and expanded by architect Kengo Kuma, is the centrepiece, but Odense has more to offer than fairy tales.

The old city centre is compact and walkable, with cobbled pedestrian streets, small cafes, and the Gothic [St. Canute's Cathedral](/europe/denmark/odense/st_canutes_cathedral) — one of Denmark's finest churches, with a gilded altarpiece by Claus Berg and the remains of King Knud the Holy in the crypt. The [Carl Nielsen Museum](/europe/denmark/odense/carl_nielsen_museum), [Brandts](/europe/denmark/odense/brandts_museum) photographic art centre, the [Danish Railway Museum](/europe/denmark/odense/danish_railway_museum), and the city history museum [Møntergården](/europe/denmark/odense/mntergrden) round out a serious roster of small museums. Odense is also the capital of Funen, the island Danes call "the garden of Denmark" for its gentle, green landscape.

[The Funen Village](/europe/denmark/odense/thefunenvillage), an open-air museum on the outskirts, reconstructs 18th and 19th century rural life with farmhouses, a smithy, a windmill, and working agriculture. It is one of the best of its kind in Scandinavia.
