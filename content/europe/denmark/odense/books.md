Any of Hans Christian Andersen's *Fairy Tales and Stories* is an obvious starting point — the standard English translation by Tiina Nunnally captures the wry, sometimes savage tone of the originals, which is largely lost in the Disneyfied versions most people know. *The Improvisatore*, his most autobiographical novel, gives a more direct sense of the man behind the stories.

For Funen as a place, Johannes V. Jensen's *The Long Journey* — a Nobel-winning prose epic from the 1920s — uses the island and its people as the cradle of Nordic civilisation. And Carsten Jensen's *We, the Drowned*, set partly on Funen and on the seas to which Funen sailors went, is the best Danish novel of recent decades.
