Ærø is a small island off the south coast of Funen, quietly doing things the rest of Denmark is still talking about. It runs almost entirely on renewable energy — wind turbines generate most of the island's electricity, and solar collectors feed hot water directly into the district heating system of centuries-old towns, cobblestones and all.

The main draw is [Ærøskøbing](/europe/denmark/r_southern_fyn/aeroskobing), one of the best-preserved small towns in Northern Europe. The streets are narrow, the timber-framed houses are intact, and many of them are available for weekly rental — you can live in a 300-year-old merchant's house rather than just photograph it. The town has a few small museums, including the [Bottle Peter's Collection](/europe/denmark/r_southern_fyn/bottle_peters_collection), a ship-in-a-bottle collection that sounds odd and turns out to be genuinely absorbing. At the other end of the island, [Marstal](/europe/denmark/r_southern_fyn/marstal) is the old seafaring town, with the excellent [Marstal Maritime Museum](/europe/denmark/r_southern_fyn/marstal_maritime_museum) recording the days when the island's tiny population owned hundreds of ships.

Beyond the towns, the island is good for [cycling](/europe/denmark/r_southern_fyn/cycling_aero), kayaking, and horseback riding. The cliffs of [Voderup Klint](/europe/denmark/r_southern_fyn/voderup_klint) on the south coast and the pastel [beach huts](/europe/denmark/r_southern_fyn/eriksholm_beach_huts) outside Ærøskøbing are the most photographed natural sights. There are campsites along the coast. The pace is slow and the distances are short — the island is about 30 km long and easily covered by bike in a day.

Getting there means a ferry from Svendborg on Funen (about 75 minutes to Ærøskøbing), from Faaborg, or from Fynshav in southern Jutland to [Søby](/europe/denmark/r_southern_fyn/soby) at the island's northern tip. The island is popular in summer; book accommodation well ahead if you want to rent one of the old houses.
