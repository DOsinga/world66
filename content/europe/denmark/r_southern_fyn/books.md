Ærø has produced a surprising amount of literature for an island of fewer than 6,000 people, much of it tied to its seafaring past.

- **We, the Drowned** by Carsten Jensen. A sprawling, multi-generational novel about the sailors of Marstal and the women they leave behind, from the 1840s through the Second World War. It is the great novel of Ærø and one of the great Danish novels of the last twenty years. Read it before you arrive and Marstal will feel like home.
- **The Borrowed Land** (*Lånt jord*) by Erling Jepsen. A darker work set in a small Jutland town in the 1960s, useful as a counterweight to romantic visions of provincial Denmark.
- **Out Stealing Horses** by Per Petterson. Norwegian rather than Danish, but its evocation of long summers in a quiet rural landscape pairs well with a week on Ærø.
