Randers is a mid-sized Danish town on the Gudenå river in eastern Jutland, best known for its tropical zoo. [Randers Regnskov](/europe/denmark/randers/randers_regnskov) (Randers Rainforest) is a domed complex built over the river that houses three climate zones — South America, Africa, and Asia — under interconnected glass domes. It is one of Denmark's most-visited family attractions, and the reason most people stop here.

Beyond the rainforest, Randers has a pleasant old town with half-timbered houses and a pedestrianised shopping street. The medieval [Sankt Mortens Kirke](/europe/denmark/randers/st_martins_church) is worth a visit — this is where the Danish Reformation began in 1526. The [Randers Museum of Art](/europe/denmark/randers/randers_kunstmuseum) holds a solid collection of Danish painting. The Gudenå — Denmark's longest river — flows through town and is good for [kayaking](/europe/denmark/randers/gudenaa_kayaking) downstream toward Randers Fjord. The [Kulturhuset](/europe/denmark/randers/kulturhuset) hosts concerts and exhibitions.

The wildcard is [Memphis Mansion](/europe/denmark/randers/memphis_mansion), a full-scale replica of Graceland built on the edge of town by an ardent Elvis fan — an improbable attraction executed with real dedication.

Randers makes a reasonable stop on a Jutland road trip, well-connected by train to Aarhus (30 minutes south) and Aalborg (an hour north).
