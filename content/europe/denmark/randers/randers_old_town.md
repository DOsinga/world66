story: Randers was an important medieval market town, and its old town preserves a denser concentration of half-timbered houses and cobbled lanes than most Danish towns of its size. The oldest surviving building is the Helligåndshuset (House of the Holy Spirit), a medieval hospital founded around 1490 that is among the finest Gothic brick structures in Jutland.
---

The historic centre of Randers clusters around Storegade and the squares near Sankt Mortens Kirke, with half-timbered houses lining the lanes that run down toward the Gudenå river. Most of the old buildings date from the 17th and 18th centuries, though the street pattern is older. The area is compact and best explored on foot.

Storegade is the main pedestrianised shopping street, lined with both historic buildings and contemporary shops. Side lanes off Storegade reveal quieter courtyards and some of the best-preserved older architecture. The area between the church and the river has the most intact character. A walk through this neighbourhood in the late afternoon, when the light catches the brick and timber, gives a better sense of what a prosperous Jutland market town looked like than the busier tourist towns further south.
