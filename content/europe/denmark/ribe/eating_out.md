---
title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

Ribe is small but eats well. The two famous old town-centre inns — **Sælhunden** ("The Seal") and **Weis' Stue** — have been pouring beer and serving traditional Jutland food since the seventeenth century and are still the most atmospheric places to eat in town. The contemporary scene is led by a handful of restaurants doing modern Danish food with local ingredients from the marshes and the Wadden Sea.

The signature local dish is **lammekølle** — slow-roasted lamb from the salt-meadow flocks that graze the marshes; the salt content of the grass gives the meat a distinctive flavour, particularly in late summer. Wadden Sea **oysters** are another speciality (now sustainably harvested by the Vadehavscentret), as are pickled herring, eel, and lightly smoked Wadden Sea shrimp.

Most kitchens close by 9.30 p.m. Reservations are a good idea on weekend evenings, especially May–September.
