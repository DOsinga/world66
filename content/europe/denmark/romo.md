Rømø is the southernmost of Denmark's Wadden Sea islands, tied to the mainland by a long causeway and facing the North Sea with an absurd amount of sand. The beaches are the reason most people come: hard, flat, and so wide that cars, horses, kites, beach sailors, and walkers can all seem lost in their own private weather system.

The island is more than a beach stop. Rømø sits inside the UNESCO-listed Wadden Sea landscape, where the tides expose mudflats and feeding grounds for migratory birds. On the west coast, [Lakolk Beach](/europe/denmark/romo/lakolk_beach) is the easy swimming beach and the focus of the big kite festival. Farther south, [Sønderstrand](/europe/denmark/romo/sonderstrand) is the place for blokarts, beach sailing, and the sheer scale of the island.

The quieter eastern side carries Rømø's older identity. [The Sea Captain's House](/europe/denmark/romo/sea_captains_house) preserves the prosperous world of eighteenth-century whaling captains, while [St Clement's Church](/europe/denmark/romo/st_clements_church) keeps ship models and sailors' names in a low, weathered building dedicated to the patron saint of seafarers. Rømø is busy in summer, but even then the island has room to breathe.
