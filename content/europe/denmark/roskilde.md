Roskilde was Denmark's capital for most of the Middle Ages, and two landmarks from that era still define the town. The [Viking Ship Museum](/europe/denmark/roskilde/viking_ship_museum) on the harbour displays five original Viking ships recovered from the fjord, where they had been deliberately sunk a thousand years ago to block the waterway. You can watch boat-builders working with period tools in the workshop, and in summer you can sail on a reconstructed Viking longship on [Roskilde Fjord](/europe/denmark/roskilde/roskilde_fjord).

[Roskilde Cathedral](/europe/denmark/roskilde/roskilde_cathedral), a UNESCO World Heritage Site, has been the burial church of Danish monarchs since the fifteenth century — over forty kings and queens rest in its chapels, each in a more elaborate sarcophagus than the last. The cathedral's twin spires dominate the skyline and are visible from the train well before you arrive. Beside it sits [Roskilde Palace](/europe/denmark/roskilde/roskilde_palace), now a contemporary art museum.

The town itself is compact and pleasant, with a good café culture around [Stændertorvet](/europe/denmark/roskilde/stændertorvet), the central square. The [Roskilde Museum](/europe/denmark/roskilde/roskilde_museum) fills in the town's history between the Viking Age and the present. Every June, the [Roskilde Festival](/europe/denmark/roskilde/roskilde_festival_site) takes over a field south of town — the largest music festival in northern Europe and one of the oldest in the world. Most visitors come as a day trip from [Copenhagen](/europe/denmark/copenhagen), just twenty-five minutes away by train, but Roskilde rewards a full day.
