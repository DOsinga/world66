Located between Aarhus and Silkeborg, Ry (pronounced like the French 'rue') sits at the heart of [Søhøjlandet](/europe/denmark/ry/soehojlandet) — the Danish Lake Highlands — the closest Denmark gets to hilly terrain. The river Gudenå runs right through town, and the surrounding area is all forests, lakes, and gentle hills shaped by the last ice age.

You can sail, canoe, or walk through the woods. Wild roe deer are common. The main natural landmark is [Himmelbjerget](/europe/denmark/ry/himmelbjerget), one of Denmark's highest points at 147 metres, reached by a traditional paddle steamer across Lake Julsø. The ruins of [Øm Abbey](/europe/denmark/ry/om_abbey), a twelfth-century Cistercian monastery, sit on a lakeside peninsula a few kilometres south. For those who want to move through the landscape, the [Gudenå canoe route](/europe/denmark/ry/gudenaa_canoe) connects the lakes in a multi-day paddling journey.

Ry is easy to reach by train from Aarhus. It is not hugely touristic, though German and Dutch visitors are common in summer. The quieter village of [Gammel Rye](/europe/denmark/ry/gammel_rye), a few kilometres west, has an older church and good lakeshore walks.
