Samsø is an island in the Kattegat, roughly equidistant between Jutland and Zealand, reached by ferry from Hou south of Aarhus (about an hour) or from Kalundborg on Zealand (about two hours). It is 114 square kilometres, home to around 3,700 people, and has been quietly generating more renewable energy than it consumes since the early 2000s — the wind turbines visible from the ferry are not decorative; they produce enough electricity to export to the mainland.

The island is perhaps best known in Denmark for its new potatoes, the Samsø kartofler that appear in supermarkets each June. The sandy soil on the southern part of the island produces an early crop of a quality that growers on the island have been marketing successfully for over a century. Visit in late June and you will see potato fields extending to the water's edge, with roadside stalls selling bags of small, thin-skinned potatoes that need nothing but butter.

Tranebjerg, in the centre of the island, is the main town — small but functional, with the shops, the bank, and the local administration. Ballen, on the east coast, and Nordby, at the northern tip, are the main harbour villages. The island is popular with cyclists: the terrain is gently rolling rather than flat, the roads are quiet, and a circuit of the island takes a full day at a comfortable pace.

The beaches on the northern coast are sandy and relatively sheltered. The southern coast is more open to wind. Samsø is a good destination for those who want an island without the package-holiday feel of Marielyst or the self-consciousness of Bornholm's artisan scene.
