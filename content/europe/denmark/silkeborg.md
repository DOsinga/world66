Silkeborg sits at the heart of Denmark's lake district, surrounded by the country's largest connected network of [lakes](/europe/denmark/silkeborg/silkeborg_lakes) and the Gudenå river, which winds its way east to Randers Fjord. It is a pleasant, prosperous town of around 45,000 people and one of the best bases for exploring the gentle landscape of central Jutland.

The main attraction is the [AQUA freshwater aquarium](/europe/denmark/silkeborg/aqua_freshwater_aquarium), one of the largest in Northern Europe, with otters, pike, and other river and lake species in naturalistic settings. The [Hjejlen](/europe/denmark/silkeborg/hjejlen_paddle_steamer) paddle steamer, which has been sailing the Silkeborg lakes since June 18, 1861, is the world's oldest operational paddle steamer — still running its original steam engine. In summer it makes regular sightseeing trips up the Gudenå and across the lakes. The illuminated [colour fountain](/europe/denmark/silkeborg/colour_fountain) on the lake is worth seeing after dark when the lights change.

The [Silkeborg Museum](/europe/denmark/silkeborg/silkeborg_museum_tollund_man) houses the Tollund Man, a remarkably preserved 2,400-year-old bog body whose leathery face and serene expression have fascinated visitors since his discovery in 1950. [Museum Jorn](/europe/denmark/silkeborg/museum_jorn) on the Gudenå bank holds one of Denmark's best collections of postwar art, centred on works by the CoBrA movement's Danish co-founder. The surrounding [Silkeborg Forests](/europe/denmark/silkeborg/silkeborg_forests) — Denmark's largest — offer hundreds of kilometres of cycling and walking trails.
