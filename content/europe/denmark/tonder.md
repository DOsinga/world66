Tønder is [Denmark's](/europe/denmark) oldest market town, sitting right on the German border in the flat, windswept marshlands of southern Jutland. For centuries it was famous for its lace — Tønder lace was exported across Europe, and the [town museum](/europe/denmark/tonder/tonder_museum) still has a definitive collection. Today it is a quiet, well-preserved town of [old gabled houses and cobbled streets](/europe/denmark/tonder/old_town), pleasant to walk around but not heavily touristed.

The town comes alive each August for the [Tønder Festival](/europe/denmark/tonder/tonder_festival), one of northern Europe's best folk and roots music events. Musicians from across the world play in the old town's churches, barns, and a big tent by the river. If you are in southern Denmark at the right time, it is worth the trip.

The marshlands around Tønder are part of the [Wadden Sea](/europe/denmark/tonder/wadden_sea), a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The flat landscape stretches to the horizon, and in autumn vast flocks of starlings perform their murmurations — the local term is "[sort sol](/europe/denmark/tonder/sort_sol)" (black sun). The German border is a few kilometres south; [Flensburg](/europe/germany/schleswigholstein/flensburg) is half an hour away.
