---
title: "Getting There"
type: section
---

Each national section of the Frisian Islands is reached differently.

**Dutch Wadden Islands** are served by ferries from the mainland ports of Den Helder (Texel), Harlingen (Vlieland and Terschelling), Holwerd (Ameland), and Lauwersoog (Schiermonnikoog). Ferries run multiple times daily in summer, less frequently in winter. Texel can also be reached by car ferry; most other islands are car-free or strictly limit vehicles.

**East Frisian Islands** (Germany) are reached by ferry from Norddeich (Norderney and Juist), Bensersiel (Langeoog), Neuharlingersiel (Spiekeroog), and Harlesiel (Wangerooge), among others. Borkum has ferry service from Emden. Most East Frisian Islands allow no cars or only island-owned vehicles; cyclists and walkers are the norm. Check the ferry schedule carefully as crossing times are tied to the tides.

**North Frisian Islands** (Germany) are more varied in access: Sylt is connected to the mainland by a rail causeway (you can take your car on the train), Föhr and Amrum are served by ferries from Dagebüll, and the smaller Halligen islands have ferry connections from Husum and other ports.

**Danish Wadden islands** are accessed from Esbjerg — ferries reach Fanø, while Mandø is accessible by tractor at low tide across the tidal flats.
