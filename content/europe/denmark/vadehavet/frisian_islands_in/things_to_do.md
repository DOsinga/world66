---
title: "Things to Do"
type: section
---

The Frisian Islands offer some of the finest cycling in Europe. Most of the islands are flat, the roads (where cars are allowed at all) are lightly trafficked, and the combination of sea views, dune landscapes, and beach access makes a slow cycling tour one of the best ways to experience the chain. Bikes can be rented on every inhabited island.

**Wadlopen** (mudflat walking) is a uniquely Frisian experience. At low tide, the Wadden Sea recedes to expose vast mudflats — the actual seabed — that can be walked across to reach islands that have no ferry connection, or to cross between islands. It requires a guide (the tides move fast and the channels are disorienting), and it is one of the more extraordinary things you can do anywhere in Northern Europe. Tours are organised from the mainland coast and from various island harbours.

**Birdwatching** is excellent throughout the islands but particularly at the designated nature reserves and bird sanctuaries. Memmert (near Juist) and the Griend reserve (in the Dutch Wadden Sea) are among the most significant breeding sites in Western Europe. Tens of millions of wading birds pass through the Wadden Sea during migration; the spectacle of vast flocks of knot, dunlin, and oystercatcher at high tide roost is one of the great wildlife experiences of the continent.

**Swimming and beaches** are the main draw for most visitors. The North Sea is not the warmest (water temperatures peak in August), but the beaches are wide, the surf reliable for bodyboarding, and the light in summer extraordinary. The Dutch islands generally have the most developed beach infrastructure; the German islands the widest open beaches.
