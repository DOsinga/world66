---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

The Frisian Islands are emphatically a summer destination. July and August are the peak months, when German and Dutch holiday-makers fill the islands to capacity and prices are at their highest. The long summer days, warm sea temperatures, and reliable westerly winds make this the best time for swimming, cycling, sailing, and Wattenmeer (mudflat) walking tours.

May, June, and September are quieter and in many ways more rewarding: the light is still excellent, the weather usually good, and the beaches less crowded. Birdwatchers particularly value the spring and autumn migration periods, when millions of birds use the Wadden Sea as a feeding and staging ground on their journeys between Arctic breeding grounds and West African wintering areas.

Winter is grey and often stormy — the North Sea at its most North Sea — but the islands are not without appeal in cold weather. The car-free islands (Juist, Borkum without cars in the resort area) feel genuinely remote and wild in November and December. Storms can be spectacular; the Watt is at its most dramatic at low tide in fading light.

Note that some ferries and island services run reduced schedules outside the main summer season, so check connections in advance if visiting in April or October.
