The Vedbæk Bøgebakken site is one of the most significant Mesolithic archaeological finds in Scandinavia. Discovered in 1975 during construction work, it revealed a cemetery of 22 graves dating to approximately 5000 BC, providing an extraordinarily complete picture of how Ertebølle culture people — the last hunter-gatherers of northern Europe before agriculture arrived — buried their dead.

The graves showed elaborate burial practices: bodies accompanied by red ochre, jewellery of tooth and bone, and, in several cases, wild animals. The most famous grave contained a young woman and her newborn, the infant placed on a swan wing — an arrangement suggesting beliefs about the afterlife. A small permanent display at the local Vedbæk Cultural Centre (Vedbæk Kulturhus) tells the story of the find with replica artefacts; the originals are at the National Museum of Denmark in Copenhagen. The site itself is under and adjacent to the existing sports hall and is not visible, but the display is worth a few minutes.
