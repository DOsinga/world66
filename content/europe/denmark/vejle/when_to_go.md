---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

**May–August** is the standard visiting season. Days are long — midsummer brings nearly 18 hours of daylight — and the fjord, the forests around Munkebjerg, and the Grejs Valley are at their most inviting. Temperatures are mild rather than hot, typically 18–24°C. Jelling is pleasant to visit on foot in fine weather, and the outdoor waterfront areas along Bryggen come alive.

**September–October** is underrated. Crowds thin, the beech forests above the fjord turn amber and gold, and the hiking around Vejle is arguably better than in summer. Most sights stay open through October.

**November–March** is quiet, cold (averaging 2–6°C), and occasionally grey, but not without appeal. The museums are uncrowded, and there is something atmospheric about seeing the Jelling Stones in an empty churchyard in winter. The Haraldskær Woman in St. Nicolai Church is equally eerie year-round.

Vejle does not have major festivals that would significantly affect visiting, though the town centre is lively during the December Christmas market season.
