Haapsalu is a small resort town on the west coast of Estonia, known for centuries for its warm sea water, curative mud baths, and peaceful atmosphere. The narrow streets lined with early twentieth-century wooden houses make the [old town](/europe/estonia/haapsalu/old_town) a pleasant place to wander, and the pace of life here is noticeably slower than Tallinn, two hours to the east.

The main sight is the [Episcopal Castle](/europe/estonia/haapsalu/haapsalu_castle), a vast thirteenth-century fortress with a cathedral built into its walls. The legend of the White Lady — a ghostly figure said to appear in one of the castle windows during the August full moon — has turned into the town's biggest annual celebration, the White Lady Days festival. The seafront promenade and the elegant wooden [Kuursaal](/europe/estonia/haapsalu/kuursaal), built in 1898, are the other centrepieces, offering a picturesque walk along the shore.

The [Haapsalu Railway Station](/europe/estonia/haapsalu/haapsalu_railway_station), though no longer served by trains, is an architectural highlight in its own right — built to receive the Russian imperial family, it has a covered wooden platform 214 metres long and houses the Estonian Railway and Communications Museum. For those seeking the traditional Haapsalu experience, the town's therapeutic mud is still available at the [Läänemaa Spa](/europe/estonia/haapsalu/haapsalu_mud_baths).

Haapsalu comes alive in summer with a string of music festivals: the Old Music Festival, the strings festival Viiulimängud, the Tchaikovsky Festival (the composer was a regular visitor), and the August Blues Festival. The town is also a convenient jumping-off point for the islands of Hiiumaa and Vormsi.
