story: >
  The Kärdla cloth factory was one of the most northerly textile factories in the
  Russian Empire when it opened in the 1830s. It was founded by Baltic German
  merchants who brought workers from mainland Estonia to operate the looms. At its
  peak it employed several hundred people and produced wool cloth for the imperial
  military. It closed in the Soviet period and its workers' housing became the
  core of modern Kärdla.
---

The ruins of the 19th-century cloth factory that effectively founded Kärdla as a town stand in a pretty riverside park near the town centre. The old stone walls have been partly stabilised and left in a picturesque state of managed decay, surrounded by mature trees and a stream. It is one of the more atmospheric corners of the town.

The park is pleasant for a short walk and works well as a stop between the museum and the main street. Children use the grounds as a play area in summer, and there are benches along the stream. The contrast between the crumbling industrial ruins and the quiet Estonian forest setting is worth a few minutes of your time.
