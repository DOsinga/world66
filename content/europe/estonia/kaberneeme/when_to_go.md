---
title: When to Go
type: section
---

The warmest and most visited months are June, July, and August, when the Gulf of Finland reaches a swimmable 18–20°C and daylight lasts well into the evening. The summer solstice in late June brings nearly twenty hours of light, and the beach and forest are at their best. July is the peak of the Estonian summer holiday season; the village fills up with Tallinn families on weekends, though it rarely feels crowded by international standards.

Spring and early autumn are quieter and genuinely beautiful — the coastal forest is vivid with new growth in May, and the shoreline empties out after the school year begins. September remains mild and is excellent for walking. The Baltic migration season peaks in autumn, when large numbers of seabirds and raptors move south along the coast.

Winter is stark and largely empty of visitors. The bay can freeze in cold years, creating wide expanses of sea ice that are eerie and spectacular in equal measure. The forest is quiet and the light low and copper-toned even at midday — a rewarding experience for those prepared for it, but not a time to expect any services to be open.
