Laulasmaa is a small coastal village about 40 km west of Tallinn on the northern Estonian coast. Its name comes from the singing sands on its beach — "laulev maa" means "the land that sings" in Estonian.

The village has been a popular holiday spot since the 1930s, and it remains a quiet retreat from the capital. The [beach](/europe/estonia/laulasmaa/laulasmaa_beach) is the main attraction: a long stretch of sand backed by pine forest, uncrowded even in summer. The nearby [Lohusalu marina](/europe/estonia/laulasmaa/lohusalu_marina) gives access to the coast by boat, and the surprising [Arvo Pärt Centre](/europe/estonia/laulasmaa/arvo_part_centre) hidden among the pines is a draw for music lovers. Day trips lead inland to [Keila-Joa waterfall](/europe/estonia/laulasmaa/keila_joa_waterfall) or south along the rail line to [Kloogaranna](/europe/estonia/laulasmaa/kloogaranna_beach).
