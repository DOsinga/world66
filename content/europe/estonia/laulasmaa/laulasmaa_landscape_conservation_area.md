A small protected area stretching back from the coast into the pine and juniper woods west of the village. It exists to safeguard the limestone cliff edge, the coastal meadows, and the rare lichen flora that survives in this corner of the Baltic. Marked walking trails of two to six kilometres loop through the forest, and most can be done in trainers.

Look out for the small information boards in Estonian and English along the trails, which point out everything from glacial erratics to old smuggling paths used during the Soviet era. The area is also good for spotting roe deer at dawn and dusk, and birds of prey wheeling above the open meadows. There is no entrance fee, and the trailheads have small wooden car parks signposted from the main road.
