The Church of Saint Magdalene is one of the oldest surviving wooden buildings in Estonia, built in 1644 during the Swedish colonial period when Ruhnu's Swedish-speaking community was at its most established. The building has been maintained but not heavily restored — it retains the patina of age that makes old wooden churches so compelling.

The interior is intimate and simply furnished, with a painted ceiling and wooden pews worn smooth by three and a half centuries of use. Beside it stands the newer stone church from 1912, built when the wooden church was already beginning to show its age. The contrast between the two buildings — one that has clearly been standing for a very long time, one that looks almost new by comparison — is quietly affecting.

Both churches are associated with the Swedish community that evacuated in 1944, leaving the island to be repopulated by mainland Estonians. That story, of a place emptied of its entire population in a matter of days, hangs over the island. The churches are a few minutes' walk from the village centre.
