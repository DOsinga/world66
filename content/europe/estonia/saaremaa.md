Saaremaa is Estonia's largest island and its most rewarding slow-travel escape. It is flat, forested, and quietly maritime, with juniper meadows, stone churches, windmills, spa traditions, and long roads where the next village may be little more than a church, a shop, and a few weathered farmhouses. The island feels close to Estonia and slightly apart from it at the same time.

Most visitors base themselves in [Kuressaare](/europe/estonia/kuressaare), the island capital, because it has the castle, restaurants, spas, and bus connections. But Saaremaa only really opens up once you leave town: the [Kaali meteorite crater](/europe/estonia/kuressaare/kaali_crater), the windmills of [Angla](/europe/estonia/kuressaare/angla_windmills), the cliffs of [Panga](/europe/estonia/saaremaa/panga_cliff), and the long road to [Sõrve Lighthouse](/europe/estonia/saaremaa/sorve_lighthouse) each show a different island.

The western coast is wilder. [Vilsandi National Park](/europe/estonia/vilsandi_national_park) protects islets, seals, and seabirds, while [Kiipsaare Lighthouse](/europe/estonia/saaremaa/kiipsaare_lighthouse) leans out on the shifting shore like a warning about what the Baltic does to maps. Saaremaa is not a place to rush. Rent a car or bike, accept the distances, and let the island's quietness do its work.
