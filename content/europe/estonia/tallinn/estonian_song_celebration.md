The Estonian Song Celebration is the great civic ritual of Tallinn: a vast choral festival in which tens of thousands of singers gather at the Song Festival Grounds and perform to a crowd that can feel like half the country. It is not an annual tourist show but a major national event, held only every few years, with roots in the 19th-century national awakening and a continuing role in Estonia's modern identity.

Even outside festival dates the song grounds are worth understanding, because this is where the "Singing Revolution" moved from metaphor to public force. The sweeping stage and grass amphitheatre are simple in form but powerful in scale, and they explain why music is not a decorative extra in Tallinn's story but one of the ways Estonians have expressed political will, memory, and belonging.
