The name means "Peep in the Kitchen" in Low German — because the tower's windows looked into the upper floors of the surrounding houses. The tower itself, built in 1475, is one of the most formidable in the Baltic, with walls up to 4 metres thick. Inside is a museum of city history; the basement connects to the Bastion Passages, a network of 17th-century tunnels dug beneath the city walls for counter-mine defence. The tunnel tour is the main event — book in advance.
