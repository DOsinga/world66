Viljandi is a small South Estonian town with a bigger cultural life than its size suggests. The setting does much of the work: castle hills above a long lake, wooden streets that still feel local, and a folk-music scene strong enough to earn the town UNESCO Creative City status for crafts and folk art.

The best place to start is the [castle park](/europe/estonia/viljandi/viljandi_castle_park), where the ruins of the Livonian Order fortress look over Lake Viljandi. The [suspension bridge](/europe/estonia/viljandi/viljandi_suspension_bridge) nearby is the town's best-known postcard view, and the paths through the hills make a relaxed circuit rather than a checklist sight.

Down by the water, [Lake Viljandi](/europe/estonia/viljandi/lake_viljandi_beach) gives the town its slower rhythm: swimming in summer, walks along the shore, and winter views when the valley goes quiet. Back in the centre, the [Museum of Viljandi](/europe/estonia/viljandi/museum_of_viljandi) and [Kondas Centre](/europe/estonia/viljandi/kondas_centre) fill in the local story without making the place feel like an open-air museum.

Viljandi is strongest as an overnight stop between [Tartu](/europe/estonia/tartu) and [Pärnu](/europe/estonia/parnu), or as a deliberate detour if you want Estonia beyond Tallinn. In late July the folk music festival changes the town completely; outside festival week it is quieter, more walkable, and easier to enjoy at its own pace.
