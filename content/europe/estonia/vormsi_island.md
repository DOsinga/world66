Vormsi is Estonia's fourth-largest island and one of its least visited. For centuries it was home to a community of Estonian Swedes — at its peak around 2,500 people — who were evacuated to Sweden in 1944. The Soviet military closed the island to civilians for decades, and when independence came in 1991, fewer than a hundred people lived here.

That emptiness is the appeal. Vormsi is flat, forested, and quiet. The main sight is the medieval [St. Olaf's church](/europe/estonia/vormsi_island/st_olaf_church) at [Hullo](/europe/estonia/vormsi_island/hullo), where the graveyard is filled with stone wheel crosses unique to the island's Swedish community. The rest is walking, cycling and birdwatching — the [Rumpo peninsula](/europe/estonia/vormsi_island/rumpo_peninsula) and the rest of the [landscape reserve](/europe/estonia/vormsi_island/vormsi_landscape_reserve) are an important stopover for migratory birds, and the cast-iron [Saxby Lighthouse](/europe/estonia/vormsi_island/saxby_lighthouse) marks the lonely western tip.

You can reach Vormsi by ferry from Rohuküla on the mainland, near [Haapsalu](/europe/estonia/haapsalu). The crossing takes about forty-five minutes. There are no shops or restaurants beyond the harbour, so bring what you need.
