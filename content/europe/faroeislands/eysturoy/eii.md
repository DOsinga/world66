Eiði sits at the north-western edge of [Eysturoy](/europe/faroeislands/eysturoy), looking across the sound toward Streymoy and the blunt northern mountains of the Faroe Islands. The village itself is quiet: turf, sheep, a small harbour, and low houses set beneath the shoulder of Eiðiskollur. The reason to come is the landscape around it.

Just offshore stand [Risin og Kellingin](/europe/faroeislands/eysturoy/eii/risin_og_kellingin), the Giant and the Witch, two sea stacks tied to one of the Faroes' best-known legends. From the road above Eiði, the view opens toward the stacks, [Tjørnuvík](/europe/faroeislands/streymoy/saksun), and the Atlantic beyond. On clear days it is one of the finest quick stops in the northern Faroes.

Eiði also makes a practical base for the high road to [Gjogv](/europe/faroeislands/eysturoy/gjogv) and [Slættaratindur](/europe/faroeislands/eysturoy/gjogv/slaettaratindur), the highest mountain in the islands. The drive is short, but the weather changes fast; low cloud can turn a sunny village morning into a white-out on the moor within minutes.
