The puffin colony is the emotional centre of a Mykines visit. In summer, Atlantic puffins stand beside their burrows in the grass, often close enough that you can see the fish in their beaks. The encounter feels intimate, and that is exactly why the rules are strict.

Keep to marked paths, avoid sudden movement, and never step onto burrowed ground for a photograph. The best viewing is usually in calm evening light, when birds return from the sea and the slopes glow green against the black cliffs.
