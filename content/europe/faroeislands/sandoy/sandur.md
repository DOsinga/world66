Sandur is the main village on Sandoy, the quietest and most pastoral of the Faroe Islands. Known locally as "Heima á Sandi", it sits at the head of a sheltered fjord surrounded by green hillsides and farmland — a slower pace than Tórshavn, and all the better for it.

The village has a small art museum, [Listasavnið](/europe/faroeislands/sandoy/sandur/listasavnid), dedicated to Faroese art, and a historic house museum called [Í Koytu](/europe/faroeislands/sandoy/sandur/i_koytu) that gives a sense of traditional island life. For most visitors, though, the draw is the landscape itself: the ferry crossing from Gamlarætt is scenic, and [Sandoy's walking trails](/europe/faroeislands/sandoy/sandur/walking_trails) are uncrowded even by Faroese standards. The [Gróthúsvatn Witch Rock](/europe/faroeislands/sandoy/sandur/grothusvatan_witch_rock), an unusual seasonal tradition where islanders cover a boulder in knitted objects each summer, is one of those Faroese peculiarities that stays with you.
