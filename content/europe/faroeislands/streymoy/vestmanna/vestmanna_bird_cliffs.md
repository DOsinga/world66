The Vestmanna bird cliffs (Vestmannabjørgini) are the standout natural sight on Streymoy and one of the best wildlife excursions in the Faroe Islands. Boat trips run directly from the harbour into a world of sheer basalt walls, sea caves, and natural arches — all packed with nesting seabirds from spring through early autumn.

The cliffs rise several hundred metres straight from the North Atlantic. Operators navigate small, nimble boats into narrow caverns where the light shifts from bright Atlantic blue to near-dark, and the noise of thousands of birds — guillemots, razorbills, puffins, kittiwakes, gannets, arctic terns, and skuas — fills the air. You get close enough to see individual birds on their ledges. The geology alone would be worth the trip; the birds make it extraordinary.

Trips take around two hours and run from multiple operators based in the harbour. Book ahead in July and August — this is a popular excursion and boats fill up. Wear a windproof layer even in summer; the sea breeze in the caverns is sharp.
