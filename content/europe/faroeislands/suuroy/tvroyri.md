Tvøroyri is one of the largest settlements on Suðuroy, the southernmost island in the [Faroe Islands](/europe/faroeislands). The town sits at the head of [Trongisvágsfjørður](/europe/faroeislands/suuroy/tvroyri/trongisvagsfjoerdur), a deep fjord cutting into the island's east coast.

Most visitors arrive by the Smyril ferry from Tórshavn, which docks at [Krambatangi](/europe/faroeislands/suuroy/tvroyri/krambatangi) on the opposite side of the fjord. The municipality of around 1,800 people includes the neighbouring villages of Trongisvágur, Froðba, and Øravík. Tvøroyri has a small harbour, a few shops, and serves as the main base for exploring Suðuroy's dramatic coastal cliffs and sea stacks — most dramatically [Beinisvørð](/europe/faroeislands/suuroy/tvroyri/beinisvord), a 470-metre sea cliff on the eastern coast. The island is quieter and less visited than the northern Faroes, which is exactly the appeal.
