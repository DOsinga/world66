**The Old Man and His Sons** by Heðin Brú. A short, deceptively simple novel about a poor old man and his son who try to pay off a debt by going whaling. First published in Faroese in 1940 and widely considered the best Faroese novel; it captures the rhythm of village life in the islands as it began to give way to the modern world.

**The Tower at the Edge of the World** by William Heinesen. The dean of Faroese literature in the 20th century, Heinesen wrote in Danish but lived all his life in Tórshavn. This is a fictionalised, vivid memoir of growing up in the small capital around the time of the First World War.

**Barbara** by Jørgen-Frantz Jacobsen. The classic Faroese novel of love and longing, set in 18th-century Tórshavn. Jacobsen died young and left the book unfinished — but it was completed from his notes and remains the romance the Faroese have not yet escaped.

**The Faroe Islands: Politics and Society** by Beinta í Jákupsstovu and others. For travellers who want to understand how 50,000 people run their own country in the middle of the North Atlantic — the constitutional arrangements with Denmark, the fishing economy, the language revival.
