Tindhólmur is the uninhabited islet visible across Sørvágsfjørður from the village of Bøur, its five serrated peaks rising steeply from the sea. It's one of the most striking natural features in the Faroe Islands and the backdrop for some of the most reproduced photographs of the archipelago.

You see it best from the western edge of Bøur, where the old grass-roofed farmhouses frame the fjord view. The island itself is inaccessible without a private boat, but the view from shore is the point. Early morning and late evening light are best for photography — in summer the sun is low even at midnight.
