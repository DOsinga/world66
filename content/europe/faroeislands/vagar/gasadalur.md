Gásadalur is a tiny village of about a dozen residents on the western tip of [Vágar](/europe/faroeislands/vagar), the Faroe Islands' westernmost island. It sits on a plateau high above the sea, ringed by the highest mountains on Vágar — Árnafjall (722 m) to the north and Eysturtindur (715 m) to the east — and overlooking the dramatic strait toward [Mykines](/europe/faroeislands/mykines). Until 2004 it was the only village in the Faroes without a road connection; supplies came by boat, and the postman arrived on foot over the mountain. A tunnel through the rock changed that overnight.

The draw for visitors is [Múlafossur](/europe/faroeislands/vagar/gasadalur/mulafossur), a waterfall that drops 30 metres directly from the cliff edge into the Atlantic. It is the most photographed spot in the Faroe Islands, and on calm days the water seems to hang in the air before disappearing into the sea far below. The short walk from the village to the viewpoint takes two minutes. In summer evenings, puffins wheel above the cliffs to the left of the falls.

The village itself has a small café and guesthouse, a souvenir shop run by a local couple, and a collection of traditional Faroese turf-roofed houses. Beyond it, hiking trails lead into the mountains with views over Mykinesfjørður and the stacks that jut from the water offshore. It is worth the detour even if you stay only an hour — few places in the Faroes feel as remote or as quietly extraordinary.
