Miðvágur is one of the larger settlements on Vágar island in the [Faroe Islands](/europe/faroeislands), home to about a thousand people. The village sits on the shore of Sørvágsfjørður and makes a practical, quieter base than [Sørvágur](/europe/faroeislands/vagar/srvgur) for exploring Vágar's most dramatic scenery.

The trailhead for the [Leitisvatn hike](things_to_do/leitisvatn_hike) — the walk to the famous lake that appears to float above the ocean — starts at the edge of the village. It is a 3.5 km walk along the southern lakeshore to the cliff at [Bøsdalafossur](things_to_do/bosdalafossur), where the lake drains directly into the Atlantic. Most visitors coming from Tórshavn or the airport pass through Miðvágur anyway, and the trailhead gate is here, not in Sørvágur.

The [Dúgvan War Museum](things_to_do/dugvan_war_museum) is housed in a preserved Nissen hut from the British military base that operated on Vágar during World War II. It is one of the few places in the Faroes where the Allied occupation — Britain took control in April 1940 to prevent a German foothold — is documented in detail.

Fifteen minutes by road through the mountain tunnel, [Gásadalur](/europe/faroeislands/vagar/gasadalur) is worth the detour: a handful of houses on a cliff above the Atlantic, with the waterfall Múlafossur dropping straight into the sea and Mykines across the water.
