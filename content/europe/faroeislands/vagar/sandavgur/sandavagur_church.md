story: The Sandavágur Church is one of the most photographed buildings on Vágar — a red-roofed wooden church that has stood in various forms on this site since the medieval period. The current structure dates from 1917, built in a cruciform plan with a distinctive red roof that contrasts sharply with the green valley and the grey Atlantic light. In the churchyard stands a runestone carved in Old Norse, one of only two such stones in the Faroe Islands, believed to date from the 10th or 11th century.
---

The church sits at the edge of Sandavágur village with views down the valley toward the sea. The building's red roof is visible from some distance and has become something of an emblem for the village. Inside, the wooden interior is simple and well-maintained, with painted decorations and a notable altarpiece. Services are held in Faroese.

The runestone in the churchyard is easy to miss but historically significant. The inscription, in a mix of Old Norse and Faroese characters, reads roughly "Þorkell Onundsson of western origins placed this stone." The stone is one of the few surviving examples of runic inscription from the Faroe Islands and evidence that the Norse settlers of Vágar left physical records as well as place names.
