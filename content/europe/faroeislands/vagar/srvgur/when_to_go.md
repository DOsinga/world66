---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

The Faroe Islands have no bad season, but the reasons to visit Sørvágur change dramatically through the year.

**Summer (June–August)** is the peak for most visitors. The Mykines ferry runs daily, the puffins are nesting, the days are extraordinarily long (near-midnight twilight in June), and the hiking trails are at their most accessible. The downside is that this is also when Vágar is busiest — the Sørvágsvatn trail and Mykines ferry fill up, and accommodation needs booking well ahead. The weather is mild but rarely warm; expect temperatures of 10–15°C and pack for wind and rain regardless of the forecast.

**Spring (April–May)** and **autumn (September–October)** offer a reasonable balance: fewer visitors, the lake trail still walkable, and a good chance of the dramatic low-angle light that makes Faroese landscapes look their most spectacular. The Mykines ferry typically starts in late April or May and ends in September.

**Winter (November–March)** closes the ferry to Mykines and shortens the days to a handful of hours, but the islands take on a different kind of appeal — austere, atmospheric, and very quiet. The airport operates year-round, and the road to Bøur and Gásadalur is accessible in all but the worst weather.
