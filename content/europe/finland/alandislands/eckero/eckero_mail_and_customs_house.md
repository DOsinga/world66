The Eckerö Mail and Customs House is far grander than a small Baltic village would seem to need. That was the point. Built in 1828 on the old Stockholm-Turku mail route, it announced the western edge of the Russian Empire to ships arriving from Sweden.

The building was designed by Carl Ludvig Engel and Carlo Bassi in a severe empire style, with a long facade, a large courtyard, and a scale that still feels theatrical. Today it is used for exhibitions, events, and a small cultural centre. Even if you do not go inside, the setting near the harbour makes it Eckerö's essential stop.
