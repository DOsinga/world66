Kökar is one of the outer [Åland Islands](/europe/finland/alandislands), small in population but unusually rich in texture. Ferries reach it from Långnäs on Åland and from Galtby in the Turku archipelago, which makes the journey part of the point: water, skerries, low horizons, and a slow arrival into a working island community.

The island has more history than its size suggests. Bronze Age seal hunters left traces at [Otterböte](/europe/finland/alandislands/kokar/otterbote_bronze_age_settlement), Franciscan monks built a monastery on Hamnö in the 15th century, and the revived pilgrimage route across the Baltic gives the old east-west travel corridor a new life. The woods are also unusual for Finland, with broadleaf trees and rare plants mixed into the rocky archipelago landscape.

Kökar is best for travellers who like quiet islands with real stories. It is not a resort strip, and that is the appeal: monastery ruins, short hikes, local black bread, and the sense of being near the edge of Åland rather than passing through its centre.
