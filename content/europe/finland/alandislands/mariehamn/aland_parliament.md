  The Åland parliament, established in 1922, is one of the oldest autonomous regional
  parliaments in Europe. Its thirty members govern education, health, local taxation,
  radio and television, and transport — powers that in most European countries rest
  entirely with the national government. The parliament's existence is protected by
  international treaty and can theoretically only be removed by a unanimous decision
  of the Finnish parliament plus a two-thirds majority of the Åland parliament itself.
  In practice, no one has tried.

The Parliament of Åland (Lagting) building stands near the eastern harbour in the center of Mariehamn. The building is from the 1930s — a solid, functionalist structure appropriate for its time — and houses the 30-seat assembly that governs the islands' domestic affairs. The parliament building can be visited on guided tours; check with the Mariehamn tourist office for current arrangements.

The parliament building is less impressive architecturally than its constitutional significance would suggest, but the fact of its existence — a functioning parliament governing 30,000 people under an international treaty that dates to 1921 — is remarkable and worth understanding. The visit is most interesting in combination with the Åland Museum.
