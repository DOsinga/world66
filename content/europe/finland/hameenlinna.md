Hämeenlinna is one of [Finland's](/europe/finland) oldest inland towns, built around a red-brick medieval castle on the shore of Lake Vanajavesi, about 100 kilometres north of [Helsinki](/europe/finland/helsinki). [Häme Castle](/europe/finland/hameenlinna/hame_castle) dates from the 13th century and is one of the best-preserved medieval fortifications in the country — its thick walls and courtyard are open to visitors year-round, and the interior houses exhibitions on the castle's history as a fortress, granary, and prison.

The town's most famous son is Jean Sibelius, born here in 1865. His [childhood home](/europe/finland/hameenlinna/sibelius_childhood_home) on Hallituskatu has been preserved as a small museum, and the connection is celebrated throughout the town. But Hämeenlinna has more to offer than Sibelius pilgrimage. The [Hämeenlinna Art Museum](/europe/finland/hameenlinna/hameenlinna_art_museum) and the nearby Iittala glass village, where you can visit the factory and museum of Finland's most iconic glassware brand, are both worth a stop. Beside the castle, the [Artillery Museum of Finland](/europe/finland/hameenlinna/artillery_museum_of_finland) holds a remarkable collection of cannon and field guns covering Finnish military history from the 15th century.

The real highlight of the area is [Aulanko Nature Reserve](/europe/finland/hameenlinna/aulanko_nature_reserve), a landscaped park and forest on a promontory jutting into Lake Vanajavesi. A granite observation tower at the top of the hill gives panoramic views across the lake district — endless water, islands, and forest stretching to the horizon. Hämeenlinna is an easy day trip from Helsinki by train, with the journey taking just over an hour, or a worthwhile stop on the way to [Tampere](/europe/finland/tampere) and the Finnish lake district.
