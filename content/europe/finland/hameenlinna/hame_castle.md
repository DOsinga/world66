Häme Castle is one of the best-preserved medieval fortifications in Scandinavia — a massive red-brick structure built in the 13th century on a small promontory jutting into Lake Vanajavesi. The Swedes built it to assert control over the inland Häme region, and it served successively as a military stronghold, a granary, and a prison before being restored and opened as a museum.

The interior is thoughtfully presented: rooms have been dressed with period furniture and explanatory panels, and the castle's evolution over seven centuries is traced through well-curated exhibitions. The central courtyard is imposing, enclosed on all sides by the thick brick walls. Guided tours in Finnish and occasionally English are available in summer.

The walk along the lake shore to the castle from the city centre takes about fifteen minutes and passes the old railway bridge. In winter, when the lake is frozen, visitors sometimes cross to the castle on foot across the ice — a Finnish experience that can't be replicated in summer.
