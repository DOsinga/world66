---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

Inari sits at 69° north and divides cleanly into two very different seasons.

**Winter (November–April)** is the deep dark season — temperatures regularly below –20°C, the lake frozen solid, several centimetres of snow on the ground, and the Northern Lights overhead on clear nights. The polar night (*kaamos*) — when the sun does not rise at all — runs from late November into mid-January, though there are still a few hours of blue twilight even then. February to early April combines hard snow, increasing daylight, and the best chance of aurora. Pack seriously warm clothes; nothing British or Central European will do.

**Summer (June–August)** is the opposite: long warm days (the sun never sets between mid-May and late July), the lake open, the fells walkable, and the mosquitoes furious. This is the time for hiking, boat trips, and gold panning in Lemmenjoki.

**Shoulder seasons (May, September, October)** are quiet and atmospheric — autumn colours (*ruska*) peak in mid-September, the first snow falls in October. Few tourists either side of the busy aurora and Christmas season.
