Jurmo is one of the starkest islands in Finland's Archipelago Sea. It sits far out between [Nagu](/europe/finland/nagu) and [Utö](/europe/finland/uto), with low heath, cobble beaches, a tiny village, and Baltic water all around. The island is reachable by ferry, but it still feels like a place at the edge.

The draw is not a long list of attractions. Jurmo works because the whole island is the sight: open sky, rare plants, migrating birds, old stone traces, and a village that has had to live with wind and distance. More than 300 bird species have been recorded here, and spring and autumn migration can be excellent.

The walk from the harbour to the village and chapel is short, but the island is about six kilometres from end to end. Stay on marked trails where possible, both because the nature is fragile and because parts of the island are closed during bird nesting season. Come for a slow day of walking, birding, and quiet, not for services.
