Jurmo Chapel dates from 1846 and sits in the village with a small graveyard beside it. Its plainness suits the island: this was a working chapel for a remote community, not a showpiece.

The most memorable detail is the votive ship, made by a sailor who survived a shipwreck. Until the 1960s, the graveyard also served people from nearby Utö, which says a lot about how thinly settled and interdependent these outer islands were.
