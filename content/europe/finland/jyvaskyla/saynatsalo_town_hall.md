The Säynätsalo Town Hall is considered one of Alvar Aalto's masterpieces and one of the finest small public buildings of the 20th century. It sits on the island of Säynätsalo, about 14 kilometres south of Jyväskylä, overlooking a lake. The complex is built around a raised courtyard — you climb a grassy stepped approach to enter — and is constructed of red brick, timber, and copper. The council chamber, with its soaring wooden ceiling structure, is the centrepiece.

What makes the building exceptional is the way it achieves civic dignity without monumentality. It is scaled for human use, warm in its materials, and deeply embedded in its landscape. The complex also includes a library, council offices, and a small housing block. Today the council chamber is occasionally rented for events. The town hall is about 30 minutes from central Jyväskylä by bus or car and is well worth the trip for anyone with an interest in architecture.
