Kajaani sits on the shore of Lake Oulujarvi in the Kainuu region of eastern Finland, a quiet town that still carries the rural character of the surrounding forests and lakes. It is small enough to walk around in an afternoon but has a few things worth stopping for.

The ruins of [Kajaani Castle](/europe/finland/kajaani/kajaani-castle), on an island in the Kajaaninjoki river rapids, date from 1604 and were destroyed by the Russians in 1716. The old wooden houses in the town centre give it a distinctive feel, and the [Kajaani Art Museum](/europe/finland/kajaani/kajaani-art-museum) is worth a visit. Elias Lonnrot, who collected the folk poetry that became the Kalevala -- the Finnish national epic -- lived and worked here as a district doctor in the 1830s.

In winter, the surrounding area offers good cross-country skiing. In spring, Kajaani hosts a well-regarded jazz festival that draws visitors from across Finland.
