Elias Lönnrot spent eleven years in Kajaani, from 1833 to 1853, during which time he produced the Kalevala — Finland's national epic, assembled from oral folk poetry he collected across Kainuu, Karelia, and the surrounding regions. He came as a district doctor, a position that gave him the freedom to travel extensively through terrain few outsiders bothered to explore, and he used that access to track down the last singers who carried the old runo tradition. The town takes quiet pride in this connection, and there are a number of markers and memorials around the centre acknowledging his time here.

The Kalevala was not simply transcribed — Lönnrot edited, arranged, and in places augmented the material he collected, turning thousands of individual verses into a coherent fifty-poem cycle. The first edition appeared in 1835, the expanded second edition in 1849. The work had enormous influence on Finnish art, architecture, and music through the late 19th century, most visibly in the compositions of Jean Sibelius. Walking the riverside areas in Kajaani, it is not difficult to see what kind of landscape Lönnrot was moving through when he made his field trips — the forests and waterways of Kainuu have not changed dramatically in the intervening two centuries.
