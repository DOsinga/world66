The Kerimäki Church is the largest wooden church in the world, and it achieves that distinction without any apparent desire to show off. Completed in 1847, it can seat 3,000 worshippers and has standing room for another 2,000 — remarkable for a parish in the Finnish lake district. The building is a simple neoclassical structure in pale timber, its scale only apparent once you step inside and look up at the vast vault of the ceiling.

The interior is cool and spare in the Lutheran tradition, with white-painted woodwork and clear glass windows looking out to the birch trees. The acoustic is extraordinary — whispered words carry across the nave. Services are held in summer, and the church draws visitors from across Finland who come specifically to see what an act of accidental architectural ambition looks like.

The church stands on a small hill above the village, visible from a distance across the lake. The surrounding graveyard and birch forest make for a quiet walk before or after entering. The church is open to visitors during summer; hours vary by season.
