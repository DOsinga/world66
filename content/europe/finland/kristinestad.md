Kristinestad (Kristiinankaupunki in Finnish) is one of the loveliest small towns on Finland's west coast, a bilingual Ostrobothnian port where Swedish is still widely heard in the streets. It is not a blockbuster destination, but it has a rare, intact feel: timber houses, a strict 17th-century grid, quiet lanes running down to the bay, and a pace slow enough to earn it membership of the Cittaslow movement.

The pleasure here is mostly walking. The old centre slopes gently toward the water, with painted wooden houses, narrow lanes and a market square that still feels scaled for a sailing town rather than modern traffic. The most photographed corner is [Kattpiskargränden](/europe/finland/kristinestad/kattpiskargranden), a famously skinny alley whose name is usually translated as Cat Whipper's Alley.

Kristinestad's history is maritime and mercantile. [Lebell's Merchant House](/europe/finland/kristinestad/lebells_merchant_house) preserves the interiors of a wealthy trading family, while the [Maritime Museum](/europe/finland/kristinestad/maritime_museum) and [Carlsro Museum](/europe/finland/kristinestad/carlsro_museum) fill in the story of shipowners, summer villas and the coast north of town. It is a good half-day stop if you are driving the Gulf of Bothnia coast between Vaasa and Pori.
