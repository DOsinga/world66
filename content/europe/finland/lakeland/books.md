**The Year of the Hare** by Arto Paasilinna (1975) — A journalist abandons his life after hitting a hare on a country road, and wanders through the Finnish countryside in a comic picaresque. Paasilinna captures the relationship between Finns and their landscape with both affection and dry humour. It is set partly in the lake district and gives a feel for the Finnish interior that no travel guide can quite replicate.

**Seven Brothers** by Aleksis Kivi (1870) — The foundational Finnish novel: seven brothers flee civilisation to live in the forest, making chaos and gradually finding their way back to society. It is set in an earlier era but the forest, the lakes, and the Finn's complicated relationship with both are as central to the book as to the country today.

**Tove** by Boel Westin (2014) — A biography of Tove Jansson, creator of the Moomins, who spent her summers on an island in the Finnish archipelago. The Moomin stories are soaked in the atmosphere of Finnish island summers; the biography explains where that atmosphere came from.
