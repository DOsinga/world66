  SS Heinävesi, launched in 1905, is one of the oldest operating passenger steamships in Finland and still runs scheduled summer services on Lake Saimaa. The original steam engine has been preserved, and the ship runs on a coal-fired boiler under a captain whose uniform bears no hint of irony. The Finnish lake steamer network was once the region's primary passenger transport; the boats that survive are now heritage attractions, but they run to timetable and you can genuinely use them to travel between towns.

The Finnish Lakeland has a tradition of passenger steamships dating from the late 19th century, when steam-powered vessels were the primary means of travel across the lake network. Several historic steamers survive and run summer services on Saimaa, including routes between Savonlinna, Kuopio, and Joensuu. The most atmospheric are the oldest vessels, which retain their original wood-panelled interiors and leisurely pace.

Cruises range from short harbour trips to multi-day voyages between cities. The two-day Savonlinna to Kuopio route via the Heinävesi Canal passes through classic Lakeland scenery: archipelagos, canal locks, forested ridges, and small lakeside communities. Meals are served on board; some services include overnight cabin accommodation. This is slow travel in the original sense — the kind of journey that was once the only way to get anywhere in the Lakeland.
