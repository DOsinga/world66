  Punkaharju Ridge is a glacial esker — a sinuous ridge of sand and gravel deposited by meltwater rivers running under the ice sheet during the last Ice Age. As the glaciers retreated, this ridge was left behind as an improbably narrow strip of land between two lake systems. The Tsar Alexander I of Russia visited in 1803 and was so taken with the landscape that he declared it a protected area — making Punkaharju one of Finland's oldest nature reserves and one of the world's earliest instances of landscape conservation.

Punkaharju Ridge is a seven-kilometre-long glacial esker running between Lake Puruvesi and the Saimaa system in South Savo. At its narrowest, the ridge is barely wide enough for the road and footpath that run its spine, with lake water visible on both sides through the trees. The pines and birches growing on the sandy slopes are unusually tall and straight, and the filtered light through the canopy creates a quality of landscape that has drawn Finnish painters for two centuries.

The ridge runs roughly between the train stations of Punkaharju and Retretti. The full walk along the ridge takes about two hours. The Lusto Forest Museum sits at one end and provides excellent context about Finnish forests and the landscape. The area is quiet on weekdays outside July; in summer it draws both Finnish families and international visitors who have read about it in landscape photography books.
