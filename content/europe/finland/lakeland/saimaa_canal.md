The Saimaa Canal connects Lake Saimaa to the Gulf of Finland at Vyborg, running 43 kilometres from Lappeenranta through territory partly in Finland and partly in Russia. It was built in the 1850s to give the inland lake system access to the sea, and after a complex post-WWII history involving Soviet occupation of the southern section, it has been operating under a long-term lease since 1968.

Boat trips through the canal's eight locks, passing from Finnish territory into Russian-leased land and back, make for an unusual day excursion from Lappeenranta. The landscape changes along the route from gentle Finnish lakeland to the more dramatic granite cliffs of the Karelian section. Cargo barges still use the canal commercially; sharing the locks with working vessels gives the trip an industrial authenticity that purely tourist waterways lack.
