  The Saimaa ringed seal has been isolated in Lake Saimaa since the last Ice Age, when rising land cut it off from the Baltic. Over 10,000 years, it evolved into a distinct subspecies found nowhere else on earth. The Saimaa ringed seal (Pusa hispida saimensis) is one of the world's rarest seals, found exclusively in Lake Saimaa. Around 400 individuals remain, and seeing one of them hauled out on a lakeside rock or surfacing near a boat is among the most memorable wildlife encounters available in Finland. Organised seal-watching cruises operate from Savonlinna during summer, typically in the early morning when seals are most active.

The best areas for seal sightings are in the Linnansaari National Park archipelago and around the Pihlajavesi lake area east of Savonlinna. Independent kayakers sometimes encounter seals, which are generally not disturbed by quiet watercraft approaching slowly. Boat tours and responsible kayak rentals emphasise the 100-metre buffer distance required by conservation rules during the breeding season (February to May).
