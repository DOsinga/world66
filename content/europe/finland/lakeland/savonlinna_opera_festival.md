  The festival began in 1912 when the soprano Aino Ackté had the idea of staging opera inside Olavinlinna Castle. The first performance was of Giacomo Puccini's Madama Butterfly. The festival lapsed during the wars and only revived permanently in 1967, but Ackté's original instinct — that a medieval island castle on a Finnish lake would make an extraordinary opera venue — has been proven correct every summer since.

The Savonlinna Opera Festival takes place throughout July in the courtyard of Olavinlinna Castle. It is one of the most celebrated opera events in the Nordic countries, and the setting — a medieval castle rising from a lake in the quiet Saimaa archipelago — is unlike any other opera venue in the world. Productions typically run for four weeks with a mix of established repertoire and occasional premieres or Finnish works.

Tickets should be booked well in advance; the festival attracts audiences from across Finland and internationally. The castle courtyard seats around 2,300 people; performances happen in the open air, and rain gear is standard audience equipment even in July. The evening ferry crossings to the castle and the summer light lingering past midnight are as much a part of the experience as the opera itself.
