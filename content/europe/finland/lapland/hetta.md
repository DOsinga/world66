Hetta is the main village of Enontekiö, set beside Ounasjärvi in the high northwest of [Finnish Lapland](/europe/finland/lapland). It is a small place, but the setting is big: open fell country, reindeer pastures, Sámi place names, and the northern edge of [Pallas-Yllästunturi National Park](/europe/finland/lapland/kittila/pallas_yllastunturi_national_park) just across the water.

The village works because the wilderness starts almost at the door. From the [Fell Lapland Nature Centre](/europe/finland/lapland/hetta/fell_lapland_nature_centre), you can walk up [Jyppyrä Hill](/europe/finland/lapland/hetta/jyppyra_hill) for a quick view over the Ounastunturi fells, follow short nature trails, or get advice before attempting the classic Hetta-Pallas route.

Hetta also has a cultural weight that many Lapland resort villages lack. Reindeer husbandry is part of daily life in Enontekiö, and late winter brings Sámi gatherings, music, and reindeer events rather than just ski traffic. It is not a polished resort, and that is the appeal: Hetta feels like a lived-in Arctic village with real trails, real weather, and a clear sense of where Finland thins out toward Norway.

Come with time and proper clothes. Even the short walks can feel exposed when fog or wind comes over the fells.
