Salla is deep eastern Lapland, north of the Arctic Circle and close enough to Russia that the border has shaped its modern history. Its own tourism slogan, "in the middle of nowhere", is not far off: the appeal is space, snow, old forest, reindeer country, and the feeling that Finland has thinned out to its raw edge.

The strongest reason to come is the fell landscape around [Salla National Park](/europe/finland/lapland/salla/salla_national_park) and Sallatunturi. Trails, ski routes, snowshoe tracks, and quiet viewpoints make it a good low-key alternative to the busier Lapland resorts. [Salla Reindeer Park](/europe/finland/lapland/salla/salla_reindeer_park) gives the reindeer culture a practical face, while the [Giant's Kettles at Aholanvaara](/europe/finland/lapland/salla/giants_kettles_at_aholanvaara) are a strange geological reward for travelers willing to follow a rougher forest trail.

Salla also has a hard wartime story. Old Salla was ceded to the Soviet Union after the Second World War, and the population rebuilt farther west. The [Salla Museum of War and Reconstruction](/europe/finland/lapland/salla/salla_museum_of_war_and_reconstruction), the [Salpalinja Fortifications](/europe/finland/lapland/salla/salpalinja_fortifications), and small memorial sites around the municipality explain why this quiet place carries more history than its size suggests.
