Tankavaara is a tiny roadside village in Finnish Lapland, but it has a clearer reason to exist for travellers than many larger towns. Gold was found in the area in the 1930s, and the village has turned that history into a compact gold-prospecting stop on the E75 between Sodankylä and Saariselkä.

The main anchor is the **Gold Prospector Museum**, which treats Lapland's gold rush as part of a wider world of prospectors, minerals, and rough northern ambition. Around it, the Gold Village keeps the theme alive with panning and a frontier-style restaurant atmosphere that could easily be silly but mostly works because the place is so small.

Tankavaara also sits against **Urho Kekkonen National Park**, with short nature trails and longer routes starting near the village. It is not the best base for deep wilderness trekking, but for a traveller already crossing Lapland with one spare day, the mix of gold history and easy fell-edge walking is strong enough to justify the stop.
