Lappeenranta sits at the southwest corner of Lake Saimaa, Finland's largest lake, just 30 kilometres from the Russian border. For over 350 years it has been a crossing point between Finnish and Russian cultures, a garrison town guarding the eastern frontier. That history is still visible in the [Lappeenranta Fortress](/europe/finland/lappeenranta/lappeenranta_fortress) on the lakeside hill, where 18th-century ramparts now house museums, craft workshops, and summer cafes.

The [harbour](/europe/finland/lappeenranta/lappeenranta_harbour) below the fortress is the heart of summer life. [Boat trips](/europe/finland/lappeenranta/lake_saimaa_cruises) head out onto the labyrinthine Saimaa lake system, and the 43-kilometre [Saimaa Canal](/europe/finland/lappeenranta/saimaa_canal) connects the lake to the Gulf of Finland. The sandcastle competition held on the harbour beach each July draws surprisingly ambitious entries.

Lappeenranta has about 75,000 people and serves as the administrative centre of South Karelia. The [South Karelia Museum](/europe/finland/lappeenranta/south_karelia_museum) in the fortress tells the region's story, including the traumatic loss of Finnish Karelia to the Soviet Union in the 1940s. It is a pleasant city to spend a day or two, especially between June and August when the long evenings and warm lakeside make it worth lingering.
