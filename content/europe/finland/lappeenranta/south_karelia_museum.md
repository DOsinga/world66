  The museum's most affecting section covers the Winter War of 1939–40 and the subsequent Continuation War, which ended with the Soviet Union annexing the Karelian region. Over 400,000 Finnish Karelians were evacuated westward in 1940 and again in 1944 — an event that fundamentally reshaped Finnish society. The museum holds personal objects, photographs, and testimonies from the evacuated families, many of whom had lived in their villages for generations.

The South Karelia Museum within the fortress area covers the history of the South Karelia region from Neolithic times through to the present. Its strongest sections focus on the 19th and early 20th centuries, when this border region between Finland and Russia was a cultural crossroads, and on the traumatic mid-20th century when the Karelian region was lost to the Soviet Union.

The museum also has exhibits on the Battle of Lappeenranta (1741), Finnish-Russian relations over the centuries, and the daily life of pre-war Karelia. The collection of objects brought by evacuees from the lost territories is particularly poignant. The museum charges a modest admission and is well labelled in Finnish and English.
