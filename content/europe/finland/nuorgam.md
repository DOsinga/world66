Nuorgam is a tiny riverside village in far northern Finland, right beside the Norwegian border and about as far north as the European Union gets. It is not a town of museums and busy streets. The point is the edge-of-the-map feeling: the Teno River, treeless fells, long Arctic light in summer, and deep blue winter days when the sun barely troubles the horizon.

The village works best for travellers already moving through northern Lapland, [Inari](/europe/finland/inari), or Finnmark. From here you can stand at the EU's northernmost roadside marker, follow the old river route around the Alakongas rapids, or drive into the high country around Lake Pulmanki. The landscape is wide, spare, and quiet in a way that feels almost physical.

Nuorgam also sits in a living Sami river valley. The Teno has long been one of Europe's great salmon rivers, though fishing is tightly regulated and sometimes closed to protect stocks. Even if you never cast a line, the river road between Nuorgam and Utsjoki is one of Finland's most memorable drives.
