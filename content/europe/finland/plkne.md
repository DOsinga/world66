Pälkäne is a small lakeside municipality in the [Tampere](/europe/finland/tampere) region of southern Finland, with around 4,300 inhabitants. The landscape is dominated by lakes — [Pälkänevesi and Mallasvesi](/europe/finland/plkne/palkane_lakes) are the largest — connected by the Kostianvirta channel, which formed suddenly in 1604 when the water broke through between the two lake basins.

The area is historically significant as the site of the [Battle of Pälkäne](/europe/finland/plkne/battle_of_palkane) in 1713, where a Swedish-Finnish army was defeated by Russian forces in the Great Northern War, leading to eight years of Russian occupation of Finland — a period remembered as the Great Wrath. Today the Kostianvirta is a quiet riverside spot with historical markers and good walking paths. The [Pälkäne church](/europe/finland/plkne/palkane_church) in the village of Onkkaala beside the strait has served this community since medieval times.

Pälkäne is primarily a summer cottage destination for Finns, with good swimming, fishing, and rowing on the lakes. In winter, the [Sappee ski resort](/europe/finland/plkne/sappee_ski_resort) a few kilometres north offers downhill skiing and cross-country tracks, and is the nearest ski area to Tampere.
