Raahe is a small coastal city on the Gulf of Bothnia, about 75 km south of [Oulu](/europe/finland/oulu). Count Pehr Brahe founded the town in 1649, and its history as a trading and shipbuilding port is still visible in the [old town quarter](/europe/finland/raahe/old_town) — one of the best-preserved wooden town centres in Finland. The old streets and 19th-century timber houses have been carefully maintained and still function as a lived-in neighbourhood, centred on the market square of [Pekkatori](/europe/finland/raahe/pekkatori).

The [Raahe Museum](/europe/finland/raahe/raahe_museum), housed in an old warehouse, contains an unusual collection of maritime artefacts including what is believed to be the oldest surviving diving suit in the world — an 18th-century leather contraption donated by a local sea captain. Out in the Kummatti suburb, the [Matti Lepistö sculpture garden](/europe/finland/raahe/lepisto_sculpture_garden) is one of Finland's odder pieces of folk art, with more than 200 concrete and metal figures crowded into one steelworker's back yard.

The Rautaruukki steelworks dominate the modern economy. The town is not a major tourist destination, but the old wooden town and the sea breezes of the Bothnian coast make a stop worthwhile if you're travelling the west coast route.
