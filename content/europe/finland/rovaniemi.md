Rovaniemi sits right on the Arctic Circle, the capital of Finnish Lapland and the self-proclaimed hometown of Santa Claus. Gold fever brought adventurers here in the early 1800s and kicked off the town’s growth, but the German army burned Rovaniemi to the ground during their retreat in 1944. What rose from the ashes is an entirely modern city — Alvar Aalto designed the town plan in the shape of reindeer antlers.

Today Rovaniemi is a university town with good museums, including the excellent [Arktikum](/europe/finland/rovaniemi/arktikum) science centre, which tells the story of Arctic life under a long glass tunnel. The [Santa Claus Village](/europe/finland/rovaniemi/santa_claus_village) on the Arctic Circle line draws families year-round, but the real draw is the wilderness just beyond the city limits — [snowmobile safaris](/europe/finland/rovaniemi/snowmobile_safari) and [northern lights](/europe/finland/rovaniemi/northern_lights) in winter, midnight sun and hiking along the [Ounasjoki river](/europe/finland/rovaniemi/ounasjoki_river) in summer. [Reindeer safaris](/europe/finland/rovaniemi/reindeer_safari) offer a connection to Sami culture that is genuine rather than merely decorative.

A good day trip is Ranua Wildlife Park, about an hour south, home to the only polar bears in Finland alongside Arctic foxes, wolverines, and lynx.
