---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

The two main seasons are the polar night winter (late November to mid-January, when the sun does not rise) and the midnight sun summer (late May to late July, when it does not set). Both are reasons to come; the in-between months are quieter and cheaper.

**December to March** is peak winter — snow, husky rides, Northern Lights, skiing. Christmas and February school holidays are the busiest weeks. Temperatures regularly drop below -20 °C and can hit -30 °C. The aurora is most often seen from September to early April; clearer skies in February and March make those months the best of the season.

**Late June to July** is the midnight sun, with the sun above the horizon 24 hours a day for several weeks. Hiking, fishing, and berry picking peak in July. Mosquitoes are at their worst in the same window — bring repellent.

**Autumn (mid-September to mid-October)** is the ruska, when the birch and dwarf shrubs turn red, orange, and gold across the fells. Many hikers consider this the most beautiful time to walk in Lapland.

**April to mid-May and November** are the shoulders — the snow is melting or not quite arrived, many activities have stopped, and the village is sleepy. Avoid these months unless you specifically want quiet.
