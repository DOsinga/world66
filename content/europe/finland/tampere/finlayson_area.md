story: "James Finlayson, a Scottish Quaker engineer, built the first major cotton mill in Tampere in the 1820s using the power of the Tammerkoski Rapids. He sold the operation a few years later to Russian merchants who expanded it into one of the largest industrial complexes in the Russian Empire. The factory's own power plant lit Tampere's streets before most European cities had electricity, and the workers' housing it built became an entire neighbourhood — Finlayson's private city within the city."
---

The Finlayson industrial area along the north bank of the Tammerkoski Rapids is now Tampere's most vibrant cultural and commercial district. The 19th-century red-brick factory buildings have been converted into cinemas (Finnkino Plevna), restaurants, design shops, studios, and event spaces without stripping the industrial character from the architecture.

Väinö Linna Square at the heart of the complex is a good starting point for exploring. The Plevna building houses a microbrewery restaurant and cinema, and is one of the better places in the city for an evening out. The Finlayson Church on the western edge of the complex was built for the factory workers in the 1870s and is one of the most atmospheric small churches in Tampere.
