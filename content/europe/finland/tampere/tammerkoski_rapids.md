story: "The Tammerkoski Rapids are the reason Tampere exists where it does — a 635-metre stretch of fast water dropping 18 metres between Lake Näsijärvi and Lake Pyhäjärvi, which provided the power source for the textile mills that built the city. The rapids are now protected, and the walk along both banks has been restored into one of the best urban waterfronts in Finland. At night the illuminated factory buildings on both banks reflect on the dark water."

Tammerkoski is the central axis of Tampere — the rapids run through the city centre, flanked on both sides by the old factory districts of Finlayson and Tampella. The Hämeensilta bridge crossing the rapids is decorated with four bronze sculptures by Wäinö Aaltonen representing figures from Finnish folklore and history.

Walk both banks and cross both bridges (Hämeensilta and Satakunnansilta) for the full picture. The north end of the rapids, where the water flows from Lake Näsijärvi, has a fish ladder and observation point. In autumn the salmon run attracts serious watchers. The stretch from Laukontori in the south to the north end of the rapids takes about 30 minutes at a casual pace.
