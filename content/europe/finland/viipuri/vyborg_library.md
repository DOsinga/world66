The Vyborg Library is one of Alvar Aalto's most important early works and a pilgrimage site for anyone interested in 20th-century architecture. Designed for Finnish Viipuri and completed in 1935, the building introduced many of the principles — natural top lighting, organic forms, seamless indoor-outdoor integration — that would define Aalto's mature style. It is a quiet but unmistakably significant piece of architectural history.

The lecture hall's undulating wooden ceiling is the most celebrated detail: a wave of pine fins that distributes sound without the harsh reflections of a flat ceiling. The library returned to use as a public library after a long restoration completed in 2013, and it now operates in Russian. The building is open to visitors during library hours. It sits a short walk from the market square, on Pushkinskaya Street.
