Alpe d'Huez is one of cycling's sacred mountains. The [21 hairpin bends](/europe/france/alpes/alpdhuez/the_21_hairpins) climbing 1,100 metres from Bourg-d'Oisans to the resort at 1,860 metres have featured in the Tour de France since 1952, and the road is a pilgrimage for anyone who has ever watched the race. Each bend is numbered and named after a stage winner.

In winter, Alpe d'Huez is a major ski resort with over 250 kilometres of pistes, excellent sunshine (it markets itself as "the island in the sun"), and [La Sarenne](/europe/france/alpes/alpdhuez/sarenne_run) — one of the longest black runs in Europe at 16 kilometres. The [Pic Blanc](/europe/france/alpes/alpdhuez/pic_blanc) cable car rises to 3,330 metres for the views and the start of the descent. In summer the skiing gives way to mountain biking — the [Megavalanche](/europe/france/alpes/alpdhuez/megavalanche) is the biggest event of the year — hiking, and of course the climb itself.
