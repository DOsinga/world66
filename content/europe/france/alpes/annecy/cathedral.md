Annecy's cathedral is the church of Saint-Pierre, an early-sixteenth-century Franciscan building converted to cathedral use in the nineteenth century. The exterior is sober, almost austere — a single high gable, a modest doorway, no flanking towers — quite unlike the more flamboyant Savoyard parish churches.

The interior is more rewarding: a soaring single nave with rib vaults, a fine seventeenth-century organ that is still played for Sunday services, and several side chapels with paintings and sculptures connected with Saint Francis de Sales. Open daily for visits outside service times; free.
