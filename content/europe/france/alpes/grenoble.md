Grenoble sits at the confluence of the Drac and Isère rivers, hemmed in by three mountain ranges — the Chartreuse, Vercors, and Belledonne. The setting is dramatic: snow-capped peaks visible from almost every street corner, [La Bastille](/europe/france/alpes/grenoble/labastille) fortress looming above the old town. It hosted the 1968 Winter Olympics, and the outdoors still define the city's character.

The old town is compact and walkable, centred on [Place Saint-André](/europe/france/alpes/grenoble/place_saint_andre) and its medieval parliament building. The iconic [cable car](/europe/france/alpes/grenoble/cable_car_bubbles) — known as "les bulles" (the bubbles) for its transparent spherical gondolas — rises from the Jardins de Ville to the Bastille in five minutes, giving panoramic views over the city and all three mountain ranges. North of the Isère, the Saint-Laurent quarter has a bohemian edge — galleries, small restaurants, and the [Musée de l'Ancien Évêché](/europe/france/alpes/grenoble/musee_ancien_eveche) built above centuries of excavated Roman remains.

Grenoble is also a serious university city, home to one of France's largest student populations. That means affordable restaurants, lively bars, and a cultural calendar that punches above its weight. The [Musée de Grenoble](/europe/france/alpes/grenoble/museedegrenoble) holds one of France's best art collections outside Paris, while the [Musée Dauphinois](/europe/france/alpes/grenoble/museedauphinois) covers Alpine history in a beautiful hillside convent. And Grenoble claims Jacques de Vaucanson — creator of the famous [Digesting Duck](/europe/france/alpes/grenoble/vaucanson_duck), forerunner of the automaton — among its native sons. Come for the mountains, but give the city itself a couple of days — it rewards the time.
