A **bouchon** is a small, informal restaurant serving traditional Lyonnais food — the cooking of the working city before it became the gastronomic capital of France. The dishes are rich, pork-heavy, and unpretentious: **salade lyonnaise** (frisée lettuce with lardons, croutons, and a poached egg), **tablier de sapeur** (breaded tripe marinated in wine), **andouillette** (offal sausage, an acquired taste), **quenelles de brochet** in Nantua crayfish sauce, **saucisson brioché** (sausage baked inside brioche), and **tarte aux pralines** (flaky pastry filled with crushed pink pralines).

Look for the "Authentique Bouchon Lyonnais" certification sticker on the window — it means the restaurant has passed a review by a committee of Lyonnais chefs. The best concentration is in Vieux Lyon and around the Cordeliers area of the Presqu'île. A litre of red Beaujolais or a pot of rosé Côtes-du-Rhône is the standard accompaniment. Lunch is often the better-value option.
