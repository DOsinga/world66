The Queyras Regional Natural Park sits in the far southeast of France, south of Briançon and right against the Italian border. It enjoys a microclimate of its own — over 300 days of sunshine a year — and feels like a well-kept secret compared to the busier resorts of Savoie and Haute-Savoie further north.

The terrain is somewhat lower than the northern Alps but strikingly beautiful, with some of the best alpine flower meadows in France. Walking towards [Monte Viso](/europe/france/alpes/queyras/monte_viso) from the eastern edge of the park, you are almost guaranteed to spot marmots and hear their piercing warning whistles echoing across the valleys.

Within the park, the highest village is [Saint-Véran](/europe/france/alpes/queyras/saint_veran) — a contender for highest permanently inhabited village in Europe — while [Fort Queyras](/europe/france/alpes/queyras/chateau_queyras) commands the main valley, and the road over the [Col Agnel](/europe/france/alpes/queyras/col_agnel) is one of the highest paved passes in the Alps.
