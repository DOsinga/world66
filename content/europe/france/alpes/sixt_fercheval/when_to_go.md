---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

**Summer (June–September)** is the prime season. Hiking trails above the cirque open as snowmelt recedes, typically by late June; the waterfalls are at their most dramatic in May and June when the melt is running hard. July and August bring the most visitors to the Cirque du Fer-à-Cheval, but the high trails above 2,000 metres stay quiet. September is excellent: stable weather, fewer people, and the first autumn colour on the larch forests.

**Winter (December–March)** draws skiers to the Grand Massif — the linked ski area centred on Flaine and Samoëns that includes 265 kilometres of pistes. The village itself is small for a ski base; most skiers stay in Samoëns or Flaine and visit the cirque as a day trip.

**Spring (April–May)** is the shoulder: the cirque is spectacular and relatively uncrowded, but the high alpine trails can be snowbound until late May, and some mountain huts are not yet open. Waterproof boots and poles are advisable.

**Autumn (October–November)** is quiet to the point of closure — most accommodation and restaurants shut after mid-October.
