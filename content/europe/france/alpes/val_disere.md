Val-d'Isère sits high in the Tarentaise Valley, close to the Italian border and pressed against the edge of the Vanoise National Park. It is a ski resort first and a village second, but it has more shape than many purpose-built Alpine stations: stone-and-wood chalets, an old church, and a compact centre where the mountains feel close on every side.

The reason to come is the skiing. Val-d'Isère shares the Tignes-Val d'Isère domain with Tignes, giving access to about 300 kilometres of marked runs between 1,550 and more than 3,400 metres. The altitude makes the season unusually reliable, while the terrain has a proper bite: experts come for off-piste routes and the [Face de Bellevarde](face_de_bellevarde), but there are enough broad blue runs and ski-school areas to keep mixed groups happy.

Summer is quieter and often underrated. Lifts, buses, and high roads open up walks into the Vanoise, mountain-bike trails on the linked bike park, and the long climb to the [Col de l'Iseran](col_de_liseran), one of the great paved passes of the Alps. It is not the place for a gentle cafe holiday, but if you want high mountain air with serious outdoor options, Val-d'Isère earns its reputation.
