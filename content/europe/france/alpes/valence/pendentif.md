The Pendentif is a small but remarkable Renaissance funerary chapel standing just outside the cathedral. Built in 1548, it is named for its pendent keystone, a decorative element hanging from the vault interior that shows the influence of Italian Renaissance architecture. The chapel is tiny — essentially a single vaulted space — but the quality of the stonework and the confidence of the design are exceptional for a provincial commission of this date.

The chapel is generally kept locked but visible through the gate, and the exterior itself is worth examining closely. The carved details are in excellent condition, which is not always the case with 16th-century stonework exposed to the Rhône valley weather. It stands as evidence that Valence was, in the 16th century, connected to the wider European humanist culture — a university town with ambitions above its size.
