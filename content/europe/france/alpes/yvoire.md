Yvoire sits on a small promontory on the French side of Lac Léman, about twenty kilometres south-west of Geneva. It is one of the most intact medieval villages in the French Alps: the 14th-century walls are largely complete, the streets within them are narrow and cobbled, and the stone houses have not been rebuilt into something unrecognisable. In summer it draws visitors from across the region, but arrive on a weekday morning in spring or autumn and you have much of the place to yourself.

The village is centred on the Château d'Yvoire, a 14th-century castle that is still privately owned and occupied by the same family that has held it for seven centuries. The exterior, with its square keep and round towers, is open to admire from the village streets; parts of the grounds are open seasonally. The Jardin des Cinq Sens — the Garden of the Five Senses — occupies the old kitchen garden and orchard behind the ramparts, planted with herbs, vegetables, and flowers chosen for their smell, texture, and flavour. It is a genuinely lovely place to spend an hour.

The waterfront is a natural gathering point. Small restaurants line the quayside, and the view across the lake towards the Swiss Jura and the Nyon coast is one of the better ones on this part of the lake. The lakeside path heading east from the village is quiet and well-maintained.

The easiest way to reach Yvoire from [Nyon](/europe/switzerland/nyon) is by CGN lake boat: the crossing takes about twenty minutes and runs on a regular summer timetable. Driving around the lake takes longer and involves border formalities. The boat is the right approach.
