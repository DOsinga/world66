Bidart sits on the cliffs between [Biarritz](/europe/france/aquitaine/biarritz) and Guéthary, and is the highest point on the Basque coast — the village square, with its church, fronton and whitewashed houses under red timber, is a genuine Basque village rather than a resort dressed as one. The *fronton* is not decorative: pelota is played on it.

Below the cliffs are six beaches, and they are the reason most people come. The Plage du Centre and the long sandy stretch at Uhabia to the south are the swimming and surfing beaches; Erretegia to the north is smaller and sheltered by the headland. Uhabia is where the surf schools teach, because it is sand rather than the rock shelf that makes Guéthary a serious wave and a bad classroom.

The coastal path runs the whole way through, and walking it north to Biarritz or south to Guéthary and Saint-Jean-de-Luz is the best way to see this stretch — about an hour each way, with the Pyrenees visible inland on a clear day.
