Napoleon himself never saw the bridge.

The Pont de Pierre is the oldest bridge across the Garonne in Bordeaux, built between 1810 and 1822. Constructed of local stone in the form of seventeen arches — the stone facade gives it an elegance that concrete bridges lack — it was for over a century the only fixed crossing of the Garonne in the city.

Today the bridge carries trams and pedestrians but no private cars, which makes it a pleasant way to cross to the Right Bank on foot. The view from the bridge in either direction is excellent: south toward the sweep of the 18th-century waterfront, north toward the towers and spires of the old city.
