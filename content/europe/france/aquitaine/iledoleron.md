The Ile d'Oleron is the largest French island on the Atlantic coast, connected to the mainland by a toll-free bridge. It is enormously popular with French holidaymakers in summer but still largely unknown to international visitors — which is part of its charm.

The island runs about 30 km north to south and is remarkably varied. The western coast has long sandy beaches backed by dunes, with good surf. The eastern side faces the sheltered waters between the island and the mainland, where [oyster beds](/europe/france/aquitaine/iledoleron/huitres_doleron) stretch out at low tide — Oléron oysters are considered among France's finest, and you can buy them straight from the producers at the port of La Cayenne. The working fishing port of [La Cotinière](/europe/france/aquitaine/iledoleron/la_cotiniere) on the western shore has the island's best morning fish market.

The main town, [Saint-Pierre-d'Oléron](/europe/france/aquitaine/iledoleron/saint_pierre_doleron), has a lively market and a striking Romanesque lantern of the dead in the cemetery. The northern tip of the island is dominated by the [Phare de Chassiron](/europe/france/aquitaine/iledoleron/phare_de_chassiron), with panoramic views over the Atlantic. [Fort Boyard](/europe/france/aquitaine/iledoleron/fort_boyard), the famous sea fortress, sits just offshore to the east and is visible from [Boyardville](/europe/france/aquitaine/iledoleron/boyardville) harbour, where boat tours depart to circle it in summer.

Cycling is the best way to get around — the island has an excellent network of bike paths through pine forests and along the shore.
