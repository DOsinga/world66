La Rochelle is one of the most attractive coastal towns in France — a handsome port city with a seventeenth-century waterfront, arcaded streets, and a rich maritime history. The [Vieux Port](/europe/france/aquitaine/larochelle/vieux_port), flanked by the medieval [Tour Saint-Nicolas](/europe/france/aquitaine/larochelle/tour_saint_nicolas) and [Tour de la Chaîne](/europe/france/aquitaine/larochelle/tour_de_la_chaine), with the [Grosse Horloge](/europe/france/aquitaine/larochelle/grosse_horloge) gateway behind, is the heart of the town and one of the most photographed waterfronts on the Atlantic coast.

The centre is compact and largely pedestrianised, with [arcaded streets](/europe/france/aquitaine/larochelle/rue_des_merciers) full of independent shops along the Rue du Palais. The [Musée du Nouveau Monde](/europe/france/aquitaine/larochelle/musee_du_nouveau_monde), housed in a former ship-owner's mansion, documents La Rochelle's role in the transatlantic trade — including the slave trade in which the town was deeply implicated. The [Hôtel de Ville](/europe/france/aquitaine/larochelle/hotel_de_ville) is one of the finest Renaissance buildings in France. The [Aquarium](/europe/france/aquitaine/larochelle/aquarium) near the harbour is excellent and popular with families.

La Rochelle's history is dramatic — it was a Huguenot stronghold besieged by Cardinal Richelieu in 1627–28 from the [Fort Louis](/europe/france/aquitaine/larochelle/fort_louis) built specifically for the purpose, an episode that features in Dumas's Three Musketeers. The town was also an important departure point for settlers heading to New France (Canada) — and one of France's most active slave-trade ports, a history confronted directly by the [Musée du Nouveau Monde](/europe/france/aquitaine/larochelle/musee_du_nouveau_monde) and the [Statue of Toussaint Louverture](/europe/france/aquitaine/larochelle/statue_toussaint_louverture) near the waterfront. The Second World War left scars too: the [U-boat pens at La Pallice](/europe/france/aquitaine/larochelle/u_boat_pens_la_pallice) on the western edge of the city are one of the largest surviving Nazi concrete structures, and the [Bunker de la Rochelle](/europe/france/aquitaine/larochelle/bunker_de_la_rochelle) hidden in the old town preserves a German command post. The [Jardin des Plantes](/europe/france/aquitaine/larochelle/jardin_des_plantes) behind the natural history museum is one of the few shaded retreats in the centre on a summer afternoon.

Just south of La Rochelle is the [Île d'Oléron](/europe/france/aquitaine/iledoleron), France's second-largest island, with long sandy beaches. The [Île de Ré](/europe/france/aquitaine/ile_de_r) to the north is more fashionable and busier in summer.
