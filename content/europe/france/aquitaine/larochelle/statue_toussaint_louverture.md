The statue of Toussaint Louverture stands in La Rochelle as a pointed counterpoint to the city's history as one of France's leading slave-trade ports. The bronze work — by a prominent African sculptor — depicts Louverture in a heroic posture, holding the Haitian constitution he helped draft. Haiti's 1804 independence was the first successful slave revolt in history, and Louverture's defeat of Napoleon's expeditionary force remains one of the most remarkable military upsets of the era.

The statue sits near the waterfront, not far from the Musée du Nouveau Monde, which documents La Rochelle's extensive involvement in the transatlantic slave trade. Together, they make for a pointed historical circuit: the wealth of the old merchant houses, and the human cost on which it was built.
