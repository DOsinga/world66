The street name is not decoration: it was on Rue de l'Indépendance-Américaine (then part of the Hôtel de la Guerre) that France signed the Treaty of Paris on 3 September 1783, recognising American independence and ending the American Revolutionary War. The street runs through the town of Versailles, a reminder that the palace complex was not just the setting for French history but for international diplomacy on a global scale.

France's support for the American revolutionaries — financially, militarily, and diplomatically — was one of the most consequential acts of Louis XVI's reign. The treaty signed here recognised not just American independence but effectively announced the limits of British power. It also helped bankrupt France, accelerating the financial crisis that led directly to the Revolution six years later.

The street today is unremarkable — 18th-century town houses, shops, a couple of cafés. But it connects two events that changed the world, separated by six years and a few hundred metres: the treaty that secured American liberty in 1783, and the tennis court that began the end of French monarchy in 1789.
