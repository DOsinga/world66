The Auvergne region surrounding Vichy is one of the most dramatically underrated landscapes in France — a chain of extinct volcanoes (the Chaîne des Puys), river gorges, medieval villages, and vast empty uplands. Vichy makes a decent base for exploring it.

**Clermont-Ferrand** (45 minutes by train) is the regional capital, with a striking black-lava cathedral and good access to the Chaîne des Puys volcanic park. The Puy de Dôme, the most accessible of the volcanoes, can be reached by a rack railway from a point near the city.

**The Gorges de l'Allier**, the deep river canyon south of Vichy, are accessible by car and offer dramatic scenery along a route that runs south through Issoire and into the volcanic uplands. The train line through the gorge is itself an engineering achievement worth knowing about.

**Moulins** (an hour north by train) is the old capital of the Bourbonnais region, with an exceptional collection of medieval art in its cathedral treasury and a good museum. It is quiet and often overlooked, which is part of the appeal.

**Cusset**, just 3 km east of Vichy, is a small town with a medieval core quite different from Vichy's Belle Époque character. The Église Saint-Saturnin has notable Romanesque features. It makes a reasonable short excursion on foot or by bike along the river.
