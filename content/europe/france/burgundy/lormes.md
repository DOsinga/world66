Lormes is a small hilltop town in the Nièvre département, on the western edge of the [Morvan](/europe/france/burgundy/morvan) regional natural park. Recognised as a "Cité de Caractère de Bourgogne-Franche-Comté" in 2024, it makes a practical base for exploring the Morvan's forests and lakes, or a natural stopover on the way to [Vézelay](/europe/france/burgundy/vezelay), about 40 kilometres to the north.

The town is quiet and agricultural, but it has more texture than it first appears. The [Église Saint-Alban](/europe/france/burgundy/lormes/eglise_saint_alban) — a confident neo-Romanesque building from 1865 — stands on a rise above the old centre, and the [Gorges de Narvau](/europe/france/burgundy/lormes/gorges_de_narvau) cut through the hillside just south of town, giving walkers a surprisingly wild half-day route down to the [Étang du Goulot](/europe/france/burgundy/lormes/etang_du_goulot), one of the Morvan's seven official lakes. The weekly market on Tuesday mornings is the social hub of the surrounding cantons.

The [Lac de Chaumeçon](/europe/france/burgundy/lormes/lac_de_chaumecon), a forest reservoir about 7 kilometres east, is the nearest swimming lake and the best reason to linger for more than a night. The [Château de Villemolin](/europe/france/burgundy/lormes/chateau_de_villemolin) in Anthien, a few kilometres to the west, adds a listed historic house to the list of reasons to base yourself here rather than passing through.

The municipal campsite is cheap and well-maintained, with hot water. Lormes has a handful of shops and a couple of restaurants, enough for a comfortable two-night stay while you explore the surrounding Morvan countryside.
