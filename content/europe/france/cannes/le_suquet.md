  Le Suquet was the original settlement that became Cannes, built on the hill above the natural harbour where Provençal fishermen lived. When Lord Brougham, the British Lord Chancellor, was stranded here in 1834 by a cholera quarantine and decided to stay the winter, he effectively invented Cannes as a resort. Within a generation the elegant Croisette below had been built for his fellow aristocrats.

Le Suquet is the old quarter of Cannes, climbing the hill above the Vieux Port in a tangle of narrow cobbled lanes, terrace restaurants, and medieval stonework. It was here before the grand hotels, before the film festival, before any of it — a genuinely old Provençal hill village that happens to sit above one of the most famous seaside boulevards in the world.

At the top stands the 14th-century Tour du Suquet and the 12th-century Chapelle Sainte-Anne, with a sweeping panorama of the bay and the Lérins Islands. The Musée de la Castre occupies the old castle and holds a collection of Mediterranean antiquities and ethnographic objects from around the world. The streets below, especially Rue Saint-Antoine, are lined with restaurants that tend to be better value and less touristy than those on the Croisette.
