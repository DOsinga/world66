Millau sits in the valley of the Tarn, a pleasant town in the Aveyron département that was long known for two things: leather glove production and appalling traffic jams on the route south. The second problem was solved spectacularly in 2004 with the opening of the [Millau Viaduct](/europe/france/centre/auvergne/millau/millau_viaduct) — the world's tallest bridge, designed by Norman Foster, its slender cable-stayed pylons rising over 340 metres above the valley floor. It is as much a work of art as a feat of engineering, and worth seeing from below as well as driving across.

The town itself is agreeable without being a major destination. The [old centre](/europe/france/centre/auvergne/millau/old_town) has some attractive squares, the weekly market is good, and the [Musée de Millau](/europe/france/centre/auvergne/millau/musee_de_millau) covers the local trades of leather and Roman pottery. The surrounding Grands Causses — vast limestone plateaus cut by deep gorges — offer excellent hiking, climbing, and canoeing on the Tarn. The [Gorges du Tarn](/europe/france/centre/auvergne/millau/gorges_du_tarn) and nearby gorges of the Dourbie and Jonte are spectacular, and the strange dolomite city of the [Chaos de Montpellier-le-Vieux](/europe/france/centre/auvergne/millau/chaos_de_montpellier_le_vieux) is twenty minutes east. The Roman pottery site of [La Graufesenque](/europe/france/centre/auvergne/millau/la_graufesenque) is a short walk from the centre, and [Roquefort-sur-Soulzon](/europe/france/centre/auvergne/millau/roquefort_caves), where the famous cheese is aged in natural caves, is just a short drive south.
