Pontgibaud is a small medieval village about 25 km west of Clermont-Ferrand in the Auvergne, straddling the Sioule river at the edge of the Chaîne des Puys volcanic range. The village is easy to miss on the road west toward the Puy de Dôme, but it rewards a stop with a genuinely impressive castle, a fine Romanesque church, and some of the best riverside walking in the region.

The main attraction is [Château Dauphin](/europe/france/centre/auvergne/pontgibaud/chateau_dauphin), a well-preserved example of medieval military architecture built in the 12th century by Robert Dauphin, Count of Auvergne, to defend the Sioule bridge. The keep and six towers survive from the original fortification, later completed in the 15th century by Field Marshal Gilbert III de La Fayette. The château is still inhabited by descendants of the Moré de Pontgibaud family, who have owned it since 1756, and is open for visits in summer.

The [Sioule Valley Walk](/europe/france/centre/auvergne/pontgibaud/sioule_valley_walk) offers easy rambling along a gorge of volcanic basalt and connects to the GR 441 long-distance trail through the Chaîne des Puys. The [Église Saint-Pierre](/europe/france/centre/auvergne/pontgibaud/eglise_saint_pierre) in the village centre is a solid example of Auvergne Romanesque, built in the same dark volcanic stone as the château.
