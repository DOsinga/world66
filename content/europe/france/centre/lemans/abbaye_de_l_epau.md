Abbaye Royale de l'Épau is the calm, stone-built counterpoint to Le Mans' old city walls and racing mythology. Founded in the 13th century by Berengaria of Navarre, widow of Richard the Lionheart, the Cistercian abbey still has the clean lines, cloistered spaces, and long historical shadows that make monastic architecture so satisfying.

The abbey sits just outside the centre, so it is easy to combine with the Plantagenet quarter if you have a full day in Le Mans. Check opening times before going, especially outside peak season, and look for temporary exhibitions or concerts: the buildings are often at their best when they are being used, not merely admired.
