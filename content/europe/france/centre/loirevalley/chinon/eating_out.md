---
title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

Chinon eats and drinks well, with a small but reliable cluster of restaurants in the old town. The local specialities are **fouées** — small puff-pastry pockets stuffed with rillettes, beans, or goat's cheese, baked in a wood-fired oven — and **rillons**, slow-cooked chunks of pork belly that come as a starter or in salads. Goat's cheese from Sainte-Maure or Selles-sur-Cher pairs well with the Cabernet Franc reds the region produces.

**Les Années 30** on Rue Voltaire is a steady choice for traditional Loire cooking with good wine pairings. **L'Ardoise** offers smaller, more modern plates and a curated Chinon list. For something quick, the fouée bakeries on Rue Voltaire serve a dozen filled pastries with a glass of wine for a fair price.

Most kitchens close between 2 and 7 pm — eat lunch by 1 or wait for dinner.
