The streets between the seafront and the railway line are a free outdoor museum of Belle Époque and Art Deco seaside architecture. The villas were built between roughly 1880 and the 1930s by wealthy Parisians, Nantais, and Anglo-Saxons who took the train down for the summer.

Walk the **Avenue des Pétrels**, **Avenue de la Vendée**, and **Avenue de Concarneau** for the best concentration. Look for the slate roofs with elaborate cresting, the polychrome brickwork, the half-timbered Norman pastiches, and the wrought-iron balconies. The tourist office sells a self-guided walking map.
