Bastia eats well and locally. Corsican cuisine stands apart from mainland French cooking, drawing on the island's own larder: charcuterie from the native black pig (*porc nustrale*), brocciu (a fresh whey cheese made from ewe's milk), chestnuts in pasta, soup, beer, and liqueur, and the aromatic herbs of the *maquis* — rosemary, thyme, wild fennel, and the dense shrubs that give the Corsican countryside its distinctive scent. Seafood is fresh and simply prepared.

The [cafes and restaurants of the Vieux Port](cafes_du_vieux_port) are the most atmospheric places to eat in Bastia, with tables on the cobbled quayside and menus built around the day's catch. The Terra Vecchia lanes nearby have a cluster of more traditional restaurants where [Corsican home cooking](a_famiglia) is done seriously and cheaply.

For a Corsican charcuterie and cheese board with a glass of Patrimonio, almost any of the cafes around Place Saint-Nicolas will do the job competently. The Muscat du Cap Corse — a sweet wine from the peninsula north of Bastia — is worth trying with dessert.
