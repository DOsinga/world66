The Place Saint-Nicolas is Bastia's grand public square, a vast tree-lined expanse running parallel to the coast at the northern end of the city centre. Over a kilometre long, it is one of the largest public squares in France, shaded by plane trees and overlooking the ferry terminal and open sea. The pace here is deliberately unhurried: locals play pétanque on the gravel, old men occupy the benches for hours, and the cafes on the west side stay busy from mid-morning to midnight.

The square is the social hub of Bastia. Market stalls set up here on Sunday mornings, and it doubles as a venue for concerts, festivals, and national celebrations. It makes a useful orientation point in the city: the old town and Vieux Port lie to the south, the ferry port to the north, and the main commercial streets of the Centre Ville extend inland from its western edge.
