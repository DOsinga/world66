On 26 May 1993 at Munich's Olympiastadion, Olympique de Marseille did what no French club had done before — and what none has managed since. A single goal, Basile Boli's thumping header from Abedi Pelé's corner in the 43rd minute, was enough to defeat AC Milan and claim the UEFA Champions League. From that evening forward, the phrase *À Jamais Les Premiers* — Forever the First — became the motto of every Marseillais who ever wore blue-and-white.

The marker sits in the shadow of the Stade Vélodrome, the 67,000-seat cauldron in the 8th arrondissement that is the temple of OM and, on match days, one of the loudest sporting venues in Europe. The phrase is not merely a boast; it is a statement of identity. In a city with a reputation for pride and a complicated relationship with Paris and with France's football establishment, the 1993 triumph remains the single fact that cannot be argued away. The coach that season was Raymond Goethals; the squad included Rudi Völler, Alen Bokšić, and Didier Deschamps.

Whether rendered on a plaque, a mosaic, or a painted installation, the tribute belongs to a city that organises much of its civic emotion around the club. On any given Sunday in the season, half of Marseille's buses and trams seem to be converging on this stadium. Finding the marker, you understand that for Marseillais, 1993 is not history — it is an ongoing fact, renewed every time the words are read aloud.
