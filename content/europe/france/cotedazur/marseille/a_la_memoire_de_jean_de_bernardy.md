This is the kind of memorial cities raise for someone the neighborhood loved, not the whole nation. A small stone tablet, tucked into the northern arrondissements well above the Vieux-Port. No street-level inscription, so who Jean de Bernardy actually was stays a bit of a mystery.

The name hints at a family with deep Marseille roots. He could've been a doctor, a teacher, a resistance fighter, a local politician — the kind of person who shapes a quarter without ever making the history books.

What matters is where you find him: the 13th and 14th, working-class districts proud of their own story. This is the real Marseille, quieter and rarely visited, where most of France's second city actually lives.
