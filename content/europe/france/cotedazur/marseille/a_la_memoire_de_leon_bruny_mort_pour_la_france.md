"Died for France" sounds like sentiment. It's actually official French law — a designation the state grants to soldiers and resistance fighters killed in combat, unlocking honours and pensions for the family. So someone filed the papers, chased the recognition, and pinned that category to this exact name on this exact wall.

Léon Bruny's plaque sits in Marseille's working-class east. The name reads like an old Provençal family, but there's no date, no unit — so pick your war. The Western Front trenches, North Africa, the Resistance maquis: the formula covers all of them.

Here's what's certain — the state rarely paid for plaques like this. Families did. Neighbours, veterans' associations, people who actually knew the man. Bruny mattered enough that someone cast him in stone and hung him where you'd have to notice. Don't walk past without a look.
