The Carasso name is Sephardic, carried by families expelled from Spain in 1492 who scattered across North Africa, the Ottoman Empire, and southern France's port cities. That three brothers from one household died for France tells you how deeply this community wove itself into French life.

The plaque never names the war, but Marseille's Jews carried a double weight in the 1940s: sons killed in uniform, then Vichy. In January 1943 the Nazis razed the old Jewish quarter in the Panier and deported thousands to the death camps. This is a memorial to a community that gave everything to a republic that didn't always protect it.

You'll find it in a residential neighbourhood north of the centre, drawing almost no one. That emptiness makes it hit harder. Three names, one family, one city.
