Rawa-Ruska — now Rava-Ruska, in western Ukraine near the Polish border — was a German transit and holding camp in occupied Eastern Europe that served as an intermediate stop on the route to the Bełżec extermination camp. For the families of those who passed through it, the name carries the full weight of the Shoah in the South of France. French Jews and others deported from the Southern (Vichy) Zone were transported eastward through this camp from 1942 onward, and very few survived the journey that followed.

The Marseille deportations of 1942–1943 represent one of the most traumatic chapters in the city's modern history. In January 1943, in an operation coordinated between the SS and the Vichy police, German and French authorities carried out a mass roundup in the Vieux-Port area and the ancient neighbourhood of Le Panier, arresting thousands of Jews, foreign nationals, and others deemed undesirable. An estimated 1,600 Jewish residents of Marseille were deported to the death camps during the war years. The city's Jewish community, many of whom had deep roots in Marseille or had fled there as refugees from elsewhere in Europe, was devastated.

This plaque — located in the eastern arrondissements near the coordinates given, in a district that may have housed some of those caught in the roundups — serves as a local act of memory in a city that has worked to acknowledge its wartime history. Standing before it, the abstract statistics of the Holocaust acquire the weight of specific names, specific streets, and specific absences.
