Here's the twist most people miss: Frank Sinatra's 'My Way' began life as a French song about a fading relationship. Claude François — 'Cloclo' to the whole country — co-wrote it as *Comme d'habitude* in 1967. Paul Anka heard it, bought the rights, rewrote every word, and handed it to Sinatra. François kept singing his own version, a sadder story entirely.

He grew up right here after his family moved from Egypt, learning drums and playing in local bands before Paris made him a star. Through the '60s and '70s he owned French radio with his backing group the Claudettes — *Alexandrie Alexandra*, *Le Téléphone pleure*, *Belles, Belles, Belles*. Marseille claims him as its own, and this memorial in the northern arrondissements says so.

He died in March 1978, electrocuted by a bedside lamp while standing in his bathtub. He was 39. France was gutted, Marseille especially — and this spot insists his story started on the Mediterranean shore, not in a Paris studio.
