Claude François was born in 1939 in Ismailia, Egypt, to a French family, and it was in Marseille that he came of age after his family relocated to the city. Before he became one of France's most adored pop stars — known to the nation simply as Cloclo — he was a teenager in Marseille learning the drums, playing in local bands, and absorbing the Mediterranean energy that would later give his performances their irrepressible warmth. The city claims him, and this memorial, placed in the northern arrondissements near where coordinates suggest a neighbourhood of ordinary life, reflects that local pride.

His career reached national scale in the early 1960s when he moved to Paris and began releasing hits with his backing group the Claudettes. Songs like *Alexandrie Alexandra*, *Le Téléphone pleure*, and *Belles, Belles, Belles* made him a fixture on French radio and television for nearly two decades. But his greatest international legacy arrived in 1967 when he co-wrote *Comme d'habitude*, a song about a relationship wearing thin. An English lyricist named Paul Anka heard it, bought the rights, rewrote the words entirely, and gave it to Frank Sinatra — who recorded it as *My Way*. François never begrudged Sinatra the version, though he continued to perform the original French lyrics, which tell a very different, sadder story.

Claude François died in Paris in March 1978, electrocuted by a bedside lamp while standing in his bathtub. He was 39. The shock of his death was enormous in France, and in Marseille it was felt with particular sharpness. A memorial in his adopted city is a way of insisting that his story did not begin in the Paris studios — it began here, on the shore of the Mediterranean, in a city that recognises its own.
