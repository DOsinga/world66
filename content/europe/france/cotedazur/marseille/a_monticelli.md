Adolphe Monticelli layered pigment so thick his canvases looked encrusted with jewels. He sold almost nothing, drank hard, and died broke in Marseille in 1886 — the same year Van Gogh reached Paris and saw his work.

Van Gogh owned twelve Monticellis and wrote about him with open admiration, calling him a direct predecessor. When Van Gogh and Gauguin talked about Marseille, they were partly talking about this man — the painter who cracked open the colour and technique that led straight to Post-Impressionism.

Here's the irony: Marseille's own critics dismissed him as eccentric and marginal his whole life. Turns out he stood at the origin of one of modern art's biggest shifts. He painted the city; the city finally built him a monument.
