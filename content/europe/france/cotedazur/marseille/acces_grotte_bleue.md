The Grotte Bleue — the Blue Grotto — is one of several sea caves punctuating the limestone cliffs between Marseille and Cassis. Like the more famous caves of the Mediterranean, it takes its name from the play of light on the water inside: sunlight filtered through a submerged entrance illuminates the interior with an otherworldly blue glow, particularly striking at mid-morning when the angle is right. The cave sits within the coastal zone that adjoins the Calanques National Park, in waters whose clarity owes much to the protected status of the surrounding calanques.

Access is by boat or sea kayak from the Marseille coast — there is no land approach. Several outfitters operating from the Old Port and from the beaches near Pointe Rouge run guided kayak excursions that include the Grotte Bleue as one stop among the sea caves and calanques of the southern shoreline. Independent kayakers with experience in open Mediterranean conditions can reach it under their own power; the sea state here can change quickly, and the cave entrance is unsuitable in any swell. Boat tours from the Vieux-Port also pass the cave on their way to Les Calanques.

This access point marks the shore-side departure for organised excursions to the cave. It is worth arriving early: the light effect is best in the morning, tourist numbers are lower, and the summer mistral, when it arrives, closes kayak access rapidly. Swimmers should note that the water temperature, even in July and August, stays cool enough to surprise those accustomed to bathing beaches further east.
