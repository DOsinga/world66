title: Allée Gaby Deslys

Gabrielle Caire — better known as Gaby Deslys — out-earned every other woman on the music-hall circuit. In a single decade she jumped from provincial variety halls to London's Palace Theatre and New York's Winter Garden. She's the one who taught Broadway the shimmy, and sold out London runs that ran for months.

Then came the deposed king. Her affair with Manuel II of Portugal, exiled to England after 1910, kept her splashed across Europe's gossip pages for two years. She died here in January 1920 at just 38, undone by the flu pandemic — and the city paraded her coffin through the streets to a monument she'd designed herself.

Today the alley bearing her name sits quiet and residential in the 13th, giving away nothing. France has mostly forgotten her, save for a footnote about the woman who brought the shimmy to America.
