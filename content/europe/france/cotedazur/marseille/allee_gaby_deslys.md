At the height of her fame, Gabrielle Caire — performing as Gaby Deslys — earned more than any woman on the music-hall circuit. Born in Marseille in 1881, she worked her way from provincial variety halls to the stages of London's Palace Theatre and New York's Winter Garden in the space of a decade, becoming the act that producers booked months in advance. She introduced the shimmy to Broadway audiences who had never seen it, and sold out London runs that lasted months.

Her affair with King Manuel II of Portugal — deposed in 1910 and exiled to England — turned her into the subject of European gossip pages for two years, a scandal that only amplified her fame. She died in Marseille in January 1920, aged 38, weakened by complications from the influenza pandemic. The city gave her a public funeral procession through the streets; she was interred in the Saint-Pierre cemetery beneath a monument she had commissioned herself before she died.

The alley in the 13th arrondissement that carries her name is quiet and residential, nothing about it advertising the extraordinary trajectory of the woman it commemorates. She remains largely unknown in France today, her reputation surviving mainly in specialist histories of the Belle Époque stage and in occasional references to the shimmy's introduction to America.
