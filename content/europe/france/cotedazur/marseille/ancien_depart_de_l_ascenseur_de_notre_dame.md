From 1892, you didn't have to walk up to Notre-Dame de la Garde — a funicular climbed the rock face straight to the basilica terrace, 162 metres up. Look closely and you can still spot part of the old track cut into the cliff. This is where the lower station stood.

Sailors loved it. They'd ride up to give thanks or leave votive offerings before shipping out, and the devout and merely curious packed in alongside them. Then cars took over, the summit road improved, and in 1967 the whole thing shut down and got dismantled — leaving just this spot.

Now it's your feet, a car, or the tourist petit train from the Vieux-Port. Do the walk if you can. The climb rewards you with the Frioul islands, Cap Croisette, and the Étoile massif spread out from the terrace.
