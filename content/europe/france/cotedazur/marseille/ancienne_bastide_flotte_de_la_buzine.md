This is the "Château de ma mère" — the big country house Pagnol's family had to cross, partly illegally, to reach their own rented bastide nearby. His mother got caught trespassing, and her distress became one of the defining scenes of his childhood books. The whole autobiographical tetralogy circles back to these summer walks.

Here's the twist: Pagnol actually bought the place in 1941, mid-occupation, to build his own Provençal Hollywood. He had the money and the track record to pull it off. Then liberation shifted everything, the film industry piled back into Paris, and the studio never happened. The bastide sat empty, and after his widow sold it in 1973 it slid into dereliction.

Marseille bought it in the 1990s and turned it into a cinema and cultural centre for his work — screenings, exhibitions, the grounds kept up. So the house Pagnol bought and never got to use became the biggest monument to his life in the city.
