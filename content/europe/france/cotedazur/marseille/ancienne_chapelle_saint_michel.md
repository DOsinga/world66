Medieval Marseille planted Saint-Michel chapels at the edgy spots — hilltop spurs, road junctions, the frontier between town and wild garrigue. Fitting, since Michael is the warrior archangel who weighs souls. This one sits out in the rolling 10th arrondissement, well clear of the grand city-centre churches.

Then came 1789. The Revolution nationalised religious property, and small chapels like this got sold off, secularised, turned into stables and warehouses — or simply demolished. The survivors kept their vaulted ceilings as a quiet clue to what they once were.

Don't expect a spectacle. The façade is plain stone, maybe a small oculus or an arched Provençal doorway, no bell-tower shouting for attention. It's one to clock in passing, not to make a pilgrimage for.
