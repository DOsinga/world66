The name comes from the Présentation nuns who held this site before it became a prison. It ran from the 17th century all the way through World War II. Then, after 1942, Vichy and the German occupiers took control — and turned it into a holding pen for Jews and political prisoners headed to the camps.

Marseille was the main staging point for deportations across France's Mediterranean zone. The port, the rail lines, the existing prisons — all of it made a machine. Tens of thousands passed through the city's detention network between 1942 and 1944. The January 1943 roundup, the one that flattened the old Vieux-Port, fed straight into it. Most who entered the Présentines under occupation never came back.

Here's the hard part: the same city that ran this also sheltered thousands of refugees, and the resistance was busy here too. Both things were true at once, sometimes in buildings next door to each other. The walls remember it whether the city wants them to or not.
