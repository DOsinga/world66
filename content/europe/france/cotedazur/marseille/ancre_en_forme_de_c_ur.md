Ancre en forme de cœur sits on the Quai du Port, the long harbourside promenade that faces the Vieux-Port and the hill of Notre-Dame de la Garde beyond. The piece does exactly what its name promises: the familiar silhouette of a ship's anchor — stock, ring, flukes and all — has been formed into a heart shape, collapsing two of Marseille's most persistent symbols into a single object. The anchor represents the city's identity as one of the great ports of the Mediterranean, a place where sailors have arrived, departed and failed to return for more than two millennia. The heart points upward to the basilica on the hill, whose interior is lined with ex-votos — votive offerings from sailors and their families, many of them small painted hearts left in gratitude or supplication to Our Lady of the Guard.

The ex-voto tradition at Notre-Dame de la Garde is one of the most vivid survivals of Mediterranean popular devotion. Sailors who survived a wreck, families who waited for a vessel to return, fishermen who came home from the Alborán or the Ligurian Sea against the odds — all left their tokens at the basilica. Hundreds of model ships hang from the vaulted ceiling; the walls are covered with painted plaques, photographs, and heart-shaped offerings in silver, tin and painted wood. This sculpture on the quay below takes those two recurring symbols and welds them into a single object, anchoring gratitude and hope in iron on the waterfront where journeys begin and end.

The Quai du Port is a busy, tourist-facing stretch of the city, but Ancre en forme de cœur earns its place here rather than feeling merely decorative. It is small enough to discover by chance while walking between the ferry terminal and the cours Honoré d'Estienne d'Orves, and large enough to stop you when you do. Seen against the backdrop of the old harbour and the hillside basilica, the symbolism clicks into place with unusual tidiness.
