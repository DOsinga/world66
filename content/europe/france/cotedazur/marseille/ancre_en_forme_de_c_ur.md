It's exactly what the name says: a ship's anchor — stock, ring, flukes and all — twisted into a heart. Two of Marseille's stubbornest symbols welded into one. The anchor is the port, 2,600 years of sailors arriving, leaving and failing to come back. The heart points up the hill to Notre-Dame de la Garde.

Up in that basilica, the walls tell you why. Sailors who survived wrecks, families who waited, fishermen who beat the odds — they all left tokens. Hundreds of model ships dangle from the ceiling, and the walls drip with painted plaques and little hearts in silver, tin and wood. This anchor on the quay just gathers all of it into one lump of iron.

You'll probably stumble on it walking between the ferry terminal and the cours Honoré d'Estienne d'Orves. Small enough to miss, big enough to stop you. Line it up against the old harbour and the hillside basilica and the whole thing clicks.
