At 9am on 5 November 2018, two 19th-century apartment buildings at 63 and 65 Rue d'Aubagne collapsed without warning. Eight people died. Inspectors had already flagged both as structurally unsafe — nobody fixed them, nobody evacuated the tenants.

The fallout was brutal. Within weeks the city evacuated over 2,000 people from similarly dangerous buildings, leaving many homeless overnight. Mayor Jean-Claude Gaudin took the blame, and it helped end his decades in power.

Residents planted the Arbre de la Dignité — the Tree of Dignity — as a spontaneous memorial in the days after. Every 5 November, people gather here. The name makes its point: decent housing is a right, not charity, and this was a moral failure, not just paperwork.
