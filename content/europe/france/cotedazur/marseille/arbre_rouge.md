A tree with no leaves, coated in flat, arterial red — you can't miss it against the pale render of Avenue de la Capelette. The red is deliberate. It's the colour of warning signs and beating hearts, and it refuses to fade into the background the way a bronze or grey-steel sculpture would.

Why a tree here, in Marseille's 10th arrondissement? This is no parkland — it's a quiet stretch of residential streets and light industry east of the centre. The piece drains a tree of its natural greens and browns and dares you to ask what it's doing on a working-class street at all.

That's the whole point. Marseille scatters public art far beyond the Vieux-Port and the glossy waterfront projects, and this one belongs to the people who actually live here. Walk past on a bright morning and it throws a long red shadow across the pavement — a jarring note in an understated street.
