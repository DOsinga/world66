Marseille's consuls have been keeping records since at least the twelfth century — and a lot of it survived. That means an unbroken thread of civic life going back eight hundred years: consulate registers, notarial deals from one of the Mediterranean's busiest ports, and an extensive paper trail of the catastrophic 1720 plague.

The port registers are the real treasure. They track goods and people flowing through France's gateway to the Levant, North Africa, and — after the Suez Canal opened in 1869 — the Indian Ocean. The archive also holds the story of the hundreds of thousands of pieds-noirs and Algerian immigrants who reshaped the city from the 1950s on.

Bring ID and you can use the reading room. Staff will help if you can't decode nineteenth-century French handwriting, and an online catalogue lets you search before you go. It's north of the centre, away from the tourists, easy by metro or bus.
