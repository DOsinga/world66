An aspirant is the most junior officer rank in the French military: a cadet who's passed his exams but isn't yet a sous-lieutenant. It's the rank of youth and promise, the moment just before a career starts for real. Jean Versini died right there, at the threshold — and that's what makes this plaque sting.

No dates, no unit. That's normal for these small neighbourhood memorials Marseille scattered across its streets after the wars. Versini is a Corsican name, and the island's ties to this city run centuries deep — so he could've fallen in 1914, 1940, or some colonial conflict. We simply don't know.

This isn't the grand marble of the Vieux-Port or Palais Longchamp. It's grief at the scale of a staircase — neighbours writing their dead onto walls they walked past every day. Versini lives on here, if only in passing.
