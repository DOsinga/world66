The name says everything about the intended audience: *badaboum* is the sound a tumble makes — a word that French children understand immediately, that needs no translation or explanation, that already belongs to the child's world before they have even walked through the door. It is a deliberate choice, a theatre that announces what it is in its name.

Badaboum Théâtre programmes puppet shows, clown performance, physical comedy, and interactive work for young children and their families. These forms have deep roots in French theatrical culture — the *théâtre de rue*, street theatre, and *cirque contemporain*, contemporary circus, have produced generations of performers who work with young audiences using techniques that do not depend on text or narrative alone, that communicate through gesture, rhythm, and surprise.

Marseille has a particularly strong network of venues for young audiences, connected to the city's broader investment in cultural infrastructure across all its arrondissements. Badaboum sits within this network, serving a local population rather than a tourist circuit, and functioning as many children's first encounter with live performance.
