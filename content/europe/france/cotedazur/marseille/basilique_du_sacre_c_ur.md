After 1871, France was reeling from defeat in the Franco-Prussian War and the bloodshed of the Paris Commune. So Catholic leaders vowed to build churches to the Sacred Heart as acts of national atonement. The famous one crowns Montmartre in Paris — this is Marseille's quieter cousin.

Don't expect the drama of Notre-Dame de la Garde up the hill. This one is neighbourhood-scaled, a working parish church rather than a pilgrimage magnet. That's exactly why it's interesting: same historical moment, very different budget.

Step inside during worship hours and you get pure late-19th-century French Catholic devotion — statuary, stained glass, and the Sacred Heart imagery that gives the place its name.
