TR stands for *Tir Rapide* — rapid fire. These 100mm guns near L'Estaque could hit fast torpedo boats and light cruisers up to twelve kilometers away, sweeping the sea lanes that funnel into Marseille's harbor from the north.

The French built it, but the Germans took it over after November 1942, when the Wehrmacht rolled into the southern zone and started hardening the coast against an Allied landing. They poured concrete casemates and wired the battery into their patchy Mediterranean stretch of the Atlantic Wall. The rapid-fire guns were exactly what you'd want against incoming landing craft.

Now it's overgrown, concrete ruins buried in scrub. No signs, no visitor center — just walk in and look around. You get big sea views and the Chaîne de l'Estaque at your back, plus the quiet reminder that this coast spent two years as a war zone.
