These coastal guns never fired a shot in anger. They sat on the limestone headland south of Marseille, aimed at the sea, ready to make any naval assault on the Vieux-Port brutally expensive. Then in August 1944 liberation rolled in overland from the north and west — and the battery was simply abandoned.

German engineers had spent 1942 to 1944 pouring reinforced concrete casemates and hauling in heavier guns, folding Marseille into the Mediterranean extension of the Atlantic Wall. All that effort for a threat that arrived from the wrong direction. The emplacements still stand, some intact enough to walk into, others swallowed by holm oak and rosemary.

The coastal paths of the Calanques National Park bring you right to it. Come for the views: on a clear day the Frioul islands cut sharp against the sky. The shipping lane those guns once watched still runs tankers and ferries in and out of port.
