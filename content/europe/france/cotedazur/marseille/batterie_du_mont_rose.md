Height was the whole point. From Mont-Rose — the Pink Mountain, named for the way the stone glows in evening light — gunners could track ships approaching from south and southeast, far beyond the reach of any lower battery. Elevation lets a gun fire longer and drop shells onto a warship's thin deck armor instead of its heavily protected waterline.

The French Navy loved high ground like this all along the Mediterranean. Under German occupation the site got folded into Marseille's southern coastal arc, alongside the batteries at Croisette and the headlands further east.

Now it's all garigue — kermes oak, rosemary, Aleppo pine — creeping back over the old French and German concrete, inside the Calanques national park. The GR98 coastal path and trails from Mazargues pass close by. On a clear day you can see the limestone ridges east, open sea south, and the islands of Riou and Planier offshore.
