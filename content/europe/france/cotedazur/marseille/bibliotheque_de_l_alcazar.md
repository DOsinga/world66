For over a century, the Alcazar was one of France's great music halls. Maurice Chevalier, Édith Piaf, Charles Aznavour — they all passed through. Vincent Scotto built his early reputation here, composing some of the best-known songs in the French repertoire. It ran from the 1850s until 1966.

When the architects converted it in 2004, they made one brilliant call: put the main reading room exactly where the stalls and stage used to be. You get the same volume, the same proportions, a hint of the old theatrical drama. High ceilings, great light — one of the best public interiors in the city.

This isn't a museum piece. The place is genuinely busy at all hours, packed with the mix of people who actually live nearby. The local history archive is also the best spot in Marseille to dig into the city's 19th and early 20th-century life.
