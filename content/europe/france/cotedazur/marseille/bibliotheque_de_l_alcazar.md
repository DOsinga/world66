From the 1850s until its closure in 1966, the Alcazar was one of the great music halls of France. The roster of artists who passed through it reads like a history of popular French music: Maurice Chevalier, Édith Piaf, Charles Aznavour. Vincent Scotto, who composed some of the best-known songs in the French repertoire, built his early reputation here. The building absorbed the noise of a century of performances, and now it keeps the silence of reading rooms.

The conversion was completed in 2004. The most striking decision the architects made was to place the main reading room exactly where the stalls and stage had been — preserving the volume, the proportions, and something of the theatrical character of the original space. The result is one of the better public interiors in Marseille: high ceilings, good light, the particular atmosphere of a room that has held many different kinds of attention.

The library serves the entire city and is genuinely used, not just admired — busy at all hours, with a range of people that reflects the neighbourhood around it. The archive and local history collection is the best place in Marseille to find primary material on the city's 19th and early 20th-century commercial and cultural life.
