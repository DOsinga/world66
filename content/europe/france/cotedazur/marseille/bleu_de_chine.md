That saturated blue has a name: bleu de Chine, the exact cobalt-indigo of Chinese blue-and-white porcelain. European collectors chased it for centuries, and Marseille traders shipped it home via the Compagnie des Indes routes and the city's own networks through the Levant. Cool, deep, heavy with distance — the perfect color for an artwork facing the open sea.

Marseille's China connection runs deeper than most people remember. From the 1600s, Chinese ceramics, lacquerware and silk poured through this port toward the salons of France, landing right here at the Joliette docks. Now the quarter is becoming Euroméditerranée, and the Quai de la Tourette sits on the seam between old industrial grit and the new city rising around the MuCEM and the Major.

Come back in different light. Midday makes it simply intense, but at golden hour or in a grey winter dawn the blue deepens and drifts, turning atmospheric and hard to pin down. It's a color that has always meant travel, rarity and longing — fitting, on a quay that once sent ships toward horizons that felt genuinely out of reach.
