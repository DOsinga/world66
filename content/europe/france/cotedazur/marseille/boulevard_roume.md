Ernest Roume (1858–1941) was one of the most senior French colonial administrators of the early twentieth century. He served as Governor-General of French West Africa from 1902 to 1908, presiding over a vast territory that stretched from Senegal to what is now Mali, Guinea, and Côte d'Ivoire. Under his administration the colonial capital was transferred from Saint-Louis to Dakar, a decision that shaped the geography of West Africa for decades. Roume was a technocrat of empire: trained at the École Polytechnique, focused on infrastructure, railways, and the extraction of groundnuts and rubber. He was neither a soldier-adventurer nor a humanitarian reformer but a manager of a system whose violence was structural.

Marseille named streets, squares, and boulevards after colonial figures in greater numbers than almost any other French city, because Marseille was the empire's gateway. The city's port handled troops, missionaries, administrators, and goods flowing between metropolitan France and Africa, the Levant, Indochina, and Madagascar. Colonial officials retired there; trading companies maintained offices along the Canebière; and the municipal council saw in these naming ceremonies a way of announcing the city's global importance. Boulevard Roume, located in the northern suburban zone, is one of dozens of such streets that have remained largely unchanged since they were named in the early to mid twentieth century.

The question of renaming colonial streets has been debated in Marseille as in other French cities, intensifying after 2020. France has moved more slowly than some European countries to act on such debates: a 2022 national discussion produced recommendations but no legislative mandate. Some Marseille streets named after figures connected to slavery or atrocities have been quietly renamed; others, like Boulevard Roume, remain as-is, carrying names that most residents pass without reading. The boulevard itself is unremarkable — wide, traffic-heavy, lined with low-rise commercial buildings — which perhaps explains why the debate has been less urgent here than on more prominent arteries.
