Sugiton is the Calanque most visitors reach first, partly because the walk from the Luminy campus is brief enough (45 minutes on a marked path) and partly because the inlet gives you, with relatively little effort, exactly what the Calanques are: white limestone descending to water of an improbable blue-green, Aleppo pines holding to the cliff faces, the Mediterranean framed by rock on three sides. Bus 21 from the city stops at Luminy; the path begins at the campus car park and climbs before dropping to the sea.

The Parc National des Calanques was established in 2012 — the first national park in France to incorporate both land and seascape — and Sugiton is subject to its most active management. Access is restricted or closed entirely from July through August on days when fire risk reaches the highest levels, sometimes for weeks at a stretch during dry summers. The restriction is not decorative: the dry garrigue vegetation burns fast and the park's infrastructure for controlling fires in this terrain is limited. Check the park website before making a journey in high summer; the closure decision is typically posted the evening before.

The beach at the bottom is small and pebbly, shared between swimmers and a few kayakers who come in from the sea. Rocky platforms at either side of the inlet extend the swimming space. The water is clear to a depth that makes the bottom legible.
