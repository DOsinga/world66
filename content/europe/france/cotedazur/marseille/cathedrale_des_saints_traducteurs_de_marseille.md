The feast of the Holy Translators — *Surb Tarkmanchats* in Armenian — honours Mesrop Mashtots and his disciples, who in the early 5th century created the Armenian alphabet and translated the Bible into classical Armenian. Before Mashtots's invention, around 405 CE, Armenians had no script of their own and liturgy was conducted in Greek or Syriac. The translation project that followed the alphabet's creation was so extraordinary in scope and literary quality that the period is called the Golden Age of Armenian literature; the disciples who carried out this work alongside Mashtots — Sahak Partev, Koriun, and others — are venerated as saints in the Armenian Apostolic Church. The feast falls in October and is one of the most important in the Armenian liturgical calendar, a celebration of language, identity, and survival.

Marseille's Armenian community is one of the largest in France and one of the oldest in Western Europe. Most of its families are descended from survivors of the 1915 genocide who reached the Mediterranean coast via the shipping routes of the eastern sea: some came directly from Anatolia and Cilicia, others by way of Beirut, Aleppo, Alexandria, or Piraeus. The city received waves of refugees through the early 1920s, and Marseille became the entry point for Armenians heading north to Lyon and Paris or settling permanently on the coast. The community established churches, cultural associations, newspapers, and schools that have persisted for more than a century.

This cathedral, in the southern arrondissements at coordinates that place it in a quiet residential neighbourhood, is the principal seat of the Armenian Apostolic Diocese in Marseille. The dedication to the Holy Translators is a statement of cultural identity as much as religious devotion: a community that survived the attempt to erase its language and culture honours the saints who first gave that language a written form. Services are conducted in classical Armenian (*Grabar*), the same liturgical language Mashtots's translators used sixteen centuries ago.
