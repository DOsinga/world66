In 405 CE, a scholar named Mesrop Mashtots invented the Armenian alphabet from scratch. Then he and his disciples translated the entire Bible into classical Armenian. The people who did that work are venerated as saints — the Holy Translators, *Surb Tarkmanchats* — and this cathedral is dedicated to them.

Marseille's Armenian community is one of the largest in France. Most families here are descended from survivors of the 1915 genocide who arrived via the Mediterranean shipping routes — from Anatolia, from Beirut, from Alexandria. The city was the entry point; many stayed.

Services here are still conducted in classical Armenian — *Grabar* — the exact same liturgical language Mashtots's translators used sixteen centuries ago. Dedicating a cathedral to the saints who created your written language is an act of defiance as much as devotion. This community knows what it means to have its culture nearly erased.
