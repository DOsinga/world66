Baumettes prison operated for decades as Marseille's main house of detention. During the Second World War it held political prisoners, résistants, and collaborators in the same compound. In later decades it became notorious for overcrowding and violence. The name carries weight in this city.

A cell number is the most reductive thing a state can assign a person — not a name, just a coordinate in a grid of confinement. This installation takes the number 516 out of the prison register and puts it in a residential street in Grand Saint-Giniez, a quiet neighbourhood that has nothing to do with prisons. That displacement is the work.

The point is that confinement happens behind walls the free city never sees. Placing it here, in an ordinary domestic setting rather than on the prison's facade, makes the street an unwilling witness. The weight of cell 516 — as a number standing in for all the other numbers — belongs to the whole city.
