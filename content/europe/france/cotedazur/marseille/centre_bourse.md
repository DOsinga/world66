They broke ground on a standard 1970s shopping centre near the Vieux-Port and hit ancient Massalia — the Greek and Roman port that Marseille grew from. Rather than pave over it, the city stopped construction and sent in archaeologists. They found the old harbour basin, Roman merchant ships still holding their timber hulls, and several layers of ancient streetscape.

The result is a building with a split identity. The Jardin des Vestiges — a walled open-air archaeological garden — sits against the shopping centre's north face. The Musée d'Histoire de Marseille was built into the lower level, housing the Roman ships and other finds. Unremarkable concrete mall above; city history museum below.

The Centre Bourse itself has aged into a perfectly ordinary provincial shopping centre. But the garden alongside it is genuinely affecting — Roman quays in the shadow of a 1970s concrete block, two thousand years of Marseille compressed into one corner. It's free to enter and easy to miss. Look for the entrance off Rue Henri Barbusse.
