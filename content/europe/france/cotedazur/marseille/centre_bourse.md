The Centre Bourse opened in 1977 as part of a postwar ambition to give Marseille a modern commercial heart near the Vieux-Port. Conceived as a multi-level indoor shopping centre in the concrete commercial idiom of the era, it replaced a built-up block in the 1st arrondissement and brought department stores and chain shops into the city core. The building itself is unremarkable architecture — the kind of functional brutalism that dated quickly — but what happened beneath it gave the project an entirely different significance.

During foundation work, excavators struck remains of the ancient Greek and Roman port of Massalia. Rather than demolish the finds, the city paused construction and brought in archaeologists, who uncovered a substantial portion of the old harbour basin, timber-hulled Roman merchant vessels, and layers of ancient streetscape. The excavated ground became the Jardin des Vestiges, a walled open-air archaeological garden that sits against the shopping centre's north face. The finds themselves — including the Roman ships — went into the Musée d'Histoire de Marseille, which was incorporated into the lower level of the building, giving it an unusual double identity: shopping mall above, city history museum below.

The Centre Bourse has aged into a fairly ordinary provincial shopping centre, useful for practical errands and chain restaurants, but the archaeological garden alongside it remains genuinely affecting. Walking among the exposed Roman quays in the shadow of a 1970s concrete commercial block, you get a compression of Marseille time that no purpose-built museum could replicate. The garden is free to enter and easily missed — look for the entrance off Rue Henri Barbusse or through the museum foyer.
