The Centre de Conservation et de Ressources is the part of the MuCEM that most visitors to the waterfront J4 building never know exists. Located in Belle de Mai rather than the Joliette seafront, it houses approximately 250,000 objects in open, climate-controlled storage — accessible to researchers and scholars by appointment, and occasionally open for guided visits. The building was designed by Corinne Vezzoni and opened in 2013 as part of Marseille's European Capital of Culture programme, at the same time as the main MuCEM building by Rudy Ricciotti.

Open visible storage — where collection objects are arranged systematically in glass-fronted cases or open shelving rather than hidden in closed reserve — is a relatively recent approach in French museum practice. The CCR makes the depth of the MuCEM's collections legible in a way that curated exhibition galleries cannot. Objects that have never been exhibited, and may never be, are physically present and attributable rather than filed in basement boxes.

The MuCEM's collection focuses on the civilisations of the Mediterranean and Europe — covering material culture, folk art, religious objects, agricultural tools, and everyday items from across the region. Researchers working on ethnography, material culture, or social history of the Mediterranean world can apply for access. The building itself, in the regenerating Belle de Mai district, is among the better examples of contemporary public architecture built during the Capital of Culture investment wave.
