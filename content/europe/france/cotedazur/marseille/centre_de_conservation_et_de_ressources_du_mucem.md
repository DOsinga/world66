Everyone knows the MuCEM building on the Joliette waterfront — Rudy Ricciotti's concrete-lattice box on the sea wall. Almost nobody comes here. The Centre de Conservation et de Ressources is the collection that didn't make it into the exhibition galleries, which is to say: most of it. Around 250,000 objects, arranged in open climate-controlled storage in Belle de Mai.

Open visible storage means the objects are actually there to be seen — glass-fronted cases, systematic shelving, everything catalogued and attributable — rather than buried in basement boxes that researchers have to request item by item. It's a relatively new approach in French museum practice and a good one. The depth of a Mediterranean civilisations collection becomes physically legible.

Researchers in ethnography, material culture, or social history of the Mediterranean can apply for access. Occasional guided visits open it to the general public. The building itself, by Corinne Vezzoni, is one of the better pieces of public architecture from the Capital of Culture investment wave that reshaped Marseille around 2013.
