A large stag — *cerf* — stands on a street corner in the northern stretch of Marseille. In aristocratic parks, in the hunting grounds of Versailles and Fontainebleau, the deer was the creature of royal leisure. Here it's standing on Mediterranean pavement in a working-class neighbourhood, catching the same flat light as the apartment blocks around it. The displacement is the point.

The title pairs the animal with FREC — most likely a reference to the FRAC, Marseille's regional contemporary art fund, which has a long record of placing commissions directly in neighbourhood space rather than gallery walls. If that attribution is right, the stag belongs to that tradition.

Most people encounter it without knowing any of this. It reads as a pleasant surprise — one of those unannounced gestures that distinguish a city genuinely invested in its public realm.
