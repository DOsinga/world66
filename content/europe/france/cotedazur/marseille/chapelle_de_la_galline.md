*Galline* is the Provençal word for hen. The chapel is up in the far north-western hills behind l'Estaque, where Marseille thins into limestone scrub and old tile-works. Cézanne and Braque came out here for the light; the Chapelle de la Galline is further into the hills than most visitors get.

The name has two possible explanations. The practical one: a medieval landholding belonging to a family called Galline would lend its name to any chapel built on the estate. The more interesting one: across Provence and Languedoc there are dozens of legends in which a miraculous glowing hen reveals a hidden statue of the Virgin or a sacred spring, and a chapel gets built on the spot. Whether that kind of story ever attached to this site is now impossible to verify.

The surrounding quartier kept something of its village character well into the twentieth century — small, close-grained, slightly separate from the main city. Worth the detour if you're already out at l'Estaque.
