A hooded brotherhood, not a scary one. The Pénitents Noirs wore black robes and followed condemned prisoners from the jail to the scaffold — making sure a priest was present, collecting the body afterward, giving it a decent burial. That was their job, and they volunteered for it. In medieval Marseille, someone had to.

They built this Baroque chapel on the slopes of Le Panier in the 17th century. When the Revolution came and stripped most of the city's churches bare, this one survived — because the brotherhood survived. They were never dissolved. The 18th-century altarpiece inside is still there, which makes it one of the most intact Baroque interiors in the city.

The brotherhood still meets here. Duck inside if the door is open — the interior is a genuine surprise compared to what the narrow Le Panier street outside prepares you for.
