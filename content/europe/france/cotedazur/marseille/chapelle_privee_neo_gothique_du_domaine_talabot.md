Paulin Talabot was the engineer who pushed through the Paris–Lyon–Marseille railway in the mid-19th century, when the question of which corridor would win was still open. The PLM became the spine of French rail. Talabot became very rich, and the family established their country seat in the hills south of the city.

The Gothic chapel they built on the estate says everything about how 19th-century industrial money wanted to be seen. Pointed arches, ribbed vaults, stone tracery — medieval Catholic associations of piety and old lineage, deliberately chosen by people whose actual wealth came from steam engines and share capital. It's a very specific kind of statement.

The estate has since been subdivided and the neighbourhood built up, but the chapel is still there: slightly incongruous in a Mediterranean landscape, its carved gables doing their best impersonation of Normandy.
