The fort here is 17th century — Louis XIV had it built after a dispute with the city, as a reminder of who was in charge. But the name Saint-Jean is older. The Knights Hospitaller, who called themselves the Knights of Saint John, had a commandery on this exact spot in the medieval period. Their red cross was a fixture at Mediterranean ports for centuries.

A chapel to Saint John at the harbour mouth was more than a gesture. The 24th of June — his feast day, Midsummer — was one of the great celebrations of the year in Provence, and port cities felt the solstice with particular intensity. Sailors and pilgrims passed through here. The chapel gave them somewhere to stop.

Since 2013, the fort has been connected to the MuCEM by a footbridge over the water. Walk the ramparts, find the chapel tucked into the fortifications, and look back at the harbour. This spot has been watching ships come in for about eight hundred years.
