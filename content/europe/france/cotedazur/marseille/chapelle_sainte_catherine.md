Saint Catherine of Alexandria was executed on a spiked wheel — except the wheel broke, so they beheaded her instead. That's the legend. She became the patron of philosophers, lawyers, students, and wheelwrights, which is an unusual combination that tells you something about how medieval people thought about saints.

In France, her feast on 25 November had its own custom: unmarried women of marriageable age wore hats of yellow and green and paraded through town. The city's merchants and scholars claimed her especially. A chapel in her name in this Le Panier district makes sense — this neighbourhood housed notaries, traders, and anyone in the city who dealt in literacy.

The chapel sits at the edge of Le Panier, almost level with the mouth of the Vieux-Port. If the stonework around the entrance has survived the Revolution's clearances, look for the wheel in the carved details. It was everywhere once.
