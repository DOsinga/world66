Clare of Assisi followed Francis into radical poverty in 1212 and spent the rest of her life fighting to keep it that way. Most religious orders ended up softening their vows over time. Clare refused. Her Rule — the first monastic rule written by a woman and given papal approval — insisted that not just individual nuns but the whole community together had to own nothing. The Pope confirmed it two days before she died in 1253.

The Poor Clares are an enclosed order. The sisters stay inside; their work is prayer. In the decades after Francis's death, the Franciscan network spread fast across Mediterranean port cities — ships carried friars and Clarissines between Spain, southern France, Italy, and the Levant. Marseille, as the main French port on that sea, was a natural stop.

The chapel sits in the quieter southern arrondissements, away from the waterfront — consistent with an enclosed community seeking some distance from port-city noise. The Franciscan sisters are likely gone now, but the dedication remains.
