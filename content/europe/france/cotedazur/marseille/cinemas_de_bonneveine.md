Bonneveine is at the southern edge of Marseille, between the Prado roundabout and the sea. Apartment buildings, small squares, everyday commerce. The Prado beaches nearby weren't always there — they were built in the 1970s using rubble excavated during urban clearance around the Vieux-Port. The city literally extended its coastline with demolition waste and gave this neighbourhood a seafront it had never had.

The cinema here serves the people who live in the surrounding streets, not the summer crowds passing through on their way to the beach. That's increasingly rare. Marseille has a genuine film culture — the city's role as a backdrop for French crime thrillers runs deep, and FIDMarseille brings international documentary and fiction films here every summer — but the neighbourhood cinema operating on weekday-evening rhythms is its own thing.

It's not a destination. That's the point — it's where Bonneveine goes.
