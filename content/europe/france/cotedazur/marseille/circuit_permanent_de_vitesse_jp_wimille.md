Jean-Pierre Wimille won Le Mans in 1937 and again in 1939, driving Bugattis. After the war he was widely considered the fastest driver alive. He never got to race in Formula 1 — in January 1949, six months before the World Championship's first season, he was killed during practice in Buenos Aires.

That timing makes him one of the great what-ifs of motor racing. A driver who beat the best of the pre-war era, who came through the war and was still at the top of his form, gone before the sport's defining era began. France named things after him.

The circuit in Bonneveine carries his name. It's a local facility — karting, lightweight vehicles — in the coastal lowland near Parc Borély. Not glamorous, but the name on the gate is worth a second look.
