This bell tower has been standing since the 12th century. The church it belonged to, Notre-Dame-des-Accoules, was one of the most important in the city for hundreds of years. The Revolution requisitioned it as a saltpetre depot, then tore most of it down. The tower stayed. Eventually the city built apartments against it, and it's been embedded in the streetscape of Le Panier ever since — you notice it mainly when you round a corner and find a Romanesque tower growing out of a 19th-century apartment block.

The name Accoules comes from Latin for springs. There was fresh water on this hill, which is why the Greek settlers of Massalia established themselves here first. The site is effectively the origin point of the city, which is why a church stood here for so long.

Approach from Rue du Panier or Place des Moulins. The stonework is plain and functional — no decoration, built to endure. It reads very differently from Marseille's Baroque churches and later monuments. That contrast, and the knowledge of what's underneath you, is the whole point.
