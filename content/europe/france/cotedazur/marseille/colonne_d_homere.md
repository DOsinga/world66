There is no connection between Homer and Marseille. Marseille was founded by Greeks from Phocaea around 600 BC, a century or more after the Homeric poems are dated, and the Phocaeans don't appear in the Iliad or the Odyssey. The column on the Allées de Meilhan, erected in 1841 with Homer's bust on top, was not an act of historical scholarship.

It was a political move. In the 1830s and 1840s, Marseille was pushing back against Parisian cultural dominance — the centralisation of French national identity around the capital. A city with Greek origins could claim Homer as a symbolic ancestor. And a symbolic ancestor who predated Rome gave Marseille a cultural depth that no northern French city could match.

The column is still there, on the Allées de Meilhan near the flower market. Easy to miss. The interest isn't the object — it's what it tells you about how Marseille argued for itself.
