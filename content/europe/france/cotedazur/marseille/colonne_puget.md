Pierre Puget grew up in Marseille, trained in Rome, and became the finest sculptor France produced in the 17th century. Two of his works are in the Louvre: *Milon de Crotone* — an athlete's hand trapped in a split tree while a lion tears at him — and *Perseus and Andromeda*. Both were commissioned for Versailles. Both ended up in Paris instead.

That rejection is the whole story. Colbert championed him. Louis XIV kept finding reasons to set his work aside. The expressiveness, the drama — it didn't fit Le Brun's tightly managed version of royal grandeur. So Puget spent most of his career in Toulon and Genoa, building a reputation at a remove from the court that controlled all the major French commissions.

This column near the Cours Belsunce is a modest nod to him. Walk north into the Panier district and you'll find a more fitting memorial: the oval Baroque chapel at the Vieille Charité, which he designed. That one they kept.
