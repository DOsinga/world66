These are big figures in rough space — the kind of pairing that became a signature move in Marseille around its 2013 European Capital of Culture year. The city poured public art into the northern districts, where derelict industrial land had been accumulating since the port shrank. The idea was deliberate: put ambitious work where polished galleries wouldn't go.

The Friche la Belle de Mai is nearby — an old tobacco factory turned into one of France's most interesting cultural complexes. This part of the city has absorbed wave after wave of creative investment since the 1990s, and the Colosses Cosmiques belong to that current.

The name itself is a statement. Colossi, in the ancient world, were statues so large they became landmarks. *Cosmic* pushes further: not just big, but belonging to the order of planets and stars. Whether the work delivers on that — come and decide for yourself.
