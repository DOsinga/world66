In 1720 a ship called the *Grand Saint-Antoine* arrived from the Levant and brought bubonic plague into the port. Within two years, roughly half of Marseille's population was dead. It was the last great outbreak of plague in Western Europe, and it permanently rewrote how Europe thought about quarantine, port sanitation, and public health governance.

The *lazaret* system — holding ships and cargo in isolation before allowing them ashore — was already in use here before 1720. The catastrophe accelerated its codification and spread it across Europe. Marseille, improbably, became a pioneer of infectious disease control.

That history is what this conservatory holds. The collections cover surgical instruments, apothecary equipment, hospital records, and tropical medicine — a discipline that grew directly from Marseille's role as gateway to France's colonial empire in North Africa and beyond. Every ship that docked here carried something new, and the city's doctors had to deal with it.
