Between 1890 and 1905, Marseille was one of the biggest construction sites in France. The colonial empire was generating enormous port traffic — North Africa, West Africa, Indochina — and the Joliette waterfront was being rebuilt from scratch to handle it. New docks, warehouses, transit sheds, covered arcades: the whole northern edge of the city was going up at once.

The Galerie de la Mer — an arcade facing the sea — was part of this surge. The architecture of the era mixed classical and Orientalist motifs freely, which was not accidental. Marseille understood itself as the hinge between Europe and the Mediterranean south, and the buildings were meant to say so to every ship pulling in.

The city genuinely believed it was on its way to becoming the greatest port in the world. Genoa and Barcelona were the competition. This marker records what was built here in those fifteen years of confidence.
