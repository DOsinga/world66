On 6 February 1998, Claude Érignac — Prefect of Corsica — was shot dead in Ajaccio. He was the first senior French state representative killed since the Second World War. The country was in shock. In Marseille, with its dense ties to Corsica through migration and family, it landed differently: this wasn't abstract news.

The courtyard itself is ordinary — benches, a few trees, the breathing room between buildings that Marseille tucks into its denser quarters. That modesty fits. This isn't a grand monument. It's a neighbourhood space where daily life plays out around a name that carries real weight.

Street names and square names are how French cities write their political history. Walking through here, the name is a quiet reminder that the violence of that history is not always somewhere else.
