Cour Claude Érignac is a quiet urban courtyard in the heart of Marseille, named after Claude Érignac, the Prefect of Corsica who was shot dead in Ajaccio on 6 February 1998. The assassination — the first killing of a senior French state representative since the Second World War — shocked the country and sent a jolt through the French civil service. Naming this public space in Marseille after Érignac reflects both the gravity of the event and the particular resonance it held in a city historically connected to Corsica through migration, commerce and family ties.

The cour itself is a sheltered, pedestrian-friendly space typical of Marseille's denser residential quarters — a kind of breathing room between apartment blocks and traffic, with benches and a few trees softening the urban fabric. Its modest scale suits the human character of the commemoration: not a grand monument but a neighbourhood space where ordinary city life plays out around a name that carries real weight.

For visitors passing through this part of the city, the dedication is a small but pointed reminder that French political history — including its violence — is written into street names and squares as much as into textbooks. The memorial choice here was deliberate: in Marseille, where Corsican and Provençal identities have long overlapped, the killing of Érignac was felt as something close to home.
