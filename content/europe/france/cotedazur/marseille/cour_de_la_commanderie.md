Marseille was one of the main embarkation points for the Crusades, and the Knights Hospitaller ran serious operations here. A commanderie was their local logistics hub: part warehouse, part hostel, part counting house. Pilgrims and soldiers bound for the Holy Land came through, were fed, housed, and dispatched — all managed from a building that stood on or near this spot.

The building is long gone, absorbed into later construction. The name survived. That's Marseille: the medieval ecclesiastical and military topography lives on in street names and courtyard names even when nothing physical remains.

What you're standing in now is an ordinary urban enclosure. The name is the monument.
