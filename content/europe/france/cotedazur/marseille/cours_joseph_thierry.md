The Cours Joseph Thierry was laid out during the Haussmann-era transformation of Marseille in the 1850s and 1860s, part of the wholesale restructuring of the city's street network that accompanied major population growth and the expansion of the port. The boulevard was designed as a broad planted cours in the tradition of the Allées de Meilhan — a tree-lined promenade cutting through the medieval street pattern to improve circulation and assert civic order. It was named after a 19th-century Marseille parliamentarian and mayor, in the standard practice of attaching institutional authority to new urban infrastructure.

The cours connects Belsunce — the dense North African commercial district around the Rue d'Aix — to Belle de Mai, the mixed working-class neighbourhood to the northeast, passing through a transition zone that has not benefited substantially from Marseille's post-2000 cultural investments. The buildings along it are a mix of 19th-century apartment blocks in variable condition, small shops, and some institutional buildings. The trees and the width of the roadway are about the only physical evidence of the original civic programme.

It is not a destination in itself, but it is a useful route between the central Belsunce area and the Belle de Mai cultural complex (the former tobacco factory that now houses La Friche, the CCR, and several other institutions). Walking it gives a more accurate sense of ordinary Marseille than either the tourist precinct around the Vieux-Port or the regenerated Joliette waterfront.
