Louis IX used Marseille as his embarkation point twice: in 1248 for the Seventh Crusade, which ended with his capture by Egyptian forces, and in 1270 for the Eighth, which ended with his death near Tunis from illness. Both times the troops assembled on the medieval square that once stood here. The 19th-century reconstruction obliterated it — the Bourse shopping centre now sits directly to the north.

What remained is a short 150-metre street, and what happens on it every morning is a flower market. Cut flowers and potted plants, running from dawn to early afternoon, drawing professional buyers from the restaurant trade alongside ordinary shoppers. It operates in almost all weathers.

This is exactly where the Canebière — the city's main commercial axis — meets the Vieux-Port. Everything converges here: tram line, ferry terminal for the Château d'If, tourist foot traffic. The flower market is the one thing that stays obstinately local in a space that is otherwise completely taken over.
