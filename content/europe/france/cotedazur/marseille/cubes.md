These are not museum pieces. They sit in Bonneveine, the quiet southern margin where the city flattens out before the Calanques take over — a neighbourhood of supermarkets and apartment blocks, not galleries. The Cubes ended up here because someone decided that abstraction shouldn't only happen where people go looking for it.

Up close the scale surprises you. Primary geometric forms do something different in Midi light than they do in a courtyard in Paris — the shadows are sharper, the surfaces more insistent. Walk around them. The piece changes depending on where you stand.

Whether they're stone or concrete, the gesture is the same: here is something with no practical function and no obvious narrative, placed where people walk their dogs on Tuesday afternoons. Marseille has more of this than it gets credit for.
