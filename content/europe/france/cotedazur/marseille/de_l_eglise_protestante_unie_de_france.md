In 2013, France's two main Protestant churches — the Reformed and the Lutheran — merged into a single body, the Église Protestante Unie de France. In a country where Protestants are under three percent of the population, running parallel denominations had stopped making sense. The resulting church is now one of the larger Protestant bodies in France, with congregations in cities and towns across the country.

Protestantism has been in Marseille since the Wars of Religion. The port helped — trade connections with Geneva and the Huguenot towns of the Languedoc kept Reformed ideas circulating here even when the city wasn't a stronghold. Marseille's commercial habit of tolerating minority communities kept congregations alive through the harder centuries.

This is one of those congregations. The neighbourhood around it is typical Marseille — layered, various, several languages within half a block. The Sunday services continue a thread that runs back four hundred years.
