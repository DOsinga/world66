*Désordre* — disorder — sits near the northern quays, close enough to the Joliette docks that you can hear the port machinery when the wind is right. The title is either ironic or completely sincere, depending on your read of Marseille.

Look at it slowly. What first appears accidental — forms bent or scattered — starts to show internal logic on closer inspection. The Panier quarter works the same way: the lanes look tangled, but underneath runs a grid the Greeks of Massalia laid down 2,600 years ago. Marseille's disorder has always been structured disorder. The improvised architectures of communities arriving with nothing.

Container cranes to the north, the old port behind you, and this piece in the middle of it. It doesn't feel like a provocation here. It feels like a description.
