*Divadlo* is the Czech and Slovak word for theatre. Finding it on a Marseille venue sign tells you something about the neighbourhood and the city. Cours Julien has been the alternative arts belt for decades — small stages, painted walls, cafés where Arabic, French, and the clipped vowels of the south all share the same room.

Community theatres have a long history in Marseille that rarely gets told. Each wave of immigrants — Italians, Armenians, Comorians, Algerians — built their own stages and festival circuits. Places where the language wasn't French, where homesickness got addressed through performance, where identity could survive the pressure to assimilate. Divadlo fits that tradition exactly, whatever the current programme.

The Wednesday and Saturday morning market fills the square outside with faces from across the Mediterranean. A theatre with a Slavic name next to it isn't exotic here. It's just one more thread in the weave.
