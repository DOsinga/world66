Dante wrote the *Divina Commedia* in Tuscan vernacular rather than Latin — a deliberate choice, a statement that spoken Mediterranean languages were adequate to the highest literary ambitions. Naming a theatre after it is the same kind of claim: that what happens here matters, that this stage belongs in a long conversation.

Marseille sits inside that same Mediterranean inheritance — founded by Greek colonists, built on trade routes that connected it to the Adriatic, the Levant, the North African coast. The city Dante knew exists within the same sea.

The venue is small, in the southern arrondissements. The programming takes itself seriously — contemporary work that isn't embarrassed by the past. The name is too large for the building, and that's entirely the point.
