Marcel Pagnol was born in Aubagne but Marseille shaped him. When his father Joseph — a primary school teacher — was posted to the city, the family moved into the schoolhouse itself. That was normal then: teacher's lodgings were built into the building. The playground and corridors were the boy's neighbourhood.

Pagnol turned those years into *La Gloire de mon père* and *Le Château de ma mère*, two of the most-loved books in French. The hills outside Aubagne, the summers in the garrigue, the figure of Joseph — earnest, devoted, slightly comic in his earnestness — all of it entered French cultural memory as a portrait of Provençal childhood before the wars.

He went on to become a playwright, filmmaker, and the first film director elected to the Académie française. But the source material was this: a boy watching his father teach, living inside the school, spending summers in the hills. That's where it started.
