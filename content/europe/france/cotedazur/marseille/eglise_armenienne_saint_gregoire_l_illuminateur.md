Armenians had maintained a presence in Marseille through trade before 1915, but the genocide transformed the port into the principal entry point for survivors. The established community, French colonial connections, and the port's role as a transit hub for people coming from the Ottoman territories by sea all converged: Marseille received more Armenian refugees than any other French city. In the interwar years the community built the institutions — schools, cultural associations, newspapers, churches — that would allow it to persist across generations.

The church is dedicated to Saint Gregory the Illuminator, the figure credited with converting the Armenian king Tiridates III to Christianity in 301 CE and establishing the Armenian Apostolic Church. The date makes Armenia the first state in history to adopt Christianity as its official religion, a fact that carries considerable weight in Armenian identity — particularly for a community whose existence had just been threatened with extinction. The building dates from the interwar period, constructed once the community had achieved sufficient stability to invest in permanent infrastructure.

The Armenian Apostolic Church uses a liturgical tradition distinct from both Roman Catholicism and Eastern Orthodoxy, conducting services in Classical Armenian. The Belsunce church continues to serve the Armenian community of Marseille and stands as a monument to the survival and reconstruction of a diaspora community formed by one of the 20th century's defining catastrophes.
