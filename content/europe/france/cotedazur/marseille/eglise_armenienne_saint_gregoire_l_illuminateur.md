From 1915 onward, Marseille became the principal entry point for Armenian survivors fleeing the Ottoman territories by sea. The port, the colonial connections, the existing Armenian trading community — everything converged here. Marseille received more Armenian refugees than any other French city. In the interwar years the community built schools, newspapers, cultural associations, and this church.

The dedication is to Saint Gregory the Illuminator — the man credited with converting the Armenian king Tiridates III to Christianity in 301 CE. That date made Armenia the first state in history to adopt Christianity as its official religion, a fact that carries enormous weight in Armenian identity, and even more weight for a community that had just survived an attempt to erase it.

The Apostolic Church uses a liturgical tradition distinct from both Roman Catholicism and Eastern Orthodoxy, conducting services in Classical Armenian. The congregation in Belsunce continues that tradition. The building is both a church and a monument to survival.
