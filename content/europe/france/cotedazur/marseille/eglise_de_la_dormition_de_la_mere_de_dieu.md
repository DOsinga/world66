August 15 in the Eastern Christian calendar is the Dormition of the Mother of God — the feast the Western church calls the Assumption. Eastern theology emphasises the Dormition, the peaceful falling-asleep (*koimesis* in Greek) before Mary's glorification. It's one of the twelve great feasts of the Orthodox year, preceded by a two-week fast, and celebrated with some of the most beautiful choral music in the Eastern tradition.

Marseille has had Eastern Christian communities since the Russian émigrés came after 1917, the Greek merchants who never really left, and more recent waves of Romanian, Serbian, and other arrivals. North of the Canebière, in the part of the city that absorbed wave after wave of working-class and immigrant settlement, communities like this one built their own spaces — often modest from the street, occasionally a converted building, with no hint of the elaborate Byzantine liturgy conducted inside.

The defining interior feature of any Orthodox church is the iconostasis — the screen of sacred images separating the nave from the sanctuary. Visitors are generally welcome to stand quietly in the nave and light a candle. The liturgical chanting alone is worth experiencing.
