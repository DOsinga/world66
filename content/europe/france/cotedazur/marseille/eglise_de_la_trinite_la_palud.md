La Palud is not on any itinerary. That's the point. Sit at a café table near the Plaine on a Sunday morning and you'll hear the bells of La Trinité threading through the noise of vendors setting up. It's that kind of church — planted in the life of its neighbourhood, not performing for anyone else.

The quarter has always been working-class and a little bohemian. Market traders, artists, the café regulars who haven't changed in years. Cours Julien nearby brought the murals and record shops; La Palud itself stayed quieter, more lived-in. The church has the same character — no architectural showpiece, no famous relics, just a building that has marked time with the people around it.

Push open the door between services and you'll find a still interior that earns its place by being exactly where it is.
