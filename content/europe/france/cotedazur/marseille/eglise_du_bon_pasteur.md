"Bon Pasteur" was never just a devotional title. The Congregation of Our Lady of Charity of the Good Shepherd, founded in Angers in 1835, ran houses across Europe for women trapped in poverty, prostitution, and social marginalisation. The name on the door was a promise: the shepherd goes looking for the lost sheep. In a port city like Marseille — full of sailors, migrants, and people who had slipped through every other net — that mission landed hard.

The church sits in the northern reaches of central Marseille, dense and working-class. The building is modest. No celebrated artwork, no famous relics. What it has is continuity: the same stone, the same dedication, the same insistence that the job is to go out rather than wait inside.

Step in between services and it's just a quiet room. But the neighbourhood outside explains the name better than the interior does.
