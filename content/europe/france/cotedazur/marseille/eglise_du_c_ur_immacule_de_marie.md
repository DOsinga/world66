In 1917, three shepherd children in Fatima, Portugal, claimed six apparitions of the Virgin. She asked for prayer, penance, and the consecration of Russia to her Immaculate Heart. The Church approved the apparitions in 1930. By 1942, with the world at war, Pope Pius XII consecrated the entire world to the Immaculate Heart. Churches dedicated to it went up across the Catholic world in the decades that followed — almost all of them twentieth century, almost all bearing the stamp of that specific moment of global anxiety.

This one fits that pattern. Built between the Marseille city centre and the southern arrondissements, it carries the Marian thread that runs through this city's devotional life. Notre-Dame de la Garde watches from the hill above the port; the Heart of Mary devotion fills the streets below.

Marseille has always leaned Marian. This parish is the quieter, neighbourhood expression of something the great basilica on the hill makes loud.
