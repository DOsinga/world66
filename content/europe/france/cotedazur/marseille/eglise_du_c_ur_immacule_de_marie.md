The devotion to the Immaculate Heart of Mary is distinct from — though related to — the older doctrine of the Immaculate Conception. Where the Immaculate Conception concerns the sinless state of Mary's soul from the moment of her own conception, the Heart devotion focuses on Mary's interior life of love, sorrow, and intercession for humanity — particularly as expressed in her reported appearances at Fatima, Portugal, in 1917. Between May and October of that year, three shepherd children claimed a series of apparitions of the Virgin, who asked for prayer, penance, and the consecration of Russia to her Immaculate Heart. The Fatima apparitions were approved by the Church in 1930 and acquired enormous force during and after the Second World War; Pius XII consecrated the world to the Immaculate Heart in 1942.

Churches dedicated to the Immaculate Heart of Mary are almost invariably twentieth century, typically built in the decades following Fatima when Marian devotion was at its height across the Catholic world. The dedication signals a particular strand of twentieth-century Catholicism: global in its Marian focus, shaped by the trauma of two world wars, and explicitly intercessory in its theology — the Heart of Mary as the channel through which humanity's prayers reach Christ.

This Marseille parish, in a neighbourhood between the centre and the southern arrondissements, bears that twentieth-century stamp. Marseille has always had a strong Marian culture — Notre-Dame de la Garde, the golden Madonna on the hill above the port, is the dominant symbol of the city — and the Heart of Mary dedication fits naturally into that landscape of female intercession. The church serves as a neighbourhood parish, continuing the Marian thread that runs through Marseille's devotional life from the great basilica on the hill to the quiet streets below.
