Everyone climbs to Notre-Dame de la Garde. Golden Madonna, neo-Byzantine dome, votive ships hanging from the ceiling, tour groups. This is the other Notre-Dame — a working parish tucked into the residential south, where Marseille quietly shades into suburb. No gilded offerings, no pilgrims, no view of the harbour. Just families, a liturgical calendar, and the rhythm of baptisms, marriages, and burials.

The southern arrondissements — Mazargues, Sainte-Marguerite, the hills before the calanques — are the Marseille that doesn't appear on postcards. Residential blocks, local shops, long streets that go nowhere tourists are heading. A church here anchors people rather than territory.

Worth a brief stop if you're passing through. Not for architecture — for the reminder that Marseille's religious life isn't only happening on its hilltops.
