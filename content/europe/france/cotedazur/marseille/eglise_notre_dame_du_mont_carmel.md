The Carmelites started as hermits on Mount Carmel in present-day Israel — the same mountain the prophet Elijah is said to have used as his base. When they moved to Europe in the twelfth century, they brought an intense Marian devotion with them, along with a scapular devotion and a feast day fixed on 16 July. Pass this church on that date and you may find the exterior decorated and a special mass pulling people in from the surrounding streets.

In the sixteenth century, Teresa of Ávila and John of the Cross transformed the order. Their writings on contemplative prayer became classics of Christian mysticism. That interior tradition — rigorous, poetic, demanding — runs under the surface of every Carmelite parish.

This one is a working neighbourhood church, not a grand monument. But the dedication connects it to eight centuries of Marian mysticism stretching from a rocky Israeli hillside to a Marseille street corner.
