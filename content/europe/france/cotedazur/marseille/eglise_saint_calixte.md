Callixtus I had one of the more improbable careers in early Christianity. Born into slavery, he was imprisoned for debt, exiled to the Sardinian mines, and somehow emerged to climb the ranks of the Roman church. He became Pope in 217 CE and died a martyr in 222. His lasting achievement was practical: he established and organised the Catacomb of Callixtus on the Appian Way, the first burial ground administered by the Church — where dozens of early popes and thousands of Roman Christians now rest.

Dedications to Callixtus are rare outside Italy, which makes this church in the eastern twelfth arrondissement an intriguing exception. The neighbourhood is mid-century housing and everyday Marseillais life, far from the tourist circuits. The church serves it accordingly — interior plain, built for the people nearby rather than pilgrims passing through.

A story about a slave who became a pope has its own appeal in a port city shaped by migration. The name above the door says more about this neighbourhood than the building does.
