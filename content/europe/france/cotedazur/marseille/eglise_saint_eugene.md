Saint Eugene of Carthage spent most of his life being pushed around by Vandal kings. Exiled in 484 CE after refusing to convert to Arian Christianity, he ended up in southern Gaul — the region that eventually became Provence. He went back to Carthage when the political wind shifted, then got exiled again by the next ruler. He died in the Tarn without ever returning home.

That's the patron of this church in Marseille's western hills: a bishop who got knocked down repeatedly and kept refusing to compromise. His cult took root in southern France precisely because he'd spent years living among the local clergy here.

The neighbourhood climbs toward the hills above the Anse du Pharo, looking out toward the Frioul islands. It developed as a prosperous residential quarter in the Second Empire — villas with sea views, afternoon strolls, café terraces. The church fits that pace: unhurried, grounded, quietly carrying an obscure North African story in its dedication.
