Augustinian friars completed this church in 1542, right on the Quai de Rive Neuve. That makes it the oldest post-medieval church still standing in the city centre. The Provençal Gothic facade is austere by design — less ornamented than northern French Gothic, Mediterranean in its restraint, looking more to classical simplicity than to elaborate stonework.

The Revolution stripped it bare and turned it into a depot. Napoleon's 1802 Concordat got it reconsecrated, and the interior was gradually refurnished through the 19th century. The 1876 organ is the result: one of the finest instruments of its era in the region, still used for concerts and liturgy.

From the quay, it's easy to walk straight past — the ferry terminals and the fish market compete for attention. But step inside and that organ makes clear this isn't just another neighbourhood church.
