Francis of Assisi founded his order on radical poverty and simplicity, and it spread across the Mediterranean world within a generation of his death in 1226. Marseille was a natural landing point — in the 13th century it was the main embarkation port for Crusaders and pilgrims sailing to the Holy Land, one of the most cosmopolitan cities in Christendom. The friars set up convents to serve soldiers leaving and pilgrims returning.

That Franciscan presence stuck. Churches dedicated to Francis show up across Marseille's arrondissements, and they tend toward the unassuming — plain facades, devotional chapels, nothing grand. The founder distrusted architectural spectacle, and his successors took the hint.

This one sits between the old port and the southern residential districts, a 19th-century parish rebuild with the solidity of an institution that knows exactly what it's for. Seven centuries of Franciscan life in one of the Mediterranean's great ports, concentrated in one block.
