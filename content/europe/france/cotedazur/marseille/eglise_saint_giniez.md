Genisius of Arles wasn't a soldier or a bishop. He was a court stenographer. In 303 CE, during the Diocletianic persecution, he was handed an imperial edict ordering action against Christians. He refused to write it, threw down his instruments, and fled. Soldiers caught him at the Rhône and beheaded him. He is now the patron of notaries, stenographers, and — because they work from scripts — actors.

The neighbourhood around the church still carries his name: Saint-Giniez, in Marseille's 8th arrondissement. That's how deep these early Provençal martyrs sank into local geography — they named the streets. The church itself is a 19th-century parish building, calm and residential, wedged between the Prado axis and the Parc Borély.

This is not a tourist stop. It's tree-lined streets, school pickup, Saturday market feel. The church is the neighbourhood's quiet centre. If you're near Parc Borély, it's a short detour — worth it just to meet the saint whose career began at a writing desk.
