For most of Christian history, Joseph was an afterthought — present in nativity scenes, then largely offstage. The Counter-Reformation began elevating him. Then in 1870, as workers' movements and industrial upheaval were remaking Europe, Pope Pius IX declared Joseph patron of the universal Church. A carpenter who provided for a holy family was exactly the saint the Church wanted promoting.

Churches dedicated to Joseph multiplied across France in the latter half of the 19th century. This one, on the western edge of Marseille's 7th and 8th arrondissements, is a product of that wave — built as the city expanded into new residential quarters and every new neighbourhood got its parish church.

The streets around it are residential, well clear of the tourist circuits around the Vieux-Port. That suits it. This is the Marseille that goes to work, picks up children, and stops at the boulangerie on the way home.
