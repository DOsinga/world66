Justus of Lyon was Bishop of Lyon in the late 4th century. At a synod, he vouched for a convicted murderer's release. The man immediately killed again. Justus resigned his bishopric, travelled to Egypt, and spent the rest of his life as a monk in the Theban desert. He never came back. Lyon eventually retrieved his relics. He was declared a saint — not for dying under persecution, but for choosing to live a harder life than the one he had.

The church sits in the northern reaches of Marseille, in territory that stayed semi-rural until the 20th century's expansion. The neighbourhood is built around older village cores, each with its own parish church that predated the surrounding urbanisation. Saint-Just gives its name to the area — the church is the oldest institutional anchor here.

No beheadings, no imperial confrontations. Just a man who decided he had failed and went to the desert to think about it for the rest of his life. The parish bearing his name is itself quietly off the tourist map, which feels almost right.
