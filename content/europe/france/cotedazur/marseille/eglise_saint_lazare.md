This site was a leper hospital first — built outside the city walls when leprosy was still endemic and the sick were kept at a distance by law. By the time the current 18th-century church went up, leprosy had long since disappeared from Provence. The name stuck; the function had been gone for generations.

The Cours Belsunce, on which the church faces, became one of the main commercial arteries running north from the old port. From the 20th century onward it developed into the centre of Marseille's North African trading economy. Wholesale shops, money transfer agencies, restaurants serving North African, sub-Saharan, and Middle Eastern food — the old saint's name and the old hospital feel very distant from this particular stretch of street.

The church keeps going as an active parish, its congregation drawn mostly from the surrounding immigrant community. This is not a monument — it's an ordinary working church embedded in one of Marseille's most commercially alive streets.
