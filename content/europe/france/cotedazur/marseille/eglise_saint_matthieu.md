Before he was an apostle, Matthew sat at a toll booth on the road out of Capernaum, collecting duties for Rome. Fishermen and traders had to pass him every day. Then Jesus stopped and said two words: "Follow me." Matthew stood up and left.

Caravaggio was so gripped by that moment he painted it twice — the mundane and the miraculous, caught in a single shaft of light across a room full of coin-counters. The scene has never stopped attracting painters.

Matthew became patron of accountants, bankers, tax collectors, and customs officers. His Gospel, the first in the New Testament, is the most Jewish of the four. In Marseille — a city where traders, port officials, and creative accounting have always been part of the fabric — the dedication fits.
