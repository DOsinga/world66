Michael is the one who throws Satan out of heaven in the Book of Revelation — commander of the heavenly army, sword drawn, dragon underfoot. He accumulated patronages across the centuries: soldiers, police, the dying. His feast, Michaelmas on 29 September, was one of the four great quarter-days of the medieval calendar when rents were paid and contracts settled.

There are thousands of Saint Michael churches across Europe alone. Mont Saint-Michel in Normandy, crypt chapels in Irish monasteries, cathedrals in Brussels — he's everywhere. What distinguishes them is almost never the dedication. It's the location.

This one is in a residential quartier between the Vieux-Port and the heights of the city, among apartment buildings and small shops. The contrast is the point: the archangel of cosmic warfare, rendered at neighbourhood scale, a stone façade among the stucco of daily life.
