Simon, son of Jonah. Renamed by Christ "the rock on which I will build my church." He walked on water and then sank. He denied knowing Jesus three times before the cock crowed. He pulled in the miraculous catch of fish when the risen Lord appeared on the shore of Galilee. He is the most fallible, most human, and most widely venerated apostle in Western Christianity.

Roman tradition holds that Peter came to Rome, founded the see that bears his name, and asked to be crucified upside down — his own modification, out of humility. The two crossed keys he holds in every painting and statue are the keys of heaven, the symbol of apostolic authority the papacy has claimed ever since.

In Marseille, where tradition credits Mary Magdalene and Lazarus with bringing Christianity from Palestine, dedication to Peter carries extra weight. He was a fisherman before he was an apostle — and fisherman's patron as well as keeper of the celestial gate. This parish is away from the tourist circuits, doing the ordinary work of Catholic neighbourhood life: baptisms, weddings, Sunday mass. Peter would recognise the rhythm.
