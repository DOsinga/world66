Saint Roch was born around 1295 in Montpellier — then part of the Crown of Aragon — and on inheriting his father's wealth distributed it to the poor and set out on pilgrimage to Rome. Reaching northern Italy during an outbreak of plague, he turned aside to nurse the sick in the hospitals of Acquapendente, Cesena, and Piacenza. According to his legend, he himself contracted plague near Piacenza, retreated to a forest to die, and was kept alive by a dog who brought him bread each day and licked his sores clean. He recovered, was imprisoned on his return to Montpellier apparently unrecognised, and died in his cell in 1327. He is depicted almost invariably with the dog and with his robe lifted to show the plague bubo on his thigh.

In Marseille, Saint Roch is not merely a generic plague saint but carries a specific and terrible resonance. The Great Plague of 1720 — the last major outbreak of bubonic plague in Western Europe — struck the city with catastrophic force: roughly 50,000 of Marseille's 90,000 inhabitants died within two years. The epidemic began when the cargo ship Grand Saint Antoine docked in the Vieux-Port with infected bales of cloth from the Levant, and spread with devastating speed through the cramped streets of the port. Churches and parishes organised what response they could; devotion to Roch intensified across Provence as the only intercessor who had been where these people now found themselves — a man who had touched plague victims and survived.

This church, dedicated to that plague saint, stands in the southern reaches of the city in a neighbourhood that in 1720 would have been on the edge of urban Marseille, close enough to feel the epidemic's full weight. Churches of Saint Roch proliferated across France after the Black Death of 1347–52 and again after 1720, often built or rededicated by survivors making vows during the worst of it. Whether this building dates from such a vow is not documented, but the dedication itself speaks to Marseille's long and painful intimacy with pestilence — a city that knows better than most what it means to invoke a healer who survived by grace and a dog's loyalty.
