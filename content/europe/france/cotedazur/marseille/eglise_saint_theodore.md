Theodore Tiro was a Roman legionary martyred around 306 CE at Amasea in Pontus, now northern Turkey. He refused to sacrifice to the Roman gods, confessed Christianity, and was burned alive. His body was reportedly recovered intact from the flames. He became one of the earliest soldier martyrs of the Eastern church, and his cult spread fast to Constantinople and eventually western Europe.

Before merchants from Venice sailed to Alexandria in 828 and stole the relics of Saint Mark, Theodore was Venice's patron saint. His column still stands in the Piazzetta San Marco, facing the winged lion across the water entrance to the republic — the city quietly kept both saints even after upgrading.

This church in the northwestern arrondissements of Marseille carries that Eastern tradition. The city's Greek founding myth — the Phocaean settlers who arrived around 600 BCE — and its centuries of eastern Mediterranean trade gave Marseille channels that carried soldier saints westward long before the crusades made them fashionable everywhere. Theodore arrived here along a shipping route, the way most things in Marseille always did.
