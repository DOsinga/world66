The Revolution shut down almost every religious school in France. Madeleine Sophie Barat's response was to build them back. In 1800, she co-founded the Society of the Sacred Heart and spent the next six decades planting schools from Paris to Missouri.

She was no soft saint. Her brother had put her through a rigorous education in Latin, Greek, history, and theology — unusual for a girl in the 1780s. That formation shaped every school the Society opened. The Sacred Heart houses educated Catholic elite families and ran free schools for the poor in parallel.

Barat governed the congregation for sixty-three years, dying in 1865. This Marseille parish bearing her name is a neighbourhood church, but it also marks the reach of what she built — all the way to the Mediterranean.
