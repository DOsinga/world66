Teresa of Ávila spent twenty-five years in a comfortable convent and decided she'd had enough of comfortable convents. In 1562 she founded the first Discalced Carmelite house — discalced meaning barefoot, a deliberate provocation — and spent the rest of her life founding more, facing down bishops, abbots, and the Spanish Inquisition along the way.

She also wrote. The *Interior Castle* maps the soul's journey toward God through seven concentric mansions. It's still in print. So is her autobiography. Mystic literature rarely travels four centuries intact, but hers does.

In 1970, Pope Paul VI named her a Doctor of the Church — the first woman to receive that title, shared with Catherine of Siena. This Marseille church carries her name into a port city that understood, in its way, what it meant to refuse comfortable arrangements.
