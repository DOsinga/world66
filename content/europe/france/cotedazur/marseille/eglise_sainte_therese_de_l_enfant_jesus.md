Thérèse Martin entered a Carmelite convent at fifteen, never left, and died of tuberculosis at twenty-four. No dramatic acts, no missions, no miracles. Just what she called the Little Way — ordinary things done with complete trust in God's mercy. She wrote it all down in *Story of a Soul*, under obedience, with no expectation anyone outside the convent would read it.

The book went everywhere. She was canonised in 1925, twenty-eight years after her death, before half a million people in St Peter's Square. In France, the timing landed hard — the inter-war years, the country still bleeding from the trenches, a saint who said smallness was no obstacle to holiness. Parishes built in the 1920s and 1930s chose her almost instinctively.

This church is one of them. The name on the facade is a timestamp: this neighbourhood was built up between the wars, and the parish that formed here put Thérèse on the door.
