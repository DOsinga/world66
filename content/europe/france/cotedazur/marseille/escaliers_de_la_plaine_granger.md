Marseille is full of stairs. The limestone hills demanded it: every neighbourhood in this city negotiated gradient rather than ignored it. The Escaliers de la Plaine Granger are in the northern arrondissements, well away from tourist routes, in the working city that visitors rarely see.

These are not ceremonial stairs. The Saint-Charles staircase was built to make arrival feel like theatre. These were built to get people and their shopping up and down the hill. That distinction matters — it's the difference between civic performance and actual urban life, and this city has both.

Come in the morning, when the light is coming from the east. From the top, the northern city spreads below you. The best panoramas in Marseille are often not at the designated viewpoints but on the way between two unremarkable addresses.
