Grand Pin means great pine, and the name is the whole description. A pinus pinea — the Mediterranean stone pine, the one with a tall trunk and a flat parasol crown — either stood here or still does. In a city with long, punishing summers, a single large specimen like that is genuinely useful infrastructure.

The coordinates put this in the southeastern reaches of Marseille, near Mazargues and the limestone approaches to the Calanques. Lower density, quieter streets, the massifs beginning to press in from the south. This is the kind of neighbourhood square that exists for the people who live around it: benches, pétanque terrain, local use.

The word esplaï itself is Occitan — used across southern France and Catalonia for a leisure space — a reminder that Marseille's cultural geography belongs to a wider Mediterranean world than just France.
