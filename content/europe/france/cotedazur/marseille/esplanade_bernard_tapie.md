Bernard Tapie bought Olympique de Marseille in 1986 and spent eight years turning it into a European power. Five consecutive French titles. And in May 1993, Munich: OM 1, AC Milan 0. Basile Boli's header in the 43rd minute. The only Champions League a French club has ever won. In Marseille, that night is the emotional summit of living memory.

The fall arrived weeks later. Before the Munich final, OM had paid Valenciennes players to throw a French league match — protecting the squad for Europe. When the scandal broke, the consequences came fast: stripped of the 1993 French title, barred from European competition, eventually relegated. Tapie faced criminal proceedings and served time. The Champions League was not taken away. Marseillais note that distinction carefully.

He came back to Marseille and died here in 2021. The city mourned him genuinely, and then named this esplanade after him. The scandal was part of the man, not a reason to erase him. Whether that's loyalty or convenient amnesia depends on your vantage point.
