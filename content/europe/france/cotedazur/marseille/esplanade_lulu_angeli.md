Marseille names its squares and streets with genuine feeling. Not just generals and politicians — café owners, local coaches, activists who fought for better housing, neighbourhood figures who made life better for people who lived nearby. The Angeli name has Corsican or Italian roots, both deeply embedded in a port city that absorbed wave after wave of Mediterranean migration across the nineteenth and twentieth centuries.

No plaque explains who Lulu Angeli was. That's almost the point. The people who named this esplanade knew.

The southern arrondissements where this sits are residential Marseille: families, deep neighbourhood loyalties, the kind of place where local memory outlasts official record.
