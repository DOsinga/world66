The Fontaine Cantini stands at the junction of Rue Paradis and Boulevard Périer as one of the most elaborate public fountains in France from the Beaux-Arts period. It was commissioned by the city in 1911 from sculptor Jules Cantini, who donated it to Marseille on his death in 1916. The allegorical composition represents the city and the sea: a monumental central figure rises above a basin supported by mythological and marine elements, executed in a style that combines academic rigour with the decorative exuberance characteristic of the period.

Cantini himself was a significant figure in Marseille's cultural life. Born in 1826 to an Italian family who came to Marseille as marble merchants, he built a substantial fortune in the stone trade and used it to accumulate an art collection and to commission public works throughout the city. He was among the last of the great bourgeois patrons who shaped the appearance of French provincial cities in the 19th century, channelling private wealth into public space at a scale that municipal budgets rarely permitted.

On his death Cantini bequeathed his personal art collection to the city, and it became the founding core of the Musée Cantini on Rue Grignan — a collection strong in 19th-century painting and 20th-century works by artists with connections to Marseille and the south. The fountain and the museum together constitute a legacy that is more visible in the city's daily life than most private philanthropists manage to achieve.
