Henri-Jacques Espérandieu died at forty-five, before Notre-Dame de la Garde was even consecrated. He never saw his most famous building finished. But his drawings governed everything: the Romano-Byzantine striping, the round tower, the dome — all his. He also designed the central cascade of the Palais Longchamp. Two projects, two of Marseille's defining silhouettes.

This fountain sits near the Notre-Dame de la Garde hill — the landscape Espérandieu reshaped more than anyone. Placing a monument to an architect within sight of his building is a good instinct. Look up from the fountain and you're looking at his life's work: the basilica rising above the rooftops, the golden Virgin at the top added after his death but drawn after his plan.

He was a pupil of Viollet-le-Duc. He brought the neo-Byzantine vocabulary of Romanesque revival to Marseille's hillsides and dressed civic ambition in polychrome stone. The fountain is modest beside the monument — which is exactly right.
