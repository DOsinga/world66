Fort Saint-Jean guards the northern side of the Vieux-Port entrance. Louis XIV ordered it built in 1660 — officially to defend the city from foreign attack, though the cannons actually pointed inland. Marseille had revolted against royal authority the year before, and the king wanted the city to know it.

The fort served military and then prison functions for three centuries before being absorbed into the MuCEM complex. A pedestrian bridge now connects it to the Mucem's J4 building across the harbour mouth; another spans to the Esplanade de la Tourette near the Cathédrale de la Major. Walk the ramparts for free — museum exhibits inside need a Mucem ticket, but the views are wide open to everyone.

From up here: the Vieux-Port behind you, the Frioul islands and Château d'If straight out to sea, the cathedral and the Panier rising to the north. It's one of the best vantage points in central Marseille, and you're standing on four hundred years of the city's defences to get it.
