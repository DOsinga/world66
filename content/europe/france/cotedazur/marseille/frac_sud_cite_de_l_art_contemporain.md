The Fonds Régional d'Art Contemporain — Frac Sud — was established in 1982 under France's decentralisation policy, when the government created regional contemporary art funds across the country. The Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur collection now holds over 1,200 works assembled over four decades, with a programmatic emphasis on artists from the Mediterranean basin and North Africa — a perspective deliberately distinct from that of the major Paris institutions and from the Côte d'Azur's more familiar Matisse-and-Picasso narrative.

The building was designed by Japanese architect Kengo Kuma and completed in 2013 as part of the Marseille-Provence European Capital of Culture programme. It sits on the J4 esplanade — the reclaimed port land that also holds the MuCEM and Fort Saint-Jean — and is clad in translucent polycarbonate panels that diffuse the intense Mediterranean light into the galleries without direct exposure. The material is deliberate: Kuma's response to a climate in which glass would cook most things behind it.

The Frac operates explicitly as a regional institution: a significant share of the collection circulates at any given time to venues across Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur, and the Marseille building shows only a fraction of what is owned at once. Temporary exhibitions tend toward the thematic and argumentative — more interested in questioning how Mediterranean culture is framed than in displaying it.
