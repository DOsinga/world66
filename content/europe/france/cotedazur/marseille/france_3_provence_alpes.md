France 3 was created in 1972 as a deliberate counter to French broadcasting's chronic Paris-centrism — local editions of a national network, staffed by people who actually lived in the regions they covered. The Provence-Alpes edition runs from the Rhône delta to the Italian border: Marseille, Aix, Avignon, Nice, the Alpine departments, Corsican affairs.

The Marseille building is where the regional news gets made. The *journal de France 3* has long been one of the most-watched broadcasts in the south, with correspondents filing from across the territory on local politics, the economy, football, weather. The Mediterranean climate and the particularities of Provençal life give it a character you won't find in the Paris-facing national editions.

This is a working broadcast facility, not a visitor attraction. But as an institution it represents something worth noting: public media that tries to reflect the places where people actually live, rather than the city the rest of the country talks about.
