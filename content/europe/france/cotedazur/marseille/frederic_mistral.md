Frédéric Mistral won the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1904. He took the money and used it to found an ethnographic museum of Provençal life in Arles. That tells you everything about him.

Back in 1854, when French was actively erasing regional languages, Mistral co-founded the Félibrige — a movement to revive Occitan as a living literary language. He spent sixty years writing epic poetry in Provençal, compiling its dictionary, and arguing that Provence was not just a corner of France but a civilisation with its own literature and calendar. His poem Mirèio brought him European fame, letters from Lamartine, and eventually the Nobel.

The monument here is Marseille staking its claim to a poet who actually preferred Maillane and Arles. He would have understood that move. The Félibrige's journal was published here, and the city's commercial weight made it the movement's institutional centre, even if its soul lived in the villages.
