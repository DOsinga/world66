The Manufacture des Tabacs opened in the 1860s in the Belle de Mai district, a working-class neighbourhood north of the city centre. At its peak it employed over 2,000 workers, the majority of them women — a significant industrial workforce in a city where industry was concentrated in the port. The factory closed in 1990, leaving a large complex of 19th-century brick buildings and 45,000 square metres of floor space.

The conversion began in 1992, not with a master plan but with artists and cultural organisations moving into vacant buildings, negotiating use informally, and gradually formalising a collective structure. By the time the Friche became a flagship project in France's cultural policy, it already had a character developed from the inside out rather than imposed by a developer. Today more than 80 organisations work here — theatre companies, music producers, graphic designers, architects, restaurants, a cinema, a skateboard park, a gallery that can handle very large-format work.

The rooftop is open to the public and is one of the best vantage points in Marseille — a wide panorama from the hills behind the city to the sea, with the domes of Notre-Dame de la Garde and the Major visible simultaneously. Come in the evening when the light is right and the rooftop bar is open.
