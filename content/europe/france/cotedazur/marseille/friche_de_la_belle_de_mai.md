The Manufacture des Tabacs opened in the 1860s in a working-class neighbourhood north of the centre. At its peak, over 2,000 workers — mostly women — rolled cigarettes here. It closed in 1990 and left behind 45,000 square metres of 19th-century brick.

What happened next wasn't a redevelopment plan. Artists and theatre companies started moving in informally, negotiating space, building something from the inside out. By the time the Friche became a flagship of French cultural policy, it already had a character nobody had designed. Today over 80 organisations work here — theatre groups, music producers, a cinema, a skate park, a gallery big enough for large-format work, restaurants.

Go up to the rooftop. It's open to the public and it's one of the best vantage points in Marseille — Notre-Dame de la Garde and the Major visible at the same time, the sea beyond. Come in the evening when the bar is open.
