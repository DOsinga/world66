Marseille has one of the largest communities of Spanish, Portuguese, and Catalan origin in France. Workers from Almería and the Portuguese Alentejo came for the docks, the soap factories, the construction trades. Their descendants stayed. You still find traces in the surnames, the food markets, and the occasional shop name that slips into Iberian languages.

Dos Mares — of the seas — is exactly that kind of trace. A gallery name that looks south and west across the Mediterranean rather than north toward Paris.

It sits in the dense commercial fabric north of the Vieux-Port, where the city's successive waves of immigration have layered on top of one another. Art here tends to pull from the whole basin: North Africa, the Iberian Peninsula, the Levant. In Marseille, that's not a curatorial stance — it's geography.
