Standing quietly near the southeastern edge of the city, *Génèse* — "Genesis" in English — is a public artwork whose title reaches back to the very idea of beginning. In a city as ancient as Marseille, founded by Greek sailors from Phocaea around 600 BCE, the word carries an unusual weight: every stone, every harbour wall, every trading route here has an origin story layered beneath the next.

The piece occupies its site as a meditation on creation rather than commemoration. Where most civic sculpture celebrates a person or event, a work titled *Génèse* turns attention to the moment before form — the point at which something comes into being. Port cities have always been places of genesis in this sense: goods arrive as raw material and leave as finished product, ideas cross with passengers, and communities form from the collision of languages. Marseille, which became France's leading Mediterranean gateway, has been generating such collisions for over two and a half millennia.

Visitors approaching from the coastal path often encounter the sculpture unexpectedly, its forms emerging from the landscape rather than commanding it. The effect suits the title: genesis is rarely loud. Take a moment to sit with it before moving on to the calanques beyond.
