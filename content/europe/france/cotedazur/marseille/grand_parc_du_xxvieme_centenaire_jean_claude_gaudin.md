Marseille was founded by Greek settlers from Phocaea in 600 BCE. When the year 2000 arrived, the city was exactly 2,600 years old — and civic ambition demanded a public park worthy of the occasion. The Grand Parc du XXVIème Centenaire was laid out in the southern reaches of the 8th arrondissement as Marseille's major new green space for the millennium, combining formal gardens with broad lawns and tree-lined promenades designed to last another century.

Jean-Claude Gaudin served as mayor of Marseille from 1995 to 2020, the longest tenure in modern municipal history, and the park was a signature project of his administration. Following his death in 2022, the city council voted to attach his name to the park he had championed. The double-barrelled name — centenaire and Gaudin — now marks both the historical occasion and the man whose twenty-five-year mayoralty shaped so much of contemporary Marseille.

The park occupies a mid-sized green wedge convenient to the residential blocks of the 8th arrondissement south of the Prado boulevard. Families come for the open lawn; joggers use the perimeter paths; and the Mediterranean light, which softens to amber in the late afternoon, turns even a municipal park into something worth stopping for.
