Marseille is built on a limestone massif of narrow valleys and flat-topped rises. The Grand Plateau sits in the northern residential arc — above the Vieux-Port basin, between Cinq-Avenues and Belle-de-Mai. High enough to get above the noise; not famous enough to draw tourists.

On clear days the view south takes in the Frioul archipelago and the Château d'If. On the clearest days you can see the limestone ridges of the Calanques. This is the kind of prospect Marseillais take for granted and visitors discover with surprise. The city's terrain is relentlessly three-dimensional: wherever you find height, you find a different city below you.

The surrounding streets are working-class residential Marseille — bakeries, halal butchers, Maghrebi grocery stores, small cafés. A flat open terrace with a view is a serious amenity in a dense neighbourhood. People treat it as one.
