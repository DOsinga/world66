Henri Cosquer found the cave in 1985 thinking it was empty. He went back in 1991 and found walls covered in art from two separate eras: horses, bison, and ibex from around 27,000 BCE, and a later layer from around 19,000 BCE with auks, great auks, and seals — the most complete prehistoric depiction of marine life found anywhere in Europe.

The cave is submerged because when those older paintings were made, sea levels were about 120 metres lower. As the glaciers retreated, rising water sealed the entrance around 10,000 BCE. The cave sat isolated for roughly 10,000 years. To reach it today, divers swim 175 metres through an underwater passage. Some caused damage after Cosquer went public. The replica was built partly to remove the reason to attempt the dive.

The Stefano Boeri building holding the replica cantilevers over a reflecting pool — an intentional nod to the submerged original. The reproduction is precise enough to give you a real sense of the space. You just won't need a wetsuit.
