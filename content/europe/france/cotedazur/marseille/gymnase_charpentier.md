You're in Belle de Mai — the cigarette factory district, now home to the Friche cultural complex a few blocks away. The Gymnase Charpentier is the kind of facility that anchors a working-class neighbourhood: school teams, local clubs, Tuesday evening training sessions.

The name means "carpenter" in French, and it probably honours a local councillor or sports administrator from the early 20th century rather than any celebrated Charpentier you'd recognise. That's very much the point. French municipalities put names like this on gymnasiums so that people who mattered to the neighbourhood don't disappear entirely — absorbed into the rhythms of daily life rather than commemorated on a pedestal.

Walk around and you'll notice the streets still have the narrow, slightly ragged grain of an older Marseille that urban renewal mostly missed.
