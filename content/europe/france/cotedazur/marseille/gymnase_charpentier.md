The Gymnase Charpentier occupies a position in the central-northern fabric of Marseille, in a zone of mixed residential and light-industrial streets that includes the Belle de Mai district — historically a working-class neighbourhood built around the La Belle de Mai cigarette factory and now home to the Friche de la Belle de Mai cultural complex. The name Charpentier — meaning "carpenter" in French and a common family name — likely honours a local dignitary, councillor, or benefactor rather than the celebrated composer Gustave Charpentier, whose connection to Marseille, while plausible given his southern touring, is not well documented in local records.

In the tradition of French municipal naming, gymnasiums and sports halls in working-class districts were often dedicated to local political figures, educators, or sports administrators who had contributed to neighbourhood life. A Charpentier who served on the city council, directed a local school, or founded an athletics club in the early or mid-20th century would have been a natural candidate for this kind of commemoration — the facility's name embedding a figure who might otherwise have vanished from public memory into the everyday geography of the neighbourhood.

The surrounding streets retain the grain of an older Marseille, with narrow blocks and corner cafés that have survived the urban renewal projects that reshaped more prominent parts of the city. The gymnasium serves the local schools and clubs whose members would rarely think to question the name above the entrance, which is perhaps the most honest form of civic memory: not celebrated but simply present, absorbed into the rhythm of a Tuesday evening training session.
