Jean-Claude Gaudin ran Marseille from 1995 to 2020. Twenty-five years. He inherited a city in economic freefall and left one with a rebuilt waterfront, a metro extension, and the MuCEM — all capped by the 2013 European Capital of Culture year. His critics will tell you he also left behind stark inequality between the wealthy south and the neglected north, and governance that got messier toward the end. Both things are true.

They named a gymnasium after him in the southern arrondissements rather than, say, a building in the Euroméditerranée zone he championed up north. There's something deliberate in that — an attempt to tie his name to the whole city, not just the flagship regeneration project.

A gymnasium is a particular kind of civic honour in France. Not a boulevard, not a bridge — something the local kids use every week. He died in 2023. Whatever history eventually decides about him, the schoolchildren training here probably don't think about it much.
