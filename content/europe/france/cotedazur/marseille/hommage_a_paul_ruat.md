Paul Ruat was born here in 1860 and trained at the École des Beaux-Arts before going to Paris to study. Then he came back, essentially for good. He worked in a post-Impressionist register and spent his career painting the things that absorbed every landscape painter who looked at this stretch of coast: the limestone outcrops of the Calanques, the pine-covered hills east of the city, the particular shimmer of water in the Vieux-Port.

He exhibited at the Salon and was respected in regional circles. He never became famous nationally, which is fine — his work reads now as a precise record of a Marseille landscape that has since changed considerably.

The word "hommage" rather than "monument" tells you something about how the city remembers him. This isn't a general on a horse. It's the kind of tribute a Mediterranean city gives to someone who spent a lifetime looking at it carefully.
