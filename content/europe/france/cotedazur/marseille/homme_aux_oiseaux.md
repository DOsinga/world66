"Man with Birds" — the title is exactly what it looks like. A figure with arms extended, birds landing or about to. The posture and handling of form place it squarely in the mid-century European humanist tradition: the kind of sculpture that proliferated after the wars, when artists kept returning to the relationship between people and the natural world as though looking for something that hadn't been destroyed.

Marseille is actually a relevant setting for this. The Calanques and the hills around the city are major stopover points for migrating birds moving between Africa and Europe. The air over this coast has always been full of things passing through — same as the port.

The sculpture doesn't compete for attention. Come past early in the morning when the light off the sea is low and the actual birds of Marseille are going about their business, and it earns its place quietly.
