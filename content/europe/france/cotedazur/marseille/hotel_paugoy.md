The Nazis levelled most of this neighbourhood in 1943. The Germans cleared the northern Vieux-Port in a single January weekend — dynamite, deportations, 1,500 buildings gone. The Hôtel Paugoy is one of the few things left from before any of that.

It dates to the 17th or 18th century, built by the Paugoy family — merchants who made their money in Mediterranean and Atlantic trade and wanted a house that said so. Classical proportions, enough ornament to signal wealth without shouting it. The ground floor handled business; the upper floors maintained the polite fiction that the family had transcended commerce.

What makes it remarkable is simply that it survived. This part of Marseille has been demolished twice — once by time and once by occupation. The Hôtel Paugoy is standing in a city it no longer quite belongs to.
