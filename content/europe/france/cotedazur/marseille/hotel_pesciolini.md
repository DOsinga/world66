The name says it all. Pesciolini is unmistakably Italian, and Italian merchants had been working Marseille's port since the medieval period. Genoese, Neapolitan, Ligurian — their trading networks ran through the Mediterranean and Marseille was a natural stop.

The Pesciolinis built in the 18th century when Italian families already formed the largest foreign community in the city. The facade follows the Marseille bourgeois playbook exactly: classical proportions, a commanding piano nobile, ornamental details calibrated to announce a family of substance. They fit in while keeping the name.

The 19th century brought Italian labourers by the hundred thousand — the great immigration wave that swelled the city's population. The Hôtel Pesciolini is an earlier chapter: the merchant who preceded the worker, and who built in stone to prove the difference.
