Built in 1653 on the Quai du Port, this is the building that has run Marseille for nearly four centuries. The baroque facade faces the Vieux-Port directly — whoever designed this knew exactly what they were doing with the location.

The coat of arms medallion on the front is by Pierre Puget, Marseille's greatest sculptor. He grew up a few streets from here and went on to become one of the most important artists in Louis XIV's France. The medallion is easy to miss, but now that you know it's Puget's work, look again.

The interior is closed to visitors, but you don't need to go inside. Just stand on the quayside opposite and look at the whole composition: the building, the water, the boats. This is what the old port looked like before everything else was added around it.
