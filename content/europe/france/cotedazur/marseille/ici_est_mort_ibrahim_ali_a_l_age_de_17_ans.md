On 21 February 1995, Ibrahim Ali was 17. Three young men who had been distributing Front National electoral material that evening came back and shot him in the back. He was Comorian, and that was why. They were convicted of murder — 15 to 20 years each.

What followed was bigger than the trial. Hundreds of thousands marched across France. Parliament changed the criminal code: murder in the context of distributing political material now carries an aggravated charge. The case set legal precedent on the relationship between party activity and racially motivated violence. One killing, one legislative change.

The city of Marseille placed this plaque in 2021 — 26 years after the fact. Ali's family and anti-racism groups had been asking for decades. The text reads simply: "Ici est mort Ibrahim Ali à l'âge de 17 ans." No elaboration. This is the 14th arrondissement, home to Marseille's largest Comorian community.
