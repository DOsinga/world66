On the night of 21 February 1995, three young men who had been distributing Front National electoral material in the neighbourhood returned and shot Ibrahim Ali, a 17-year-old of Comorian origin, in the back. He died at the scene. The perpetrators were convicted of murder and sentenced to between 15 and 20 years. The killing was politically motivated: all three were connected to the party's youth wing and had targeted Ali on racial grounds.

The murder's political consequences extended beyond the convictions. It sparked one of the largest anti-racism demonstrations in French history, drawing hundreds of thousands across the country. Parliament subsequently amended the criminal code to make murder committed in the context of distributing political material an aggravating circumstance under French law — a direct legislative response to the case. The prosecution also established legal precedent on the relationship between party political activity and racially motivated violence.

The municipality of Marseille placed the commemorative plaque in 2021, more than 25 years after the killing. The delay became a point of sustained public debate; Ali's family and anti-racism organisations had long campaigned for official recognition. The plaque reads simply "Ici est mort Ibrahim Ali à l'âge de 17 ans" — here died Ibrahim Ali at the age of 17 — without elaboration. The 14th arrondissement is home to Marseille's largest Comorian community; France has the largest Comorian diaspora in the world.
