The IHU Méditerranée Infection was founded in Marseille with support from the French government's Investments for the Future programme, designed to create internationally competitive clusters linking clinical care, teaching, and research under one roof. The institute focuses on infectious diseases with particular attention to those affecting the Mediterranean basin and sub-Saharan Africa — including tropical and neglected diseases, antimicrobial resistance, and the human microbiome. Its sequencing laboratory has published an impressive stream of work on newly described bacteria and viruses, establishing it as one of Europe's more prolific centres for microbial genomics.

The institute became globally known — and globally controversial — in March 2020 when its long-time director, Professor Didier Raoult, announced results from a small, non-randomised trial suggesting that hydroxychloroquine combined with azithromycin could reduce the viral load of COVID-19 patients. The claim spread with extraordinary speed, endorsed by prominent politicians in France and the United States and seized upon by a public desperate for treatment options. Mainstream epidemiologists and regulatory bodies quickly challenged the methodology: the trial lacked a proper control group, the sample was small, patients were excluded after the fact, and serious cardiac risks were not addressed. Subsequent large randomised trials found no benefit. The episode became a case study in how the pressure of a pandemic can amplify preliminary findings far beyond what the evidence supports.

Raoult retired in 2022 amid a disciplinary investigation by France's medical council. The institute he built continues its research programme, and the building — a striking contemporary structure near the university hospital campus — remains a working facility rather than a monument to the controversy that surrounded it. For visitors with an interest in the intersection of science, media, and public health, the site is a tangible reminder of how rapidly information and misinformation travel in a crisis.
