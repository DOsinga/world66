The tango was invented in the port slums of Buenos Aires — by Spanish and Italian immigrants, West Africans, people from the Argentine interior, everyone packed into the same neighbourhood with nowhere to be alone. The dance is structurally incapable of being done solo. Two bodies, constant negotiation, neither one leading all the time.

That origin story lands differently in Marseille. This is a city where Genoese merchants, Corsican fishermen, Armenian refugees, North African workers, and Comorian families have shared the same streets across different centuries. The rue du Panier has been home to all of them at one point or another. The productive friction is the point.

The title is in English — not translated, not adapted. Because the phrase went from Buenos Aires to Paris to the whole world and became universal currency. In Marseille, the tango's grammar isn't a metaphor. It's almost a description of how the place works.
