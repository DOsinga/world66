*Bagatelle* is French for a small pleasure, a trifle. The Parisians named a garden in the Bois de Boulogne that in the 18th century and the word stuck to small, formally composed gardens across the country. Marseille's version is in the 8th arrondissement, on the prosperous south side of the city.

The 8th is a different Marseille from the northern districts — wider streets, better-kept buildings, gardens that actually get watered. Jardin Bagatelle fits that context: trimmed, planted, pleasant.

The name is honest. This is an afternoon pause, not a half-day visit. Find a bench, look at the planting, let the sun do its work. The trifle, as advertised.
