The word *bagatelle* — French for trifle or small pleasure — has been attached to decorative gardens across France since the Parisian Bagatelle in the Bois de Boulogne was laid out in the eighteenth century. Marseille's Jardin Bagatelle, in the 8th arrondissement on the south side of the city, carries on that tradition: a small, formally composed garden meant to be a pleasant diversion rather than a grand public park.

The 8th arrondissement is one of Marseille's more prosperous residential districts, stretching between the Prado boulevard and the coastline. Gardens in this part of the city tend to be better maintained and more planted than the pocket squares of the northern arrondissements.

Jardin Bagatelle is a genuine small pleasure — a trimmed green space with decorative planting suited to an afternoon pause rather than a half-day visit. The name promises nothing more than that, and the garden delivers.
