When the nineteenth-century planners needed a name for a new garden near the Panier, they reached for antiquity. Corinth — the Greek city-state. It sounds affected, but Marseille has a specific claim on classical names: the city was founded as the Greek colony of Massalia around 600 BCE. Educated Marseillais were proud of that. Ancient references felt less like affectation and more like genealogy.

The Panier is the oldest surviving neighbourhood — narrow streets climbing the hill above the original port. Open space here is genuinely rare. Every gap between buildings that gets a tree and a bench is worth knowing about.

Jardin de Corinthe is small. The name is larger than the garden. That's also very Marseille.
