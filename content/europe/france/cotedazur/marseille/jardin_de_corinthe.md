Jardin de Corinthe takes its name from the ancient Greek city of Corinth — a classical reference of the kind that was fashionable in nineteenth-century urban planning, when newly laid out streets and squares across France were given names drawn from antiquity. Marseille, with its own Greek origins as the colony of Massalia, had particular reason to reach for such names: the city's educated classes were proud of the ancient heritage visible in the stones beneath the modern city.

The garden sits in the inner city, not far from the Panier — the oldest surviving neighbourhood of Marseille, built on the hill above the ancient port. The streets here are narrow and the buildings close, which makes any green space valuable.

Jardin de Corinthe is a small garden with a resonant name, set in one of the most historically layered parts of the city. It offers residents of the Panier and its surroundings a patch of open air in a quarter where open air is in short supply.
