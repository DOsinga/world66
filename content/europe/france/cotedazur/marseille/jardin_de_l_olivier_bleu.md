Jardin de l'Olivier Bleu — the Garden of the Blue Olive Tree — is named for the characteristic colour of olive foliage in strong sunlight: the silvery, blue-grey underside of the leaves that makes an olive grove shimmer in the Provençal wind. The olive tree is the iconic plant of Mediterranean Provence, planted across Marseille's gardens and hillsides wherever the thin soil and summer drought allow.

The garden sits in the 15th arrondissement, in the far north of Marseille — a district that is largely residential and industrial, some distance from the grand parks and historic sites of the city centre. Green spaces here are neighbourhood resources, and a garden named for a tree as symbolically freighted as the olive carries a certain civic weight.

Jardin de l'Olivier Bleu is a small garden in a northern neighbourhood that has fewer green amenities than the more affluent southern arrondissements. The name is better than the description demands — but names in this city have always been chosen with a certain Marseillais flair.
