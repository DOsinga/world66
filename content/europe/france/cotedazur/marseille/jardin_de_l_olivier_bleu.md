The name gets the colour right. Olive leaves have a silver-blue underside that catches the Provençal wind and flickers. In strong summer light an olive grove looks almost metallic. Whoever named this garden was paying attention.

The 15th arrondissement is the far north of Marseille — residential and industrial, a long way from the calanques coast or the historic centre. Green space here is a neighbourhood resource, and the 15th has fewer of those than the more affluent southern districts.

The name carries more poetry than most neighbourhood gardens manage. And the olive tree — this particular Mediterranean plant that survives drought and thin soil and two thousand years of human attention — is the right symbol for a city that has always been hard and enduring.
