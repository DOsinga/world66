Pierre Puget was born here in 1620 and became one of the great Baroque sculptors of his era — his work is in the Louvre, but the hill above the Vieux-Port still carries his name. Climb it and you get the view he grew up with: the harbour mouth, Saint-Victor on the far shore, the whole sweep of the port below.

This isn't a manicured park. It's terraced paths cut into a rocky slope, maritime pines and Mediterranean scrub holding the hillside together. It connects to the Pharo gardens further along the promontory, but up here it feels more like garrigue than city.

The 7th below is one of Marseille's most fashionable addresses. The contrast between the animated seafront and this quiet hillside is exactly the point.
