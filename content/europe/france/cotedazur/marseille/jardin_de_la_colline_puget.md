Jardin de la Colline Puget sits on the rocky hillside that forms the southern flank of the Vieux-Port's entrance, in the 7th arrondissement. The Puget hill — named after the Baroque sculptor Pierre Puget, Marseille's most celebrated artist, who was born in the city in 1620 — rises steeply above the waterfront and offers views across the harbour mouth toward the Basilique Saint-Victor on the opposite shore.

The garden is less a formal park than a series of terraced paths and planted areas cut into the rocky slope, where maritime pines and Mediterranean scrub hold the hillside together. The site connects to the broader green network around the Pharo palace and gardens, which occupy the promontory at the Vieux-Port's southern tip. Walking up from the water's edge, the Colline Puget feels like the city giving way to the garrigue.

Access is steep but worth the climb for the views. The 7th arrondissement below is one of Marseille's most fashionable addresses, and the contrast between the quiet hillside garden and the animated seafront is part of its appeal.
