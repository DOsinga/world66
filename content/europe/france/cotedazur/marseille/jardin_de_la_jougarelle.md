Jardin de la Jougarelle sits at the northern edge of Marseille in the 15th arrondissement, in a zone where the city shades gradually into the suburban municipalities — Les Pennes-Mirabeau, Saint-Antoine — that surround it on the inland side. The Jougarelle quarter is a quiet residential area, some distance from the tourist circuits of central Marseille.

The garden is a community amenity in the most direct sense: a patch of grass and shade trees serving the families of the neighbourhood, with the kind of unpretentious provision — benches, a play area, some planted beds — that defines the municipal park at this scale. In a district where private gardens are more common than in the dense inner arrondissements, a public garden like this one fills the gap for residents in apartment buildings.

Few visitors to Marseille will find their way here, but the Jougarelle area gives a candid picture of the city's residential north: working-class, well-functioning, and a long way in mood from the Vieux-Port.
