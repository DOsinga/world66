Jardin de la Magalone surrounds the Château de la Magalone, an eighteenth-century *bastide* — the Provençal term for the country houses that Marseille's wealthy families built in the hills outside the old city. The Magalone, in the 8th arrondissement, is one of the better-preserved examples: a formal house set within walled grounds that have been opened to the public as a garden.

The garden retains some of the character of its original layout, with alleys of plane trees, a vegetable garden section, and formal beds giving way to wilder plantings toward the perimeter wall. The city acquired the property in the twentieth century, and it now functions as one of the more pleasant parks in this prosperous southern district. The 8th arrondissement is bounded to the north by the Prado and to the south by the coastal corniche, and its residents are among the more affluent in Marseille.

The Magalone is worth a detour for anyone interested in the *bastide* tradition — the working country estates that ringed Marseille before the city expanded to absorb them. The house itself is sometimes used for civic functions.
