Jardin des Anciennes Fonderies de Provence — the garden of the old Provençal foundries — occupies land in the 10th arrondissement that was previously given over to industrial production. The foundries of Provence were part of the metalworking and manufacturing activity that spread through Marseille's inner eastern districts in the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, when the city was one of the major industrial ports of the western Mediterranean.

The conversion of former industrial land into green space is a familiar story in post-industrial European cities, and Marseille has pursued it with varying ambition across different sites. This garden is a relatively modest example: the old foundry buildings are gone, and the site has been planted and equipped as a neighbourhood park. Some trace of the industrial past may persist in the layout — a retaining wall, an unusual ground level — but the garden's primary role now is recreational.

The 10th arrondissement is a mixed district of residential streets, small businesses, and the transport infrastructure of the Blancarde rail corridor. A garden on reclaimed industrial land fits naturally into a neighbourhood in the long process of changing its economic character.
