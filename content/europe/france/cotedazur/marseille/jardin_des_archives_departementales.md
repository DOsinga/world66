The Bouches-du-Rhône departmental archives hold the paper record of everything that happened in this corner of France — medieval charters, port registers, family documents, twentieth-century administrative files. The building is in the 3rd arrondissement, in the zone where Marseille parks its civic institutions.

The garden wraps around it in the way French public buildings of this type almost always have: a planted perimeter that softens the institutional architecture and signals that the public is welcome. You don't need to be consulting the archives to sit here. It's open.

For anyone doing genealogical research or digging into the city's history as a port — the collections are serious — the garden is a good place to read notes between sessions.
