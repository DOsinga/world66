*Chutes* means falls. The Lavie was once a stream with enough drop to earn that name — a series of cascades running down through the hills of eastern Marseille before the city grew over it and diverted most of the water underground. The garden in the 10th follows the old valley course, preserving the green corridor the stream left behind.

Linear parks that trace buried watercourses are scattered through Marseille's topography. The city was built on a complicated terrain of hills and valleys, and where the valleys survived the urbanisation, they often became the city's hidden green threads. The Jardin des Chutes Lavie is one of the better examples — a strip of trees and planting that feels genuinely different from the flat neighbourhood gardens nearby.

Coming across it from the dense streets of the 10th, it reads as a surprise. That's the point: the landscape that existed before the city arrived is still faintly legible here.
