Jardin des Chutes Lavie follows the valley of the Lavie, one of the small watercourses that drain the hills of eastern Marseille into the lower city. The name — *chutes* means falls — suggests there was once a cascade or a series of drops along this stream before the surrounding area was urbanised and the water largely contained or culverted. The garden in the 10th arrondissement preserves a green corridor along the old course of the Lavie.

Linear parks that follow stream valleys are a distinctive feature of Marseille's topography. The city is built on a complicated terrain of hills and valleys, and where the valleys have not been entirely built over or converted to roads, they often remain as public green space threading through the urban fabric. The Jardin des Chutes Lavie is one such remnant, connecting a stretch of the 10th arrondissement in a band of trees and planting that feels different in character from the flat neighbourhood gardens nearby.

The 10th arrondissement is a dense inland district with the Blancarde station as its main transport node. Visitors exploring this part of the city away from the tourist circuits will find the Lavie valley garden a genuine surprise — a strip of greenery that gives a sense of the landscape that existed before the city grew over it.
