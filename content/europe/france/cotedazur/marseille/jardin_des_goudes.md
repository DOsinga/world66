Les Goudes is where Marseille's road network gives up. A tiny fishing village at the far southern tip of the 8th arrondissement, technically within city limits but surrounded by limestone cliffs and the sea on three sides. The road dead-ends here. Beyond is the calanques and, eventually, Callelongue — even more remote.

Marseillais come to Les Goudes for the Sunday long lunch and the walk on the cliffs. A handful of houses, a few fishing boats, a restaurant or two. The Jardin des Goudes is necessarily small — the terrain is rock and garrigue, there's not much flat ground. But its position is the thing.

Sitting here with the Mediterranean visible in almost every direction and the city invisible over the hills behind, it is genuinely hard to believe you haven't left Marseille.
