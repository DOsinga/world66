Les Goudes is where Marseille stops — or nearly stops. This tiny fishing village, technically within the city's boundaries at the far southern edge of the 8th arrondissement, sits at the end of a road that dead-ends at the limestone cliffs above the calanques. Jardin des Goudes is the small patch of maintained green space in this remote and singular place.

The village has an end-of-the-world quality that Marseillais love: a handful of houses, a few fishing boats, a restaurant or two, and the calanques beginning just beyond. The road from Marseille passes through the Madrague de Montredon before climbing through the limestone to Les Goudes and the even more remote hamlet of Callelongue further along. Most Marseillais come here for a long Sunday lunch and a walk on the cliffs.

The garden at Les Goudes is necessarily modest — the village is tiny, the terrain is rock and garrigue — but its position is remarkable. Sitting here, with the Mediterranean on three sides and the city invisible over the hills behind, it is difficult to believe you are still within city limits.
