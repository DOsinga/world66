In French cities, gardens named for heroes almost always mean the same thing: the Resistance, the war dead, the occupation. In Marseille, that history has a specific shape. Before the Germans took the southern zone in 1942, the city was a major escape route — the Emergency Rescue Committee operated here, helping Jewish refugees and resistance operatives get out of Europe. Then the Germans arrived and dynamited much of the Panier quarter in 1943.

A garden named *des Héros* in this city carries that weight. It sits in the 6th arrondissement, the dense residential district between the Vieux-Port and the Préfecture. It's a neighbourhood green space, useful to families and residents, with little open space in the surrounding streets.

The history is in the name. The garden itself is what the 6th needs: somewhere to sit.
