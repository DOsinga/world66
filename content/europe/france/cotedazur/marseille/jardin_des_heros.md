Jardin des Héros sits in the 6th arrondissement, one of the denser and more central of Marseille's residential districts, in the zone between the Vieux-Port and the Préfecture. The name — heroes — places the garden in a long tradition of French commemorative public spaces, which in Marseille almost always refers to the resistance fighters, soldiers, and civilians who died in the two world wars and, more specifically, in the German occupation of 1942–44.

Marseille's role in the Second World War was complicated. The city was a major escape route for Jewish refugees and resistance operatives — through networks like the one that sustained the Emergency Rescue Committee — before the German takeover of the southern zone. It also suffered the destruction of its old port quarter in 1943, when the Germans dynamited much of the Panier district. Gardens named for heroes in this city carry that specific weight.

The garden itself is a neighbourhood space in a busy residential district, useful to the families and residents of the surrounding streets. The 6th arrondissement has good transport connections and is a practical base for visiting central Marseille, and a garden like this one provides a moment of pause in a quarter with relatively little open space.
