Jardin des Hévéas takes its name from the hévéa, the rubber tree (*Hevea brasiliensis*), whose latex was the source of natural rubber and one of the major commodities flowing through Marseille's port in the colonial era. The city was the dominant entry point for goods from French Indochina, Madagascar, and sub-Saharan Africa throughout the late nineteenth and twentieth centuries, and the naming of a garden after a tropical plantation tree fits the port city's relationship with the wider world.

Whether hévéas were ever grown here — they require a tropical climate — or whether the name simply commemorates the trade is an open question. The garden sits in the 6th arrondissement, a central residential district close to the Préfecture and the Castellane area. Like many gardens in this part of the city, it functions as a neighbourhood amenity: a few trees, some seating, a break in the urban fabric.

The 6th arrondissement is lively and centrally located, and the Jardin des Hévéas gives residents a small outdoor escape without the walk to the larger Parc Borély further south.
