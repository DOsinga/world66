The hévéa is the rubber tree — *Hevea brasiliensis* — and its latex built fortunes. Marseille was France's gateway for colonial goods from Indochina, Madagascar, and West Africa, and rubber was one of the big commodities moving through. Someone decided a neighbourhood garden deserved that name.

Whether it's pure commemoration or there was ever a specimen here is unclear. Rubber trees need a tropical climate, so almost certainly not. It's a small residential garden in the 6th arrondissement, close to the Préfecture — trees, benches, a break from the pavement.

What's worth knowing: the name is a small, accidental monument to the colonial trade that ran through this port for over a century.
