Jardin du Baignoir sits in the 1st arrondissement, in the area north of the Canebière where the historic Belsunce quarter — traditionally the heart of Marseille's North African immigrant community — meets the older grid of streets stretching toward the Gare Saint-Charles. This is among the densest and most intensely urban parts of the city, where public outdoor space is scarce and highly used.

The name *baignoir* — which in standard French refers to a bathtub or a low-set theatre box — may derive from a local toponym, a historic building, or a property that occupied the site. Gardens in this part of the city are small by necessity: the 1st arrondissement was built up long before the era of planned open space, and parks were retrofitted into gaps in the existing fabric.

The garden serves the immediate community of one of Marseille's most diverse and commercially active neighbourhoods. The market streets of Belsunce, the Rue d'Aix, and the approach to the station are all within a short walk, and the garden provides a brief pause in a district that is always in motion.
