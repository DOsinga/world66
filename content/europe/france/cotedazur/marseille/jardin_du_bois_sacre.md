Jardin du Bois Sacré — the garden of the sacred wood — occupies a site in the 7th arrondissement on the southern hillside above the Vieux-Port. The name invokes the *lucus* or sacred grove of classical antiquity, the woodland space set apart for religious or spiritual purposes that appears across ancient Mediterranean cultures. Whether any ancient cult occupied this particular hillside is unlikely, but the Provençal and Italian tradition of naming distinguished gardens after classical precedents was well established by the time this site was laid out.

The 7th arrondissement is one of the more exclusive residential districts of Marseille, running from the old Endoume quarter down to the calanque at Vallon des Auffes and along the southern corniche. The hillsides here, above the Pharo and the Vieux-Port entrance, are partly covered in *garrigue* scrub and partly in private and public gardens that take advantage of the terrain and the views.

The Jardin du Bois Sacré has a wilder, more naturalistic character than the formal parks of the flat city: paths through Mediterranean woodland, rocky outcrops, and views across the harbour mouth that have been drawing people to this hillside since Marseille was Massalia.
