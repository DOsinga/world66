From the 17th century onward, Marseille's prosperous families built *bastides* — country houses — in the hills outside the city. The Château Saint-Jacques in the 12th arrondissement is one of them: a formal residence, agricultural land, a chapel, ornamental gardens. The Saint-Jacques dedication probably points to a chapel on the site, or a connection to the pilgrimage routes that passed through Provence.

As the city expanded, these estates were gradually absorbed. Many became public parks. The château garden is now open to all, and it carries more character than the standard municipal square: old plantings, a proper house at the centre, some history you can see.

The 12th arrondissement is suburban and relatively well supplied with open space. This garden adds something the newer parks lack — a sense of the Provençal countryside the city grew over.
