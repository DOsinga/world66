Jardin du Château Saint-Jacques surrounds a historic *bastide* — the Provençal country house known as the Château Saint-Jacques — in the southeastern 12th arrondissement of Marseille. The 12th is one of the city's more suburban eastern districts, where the hills above the Huveaune valley contain a number of these old estate properties, many of which were absorbed into the expanding city and converted to public use during the twentieth century.

The château itself is a house of the type that Marseille's prosperous families built in the hills outside the old city from the seventeenth century onward: a substantial formal residence with agricultural land, a chapel, and gardens that combined productive and ornamental functions. Saint-Jacques — the French name for Saint James — suggests a possible religious dedication, perhaps a chapel on the site, or a connection to the pilgrimage route via Aix-en-Provence.

The garden opens to the public and provides a park of some character in a district that is largely residential. The 12th arrondissement is relatively well supplied with open space by the standards of the inner city, and the château garden adds a historical dimension to the local green network.
