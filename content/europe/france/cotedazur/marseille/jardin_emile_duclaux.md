Émile Duclaux (1840–1904) spent his career alongside Louis Pasteur, turning microbiology from a laboratory curiosity into a practical science with consequences for food safety and medicine. After Pasteur died in 1895, Duclaux ran the Institut Pasteur. He was one of the architects of the germ theory era.

He also publicly backed Dreyfus — which in 1890s France meant risking real professional damage. He did it anyway, on the grounds that evidence should matter more than authority. It cost him relationships but added something to his reputation.

The 6th arrondissement's street names follow a pattern: republican scientists and writers from the Third Republic, which treated science as a civic virtue. Duclaux fits perfectly. The garden itself is small — benches, shade, somewhere to sit between errands — but the name carries more than most.
