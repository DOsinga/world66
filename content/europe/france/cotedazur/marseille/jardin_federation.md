The garden sits in the 11th arrondissement, one of the larger administrative divisions in Marseille's northeast — an area of mid-century housing stock, commercial strips, and working-class residential fabric that developed rapidly after the Second World War. The name "Fédération" in this context most likely refers to a local association, union federation, or street-naming convention from the postwar period, though it may also echo the revolutionary calendar's spirit of civic solidarity that French municipalities have long drawn on when naming public spaces.

This part of the city lacks the tourist infrastructure of the Vieux-Port or the cultural density of the Panier, but it sustains a large and active residential population. Parks and jardins here function primarily as neighbourhood amenities — places where children play, older residents sit, and daily life takes a brief outdoor pause — rather than as destinations in their own right.

The garden is a straightforward urban green space of modest scale. It is best understood as part of the network of small municipal gardens that the city of Marseille has built and named systematically across its outer arrondissements since the 1970s, bringing green space to neighbourhoods that developed without the organic squares and cours found in the older city centre.
