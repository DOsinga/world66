The Fraissinet name belongs to one of the major merchant and shipping families of 19th-century Marseille. The Fraissinet company operated steamship lines from Marseille to Corsica and other Mediterranean ports — a business of genuine regional importance during the era when Marseille was consolidating its role as France's principal port to the Mediterranean and beyond. The family's commercial prominence gave them a presence in the civic and philanthropic life of the city, and street names, public spaces, and institutional dedications reflect that weight.

The garden sits in the vicinity of Belle de Mai, a neighbourhood whose character was shaped by the large tobacco factory that dominated it for over a century and by the working-class population that grew up around it. Belle de Mai has reinvented itself since the factory's closure — arts centres, media organisations, and creative industries have moved into the industrial buildings — but the residential fabric around the garden remains largely as it was.

The space functions as a neighbourhood park serving the immediate streets rather than drawing visitors from across the city. A garden named for an industrial shipping family in a neighbourhood built by factory workers has a particular social layering to it, though the naming itself is simply commemorative — the kind of quiet civic acknowledgement that French municipalities extend to figures who shaped the local economy in earlier generations.
