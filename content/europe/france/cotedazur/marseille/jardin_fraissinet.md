The Fraissinet company ran steamships from Marseille to Corsica and across the Mediterranean throughout the 19th century — real regional power, in an era when Marseille was cementing its position as France's principal port. The family was prominent enough to have their name attached to streets, buildings, and eventually this garden.

The garden sits near Belle de Mai, a neighbourhood shaped by the massive tobacco factory that once dominated it. The factory workers who lived around here and the shipping merchants who ran the port were different worlds. The naming is simply commemorative — French municipalities have always acknowledged the local economy's builders this way, without irony.

Belle de Mai has reinvented itself since the factory closed — arts centres, media organisations, creative industries in the old industrial buildings. The residential streets around the garden are quieter, much as they were. Small park, layered history.
