Gérald Neveu (1922–1960) was a Marseille-born poet who published during the 1940s and 1950s but remained little-known outside French poetry circles, in part because of the brevity of his life and the narrowness of mid-century literary distribution. He was associated with surrealist currents and with a generation of southern French poets who worked in the shadow of the more celebrated Paris-centred writers of the same period. His collected work was reassembled and published posthumously, and he is now considered one of the more significant Provençal-influenced voices of his generation.

The 8th arrondissement where the garden sits is one of the city's more prosperous southern districts, running from the hillier residential streets above the Prado beaches down towards the sea. It is an area of established bourgeois and upper-middle-class residential character, with a quieter ambience than the city centre. Gardens here tend to be well-maintained and serve a relatively stable population of local residents.

Naming a garden after a poet — even a minor one — reflects a municipal tradition of treating literary figures as legitimate civic subjects, alongside scientists, politicians, and military officers. For Neveu, who lived and wrote in Marseille and died young, the gesture is a local acknowledgement of a career that made little noise outside the city while it was happening.
