Gérald Neveu was born in Marseille in 1922 and died in 1960 at 38. He published poetry through the 1940s and 50s — surrealist-inflected, rooted in the south — and was almost entirely ignored by the Paris literary world while he was alive. His collected work came out posthumously. Now he's considered one of the more interesting voices of his generation.

The garden is in the 8th, one of the city's more comfortable southern districts, running down toward the Prado beaches. Well-maintained streets, established residential feel, a long way from the bohemian Marseille where Neveu actually lived.

Naming a garden after a minor poet is a nice municipal instinct. He wrote here, he died here, and the city remembered him when the wider world didn't.
