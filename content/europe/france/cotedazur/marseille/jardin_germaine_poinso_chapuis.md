Germaine Poinso-Chapuis (1901–1981) was a lawyer and Christian democrat politician from Marseille who became the first woman to hold a full ministerial post in France, serving as Minister of Public Health and Population in the government of Robert Schuman in 1947. She was a member of the Mouvement Républicain Populaire, the centrist Catholic party that was one of the dominant forces in early Fourth Republic politics, and had been active in the Resistance during the occupation. Her appointment predated both de Gaulle's constitution and the better-remembered wave of feminist political gains in France — it was achieved within the still-fragile framework of a country that had only granted women the vote in 1944.

After her ministerial year, Poinso-Chapuis returned to Marseille and continued in regional and municipal politics, as well as her legal career. She remained a recognisable figure in the city's civic life through the 1950s and 1960s. A garden named after her is a fitting form of recognition for someone whose major achievement was political and who remained deeply connected to the city where she was born and practiced.

The coordinates place this garden in the central-southern arc of Marseille, in a neighbourhood close to the transition between the historic centre and the more residential 6th arrondissement. The area is well-served by public transport and sees a mix of daily commuters and residents, making a named garden here a genuinely used public space rather than a remote commemoration.
