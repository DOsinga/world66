The Guigou name appears in Marseille's 19th-century administrative and commercial records — it is a Provençal family name found in the city's bourgeois and professional classes during the period when the city was expanding rapidly under the Second Empire and early Third Republic. Whether the garden commemorates a specific individual or simply carries the name of a local family with a presence in the neighbourhood's history is not firmly documented in public sources.

The garden sits in the northern arrondissements of Marseille, in an area that developed substantially during the 19th and early 20th centuries as the city's port-related industries and population expanded northward from the old centre. This part of the city has a layered urban character — older residential streets from the Belle Époque era alongside later housing stock — and a strongly working-class tradition.

As a named public green space, the Jardin Guigou functions primarily as a local park for the surrounding streets. The northern arrondissements are more densely populated and have less green space per capita than the southern coastal districts, which makes even modest gardens significant as daily amenities for residents.
