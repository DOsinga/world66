Guigou is a Provençal family name that turns up in Marseille's 19th-century commercial and civic records — the kind of bourgeois family that mattered locally during the city's rapid expansion under the Second Empire. Whether the garden commemorates a specific person or just the name itself isn't clearly documented.

The northern arrondissements developed fast in the 19th and early 20th centuries, driven by port industry and population growth pushing north from the old centre. The streets here have a layered quality — Belle Époque residential blocks alongside later construction, a working-class tradition running through all of it.

The north has less green space per capita than the southern coastal districts. That makes even a modest neighbourhood garden like this one a real daily asset for the people living around it.
