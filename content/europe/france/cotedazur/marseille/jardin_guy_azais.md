Guy Azaïs is not a nationally documented figure; the garden named after him belongs to the category of Marseille public spaces that commemorate local residents — neighbourhood figures, community organisers, or minor municipal officials whose names are preserved in the city's geography without broader historical record. This is a consistent municipal practice across French cities: the texture of public space carries the names of people who mattered locally but not nationally.

The 9th arrondissement where the garden sits is Marseille's eastern district, abutting the Calanques National Park to the south and extending through a varied landscape of residential suburbs, older village cores like Les Goudes and La Madrague, and the hillier terrain that separates the urban fabric from the coastal wilderness. It is a mixed district — some areas prosperous and residential, others more modest — with a character distinct from both the city centre and the heavily industrial northern port.

The garden serves the surrounding residential streets as a local amenity. The proximity to the Calanques means that this part of Marseille has more access to natural outdoor space than most of the city, which gives smaller neighbourhood gardens a somewhat different role here — they are less the only green option and more one element in a richer landscape.
