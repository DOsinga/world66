Guy Azaïs didn't make the history books. His garden is one of dozens across Marseille named for neighbourhood figures — community organisers, local officials, people who mattered to their block without mattering to anyone else. The city preserves these names in its street geography. It's a decent instinct.

The 9th arrondissement is Marseille's eastern district, and this part of it sits close to where the city starts giving way to the Calanques. The landscape shifts noticeably — rockier, drier, limestone massif pressing against the residential streets. It's distinct from both the city centre and the heavily industrial north.

With the national park nearby, small neighbourhood gardens here play a different role than they do in denser parts of the city. Less the only green space, more one piece of a richer outdoor picture.
