Chabaud is a Provençal family name common in the Marseille region; Joseph Chabaud is not a nationally recorded figure, and the garden bearing his name belongs to the category of local commemorations that French municipalities use to preserve the names of residents who contributed to neighbourhood or civic life without entering wider historical documentation. This naming practice means that the city's smaller green spaces function collectively as a distributed record of local memory.

The coordinates place this garden in the far south of Marseille, in one of the most dramatic settings available to a city park — close to the point where the urban fabric of the 9th arrondissement meets the limestone massif and the beginning of the Calanques. The landscape here is markedly different from the city centre: drier, rockier, with scrubland and pine forest pressing against the residential streets.

The garden serves the local residential community in an area where the Calanques National Park provides substantial outdoor space but is not immediately walkable from every doorstep. A small local garden here has a particular character — quieter and more enclosed than the open landscape nearby, functioning as a genuine neighbourhood space rather than a scenic destination.
