Jean-Andoche Junot (1771–1813) was one of Napoleon's earliest companions and most loyal generals, a figure more notable for his personal relationship with Bonaparte than for any single decisive campaign. He served in Egypt, Portugal — where he commanded the French invasion of 1807 and earned the title Duke of Abrantes — and later in Spain and Russia. His military career was marred by the defeat at Vimeiro in 1808, the first significant British land victory of the Peninsular War, though Napoleon consistently protected him from the consequences. He died in 1813, having shown signs of mental breakdown in the final years of his life.

Gardens, streets, and public spaces named after Napoleonic generals are scattered across French cities as a legacy of the Second Empire's programme of civic commemoration, which rehabilitated the military figures of the first Napoleonic period as part of Napoleon III's political use of his uncle's legacy. Junot appears frequently in this geography, his name attached to streets in cities across France that had no particular connection to his biography.

The garden in the 4th arrondissement is a neighbourhood space in the northern city, serving a residential quarter removed from the tourist circuits of central Marseille. The Napoleonic name sits in the streetscape as an unremarkable administrative designation — one of dozens of such commemorations that give French urban geography its particular historical texture.
