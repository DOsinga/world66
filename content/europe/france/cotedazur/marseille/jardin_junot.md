Jean-Andoche Junot was one of Bonaparte's earliest companions — they went back to the Egyptian campaign — and one of his most loyal generals. In 1807 he led the French invasion of Portugal and was made Duke of Abrantes. Then in 1808 the British beat him at Vimeiro, the first significant land victory of the Peninsular War. Napoleon kept protecting him anyway. He died in 1813, his final years marked by mental breakdown.

His name is scattered across French cities on streets and public spaces — a legacy of Napoleon III's programme of rehabilitating the first Empire's military figures as part of his own political project. Junot appears all over France in places that had nothing to do with him personally.

The garden in the 4th arrondissement is a neighbourhood space in the northern residential city, far from the tourist circuits. The Napoleonic name sits in the streetscape without anyone much noticing it. Most people walking past probably couldn't tell you who he was.
