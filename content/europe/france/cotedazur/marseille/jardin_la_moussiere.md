La Moussière is a toponymic name rather than a personal one — it derives from "mousse" (moss) and refers to a mossy or damp terrain feature, the kind of descriptive property name common in Provençal land registers. In the pre-urban landscape of Marseille's northern and eastern outskirts, bastides and agricultural properties frequently carried such names, and they often survive into the modern street geography even when the original property has long since been built over.

The garden sits in the 14th arrondissement, Marseille's far northeast — one of the city's least-visited arrondissements for outsiders, though home to large residential populations. The area developed primarily in the postwar decades as the city expanded to accommodate population growth, and the urban landscape here is characterised by housing estates, commercial zones, and the gradual thinning of the urban fabric as it approaches the hills and the Étoile massif to the north.

At this distance from the city centre, a named public park serves a genuine daily function for the surrounding neighbourhoods, which have less access to the waterfront and parkland that characterise the southern arrondissements. The name La Moussière preserves a fragment of the pre-suburban landscape in the street geography, even as the terrain it once described is now fully built over.
