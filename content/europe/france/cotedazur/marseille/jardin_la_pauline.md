La Pauline is a place name rather than a personal name in this context — or rather, it is the kind of feminised property name (likely from "paulin" or "Paul," carried through a bastide or landholding) that is common in Provençal topography. The southern and eastern outskirts of Marseille were historically dotted with private bastides — the country houses of the Marseille bourgeoisie — each with its own name, many of which have been absorbed into the urban geography as the city expanded.

The 9th arrondissement where this garden sits is the southernmost of Marseille's administrative divisions, covering a wide arc that includes both dense residential suburbs and the semi-rural terrain approaching the Calanques. La Pauline as a locality name suggests an area that was once at the edge of the city or beyond it — a named place before it became a named garden.

The garden itself is a neighbourhood amenity for the surrounding residential streets, which represent the kind of postwar and later suburban development that brought large populations into what had previously been the agricultural and bastide belt of the city. Like other named small parks in the outer arrondissements, it brings structured green space into a suburban landscape where the open countryside of the Calanques is nearby but the immediate street fabric is fully urban.
