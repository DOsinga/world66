La Pauline is a place name, not a person — or rather it started as the kind of feminised name attached to a bastide, the country houses that the Marseille bourgeoisie built on the eastern outskirts of the city. Each one had a name. When the city expanded and swallowed the bastide belt, many of those names survived in the street geography, carried forward without their original buildings.

The 9th arrondissement covers a wide arc from dense suburban streets down to the edge of the Calanques. This part of it was agricultural territory before the postwar building push brought large populations into what had been the bastide belt — private estates and farmland turned into residential blocks in two or three decades.

The garden is a neighbourhood amenity for the surrounding streets. The Calanques are close, but the immediate environment is fully suburban. La Pauline the locality became La Pauline the park — the oldest layer of the name still showing through.
