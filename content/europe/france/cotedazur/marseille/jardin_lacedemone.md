Marseille was founded around 600 BCE by Greek colonists from Phocaea. The Romans called it Massalia. So when 19th-century French city planners needed a name for a southern garden, reaching back to classical Greece wasn't pretension — it was local history.

Lacédémone is the French form of Lacedaemon, the Greek name for Sparta's territory. The connection to Sparta specifically was about prestige: French republicanism loved classical references, and the ancient world gave civic spaces a weight that a local official's name couldn't.

The garden sits in the hilly 7th arrondissement above the Vieux-Port, in a neighbourhood of irregular streets and stone buildings that grew without a plan. That organic texture makes the classical name feel at home — Marseille has always worn its deep past lightly.
