Lacédémone is the French rendering of Lacedaemon, the classical Greek name for the territory of Sparta and its surrounding region in the southern Peloponnese. Naming a Marseille garden after a Greek place reflects the city's founding mythology: Marseille was established around 600 BCE by Greek colonists from Phocaea, a city on the Ionian coast of Asia Minor, and the Massalia they founded was one of the most prosperous Greek colonies in the western Mediterranean. The city has maintained a continuous self-identification with its Greek origins — the Jardin des Vestiges nearby preserves the actual archaeological remains of the original Greek settlement.

Sparta and Lacedaemon appear in this naming because of the broader prestige of classical Greek geography in French civic culture, particularly in the 19th-century when French republicanism drew heavily on classical references for its public symbolism. A garden called Lacédémone is part of that same tradition — a reach toward the classical world as a source of civic legitimacy and cultural depth.

The garden sits in the southern 7th arrondissement, a neighbourhood of hillside streets above the Vieux-Port with views towards the sea. The area is residential and characterful, retaining the texture of older Marseille — irregular street patterns, stone buildings, the sense of a city that grew organically before regularisation — and a small garden with an ancient Greek name fits naturally into this historically layered landscape.
