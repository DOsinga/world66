Lamy is a family name with a modest presence in French civic and administrative history; the most notable bearer at the national level was Frank Lamy, a republican politician of the early Third Republic, though the garden may as easily commemorate a local Marseille figure of the same surname. The 6th arrondissement's gardens frequently carry the names of 19th-century professional and civic figures — lawyers, doctors, administrators — whose contribution to municipal life warranted a small public acknowledgement.

The 6th arrondissement where this garden sits is one of Marseille's central bourgeois districts, located in the zone between the Vieux-Port and the northern boulevards, densely residential and relatively prosperous. The neighbourhood has the character of established urban France — wide pavements, apartment buildings from the Third Republic era, a settled civic atmosphere. It is the kind of district where named gardens function not as distant green infrastructure but as genuine daily spaces for a dense residential population.

The garden is modest in scale, characteristic of the many small square jardins that punctuate the 6th's residential fabric. Like the best of these spaces, it provides shade, seating, and a brief remove from the surrounding streets — performing the simple but essential function of a neighbourhood green space in a densely built city.
