The 6th arrondissement is old Marseille bourgeois: wide pavements, Third Republic apartment buildings, the zone between the Vieux-Port and the northern boulevards. Dense, residential, prosperous enough to have decent boulangeries on every corner.

Jardin Lamy is the kind of small square that this type of neighbourhood produces — named after a local figure (possibly a lawyer, possibly an administrator, possibly Frank Lamy the Third Republic politician, the records are quiet on it), maintained by the parks service, genuinely used by people who live nearby.

The point is not the name. The point is shade and a bench in a neighbourhood where the streets are otherwise continuous stone and asphalt. It does its job without announcing itself.
