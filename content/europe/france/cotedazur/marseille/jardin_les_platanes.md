Plane trees are the defining shade tree of the south of France. They line every cours in Aix, shelter the boules players in every village square, and turn a patch of gravel into somewhere you actually want to sit. Municipal planners in the 19th century planted them everywhere because they grow fast, spread wide, and handle the heat without complaint.

A garden named simply "Les Platanes" is making a direct statement about what it offers. No civic hero, no historical reference — just: come here, there is shade.

The garden sits in the northern inner arrondissements, close to the Panier and the port. This part of the city has a complicated 20th century — parts were demolished under the occupation and rebuilt quickly in the postwar decades. The platanes, if they're old enough, saw some of that.
