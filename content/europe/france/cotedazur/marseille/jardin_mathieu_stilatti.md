Stilatti is an Italian-origin surname, and its presence in Marseille's public geography reflects the large and historically important Italian immigrant community that settled in the city across the 19th and early 20th centuries. At the peak of Italian immigration to France, Marseille had one of the largest Italian-born populations of any city outside Italy — Italians worked in the port, in construction, in the fishing industry, and across the commercial economy. Their descendants are now deeply embedded in Marseille's social and civic fabric, and surnames of Italian origin appear regularly in the city's commemorative geography.

Mathieu Stilatti is not a nationally documented figure, but a garden bearing his name in the 4th arrondissement suggests someone who was recognised at the neighbourhood or municipal level — a community figure, a local councillor, or a resident whose contribution to the immediate neighbourhood warranted commemoration. This is the granular level at which French civic memory operates: not every name on a park fence needs to belong to a historical figure if it belonged to someone the neighbourhood remembers.

The 4th arrondissement location situates this garden in the northern inner city, close to where the old port district gives way to denser residential streets. It is a neighbourhood with a working-class and immigrant heritage — the Italian connection in the name fits naturally into the social history of this part of Marseille.
