The Mattei name is best known in the context of Cap Corse Mattei, the Corsican aperitif brand founded by Louis-Napoléon Mattei in the late 19th century. Mattei was a Corsican-born entrepreneur who settled in Marseille and built a business importing and commercialising the bitter herbal aperitif wines of Cap Corse, the narrow northern peninsula of Corsica. The brand became one of the commercially successful regional drinks of the Mediterranean coast and remains in production; the iconic Belle Époque advertising poster by Leonetto Cappiello is one of the best-known images associated with Marseille's café culture.

Whether the garden commemorates a member of this family specifically or another bearer of the same Corsican-Italian surname is not documented in public sources. Corsicans have long had a significant presence in Marseille — the city is effectively the gateway between mainland France and the island, and Corsican families have been integrated into its commercial and civic life for generations. The Mattei surname in Marseille's street geography is consistent with that presence.

The garden sits in the 4th arrondissement, in the northern inner city, serving the residential streets of a neighbourhood with a long and mixed working-class and immigrant heritage. It is a modest local space, maintained as part of the city's distributed network of neighbourhood parks.
