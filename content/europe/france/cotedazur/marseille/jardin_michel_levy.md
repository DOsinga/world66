Lévy is one of the most common Jewish surnames in France. Michel Lévy as a specific figure could be the famous 19th-century Parisian publisher who printed Flaubert and Dumas fils, or a local Marseille figure, or someone else entirely — the public record doesn't settle it.

What is clear is the context. Marseille has a long Jewish history, deepened substantially after 1962 when Algerian independence brought large numbers of Sephardic Jewish families to the city. Today Marseille has one of the largest Jewish communities in France. Names from that community appear in the civic geography in the same way that Italian, Armenian, and Corsican surnames do — because these communities built the city.

The garden sits in the transition zone between the commercial centre and the residential 6th arrondissement. It's dense daily-life territory — workers, residents, the ordinary rhythm of a busy urban neighbourhood.
