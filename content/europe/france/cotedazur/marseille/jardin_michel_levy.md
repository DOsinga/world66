Lévy is one of the most common Jewish surnames in France, and Michel Lévy is a name that appears in several contexts in French history — most notably as the 19th-century Parisian publisher Michel Lévy Frères, who published Flaubert, Dumas fils, and other major writers of the Second Empire. Whether this garden commemorates that publisher, a Marseille-based member of the same family, or an entirely different local figure named Michel Lévy is not established in public sources.

What is clear is that the garden's naming reflects Marseille's long Jewish history. The city had a significant Jewish population, augmented by successive waves of migration — from North Africa in particular, especially after Algerian independence in 1962, which brought large numbers of Sephardic Jews to Marseille. The city today has one of the largest Jewish communities in France, and names from that community appear in the civic geography in the same way that Italian, Armenian, Corsican, and other heritage surnames do.

The coordinates place this garden in the central southern arc of the city, in the transition zone between the commercial centre and the residential 6th arrondissement. It is a neighbourhood with a dense daily population of residents and workers, and the garden functions as a local green space in what is otherwise tightly built urban fabric.
