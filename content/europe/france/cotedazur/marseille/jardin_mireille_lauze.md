Mireille is about as Provençal as a name gets. It comes from Frédéric Mistral's 1859 epic poem — the work that anchored the Félibrige movement's revival of Occitan language and culture — and it stuck. Pairing it with a local surname like Lauze for a garden in the southern 9th arrondissement is almost too on-brand for Marseille.

Mireille Lauze herself isn't in the national record. She was someone the neighbourhood knew, which is the category most small garden names fall into.

The 9th arrondissement is large and southern, covering the residential suburbs east of the city centre and the approach to the Calanques. The area around this garden is postwar residential — apartment buildings on terrain that used to be countryside. The Calanques are close enough to see from higher ground, but you don't walk there from every doorstep. The garden is the daily green space.
