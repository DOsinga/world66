Mireille Lauze is not a nationally documented figure; the garden named after her is one of several in Marseille's outer arrondissements that carry the names of women from the city's civic and community life. The name Mireille itself is deeply Provençal — it is the name of the heroine of Frédéric Mistral's 1859 epic poem that anchored the Félibrige movement's literary revival of Occitan language and culture. A garden whose name pairs a quintessentially Provençal first name with a local surname sits comfortably in Marseille's cultural landscape.

The 9th arrondissement where this garden sits is Marseille's large southern district, covering the residential suburbs east of the city centre and the area approaching the Calanques. The neighbourhood around the garden is a postwar residential zone — streets of apartment buildings and houses built as the city expanded into terrain that had previously been countryside or scattered bastides.

The garden is a local amenity, providing green space for the surrounding residential streets in a district where the Calanques National Park forms a dramatic backdrop but is not accessible on foot from every doorstep. It serves the community of the immediate neighbourhood as a daily outdoor space rather than a destination.
