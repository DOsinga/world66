Missak Manouchian survived the 1915 Armenian genocide as a child and arrived in France with nothing. He became a poet, a communist, and one of the most effective resistance organisers in occupied Paris — his network of foreign-born fighters carried out dozens of attacks on the German occupation. When the Nazis arrested him in 1943, they plastered Paris with a propaganda poster designed to discredit the resistance by emphasising how foreign and Jewish his fighters were. It became known as the "Affiche Rouge." It backfired spectacularly, and Manouchian was shot at Mont Valérien on 21 February 1944 alongside twenty-one of his fighters.

He was panthéonised in 2024, eighty years later. This garden near the Vieux-Port was named before that — it reflects a long-standing campaign by the French-Armenian community who never forgot him.

The neighbourhood here was shaped by Armenians who arrived after 1915, disembarking at Marseille and never leaving. Their descendants are now four or five generations deep. The street names and community institutions are a quiet record of that history. This garden is part of it.
