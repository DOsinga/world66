Mulberry trees were planted across Provence to feed silkworms. The silk industry was a serious part of the regional economy in the 18th and 19th centuries, and the mûriers — mulberry groves — covered land that is now deep inside Marseille's urban fabric. The industry declined before the modern city expanded to cover it, but the names stuck.

The garden is in the far northwest, in the 15th or 16th arrondissement — the coastal northern districts bordering the industrial port zone. This is the part of the city built fast and dense from the 1950s through the 1980s to house port workers and subsequent generations. The grands ensembles here — La Savine, Les Lauriers, the northern coastal strip — were not built with green space as a priority.

A "jardin municipal d'arrondissement" signals that this is a formal piece of civic infrastructure: green space distributed to the arrondissement's population by municipal decision. The mulberry name is the only thing connecting it to the landscape that stood here before.
