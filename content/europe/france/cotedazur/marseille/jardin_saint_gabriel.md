Up here in the northern 4th, the streets loosen. The apartment blocks space out, the traffic thins, and the Chartreux hill starts to make itself felt. Jardin Saint-Gabriel marks where the dense city core gives way to something slightly different — still residential, but at a different pitch.

The neighbourhood is named for a religious community that once operated in this transitional zone between the Canebière corridor and the industrial north. The garden does what neighbourhood gardens do: shade, benches, somewhere for kids to run without stepping into traffic.

For the visitor it's mostly useful as an orientation point. The bus routes nearby connect to Réformés-Canebière metro. The streets around it have corner shops and cafés that haven't been restyled for anyone but locals.
