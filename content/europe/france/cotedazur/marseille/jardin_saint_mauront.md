Saint-Mauront is wedged between the Saint-Charles rail yards and the Arenc industrial canal. One of the oldest continuously settled quarters in Marseille's northern arrondissements, it's been working-class since before that term existed. Today the neighbourhood is largely North African and Comorian — its markets, mosques, and street life reflect that without apology.

In a district where apartments are small and streets are narrow, the Jardin Saint-Mauront punches above its size. It's one of the few open spaces where children can actually play and adults can sit somewhere that isn't a pavement. On warm evenings — so, eight months of the year — the park fills up properly.

Take the metro from Vieux-Port and spend an hour here. Saint-Mauront makes no concessions to spectacle. That's exactly why it tells you something true about Marseille.
