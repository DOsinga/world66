Sarah Halimi was a retired doctor from Marseille's Jewish community, killed in her Paris apartment in April 2017 in an antisemitic attack. What followed was years of legal argument: the perpetrator had smoked cannabis before the attack, entered a psychotic state, and was ruled criminally irresponsible. He was never tried for murder. The ruling triggered protests across France and internationally — many demonstrators came from Marseille, which has one of France's largest Jewish communities.

The garden is in the eastern 13th arrondissement near Malpassé, a residential area with a significant Jewish population. The city named this public space for her as an act of municipal memory — a statement that the case would not simply be absorbed and forgotten.

The garden itself is small and unremarkable. A bench, some shade, some planting. The name is what matters here.
