Sarah Halimi was a retired physician and member of Marseille's Jewish community who was killed in her Paris apartment in April 2017 in a violent antisemitic attack. The case became one of the most contested legal proceedings in contemporary France: the perpetrator was found to have been in a self-induced psychotic state from cannabis and was ruled criminally irresponsible, meaning he could not be tried for murder. The acquittal provoked widespread protests in France and internationally, with demonstrators — including many from Marseille's large Jewish community — arguing that the ruling left the crime without justice.

The garden bearing Sarah Halimi's name is located in the eastern 13th arrondissement, in the residential area near Malpassé. The naming is an act of municipal memory: a public space in a neighbourhood with a significant Jewish population, dedicated to a woman whose death and the legal proceedings that followed became a reference point in French debates about antisemitism, justice, and the limits of criminal culpability.

The garden itself is a neighbourhood green space with the standard amenities — benches, planting, shade. Its significance is carried by its name rather than its size.
