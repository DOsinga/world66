The Jardin Sévigné is named, in the French tradition of honouring literary figures with public spaces, for Marie de Rabutin-Chantal, Marquise de Sévigné (1626–1696), the celebrated letter-writer whose correspondence is considered a monument of 17th-century French prose. Sévigné spent time in Provence and is one of the canonical writers whose name appears on schools, streets, and gardens across southern France.

The garden sits in Bonneveine, the residential neighbourhood of the southern 8th arrondissement between the Prado roundabout and the coastal road. Bonneveine is primarily a neighbourhood of apartment blocks and villas inhabited by middle-class Marseillais — quieter than the Prado axis and more residential in character. The garden provides the neighbourhood with a green anchor and a meeting point for families from the surrounding streets.

Bonneveine is within comfortable reach of the Prado beaches, which makes it a practical base and a pleasant area to walk in the early morning or evening when the beach crowds have thinned. The Jardin Sévigné, like the neighbourhood it serves, rewards an unhurried approach.
