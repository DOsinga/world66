Marie de Rabutin-Chantal, Marquise de Sévigné, never actually lived in Marseille — but her correspondence is so embedded in the French literary canon that her name appears on schools, streets, and gardens across the south. The 17th-century letter-writer as civic infrastructure. Very French.

The garden is in Bonneveine, the quieter residential quarter of the southern 8th arrondissement, tucked between the Prado roundabout and the coastal road. This is apartment blocks and villas territory — middle-class Marseillais who want to live near the sea without paying Prado-frontage prices.

The Prado beaches are a short walk. Come early morning or after six in the evening and the crowds have moved on. The Jardin Sévigné is where Bonneveine locals recover from the beach, or avoid it entirely.
