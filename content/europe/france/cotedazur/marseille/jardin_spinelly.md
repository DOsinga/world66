Henri Spinelly was born in Marseille in 1893 and became one of the leading cabaret and music-hall performers of early 20th-century France, appearing on stages in Paris and throughout the country before his death in 1958. His is the kind of name that Marseille attaches to neighbourhood gardens and secondary streets — a local success story honoured at a scale proportionate to neighbourhood pride rather than national fame.

The Jardin Spinelly occupies a small plot in the Belle de Mai district, the working-class quarter of the 3rd arrondissement that in recent decades has been partially transformed by the installation of cultural institutions — notably the Friche la Belle de Mai, a major arts complex housed in a former tobacco factory a short walk away. The garden itself predates or exists alongside that transformation, serving the older residential community of the neighbourhood.

Belle de Mai is one of Marseille's most interesting quarters for a traveller willing to leave the waterfront. The Friche brings artists, concerts, and a rooftop with views over the city; the surrounding streets retain the character of a working neighbourhood where the renovation has been gradual rather than wholesale. The Jardin Spinelly sits squarely in that quieter part.
