Henri Spinelly was born in Marseille in 1893 and spent the first half of the 20th century on cabaret and music-hall stages in Paris. He was a genuine success — the kind of local boy who left and conquered, then got a garden named after him back home. He died in 1958.

The garden sits in Belle de Mai, the working-class quarter of the 3rd arrondissement that's been quietly transforming for two decades. The Friche la Belle de Mai — a former tobacco factory turned major arts complex — is a short walk away. It brings a rooftop bar, concerts, and galleries into a neighbourhood that still mostly looks like what it always was.

The Jardin Spinelly belongs to the older layer. The residential community around it has been here longer than the arts crowd. Both coexist without much friction, which is its own small miracle.
