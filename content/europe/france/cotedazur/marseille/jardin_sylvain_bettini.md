Sylvain Bettini is not a name that appears in the major reference works on French history, which suggests that the Jardin Sylvain Bettini commemorates a figure of local significance — a municipal councillor, a resistance member, a community organiser — whose contribution was important enough to the neighbourhood of Saint-André to earn a place-name rather than a plaque.

Saint-André is a residential district in the 15th arrondissement, in the far northwest of Marseille proper, close to the commune boundaries. This is peripheral Marseille: lower density than the central arrondissements, with a mix of housing estates and older residential streets, well served by bus lines connecting to the Gèze metro station. The garden serves this community as a local green space with the typical provision of seating and shade.

The 15th arrondissement is rarely visited by tourists, which is in itself an argument for visiting it. The quarter around Saint-André gives a clear picture of how most Marseillais actually live — far from the Vieux-Port, dependent on neighbourhood shops and public transport, proud of their corner of a city that has never been simple or singular.
