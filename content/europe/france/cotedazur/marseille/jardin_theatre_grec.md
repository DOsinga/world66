Marseille started as a Greek colony. Around 600 BCE, settlers from Phocaea on the Aegean coast landed here and called the place Massalia. They traded up the Rhône, along the Iberian coast, and built one of the most prosperous Greek outposts in the western Mediterranean. That origin story is not ancient history to Marseille — it's still part of the city's active identity.

So building a Greek-style amphitheatre here closes a particular loop. Stone tiers rising on a hillside, a circular performance space below — the exact form invented for Mediterranean summer evenings. Back in the climate it was designed for, used for the same purpose it always served.

The venue runs in summer, when Marseille's long dry season makes outdoor performance dependable. The repertoire tends toward work that can hold an outdoor audience — classical material, large casts. The garden setting makes it feel like a civic occasion rather than a night at the theatre.
