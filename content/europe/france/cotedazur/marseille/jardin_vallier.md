The Palais Longchamp was built in the 1860s to celebrate the arrival of the Canal de la Durance — Marseille finally getting a reliable water supply — and they built it extravagantly: fountains, colonnades, twin museum wings. The bourgeoisie took notice and moved in around it. The streets between Longchamp and the Cinq-Avenues quarter still have that Third Republic feel, wide and tree-lined in a way rare for a city that mostly escaped Haussmann.

The Jardin Vallier sits in this better-maintained stretch of the 4th arrondissement. The surrounding architecture sets the tone — solid, well-kept apartment buildings, a neighbourhood that's never really had to reinvent itself.

The name is Provençal, probably commemorating a local family, possibly of some civic standing. The specific dedication isn't well documented. In this city, that's not unusual — the naming layers go deep.
