The Palais Longchamp, built between 1862 and 1869 to celebrate the arrival of the Canal de la Durance into Marseille, is one of the grandest pieces of 19th-century civic architecture in the south of France. Its fountains, colonnades, and twin museum wings attracted bourgeois settlement in the surrounding streets, and the 4th arrondissement around Longchamp remains one of Marseille's more genteel residential quarters.

The Jardin Vallier sits in this context: a neighbourhood garden that derives some of its character from the quality of the surrounding architecture. The streets near Longchamp — wide, tree-lined, with well-maintained apartment buildings from the Third Republic period — give the area a Haussmannian feel unusual in a city that generally escaped the kind of systematic reconstruction Paris underwent. The garden provides shade and benches in a part of the 4th arrondissement where the residential population is relatively settled and prosperous.

Vallier is a Provençal name with roots in the region's history, though without further documentation it is difficult to identify precisely which Vallier the garden commemorates. The name itself is a reminder that Marseille draws its civic vocabulary from layers of Provençal, French, Italian, and Mediterranean naming traditions that predated French standardisation.
