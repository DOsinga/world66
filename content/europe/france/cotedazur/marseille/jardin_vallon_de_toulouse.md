A vallon in Provençal topography is one of those shallow limestone gullies that the terrain carves between ridges. This one, in the far southeastern 8th arrondissement, funnels down toward the coast at Pointe-Rouge. The garden occupies part of that natural depression.

Pointe-Rouge is one of the most popular local beaches, a marina, and a departure point for boat trips into the calanques. The residential streets behind it are solidly middle-class and sea-oriented — apartments here are sought after for the access to water and the calanque footpaths more than anything else.

Practically useful: the vallon's natural shape provides shelter from the Mistral, which can make exposed positions unpleasant even in warm months. If you're walking from the bus terminus at Pointe-Rouge toward the coastal path, this is a reasonable place to stop and reorganise before the climb.
