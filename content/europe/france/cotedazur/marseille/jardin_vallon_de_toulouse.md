The Vallon de Toulouse is a small valley — a vallon in the Provençal topographical vocabulary that names the shallow dry gullies carved into Marseille's limestone terrain — in the far southeastern 8th arrondissement. The garden occupies part of this natural depression, where the terrain funnels between residential blocks toward the coastline.

This corner of Marseille is dominated by the Pointe-Rouge marina and beach, one of the most popular local bathing spots and a departure point for boat trips into the calanques. The residential streets inland from Pointe-Rouge are solidly middle-class and oriented toward the sea: many apartments here are sought after precisely for their proximity to the water and to the calanque access paths. The Jardin Vallon de Toulouse fits into that landscape as a green transition between street and shore.

The garden is a practical stop for anyone walking from the bus terminus at Pointe-Rouge toward the coastal path. The natural topography of the vallon provides shelter from the Mistral wind, which can make exposed coastal positions uncomfortable even in warm months, and the shade trees make it a reasonable resting point in midsummer heat.
