The name Velten likely recalls a family or individual of German or Alsatian origin who settled in Marseille during the 19th century, when the city attracted substantial immigration from across France and from neighbouring Italy, Spain, and Central Europe. The Velten family were prominent in Marseille's merchant community, and a garden bearing the name suggests civic recognition of some standing, though the specific dedication has not been publicly documented in detail.

The Jardin Velten occupies a position in the northern reaches of the 4th arrondissement, in the residential area north of the Palais Longchamp and east of the Chartreux hill. This part of the 4th sits between the major infrastructural interruption of the Saint-Charles railway station to the southwest and the quieter residential blocks climbing toward the Chartreux. It is a mid-century neighbourhood of apartment buildings and local commerce, served by the metro at Réformés-Canebière.

The garden provides the standard neighbourhood amenities and functions as a local meeting point. For a traveller arriving at or departing from Saint-Charles, the Jardin Velten is a short walk away and offers a quieter alternative to the station forecourt if time needs to be filled before a train.
