Jean Bouin grew up in working-class Marseille and became the best distance runner in France. At the 1912 Stockholm Olympics he ran the 5,000 metres in 14:36.7 — a world record. Finland's Hannes Kolehmainen beat him by less than a second. The silver medal and the manner of losing it made him more famous than a gold might have.

Two years later he was dead. Mobilised in August 1914, killed near Crouy-sur-Ourcq on 29 September. He was 26. His death hit French sporting culture hard — here was a man whose physical gifts had seemed almost superhuman, gone in the first weeks of the Western Front alongside tens of thousands of others nobody would remember.

His name went onto stadiums in Marseille and Barcelona. This monument near the Stade Vélodrome keeps him in the city that made him. The track and the battlefield — France has always understood them as connected.
