This khachkar went up in 2015, a century after the genocide that killed somewhere between six hundred thousand and one and a half million Armenians. France recognised the genocide by parliamentary resolution in 2001 — early, when many Western governments still hadn't. The centenary commemorations here were among the most extensive anywhere in the world.

The stone itself is the Armenian tradition in concentrated form: every khachkar is unique, the cross surrounded by interlocking geometric patterns cut to a standard that medieval craftsmen turned into an art form. They've been produced continuously since the ninth century. You find them wherever Armenians have settled, which now means every continent.

Marseille's Armenian community traces its roots directly to 1915. Survivors who escaped the deportations reached the Mediterranean coast by various routes; many passed through here, some staying, some continuing north. A century later their descendants number in the tens of thousands. The memorial near the Vieux-Port makes that presence impossible to ignore.
