L'Estaque was a fishing village before Marseille's industrial sprawl swallowed it. Cézanne painted here repeatedly over two decades — the viaduct arches, factory chimneys, the bay flattened into geometric planes. When Braque arrived to paint the same subjects after seeing those canvases, the results were so fragmented that Matisse called them "little cubes." The term stuck. Cubism was invented, more or less, in this working-class neighbourhood on Marseille's northern edge.

The Alhambra building has its own story. Its Moorish-influenced Art Deco facade dates from the 1930s — one of the best-preserved cinema interiors of that era anywhere in the city. It programmes mostly French-language films and the audience is local. This place serves L'Estaque and the surrounding quartiers, not visitors chasing art history.

Take the Côte Bleue regional train from central Marseille — fifteen minutes. The Chemin de l'Estaque still passes most of what Cézanne painted: the viaduct, the bay, the chimneys. Recognisable, over a century later.
