L'Estaque was a fishing village before Marseille's industrial expansion absorbed it into the city's northern edge. Cézanne painted there repeatedly over two decades, drawn by the geometric planes of the viaduct, factory chimneys, and the bay. When Braque arrived to paint the same subjects after encountering Cézanne's canvases, the result was a set of paintings Matisse described as "little cubes" — coining the term. The neighbourhood's industrial character persists: it is still emphatically working-class, distinct from central Marseille.

The Alhambra building, with its Moorish-influenced Art Deco facade, dates from the 1930s and is one of the best-preserved examples of that era's cinema architecture in the city. It programmes mostly French-language cinema with occasional international releases. The audience is local — this cinema serves L'Estaque and the surrounding quartiers, not visitors making a day trip to trace Cézanne's views.

Getting here from central Marseille by the Côte Bleue regional train takes fifteen minutes. Come for the cinema, stay to walk the Chemin de l'Estaque past the sites Cézanne documented — the viaduct, the bay, the factory chimneys — most of which still exist in recognisable form.
