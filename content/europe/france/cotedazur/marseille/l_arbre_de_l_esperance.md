During the Revolution, municipalities across France planted *arbres de la Liberté* — liberty trees, young oaks or poplars — in public squares as living monuments to democratic renewal. *L'Arbre de l'Espérance* continues that tradition in metal and stone, permanently fixing the gesture into the streetscape near Cours Julien.

The shift from liberty to hope is deliberate. Liberty trees were declarative — planting one was a political statement. A tree of hope is more searching. Hope implies something still to come, a condition not yet achieved. In a city where different quartiers live very different versions of opportunity, that openness reads as honest.

The branching structure changes character across the day depending on the light — brilliant in summer, softer in autumn. It's the kind of piece worth slowing down for.
