*L'Aventure* occupies a spot in the quieter northeastern reaches of Marseille, where the city loosens into a patchwork of residential streets, light industry, and the kind of uncurated urban landscape that rarely appears on postcards. The title — simply "The Adventure" — carries enormous weight in a port city. Marseille's foundation myth is itself an adventure story: the Greek sailor Protis landing at the creek of Lacydon around 600 BCE, encountering the Ligurian chief Nanus, and marrying his daughter Gyptis, who had chosen him above all suitors. The city was born from a journey into the unknown.

In this context, the work functions as a small monument to that restless impulse. Ships have left Marseille for the Levant, for North Africa, for the Americas and Asia for over two and a half millennia. The word "aventure" in Old French carried the sense of fate, of what befalls you when you venture out — something happening to you rather than something you control. The artwork sits with both meanings: the planned expedition and the unexpected encounter.

The northeastern location, away from the theatrical grandeur of the waterfront, gives the work a grounded quality. This is adventure understood at a human scale — not the epic sweep of naval history, but the everyday courage of going somewhere unfamiliar and seeing what you find.
