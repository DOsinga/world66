Marseille's foundation story is itself an adventure: the Greek sailor Protis landing at the creek of Lacydon around 600 BCE, meeting the Ligurian chief Nanus, marrying his daughter Gyptis — who chose him above all other suitors. The city was born from a journey into the unknown. Ships have been leaving from here for the Levant, North Africa, the Americas, and Asia ever since.

*L'Aventure* sits in the quieter northeastern reaches, where the city loosens into residential streets and light industry — the kind of uncurated urban landscape that doesn't appear on postcards. The title's Old French meaning carried a particular shade: *aventure* was what befalls you when you venture out, something happening to you rather than something you control. The planned expedition and the unexpected encounter both live in the word.

This far from the theatrical grandeur of the waterfront, adventure reads at human scale. Not naval history — the everyday courage of going somewhere unfamiliar and seeing what you find.
