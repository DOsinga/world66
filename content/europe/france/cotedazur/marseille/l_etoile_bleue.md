Few emblems run as deep in Marseille as the eight-pointed blue star that has graced the city's heraldry for centuries. L'Étoile Bleue brings that symbol into three dimensions: a bold sculptural piece whose electric-blue form reads, from a distance, as both seafarer's compass rose and burst of Mediterranean light. The piece sits in the eastern reaches of the city, where the urban grid begins to give way to hillside scrub and the sea glitters just below the horizon.

The star of Marseille is older than the modern coat of arms. Scholars trace its origin to the ancient colony of Massalia, where a stellar motif appeared on coins minted as early as the fourth century BC, possibly referring to Venus as the morning star or to the Pole Star that guided Phocaean sailors across the open water. By the medieval period the symbol had been absorbed into the city's official imagery, and it has never really left — appearing on façades, gate-posts, and ironwork throughout the old quarters.

Standing before L'Étoile Bleue, the colour itself does most of the work. Marseille blue is not the navy of the north or the powder blue of the high Alps; it is the saturated, slightly unreal blue of the Calanques seen at noon, the colour that convinced painters and poets that this city exists on the threshold between Europe and somewhere else entirely.
