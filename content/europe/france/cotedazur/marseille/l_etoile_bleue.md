The eight-pointed blue star has been on Marseille's heraldry for centuries. Scholars trace it back further — to the ancient colony of Massalia, where a stellar motif appeared on coins minted as early as the fourth century BCE, possibly referencing Venus as the morning star or the Pole Star that guided Phocaean sailors across open water. The medieval city absorbed the symbol into its official imagery and it has never left.

*L'Étoile Bleue* pulls that symbol into three dimensions. From a distance the form reads as both seafarer's compass rose and burst of Mediterranean light. It sits in the eastern reaches of the city, where the urban grid starts giving way to hillside scrub and the sea glitters just below the horizon.

The colour does most of the work. Marseille blue isn't the navy of the north or the powder blue of the high Alps. It's the saturated, slightly unreal blue of the Calanques seen at noon — the colour that convinced painters the city exists on the threshold between Europe and somewhere else entirely.
