The whale is big enough to stop you. Not aquarium-big — genuinely large, the kind of scale that makes you recalibrate what you thought you knew about ocean animals. Marseille's relationship with the sea runs 2,600 years deep, and fin whales and sperm whales still pass through the Gulf of Lion. The city has always known they're out there.

The sculpture sits south of the Vieux-Port, away from the tourist circuit. That's the right place for it. Great whales don't belong in harbours — they belong in open water, which is exactly the point. At low tide, when the stone around it is bare and the brine smell is strong, the thing looks stranded. Come back in evening light and it reads differently: something surfacing, rather than beached.

It doesn't explain itself. No plaque will tell you whether this is celebration, elegy, or warning. Walk around it and decide.
