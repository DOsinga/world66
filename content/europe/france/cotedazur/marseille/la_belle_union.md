After 1848, France's industrial cities filled with *sociétés de secours mutuels* — mutual aid societies that gave workers sickness pay, funeral funds, and somewhere to meet that wasn't a factory or a church. Marseille, one of the largest ports in the country, had dozens. La Belle Union was one of their halls — and the name was deliberate. The Beautiful Union. Workers chose that, not irony.

The building's facade borrows the formal proportions of bourgeois architecture. That was intentional too. This neighbourhood cycled through Italians, Armenians, North Africans — each wave adapting the hall to its own associational life. A Corsican singing society, a Neapolitan mutual fund, a dockworkers' syndicate might all have used it in the same decade.

The original members are long gone. The name survives on the facade anyway, a small monument to the idea that if you brought people under one roof, they might look after each other.
