The name comes from *canebe*, Provençal for hemp. Before the city expanded this way, rope-makers worked just outside the walls, supplying the port's rigging. When Marseille laid out a broad boulevard down to the Vieux-Port, the trade name stuck. Walk it now and you're walking on rope.

At its peak — roughly 1870 to 1940 — the Canebière was everything. Department stores, grand hotels, merchant cafes, the major banks. Sailors from every port in the world knew it by name. Marseillais compared it, without irony, to the Champs-Élysées. The comparison wasn't insane.

The decline after the 1960s was fast. Suburban shopping centres pulled the commerce out; the luxury never came back. The buildings are mostly intact, the tram runs the full length, the grand scale is unbroken — but you can feel the gap between what this was meant to be and what it became. Walk it end to end. It's one of the more honest urban stories in France.
