The building operated as Marseille's fish auction house until the fishermen moved operations to the industrial port at Saumaty in the 1970s. Director Marcel Maréchal saw something in the emptied hall: its volume, its iron skeleton, and the acoustic signature of a market shed were theatrical assets rather than problems to solve. The conversion that opened in 1981 kept the structure essentially intact — the name itself, from the auctioneer's cry (la criée), remained.

La Criée became a Centre Dramatique National in 1983, one of France's network of state-supported regional theatres with a mandate to produce new work. The programming leans toward contemporary French and European drama. The building's industrial character shapes what works on its stage: productions with weight and scale rather than drawing-room naturalism.

The location adds a layer that no other French theatre can claim. Standing on the Quai de Rive Neuve with the Vieux-Port directly in front and the Abbaye Saint-Victor immediately beside, La Criée sits at the intersection of Marseille's commercial history and its oldest religious site. The walk from the ferry terminal takes three minutes.
