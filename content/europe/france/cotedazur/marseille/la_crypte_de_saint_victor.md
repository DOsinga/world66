The nave of Saint-Victor was built in the 11th and 14th centuries. The crypt beneath it dates from the 5th. Descend the stairs and you've gone back nearly a thousand years past the church above you, into a structure built over a 3rd-century Christian necropolis on the south shore of the Vieux-Port. This site was sacred before Rome fell.

The Black Madonna — Notre-Dame-de-la-Confession — is a 3rd-century Egyptian statue, predating the Christianisation of the empire. Every 2 February, the Chandeleur procession carries her through Marseille's streets. It's one of the oldest continuous Christian processions in France. They hid the statue during the Revolution to stop it being destroyed. Then they brought it back out.

The sarcophagi lining the walls are 3rd and 4th century, from when Marseille was a significant early Christian community on the Rome–North Africa trade routes. The tomb marked as Saint Victor's — a Roman soldier martyred in 303 CE — is disputed by scholars. Saint Cassian's nearby, dated 304 CE, is considered more certain. Either way, you're standing in a room where people have been coming to pray for seventeen centuries.
