Marseille's hills were once studded with windmills. The mistral — cold, dry, relentless, funnelled down the Rhône valley and out across the Gulf of Lion — was free power. The mills ground grain, crushed olives, processed whatever the city needed. By the mid-19th century, steam had made them redundant, and their towers started crumbling into the landscape.

The Vieux Moulin that gives this site its name is one of those ruins, up in the eastern hills where the terrain climbs toward the Calanques. La Fée du Vieux Moulin places a fairy figure alongside it — which is exactly the Romantic instinct: animate a ruin with a supernatural presence, because a ruin without a story is just rubble. In Provençal folklore, mills were already liminal places, active at night, subject to strange sounds.

It's a long way from the tourist circuit, in a part of Marseille most visitors never reach. The views from the elevation span the whole city to the coast. On clear days you can see the Château d'If offshore. Another ruin, another story. The impulse is the same.
