Fidelity, Justice, Liberty, Strength — the 19th century had a standard vocabulary for civic virtues, and it put them in stone on courthouses, town halls, and monuments. This figure follows the type: composed, draped in classical robes, holding whatever attribute signals her identity to anyone who knows the iconography.

In a port city defined by transience — sailors leaving, migrants arriving, emigrants departing — Fidelity carries extra weight. She's about the bonds that hold communities together across distance. Loyalty between partners, between citizens and state, between Marseille and the sea that gives it everything and occasionally takes it back.

She's easy to walk past without noticing. That's worth reversing: the city invested in this kind of moral statement in stone, permanently, and the investment is still quietly there. Pause for a moment and read what Marseille chose to say about itself when it had to pick just one word.
