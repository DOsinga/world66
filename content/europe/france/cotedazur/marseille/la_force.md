La Force and La Fidélité were almost certainly designed as a pair for the same building. The 19th century understood the virtues as a system: fidelity without force is fragile; force without fidelity is mere power. Put them together and you're making an argument about how a city intends to govern itself.

The figure follows the classical type — muscular bearing, perhaps a column shaft or a lion, the conventional attributes for Strength across European sculpture from antiquity onward. The carving is competent and serious, the work of a stonemason's trade producing institutional commissions in considerable numbers. Not inspired, which gives it the right kind of gravity.

Stand between the two figures for a moment. They were placed to be read together, two terms in a sentence. The building in the middle is the punctuation.
