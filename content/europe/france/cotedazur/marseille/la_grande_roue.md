Giant Ferris wheels became a French urban fixture in the early 2000s — Paris got one on the Place de la Concorde, then dozens of other cities followed. Marseille's version turns up seasonally, dominates the skyline for a few months, then disappears. No permanence intended.

But at the top of the arc, the view is the real thing. The old quarters fall toward the Vieux-Port. The hills rise behind. The Frioul islands float offshore. On a clear day the open Mediterranean stretches south until there's nothing left to see. The ride is slow enough that everything settles before you come back down.

It's a fairground pleasure — deliberately low in pretension. The city allows itself that. A few minutes at the top of La Grande Roue is one of the cheapest ways to understand why people have been sailing into this harbour for two and a half thousand years and staying.
