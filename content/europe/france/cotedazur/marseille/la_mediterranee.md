Marseille was founded by Greek colonists around 600 BC. That means the city's relationship with the Mediterranean predates France by more than a millennium. Every trade route, every wave of migration, every war that shaped the western basin of the sea passed through this harbour. Putting a personification of the Mediterranean on a building here isn't decoration. It's a statement about what the city is made of.

La Méditerranée follows an ancient type: a woman, calm and abundant, draped in classical robes, holding attributes from the sea's mythology — a trident, a prow, fish, or simply the open posture of someone who belongs to the water. The line runs back through 19th-century allegorical sculpture to ancient representations of Thalassa and Amphitrite. Marseille didn't invent the figure — but it has a stronger claim to her than any other city in France.

She's built into a cornice or set into a niche somewhere along a street now organized for traffic. Look up. The upward glance is how you find what the city chose to say about itself before the language of public space changed.
