The real thing is specific: at least 72% vegetable oil, cooked in large copper vats for 72 hours across four stages, no additives, no colourants. What comes out is a solid homogeneous block with different chemistry from the industrial stuff — measurably less irritating to skin, which is why dermatologists still recommend it. The industrial versions using continuous saponification are not the same product.

This factory is one of the last places doing it the original way. At the height of the trade, Marseille had over 90 such factories employing thousands. Synthetic detergents killed most of them after World War II. The handful left survive on authenticity — which in this case is not marketing language but a verifiable chemical difference.

You can watch the production: the copper vats, the pasting, the cutting and stamping of bars. It's an industrial process, not a craft demonstration. That's worth seeing too.
