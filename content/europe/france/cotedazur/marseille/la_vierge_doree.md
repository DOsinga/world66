Everyone knows the golden Virgin on Notre-Dame de la Garde, the one ships navigate by, visible from the Calanques on a clear day. This isn't her. This one sits in the residential streets north of the Canebière — smaller, the gold worn by decades of Provençal sun and sea air into something warmer and less triumphant than the cathedral statue above.

That patina changes the feeling entirely. The basilica Virgin is a civic announcement. This one is a neighborhood presence — the kind of figure that accumulates quiet attention from people walking past, not from pilgrims arriving on boats.

Gilded statues lose their bombast over time. The periodic re-gilding, the cleaning, the decades of attention — it accumulates into something almost devotional in itself, regardless of what you believe. Give it a few quiet minutes.
