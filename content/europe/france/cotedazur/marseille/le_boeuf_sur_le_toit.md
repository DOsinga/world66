In 1920 Jean Cocteau attached a surrealist title to a short theatrical piece: The Ox on the Roof. Darius Milhaud scored it with Brazilian popular tunes for a clown act at the Cirque Medrano. The following year, a Paris bar opened under that name and became the living room of the Groupe des Six — Milhaud, Poulenc, Honegger — alongside Picasso, Cocteau, and the rest of the interwar crowd. It relocated near the Champs-Élysées and became one of the grand Parisian brasseries.

Marseille took the name for a bar in a residential quarter well away from the Old Port tourist circuit. The city's café culture runs deeper than Paris's anyway — fed by centuries of Mediterranean trade, not bourgeois fashion. A bar with this name here sits at the intersection of two French drinking traditions: the formal northern brasserie and the loose southern terrasse.

The Groupe des Six preferred the informal to the ceremonious. They would have approved of ending up somewhere like this.
