On 21 April 1960, Brazil inaugurated a brand-new capital carved from the central plateau. Oscar Niemeyer's sweeping concrete curves went around the world in photographs. France was particularly struck — Niemeyer maintained a Paris studio and later designed the Communist Party headquarters there. Bars, hotels, and restaurants named Le Brasilia appeared across the country through the decade, trading on the excitement of a utopia under construction.

Marseille had its own connection. Centuries of trade with Brazil through coffee and textiles meant the southern port knew the South Atlantic in a way Paris didn't. The name carried warm familiarity alongside cosmopolitan glamour. It landed here near the Prado beaches during the postwar expansion of the southern districts, when the new and the exotic felt like the same thing.

Today the Niemeyer connection is just part of the furniture. The city on the plateau got UNESCO-listed in 2016. The brasserie carries on in the Mediterranean sun.
