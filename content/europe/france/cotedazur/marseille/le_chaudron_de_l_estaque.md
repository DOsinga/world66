L'Estaque is a former fishing village absorbed into northwest Marseille — painted facades above a small harbour, white limestone ridges behind. Paul Cézanne first came in the 1870s, drawn by the hard geometry of the bay, and returned for three decades. The viaduct, the rooftops, the factory chimneys reflected in the water: some of the most studied images in Western art.

In 1908 Georges Braque took a studio here and painted the same hillsides in a way nobody had seen before. When he showed the results in Paris, the critic Louis Vauxcelles dismissed them as painted with "little cubes." Cubism got its name from an insult.

The neighbourhood today is residential and working-class. The tourist circuit mostly skips it. Stand near the water and look back toward the bay: you're in the same light that cracked the history of painting open. A chaudron — a cauldron — is exactly right. Things go in and come out transformed.
