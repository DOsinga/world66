Marseille sits at roughly the latitude of Rome, but the light is different. The mistral comes down from the Alps every few days and strips the humidity out completely. What's left is a transparency that makes shadows look cut with a scalpel. Painters who worked here — Cézanne, Braque, Dufy — consistently described it as a challenge rather than a pleasure. The brightness is too even, the blue too saturated, the contrast too extreme for northern European conventions.

Cézanne painted the Estaque headland dozens of times and never quite solved it. He kept coming back. That's the thing about the Marseille sky: it doesn't flatter. It just is, blazing and indifferent, available to anyone who looks up on a day when the mistral has done its work.

Le Ciel de Marseille takes its name from that tradition. One of the few genuinely inexhaustible spectacles the city offers, and it costs nothing.
