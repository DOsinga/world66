Le Dôme is one of Marseille's principal large-capacity entertainment venues, a multipurpose arena built to attract the touring concerts, sporting events, and family shows that require more space than the city's historic theatres and opera house can offer. The building sits in the northern arrondissements, away from the tourist centre, and serves an overwhelmingly local audience — the crowds it draws are Marseillais rather than visitors, which gives it a different atmosphere from the city's more architecturally celebrated cultural institutions.

The venue can configure its floor and seating to accommodate a range of capacities and formats, from standing concerts to seated galas. Its programming mixes international touring acts — rock bands, electronic DJs, hip-hop artists — with French variety, comedy, and the kind of spectacular family entertainment that fills arenas across Europe. For many residents of the northern and eastern arrondissements, Le Dôme is the live entertainment venue closest to home, far easier to reach than the Opéra or the Silo in the port district.

Architecturally, the building is functional rather than expressive, a curved roof form in the tradition of mid-scale arenas built across French cities from the 1980s onward. It lacks the ambition of Marseille's more recent cultural landmarks but fulfils a genuine civic purpose: making large-scale live entertainment available in parts of the city that the grands projets of the Euroméditerranée era have not yet reached.
