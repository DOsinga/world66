The génie is an allegorical type borrowed from Roman funerary sculpture — winged, male, posed to suggest some elevated purpose. Paris has one on top of the July Column. Here the idea lands differently.

Marseille was a Greek trading colony from 600 BCE. The sea was not a backdrop — it was the reason the city existed at all. Medieval spice routes, Ottoman trade, colonial shipping lines to North Africa, the industrial port — everything ran through this harbour. Twenty-six centuries of that will earn you a monument.

The winged figure compresses all of it into one allegorical gesture. In a city less obsessed with its own maritime identity it would read as decorative. Here it reads as literal.
