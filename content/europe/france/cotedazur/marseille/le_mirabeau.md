Mirabeau was born near Aix in 1749, aristocratic by family but bankrupt and scandalous by reputation — his own father had him imprisoned multiple times by royal order. By 1789 nobody wanted him. The Provençal nobility refused him. He ran for the Third Estate of Aix instead and became the dominant orator of the Revolution's opening act.

The contradiction was the point. He had the bulk, the scarred face, and the rhetorical force of someone who didn't need to be liked — and used it to argue for constitutional monarchy while secretly advising the king. He died in April 1791, before the Terror made that position fatal.

A bar calling itself Le Mirabeau is invoking a specific Provençal type: aristocratic in origin, populist in performance, ambiguous at the core. The region produced him and still remembers him that way.
