Le MUR Marseille is part of a France-wide association that transforms a single, carefully chosen exterior wall into a revolving canvas for contemporary street art. Every few weeks a new artist is invited to take over the surface — painting it from scratch, often in full public view over a single weekend. The result is a living timeline of urban art, where the next piece is always imminent and the previous one already belongs to history.

The wall sits in the Cours Julien quarter, Marseille's most consistently creative neighbourhood. This sun-bleached hillside of painted façades, independent bookshops and open-air café terraces has been the city's bohemian heartland since the 1980s. Works here have come from artists across Europe and beyond, ranging from hyper-detailed portraiture to graphic abstraction and political commentary — each piece shaped partly by whoever holds the spray can and partly by the relentless Marseille light.

Visitors can follow Le MUR Marseille on social media to time a visit around the next whitewash-and-repaint event, when the transformation happens live. Between editions the wall stands as a finished artwork in its own right, typically for two to four weeks before the cycle begins again.
