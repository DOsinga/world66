Soap has been made in Marseille at industrial scale since the 14th century. By 1688 it had enough reputation that Louis XIV's minister Colbert formalised the name: savon de Marseille must contain a minimum olive oil content and must come from the Marseille region. Same era as the Champagne regulations — one of the earliest geographic indications in French history.

The 20th century dismantled the industry. Cheaper vegetable oils replaced olive oil; synthetic detergents ate the laundry market; and the name, never adequately enforced, spread to soaps made anywhere with no olive oil at all. Genuine production — hard soap, 72% olive oil minimum, traditional cauldron method — now rests with fewer than five manufacturers.

The museum covers the whole process: saponification in open cauldrons, the taillage where the hardened block is cut, the stamp on the traditional 300-gram cube. It is also, politely, damning about everything sold as savon de Marseille within walking distance.
