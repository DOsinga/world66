The coordinates put Le Poisson out near Les Goudes or Callelongue, small fishing hamlets at the end of the coastal road where Marseille runs out and the Calanques begin. These communities pulled rascasse, rouget and Saint-Pierre from these clear waters for centuries. A fish sculpture here is not decorative — it names the thing that made this shore worth settling.

The connection runs deep. Bouillabaisse was born from the practice of simmering the unsellable catch at the end of the day — fish too bony or too small to sell at the morning market. The Vieux-Port fish market still runs every morning on the quai des Belges, selling fish that swam a few hours before dawn. The sea has fed this city for over two millennia.

The Calanques became a national park in 2012. The water out here is clear enough to see the bottom. Standing at the edge of the city with the sea ahead, the sculpture makes complete sense.
