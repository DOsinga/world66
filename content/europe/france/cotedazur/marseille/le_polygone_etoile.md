Le Polygone Étoilé sits near the Joliette docks in a part of Marseille that predates the Euroméditerranée regeneration a short walk south. The programming is rigorous and occasionally demanding: essay films, artists' video, work-in-progress screenings, live events that blur the line between cinema and theatre. It exists for people who think of film as an art form.

The name — star polygon — is a quietly provocative geometric figure, a shape that crosses itself: regular and unsettling at once. That describes the spirit of the place. Le Polygone Étoilé has kept alive the work of Marcel Hanoun (1929–2012), the Marseille filmmaker who made spare, formally inventive films at his own pace across five decades, treating cinema as a philosophical act.

Getting here takes you through working streetscapes most visitors never see. Screenings are followed by open conversation — filmmakers, curators, the audience stays. The bar is open. The arguments continue there.
