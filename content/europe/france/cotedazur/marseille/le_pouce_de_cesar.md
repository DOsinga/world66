That oversized thumb is a direct cast of the artist's own hand, blown up to two metres. César Baldaccini grew up in the working-class Belle de Mai neighbourhood, the child of Italian immigrants. He came back to the city famous, and Marseille named a square after him.

He made three great series: Compressions — cars and motorcycles crushed into dense rectangular blocks; Expansions — polyurethane foam poured and left to spread like lava; and Empreintes — direct casts of human body parts, enlarged to monumental scale. The Thumb started in 1965. It exists in multiple sizes; the largest is twelve metres.

The silhouette of his thumb also became the shape of the Trophée César — the French film industry's equivalent of the Oscar, awarded since 1976. A sculptor from a working-class Marseille neighbourhood ended up giving French cinema its most recognisable symbol.
