Le Silo is a concert and events venue in the Joliette district, housed inside a grain silo built in the 1920s to serve the working port. The building is one of the most recognisable pieces of industrial architecture along the northern waterfront: a tall concrete cylinder that once held grain unloaded from cargo ships is now a column of sound and light, surrounded by the regenerating Euroméditerranée zone. The conversion, completed in 2011, preserved the silo's cylindrical form while fitting the interior with a main hall, a smaller auditorium, and the technical infrastructure of a contemporary venue.

The main hall has a capacity of around two thousand, making it one of the larger concert venues in the city. The acoustic transformation of a grain silo was not a simple exercise: the interior required significant treatment to convert from a space designed to hold bulk commodities into one capable of amplified music across multiple genres. The result is used for pop, rock, and world music concerts as well as corporate events and conferences.

Le Silo's opening coincided with the period when the Joliette was being remade as a district: alongside the Mucem, the new headquarters of major shipping companies, and the Théâtre de la Joliette, it became part of the cultural and economic layer that was being laid over what had been a purely industrial port quarter. The building's tower is visible from considerable distance, and the grain silo that was once a working part of the port is now one of the clearest landmarks of the transformed waterfront.
