Le ZEF takes its name from Marseille slang for the mistral, the fierce north wind that scours the city for days at a stretch — an apt name for a venue in the northern arrondissements that aims to shake things up. As a scène nationale, it belongs to a network of around eighty state-funded regional theatres across France, supported by the Ministry of Culture to present a mixed programme of theatre, dance, circus, music, and new performance forms. Scènes nationales are not prestige opera houses; they are supposed to take risks and serve the full range of a city's population, especially those with least access to major cultural institutions.

In Marseille's case that mission has particular weight. The northern arrondissements — the 13th, 14th, 15th, and 16th — are among the most deprived in France, and the cultural infrastructure that clusters around the Vieux-Port and the Canebière has rarely made it this far north with any consistency. Le ZEF operates from a purpose-designed building with a large main stage and a flexible black-box space, and it commissions and co-produces work as well as receiving touring productions. The programming ranges from established French theatre companies to emerging choreographers and international circus artists.

The venue is also a working neighbourhood resource: workshops, residencies, and school projects run alongside the main season, and the bar-restaurant that fronts the building is used by locals who may never come for a show but treat the building as part of their public life. Coming from the city centre takes around twenty minutes by metro and bus — a journey that is itself a useful reminder of how large and internally varied Marseille actually is.
