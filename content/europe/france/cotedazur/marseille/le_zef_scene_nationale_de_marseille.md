The northern arrondissements — the 13th, 14th, 15th, 16th — are among the most deprived in France. The cultural infrastructure that clusters around the Vieux-Port rarely made it this far north with any consistency. Le ZEF is the counterweight: a state-funded scène nationale with a large main stage, a black-box space, and a bar-restaurant that the neighbourhood uses whether or not anyone is coming to a show.

As a scène nationale it belongs to a national network of about eighty venues funded by the Ministry of Culture to take creative risks and serve populations with least access to major institutions. The programming ranges from established French theatre companies to emerging choreographers and international circus artists — work commissioned here alongside touring productions.

Getting here from the city centre takes twenty minutes by metro and bus. That journey is itself a reminder of how vast and internally varied Marseille actually is.
