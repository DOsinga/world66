The Cité de la Verdière went up in the postwar decades of rapid growth — towers and slab blocks packed with working-class families, many of them immigrants or the children of immigrants. By the 1980s, underinvestment had done its usual work. The estate's name became shorthand for a problem rather than a place.

Les Enfants de la Verdière — the Children of the Verdière — refuses that framing. It puts the residents, and specifically the younger generations who came of age here, at the centre of the story instead of the margin. Not urban policy. Not crime statistics. The children themselves.

Marseille has a long tradition of celebrating itself through its port, its football club, and its cuisine while its northern arrondissements go unrepresented. A work that names the children of a specific cité is, in that context, a quiet political act.
