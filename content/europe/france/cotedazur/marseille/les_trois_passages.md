Les Trois Passages stands in the eastern arrondissements, in a part of Marseille that is residential and largely unvisited by anyone from the rest of the city. Three openings, three thresholds — the number carries symbolic weight in architecture and narrative alike. The piece plays on that resonance without labouring it.

It is an apt subject for this city. Marseille has been a point of arrival and departure for most of its 2,600-year history: goods, people, and ideas crossing through rather than settling, or settling after a crossing that started somewhere else. The eastern neighbourhoods have their own version of that — postwar housing built for families leaving the overcrowded centre, later home to immigration from North Africa and the Comoros.

The work invites you to notice transitions that everyday movement makes invisible: between street and building, between neighbourhood and neighbourhood, between the Marseille you know and the Marseille you have never been to.
