title: Les Variétés

Almost every other pre-war cinema in Marseille has closed or been converted to other uses. The Variétés survived because it never tried to be anything other than a neighbourhood picture house. Its ornate Art Nouveau facade has been preserved not as a heritage project but because nobody ever had reason to tear it down and rebuild. The programming is mainstream — French and international releases — which is precisely why it has outlasted the specialised cinemas that opened and closed around it.

The Canebière was the artery of 19th-century Marseille: the boulevard where the whole city came to promenade, shop, and be seen. Since the Second World War, commercial activity migrated to the Prado axis and the shopping centres of the outskirts. The Canebière declined into a transit corridor with few functioning institutions of any kind. The Variétés is the anomaly.

Going to the Variétés is not principally about what's on screen — you can see the same releases anywhere in the city. It is about sitting in a cinema that carries a century of continuous use and watching Marseille carry on around it.
