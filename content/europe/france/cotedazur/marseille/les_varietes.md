Almost every pre-war cinema in Marseille has closed or been converted. The Variétés survived because it never tried to be anything other than a neighbourhood picture house. Nobody ever had reason to tear down that ornate Art Nouveau facade. The programming is mainstream — French and international releases — which is exactly why it outlasted the specialised cinemas that opened and closed around it.

The Canebière was 19th-century Marseille's main artery: the boulevard where the whole city promenaded and was seen. Since the war, commercial activity migrated south. The Canebière became a transit corridor. The Variétés is the anomaly — a century of continuous use in a building that looks like it was made to last.

Going here is not mainly about what is on screen. You can see the same film anywhere in the city. It is about sitting in a room that has been doing this since 1907, while Marseille carries on around it.
