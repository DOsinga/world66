From any hilltop in Marseille you can see the islands. Most visitors glimpse them from the Vieux-Port and go no further. The Maison des Îles et du Littoral exists to fix that: maps, information, and programming for the Frioul archipelago, the Château d'If, and the Calanques stretching south towards Cassis.

The islands carry specific histories. Château d'If was a royal fortress turned state prison for nearly three centuries — its most famous inmate was fictional (the Count of Monte Cristo), but the cell is pointed out with conviction. The Frioul islands served as a plague quarantine station during the 1720 epidemic that killed nearly half of Marseille's population. Today Pomègues and Ratonneau are linked by a causeway and feel improbably wild for somewhere three kilometres offshore.

The Calanques became a national park in 2012, the first in France to combine land and sea in a single protected area. If you are planning a boat trip to If, a hike into the Calanques, or simply want to understand the coast you can see from the city, start here.
