The name on this building goes back to Beaumarchais. His Figaro — the quick-witted barber who outmanoeuvres his social superiors in The Barber of Seville and The Marriage of Figaro — was so politically charged that Louis XVI banned the second play for five years. Napoleon later called it "the Revolution already put into action." Naming your newspaper after that character was a declaration of intent.

The paper launched in Paris in 1826 but had a southern outpost. Marseille was a serious centre of French print culture before Paris pulled everything north in the nineteenth century. This building is a trace of that moment — when provincial cities had their own reading publics, their own journalists, their own debates.

It is a curbside detail more than a destination. But the story behind the name is worth knowing as you walk past.
