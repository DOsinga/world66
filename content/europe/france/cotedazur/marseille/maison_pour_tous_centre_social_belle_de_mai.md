The *Maison Pour Tous* (House for Everyone) is a French municipal institution — a network of publicly funded community centres established in the post-war decades to bring social services, leisure programmes, and collective life to working-class urban neighbourhoods. Each centre is run by the city in partnership with local associations, offering everything from after-school activities and language classes to counselling, workshops, and meeting space for neighbourhood groups. The concept reflects a distinctly French approach to civic welfare: the idea that a physical, accessible, publicly owned place can anchor social cohesion in areas the market would otherwise neglect.

The centre in Belle de Mai sits in the 3rd arrondissement, one of the most deprived districts in metropolitan France by standard measures of unemployment, poverty, and overcrowded housing. Belle de Mai is a dense, heterogeneous neighbourhood built around the former tobacco factory — La Manufacture — whose industrial conversion gave birth to La Friche de la Belle de Mai, now one of France's most celebrated cultural laboratories. The coexistence of a community social centre and an internationally recognised arts complex in the same neighbourhood captures Belle de Mai's contradictions: genuine deprivation alongside genuine creative energy.

For visitors, the Maison Pour Tous is primarily a neighbourhood landmark rather than a tourist destination, but it gives a clear sense of how the city's social infrastructure is woven into residential streets. Passing through gives a glimpse of the layered life of Belle de Mai — the market stalls, the social housing blocks, the community noticeboards — that the more photographed parts of Marseille rarely offer.
