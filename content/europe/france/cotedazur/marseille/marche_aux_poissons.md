The market operates every morning except Monday. What is available is not a supply chain decision; it is whatever the boats brought back from the previous night on the Mediterranean. Rascasse (scorpionfish), grondin (gurnard), and saint-pierre (John Dory) are the bouillabaisse staples — present when the fishing was good, absent when it wasn't. Sea urchins, sardines, and shellfish appear according to season and catch, not supermarket logic. The restaurants around the port buy here; the connection between market and table is direct and same-day.

Massalia was founded around 600 BCE as a Phocaean trading post, and fish and salt were among the original commodities that made the site viable. The market on the Quai des Belges has operated in some continuous form for most of the port's history — the current arrangements are recent; the underlying activity is not.

Arrive before 8am to see the full market. By 10am the best fish has gone and some stalls are already packing up. The market is small — a dozen stalls at most — and functions without ceremony. No English is required if you can point at what you want.
