The Marché aux Puces at Plan d'Aou is one of the largest second-hand markets in the south of France, and one of the few that operates without any pretension to being anything other than what it is. The northern arrondissements of Marseille are the most economically deprived districts of France's most economically unequal major city, and the market reflects that reality directly: the goods on sale are practical, the prices are low, and the buyers are neighbours rather than day-trippers from the centre. Professional dealers occupy regular pitches alongside amateur collectors and families clearing out a relative's apartment, and the boundaries between the three are not always clear.

The market has operated in some form in this neighbourhood since the early 20th century, making it older than the housing estates that now surround it. The Plan d'Aou itself is a large post-war social housing development — towers and connecting streets on a hillside above the autoroute — and the flea market functions as a kind of commercial commons for residents who have limited access to the shops of the city centre. A Saturday morning visit yields a different sociology from the antique dealers and vintage-clothing boutiques of the Cours Julien: the social register here is of genuine necessity and neighbourhood exchange.

The scale is large enough that it rewards systematic exploration rather than a quick pass. Electrical components, tools, and spare parts occupy one end; furniture and household goods spread across the central area; clothing and shoes take up another section. The vendors arrive early and pack up by early afternoon. Getting there requires the 35 or L2 bus from the city centre — the journey through the northern arrondissements is itself a counter-tour of the Marseille that tourist itineraries leave out.
