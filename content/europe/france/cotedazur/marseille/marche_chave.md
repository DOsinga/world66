The Marché Chavé does not appear on most visitor itineraries, and this is part of what makes it useful to understand. It runs on Boulevard Chavé in the 5th arrondissement on Tuesday, Thursday, and Saturday mornings — fish from the overnight boats, vegetables from the Var plain and the market gardens east of the city, cheese from the Alpine producers who have their Marseille rounds, bread from the boulangers who supply the neighbourhood rather than the tourists. Transactions are brisk and in the patois of familiarity: people who have shopped here for thirty years know which stall has the better tomatoes this week.

The 5th is solid working-class residential Marseille — apartment blocks, neighbourhood cafés, the occasional tabac — without the gentrification pressure of the 6th or the tourist density of the 1st and 2nd. The market reflects the neighbourhood's composition: a cross-section of southern French provisioning that has not been curated for outside consumption. There is no ethnic specialisation, no particular story the market is trying to tell. It serves people who live nearby and need their week's food.

Hours are roughly 7am to 1pm on market days. Arrive before 10am for fish; the stalls wind down from the outside in as stock depletes.
