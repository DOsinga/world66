The supermarket never fully displaced the covered market in France. This one in the northern arrondissements runs its own rhythm: morning stalls, seasonal stock, the same vendors on the same days each week. Summer brings courgettes with their flowers still attached, Lubéron melons, the first Camargue tomatoes. Autumn brings wild mushrooms and new olive oil.

The fish counter carries rouget, sea bream, and whatever came off the boats at La Joliette — including bouillabaisse offcuts for anyone still making the stew at home. Olives and tapenade from Provençal producers with regular stalls.

The neighbourhood is not a destination. Prices are local. The language at the stalls shifts between French, Arabic, and Comorian depending on who you are talking to. Come in the morning; it winds down by noon.
