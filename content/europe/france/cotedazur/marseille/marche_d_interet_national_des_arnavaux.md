The city is asleep. The trucks are not. At 2am the loading bays fill with refrigerated lorries from Spain and the Maghreb. Wholesalers' stands stack with pallets of peppers, crates of tomatoes, boxes of fish transferred from the overnight port auction. By 7am it is all gone — dispersed into restaurant kitchens, outdoor markets, and supermarket supply chains across Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur.

De Gaulle created the MIN network in 1953 to rationalise France's chaotic post-war food distribution. Arnavaux is one of the original nodes: the largest wholesale food market between Paris and the Italian border. Hundreds of accredited buyers hold passes. The scale, seen from outside the gates at 3am, reads as industrial rather than commercial.

You cannot enter without trade credentials. But every good fish you eat at a restaurant around the Vieux-Port passed through here the previous night.
