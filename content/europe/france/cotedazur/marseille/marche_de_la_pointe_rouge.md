The Corniche Kennedy ends at the Pointe Rouge, where the city's ceremonial coastal road dissolves into the marina car park and the residential streets of the 8th. The market here — Wednesday and Saturday mornings — has the character of the neighbourhood: middle-class, oriented toward the water, with a beach-town looseness in summer that tightens into focused provisioning in winter when the sailing community is in maintenance mode.

The stalls cover the practical range: vegetables, fruit, olives, charcuterie, a cheese van, a fishmonger who sources locally when he can and from the wholesale market at Arnavaux when he cannot. What distinguishes it from the larger city markets is scale — it remains small enough that regulars know each trader by name — and position. Shopping here on a Saturday morning, with the marina at your back and the Calanques beginning their limestone rise a few kilometres south, is a particular version of Marseillais life that the city's more celebrated quarters don't offer.

The market is an institution for the boating community: chandlers and yacht-club members come for provisions between tides. In July and August the foot traffic swells with beach visitors; in October it reverts to neighbourhood rhythm.
