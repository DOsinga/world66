Saint-Antoine occupies the northern reaches of Marseille's 15th arrondissement, a district that sits well beyond the tourist circuits of the Vieux-Port and the polished boulevards of the south. The northern arrondissements are the city's working fabric: densely populated, culturally mixed, and rarely visited by outsiders who have not come specifically to be here. Saint-Antoine has a long history as a quarter of artisans and small traders, and the market keeps that tradition alive.

The stalls cater to everyday needs rather than the weekend food-tourism market. Seasonal vegetables, fruit, dried goods, and bolts of fabric compete for space alongside stalls selling household items and clothing at prices pitched for local budgets. North-African produce — harissa, preserved lemons, ras el hanout, fresh coriander — appears alongside the Provençal staples, reflecting the community that has made this part of the city its own over several generations.

Visiting Saint-Antoine requires a decision to come, since nothing passes through on the way to somewhere else. That self-containment is part of its character: the market is purely functional, purely local, and entirely unpretentious.
