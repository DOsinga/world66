Saint-Antoine is well past the last metro stop the tourist map bothers to label. This is the 15th arrondissement's working fabric — dense, mixed, unpolished. The market has been here as long as the neighbourhood, serving artisans and families who live around it.

The stalls are strictly practical. Seasonal vegetables, bolts of fabric, household goods at prices that actually work for local budgets. Harissa, preserved lemons, ras el hanout sitting next to Provençal herbs — the community that's made this quarter its own over several generations is exactly who the market is for.

You come here on purpose, or you don't come at all. That's fine with the market.
