The Marché de Sainte-Marthe takes place in one of northern Marseille's quieter residential quarters, far from the tourist circuits of the Vieux-Port. The market draws locals from the surrounding 14th arrondissement and nearby communes, offering the everyday staples — vegetables, olives, dried herbs, cheap household goods — that Marseille's street markets have traded in for centuries. It has none of the showroom polish of the big covered marchés but all of the neighbourhood candour that makes it worth the trip.

The name Sainte-Marthe ties the quarter to one of the most persistent legends of Provençal Christianity. According to medieval tradition, Martha of Bethany — sister of Mary and of Lazarus — sailed from the Holy Land after the Crucifixion and landed on the Provençal coast. She is said to have made her way inland to Tarascon, where a fearsome river monster known as the Tarasque was terrorising the population. Martha subdued the creature with nothing but holy water and a makeshift leash of her own belt, and led it peacefully into the town. The people then destroyed it, and Tarascon held that story so tightly that it became the town's founding myth — the annual Fête de la Tarasque is still celebrated there, and the monster even lent its name to the town.

Marseille's own Sainte-Marthe quarter keeps a smaller flame for the saint. A chapel dedicated to Martha stands in the neighbourhood, and the feast day on 29 July is observed locally with some warmth. The market itself is simply a market, but its setting in this saint-named corner of the city gives it a faint resonance that the produce stalls and bargain tables alone would not supply.
