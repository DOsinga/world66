Paris put its bouquinistes in green metal boxes along the Seine. Marseille's version operates on a more Mediterranean schedule — stalls appear and disappear according to the weather and the bookseller's mood.

What you find: maritime history, colonial postcards, old Provençal dictionaries, pulp detective novels in brittle paperback, illustrated magazines from the mid-twentieth century. Prices are negotiable. The prewar guide to the Calanques, the 1960s photo survey of the Vieux-Port — those things surface only if you have the patience to look.

Marseille ran its own revolutionary newspapers in 1848 and during the Commune. Its cafés were reading rooms as much as drinking rooms. The bouquinistes carry a small fragment of that tradition on their folding tables.
