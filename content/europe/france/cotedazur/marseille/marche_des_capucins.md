The market and the surrounding streets of Noailles are where Marseille's North African, Sub-Saharan African, Middle Eastern, and South Asian communities shop for ingredients unavailable anywhere else in the city. The spice merchants stock things no supermarket carries. The halal butchers serve cuts prepared to specific community specifications. The stalls selling produce for Comorian, Malian, and Senegalese cooking are not concessions to diversity — they are the market.

Noailles has absorbed successive waves of immigration since the 19th century. Italians came first in large numbers, then Armenians fleeing the genocide, then Algerian workers, then sub-Saharan Africans and Comorians from the 1980s onward. Each community shaped the neighbourhood and left institutions in it. The Capucins reflects this archaeology: you can read the history of Marseille's relationship with migration in what the stalls sell.

This is the most genuinely multicultural food market in France, which means it is one of the most useful markets in France if you are cooking seriously. It is not a tourist market and it does not perform its diversity. Arrive with a shopping list.
