In 1949, a man who had attempted suicide came to Abbé Pierre looking for help. Pierre told him: stay, and help others. That conversation became the founding ethic of Emmaüs — those with nothing to lose helping those with even less. The name comes from the biblical village where two disciples walked with the risen Christ and didn't recognise him.

The model is simple. People donate unwanted possessions. Emmaüs "companions" — many formerly homeless — sort, repair, and price the goods. The public buys them. The proceeds fund the communities. Abbé Pierre became a national figure in the brutal winter of 1954, when a radio appeal moved France to flood emergency shelters with donations overnight.

The Marseille market runs on exactly that ethic. Not charity handed downward. Second-hand goods at prices that work, sold by people who needed this too.
