Operating since the 19th century, the market stocks fish, cheese, charcuterie, vegetables, and flowers. The fish is notably good — the 7th arrondissement is close enough to the calanques that local catches arrive here — and the cheese selection reflects the city's Italian and Corsican connections as much as the standard French repertoire. Prices are higher than the Capucins; the clientele expects quality over volume.

The neighbourhood the market serves — Endoume below the Corniche Kennedy, Bompard above — is Marseille's most quietly prosperous residential quarter. The streets are calm in a way the city centre never is. The market fits this character: unhurried, reliable, untheatrical. Stallholders know their customers by name. Conversations about fish last longer than the transaction.

The market is not a destination in the way the Vieux-Port fish market or the Capucins are destinations. It is worth visiting if you are staying in the 7th arrondissement or walking the Corniche — it represents a Marseille the tourist apparatus largely ignores: a functioning, prosperous daily-life neighbourhood with no particular mythology attached to it.
