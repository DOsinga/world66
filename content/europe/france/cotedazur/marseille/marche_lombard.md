The eastern arrondissements are where Marseille's urban fabric thins toward the hilly hinterland — mid-century blocks, village-scale quarters absorbed into the city, a daily rhythm that has nothing to do with the Vieux-Port. The Lombard is a neighbourhood market serving exactly this.

What you find is what the week offers: produce heavy on whatever's cheapest and best right now, a butcher or two, North African groceries and spices. The communities here — Comorian, Algerian, sub-Saharan African — have been in these streets long enough that the market reflects them without thinking about it. Olives next to whole cumin next to fresh coriander.

It's not a destination. But on a market morning, this part of the city is at its most legible — the same faces, the same stalls, the quarter as it actually is.
