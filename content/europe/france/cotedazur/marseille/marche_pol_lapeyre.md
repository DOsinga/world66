Pol Lapeyre was a local figure — the kind of name attached to a market or square in the civic habit of remembering worthies through spaces people use daily. A more fitting monument than most.

The market serves the eastern arrondissements. Fruit, vegetables, fish from the nearby coast, loose eggs, cuts of meat. North African produce reflecting who lives here: dried harissa, merguez, pickled lemons, fresh mint in cooking quantities. It's practical. It's for the people within walking distance.

The eastern spreads of Marseille absorbed successive waves of migration through the twentieth century. That history is live in the stalls and the languages crossing them — more revealing than anything you'll find in a museum.
