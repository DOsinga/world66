Provence has always known its regional identity could be made edible. This market trades in that knowledge. Herbes de Provence in bundles or ceramic pots; tapenade from the classic black olive blend to versions with anchovies or sun-dried tomatoes; pastis in miniature bottles alongside the anise biscuits that taste of summer.

Santons — the small fired-clay nativity figures — appear here year-round. They started in the post-Revolutionary period, when public crèches were banned and families built their own at home. The figures expanded over generations to cover every character of village life: the knife-grinder, the fishwife, the drunk sleeping under a wall. The best ones are folk art.

The market is tourist-facing in a way most Marseille markets aren't, but the quality is real. Vendors who've held their spots for years know their suppliers. The savon de Marseille carries a protected designation for the traditional 72% olive oil formula. Ask a question and you'll get an actual answer.
