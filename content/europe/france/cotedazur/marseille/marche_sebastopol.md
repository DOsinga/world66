Boulevard de Sébastopol sits in the part of the city tourists don't bother with: inland from the Vieux-Port, east of Cours Julien, apartment blocks and small businesses. The market sets up here on fixed weekly mornings — produce, poultry, dry goods — and is gone before midday. No heritage designation, no tourism pitch.

The 4th arrondissement has a mixed North African and Armenian population, both communities leaving traces in what gets sold. Spice vendors alongside Provençal vegetables, North African pastries next to cheese stalls. The vendors are small producers from the Aix plain or buyers from the early-morning fish auctions.

What this market does that supermarkets don't: regulars and vendors know each other by name, keep things aside, negotiate. Marseille's open-air market culture is genuinely tenacious. Show up on the right morning and you'll see a significant part of the city provisioning itself the way it always has.
