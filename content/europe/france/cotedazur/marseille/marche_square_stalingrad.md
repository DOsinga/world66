The square is named after the battle — post-war France scattered the Stalingrad name across hundreds of streets and squares as a gesture toward the Soviet sacrifice that helped end the war. Marseille's version landed in Belsunce, which has been a first-stop neighbourhood for immigrants for generations: southern Europeans first, then North Africans, then sub-Saharan Africans, now a mix of all of the above.

The market spreads across the paving stones on weekday mornings: seasonal produce, spices, olives, dried fruits, cheap clothing. Vendors and shoppers speak French, Arabic, Comorian, and more. The surrounding streets are dense with fabric shops, halal butchers, and North African patisseries.

Come on a weekday morning. The Vieux-Port tourist circuit feels like a different city from here. This is working Marseille — the cosmopolitan, slightly chaotic version the postcards don't sell.
