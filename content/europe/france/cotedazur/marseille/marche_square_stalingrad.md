Square Stalingrad sits in the heart of Belsunce, one of Marseille's most diverse and densely populated central neighbourhoods. The square was named after the Battle of Stalingrad (1942–43), a practice common across France in the post-war years as a gesture of solidarity and gratitude toward the Soviet Union, whose enormous sacrifice helped turn the tide of the Second World War. Dozens of French streets, squares and schools received the Stalingrad name in this period, and Marseille's version became a genuine neighbourhood anchor.

The market that animates the square is a classic southern French affair: stalls spread across the paving stones selling seasonal produce, spices, olives, dried fruits, cheap clothing and household goods. Given Belsunce's long history as a landing point for immigrants — first from southern Europe, later from North Africa and sub-Saharan Africa — the market has always reflected the neighbourhood's cosmopolitan character, with vendors and shoppers speaking a mix of French, Arabic, Comorian and more.

Come on a weekday morning for the full experience. The surrounding streets of Belsunce spill over with fabric shops, Halal butchers and patisseries selling North African pastries, making the area feel more Casablanca than Côte d'Azur. The square itself is unassuming, but the market is a genuine slice of working-class Marseille that most tourists never see.
