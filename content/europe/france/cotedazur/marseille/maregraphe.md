Every elevation in France traces back to this small building on the Endoume promontory. The geographers chose the Mediterranean over the Atlantic because the tides here are tiny — under 30 centimetres of variation, versus several metres on the Atlantic coast. The mean sea level recorded continuously at this gauge from 1884 onwards became the official French altitudinal datum in 1897. There is a brass plate on the wall marking the exact reference point.

Mont Blanc's 4,808 metres means 4,808 metres above the water level calculated here. The same datum set the depths for the Paris Métro, the levels for canals across the country, and the reference heights for every French city and department. One quiet building in Marseille, underpinning the entire national map.

The station still operates as a scientific monitoring post. Because the readings have been consistent since 1884, this is also one of the longest continuous sea-level records in the Mediterranean — 140-plus years of data against which rising sea levels can now be measured. It is not open to visitors, but you can stand outside and think about how much is calculated from this exact spot.
