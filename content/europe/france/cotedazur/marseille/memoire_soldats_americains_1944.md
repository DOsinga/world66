On 15 August 1944, Allied forces came ashore on the beaches of Provence between Toulon and Cannes — the southern landing that planners had delayed for months while arguing about where to concentrate resources. Operation Dragoon moved fast. German forces were stretched thin, the resistance had prepared the ground, and the advance up the Rhône corridor accelerated quickly.

Marseille was liberated on 28 August — two days after Paris. French troops under General de Monsabert fought street by street through the city while the German garrison tried to hold. American units provided the amphibious lift, the air support, and several of the assault divisions that came ashore on day one. The partnership had tensions, but the results came quickly.

This memorial records the American contribution in a city that had lived through four years of occupation, deportations, and resistance. Standing here alongside Marseille's other war memorials, you get something close to a complete picture of what those years cost.
