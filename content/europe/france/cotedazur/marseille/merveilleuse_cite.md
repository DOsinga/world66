"Merveilleuse" — marvellous — is a word Marseillais use for their city with a particular mixture of pride and irony. Not marvellous in the sense of polished and perfect. Marvellous in the older sense: astonishing, strange, impossible to categorise, stubbornly itself. This public artwork plants those words in the Belsunce district, where they land against a backdrop of dense streets, fabric shops, and the accumulated noise of generations of arrivals.

Belsunce has been a neighbourhood of immigrants for as long as anyone can remember: Armenians, Comorians, Algerians, sub-Saharan Africans, now artists and young professionals drawn by low rents. The work doesn't prettify this. It names what's already there if you look past the surface disorder.

"Cité" carries a double charge in French: it means city in the classical sense, but in contemporary usage it most often means a housing estate, a cité HLM, a place of concentrated social need. "Merveilleuse Cité" plays both registers at once — claiming the nobility of urban life for spaces that official culture has often written off. That's a very Marseillais move.
