Manouchian survived the Armenian genocide as an orphaned child, arrived in France as a refugee in 1924, and became a poet, Communist Party member, and factory worker in Paris. During the occupation he commanded the FTP-MOI — a resistance network assembled almost entirely from immigrants and foreign Jews: Poles, Romanians, Hungarians, Armenians, Italians, Spaniards. Between 1943 and early 1944, the group carried out more than 30 armed actions in Paris, targeting German officers and collaborationist officials.

Arrested in November 1943, Manouchian and 21 of his group were shot at Mont Valérien on 21 February 1944. The Wehrmacht immediately plastered the "Affiche Rouge" — the Red Poster — across Paris: photographs of ten fighters, their foreign names, their alleged crimes. The intent was to discredit the resistance as foreign criminality. It had the opposite effect. The poster became one of the most recognised documents of the occupation. Louis Aragon's poem and Léo Ferré's musical setting made Manouchian permanently famous in France.

In February 2024, he and his wife Mélinée entered the Panthéon — the first stateless person to receive the honour. Marseille had already given him a garden and a street near the Vieux-Port. His life compressed the twentieth century's worst events into 37 years: genocide, exile, fascism, resistance, execution, and recognition that came eighty years too late.
