The imprint is on the ground-floor pillars: a stylised human figure, arm raised, flanked by two measurement scales. That's the Modulor — Le Corbusier's proportional system, derived from the golden ratio, calibrated to a man 1.83 metres tall with one arm up. He published it in 1948. The Cité Radieuse on Boulevard Michelet was his proof of concept.

Built in 1952, it stacks 337 duplex apartments into a single block — plus internal streets, shops, a hotel, a nursery, and a rooftop running track and paddling pool. A vertical city. The Modulor wasn't decoration; it was the grammar the whole thing was written in.

The building is now UNESCO World Heritage and still fully residential. Tours of the rooftop run regularly. The Modulor stamp near the entrance is easy to miss if you're not looking — which was fine with Le Corbusier. He didn't put it there for tourists. He put it there because he believed the proportions were simply true.
