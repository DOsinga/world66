Môle A is part of the vast industrial port infrastructure that stretches north of Marseille's Vieux-Port along the Golfe de Fos. The Grand Port Maritime de Marseille — the official entity that manages the port complex — organises its quays, docks, and berthing structures with a system of numbered and lettered moles, each serving specific cargo, ferry, or operational functions. Môle A sits within this grid, a utilitarian structure of concrete and steel whose appearance gives little away but whose position in one of the busiest cargo-handling zones in the western Mediterranean makes it a working piece of serious logistics infrastructure.

The port of Marseille traces its origins to the Greek colony of Massalia, founded around 600 BC, making it one of the oldest continuously operating ports in Europe. Modern port operations, however, bear almost no resemblance to the ancient anchorage. The industrial expansion of the nineteenth and twentieth centuries pushed the working port progressively northward, freeing the Vieux-Port for leisure and fishing traffic while bulk carriers, container ships, ferries, and tankers moved to purpose-built basins. The numbered and lettered moles that result from this expansion are functional rather than picturesque — most visitors to Marseille never see them — but they underpin the city's continued role as France's leading maritime gateway, handling tens of millions of tonnes of goods annually and connecting Marseille to ports across North Africa, the Middle East, and beyond.
