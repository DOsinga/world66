The Grand Port Maritime de Marseille runs its quays on a grid of numbered and lettered moles. Môle A is one of them — a concrete working pier in the industrial port that stretches north of the Vieux-Port toward the Golfe de Fos. Container ships, tankers, ro-ro ferries. This is the machinery behind France's largest port by tonnage.

The ancient port traces back to the Greek colony of Massalia around 600 BCE. The industrial version bears no resemblance. Through the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, heavy cargo moved progressively northward, freeing the Vieux-Port for fishing boats and tourists. The lettered moles are what took its place.

Most visitors never see them. The best view is from Notre-Dame de la Garde, where the cranes and silos of the northern port stretch across the horizon — a reminder that Marseille's relationship with the sea has always been partly about moving things.
