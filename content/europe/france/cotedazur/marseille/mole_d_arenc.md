A century ago, Arenc was the kind of port district you didn't visit unless you worked there: fenced off, industrial, invisible. The Môle d'Arenc served its function — loading, unloading, berthing — and that was that. Then Euroméditerranée arrived.

Launched in 1995, it's one of the largest urban regeneration projects in France. The mandate: reconnect the city to its northern waterfront after decades of industrial severance. Les Docks Libres — a converted nineteenth-century warehouse complex — now has offices and restaurants right on the quays. MUCEM and Villa Méditerranée opened here in 2013 when Marseille was European Capital of Culture.

But the mole is still active port. Cranes and container operations continue alongside new apartment blocks and promenades. It's unresolved, which makes it interesting. You can watch Marseille mid-negotiation between what it was and what it's trying to become.
