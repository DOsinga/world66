Môle I is in the northern industrial port basin, well outside the city centre and not accessible to the public. It's part of the lettered-and-numbered grid that organises Marseille's entire working harbour — concrete fingers pushed into the Gulf of Lion to shelter berths for container ships, bulk carriers, tankers, and ro-ro ferries. France's largest port by tonnage runs on infrastructure like this.

The expansion happened across the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, each new arm adding more sheltered water and more quayside length. The naming convention is purely functional. So is the place.

The best way to take it in is from Notre-Dame de la Garde. From up there, the cranes, silos, and ship superstructures of the northern port stretch across the horizon — the working face of the city that the tourist-facing waterfront usually hides.
