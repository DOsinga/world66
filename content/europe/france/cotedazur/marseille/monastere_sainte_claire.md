The Monastère Sainte-Claire is home to a community of Poor Clares, the Franciscan order of women religious founded by Saint Clare of Assisi in 1212. The order observes strict enclosure, meaning the sisters live entirely within the monastery walls and have almost no public presence. From the street, the monastery appears as a high walled compound — easy to walk past without realising its significance.

Poor Clares communities exist in many French cities, often occupying substantial urban plots that have been walled off from the surrounding neighbourhood for centuries. In Marseille, that seclusion stands in sharp contrast to the animated streets nearby. The sisters sustain themselves largely through the sale of goods made within the monastery, and visitors are not admitted to the cloister itself.

If you pass by, the exterior is the whole of what is visible: a gate, a wall, and sometimes a small parlour window where goods can be collected. It is a rare survival — a pocket of medieval religious life absorbed into a modern Mediterranean city.
