title: Monument à Espérandieu

Henri Espérandieu shaped Marseille's skyline more than almost anyone else, and he died at forty-five without seeing either of his great commissions completed. Notre-Dame de la Garde, the Romano-Byzantine basilica on the city's highest hill, wasn't finished until 1897 — more than two decades after his death. The Palais Longchamp, that extravagant water palace with its colonnade, cascade, and two museums, also finished without him.

He had trained under Léon Vaudoyer, who started Notre-Dame de la Garde. When Vaudoyer died, Espérandieu carried the design forward and made it his own. Longchamp came later. Same pattern: designed it, didn't see it built. A city constructed largely by someone else's hands to his plans.

The monument is modest. The buildings are not. If you're visiting Notre-Dame de la Garde or the Palais Longchamp — and you should visit both — this gives those experiences a human focal point. The man who conceived them, who left before the scaffolding came down.
