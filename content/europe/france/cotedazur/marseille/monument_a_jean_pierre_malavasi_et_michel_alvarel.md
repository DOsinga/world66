This memorial sits in the southeastern corner of Marseille, close to the rocky coast where the city gives way to the Calanques national park. The neighbourhood — La Madrague de Montredon and the lanes toward Callelongue — is quiet and residential, a world away from the Vieux-Port. Streets out here carry local plaques and memorials that don't make it into guidebooks.

Jean-Pierre Malavasi and Michel Alvarel are commemorated here by name. In French civic tradition, individual memorials like this usually mark people who died in service — police officers, firefighters, civil defence volunteers — or victims of a specific local incident. The exact circumstances aren't widely documented publicly, which suggests this was erected by colleagues, family, or a local association rather than the city at large.

That's not unusual. Marseille's outer arrondissements are scattered with monuments like this — quiet acknowledgements at the neighbourhood level, for people the neighbourhood decided were worth remembering permanently.
