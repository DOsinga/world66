This modest memorial stands in the southeastern corner of Marseille's urban territory, close to the rocky coastline that gives way to the Calanques national park. The area — taking in neighbourhoods such as La Madrague de Montredon and the lanes leading toward Callelongue — is quiet and residential, a world removed from the Vieux-Port bustle, and its streets carry the kind of local plaques and memorials that rarely appear in guidebooks.

Jean-Pierre Malavasi and Michel Alvarel are commemorated here by name, which in the context of French civic monuments most often indicates individuals killed in service — as police officers, firefighters, civil defence volunteers, or members of the armed forces — or in some cases victims of a specific local tragedy. The exact circumstances of their deaths are not widely documented in public sources, which suggests this is a neighbourhood-level tribute erected by colleagues, family, or a local association rather than a citywide official commemoration.

Memorials of this kind are scattered throughout Marseille's outer arrondissements, quiet acknowledgements of lives ended in circumstances the city felt were worth marking permanently. For those walking the coastal paths toward the Calanques, the monument is a reminder that even the most leisure-oriented corners of the city carry layers of local memory that tourist maps leave blank.
