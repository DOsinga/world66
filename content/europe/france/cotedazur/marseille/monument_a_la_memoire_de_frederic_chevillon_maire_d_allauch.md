Allauch is a commune in the Bouches-du-Rhône department that sits on the limestone hills immediately to the northeast of Marseille, administratively separate but deeply intertwined with the city's urban fabric. For much of the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, the boundary between Marseille and its hillside neighbours was porous — families, commerce, and political life overlapped, and figures who served one community were often known and respected in the other.

Frédéric Chevillon served as mayor of Allauch, and the decision to place a monument to his memory within Marseille's own territory — at coordinates in the 7th or western arrondissement area — points either to a personal connection with this part of the city (perhaps he was born here, died here, or maintained property and associations here) or to a broader recognition that extended beyond his commune's administrative limits. The monument is a modest stone-and-bronze affair typical of the Third Republic tradition of civic commemoration, honouring local leadership and public service at a time when the office of mayor carried significant weight in the daily lives of small communities.

Allauch itself is worth a short trip: its old village centre, perched above the city with views toward the Chaîne de l'Étoile and the sea, retains a strong Provençal character including a Corpus Christi procession that has continued for centuries. The monument to Chevillon, tucked into a corner of Marseille, is a small biographical thread connecting the two places.
