Allauch sits on the limestone hills immediately northeast of Marseille — a separate commune, but the boundary between the two places has always been porous. Families, commerce, political connections: the hillside village and the port city have overlapped for centuries.

Frédéric Chevillon served as Allauch's mayor, and this monument to his memory was placed on Marseille's own territory. That's unusual enough to suggest a genuine connection here — born in this part of the city, perhaps, or with property and associations that ran on both sides of the administrative line. The monument is a stone-and-bronze affair typical of the Third Republic tradition: civic service honoured, office marked in permanence.

Allauch itself rewards a short trip. The old village centre perches above the city with views toward the Chaîne de l'Étoile and the sea, and a Corpus Christi procession has continued there for centuries. The monument in Marseille is a small biographical thread connecting the two places.
