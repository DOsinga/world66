Philippe Leclerc de Hauteclocque (1902–1947) — known to history simply as Leclerc — was one of the great commanders of the Free French forces and the man most associated with the liberation of Paris. After joining de Gaulle in London in 1940, he led campaigns in Chad and Libya, then commanded the 2nd Armoured Division in Normandy before receiving the surrender of the German garrison in Paris on 25 August 1944. He was killed in a plane crash in Algeria in November 1947 and was posthumously elevated to Marshal of France in 1952.

Marseille's connection to Leclerc is partly general — he is a pan-French liberation figure — and partly specific: the city's own liberation in August 1944 was part of Operation Dragoon, the Allied landings in southern France, a campaign that Leclerc's broader command context shaped. The monument sits in the northern part of the city, in a neighbourhood where the memory of the liberation and of Free France retains living resonance.

The Maréchal de France designation in the title is honoured posthumously: Leclerc died a general and was raised to the symbolic dignity of marshal by the state he had helped to restore. His monument in Marseille participates in a national network of Leclerc memorials that runs from Normandy to the Sahara — a cartography of the campaign that brought France back to itself.
