Leclerc wasn't even his real name. Philippe de Hauteclocque fled occupied France in 1940, adopted a pseudonym to protect his family, and joined de Gaulle in London. He led Free French forces across the Sahara — Chad, Libya, Tunisia — then commanded the 2nd Armoured Division into Normandy. On 25 August 1944, he received the German surrender of Paris.

Eighteen months later he was dead, killed in a plane crash in Algeria. France elevated him to Marshal of France posthumously in 1952 — the honour the Republic owed him and couldn't give him while he was alive.

Marseille's memorial sits in a northern neighbourhood where the memory of Operation Dragoon, the southern liberation of 1944, runs deep. Leclerc didn't personally command that campaign, but his name became shorthand for the whole moment when France liberated itself.
