More than two thousand names. Italian, Greek, Spanish, North African, Provençal — they run together in alphabetical order on this stone, stripped of rank or origin. Reading through them is like reading a census of pre-war Marseille: a port city where half the families had come from somewhere else within living memory.

Many of these men died nowhere near the Western Front. Marseille was France's gateway to its empire, so its sons were sent to the Dardanelles, Salonika, Egypt, Palestine. The city also sent soldiers north to Verdun and the Somme. The losses came from every direction.

This is the fixed point for Marseille's 11 November ceremonies — the wreaths, the last post, the minute's silence. At this scale, reading the names aloud is itself an act of mourning. The stone does what the city cannot do any other way: it holds them all together in one place.
