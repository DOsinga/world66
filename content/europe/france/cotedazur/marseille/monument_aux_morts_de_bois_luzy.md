Bois-Luzy means oak grove in Provençal — the holm oaks that once shaded the grounds of the eighteenth-century château at the heart of this eastern neighbourhood. The château is a school now. The area still has that slightly secluded quality: wide plots, older villas, a pace that doesn't feel like the port districts.

When a place has a stable, close-knit population, a war memorial hits differently. The names on this stone weren't strangers — they were brothers, fathers, sons that everyone in the neighbourhood could place. France's 1.4 million dead from 1914–18 were not distributed evenly across an abstract national surface. They were concentrated in exactly these small communities.

Coming across this memorial among the quiet streets near the château grounds is a reminder that even the calmest corner of a large city wasn't spared.
