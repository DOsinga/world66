On 9 October 1934, King Alexander I of Yugoslavia stepped off a ship at the Joliette dock. A Pathé camera was rolling to film the arrival. Within seconds, a Macedonian gunman broke through the crowd and shot him dead. French Foreign Minister Louis Barthou, there to receive the king, was also hit — he died within hours. The footage was broadcast worldwide. It's still one of the most recognisable pieces of political violence ever caught on film.

The ripples went far. Alexander had been holding a fragile Yugoslavia together against rising nationalist pressure. Barthou had been one of the few French politicians seriously pushing for collective security against Nazi Germany. Both gone in one afternoon on a Marseille quayside. The assassins had transited through Hungary and Italy with at least tacit official support — the League of Nations was asked to act, and largely didn't.

Stand here with that newsreel in mind. Rare that a single spot compresses so much of what was about to go wrong in Europe.
