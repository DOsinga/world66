The first Armenian refugees arrived in Marseille through the port in the early 1920s, fleeing the 1915 genocide carried out by the Ottoman Empire. Over the following decades, more followed. Today Marseille has the largest Armenian community in France — over 100,000 people — with churches, schools, cultural associations, and a presence across the city's northern and eastern arrondissements that has held through three or four generations.

This monument commemorates that genocide: the systematic mass murder and deportation of Armenians from Anatolia. France has formally recognised it as a crime against humanity. Marseille was among the first French municipalities to take that official position, and the community here has led national commemoration of April 24th for decades. The monument is both a site of grief and a public declaration.

One note: the phrase "génocide de Marseille" occasionally refers to something else entirely — the January 1943 demolition of the neighbourhood north of the Vieux-Port, when German and French police evacuated 20,000 residents and destroyed 1,500 buildings. That event has its own commemoration. This monument belongs to the Armenian memorial tradition.
