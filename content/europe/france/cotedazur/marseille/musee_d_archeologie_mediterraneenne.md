Over 1,000 objects: sarcophagi, mummies, Canopic jars, shabtis, amulets, funerary material spanning three millennia. Marseille assembled most of it during the 19th century through port connections with Egypt and North Africa — direct access that gave the city's collectors material that would have reached Paris only through more institutional, more mediated channels. The result is the largest collection of ancient Egyptian artefacts in France outside the Louvre.

The building is the Vieille Charité — a 17th-century charitable hospice designed by Pierre Puget, Marseille's most celebrated Baroque sculptor. The central chapel, Puget's masterwork, rises from a galleried courtyard. The collections occupy the arcaded galleries on three levels around it. One of the architecturally finest museum settings in France, one of the least visited relative to its quality.

The same complex houses the Musée des Arts Africains, Océaniens, Amérindiens — pre-Columbian, Pacific, and African material across several thousand objects. Single admission covers both. Allow at least half a day.
