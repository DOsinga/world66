The ex-votos are the reason to seek out this museum. Offerings left by worshippers in thanks for miraculous survival — Notre-Dame de la Garde has one of the largest and most varied accumulations in France. Painted panels show storms at sea, shipwrecks, mining accidents, and railway disasters. Hanging alongside them are model ships — brigs, barques, steam packets, naval vessels — presented by sailors and their families. Some are extraordinary pieces of craftsmanship. Plaques in marble and painted tin, crutches, military memorabilia from both world wars: the collection covers seven hundred years of the city asking the Virgin for help and surviving to say thank you.

The museum traces the site's history: a medieval chapel first occupied the summit of the 162-metre limestone outcrop in the thirteenth century. The current Romano-Byzantine basilica was built between 1853 and 1864 to designs by Henri Espérandieu — striped stone courses, a gilded copper Virgin crowning the bell tower. The museum explains how that lavish design was financed and executed.

Entry to the basilica includes the museum. The site itself — "la Bonne Mère" to every Marseillais — commands panoramic views over the city, the Calanques, and the Frioul islands. That alone is worth the climb.
