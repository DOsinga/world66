The MuCEM — Musée des Civilisations de l'Europe et de la Méditerranée — opened in 2013 as the centrepiece of Marseille's year as European Capital of Culture. Architect Rudy Ricciotti designed the J4 building: a cube sheathed in prefabricated concrete filigree that filters Mediterranean light into the galleries within. Roland Carta handled the restoration of the adjacent Fort Saint-Jean, a 17th-century sea fortification that now forms the museum's second wing. The two buildings are linked at roof height by a thin footbridge — one of the most photographed walks in Marseille, with panoramic views over the Vieux-Port and out to the islands.

The permanent collection spans 5,000 years of Mediterranean civilisation, tracing the shared histories of the peoples who have lived around the sea: olive oil and wheat, sacred objects, tools of daily life, and the stories of migration that have shaped both shores. Temporary exhibitions have tackled everything from Islamic art to the cultures of the Maghreb to contemporary Mediterranean photography. The building's rooftop terrace and café are open without a ticket, making the MuCEM a destination even on days when you are not visiting the galleries.

The location is deliberate. The J4 dock once handled passenger traffic from North Africa, and the museum stands at the point where Marseille has always faced outward — across the water rather than inward toward France. It is at the entrance to the Vieux-Port, steps from the medieval quarter of Le Panier, and visible from the sea long before you reach the quay.
