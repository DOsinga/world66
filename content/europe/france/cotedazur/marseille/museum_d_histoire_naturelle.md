The Palais Longchamp went up between 1862 and 1869 to mark something specific: the completion of the Canal de Marseille, which finally brought fresh water to the city from the Durance river. The cascading fountain at the centre of the facade isn't decorative — it's the point. Marseille had been short of water for centuries. The canal was a civic triumph, and they built an extravagant monument to say so.

The Natural History Museum occupies the right wing. Collections cover zoology, palaeontology, geology, and botany, assembled since 1819. The taxidermy galleries are the highlight — animals arranged in 19th-century style, rows of specimens in old cases, the kind of display that has been replaced elsewhere by interactive screens and walk-through reconstructions. It survived here. The fossils from the limestone hills around the city are strong, and the geological collections span Provence to the wider world.

The Musée des Beaux-Arts takes the left wing; both share the formal gardens stretching in front of the palace. It's a tram or bus ride from the Vieux-Port, but pairing the two museums with the architecture and the park makes it one of the better half-days in the city.
