The Muséum d'Histoire Naturelle shares the Palais Longchamp with the Musée des Beaux-Arts, the two institutions occupying the curved wings that flank the palace's central cascade and colonnade. The Palais Longchamp was built between 1862 and 1869 to mark the completion of the Canal de Marseille, which brought fresh water from the Durance river to a city chronically short of it — the fountain at the centre of the facade is a literal celebration of that arrival. The building's theatrical neo-classical composition, set at the end of a long formal garden, made it the symbolic heart of 19th-century Marseille.

Inside the natural history wing, the collections cover zoology, palaeontology, geology, and botany, built up since the museum's founding in 1819. The taxidermy galleries are a highlight: rows of animals arranged in 19th-century style, giving the rooms a pleasantly old-fashioned atmosphere that has survived multiple modernisations. Geological specimens from Provence and across the world sit alongside fossils excavated from the limestone hills around the city. The herbarium and archival collections are among the most significant in southern France.

The museum is a short tram or bus ride from the Vieux-Port, in the residential Longchamp neighbourhood. The park in front of the palace is popular with families, and the combination of architecture, gardens, and two museums in one visit makes the Palais Longchamp one of the most rewarding half-days in Marseille.
