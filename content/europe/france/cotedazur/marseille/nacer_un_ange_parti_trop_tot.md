On a wall in this part of the city, photographs, messages, and faded flowers mark where Nacer is remembered. *Un ange parti trop tôt* — an angel gone too soon. No civic body maintains it. Friends, family, and neighbours have kept it up, replacing candles and flowers over months and years, the way you do when the loss is still too close to let go.

These informal street memorials are common in working-class Marseille, especially in the denser northern arrondissements where tight communities grieve in public. You pass them and become briefly aware of a story you will never fully know — a life, the gap it left, the people still feeling that gap.

Marseille has an instinct for making its interior life visible. The murals commissioned through neighbourhood arts initiatives, the hand-lettered signs above market stalls, the memorial clusters on walls and railings — the city puts things in the open that other places keep private. This is one of those things.
