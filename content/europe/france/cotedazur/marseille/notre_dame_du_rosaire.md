Notre-Dame du Rosaire is a parish church near the Prado in the 8th arrondissement — broad avenues, comfortable apartment blocks, a settled neighbourhood well clear of the old-town tourist circuit. The congregation has been attending Sunday mass here for generations. It is a place of active religious life, not architectural spectacle.

The dedication to the Rosary connects it to the Dominican tradition. The feast of Our Lady of the Rosary falls on 7 October — the date of the Battle of Lepanto in 1571, when a Christian fleet defeated the Ottomans in the Gulf of Corinth. Pope Pius V attributed the victory to the Virgin's intercession through the Rosary, and the date became fixed in the Catholic calendar. Churches with this dedication carry that memory, even when the congregation filling the pews has no particular connection to 16th-century naval warfare.

Inside, expect the visual vocabulary of Rosary devotion: images of the mysteries, a statue of the Virgin with beads, the quiet of a church that people use daily. Come for the neighbourhood, not for a monument.
