Notre-Dame du Rosaire stands in the southern residential fabric of Marseille, in the 8th arrondissement near the Prado. The coordinates place it in a neighbourhood of broad avenues and comfortable apartment buildings, a world away from the dense streets of the old town. The church serves a settled parish community — families, pensioners, the kind of congregation that has attended the same Sunday mass for decades.

The dedication to Our Lady of the Rosary connects the church to a long Dominican tradition. The Rosary — the sequence of prayers meditated on the life of Christ — was championed by the Dominican order from the 13th century, and churches with this dedication often have a Dominican association or reflect the order's influence on popular Marian devotion. The feast of Our Lady of the Rosary on 7 October marks the Battle of Lepanto in 1571, after which Pope Pius V attributed the Christian victory to the intercession of the Virgin through the Rosary.

Inside, visitors will typically find the visual vocabulary of the Rosary devotion — images of the fifteen or twenty mysteries, a statue of the Virgin with the rosary beads, and the quiet atmosphere of a working parish church rather than a tourist monument. It is a place of active religious life rather than architectural spectacle.
