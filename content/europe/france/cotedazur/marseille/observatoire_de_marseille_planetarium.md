The Marseille Observatory was founded in 1702, making it one of the oldest in France. It sits on a hilltop southeast of the city centre — chosen for elevation above the coastal haze, chosen for distance from the worst of the maritime humidity. The 18th-century tower and the domed telescopes added across the 19th century are still there, and the site is a classified historic monument.

The scientific legacy is real. Marseille astronomers contributed to the broader French project of mapping the southern skies, tracking comets and minor planets, and building the systematic positional measurements that characterised Enlightenment science. The institution was part of the world that produced Messier's catalogue — one of the foundational documents of modern astronomy.

Today it's a working research institute attached to Aix-Marseille University, focused on astrophysics, planetary science, and cosmology. The attached planetarium is the public face: projection shows, educational programmes, a way in for anyone who doesn't have a research credential. The historic buildings and original telescopes go on display on open days — check ahead, since access isn't daily.
