The rebuilding after the fire was designed by Gaston Castel, who gave the interior a full Art Deco treatment while retaining the column screen of the original 1787 facade. The main auditorium seats 1,800. The acoustics, shaped by the Art Deco decorative surfaces, are warm rather than precise — suited to the Italian opera that has always been the company's strongest territory.

The Italian connection is not decorative. Marseille's large Italian immigrant community — the city's dominant immigrant group through the 19th and early 20th century — fed both the audience and the cast. Italian opera was genuinely popular music in 19th-century Marseille, not a cultural institution for the bourgeoisie alone. The company's historical strength in Verdi, Puccini, and Donizetti reflects this audience rather than curatorial preference.

The opera house sits on the Rue Molière, one block behind the Vieux-Port, in the cultural district between the port and the Canebière. Performances are scheduled September through June; the summer programming is sparse. Check the schedule before making a special trip.
