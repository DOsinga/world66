The massive Corinthian portico was Michel-Robert Penchaud's way of saying: the law is not to be argued with. Same architect who designed the Porte d'Aix. Same philosophy — build it heavy, build it symmetrical, make sure everyone walking past understands who is in charge. They finished it in 1869.

Since then, every major trial in Marseille has played out inside. Two centuries of organised crime prosecutions, political scandals, high-profile cases. On court days the steps fill with journalists and lawyers, the same scene repeated at every palais de justice in France, but with a specifically Marseillais intensity.

The Cours Pierre Puget just outside is one of the city's overlooked streets. Quieter than the Canebière, it runs north with 17th-century facades on both sides and the Jardin Pierre Puget at the far end. Worth the short detour.
