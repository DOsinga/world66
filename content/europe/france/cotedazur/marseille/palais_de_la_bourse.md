That colonnaded facade at the bottom of the Canebière was finished in 1860, at peak Marseille prosperity. The city was growing faster than anywhere else in France; its merchants were rich enough to build something that looked like a Roman temple and call it a stock exchange.

The Chamber of Commerce still works here. Inside, the Musée de la Marine et de l'Économie keeps ship models from ancient galleys to 20th-century steamships, plus navigational instruments that are among the finest in France. It's consistently undervisited — you'll probably have the rooms to yourself.

Stand on the Canebière and look south toward the Vieux-Port: the Bourse frames the view exactly as its architects intended. Commerce and the sea in one sight line, same as it's been for 160 years.
