The Palais de la Bourse sits at the foot of the Canebière with the authority of a building that was meant to be seen from a distance. Its Second Empire classical facade — columns, pediment, allegorical figures — was completed in 1860 during the most prosperous decade in Marseille's history, when the city was growing faster than any other in France and its merchants were rich enough to build a temple to their own success.

Inside, the Musée de la Marine et de l'Économie de Marseille keeps a collection of ship models and navigational instruments that tells Marseille's commercial history better than any written account. The models range from ancient galleys to 20th-century steamships; the navigational instruments include some of the finest examples in France. It is an undervisited museum, which means you are usually alone with things that deserve attention.

The Chamber of Commerce still occupies the building, which gives the whole place an unusual double life — museum and working institution in the same rooms. Stand on the Canebière and look south toward the Vieux-Port: the Bourse terminates the street's perspective exactly as its architects intended, framing commerce and the sea in a single view.
