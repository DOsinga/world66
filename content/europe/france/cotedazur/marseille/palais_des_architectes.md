The Ordre des Architectes is the statutory body that governs the architectural profession in France. Established by law in 1977, it operates through regional councils across the country; Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur is among the largest, given the density of construction and urbanisation along the southern coast. Only architects registered with the Ordre may sign building permits in France, making it a gatekeeping institution of considerable practical importance. The Marseille premises serve as the regional council's administrative base, hosting meetings, professional events, and the inscription process for new members.

The building itself reflects the identity of its occupant: a structure in a city with a remarkable architectural heritage, from the 17th-century grid of the Vieux-Port district to the post-war reconstruction of the city centre by Fernand Pouillon and, more recently, the transformations of the Euroméditerranée zone. Marseille has been a testing ground for ambitious architecture across successive eras, and the Ordre's local council sits at the centre of those debates and planning processes.

For visitors, the building is not a public museum, but it is visible from the street in the southern residential districts. Architects travelling through Marseille with a professional interest in the city's built environment may find it a useful reference point; the Ordre's website lists regional events and exhibitions open to the public.
