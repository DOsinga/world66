They finished it in the 1860s as the city's first purpose-built art building, then almost immediately moved the collection to the grander Palais Longchamp next door. Classic Marseille: ambitious, then immediately more ambitious.

The exterior is restrained — sober Second Empire, well-cut stone, careful proportions rather than grand gestures. Where the Longchamp has fountains and colonnades, this one has dignity. Worth a pause if you're heading to the Longchamp anyway; it's right there.

What you're looking at is mid-19th-century Marseille trying very hard to be taken seriously as a European city. The cultural spending was as deliberate as any port investment.
