The Palais des Arts was built in the 1860s as Marseille's first dedicated fine arts building, commissioned alongside the grander Palais Longchamp to give the city an institutional cultural presence appropriate to its ambitions as France's second port. It sits adjacent to the Longchamp complex, the two structures sharing a period and a civic purpose if not a style. Where the Longchamp is monumental and theatrical, with its cascading fountains and colonnaded wings, the Palais des Arts is restrained — a sober Second Empire volume given over to function.

For a period following its completion the building housed the municipal art collection, including the paintings and sculptures that would eventually migrate to the Musée des Beaux-Arts installed in the northern wing of the Palais Longchamp. The Palais des Arts thus served a transitional role in the city's cultural life: the first building in Marseille purpose-built to house art, it was superseded almost immediately by a more prestigious neighbour. The experience of looking at paintings here before the Longchamp museum opened would have felt provisional — a holding pattern — though the building itself was serious enough in its intentions.

The exterior is worth a pause even if you are heading to the Longchamp. The proportions are careful rather than grand, the stone well cut, the detailing restrained in the manner of official French architecture in the years of Haussmann and the grandes travaux. It stands as evidence of Marseille's mid-19th-century determination to be taken seriously as a European city, a determination expressed as much in the commissioning of cultural infrastructure as in the rebuilding of the port.
