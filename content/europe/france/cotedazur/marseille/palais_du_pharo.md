Standing on the Pharo headland, you get a view that no architect could have designed: the Vieux-Port below you, the Château d'If floating in the bay, Notre-Dame de la Garde crowning the hill behind the city, and open sea to the south. The building itself is almost secondary to the geography it was built to command.

Napoleon III commissioned the palace in 1858 as a seaside retreat for Empress Eugénie. The Second Empire architects delivered it in 1870 — just in time for the empire to collapse. Neither Napoleon nor Eugénie ever stayed here. In 1906 the Third Republic gave the building to the city of Marseille, a grand gesture of republican appropriation that converted imperial ambition into civic use. It has been a conference centre ever since.

Come for the terraces rather than the interior. Walk the full perimeter of the headland in the late afternoon and you will understand, better than any tour guide can explain, why Greek colonists chose this particular arrangement of water and rock to found Massalia 2,600 years ago.
