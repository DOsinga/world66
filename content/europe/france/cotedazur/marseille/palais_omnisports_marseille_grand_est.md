The Palais Omnisports Marseille Grand-Est stands as the main indoor arena for the eastern arrondissements, a part of the city that sits well beyond the tourist circuits concentrated around the Vieux-Port and the coastal corniche. "Omnisports" in French municipal naming means exactly what it says: a single facility engineered to shift between basketball, handball, boxing, gymnastics, judo, and other indoor disciplines without any one sport being given permanent priority. This flexibility makes it the hub of organised sport for a large residential population that has historically had fewer cultural and leisure facilities than the city's more central districts.

Basketball and handball claim the most regular calendar at arenas of this type across France's major cities, and Marseille is no exception. Club sides from the eastern neighbourhoods use the Palais for home matches, and the arena's capacity means it can host regional and national-level competitions that smaller club halls cannot accommodate. The crowd at a basketball derby or a handball cup tie here tends to be vocal, knowledgeable, and partisan — sport in these quarters is not a passive entertainment but a serious local attachment.

The arena also serves as a concert and show venue when the sporting calendar allows, and its eastern location means it occasionally attracts acts and events that would bypass a venue closer to the city centre. For residents of the 13th, 14th, and surrounding arrondissements, the Palais Omnisports is the neighbourhood's answer to the question of where to spend a Friday night when you do not want to trek across the city.
