The Phocaeans sailed from the Aegean coast of present-day Turkey and founded Massalia around 600 BC. The legend says the Greek leader Protis won the hand of a local chief's daughter — sealing the alliance that let the colony take root at the mouth of the Lacydon creek, now the Old Port. Marseille has been telling that story for two and a half millennia.

The Palais Phocéen borrows the name, which is straightforward civic pride. In French municipal vocabulary, a "palais" is a hall grand enough for concerts, shows, and large events — not a palace, just somewhere with serious purpose.

The founding myth is worth pausing on. For 2,600 years this has been where worlds meet: Greek and Ligurian, then Roman and Gallic, then French and North African. Marseille doesn't hide from that history. It names things after it.
