Henri-Jacques Espérandieu designed it between 1864 and 1874 — the same architect who gave the city Notre-Dame de la Garde and the Palais Longchamp. He knew how to handle Marseille's limestone and its light. The Second Empire facade on Place Carli has the same confident weight as his other buildings, but more restrained: this was always meant to be a working institution, not a monument.

Inside: the School of Fine Arts and the Municipal Library. The library is worth going in for — good reading rooms, local collections, and a location right in the middle of the old centre.

The square itself is one of those quiet central spots that feels discovered rather than signposted.
