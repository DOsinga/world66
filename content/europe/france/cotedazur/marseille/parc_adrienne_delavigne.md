They filled in the quarry and planted pines in the 1960s. Now it's boules courts, benches, and Mediterranean scrub at the southern edge of the 8th arrondissement — right where the city stops and the Calanques begin.

That matters: the Sentier du Président leaves from here, heading south into limestone ridge country toward Goudes and Callelongue. Five minutes from the park and you're in terrain that feels genuinely remote — wild rosemary, white rock, silence — despite being entirely inside the city's limits.

The park itself asks nothing of you. Neighbourhood yard sales on weekends, elderly Marseillais at pétanque in the afternoon, the occasional serious hiker loading a pack before heading in. A useful place to know if you want the calanques without starting from the car parks.
