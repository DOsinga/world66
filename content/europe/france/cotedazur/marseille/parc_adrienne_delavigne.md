Parc Adrienne Delavigne sits at the southern edge of the 8th arrondissement on the Chemin de Goudes, where the built city meets the beginning of the Parc National des Calanques. The park occupies ground that was once a quarry; the site was filled and planted in the 1960s and 1970s, converting an industrial scar into a neighbourhood green space. Its modest scale — a boules court, benches, lawns shaded by Mediterranean pines — belies its position as one of the main departure points for the Sentier du Président, which leads into the calanque landscape toward Goudes and Callelongue.

The Marseilleveyre departmental domain, of which this park forms a part, encompasses garrigue hillsides, limestone ridges, and coastal paths that stretch southward from here toward the sea. Walkers setting out from the park pass quickly into terrain that feels remote despite being minutes from the city: wild rosemary, holm oak, and the jagged white rock that characterises the calanques. The park itself is calmer — a place where the local neighbourhood holds its yard sales and elderly residents pass the afternoon at pétanque.

Adrienne Delavigne, for whom the park is named, was a local figure whose memory is kept in the district name rather than in the wider city's records. The park asks nothing of the visitor in the way of cultural preparation: it is simply an honest piece of neighbourhood green space, useful in itself and doubly useful as the gateway it becomes when you follow the path south into the calanques.
