Paul Barlatier's father ran *Le Sémaphore*, one of Marseille's leading newspapers, and bought this land on the edge of Château Gombert. Paul was obsessed with ancient Greece. In the 1910s he commissioned a replica of the Athens theatre — garrigue stone, open-air, the full thing — designed by architect Jean Boët.

By 1916 it was a film studio. The Phocéa Film studios ran from this garden, and by 1919 French film journals were calling the facility a model of modern production. Marseille's brief attempt at its own Hollywood ended quietly. The studios are long gone.

The theatre shell survives. Its form is still legible — stage, semicircular seating area — stripped of its purpose but not its presence. The rest of the park has a pond, a skate bowl, sports pitches, and cycle paths. It sits inside the Château Gombert technology park, surrounded by university labs and high-tech firms, which makes the ruined theatre stranger and better.
