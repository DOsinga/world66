Parc Balnéaire du Prado stretches along the Mediterranean shore south of the city centre, occupying a long strip of reclaimed land created from rubble generated during the construction of Marseille's metro in the 1970s. The land was pushed into the sea and then shaped into a coastal park, which is an unusual origin but an effective one: the result is a wide, breezy expanse of grass, paths, and artificial rocky coves that run parallel to the Prado beaches.

The park is the backbone of Marseille's main outdoor leisure district. Families come here on summer evenings when the beaches close, joggers use the paths year-round, and the grassy areas fill with picnickers on weekends. A copy of Michelangelo's *David* stands near the northern end — a replica of the Florence original, presented to the city — and serves as a low-key landmark beside the roundabout where the beach strip begins.

The surrounding neighbourhood is the 8th arrondissement's seafront, bordered inland by the Prado avenue and the large residential blocks of Bonneveine to the south. The park itself is free, unfenced, and always open. On hot summer days it can be very busy; in winter it empties almost entirely, though the walks remain pleasant with views across the bay toward the Frioul islands.
