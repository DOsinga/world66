Parc Billoux is in the 13th arrondissement, in the northern residential spread of Marseille beyond the old centre. It is a local park serving the surrounding blocks: grass, shade trees, a children's area, pétanque. The 13th is a mixed district, partly working-class residential, partly industrial on its edges, and a park like this is a straightforward piece of neighbourhood infrastructure.

The park is named after François Billoux (1903–1978), who was born in Saint-Romain-de-Popey but built his political career in Marseille and the surrounding region. Billoux was a senior figure in the French Communist Party for decades, served in the French government during the Liberation period in the mid-1940s, and held ministerial posts under de Gaulle's provisional government. He was one of the Communist ministers who left government when the Cold War deepened and the PCF was pushed out of coalition politics in 1947. In the postwar decades he remained a prominent party figure. Naming public spaces after Communist politicians was a common municipal practice in cities where the party held power, and Marseille — which had long stretches of left-wing governance — has several such commemorations.

The park is nothing elaborate: trees, paths, open ground. But it is a genuine amenity in a part of the city that is short on green space, and on a warm afternoon it fills with residents who don't have gardens of their own.
