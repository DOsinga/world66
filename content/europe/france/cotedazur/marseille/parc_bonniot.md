Parc Bonniot fills a 6,000-square-metre wedge between the boulevard and the Impasse Bonniot in the Saint-Julien quarter of the 12th arrondissement. The park is named for the street and family associated with this part of the eastern hills — Rue Fernand Charpin nearby commemorates the Marseillais comedian of the same name — and its character is emphatically that of a local amenity: mature trees, decent shade, and space for people of different ages to use without getting in each other's way.

The tree stock is the park's chief distinction: cedars, pines, linden trees, and oaks, one of which has stood for more than a century and whose canopy dominates the park's southern end. These are trees that were planted long before the surrounding neighbourhood reached its present density, and their scale gives Bonniot a weight that newer parks in the same district lack. The 12th arrondissement is a largely residential area on the plateau between the Huveaune valley to the south and the hills toward Allauch, and the park serves the families and older residents of Saint-Julien as a reliable outdoor room.

Facilities are straightforward: children's play equipment, a boules court, a small football pitch for teenagers, and paths wide enough for a pushchair or a bicycle. The park is managed by the mairie of the 11th and 12th arrondissements and maintained to a standard that reflects its role as everyday infrastructure rather than a destination. Bonniot is not a park anyone visits from across the city, but for those who live in Saint-Julien it performs exactly the function a neighbourhood park should.
