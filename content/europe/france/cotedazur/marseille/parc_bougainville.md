Parc Bougainville sits in the 3rd arrondissement, one of Marseille's more densely populated northern districts. The park is a functional green space — lawns, plane trees, a playground, pétanque courts — serving the surrounding residential streets rather than drawing visitors from further afield. It is the kind of park that matters to people who live within a ten-minute walk of it.

The park is named after Louis Antoine de Bougainville (1729–1811), the French navigator who led the first French circumnavigation of the globe between 1766 and 1769. He is also the namesake of the bougainvillea plant, which his expedition's botanist collected in South America and brought back to Europe. Bougainville died an admiral and a count, and many French public spaces bear his name. Fittingly, the climbing plant that bears his name thrives in Marseille's climate, flowering in vivid purples and pinks across the city's walls and balconies.

The 3rd arrondissement is a working-class district with a strong North African and Comorian community. The park is well-used in the afternoons and evenings, particularly by families from the surrounding tower blocks. It is not a destination in itself, but on a walk through this part of the city it is a welcome open patch in an otherwise built-up neighbourhood.
