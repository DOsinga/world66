Parc Central de Bonneveine is the principal park of the Bonneveine neighbourhood, a residential area in the southern part of Marseille's 8th arrondissement, roughly halfway between the city centre and the Calanques. The park is a shaded rectangle of lawns and tall trees — mostly plane trees and pines — with the familiar amenities of a well-maintained French municipal garden: paths, benches, a play area, and pétanque terrain where regulars gather in the late afternoon.

Bonneveine is a quiet, largely residential quarter populated by families who came here for proximity to the Prado beaches and relative distance from the noise of the centre. The Prado seafront is less than ten minutes' walk to the west, and on summer mornings residents often move between the park and the beach in the same outing. The park fills most on weekday afternoons when school is out and on weekend mornings before the heat sets in.

There is nothing exceptional about Parc Central de Bonneveine — it is exactly what its name says, the central park of its neighbourhood — but it is a genuine, well-used space. The tall plane trees provide shade that is genuinely valuable during Marseille summers when temperatures regularly exceed 30°C, and the park is one of those quiet civic assets that does its job without requiring any attention.
