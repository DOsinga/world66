Parc de Corbière lies in the hills of northwestern Marseille, in the territory of the 16th arrondissement that stretches between the industrial port and the coastal village of L'Estaque. The name likely derives from a local toponym — *corbière* can refer to a rocky outcrop or a place associated with crows (*corbeaux*) in Provençal — and the landscape here has the rough, limestone character common to the hills overlooking the Gulf of Lion.

L'Estaque, a short distance away, became famous in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries as a painting ground for Cézanne, Braque, and others drawn by the quality of light and the dramatic relationship between the ochre rooftops, the blue sea, and the bare white rock. The area around Parc de Corbière shares something of that visual character, though it is primarily a recreational space for residents of the surrounding neighbourhood rather than a scenic attraction in its own right.

The park offers shade and open space in a part of the city otherwise defined by port industry and dense suburban housing. It serves the local community without fanfare, and visitors exploring the L'Estaque area — best known for its seafood restaurants and its painters' legacy — may pass through it on foot.
