*Espérance* means hope. The name has the ring of a postwar civic gesture — the kind of optimistic christening French neighbourhoods gave their parks in the decades when creating public green space felt like a statement of intent.

It's small: plane trees, gravel, benches. Pétanque players arrive early and claim the shaded corners. At lunchtime, workers from the surrounding blocks take their hour here. In the afternoon, children from the nearby buildings.

No reason to seek it out from across the city. But the central location means you might already be nearby — and it delivers on the name. A moment of relief and human scale in a dense part of Marseille.
