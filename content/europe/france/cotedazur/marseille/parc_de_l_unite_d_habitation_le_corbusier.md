The Parc de l'Unité d'habitation Le Corbusier is not an afterthought to the famous building it surrounds: it was designed as a component of it. When Le Corbusier's Cité Radieuse was built between 1947 and 1952 on the Boulevard Michelet in the 8th arrondissement, the landscaping of the 2.8-hectare site was begun in October 1951 and completed for the building's inauguration on 14 October 1952. The park's layout was organised along the diagonal axis of the housing block, with paths creating a sequence of viewpoints from which the building's pilotis and façade could be read properly — a designed promenade as much as a recreational space.

The park divides into two distinct zones. On the boulevard side, the urban face presents grassed areas and rest spaces against the backdrop of the building's ground-level pilotis, which lift the entire eighteen-storey block off the ground and allow park and city to flow beneath it. At the rear, Le Corbusier's team created an artificial topography: large slopes and two planted hills, the kind of manipulated landscape that allows the building's residents to feel sheltered from the city while remaining within it. The western zone was developed for outdoor community activities — sports fields and a children's garden, reflecting the Cité Radieuse's founding principle that a single building should supply everything needed for collective life.

The Cité Radieuse is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and the park received its own classification as a historic monument in December 2023, formally recognising the landscape as inseparable from the architecture. Visiting the park without entering the building is a legitimate experience: standing beneath the pilotis, with the block suspended above and the park extending in both directions, the audacity of the project — housing as a vertical city set free from the ground — becomes physically legible.
