The climb is steep in places and the path is rough underfoot. Worth it. From the upper reaches of this limestone ridge, the 9th arrondissement spreads below and the sea opens up. On a clear day — which is most days — you can pick out the Archipel de Riou and the silhouette of Île de Jarre on the horizon.

The terrain is pure garrigue: limestone punching through thin soil, scrub oak, rosemary, the odd umbrella pine. It smells like the countryside five minutes from the neighbourhood. Almost no tourists come here — the Calanques pull them all south. That's exactly the appeal.
