Parc de la Germaine is a recent addition to the city's green infrastructure, opened in the Saint-Tronc quarter of the 10th arrondissement above the Boulevard Urbain Sud. The park sits on roughly one hectare between a lycée and the Haddad roundabout — a location that underscores its context: this is a dense residential neighbourhood that, until the park's creation, had little public green space to speak of. The project, led by the Metropolis of Aix-Marseille-Provence and co-financed by the Bouches-du-Rhône department at a cost of approximately three million euros, was delivered after fourteen months of work and planted with 127 new trees.

The name *La Germaine* refers to the area's local toponym rather than to any individual — the kind of place-name embedded in the neighbourhood's daily language long before any official designation. The park creates a green connection along what had been an urban motorway-scale piece of infrastructure, threading a strip of lawn, play equipment, and leisure furniture through territory otherwise dominated by road and retail. The Auchan Saint-Loup supermarket at the park's edge gives a sense of the surrounding urban grain.

As a park, Germaine is honest about what it is: a small urban green space doing necessary work in a neighbourhood that needed it. Play areas for young children, dry public toilets, and benches under the new tree canopy are its offer. It is grouped with the adjacent Parc Haddad under the title *Parcs B.U.S.* — Boulevard Urbain Sud — a branding that emphasises their shared role as a green seam stitched into the city's fastest road corridor. Give the trees twenty years and this strip will feel more established than it does today.
