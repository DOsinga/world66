The name is centuries old — Provençal for an earthenware jar, describing the limestone hollow where the park sits. This is the southern edge of the city, where Marseille gradually stops being a city. The vegetation shifts: rosemary, cistus, kermes oak, the same scrubby mix you'll find in the Calanques just beyond.

Most visitors blow straight through chasing the sea. Local walkers, trail runners, dogs on long leads — that's the crowd. The light is fierce and the shadows cast by the pines are sharp-edged. It's a buffer zone, and it feels like one.
