L'Estaque is where Cézanne came to paint — the harbour, the chimneys, the limestone hills sliding into the industrial waterfront. That friction between wild coast and working port is still the defining quality of this northern corner of Marseille. Parc de la Minerve sits in the middle of it, in the 15th and 16th arrondissements where the city and the sea-trade have always been inseparable.

The park itself is practical rather than beautiful. Grass, shade trees, paths worn smooth by the people who actually live nearby. On quiet afternoons you can hear the port — a low industrial hum in the background. If you're already out here exploring L'Estaque's old fishing quarter and its Cézanne associations, the park makes a natural shaded pause before heading back.
