Pelouque is Provençal for a soft, grassy or mossy patch — the kind of small fertile hollow that stood out in the rocky, sun-baked northern hills. The name predates the park by a long time. This is the territory near L'Estaque, where Cézanne and Braque both came to paint the industrial coast meeting the limestone massif. The painters are long gone; the landscape is still striking.

The park sits high enough that on a clear day you can see the Étang de Berre glinting to the northwest. The northern arrondissements are working-class and diverse, and green space here is valued precisely because there's less of it than in the wealthy south. From up here, the Canebière feels like a different city.
