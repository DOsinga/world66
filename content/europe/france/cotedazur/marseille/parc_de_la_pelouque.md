Parc de la Pelouque takes its name from the Provençal word for a soft, grassy or mossy piece of ground — the kind of small fertile hollow that stood out in the rocky, sun-baked landscape of the northern Marseille hills. The park sits in the upper northern reaches of the city, in the territory of the 15th or 16th arrondissement, not far from the L'Estaque quarter where Cézanne and Braque once came to paint the industrial coast meeting the limestone massif.

This is a part of Marseille that feels separate from the city's tourist circuits. The northern arrondissements are working-class and diverse, home to long-established communities and newer arrivals alike. Green space in this quarter is valued precisely because it is not as plentiful as in the wealthier southern districts. Parc de la Pelouque serves as a local breathing space — somewhere for children to run and for adults to sit outside without going far.

The terrain is characteristically hilly, and the park's elevated position means the surrounding landscape, a mix of rooftops, scrubland and the distant glimmer of the Étang de Berre on clear days, is part of the visit even if the park itself is small. It is the kind of place that reveals a different, quieter side of Marseille, far from the noise of the Canebière.
