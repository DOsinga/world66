Louis XVI ordered the arch in 1784. It wasn't finished until 1839 — 55 years, two revolutions, an empire, and a restored monarchy later. It celebrates French military victories that were already fading from living memory by the time the stonework was done. The neighbourhood has moved on even further since then.

The quarter here is one of the most ethnically mixed in Marseille: North African, West African, Comorian communities, garment traders, small shops, the smell of flatbread from the surrounding boulangeries. The garden at the arch's feet is more widened pavement than park, but it gives the monument room to breathe. Benches fill fast on warm evenings. From the south, the arch frames the view back toward the Canebière — the old gateway to the road north, now just a threshold in a neighbourhood going about its business.
