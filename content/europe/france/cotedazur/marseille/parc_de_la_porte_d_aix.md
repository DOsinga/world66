Parc de la Porte d'Aix occupies the open ground around the Arc de Triomphe de Marseille, the neo-classical arch that stands at the top of the Cours Belsunce in the 1st arrondissement. The arch was begun in 1784 on the orders of Louis XVI to commemorate French military victories, but construction stalled and the monument was not completed until 1839 — giving it a slightly incongruous presence in a neighbourhood that by then had moved on. The garden around it is modest, more a widened pavement than a park, but it gives the arch room to be seen.

The surrounding quarter is one of the most ethnically mixed in Marseille, with North African, West African, and Comorian communities well established along the streets leading down toward the old city. The Porte d'Aix neighbourhood was long associated with the garment trade and with modest commerce, and it retains that texture — small shops, busy pavements, the smell of flatbread from the boulangeries that line the surrounding streets. Arriving at the garden from the south, the arch frames a view back toward the Canebière.

The park is used by residents of the dense surrounding streets as a place to pause. The benches fill quickly on warm evenings. It is not a destination in the usual sense, but it marks an important threshold: the point where Marseille's ancient city gave way to the road north, and where the city today feels most thoroughly itself.
