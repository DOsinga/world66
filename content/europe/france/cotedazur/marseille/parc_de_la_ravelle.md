The 11th arrondissement bleeds into Plan-de-Cuques up here, the built fabric thinning out against limestone garrigue. Streets wind uphill, open ground appears between the residential blocks, and the city quietly admits it has an edge. Parc de la Ravelle sits in that zone.

Walkers heading toward the Massif de l'Étoile pass through. The views open northward toward the hills. No tourist infrastructure, no particular monument — just the northeastern corner of Marseille, which turns out to be a quieter and more unhurried place than the postcards suggest.
