The park shares its name with one of the smallest fishing hamlets on the French coast — La Redonne, a cove barely wide enough for its cluster of coloured boats, wedged between white limestone cliffs west of Marseille. The park sits at the exact point where the city's southern suburbs run out of steam.

From here, the Calanques National Park takes over. Pine woodland, rocky paths, limestone headlands — the scenery that makes this coastline famous starts essentially at this address. The park is the threshold, not the destination.

If you want a reason to be here, it's that: you're at the edge of one of France's most spectacular coastlines, and you can walk directly from the grass into it.
