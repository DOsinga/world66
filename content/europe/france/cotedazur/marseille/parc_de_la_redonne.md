Parc de la Redonne shares its name with one of the smallest and most secluded fishing hamlets on the coast between Marseille and Martigues — La Redonne, a cove barely wide enough for its cluster of colourful boats, wedged between white limestone cliffs. The park's coordinates sit in the southern reaches of Marseille, in the 8th or 9th arrondissement territory where the city meets the coastal garrigue and the Calanques National Park begins to assert itself.

This is the greener, hillier, more affluent southern edge of Marseille, where villas behind garden walls give way to pine woodland and paths that lead down to rocky inlets. A park here feels natural — the boundary between built city and protected coastline is porous in this quarter, and green space tends to be well-maintained and popular with runners, dog-walkers, and families from the nearby residential streets.

The name evokes something of the wild coast: if you follow it in spirit rather than literally, La Redonne is a reminder that remarkable scenery begins very close to where Marseille's southern suburbs end. The park itself may be small, but it sits at the threshold of one of France's most spectacular natural coastlines, and that proximity is worth something.
