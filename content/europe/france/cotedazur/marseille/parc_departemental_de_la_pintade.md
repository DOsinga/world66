Tucked into the hills of the 11th and 12th arrondissements, the Parc départemental de la Pintade is one of the larger green spaces in eastern Marseille — managed not by the city but by the Bouches-du-Rhône department, which gives it a slightly wilder, less manicured feel than the formal municipal parks closer to the centre. The name, *pintade*, means guinea fowl in French, and local tradition holds that the birds were once raised on these slopes, though they have long since ceded the territory to joggers and dog walkers.

The park sits at the edge of the garrigue, and on its upper reaches the vegetation shifts from mown grass to scrubby holm oak and rosemary thicket. Paths wind through the terrain rather than cutting straight lines, rewarding those who explore with views across the tiled rooftops of the surrounding quartiers and, on clear days, toward the limestone ridges that ring the city to the east. It is an unhurried place, popular with local families on weekend afternoons.

Because it is departmental rather than municipal, the Pintade sees fewer visitors than headline parks like the Parc Borély and offers something closer to a genuine edge-of-city ramble. Bring sturdy shoes if you intend to leave the central lawns — the upper paths can be rough — and come in spring when the surrounding scrub blooms yellow with broom.
