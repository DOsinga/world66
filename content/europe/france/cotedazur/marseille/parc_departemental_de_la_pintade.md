The name translates as guinea fowl, and yes, they actually raised them on these slopes. That was a while ago. Today the Pintade is one of eastern Marseille's larger green spaces — not run by the city but by the Bouches-du-Rhône department, which means it gets managed a little less tidily and feels more like real landscape than manicured parkland.

Climb the upper paths and the vegetation shifts from mown grass to scrubby holm oak and rosemary. On clear days you get views across the tiled rooftops toward the limestone ridges east of the city. It's a genuine garrigue edge-of-city ramble rather than a park visit.

Come in spring when the broom goes yellow. Bring shoes that can handle uneven ground — the upper trails earn it.
