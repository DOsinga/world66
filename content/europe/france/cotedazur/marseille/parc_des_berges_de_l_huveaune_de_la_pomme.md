Most people in Marseille forget the Huveaune exists. It rises in the Sainte-Baume massif sixty kilometres east, cuts through limestone hills, and reaches the sea just beyond the city's edge. Here at La Pomme, in the 11th arrondissement, a linear park traces its banks through a corridor of poplars and willows that feels surprisingly green amid the surrounding suburbs.

It's a path more than a park. You walk along the water rather than into a cleared space, with the river as your constant companion — quick after rain, slow and olive-coloured in summer. Fishermen claim the shaded bends in the early morning; families take the grassy verges in the afternoon.

La Pomme means apple — the quartier is quiet and residential in exactly the way the name suggests. For cyclists or walkers cutting through eastern Marseille, these berges are the obvious green thread through what would otherwise be an ordinary suburban stretch.
