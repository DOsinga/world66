Bruyère is heather, and heather means garrigue — that low, fragrant scrubland that carpets the limestone hills around Marseille. The park sits right at the point where the residential fabric of eastern Marseille loses confidence and the hillside scrub starts winning.

It's a neighbourhood park, not a grand garden. But it has the seasonal rhythm of the surrounding landscape: heather pink and mauve in autumn, broom yellow in spring, everything bleached towards beige in the July heat. Locals who know it come at the right moments.

If you're walking the hills east of the city and need somewhere to stop under a stone pine, this is the spot. Not worth a detour, but worth knowing about.
