The Franciscan Missionary Sisters of Mary founded their order in India in 1877 and established themselves here in the 7th arrondissement. When the convent eventually passed to the city, the grounds became a public garden — but the enclosure held. The walls are still there, old trees grown to considerable height, and a stillness that doesn't quite match the streets outside.

This quarter of Marseille is dense with 19th-century apartment buildings between the Vieux-Port and the Corniche. Convents occupied many of the larger plots back then. This is one of the few where the enclosure survived as a public amenity rather than getting broken up for housing.

Locals use it: children, retirees, anyone who finds the more exposed parks too loud. The garden doesn't announce itself — it rewards the people who find it.
