Parc des Sœurs franciscaines missionnaires de Marie takes its name from the Franciscan Missionary Sisters of Mary, an order founded in India in 1877 that established a presence in Marseille and maintained a convent on this site in the 7th arrondissement. The grounds were eventually transferred to the city and opened as a public park, retaining the enclosed, inward-looking character of a religious garden — walls on several sides, old trees grown to considerable height, and a quiet that stands apart from the streets outside.

The 7th arrondissement is one of the city's older residential quarters, its streets of nineteenth-century apartment buildings running between the Vieux-Port and the Corniche. Convents and religious institutions once occupied many of the larger plots in such neighbourhoods, their walled grounds offering a degree of seclusion that the streets themselves could not provide. This park is one of the few where that enclosure survives as a public amenity rather than being broken up for housing.

The garden is used primarily by local residents — families with children, older people taking the air, those who find the more exposed parks too crowded or noisy. There are benches under the trees and a modest lawn. It is not a garden that announces itself, but one that rewards those who find it: a small enclosure of calm in a dense and busy part of the city.
