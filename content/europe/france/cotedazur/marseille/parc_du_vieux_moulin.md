The name Vieux Moulin — Old Mill — points to a history that has otherwise almost entirely vanished from the landscape. Marseille's limestone hills were once home to dozens of windmills that ground grain for the city, powered by the mistral, the powerful north-westerly wind that scours the coast for days at a stretch. By the nineteenth century most had fallen out of use as industrial milling took over, and the towers that survived were either repurposed or demolished. The park preserves the place-name where one once stood, even if the mill itself is long gone.

The park sits in a residential quarter on the eastern side of the city, closer to the 11th and 12th arrondissements than to the centre, in a part of Marseille that most visitors never reach. It is modest in scale — a strip of grass and trees in a neighbourhood of apartment blocks — but serves the local community as a reliable green space in an area where gardens are at a premium.

There is no grand monument or formal feature here; the park's interest is mainly in what it represents — a minor geographic memory embedded in the street-level fabric of the city. Those interested in Marseille's pre-industrial history might enjoy the quiet nod to an era when the city's ridgelines were marked by sails turning in the wind rather than antenna masts.
