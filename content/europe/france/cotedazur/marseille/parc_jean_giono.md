Jean Giono spent his life writing about the Provençal countryside — peasants, the mistral, hill villages, the particular stubbornness of people who live close to the earth. He was from Manosque, 80 km north, and his novels barely mention Marseille. So naming a city park after him has a certain wry quality.

The park itself is modest: lawns, benches, Mediterranean plantings on the hills of the Saint-Antoine quarter. From the higher ground you can see the limestone ridges rimming the north of the city — scrubland that tips, eventually, toward the Luberon country Giono actually wrote about. A faint connection, at least.

If you want the real Giono, the detour is to Manosque, where his house is now a museum. The park is fine for a lunchtime walk and a bit of quiet.
