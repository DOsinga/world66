Parc Jean Giono sits in the hilly northern reaches of Marseille, in the Saint-Antoine quarter, named in honour of Jean Giono (1895–1970) — the novelist who did more than anyone to put Haute-Provence on the literary map. Giono was born in Manosque, some 80 km north of here, and his "Pan trilogy" (*Colline*, *Regain*, *Un de Baumugnes*) conjures a Provençal countryside of peasants, mistral wind, and earthy mysticism that feels a world away from the city below. Naming a Marseille park after him is a quiet act of regional pride.

The park itself is a modest neighbourhood green space — lawns, a few benches, some Mediterranean plantings — the kind of place locals use for a lunchtime walk rather than a destination visit. From its higher points you get glimpses toward the limestone hills that Giono might have recognized: the *garrigue* scrubland that backdrop northern Marseille and reach all the way toward his beloved Luberon.

If you are making your way through the 15th or 16th arrondissement and want a few minutes of quiet, the park offers exactly that. For a deeper engagement with Giono, the detour is to Manosque itself, where his house is now a museum.
