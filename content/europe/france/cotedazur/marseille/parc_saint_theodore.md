Parc Saint-Théodore takes its name from the surrounding neighbourhood, one of the quieter residential pockets in Marseille's 16th arrondissement. Saint-Théodore sits near the western edge of northern Marseille, not far from L'Estaque — the fishing village turned industrial suburb where Cézanne and Braque came to paint, drawn by its particular quality of light and the geometry of its rooftops against the sea.

The park is a local green space rather than a grand municipal garden, set among the modest streets that characterize this part of the city. The 16th arrondissement is one of Marseille's least visited by outsiders, despite its proximity to the dramatic Côte Bleue coastline just beyond the city limits — limestone cliffs and small pebbly coves running west toward Martigues.

If you are exploring L'Estaque, it is worth knowing that the neighbourhood around Parc Saint-Théodore is close enough to walk through on your way. The park itself offers shade and a bench; the real reward in this corner of the city is the coastline a short distance further.
