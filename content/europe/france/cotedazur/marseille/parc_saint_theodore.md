The park itself is a local green space — shade, a bench, nothing dramatic. But the reason to be in this corner of the 16th arrondissement is L'Estaque, a short walk west. That fishing village turned industrial suburb is where Cézanne came in the 1870s and 1880s, drawn by the particular light and the hard geometry of rooftops against water. Braque followed a generation later and moved even further toward abstraction. The views that produced Cubism are still there.

Parc Saint-Théodore sits among the modest residential streets between Saint-Théodore and L'Estaque. The 16th arrondissement is one of Marseille's least visited from outside, which is surprising given that the Côte Bleue — limestone cliffs and pebbly coves running west toward Martigues — starts right here.

Use the park for a break. Then walk to L'Estaque and figure out what Cézanne was looking at.
