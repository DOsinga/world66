Parc Soledad stands in the southeastern part of Marseille, in the 11th arrondissement, where the city starts to loosen its urban grip and give way to the residential fringes before the Calanques. The name *Soledad* — shared between Spanish and Provençal, meaning solitude — is unusual for a Marseille park, and may reflect the area's older identity as a quieter edge of the city, or simply the temperament of whoever named it.

The park occupies flat ground in what is now a well-settled neighbourhood. It is a place for morning walks and schoolchildren rather than tourists: a practical green space among streets of houses and apartment blocks. The solitude the name evokes has been mostly domesticated.

That said, the southeastern corner of Marseille sits close to the northern approaches of the Calanques National Park, and a short drive from here brings you to the trailheads at La Barasse or the coast at Les Goudes. Parc Soledad is a neighbourhood aside — pleasant enough, but the real draw in this part of the city lies further south, where the limestone meets the sea.
