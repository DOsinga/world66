Val Plan sits in the eastern reaches of Marseille, in the working residential arrondissements beyond the tourist circuits. The park occupies a natural depression — a shallow valley whose topography was awkward enough to stay undeveloped while everything around it was built over in the postwar decades. Plane trees shade the main paths. The lower section stays noticeably damper than the bone-dry slopes nearby, which in July matters.

The surrounding neighbourhood is largely postwar social housing. The park is genuinely used: football on the open ground, elderly residents on the benches, dogs on the loose paths around the perimeter. Not manicured. Honestly occupied.

In summer the valley's shade and occasional breeze offer real relief. It's the kind of park that tells you something true about a city — that most of it is lived in, not visited.
