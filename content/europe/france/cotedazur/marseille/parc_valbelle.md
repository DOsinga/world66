The Valbelles were a serious Provençal dynasty — magistrates and landowners whose reach extended from Aix to the Marseille coastline through the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries. Joseph-Alphonse de Valbelle built a celebrated garden at Tourves and was known as a patron of landscape design. The family's estates shaped this part of the coast long before it became suburban.

Whether the park preserves a direct territorial link to that estate or just inherited the toponym is unclear. Either way, the name carries weight in a local context that most visitors don't have.

The park itself is a neighbourhood green in the calmer southern arrondissements — modest size, established trees, close to the coastal paths. On windy days, the proximity to the calanques gives even this ordinary space a faint salt-and-pine quality. Shade and a bench, which in Marseille's summer is not a small thing.
