Parc Valmer climbs the hillside above the southern Corniche in the 7th arrondissement, a terraced garden attached to the Villa Valmer, a nineteenth-century private house that now serves as a conference centre. The grounds are open as a public park, and they offer some of the best unobstructed views on this stretch of coast: from the upper terraces, the eye carries south past the Malmousque inlet toward the limestone outcrops that mark the beginning of the Calanques.

The garden is laid out on several levels, with stone steps and retaining walls holding back the hillside. Mediterranean plantings — umbrella pines, agaves, oleanders — make the most of the thin soil and strong sun. Below, a path descends toward the water and the Malmousque cove, a small inlet popular with local swimmers that sits just west of the Corniche Kennedy. The combination of garden and coastal access makes Valmer a compact but satisfying place to spend a morning.

The 7th arrondissement above the Corniche is one of Marseille's most desirable residential districts, and the villas along this ridge have always competed for the best sea views. Parc Valmer preserves a fragment of that tradition in public hands. The terrace at the top of the garden, shaded by pines and facing the water, is a good place to understand why Marseille's prosperous families built their summer retreats on exactly this hillside.
