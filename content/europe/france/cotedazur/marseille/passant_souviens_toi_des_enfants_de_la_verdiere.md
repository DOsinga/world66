The phrase "Passant, souviens-toi des enfants de la Verdière" — Passerby, remember the children of La Verdière — follows the form of classical funerary address, calling on anyone who passes to stop and carry the memory forward. La Verdière is a name shared by a small village in the Var département and by localities and estates in the Marseille hinterland; this memorial, positioned in the northeastern part of the city, almost certainly refers to a local site rather than the village.

Memorials using this exact apostrophe — addressing the passerby directly — became especially common in France after the Second World War and the Algerian War, periods in which children were among the victims of deportations, bombings, massacres, and displacement. Whether this inscription marks a wartime deportation, an accident, or another loss, the deliberate invocation of "children" makes it unusually affecting. The choice not to specify a date or cause on the facing inscription, if that is indeed the case, forces the reader to seek out the fuller story.

The coordinates place this memorial in a residential part of Marseille's 13th arrondissement or thereabouts — a quiet location, easily missed. Stopping to read it is the point: the inscription was carved precisely so that someone, eventually, would.
