"Passant, souviens-toi des enfants de la Verdière." Passerby, remember the children. The phrasing is classical — the same form Romans used for tombstones. It addresses you directly and gives you a job: don't forget.

The memorial sits in a quiet residential corner of northeastern Marseille. What happened to these children isn't spelled out. In France, memorials using this exact formula — children, the direct address, the command to remember — most often mark deportations or wartime massacres. The deliberate silence about dates and causes makes you work for the story.

Which is exactly what it's designed to do. Stop. Read it. Then go find out what it means.
