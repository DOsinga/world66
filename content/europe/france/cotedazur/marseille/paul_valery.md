Valéry was born at Sète, a port town fifty kilometres west, to a Corsican father and a Genoese mother. He grew up with the Mediterranean not as scenery but as a way of thinking — precise, clear, lit from above. His monument here claims him for this coast.

His two great poems came twenty years apart. "La Jeune Parque" in 1917 — four years of work, nearly impossible to paraphrase. "Le Cimetière Marin" in 1920 — a meditation on death set at a seaside cemetery overlooking Sète, with a line that stops you cold: "Le vent se lève!… Il faut tenter de vivre!" The wind rises. We must try to live.

He also kept notebooks — the Cahiers — for fifty years, written before dawn every morning. Thousands of pages on the nature of mind. They are extraordinary. Hardly anyone reads them.
