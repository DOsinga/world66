The Petit Train de Marseille is a road-going tourist train — a motorised vehicle styled to look like a locomotive pulling open carriages — that shuttles visitors between Marseille's most-visited landmarks without the effort of climbing on foot. Departures are from the Vieux-Port quayside, and two separate circuits operate: one climbs the steep hill to the basilica of Notre-Dame de la Garde, offering panoramic views over the city and the islands; the other winds through the narrow streets of Le Panier, the oldest inhabited neighbourhood in Marseille and one of the oldest in France.

The Notre-Dame route takes roughly an hour for the round trip and passes through residential streets that most visitors on foot never reach, providing an easy introduction to the topography of a city that is more hilly than it first appears from the waterfront. The Le Panier circuit is shorter and dips into lanes too tight for ordinary traffic, past colourful shuttered facades, small squares, and the occasional glimpse of the sea.

The train is unabashedly touristic, complete with recorded commentary in multiple languages, and it is most popular with families and those for whom the gradients of the city are a physical challenge. For all that, the routes it follows are genuinely scenic and the perspective from an open carriage, especially on the ascent to Notre-Dame, is worth having. Tickets are purchased at the Vieux-Port departure point.
