It's unabashedly a tourist train — motorised vehicle, open carriages, recorded commentary in multiple languages. No apologies. Two circuits: one climbs the steep hill to Notre-Dame de la Garde, the other winds through Le Panier's lanes too narrow for ordinary traffic.

The Notre-Dame route is the better one. The ascent passes residential streets most visitors on foot never reach, and the views from an open carriage — looking back over the city toward the islands — are worth the ticket price. The Le Panier circuit is shorter and pokes into corners that are genuinely tight.

Departs from the Vieux-Port quayside; buy tickets at the departure point. Best for families, and for anyone for whom Marseille's gradients are a physical consideration — which, after a full day of this city's hills, includes most people.
