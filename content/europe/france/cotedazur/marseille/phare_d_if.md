The Île d'If is three kilometres west of the Vieux-Port: a pale limestone lump with a working lighthouse at one end and a fortress at the other. The Château d'If was built in 1524 as a coastal defence and spent most of its career as a prison — remote enough to be inescapable, close enough for Marseille to see it from the waterfront.

Real prisoners held here included Huguenots and political inconveniences of successive French governments. The Man in the Iron Mask — identity still genuinely disputed — is among those claimed for the château's cells.

What most people come for is fictional: Dumas set The Count of Monte Cristo here in 1844, and the cell attributed to Edmond Dantès has been shown to tourists ever since. The guides handle the real-versus-fictional distinction with varying degrees of clarity. The 20-minute boat crossing from the Vieux-Port, the sea views back to the city, the weight of real and invented history stacked in the same stone rooms — it adds up to one of the better half-days you can have from Marseille.
