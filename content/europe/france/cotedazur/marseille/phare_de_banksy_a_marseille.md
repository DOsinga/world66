A lighthouse beam sweeping from a crumbling urban wall. Banksy's piece is simple and typical of his method: take a symbol loaded with meaning, put it somewhere that shouldn't have it, and let the juxtaposition do the work.

In Marseille — three thousand years of arrivals and departures, a port that has guided and sometimes refused millions of people — the image hits harder than it would elsewhere. Lighthouses mean welcome, or warning, or hope glimpsed from open water. All three readings are available here.

Finding it takes a little detective work. Banksy never publishes coordinates and the work's condition changes with time and weather. That search is part of the point. When you find it, stop for a moment before you photograph it.
