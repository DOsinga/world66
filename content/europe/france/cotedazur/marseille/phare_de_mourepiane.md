The Phare de Mourepiane marks the entry channel to Marseille's northern industrial harbour — where supertankers and container ships come in, where 80 million tonnes of cargo move through every year. The lighthouse is squat, functional, and completely dwarfed by the gantry cranes and roll-on roll-off ferries surrounding it. It keeps going anyway.

At dusk the combination is striking: a nineteenth-century light station pulsing against floodlit dock machinery, stacked containers, and vessels at anchor in the Rade Nord. France's largest port, among the top ten in the Mediterranean, and this lighthouse has been watching it grow for generations.

You can't get close — active port infrastructure, access restricted — but the beam is visible for miles across the Golfe de Lyon, and the lighthouse itself is easy to see from the road along Chemin de Mourepiane.
