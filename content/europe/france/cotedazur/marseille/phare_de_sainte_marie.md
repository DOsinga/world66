The rocks out there don't care about your chart. East of the city, where the white limestone cliffs start and the Calanques begin, the seabed gets shallow fast. Reefs and submerged shelves extend offshore, invisible until they're not.

The lighthouse marks the hazard. White sector to seaward, red over the shallows — a skipper reading that sequence at night knows exactly which side of the danger they're on. The mistral can build short, steep seas here that push a vessel sideways faster than you expect. This is not the gentle Mediterranean of travel posters.

You can reach it on foot along the Sentier des Douaniers, the old customs path that traces the coast. From the headland, the Frioul archipelago sits to the west, the jagged Calanques spine to the east. Coming in by sea at night, with the lighthouse winking its sequence ahead, is one of the better arrivals the Mediterranean has to offer.
