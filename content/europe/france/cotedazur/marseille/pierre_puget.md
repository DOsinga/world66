Pierre Puget was born here in 1620 and is, by some distance, the most important artist Marseille ever produced. The problem was Versailles. Louis XIV's court commissioned his Milon de Crotone and Perseus and Andromeda, then found them too turbulent for the formal gardens and shunted them to peripheral spots. Both are now in the Louvre, recognised as among the finest Baroque sculptures in existence.

He spent long stretches working in Genoa — the Génois actually appreciated what he was doing — and when he was in Marseille, he built. The Vieille Charité hospice is his design. The Hôtel de Ville bears his influence. He shaped the seventeenth-century city in stone.

The monument near the Vieux-Port puts him back where he belongs. Puget belongs with Rubens and Bernini. He just had the misfortune of being from the wrong city.
