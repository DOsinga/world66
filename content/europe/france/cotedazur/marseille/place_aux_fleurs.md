Place aux Fleurs is a small planted square in the northern coastal quarter of Marseille near L'Estaque — the industrial village at the northern edge of the city that Cézanne and Braque painted repeatedly between 1870 and 1910. The square itself is modest: a rectangle of plane trees and benches used by the residents of the surrounding apartment blocks, with the kind of neighbourhood permanence that never makes it into guidebooks.

The name — Flower Square — suggests either a former flower market or simply the aspiration that accompanied a planted public space when the neighbourhood was laid out. In the northern arrondissements of Marseille, where public green space is sparse relative to the dense housing stock, even a small square with reliable shade carries real value to residents.

L'Estaque, immediately to the north, retains something of the atmosphere that drew the Post-Impressionists: a harbour with working fishing boats, a train station on the coastal line to the Côte Bleue, and a set of views across the Golfe de Marseille that open up dramatically once you climb above the village. The square itself gives no hint of this history, but it sits within walking distance of one of the most painted coastlines in modern art history.
