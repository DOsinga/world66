Place Basse sits in the northeastern residential districts of Marseille, in the broad band of suburbs that extends between the urban centre and the hills of the arrière-pays. Its name is purely topographic: a square at a lower elevation than the surrounding neighbourhood, in a city where the topography is sufficiently varied that high and low remain useful navigational distinctions even at the level of individual streets.

The northeastern arrondissements — the 11th through 14th — are largely residential, developed in the postwar decades to house the expanding population of a city that had lost much of its centre to wartime bombing and was rebuilding fast. These are neighbourhoods of apartment blocks, local shops, and schools; their squares function as neighbourhood amenities rather than civic monuments.

Place Basse is one of these functional squares. The surrounding streets have the character of a self-contained quarter — a baker, a tabac, a pharmacy — with residents who rarely have reason to travel to the tourist Marseille of the Vieux-Port. The square is used in the way neighbourhood squares are always used: by older men in the afternoon, by children after school, and by anyone who wants a few minutes of open air without going further.
