Marseille is hilly enough that "high" and "low" remain useful ways to navigate even at the level of individual streets. Place Basse — Low Square — sits in the northeastern residential districts at a dip in the terrain, and that's the whole story of the name.

The northeastern arrondissements, the 11th through 14th, are where the city expanded fast in the postwar decades after wartime bombing had gutted the centre. Apartment blocks, local shops, schools. Squares that function as neighbourhood amenities rather than civic gestures.

This is one of them. A baker, a tabac, a pharmacy nearby. Older men in the afternoon shade, children after school. The city lives here — it just doesn't perform.
