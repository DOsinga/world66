Place Castellane is a traffic interchange. Wide roundabout, six converging avenues, the hum of the city. What makes it worth noticing is the fountain at the centre — installed in the 1860s as part of the Haussmann-era push south. Four female figures represent the rivers of Provence: the Rhône, Durance, Huveaune, and Arc. They support a basin topped by an obelisk. The whole thing is conspicuously grand for a traffic island.

It was laid out to mark the beginning of the new city — the southward expansion that opened the residential arrondissements toward the Prado and the sea. For Marseillais, it still reads that way: the point where the old chaotic centre ends and the bourgeois southern city begins.

Metro Line 1 runs underneath, connecting here to the Vélodrome, the Prado beach, and the southern arrondissements. Most visitors never reach this roundabout. It belongs to the city that gets on with its life.
