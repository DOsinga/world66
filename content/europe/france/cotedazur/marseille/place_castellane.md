Place Castellane is a traffic interchange, not a pedestrian square — a wide roundabout where the broad avenues of the 6th arrondissement converge south of the Canebière. The fountain at its centre was installed in the 1860s as part of the Haussmann-era southward expansion of Marseille. Four female figures represent the rivers of Provence — the Rhône, Durance, Huveaune, and Arc — supporting a central basin topped by an obelisk; the ensemble is conspicuously grander than the traffic island it occupies.

The square was laid out as part of the prefect's urban plan to push Marseille toward the coast, opening the city to the residential arrondissements that spread south and east toward the Prado and the sea. The Metro station beneath it — Line 1 — connects the centre to the southern districts, the Vélodrome, and the beach at Rond-Point du Prado.

For Marseillais, Place Castellane functions as an orientation point: the place where the old city ends and the bourgeois southern city begins. Most visitors never reach it; it belongs entirely to the residential and commercial life of the city that exists beyond the tourist circuit of the Vieux-Port and Le Panier.
