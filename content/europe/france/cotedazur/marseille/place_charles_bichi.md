Place Charles Bichi sits in the Saint-Barthélemy quarter of the 13th arrondissement — one of the northern residential districts that most visitors never reach. The 13th is large: old Provençal village cores absorbed by expansion, postwar housing blocks, commercial strips. Charles Bichi was significant enough at the neighbourhood level to earn a square, not significant enough to make the history books. That's an honest description of most people.

The square is the thing itself: a gathering point, a few trees, the ordinary transactions of daily life. Neighbours crossing paths. Children after school. The afternoon shade.

This is the Marseille that exists parallel to the tourist circuit — not hidden, just unperformed. The northern arrondissements are where the city goes about its actual business.
