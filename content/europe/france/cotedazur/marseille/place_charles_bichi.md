Place Charles Bichi is a residential square in the northern 13th arrondissement, in the Saint-Barthélemy quarter of a city that extends much further north and east than most visitors realise. The 13th is a large arrondissement of mixed residential character — older Provençal village cores absorbed by urban expansion, postwar housing blocks, and commercial strips — and the square sits within this everyday fabric.

Charles Bichi himself was a local figure significant enough to earn a street-level memorial but not prominent enough to have entered the standard historical record in detail; like many squares named after local worthies in French provincial cities, the name commemorates someone whose importance was felt at the neighbourhood rather than the metropolitan scale.

The square functions as most neighbourhood squares do in this part of Marseille: a gathering point, a place for the informal transactions of daily life — a few words between neighbours, children playing after school, the old men's chairs in the afternoon shade. The northern arrondissements are where the city lives rather than where it performs, and Place Charles Bichi is an unremarkable piece of that lived-in Marseille.
