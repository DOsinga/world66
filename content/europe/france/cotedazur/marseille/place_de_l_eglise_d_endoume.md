Place de l'Église d'Endoume sits in front of the parish church of the Endoume quarter, a residential neighbourhood perched on the coastal slopes west of the Vieux-Port, above the Corniche Kennedy and the rocky calanques of Malmousque and the Vallon des Auffes. Endoume retains the character of the old Marseille coastal villages that were absorbed by urban growth in the 19th and early 20th centuries — quiet streets, stone houses, a church that functions as the neighbourhood's orienting landmark.

The church square is the traditional gathering point of a Provençal village centre: the place where the Sunday congregation disperses, where the market sets up on certain mornings, and where the daily rhythm of a neighbourhood that has not been entirely absorbed by the city is most visible. In Endoume this quality is reinforced by the topography — the quarter sits on a slope above the coast, slightly removed from the traffic of the Corniche, and has something of the self-containment of a hill village.

The Vallon des Auffes is a five-minute walk downhill — a tiny fishing calanque, almost implausibly preserved within the city limits, where small boats are still pulled up on the rocks and a handful of restaurants serve bouillabaisse looking directly onto the water. The church square is the quiet uphill counterpart to that more visited landmark.
