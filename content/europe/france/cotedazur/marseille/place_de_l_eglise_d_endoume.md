Endoume is the old coastal village that got absorbed by Marseille but didn't entirely surrender its character. It sits on a slope above the Corniche Kennedy — quiet streets, stone houses, a parish church that functions as the neighbourhood's centre of gravity. The square in front of that church is where Sunday services end, where the market sets up on certain mornings, where neighbours meet without having planned to.

The topography helps. Endoume sits slightly lifted above the coast road, with something of the self-containment of a hill village. The noise and traffic of the Corniche stays below.

Walk five minutes downhill and you reach the Vallon des Auffes: a tiny fishing calanque, almost absurdly preserved inside the city limits. Small boats pulled up on the rocks, restaurants serving bouillabaisse looking directly onto the water. The church square is the quiet counterpart to that. Go to both.
