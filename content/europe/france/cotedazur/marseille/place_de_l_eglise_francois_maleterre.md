This is the edge of Marseille. The 15th arrondissement thins out here, and the coastal strip ahead — limestone cliffs, small coves, the villages of the Côte Bleue — is more its own world than an extension of the city.

The church square serves the immediate neighbourhood, as parish squares do. François Maleterre's name distinguishes it from the many other église squares in a city with a lot of parish churches and limited naming imagination. The man himself was significant at the local level; beyond that, the record is thin.

What defines this spot is what's just beyond it. The coastal train from Saint-Charles passes through here on its way to Niolon, La Redonne, and Carry-le-Rouet — threading tunnels and viaducts along one of the most dramatic stretches of coast in the region. The square is the beginning of that coastline. Think of it as the city's last full stop before the Côte Bleue takes over.
