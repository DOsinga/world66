Place de l'Église François Maleterre sits in the northern reaches of the 15th arrondissement, in one of the outlying quartiers on the coastal strip where Marseille transitions toward the Côte Bleue — the stretch of limestone cliffs and small coves that runs northwest toward Martigues. This is the very edge of the city, where the urban fabric thins out and the relationship between sea, rock, and habitation becomes the dominant reality.

François Maleterre gives the square its secondary name, distinguishing it from the many other église squares in a city of numerous parish churches. The church itself serves a residential neighbourhood of modest scale, the kind of community that coalesces around a parish in a district where the city's administrative identity matters less than the immediate neighbourhood.

The proximity to the Côte Bleue is the defining fact of this area's geography. The train line from Marseille Saint-Charles runs along the coast through a series of tunnels and viaducts to reach the small stations at Niolon, La Redonne, and Carry-le-Rouet — a route that offers some of the most dramatic coastal scenery in the region. The square sits within the catchment of that coast, in a part of Marseille that is more easily understood as the beginning of the Côte Bleue than as an extension of the city centre.
