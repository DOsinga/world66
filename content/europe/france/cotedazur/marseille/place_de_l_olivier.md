The eastern arrondissements keep nudging toward Provence even as the apartment blocks multiply. Place de l'Olivier — Olive Tree Square — sits where the city's grid relaxes into hills and limestone outcrops. The name isn't decorative; it's a fossil, preserving the landscape that existed before the streets did.

Out here the city meets its own agricultural past. Village cores like Les Trois-Lucs were absorbed by postwar development but their names stuck, along with the botanical vocabulary: the olive, the vine, the thyme. Walk far enough east and you can actually see the garrigue on the ridgeline.

The square serves the apartment blocks around it. No particular reason to make a detour — but if you're heading east, it's a good reminder of how much city there is beyond the Vieux-Port.
