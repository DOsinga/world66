Place de l'Olivier — Olive Tree Square — is a residential square in the eastern arrondissements of Marseille, where the city spreads toward Aubagne and the limestone hills of the Huveaune valley. The olive is so central to the Provençal landscape and to Mediterranean food culture that its appearance in street names requires no particular justification; across the south of France, streets and squares named for the olive, the vine, the lavender, and the almond are a standard register of regional identity.

The eastern arrondissements of Marseille — the 11th, 12th, and 13th — contain a mix of postwar residential development, older village cores (Plan-de-Cuques, Les Trois-Lucs, La Valentine), and the industrial corridor of the Huveaune. These are the arrondissements where the city meets its own agricultural and Provençal past most directly, in old farmhouses absorbed by suburban expansion and in the names of the squares and streets laid out as those expansions happened.

The square's coordinates place it in a residential quarter without significant tourist interest, used by its immediate neighbours as neighbourhood squares are always used. The olive tree reference situates it within a landscape tradition that extends from the street level all the way to the garrigue-covered hills visible from Marseille's eastern heights on clear days.
