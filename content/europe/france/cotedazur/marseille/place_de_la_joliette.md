The Place de la Joliette sits at the seam between Marseille's historic port area and the northern commercial docks, directly in front of the Joliette ferry terminal. The terminal handles passenger ferries to Corsica, Sardinia, Algeria, Tunisia, and Morocco — making it one of the most active Mediterranean crossing points and Marseille's most direct contemporary connection to North Africa. The queues for the overnight Algeria and Morocco ferries, particularly in summer, are a more honest picture of who moves through Marseille than the tourist boats at the Vieux-Port.

The square has been reshaped repeatedly by the Euroméditerranée regeneration project, which has been rebuilding the northern port zone since the late 1990s. The most conspicuous result is visible immediately to the south: the CMA CGM Tower, designed by Zaha Hadid and completed in 2010, housing the global headquarters of the French container shipping company. At 147 metres it is the tallest building in Marseille, with a tapered glass form that reads differently from each angle. The contrast between the ferry terminal's functional concrete and the tower's corporate architecture makes the square a useful summary of what Euroméditerranée has done to the district.

The area around the square has also acquired a cluster of cultural institutions — the MuCEM, the Villa Méditerranée, the FRAC Sud, and the Gaston Defferre archives are all within walking distance. Visitors arriving or departing by ferry who have time to spare are within reach of all of them.
