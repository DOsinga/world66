Place de la Madrague de Montredon sits at the centre of one of Marseille's most anomalous quarters: a small fishing village at the southern extremity of the city, where the coast road reaches its end and the Calanques National Park begins. Madrague de Montredon is a calanque settlement — a narrow inlet community of fishermen's houses, a boat ramp, and the kind of bar-restaurant that exists to serve people who have come down the hill for lunch or an afternoon of swimming. Its survival within the administrative limits of France's second city is genuinely remarkable.

The name "Madrague" derives from a Provençal term for a tunny-fish trap — a seasonal installation using nets stretched across a bay to intercept migrating bluefin tuna on their Mediterranean circuit. Tunny fishing was a significant economic activity along this coast until the mid-20th century, and the Madrague settlements at various points along the Marseille coastline mark where those operations were based. The fish are gone; the name persists.

Montredon above the calanque is a residential neighbourhood of villas and apartment buildings looking south toward the Riou archipelago. The square at the calanque level is the functional centre of the fishing village community — a few tables in front of the restaurant, the smell of grilling fish, and a view directly out to sea that makes the proximity of a city of 900,000 people seem improbable.
