The road from the city ends here. One step further south and you're in the Calanques National Park — the limestone sea cliffs that run all the way to Cassis. Madrague de Montredon is the last settlement: a boat ramp, a handful of fishermen's houses, a restaurant with tables outside where grilled fish is basically the only thing on offer.

The name comes from madrague — a Provençal word for a tunny trap, the net installation used to intercept migrating bluefin tuna on their Mediterranean circuit. They fished here seriously until the mid-20th century, when the tuna stopped coming. The trap is gone. The name stays.

The square is a few tables in front of that restaurant. The view is straight out to sea, past the Riou archipelago. The city of 900,000 feels like a rumour from here.
