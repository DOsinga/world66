Place de la Parette sits in eastern Marseille, in a residential quarter where the city's street nomenclature still carries the traces of the Provençal agricultural landscape that preceded urban development. "Parette" — in Provençal — refers to a small plant or herb, likely the wall-rue fern (Asplenium ruta-muraria) or possibly a local variant name for another low-growing species found on the limestone outcrops of the Marseille hills. The use of botanical and topographic terms in street names is characteristic of the southern French urban fabric, where the expansion of cities across former farmland and garrigue preserved local vocabulary that would otherwise have disappeared.

The eastern arrondissements are where Marseille's residential expansion met the natural geography most directly — the Huveaune valley, the Plan de Cuques plateau, and the hills separating the city from Aubagne. Street names here combine civic commemoration (politicians, soldiers, writers) with the older layer of Provençal place-names that identified fields, water sources, and terrain features before there were streets.

The square itself is a neighbourhood amenity: a pause in the residential fabric, used by the people who live on the surrounding streets. Its significance is less historical than geographic — it marks a corner of a city that extends much further east than most visitors ever reach.
