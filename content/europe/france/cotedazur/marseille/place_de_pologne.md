France has a long habit of naming streets after countries that were suffering. Poland was partitioned out of existence in the late 18th century, and French republicans who believed in national self-determination put its name on public squares as a gesture of solidarity. The November Uprising of 1830 sent another wave of Polish exiles into France and reinforced the connection.

Place de Pologne sits in the 8th arrondissement, in the broad, tree-lined residential district south of Castellane — this is bourgeois Marseille: wide avenues, stone apartment buildings, the feel of Haussmann-era order. From here the Prado beaches are a short walk south; the metro at Castellane connects you to the centre.

The square itself is pleasant enough — benches, planted centre, the daily life of a neighbourhood that considers itself well-situated. The Polish dimension is purely in the name.
