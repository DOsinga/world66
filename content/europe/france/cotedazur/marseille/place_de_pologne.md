Place de Pologne sits in the 8th arrondissement, the large residential district that extends from the Castellane roundabout south toward the Prado beaches and the coastal road. This is bourgeois Marseille — the broad, tree-lined avenues and Haussmann-era apartment blocks that give the southern city a feel closer to Lyon than to the cramped medieval quarters of the Vieux-Port.

The name Poland entered French civic vocabulary at several distinct moments: the partition of Poland in the 18th century prompted sympathy from French republicans; the November Uprising of 1830–31 produced a wave of Polish exile communities in France; and the Franco-Polish alliance of the interwar years cemented the connection. Naming a square after Poland was a republican gesture, placing Marseille's civic map in conversation with European struggles for national self-determination.

The square itself serves the surrounding residential blocks: benches, a planted centre, the rhythm of daily life at the scale of the neighbourhood. From here the Prado is a short walk south, and the metro at Castellane connects the district to the city centre.
