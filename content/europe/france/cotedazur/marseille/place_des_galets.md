The galet is one of the defining materials of Provençal outdoor life. Smooth rounded stones, worn by river or sea into their characteristic egg-like forms, have been used for centuries to surface paths, terraces, and small squares across the south of France. The technique — setting galets on edge or flat into compacted earth or mortar — produces a surface that is firm underfoot, drains well in rain, and stays cool in summer heat. Place des Galets takes its name from this tradition, and the name alone signals something about the texture and scale of the space.

Situated in the eastern arrondissements of Marseille, the square serves the residential streets around it rather than the tourist city. This part of the city sits between the urban density of the centre and the wilder terrain that leads toward the Massif des Calanques to the south, and the neighbourhood has the calmer, more suburban character common to Marseille's outer districts. The square functions as a local node — a place to cross, to wait, to let the day slow down for a moment.

The name is also a reminder that Marseille is a city where the sea is never far from mind. Galets washed up on the beach at the Catalans or the Prado are the same stones underfoot in a village path in the Luberon, the same material threading the built and natural landscapes of the whole region together.
