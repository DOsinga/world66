Galets — smooth, rounded, worn by river or sea — are one of the defining materials of the Provençal built environment. You set them on edge in compacted earth: firm underfoot, drains well, stays cool in the heat. Every old village path from the Luberon to the Camargue uses the technique. Place des Galets is named for this, which tells you something about the texture and scale the space once had.

Out in the eastern arrondissements, where the city gets quieter and the hills get closer. The square serves the surrounding streets. Not much to look at, but the name does something — connecting a patch of urban tarmac to the material culture of the whole region.

The same galets wash up on the beaches at Catalans and Prado. Beach, path, square — the same stone, moving through different lives.
