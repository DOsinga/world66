A pistole was a gold coin — Spanish in origin, circulating across the 16th and 17th-century Mediterranean in the commerce between Marseille, Genoa, Istanbul, and Algiers. Marseille's position as France's Mediterranean gateway made it a city of coin-changers handling currencies from across the sea. A square named for the pistole is naming itself after the mechanism of everything.

Le Panier above the Vieux-Port is where this commercial life was most concentrated. The streets are steep and narrow, the buildings stacked close — the result of centuries of Mediterranean city-building in a confined space. Place des Pistoles formed the way these squares do: a junction of several medieval streets, not planned but accumulated.

Today Le Panier is residential, with artists and small businesses mixed in among the long-term residents. The coin-changers are gone, but the medieval street pattern they navigated is still here.
