Place des Pistoles sits in the upper reaches of Le Panier, the oldest inhabited quarter of Marseille, on the slopes above the Vieux-Port between the Cathédrale de la Major and the rue de la République. Le Panier is one of the densest and most historically layered neighbourhoods in France — a tangle of steep streets, vaulted passages, and narrow houses built over successive centuries of Mediterranean trade.

The pistole was a gold coin in circulation across the Mediterranean world in the 16th and 17th centuries, particularly in Spanish, Italian, and Ottoman commerce. Marseille's position as France's primary Mediterranean port made it a city of coin-changers, merchants, and traders handling currencies from across the sea. A square named for the pistole is naming itself after money: the medium of the commerce that built the neighbourhood and the city.

Le Panier today is predominantly residential, home to long-established communities and a growing population of artists and small-business owners drawn by the relatively affordable rents close to the centre. Place des Pistoles is the kind of small, irregular square that forms naturally at the junction of several medieval streets — not planned but accumulated.
