Place des Soleils — Sun Square — sits in the eastern 11th arrondissement, in the broad residential zone between the city centre and the limestone hills. Postwar apartment blocks, quiet streets, a square that functions as a local node without drawing anyone from outside the neighbourhood. The name is the most interesting thing about it.

Marseille has a consistent habit of naming streets and squares after solar imagery: the light, the sun, the warmth. In a city where the mistral scours the air clean and the summer sky turns a particular shade of blue that painters have been chasing for a century, this makes a kind of sense. Cézanne came for the light. The neighbourhood just named itself after it.

The bar on the corner does most of its trade before lunch. The children take over the square in the afternoon. Standard-issue neighbourhood life, in the sun.
