Place des Soleils is in the eastern 11th arrondissement of Marseille, in the broad residential zone between the urban centre and the limestone garrigue of the hills that close the city off from the Huveaune valley. The eastern arrondissements — the 11th through 14th — are largely postwar residential Marseille: apartment blocks, pavillons, commercial strips, and the kind of quiet squares that serve a neighbourhood without drawing anyone from outside it.

The name — Suns — is a Provençal register that appears across the south of France: sunflowers, sunlight, solar imagery. In a region where the quality and quantity of light is a civic pride, naming a square after the sun is almost a statement of geographic identity. The mistral that scours the city clean, the particular blue of the Provençal sky, the intensity of summer light — these are the conditions that drew Cézanne to Aix and generations of painters to the Marseille coast. Place des Soleils claims all of that at the level of a neighbourhood.

The square functions as a local gathering point for the surrounding residential blocks — the kind of place where children play in the afternoon and where the bar on the corner does most of its trade before lunch.
