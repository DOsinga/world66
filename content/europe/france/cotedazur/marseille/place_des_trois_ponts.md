The name records a vanished geography. Three bridges means three crossings, which means three watercourses — the Huveaune and its tributaries draining the limestone hills to the east. These channels shaped where the village clusters grew: around mills, then around bridges, then around squares. The water is largely channeled or covered now, but the name marks where the bridges stood.

The southeastern 11th arrondissement was absorbed into Marseille relatively late. Small quartiers grew here around churches and farms before the city reached them, and they kept a degree of separate identity even after the streets joined up. The Huveaune valley was the main route in from the interior, the road east toward Aubagne and the hills.

Today the square serves a quiet residential neighbourhood — morning commuters, afternoon school runs, the unhurried presence of people who've lived in the same street for decades.
