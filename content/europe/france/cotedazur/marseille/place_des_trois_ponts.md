Place des Trois Ponts sits in the southeastern 11th arrondissement, in a part of Marseille shaped by the watercourses — the Huveaune river and its tributaries — that drain the limestone hills east of the city. "Three Bridges" is a topographic name that records a vanished or altered landscape: bridges over channels or streams that were once significant enough to give a locality its identity.

The 11th and 12th arrondissements are where Marseille meets the Provence that surrounds it. The city's urban fabric here is interrupted by the valleys and ridges of a limestone landscape that resisted simple urban development; small quartiers grew around churches, farms, and mills, and were absorbed into the expanding city while retaining something of their separate character. The Huveaune valley, running east toward Aubagne, was one of the principal routes into the city from the interior, and its bridges were genuine infrastructure.

Today the square serves a residential neighbourhood with the kind of daily life that happens everywhere in this part of the city — morning commuters, afternoon school runs, and the permanent, unhurried presence of those who have lived in the same street for decades.
