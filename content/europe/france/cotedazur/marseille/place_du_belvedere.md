Marseille is built on limestone ridges that rise and dip unpredictably through the urban fabric. A square positioned on one of those ridges genuinely overlooks something: in this case, a sweep across the northern city toward the coast, visible even from residential streets tourists never reach.

The northern arrondissements feel like everyday Marseille — apartment blocks from the sixties and seventies, neighbourhood boulangeries, pharmacies, bars. The belvédère designation here is a straightforward geographic label, the same kind of practical topography-in-a-name that appears all over the city.

Stand at the right spot and you'll see exactly what it means.
