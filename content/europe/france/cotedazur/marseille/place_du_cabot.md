Place du Cabot anchors the Le Cabot neighbourhood in Marseille's 12th arrondissement, sitting in the folds of the limestone hills that separate the city's eastern districts from the Huveaune valley below. The square takes its name from the quartier itself — a name that carries a certain rough charm, since *cabot* in French slang can mean a mongrel dog or, in old military usage, a corporal promoted from the ranks. It is a fitting title for a neighbourhood that has always been working-class and proudly unpolished.

The square functions as the social hinge of the local village-within-the-city: a bakery, a tabac, a handful of bar terraces, and a bus stop connect residents to the rest of Marseille without requiring them to abandon the hillside calm. Morning markets bring a short-lived bustle; by midday the pace settles back into the rhythm of a town square that happens to have a city beyond its rooftops.

From the higher edges of the place, views open over the suburban rooftops toward the Massif de l'Étoile in the north and the pine-covered ridges of the Parc des Calanques to the southeast — a reminder that even deep inside the urban grid, Marseille is never far from wild country.
