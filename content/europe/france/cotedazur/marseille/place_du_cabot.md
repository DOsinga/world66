The neighbourhood calls itself Le Cabot. In French slang, cabot means a mongrel or, in military usage, a corporal promoted from the ranks. Working-class, proudly unglamorous — the name fits. The square anchors the quartier the way village squares do: bakery on one side, tabac on another, a bus stop connecting this hillside pocket to the rest of Marseille.

From the upper edge of the place, the views open properly. The Massif de l'Étoile to the north, the pine-covered ridges of the Calanques to the southeast. Even deep in the urban grid, the wild Provençal country sits right there on the horizon.
