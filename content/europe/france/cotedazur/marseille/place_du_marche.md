Marseille swallowed dozens of small coastal communities in the 19th and 20th centuries without always managing to digest them. The western quartiers — scattered between the Vieux-Port and the Côte Bleue — kept their own rhythms: their own church, their own bar, their own market day. Place du Marché is one of those centres. A flat open space, plane trees at the edges, a bar-tabac on the corner.

Whether a market still runs here or the name is now purely historical, the square reads as the hinge of a community that sees itself as a village with a city behind it. The market day gave it a reason to exist; the neighbourhood gives it a reason to persist.
