Place du Marché — Market Square — is a functional name that appears in villages and urban quartiers throughout France wherever a periodic market gave a square its reason for being. In Marseille's western coastal quartiers, where small communities were absorbed into the city in the 19th and 20th centuries without losing their distinct identities, such squares are often still genuinely village-scale: a flat open space surrounded by houses, with plane trees at the edges and a bar-tabac on one corner.

The western reaches of Marseille — between the Vieux-Port and the limestone coast that gives way to the Côte Bleue — contain a series of small settlements that became part of the city administratively while remaining functionally separate. Each had its own church, its own market rhythm, and its own relationship to the sea and the hills. Place du Marché in this part of the city is the kind of square where the local rhythm is set not by tourist seasons but by the weekly market itself — vegetables, local fish, seasonal produce from the surrounding countryside.

Markets in the Marseille region have particular resonance: the daily market culture of Noailles and the Capucins in the city centre is well known, but the outer quartiers have their own smaller versions, often on a single morning per week, drawing from the immediate neighbourhood. Whether a market still runs on Place du Marché today or whether the name is now purely historical, the square reads as a civic centre at the scale of its own community — the place where the neighbourhood has always gathered.
