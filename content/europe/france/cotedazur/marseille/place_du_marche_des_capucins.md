Every day, including Sunday — that alone makes it exceptional. Most French markets shut on Sunday. The Capucins market does not. It runs from early morning to mid-afternoon on the sloping square above Noailles, the most densely multicultural neighbourhood in the city.

The stalls reflect exactly who shops here: Maghrebi groceries, West African dried fish and palm oil, Antillean fruits, South Asian spices, Provençal olives and herbs. Arabic, Berber, French, and Spanish overlap in the course of a single purchase. Capucin friars established themselves here in the 17th century — today that history is entirely gone. Only the name survived. The market took over everything else.
