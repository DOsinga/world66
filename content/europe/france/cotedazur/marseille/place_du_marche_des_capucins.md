Place du Marché des Capucins is, in practice, simply the Marché des Capucins — Marseille's most atmospheric and commercially vigorous daily market, running from early morning to mid-afternoon on the sloping square that connects the upper Noailles quarter to the streets below. The Capucins market operates every day including Sunday, which distinguishes it from most French markets and makes it genuinely central to the food shopping of the surrounding district.

The range of produce reflects the neighbourhood it serves. Noailles is Marseille's most densely multicultural district: Maghrebi grocery stalls, West African dried fish and palm oil, Antillean fruits, South Asian spices, and the Provençal staples — olives, herbs, tomatoes, courgettes — that anchor the southern French kitchen. The market moves between languages; Arabic, Berber, French, and occasionally Spanish are all audible in the course of a single transaction. This is the market where Marseille cooks for itself rather than for visitors.

The square takes its name from the Capucin friars who established a presence in the area in the 17th century. The religious etymology is today entirely absent from the commercial reality of the place. The market dominates, and the Capucins are remembered only in the name.
