Place du Rocher takes its name from the limestone formations that characterise the eastern reaches of Marseille, where the city's street plan has to negotiate with the raw geology of Provence. The *rocher* — rock or crag — that gave the square its identity may be a specific outcrop that shaped a corner of the neighbourhood, or simply an acknowledgment that in this part of the city, stone is never far from the surface, visible in retaining walls, quarry cuts, and the pale bluffs that rise above the residential streets.

The square lies in the 9th or 10th arrondissement zone, in a district where Marseille's urban sprawl meets the limestone plateaus that stretch toward the Calanques National Park. The surrounding neighbourhood has the settled feel of a place that grew incrementally — houses added to houses, gardens filling the gaps between rock and road — rather than being planned from above. Locals use the place as a shortcut and a meeting point in equal measure.

Marseille is built almost entirely on limestone, and that geology gives the city its particular quality of light: the white and cream rock reflects the sun harshly in summer and glows amber at dusk. A square named for a rock is, in this city, not a modest name at all — it is a claim on the bedrock character of the place itself.
