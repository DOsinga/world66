Zola was born in Paris but raised in Aix-en-Provence, just 30 kilometres north. His father, an Italian engineer, came to build a canal — the Zola Canal still supplies water to Aix today. The Provençal landscape runs through the Rougon-Macquart novels thinly disguised as Plassans.

But cities named squares after Zola for J'accuse as much as for his fiction. His 1898 open letter defending Alfred Dreyfus against a wrongful treason conviction is one of the most consequential pieces of journalism in French history. He was convicted of libel and fled to England for a year.

The square in the northeastern residential district does nothing theatrical with the name — benches, local life, a street sign. The weight is entirely in the letters on the sign.
