Place Engalière sits in the Endoume quarter, the southern residential district that climbs the limestone ridge between the Vieux-Port and the first true calanques. This is one of Marseille's more settled neighbourhoods — stone buildings, narrow streets that follow the contours of the rock, and a population that has been here long enough to know every shortcut to the waterfront.

The name Engalière likely derives from a Provençal toponym — the region's place names frequently preserve Occitan roots that describe terrain, crops, or former landowners that official French cartography gradually absorbed. In a neighbourhood where properties were historically bounded by natural features rather than grid streets, such names stuck.

The square itself is modest: a widening of the local street pattern, a place where the neighbourhood can breathe. From the surrounding streets the Mediterranean is audible if not visible, and the light in this district has the particular quality that comes from being surrounded by white rock and water. Walkers heading toward the Corniche Kennedy or the Parc du Pharo pass through without always noticing they have crossed the square.
