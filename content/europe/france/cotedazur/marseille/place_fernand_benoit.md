Fernand Benoît (1892–1969) spent his career pulling ancient Marseille out of the ground. As director of the Borély museum — just up the road — he excavated the Greek emporion, the Roman docks, the early Christian necropolises. His work on Roman wrecks recovered from the harbour gave the city some of its most significant ancient finds.

The surrounding neighbourhood developed in the late 19th century as the bourgeoisie expanded toward the coast — villas, residential blocks, the Borély park and its château nearby. The name on this street sign has local coherence: the man dug the past out of this very landscape.

He is one of those careful, prolific figures whose names cities attach to quiet streets as a form of civic memory that most passers-by never investigate.
