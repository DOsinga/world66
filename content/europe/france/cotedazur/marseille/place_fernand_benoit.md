Fernand Benoît (1892–1969) was the leading archaeologist of Provence in his generation. As curator and then director of the Borély museum, which holds Marseille's Greco-Roman collections, he spent decades excavating and interpreting the deep layers of the city's past — the Greek emporion, the Roman docks, the early Christian necropolises. His work on the Roman wrecks recovered from the Marseille harbour gave the city some of its most significant ancient finds.

The square sits in the southern 8th arrondissement, a district of villas and residential blocks that developed in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries as the city's bourgeoisie expanded toward the coast and the Parc Borély. The park itself — with its château, rose garden, and the Borély museum — is a short walk away, which gives the name on this street sign a particular local coherence.

Benoît also wrote extensively on Ligurian and Celto-Ligurian art, and his surveys of the Provençal countryside helped establish which oppidum sites warranted serious excavation. He is one of those figures — careful, prolific, authoritative in his time — whose names cities attach to quiet streets and squares as a form of civic memory that most passers-by never investigate.
