Florence Arthaud (1957–2015) was France's most celebrated offshore racing sailor of the 1980s and 1990s. In 1990 she won the Route du Rhum — the solo transatlantic race from Saint-Malo to Guadeloupe — setting a new course record and becoming the first woman to win a major offshore race. The French press called her *la fiancée de l'Atlantique*. She raced with exceptional technical skill and physical endurance across the North Atlantic in conditions that had ended the careers of many male competitors.

This square sits close to the southern shoreline of Marseille, in the district that trails out toward Les Goudes and the beginning of the Calanques. It is a fitting address for a sailor: the Mediterranean is visible or audible from here, and the limestone cliffs that drop into deep blue water begin just beyond the last streets of the city. Arthaud grew up racing in French waters and understood the Mediterranean's particular character — deceptively calm, then violent — as well as the ocean routes she later dominated.

Arthaud died in March 2015 in a helicopter collision over Argentina during filming for a television programme. She was 57. The square was named in her memory in the years following her death, joining a pattern of Marseille's newer public spaces commemorating figures whose relationship to the sea or to French civic life gave the city a reason to remember them.
