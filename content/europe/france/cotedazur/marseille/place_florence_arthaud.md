In 1990 Florence Arthaud sailed solo across the North Atlantic and won the Route du Rhum. She was the first woman to win a major offshore race, set a course record doing it, and the French press called her la fiancée de l'Atlantique. The accolade stuck because it was accurate: she was an exceptional technical sailor in some of the hardest conditions on the ocean.

The square sits where Marseille trails out toward Les Goudes and the start of the Calanques. The Mediterranean is visible from here — pale and flat or steel-dark depending on the day, nothing like the North Atlantic that made her famous. Arthaud died in March 2015 in a helicopter accident in Argentina. She was 57.

The city named this coastal square for her in the years after.
