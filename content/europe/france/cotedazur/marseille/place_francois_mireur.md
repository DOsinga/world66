In 1792 François Mireur showed up at a Republican banquet in Marseille and sang a song he'd brought from Strasbourg: the Chant de guerre pour l'armée du Rhin, written by Rouget de Lisle. The Marseille volunteers adopted it and marched north singing it. Paris heard them in the streets and called it La Marseillaise.

Mireur was in his early twenties. He went on to serve as a military surgeon, then a general under Napoleon, and was killed in Egypt in 1798 at 28. The anthem he introduced outlived him by centuries. The song was not composed in Marseille, but it was adopted and transmitted by the city — which in this case amounts to the same thing.

The square named after him is quiet, central, unremarkable. The historical weight is entirely in the name above the street.
