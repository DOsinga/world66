Place Gaby Pasero occupies a quiet corner of the 9th arrondissement, one of the southern residential districts where Marseille spreads out from the dense urban core into a pattern of individual houses, modest apartment blocks, and local commerce that serves the neighbourhood rather than visitors. The streets here are named after figures who mattered at the local rather than the national scale — teachers, civic activists, neighbourhood administrators — whose names few people outside the city would recognise.

The square functions as the kind of space these districts produce: a widening of the street with some seating, perhaps a few trees for shade, used mostly by people who live nearby. The 9th arrondissement runs south from the Prado beaches toward the more rugged ground of the Massif des Calanques, and its character shifts block by block from urban apartment to Provençal village atmosphere.

Sainte-Marguerite, the neighbourhood name for this part of the 9th, has its own church and market traditions that predate the city's administrative absorption of the surrounding villages in the nineteenth century. Squares like this one preserve that village-scale social life within the larger grid — a place where the neighbourhood recognises itself, even if the name on the sign has faded from common memory.
