The 9th arrondissement is where Marseille starts loosening its grip. Dense apartments give way to individual houses, streets get quieter, and the Massif des Calanques begins coming into view on the horizon. Sainte-Marguerite — the village-within-the-city that gives this corner its identity — was absorbed into Marseille administratively long ago but kept its own church, its market, its pace.

Place Gaby Pasero is that kind of square: not a grand civic statement, just where the neighbourhood pauses. Shaded seating, a few trees, people cutting through on their morning errands. The name belongs to Marseille's municipal tradition of honouring local figures — teachers, neighbourhood administrators — whose work mattered here rather than anywhere else.
