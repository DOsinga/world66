Stand here and you can see both anchors of the neighbourhood: the neo-Gothic towers of the Réformés church downhill to the south, and the Palais Longchamp uphill to the north. In between, limestone apartment buildings with their wrought-iron balconies — the Second Empire version of prosperous Marseille, built for magistrates, notaries, and ship-owning families.

The Gantès were that kind of family. Provincial bourgeoisie with enough civic standing to get a square named for them, not famous enough to make it onto the national maps. The neighbourhood has kept its architectural dignity even as the population turned over. Worth walking through rather than just past.
