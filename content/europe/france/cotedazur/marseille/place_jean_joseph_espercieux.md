Most people walk past his work without knowing it. Bas-reliefs and decorative figures on the Arc de Triomphe in Paris — Espercieux carved some of those. He won the Prix de Rome in 1785, spent years in Italy absorbing antiquity, then returned to France and worked his way through three political regimes: Roman civic virtue under the Republic, Imperial grandeur under Napoleon, classicism again under the Bourbons. You had to be adaptable.

Born in Marseille in 1757, he made his career in Paris, became a professor at the École des Beaux-Arts, and died in 1840. The city named this square for him — a modest space in the neighbourhood between the Réformés church and the Palais Longchamp — the standard way French cities claim successful artists who left for the capital and succeeded there.
