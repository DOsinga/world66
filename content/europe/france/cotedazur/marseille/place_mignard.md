Pierre Mignard spent decades in Rome before Louis XIV called him back to France, and by then he had one target: to overtake Charles Le Brun, the man who ran French painting like a monopoly. When Le Brun died in 1690, Mignard got everything — First Painter to the King, director of the Académie Royale de Peinture.

His portraits defined how the court saw itself. He painted Molière, who was his close friend, and Molière returned the favour by writing a poem in praise of Mignard's great ceiling fresco at the Val-de-Grâce in Paris. A painter celebrated in verse by the greatest playwright of the age tells you roughly where Mignard stood.

He was born in Troyes, not Marseille, and made his career well away from here. The square in the quiet southern 9th is one of many across France where a name from the artistic tradition gets attached to a street corner — Marseille positioning itself as a cultural capital, not just a port.
