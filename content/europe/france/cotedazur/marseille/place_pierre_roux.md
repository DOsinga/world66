If this square is named for Pierre Roux-Alphéran, it's named for the right person. Between 1846 and 1848 he published *Les rues de Marseille*, a two-volume inventory of every street in the city: what each one was called, what had stood there before, how the names had changed across the centuries. Researchers still consult it. It's the kind of project that requires decades of obsession and the conviction that a city's layers are worth recording precisely.

The square sits in the 9th arrondissement, built up mostly after the Second World War. In a neighbourhood with little pre-war architecture, attaching a nineteenth-century historian's name to a corner does something: it anchors recent construction in a longer continuity.

The square itself is modest — a bench, some shade, a pause between errands. Most people passing through probably don't know whose name is on the sign.
