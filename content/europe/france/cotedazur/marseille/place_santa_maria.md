The chapel of Santa Maria Maggiore stood here for two centuries before it was swept away in the 1850s. The demolitions that built the Cathédrale de la Major and drove the rue de la République through the old port quarter destroyed most of what had existed before. The square's name is almost the only trace left.

The Fort Saint-Jean beside it tells a different story about power. Louis XIV built it in 1660, right after Marseille rose in revolt against royal authority. Look at how it's positioned: yes, it guards the harbour entrance — but its cannons also pointed toward the city. Paris wanted Marseille to understand exactly where it stood.

Today the fort is part of the MuCEM complex, connected by the elevated walkway that passes directly above the square. In summer this becomes a busy transition point between the museum terraces and the lanes of Le Panier climbing above. Off-season it goes quiet again — a passage between the quayside and the old quarter, used mainly by people who live nearby.
