Place Santa Maria sits at the northern edge of the Vieux-Port, near the base of Le Panier and the Fort Saint-Jean, in one of the most historically charged corners of Marseille. The name recalls the chapel of Santa Maria Maggiore — built in the 17th century and demolished in the 1850s to make way for the grand reconstruction of the harbour quarter that produced the Cathédrale de la Major and the rue de la République. That demolition swept away much of the old port neighbourhood; the square's name is one of the few traces of what stood here before.

The fort beside it — Fort Saint-Jean — was built by Louis XIV in 1660, partly to guard the harbour entrance and partly to overawe a city that had just risen in revolt against royal authority. The fort's positioning tells you something about how the French state regarded Marseille: its cannons pointed as much toward the city as toward the sea. Today it is part of the MuCEM complex, and the elevated walkway connecting it to the new museum building crosses directly over the square.

In summer, Place Santa Maria fills with visitors arriving from the MuCEM terraces and the Fort Saint-Jean gardens — a transition point between the old and new Marseille. Outside the tourist season, it is a quieter passage between the quayside and the steep lanes of Le Panier, used mainly by residents making their way up from the port to the neighbourhood above.
