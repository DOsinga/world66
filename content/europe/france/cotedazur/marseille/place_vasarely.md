Victor Vasarely invented Op Art — the geometric, kinetic abstraction that makes flat surfaces appear to ripple and pulse. He moved from Hungary to France in 1930 and spent decades developing a visual language that felt, in the 1960s, like the future arriving in colour.

His lasting monument isn't in Marseille. It's in Aix-en-Provence: a foundation he designed with architect Jean Sonnier in 1976 — a cluster of hexagonal black-and-white towers visible from the motorway between the two cities. Vasarely wanted art built into architecture and public space, not hung in galleries. The foundation is that idea at full scale.

The square here is modest. But the name keeps one of the twentieth century's genuinely original visual thinkers in the street-level conversation of the neighbourhood.
