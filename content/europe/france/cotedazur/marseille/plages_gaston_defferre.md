These beaches are not natural. The foreshore here was rocky coastline until engineers in the 1970s used rubble from Vieux-Port excavations to push the sea back and create a two-kilometre arc of sand. The Prado beaches opened in 1984, and when Gaston Defferre died in 1986, they took his name.

Defferre governed Marseille for thirty-three consecutive years — the longest mayoral tenure of any major French city in the modern era. As Mitterrand's interior minister in 1981, he passed the law that transferred real executive power from Paris-appointed prefects to elected local and regional councils. For a country with centuries of Jacobin centralisation, that was a constitutional revolution.

The beaches themselves are free, bus-served, and properly democratic — the full cross-section of the city laid out on sand from June to September. The sunset behind the Frioul islands is worth staying for.
