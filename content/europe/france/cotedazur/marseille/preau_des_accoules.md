The Préau des Accoules occupies a site of exceptional historical resonance in the Le Panier quarter, the oldest continuously inhabited part of Marseille. A *préau* is a covered outdoor space — a sheltered courtyard or arcade — and this one stands on or near the ground where Notre-Dame-des-Accoules once rose. That church, whose origins went back to at least the twelfth century and possibly to early Christian times, was the ecclesiastical heart of the ancient city before it was largely demolished in the nineteenth century; only its bell tower survives today, incorporated into a later building on the Montée-des-Accoules.

The name *Accoules* itself comes from the Latin *aqua*, a reference to the presence of water — springs or cisterns that made this hilltop habitable long before the Roman settlement. The neighbourhood around it grew into the oldest urban fabric of Marseille, a dense tangle of lanes and stepped streets that has been continuously occupied for over two and a half millennia.

Today the Préau des Accoules functions as a children's cultural centre, hosting workshops, exhibitions, and creative activities aimed at younger visitors and local families. It draws a very different audience from the museums and galleries of the waterfront, and its courtyard provides a calm, shaded refuge in what is otherwise one of the most atmospheric and historically layered corners of any city in France.
