Notre-Dame-des-Accoules went back at least to the twelfth century, possibly to early Christian times. It was the ecclesiastical heart of Le Panier before most of it was demolished in the nineteenth century. The bell tower survived — it's still visible, incorporated into a later building on the Montée-des-Accoules. Worth spotting when you're in the neighbourhood.

The name Accoules comes from the Latin *aqua* — water. Springs here made this hilltop habitable long before the Greek settlers arrived, and the name stuck across two and a half millennia. The préau itself, a covered courtyard on this historically loaded ground, now runs as a children's cultural centre: workshops, exhibitions, activities aimed at local families.

It draws a very different crowd from the waterfront museums, and the courtyard offers one of the calmest, shadiest refuges in one of the most historically dense corners of any city in France.
