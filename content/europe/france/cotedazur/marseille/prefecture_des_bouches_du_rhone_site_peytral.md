The Préfecture des Bouches-du-Rhône, the administrative seat of France's most populated southern department, operates from more than one location in Marseille. The site Peytral — named after the Rue Peytral, a street that itself commemorates a local figure — is one of the prefecture's secondary addresses, housing services that have expanded beyond the capacity of the main building near the Place Félix-Baret.

The main prefecture building, a grand nineteenth-century structure befitting the importance of a department centred on one of France's largest cities, was built to handle the administrative demands of a rapidly industrialising region. As those demands grew over the twentieth century, overflow offices were established in adjacent streets, with the Peytral site providing additional space for departments dealing with permitting, residency documentation, and other civil functions that generate high public footfall.

The surrounding neighbourhood is one of Marseille's more solidly bourgeois quarters, its wide streets lined with Haussmann-era stone buildings that give it a more Parisian character than most of the city. Visitors with administrative business at the prefecture are advised to check in advance which site their particular service occupies, as the multiple buildings can cause confusion.
