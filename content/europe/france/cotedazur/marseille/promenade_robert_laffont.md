Robert Laffont was born in Marseille in 1916 and founded his publishing house in 1941 — in occupied France, with paper rationed and German censors watching. He published Boris Vian, Henri Charrière's *Papillon*, and a succession of major foreign authors in translation. His guiding rule: follow curiosity, not formula. He chased whatever interested him for eighty years.

He died in 2021 at 104. His house survived him, absorbed into a corporate group but still bearing his name — and still, occasionally, finding the unexpected book that becomes a phenomenon.

Marseille placed this promenade near the western waterfront, in the cultural district built up around the 2013 European Capital of Culture year. A fitting spot to remember someone who spent his life arguing that good books could change how people see the world.
