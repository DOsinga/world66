The Résidence Argo takes its name from one of the most celebrated vessels in Greek mythology: the Argo, built for Jason and crewed by the Argonauts on their quest for the Golden Fleece. The choice of name is fitting for a port city. Marseille itself was founded by Greek sailors from Phocaea around 600 BCE, and the sea has defined the city's identity for nearly three thousand years. Naming a residential building after an ancient ship is a quiet nod to that maritime heritage, placing the address within a longer story of seafarers and voyagers who made the Mediterranean their highway.

The Argonauts' voyage — one of the great adventure stories of antiquity — passed through waters not far from Marseille in some tellings of the myth, as Jason's route from Iolcos through the Adriatic and down the western Mediterranean placed him in Provençal waters. Whether the building's developers had this in mind or simply liked the resonance of a famous ship's name is unknown, but *Argo* appears on residences and apartment blocks across port cities of the Mediterranean basin, each one drawing on the same cultural shorthand for maritime ambition.

The building itself is a mid-to-late twentieth-century residential block, typical of the apartment construction that filled in Marseille's northern districts during the postwar decades of rapid urban growth. It offers a place to live, not a landmark to visit — but its name, lettered above the entrance, carries more history than most building signs in the city.
