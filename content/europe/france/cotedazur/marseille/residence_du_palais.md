The Résidence du Palais sits in the administrative heart of Marseille, a district shaped in the nineteenth century by the construction of the Palais de Justice and the grand institutional buildings that cluster around it. The name *du Palais* — of the Palace — refers to that courthouse, an imposing neo-classical structure whose columns and pediment give this part of the city a weight and formality unusual in a Mediterranean port town.

Naming residential buildings after nearby landmarks or institutions has long been common practice in French property development, and *Résidence du Palais* is one of the more straightforward examples: an address in close proximity to a notable building borrows its prestige. For lawyers, court officials, and others with daily business at the Palais de Justice, an apartment here offered the practical advantage of a short walk to work in an era before widespread car ownership.

The surrounding streets contain a mixture of nineteenth-century stone buildings, postwar infill, and the occasional hôtel particulier converted to offices. This is a working neighbourhood of Marseille, occupied by legal and administrative professionals rather than tourists, and the Résidence du Palais is part of that fabric — a quietly situated address in a city that is otherwise known for its noise, colour, and maritime energy.
