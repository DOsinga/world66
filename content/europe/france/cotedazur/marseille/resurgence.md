A résurgence is where underground water, having threaded through fissures and cavities in karst rock, re-emerges at the surface. Marseille sits on dramatic Jurassic limestone — the same white calcaire that forms the calanques and the hills ringing the city — and underground systems threading through this rock are a defining feature of the landscape.

Out here in the eastern districts, the geology is close to the surface. The massifs of Saint-Cyr and the Plan-de-Cuques plateau are just beyond, and the connection between the built environment and the underlying rock is easier to feel than it is in the city centre.

Whether this résurgence is a natural spring, a channelled water feature, or a public artwork taking the geological phenomenon as its theme, the name acknowledges something true about this particular patch of Provençal ground.
