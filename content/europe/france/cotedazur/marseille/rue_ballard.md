Victor Ballard designed Les Halles de Paris in 1853 — the great iron-and-glass market halls that made buying vegetables feel like entering a cathedral. In 1971, the city tore them down.

This modest residential street in central Marseille is what remains of his reputation locally. Washing lines, a tabac, the smell of bread. It is exactly the opposite of Les Halles — human-scaled, quiet, unmonumental.

Which is either ironic or appropriate. Ballard believed good architecture should serve ordinary daily life. This is that.
