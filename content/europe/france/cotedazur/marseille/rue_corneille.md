Pierre Corneille wrote Le Cid in 1637 and it caused an immediate uproar. The Académie française weighed in. Cardinal Richelieu, who fancied himself a playwright, was reportedly not pleased. None of it stopped the play — it's still in repertoire today.

Streets named Corneille appear in virtually every French city. He wrote the heroic dilemmas — honour versus love, duty versus desire — that French theatre kept wrestling with for another century.

This Rue Corneille sits in the southern part of the city. Apartments, balconies, a cat on a ledge. The drama is all in the name.
