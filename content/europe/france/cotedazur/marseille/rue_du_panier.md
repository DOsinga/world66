Rue du Panier climbs through the oldest inhabited part of Marseille, following a ridge that was the centre of Greek Massalia and later the medieval city's densest neighbourhood. The name — "the basket" — is variously attributed to the rounded shape of the hill or to an inn sign that once marked the street's upper end. Whatever its origin, the Panier was for centuries a working district of fishermen, sailors, and artisans packed into a tight grid of alleys above the Vieux-Port.

The fabric visible today is largely 17th and 18th century. The district narrowly escaped the catastrophic German demolition of January 1943, when the Nazi occupiers cleared around 1,500 buildings at the base of the hill — displacing 20,000 residents — on the pretext of sanitation. Rue du Panier survived at the edge of that cleared zone, which is why the street still has genuine age to it while the streets below were rebuilt after the war.

Today the Panier draws visitors with its pastel-painted facades, lines of washing strung between windows, and accumulated street art — Seth's large-scale murals and Miss.Tic's stencilled text-portraits are among the most photographed. Steep staircases drop from the street to lower levels of the quarter, opening sudden views over the port. The neighbourhood remains residential and lived-in alongside its tourism; the tension between the two is part of what makes it worth the climb.
