This street climbs the oldest continuously inhabited hill in Marseille. Greek colonists from Phocaea were here around 600 BC. The name — "the basket" — might come from the hill's rounded shape, or an old inn sign at the top. Nobody's completely sure.

In January 1943, the Nazi occupiers cleared 1,500 buildings at the base of this hill and expelled 20,000 residents, citing sanitation. Rue du Panier survived at the edge of that destruction. The genuine age you see here — 17th and 18th century facades, worn stone steps — is there because of that margin.

Today it's pastel facades, Seth's large-scale murals, Miss.Tic's stencilled portraits. Still residential, still lived-in. The tension between neighbourhood and tourism is visible in every block.
