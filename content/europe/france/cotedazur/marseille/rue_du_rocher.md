Marseille's eastern arrondissements sit on limestone that barely bothers to hide itself. The same white rock that forms the calanques cliffs erupts through back gardens here, sits under the tarmac at ankle depth, reflects the midday sun until everything looks bleached.

Rue du Rocher — Rock Street — is in this eastern stretch of the city, away from the port crowds and the Canebière. More village than city out here: terracotta roofs, wrought-iron balconies, café conversations in Marseillais French.

The street routes around the outcrops rather than through them. Builders did the same thing when they laid this neighbourhood out. In the late afternoon, the limestone walls go warm and golden. Worth the detour.
