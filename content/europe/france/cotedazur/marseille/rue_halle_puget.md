Pierre Puget was born in Marseille in 1620. He went to Rome and Florence to train, came back and spent his career working in Toulon and his home city, and produced what is arguably the finest French Baroque sculpture of the 17th century — the Milon de Crotone, now in the Louvre.

The street is named for a 19th-century covered market hall that bore his name. Naming a market after a sculptor is a very Marseille thing — the city remembers its own, especially when the rest of France eventually agrees with them.

Walking between the Vieux-Port and Le Panier, you move through layers of naming. Puget's is the one that earned its place internationally.
