The Capuchin convent this street is named after vanished in the Revolution. What replaced it is better: the most aromatic block in France. Walk into Noailles and the spice market finds you before you find it — a wave of cumin, coriander, and rose water that drifts into the adjacent streets on a still morning.

The shops run wall to wall: open sacks of ras el hanout and sumac, pyramids of dried figs and dates, mint and jasmine sold loose by the handful, harissa ground to order. This has been the heart of Marseille's North African community since the postwar years. The Marché des Capucins just off the street adds fresh produce to the mix. There is nowhere else in France outside Paris that does this at this scale.

Noailles gets called the belly of Marseille. Walk this street and you'll understand why — not as a metaphor, but literally. This is where the city feeds itself.
