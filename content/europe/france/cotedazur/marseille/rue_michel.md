Rue Michel is a street in the southern reaches of Marseille, in the quieter residential arrondissements that lie between the city centre and the limestone hills of the Massif de Marseilleveyre. This part of the city has a different character from the bustling Vieux-Port or the gritty northern districts: it is more suburban in feel, with villa gardens, plane-tree-lined avenues, and a pace of life that reflects proximity to the open countryside of the calanques rather than to the port.

The name Michel is among the most common in France, and streets bearing it exist in virtually every French city. Without a commemorative plaque specifying which Michel is honoured here — whether a local mayor, a soldier from one of France's wars, a benefactor to the commune of Mazargues (the village that was absorbed into Marseille in 1852), or simply the family name of a former landowner — the street's dedication is difficult to trace. Many streets in this part of the city carry the names of individuals who were significant to the pre-annexation village rather than to the wider city.

The street serves principally the residents of its neighbourhood, connecting smaller roads in a web of local circulation that most visitors to Marseille never see. It is the kind of address that tells you something about how a large city is made up: not just monuments and grand avenues, but thousands of ordinary streets bearing ordinary names, each with a buried history of its own.
