Most visitors never get here. The southern arrondissements between the city centre and the Massif de Marseilleveyre are residential Marseille — villa gardens, plane-tree avenues, a pace set by the proximity of the calanques rather than the port.

Rue Michel is that kind of street. The name is common enough that pinpointing which Michel is honoured here is impossible without a commemorative plaque — it could be a local mayor, a soldier from one of France's wars, or simply the family name of someone who owned land here before Mazargues was absorbed into the city in 1852.

That anonymity is part of the point. A city this size is built from thousands of streets like this one — ordinary names, buried histories, and a daily life that has nothing to do with the postcard version.
