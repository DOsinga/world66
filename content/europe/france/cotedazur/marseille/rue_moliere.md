The 17th-century playwright who called himself Molière never set foot in Marseille. That didn't stop the city from naming a street after him in the 19th century — part of a national project to plant canonical French cultural names on freshly regularised streets. Corneille, Racine, Voltaire, Molière: you'll find this pattern in every town of any size in France.

The street earned its cultural name partly by luck of location. The Opéra de Marseille anchors one end. The current building dates from 1924, rebuilt after a fire, and it's one of the largest opera houses in France outside Paris. Marseille had an opera house by the 1680s — one of the earliest in the country. The season runs opera, ballet, and orchestral concerts.

The rest of the street is bars and restaurants: theatre audiences before and after shows, local workers at lunch, late-night drinkers avoiding the tourist crush at the port. Practical, a bit unglamorous, entirely functional — rather unlike the playwright it's named for.
