Rue Molière runs through the centre of Marseille's first arrondissement, one block back from the Vieux-Port, taking its name from Jean-Baptiste Poquelin — the 17th-century playwright who performed under the name Molière and never set foot in Marseille. The naming is not coincidence: it is part of a systematic 19th-century programme in which French cities assigned canonical literary and cultural names to newly regularised streets, asserting a shared national culture through urban nomenclature. Corneille, Racine, Voltaire, and Molière appear in this pattern in virtually every town of any size in France.

The street's principal landmark is the Opéra de Marseille, which anchors one end of the block. The current building dates from 1924, replacing an earlier opera house destroyed by fire; it is one of the largest opera houses in France outside Paris, with a grand neo-classical facade that announces its presence from the adjacent Place Reyer. The opera runs a full season of opera, ballet, and orchestral concerts, drawing on a long tradition — Marseille had an opera house by the 1680s, among the earliest in France.

The bars and restaurants concentrated along the rest of the street serve a mixed clientele: theatre and opera audiences before and after performances, local workers at lunch, and late-night drinkers who find the Vieux-Port end too crowded. The street occupies a middle position between the tourist strip of the port and the more residential streets inland, which gives it a practical character that the purely decorative name does not quite capture.
