S/Lt stands for Sous-Lieutenant — the most junior commissioned rank in the French army. Louis Biroard held that rank when he died, almost certainly in one of the two World Wars that put plaques like this one on walls all over Marseille.

He would have been young. Sous-lieutenants are officers who have just finished their training, or soldiers promoted from the ranks for ability or bravery. The name Biroard suggests roots in southern France or Corsica, both regions that gave heavily to France's military losses in the twentieth century.

This kind of plaque is not a monument. It is a specific name on a specific wall, placed so that neighbours would pass it every day and remember. No grand inscription, no ceremony — just a quiet insistence that this particular man existed and died and should not be completely forgotten.
