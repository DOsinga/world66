The designation *S/Lt* stands for *Sous-Lieutenant*, the junior commissioned officer rank in the French army — equivalent to a second lieutenant. The plaque or marker bearing the name S/Lt Biroard Louis commemorates a young man who died while serving France, most likely in one of the two World Wars that left their mark on virtually every street in French cities through exactly this kind of discrete, building-mounted memorial.

Louis Biroard would have been in his late teens or early twenties when he was commissioned as a sous-lieutenant, a rank typically held by officers who had just completed military training or who were promoted from the ranks for conspicuous ability or bravery. The surname Biroard suggests roots in southern France or possibly Corsica, both regions that contributed heavily to French military losses in the conflicts of the twentieth century.

Plaques of this type, bearing name, rank, and sometimes date and place of death, are found throughout Marseille, affixed to the facades of buildings near where the deceased lived or near an institution with which they were associated. They are among the most intimate forms of war memorial — not a grand monument but a specific name on a specific wall, placed so that neighbours would pass it daily and remember. S/Lt Biroard Louis is one of the many young men from this city whose life was cut short before it had properly begun, and his plaque is a quiet insistence that he should not be forgotten.
