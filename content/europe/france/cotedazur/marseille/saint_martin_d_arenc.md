Saint-Martin d'Arenc stands in the Arenc district, north of the Vieux-Port and the Joliette ferry terminals, in a neighbourhood that was shaped entirely by the industrial port. The workers who loaded and unloaded the ships, the dockers and warehouse hands of the 19th and 20th centuries, were the parish that this church served. The dedication to Saint Martin — the Roman soldier who famously cut his cloak to share with a beggar before becoming a bishop — carried a particular resonance in a community built on labour.

The Arenc district is now in the middle of one of France's largest urban regeneration schemes. The Euroméditerranée project, launched in the 1990s and still ongoing, is redeveloping the northern port fringe into a district of offices, housing, and cultural facilities. The Villa Méditerranée and the MuCEM museum have already transformed the southern end of this zone. Arenc is further along the coast and is following more slowly, with new residential towers rising alongside the surviving industrial fabric.

Saint-Martin d'Arenc is part of what remains of the old neighbourhood — a reference point for the community that has lived here through all the changes, and a reminder that beneath the regeneration branding, there was always a parish, a street life, and a working population that called this place home.
