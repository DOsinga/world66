Jerusalem's Old City has seven historic gates in its Ottoman walls — Jaffa, Damascus, Herod's, Lions', Golden (sealed since Byzantine times), Dung, and Zion. Suleiman the Magnificent built the walls in the 1530s. Each gate accumulated centuries of pilgrims, armies, and ordinary commerce.

This installation brings that symbolism to a Marseille neighbourhood with real Levantine roots. The Armenian community here — many descended from survivors of the 1915 genocide — has maintained spiritual ties to the Holy Land. The Sephardic Jewish community, with North African and Middle Eastern origins, shares that orientation. An artwork invoking Jerusalem's gates speaks to both, in a port city that has always been a point of arrival for people carrying memories of elsewhere.

Stand here and you're holding two geographies at once: the Marseille street around you, and a walled city thousands of kilometres away.
