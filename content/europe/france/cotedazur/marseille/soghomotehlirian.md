In March 1921, Soghomon Tehlirian walked up to Talaat Pasha on a Berlin street and shot him dead. Talaat was the Ottoman interior minister who had organised the deportations and massacres of the Armenian Genocide — the killing that had wiped out most of Tehlirian's own family. He made no attempt to run.

At his trial, the defence put the genocide itself on trial. Testimony about the massacres filled the courtroom. The Berlin jury heard it all and acquitted him in just over an hour.

The case mattered beyond the verdict. It fed directly into the legal thinking that produced the concept of crimes against humanity — formalised at Nuremberg twenty-five years later. Marseille has one of France's largest Armenian communities, many of them descended from 1915 survivors who arrived at this port. The memorial stands in that neighbourhood.
