Space Invader is the pseudonymous French street artist who has spent decades tiling pixelated alien characters — borrowed from the 1978 arcade game — onto the walls and ledges of cities across the world. Each city receives its own series code: Paris is PA, Los Angeles is LA, Tokyo is TYO, and Marseille is MARS. The artist maintains a meticulous map and scoring system; finding and photographing each piece earns points in an unofficial global game that has developed a devoted following.

MARS 30 is the thirtieth piece placed in Marseille, a city the artist has returned to repeatedly. The tiles are typically found high on building corners or façades, set in proper ceramic mosaic so they weather far better than spray paint. Their lo-fi, retro aesthetic — eight-bit creatures in primary colours — sits in cheerful contrast to Haussmann stonework, Belle Époque ironwork, or brutalist concrete, depending on where exactly this one landed. Part of the pleasure is craning your neck and suddenly spotting a small alien that has been watching the street below for years while pedestrians walked past oblivious.

Marseille rewards Space Invader hunters because the city's dense, walkable neighbourhoods — the Panier, the areas around the Vieux-Port, the slopes above the Canebière — pack many pieces into walkable range. Downloading the artist's official Flash Invaders app lets you scan and score each find; MARS 30 is one tile in what amounts to a distributed, permanent public art exhibition that has been accumulating since the late 1990s.
