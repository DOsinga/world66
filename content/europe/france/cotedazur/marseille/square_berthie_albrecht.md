Berthie Albrecht was born in Marseille in 1893. By the time France fell in 1940, she'd already worked as a nurse, lived in England, and built a reputation as a feminist activist. She co-founded Combat — one of the largest resistance networks in the Unoccupied Zone — with Henri Frenay, managing communications, producing the clandestine newspaper, running logistics under conditions of constant risk.

Vichy officials had her committed to a psychiatric hospital. She escaped. The Gestapo caught her in May 1943 and she died in Fresnes prison in June. The exact circumstances remain unclear.

She was the first woman awarded the Compagnon de la Libération, France's highest resistance honour. This small square near the Abbaye Saint-Victor, in the city where she was born, is her monument. It's modest in scale, which doesn't match what she did.
