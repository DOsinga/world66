Square Berthie Albrecht sits close to the Abbaye Saint-Victor on the south side of the Vieux-Port, in the residential zone between the port and the Endoume quarter. It is a small square, reflecting the small scale of the urban fabric here, where the streets run between the quay and the rising ground behind the abbey.

Berthie Albrecht (1893–1943) was born in Marseille into a Protestant family of Swiss-French origin. She trained as a nurse, lived in England for a period, and returned to France in the 1930s already politically engaged — as a feminist activist and, eventually, as a key figure in the early Resistance. After the German occupation she co-founded Combat, one of the largest Resistance networks in the Unoccupied Zone, with Henri Frenay. She managed communications, produced the clandestine newspaper, and organised logistics in conditions of extreme risk.

She was arrested multiple times, escaped once from a psychiatric hospital to which she had been sent by Vichy officials, and was finally arrested by the Gestapo in May 1943. She died in Fresnes prison in June 1943; the exact circumstances remain unclear. She was the first woman awarded the Compagnon de la Libération, France's highest Resistance honour. The square in her birth city is a small monument to a Marseillaise who risked and lost everything.
