La Verdière is a small village in the Var hills, eighty kilometres from here. In the Second World War, its children were among those taken — the specifics are the kind that get lost from official records but not from community memory.

The postwar practice of naming public spaces after wartime victims was deliberate. A name on a street map, consulted daily by people who live here, carries different weight than a plaque on a wall. Residents don't have to seek it out — they just walk past it, and the name accumulates in ordinary memory.

This square is in the quieter, more residential northern arrondissements — the Marseille of plane trees and benches and streets where people actually live. The square does what such spaces do. The name is why it's here.
