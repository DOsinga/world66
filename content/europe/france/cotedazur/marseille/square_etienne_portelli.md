Not everyone a square is named after made the history books. Marseille has always commemorated the people who shaped a particular quartier — the local official, the doctor who stayed through hard times, the schoolteacher who ran things for thirty years. Étienne Portelli almost certainly belongs to that category. The wider record of his life is thin.

The square is in the 13th arrondissement, a working district on the city's eastern edge without the tourist circuits of the Vieux-Port or the Cours Julien. The pace here is unhurried. People have lived on the same streets for generations.

Squares like this one are the punctuation marks of daily life — somewhere to sit between errands, a patch of shade, a bench, somewhere neighbours can stop and talk without standing in anyone's way. That's what this square does. It's a perfectly honourable thing to have your name on.
