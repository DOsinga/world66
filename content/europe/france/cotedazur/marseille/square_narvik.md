Narvik is a Norwegian port above the Arctic Circle. In April and May 1940, French and Norwegian forces — with British naval support — fought the Germans there and won, capturing the city in late May. It was the only significant Allied land victory of the entire Norwegian campaign, and one of the few bright spots in a catastrophic spring.

It lasted days. The French front collapsed in the south, the evacuation of Norway was ordered, and Narvik was abandoned. France fell in June. The victory that could have been a propaganda triumph became a footnote.

French towns named squares and streets after Narvik anyway — the moment of success mattered, even if it was swallowed by what followed. In Marseille, which spent the war as a Vichy free city and a transit point for thousands of refugees, these commemorative namings carry extra weight. This small planted garden near Belsunce is one of them: a modest patch of green named after a battle that was won and then immediately lost.
