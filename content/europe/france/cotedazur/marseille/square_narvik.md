Square Narvik is near the Belsunce and Noailles quarters of central Marseille, a small planted garden in the dense residential and commercial zone around the cours Belsunce. In French civic naming practice, squares named after Allied battles or Allied cities are typically commemorations of Second World War engagements and the solidarity expressed by French towns with their wartime partners.

Narvik is a Norwegian port city above the Arctic Circle, the site of two naval engagements and one land battle in April and May 1940. The Battles of Narvik were the first significant Allied land victories of the war: French and Norwegian forces, with British support, captured the city from German forces in late May 1940 and held it briefly. It was the only Allied land success of the Norwegian campaign. Within days, however, the collapse of the French front in the south forced the evacuation of Norway, and Narvik was abandoned. France fell in June 1940.

The name endures in French squares and streets as a commemoration of that brief moment of success and of the Franco-Norwegian alliance in the first months of a war that quickly became catastrophic. In Marseille, which was briefly a free city under Vichy and a major transit point for refugees fleeing occupied France, the commemorative naming of public spaces after Resistance and Allied history carries particular weight.
