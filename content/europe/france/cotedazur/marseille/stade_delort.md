Stade Delort is a neighbourhood-scale sports venue in the south of Marseille, serving local football clubs and athletic associations in a part of the city where the ground rises toward the calanques. The name Delort is associated with Marseille's civic and sporting history — Auguste Delort was a nineteenth-century figure connected to the city's public life — and the stadium carrying his name has been a fixture of local sport for decades.

Marseille's relationship with football is inseparable from the Stade Vélodrome, the vast arena where Olympique de Marseille plays and where the city's passions reach their loudest pitch. But the actual sporting life of most Marseillais happens in grounds like Stade Delort: the municipal pitches, the club terraces, the weekend leagues that fill the city's lesser-known stadiums from September to May. These venues are where youth players develop, where amateur clubs compete for local trophies, and where sport functions as social glue rather than spectacle.

The stadium's location in the southern arrondissements puts it near a residential Marseille that visitors rarely see — quieter streets, larger gardens, a slower pace than the Vieux-Port or the Cours Julien. Getting here requires local knowledge or a deliberate detour, which is precisely the point: Stade Delort belongs to its neighbourhood.
