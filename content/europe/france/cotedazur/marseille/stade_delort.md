Everyone knows the Stade Vélodrome. That is where Olympique de Marseille plays, where the city's footballing passions reach their loudest and most theatrical pitch. But the actual sporting life of most Marseillais happens in grounds like Stade Delort: municipal pitches, local clubs, weekend leagues running from September to May.

Stade Delort is a neighbourhood-scale venue in the south of the city, named after Auguste Delort, a 19th-century figure connected to the city's public life. Youth players develop here. Amateur clubs compete for local trophies. Sport functions as social glue rather than spectacle — which is what it is for most people, most of the time.

Getting here requires local knowledge or a deliberate detour. The surrounding streets are quieter, the gardens deeper, the pace slower than the centre. It belongs to its neighbourhood. That is the whole point.
