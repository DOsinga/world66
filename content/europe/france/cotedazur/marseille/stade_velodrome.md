The Stade Vélodrome was built in 1937 and has been expanded twice since — substantially for the 1998 World Cup, and again for Euro 2016, when the current roof was added. The roof changed the acoustics completely: the noise that used to escape now stays inside, and the effect during a major match is close to overwhelming. At 67,394 seats it is the second-largest stadium in France, behind only the Stade de France.

Olympique de Marseille won the Champions League in 1993, the only French club to have done so. The 1992–93 season subsequently became entangled in a match-fixing scandal — the VA-OM affair — but the European title stands, and the club's relationship with its city remains one of the most intense in European football. The ultras of the Virage Nord and Virage Sud maintain a culture of support that is choreographed, loud, and continuous for ninety minutes regardless of the score.

A match is not tourism — it is something that happens to you. If OM are playing a significant opponent at home, the city reorganises itself around the event for the entire day: the port fills up, the bars along the Corniche stay open, and the noise from the stadium is audible several kilometres away. Attend if you have any interest in football; attend even if you do not.
