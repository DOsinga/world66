The Mourepiane container terminal occupies a stretch of the northern docks where the Grand Port Maritime de Marseille handles a significant share of France's container traffic. Marseille-Fos, taken as a whole, is the largest port in France by tonnage and one of the busiest in the Mediterranean — a position it has held, in various forms, for more than two thousand years. The northern industrial zone, stretching from L'Estaque to the Étang de Berre, is the working engine of that trade.

Container shipping transformed port geography in the second half of the twentieth century. Where earlier docks required armies of dockers to load and unload break-bulk cargo by hand, containerization shifted the labour to cranes, straddle carriers, and logistics software. Mourepiane handles the steel boxes — typically twenty or forty feet long — that carry everything from electronics to clothing to food products. A single large vessel can bring three thousand containers; the terminal processes them, trucks move them inland, and the cycle repeats.

The terminal is not open to visitors, and it sits well north of the tourist itinerary — the view from the autoroute viaduct or from the hills above L'Estaque gives a better sense of its scale than anything accessible at ground level. For a city whose identity is inseparable from the sea and from trade, the northern docks are worth knowing about even if they cannot be walked through: they are where Marseille's ancient function as a Mediterranean gateway is performed, unglamorously, every day.
