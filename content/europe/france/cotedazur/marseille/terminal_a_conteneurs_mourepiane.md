The port that made Marseille great is up here in the north — and it looks nothing like the postcard. Mourepiane handles container boxes, twenty and forty feet long, arriving by the thousand on a single ship. Cranes move them, straddle carriers sort them, trucks carry them inland. No dockers unloading sacks by hand anymore; containerisation killed that in the 1960s and 70s.

You can't get in. But drive north on the autoroute viaduct and look left — the scale of it becomes clear. Or climb above L'Estaque and watch the vessels move. Marseille has been a Mediterranean gateway for 2,600 years. This is just the latest version of that function.
