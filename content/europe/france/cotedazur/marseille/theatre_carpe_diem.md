The name comes from Horace — *carpe diem*, seize the day, from the Odes — and in a theatrical context it frames live performance as an act of presence: something that happens now, that cannot be recorded or replayed, that requires you to be here. It is a statement about what theatre is for, placed in the name before any programme has been announced.

Théâtre Carpe Diem operates as a small independent venue, programming a mix of amateur theatre, semi-professional companies, and professional touring productions. This mixed ecology is characteristic of the smaller Marseille venues: they serve the local theatrical community — the companies that need a stage, the performers who are developing their craft — as much as they serve the general audience looking for an evening's entertainment. The two functions reinforce each other; a venue that only programmes established professional work cannot develop the next generation of companies.

The scale of the space keeps the work intimate. Productions here tend toward plays that depend on text and acting rather than technical apparatus — work that fits a small stage, a close audience, and a budget that does not require an armoured sponsorship deal to survive.
