The name *l'Œuvre* — the work, the opus — frames theatre as artistic labour: something made, not just performed, a product of creative effort that has its own integrity independent of its reception. It aligns this theatre with the *théâtre de création* tradition in France, in which the director or playwright's artistic vision is the primary value, in contrast to commercial theatre where the audience's preferences shape the programme.

This tradition has deep institutional support in France: the system of *théâtres nationaux* and *scènes nationales* was built on the premise that publicly funded theatre should produce work that pushes at the boundaries of what theatre can do, rather than simply reproducing what audiences already know they enjoy. Théâtre de l'Œuvre, as a smaller independent venue, works within this tradition without the resources of the national institutions — making its commitment to contemporary creation a matter of principle rather than subsidy.

Programming mixes Marseille-based directors and companies with touring productions from France's broader contemporary theatre circuit. The work is text-driven and directorial, tending toward productions where the interpretation is as significant as the play itself — where what the director does with the material is the point.
