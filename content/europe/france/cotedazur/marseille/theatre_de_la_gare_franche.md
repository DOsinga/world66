A *gare de marchandises* — a goods yard or freight depot — is a different kind of railway building from the passenger station: larger, rougher, designed for the movement of bulk goods rather than people. The railway freight infrastructure that served Marseille's industrial economy in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries left large footprints across the northern parts of the city; as rail freight declined, these buildings became available for other uses.

The Théâtre de la Gare Franche occupies one such repurposed space, in an area — connected to the Belle de Mai cultural cluster — where post-industrial conversion has created one of the densest concentrations of cultural infrastructure outside central Marseille. The *franche* of the name suggests a free zone: space without conditions, where creation is not subject to the constraints of the market or the institution.

The spatial character of the converted depot — volume, light, industrial rawness — shapes the programming. Work staged here can use the full scale of the space, which suits theatre that thinks architecturally, that treats the room itself as part of the dramaturgy. The venue hosts both resident companies and visiting productions, within the broader ecology of the Belle de Mai area.
