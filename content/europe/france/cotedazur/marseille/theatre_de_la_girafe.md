title: Théâtre de la Girafe
type: poi

A small theatre in Marseille's 2nd arrondissement carrying one of the city's more historically resonant animal names — the giraffe arrived in France for the first time through this very port. In 1827, a young female giraffe named Zarafa disembarked at Marseille after a sea voyage from Alexandria, a diplomatic gift from Muhammad Ali, Ottoman Viceroy of Egypt, to King Charles X. She wintered in the city, was fitted with a custom leather coat against the Provençal cold, and walked north to Paris the following spring, becoming the sensation of the age.

The Théâtre de la Girafe takes that improbable history as its emblem. Housed in a small storefront space in the port quarter, it programs intimate productions — contemporary drama, devised work, and pieces that sit at the edges of convention. The giraffe is a fitting mascot for a theatre that looks at ordinary things from an unusual height, or arrives in unexpected places.

Productions tend toward the small-scale and experimental, with a leaning toward new French writing and work that wouldn't survive in the city's subsidised houses. It is the kind of theatre where the director might be selling tickets at the door, and where the performance begins before you realise it has.
