The Théâtre de la Mer operates in summer at the Vallon des Auffes, where the coastal rock forms a natural bowl above the tiny harbour. The setting is one of the most unusual in Marseille's cultural calendar: performers on a stage at water level with the Mediterranean as backdrop, and the audience arranged on stepped terracing cut into the limestone above. The programme runs to jazz concerts, flamenco, classical music, and theatre — genres that travel well outdoors and can contend with the ambient noise of the sea.

The Vallon des Auffes itself is worth visiting before or after any performance. It is one of the few corners of Marseille's coastline that has survived largely unchanged from the pre-industrial era — a crescent-shaped inlet just wide enough for a cluster of painted pointu fishing boats and the stone cabanons (traditional boathouses) built directly onto the rock above the waterline. The pointu, with its narrow hull and high prow designed for the Mediterranean swells, is the traditional fishing boat of the coast; those that remain here are still used, though fewer each decade.

Access to the Vallon des Auffes is from the Corniche Kennedy, reached via a steep staircase that drops from the roadside down to harbour level. The transition from the busy road to the silence of the little harbour is abrupt enough to feel like a discovery — which is what the neighbourhood has always relied on to keep some of its character intact. The theatre programme is published each spring; booking ahead is advisable for the busiest nights in July and August.
