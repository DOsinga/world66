Sugar is how you follow the money in Marseille's colonial port economy. Ships from Martinique and Guadeloupe arrived at the quays with raw cane; the sucreries stored and refined it before it went inland. The warehouses were built to hold a lot of weight and a lot of heat.

This one is a theatre now. High ceilings, raw materials, a floor plan that allows staging a conventional theatre wouldn't. The conversion happened in the years before Marseille's 2013 European Capital of Culture, when the Joliette and Belle de Mai areas were being remade. The industrial character was kept — it's part of what makes the space work.

Expect productions that use the full volume of the building. Immersive theatre, large-cast work, events that treat the whole room as the stage.
