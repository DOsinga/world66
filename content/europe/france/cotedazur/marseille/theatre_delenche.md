The Théâtre de Lenche occupies a converted fish market on the Place de Lenche, one of the oldest public spaces in Marseille. The place is believed to sit on or near the site of the Greek agora of Massalia — the central meeting space of the city that Phocaean colonists founded around 600 BCE. The fish market that preceded the theatre was therefore one in a long sequence of public functions on a square that has been part of city life for more than two millennia.

The conversion to a theatre preserved the character of the market building: the space is intimate, its ceilings not particularly high, its materials plain. This suits the programming, which centres on contemporary creation and work that does not require the resources of a large institutional theatre. Small-scale productions, new writing, and work in development sit naturally in a room of this size, where the relationship between performers and audience is close and the atmosphere informal.

The theatre serves the mix of people who live in Le Panier — the old quarter immediately north of the Vieux-Port that has long been the first home of successive waves of new arrivals to Marseille, and more recently also a home to artists, craftspeople, and galleries. Its neighbourhood character is part of what distinguishes it from the larger institutional theatres elsewhere in the city.
