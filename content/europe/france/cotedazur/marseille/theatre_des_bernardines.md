The Cistercians built this chapel in the seventeenth century in the Belsunce quarter. After the Revolution dissolved the religious orders, it went through the usual sequence: warehouse, depot, storage. The stone vaulting stayed, accumulating other people's goods for two centuries until someone recognised what it actually was — an extraordinary acoustic space.

The conversion to theatre came in the 1980s. Vaulted stone is unforgiving: it reflects and sustains sound in ways a purpose-built modern theatre would suppress. That suits certain kinds of performance exceptionally well — music, spoken word, anything that can use silence as a structural element.

The location in Belsunce, long associated with North African and Middle Eastern communities, gives the Mediterranean-focused programming a particular resonance. This part of the city is part of the programming.
