The Théâtre des Bernardines is housed in a former chapel of the Cistercian order, built in the 17th century in the Belsunce quarter — a dense, historically working-class neighbourhood between the Canebière and the station. After the dissolution of religious communities during the Revolution, the building passed through various uses: it served as a warehouse, a depot, a storage space. The conversion to a theatre came in the 1980s, when the stone vaulting and thick walls that had accumulated several centuries of other people's goods were recognised as the qualities of a performance space rather than obstacles to one.

The chapel's acoustics are its defining characteristic. Vaulted stone is an unforgiving medium — it reflects and sustains sound in ways that a purpose-built modern theatre would not — but this suits certain kinds of performance exceptionally well. Music, spoken word, and productions that can use silence as a structural element all find the Bernardines to be a space that adds something to them.

The programme has generally emphasised contemporary creation with a Mediterranean or international scope: work that engages with the cultures and histories that flow through the city. The theatre's location in Belsunce — a quarter long associated with North African and Middle Eastern communities — gives that orientation a particular resonance.
