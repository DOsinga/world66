The calanques are the defining feature of Marseille's southern edge: narrow inlets cut into white limestone cliffs, the rock dropping directly into clear turquoise water, the vegetation reduced to what can survive in thin soil and salt wind. They are protected now as a national park — the only national park in France that begins within the boundaries of a major city — and they give this part of Marseille its particular identity, different from the industrial north or the bourgeois south of the 8th arrondissement.

Théâtre des Calanques takes its name and, by implication, its orientation from this landscape. The southern arrondissements where the theatre is situated are closer to the calanques than to the city centre, and the venue's identity is rooted in that specific geography — a theatre that belongs to the south of Marseille, to the part of the city defined by its relationship with the coast and the limestone.

Whether the programming explicitly engages with Mediterranean themes, ecology, or landscape varies by season and company, but the name establishes a frame: this is a theatre that knows where it is, that places itself in relation to the natural world that begins at the edge of the city. Open-air or partially open performances may feature in summer, when the climate makes outdoor staging in the southern arrondissements genuinely pleasurable.
