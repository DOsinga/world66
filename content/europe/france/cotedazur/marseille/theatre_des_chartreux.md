The Théâtre des Chartreux takes its name from the Carthusian monastery that once occupied this part of the 4th arrondissement. The Chartreux order established their priory here in the seventeenth century; after the Revolution the monastery was dissolved and its gardens were opened to the public, but the quarter retained the name — and the atmosphere of a neighbourhood set slightly apart from the city's busy centre.

The theatre occupies this residential setting deliberately. Its programming focuses on contemporary creation: new writing, experimental work, and productions by emerging companies that might not find a home in Marseille's larger institutional venues. Being embedded in a working neighbourhood rather than a tourist district gives it a dual function — as an artistic space and as a community anchor for the people who actually live in the Chartreux quarter.

Programming mixes local Marseille-based companies with touring productions from across France. The intimate scale of the space suits work that depends on close contact between performers and audience — text-driven theatre, physical performance, and forms that reward attention rather than spectacle.
