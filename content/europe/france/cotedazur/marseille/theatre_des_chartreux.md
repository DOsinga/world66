The Carthusians had their priory here in the seventeenth century. The Revolution dissolved the order, their gardens were opened to the public, and the neighbourhood kept the name and, somehow, the atmosphere — a working residential quarter that sits slightly apart from the busier city.

The theatre programs contemporary creation: new writing, experimental work, emerging companies that can't get a slot in the big institutional venues. Being in a residential neighbourhood rather than a tourist district shapes what it does — it serves the people who actually live in the Chartreux quarter as much as it serves the wider city.

The space is intimate. Close contact between performers and audience, text-driven work, forms that reward attention rather than spectacle. No room to hide in here.
