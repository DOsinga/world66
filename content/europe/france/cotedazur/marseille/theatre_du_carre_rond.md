A theatre whose name announces its intentions from the outset: the Carré Rond — the Round Square — is a geometric impossibility, a contradiction folded into two words. The name is not a mistake or an eccentricity but a statement of artistic purpose: this is a space where things that should not coexist are made to share the stage. It sits in the 5th arrondissement, south of the Vieux-Port, in a part of the city that mixes residential streets with small cultural venues.

The programming reflects the oxymoron. Carré Rond tends toward work that resists easy categorisation — performances that blend theatre with dance, spoken word with visual art, comedy with political urgency. It draws on the long French tradition of the théâtre de recherche, the research theatre committed to formal experiment, while staying rooted enough in Marseillais social reality to avoid disappearing into pure abstraction.

There is something very Marseillais about calling a theatre the Round Square. The city itself is a kind of contradiction — Mediterranean and northern French, ancient and rawly contemporary, beautiful and broken — and a venue that builds its identity on that tension fits the place. The impossible shape turns out to be the right one.
