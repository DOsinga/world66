The Théâtre du Gymnase dates from 1804, making it one of the oldest working theatres in France. It takes its name from the school — a *gymnase* — that occupied the site before the theatre was built. The building was designed in the Italian manner, with a horseshoe-shaped auditorium, tiered boxes, and the ornamental plasterwork typical of the period. Despite the changes Marseille has undergone in two centuries, the theatre has remained in continuous use on the same spot.

The auditorium's proportions were settled by convention in the early 19th century: an enclosed horseshoe places the audience close to the stage and gives the spoken word a warm presence. The Gymnase retains that quality, which makes it particularly suited to drama rather than grand opera or spectacle. As a Centre Dramatique, it has been steered by a succession of directors whose programmes have centred on contemporary French writing and productions that would be at home at peer theatres in Paris or Lyon.

The theatre sits on the Boulevard Garibaldi, within walking distance of the Canebière. Its survival as a working venue, rather than a listed monument put behind glass, is part of what gives it its character: the stage is still in use, the seats still fill, and the building still smells of old timber and upholstery the way theatres should.
