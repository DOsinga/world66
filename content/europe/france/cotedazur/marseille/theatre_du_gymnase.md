This place has been putting on shows since Napoleon was Consul. The Gymnase opened in 1804 — named after the school that occupied the site before it — and the Italian-style horseshoe auditorium has barely changed. Tiered boxes, ornamental plasterwork, old timber and upholstery. The proportions were set by convention two centuries ago and they still work.

It's on Boulevard Garibaldi, walking distance from the Canebière. As a Centre Dramatique it leans toward contemporary French writing rather than grand opera or spectacle. That's what the room is good for: intimacy, spoken word, an audience close enough to the stage that nothing gets lost.

The important thing is that it's still a working theatre, not a listed monument behind glass. The stage is in use, the seats fill, and the building smells the way theatres should.
