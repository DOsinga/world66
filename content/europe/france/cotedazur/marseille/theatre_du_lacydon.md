The Théâtre du Lacydon takes its name from the Lacydon — the ancient creek that Phocaean sailors entered around 600 BCE and chose as the site for the trading post that would become Massalia, and eventually Marseille. The Lacydon was silted up and built over long ago; today the Vieux-Port occupies roughly the same waters. The name on this theatre is one of the ways the city keeps that origin story alive in everyday language.

The theatre is a small, intimate space in the old centre, suited to productions that work with closeness: the distance between actor and audience is short, and the scale encourages a directness that larger theatres make difficult. Contemporary drama and work in development sit naturally in rooms like this, where the machinery of spectacle is stripped back and the performance has to carry itself on its own terms.

The Lacydon's location near the Vieux-Port puts it in a part of the city that functions as both a tourist centre and a working neighbourhood. The theatre draws from the mix of people who live and work around the old harbour, contributing a small but persistent strand of contemporary culture to a quartier better known for its restaurants, ferries, and the daily theatre of the fish market on the quay.
