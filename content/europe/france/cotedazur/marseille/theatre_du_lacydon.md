The Lacydon was the natural inlet where Phocaean sailors landed around 600 BCE and decided: this is the one. That creek is long gone — silted up, built over, turned into what is now the Vieux-Port. The name survives on this small theatre a few streets away.

It's an intimate space. The distance between actor and audience is short, and that's the whole point. No machinery, no spectacle — just the performance carrying itself on its own terms. Contemporary drama and work in development fit a room like this.

Step outside and you're in the thick of the Vieux-Port quarter: tourist restaurants, ferry terminals, the fish market on the quay. The theatre is one of the quieter things happening in one of the city's loudest neighbourhoods.
