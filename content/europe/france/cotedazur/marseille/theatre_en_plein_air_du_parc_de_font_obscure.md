The Parc de Font Obscure sits in the northern quarters of Marseille, a green pocket of hillside woodland that the city's residents use as a retreat from the heat and the noise. Within it, this open-air theatre occupies a natural hollow, its seating arranged on a gentle slope so that even the back rows catch the action below without difficulty.

Outdoor theatre in Marseille benefits enormously from the climate. From late spring through early autumn, evenings are warm and reliably dry, and performances here take on a quality that indoor venues simply cannot manufacture — the backdrop of darkening sky, the occasional shimmer of a passing aircraft, cicadas doing their part from the treeline. The acoustics in a well-chosen natural bowl are surprisingly good, and the Font Obscure stage uses the terrain well.

The programme varies year to year, but summer festivals and touring companies from across Provence make regular use of the site. Bring a jacket for later in the evening even in July — the park holds the cool air once the sun drops — and arrive early enough to find a seat on the upper terraces, where the view of the stage extends all the way to the trees beyond.
