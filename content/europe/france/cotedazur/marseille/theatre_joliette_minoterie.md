The Théâtre Joliette-Minoterie sits in the Joliette district, immediately north of the Vieux-Port, in a part of the city that was long defined by its port function. Warehouses, freight terminals, and the infrastructure of maritime commerce occupied the land between the docks and the old city streets. The Euroméditerranée project, one of the largest urban regeneration operations in France, has been transforming this zone since the 1990s — replacing industrial uses with offices, housing, cultural institutions, and public space.

The theatre is part of the cultural layer of that transformation. In a district that is still in the process of acquiring a civic character, the presence of a working theatre — bringing audiences in and programming that connects to wider networks of contemporary performance — is one of the ways a new neighbourhood acquires identity. The Joliette is now the site of several significant cultural institutions, and the theatre has been a consistent presence through a period when the surrounding streets were being rebuilt around it.

The programme mixes contemporary creation with touring international productions. The Joliette's Mediterranean position makes it a natural point of exchange for theatre and performance from across the sea, and the programme reflects that geography in its international outlook.
