Le Petit Merlan is a Scène Nationale in the 14th arrondissement, the northernmost and most economically deprived part of Marseille. The location was not incidental: the decision to place a nationally recognised theatre here reflected an acknowledgement that the northern *cités* — large housing estates with almost no cultural infrastructure — had the same claim to quality programming as any other part of the city. The nearest equivalent institutions are all in the south or centre, separated from these neighbourhoods by distance and circumstance.

The programming has been shaped by its context. Circus arts, physical theatre, and contemporary dance feature prominently alongside drama, partly because these forms have historically attracted audiences less dependent on a prior relationship with literary culture. The theatre works with local schools and community organisations, and has developed a reputation for the quality of its youth and outreach programming as well as its main-stage productions.

Le Petit Merlan is named after the neighbourhood in which it sits. In French, *merlan* is the word for whiting, the fish — an unassuming name for a quarter that has rarely featured on the mental map of Marseille as outsiders imagine it, and a fitting one for an institution that has worked against that invisibility.
