Massalia was what the Greeks called this place when they founded it around 600 BCE — before it became Massilia, before it became Marseille. The name on this theatre is a reminder that the city's oldest and newest impulses share the same ground.

Théâtre Massalia makes work for children and adolescents that treats them as serious theatrical subjects. Not educational entertainment — actual theatre, with puppetry, live music, object theatre, and bold visual staging. The productions have toured widely, and the venue has developed strong ties with artists from across the French-speaking world.

It sits inside the Friche la Belle de Mai — a former tobacco factory that became one of France's best creative campuses. Coming for a show means arriving into an ecosystem of studios, galleries, rooftop terraces, and restaurants. An evening here feels genuinely unlike anything else in the city.
