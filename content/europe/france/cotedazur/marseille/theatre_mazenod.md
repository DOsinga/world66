Eugène de Mazenod was born in Aix-en-Provence in 1782, grew up in exile as his aristocratic family fled the Revolution, and returned to Provence determined to repair what the upheaval had broken. Ordained a priest in 1811, he threw himself into pastoral work among the poorest inhabitants of Marseille — preaching in Provençal rather than Latin so that his congregation could actually follow him — and founded the Missionary Oblates of Mary Immaculate in 1816, a congregation that would eventually spread across five continents. He became Bishop of Marseille in 1837 and held the post until his death in 1861, overseeing the early stages of construction on the Basilica of Notre-Dame de la Garde that crowns the city to this day. John Paul II canonised him in 1995.

The theatre that bears his name sits in the southern part of the city, near the neighbourhood where Mazenod's own ministry was concentrated. It is a community-scale venue rather than a grand civic house, and its programme reflects that orientation — local companies, visiting productions with a social conscience, and events that serve the quartier as much as the wider city.

For visitors with an interest in Marseille's Catholic heritage, the theatre's name is a useful reminder to look up: the Basilica of Notre-Dame de la Garde, visible from much of the city, and the Cathédrale de la Major near the Vieux-Port both bear the architectural stamp of the Mazenod years, when the bishop oversaw an ambitious campaign to rebuild and commission religious buildings across the diocese.
