Eugène de Mazenod grew up in exile — his aristocratic family fled the Revolution — and came back to Provence with a mission. Ordained in 1811, he immediately went to work among the poorest people in Marseille. He preached in Provençal because his congregation couldn't follow Latin. In 1816 he founded the Missionary Oblates of Mary Immaculate, a congregation that would eventually spread to five continents. He became Bishop of Marseille in 1837, overseeing the early construction of Notre-Dame de la Garde, and held the post until his death in 1861. John Paul II canonised him in 1995.

The theatre bearing his name sits near the neighbourhood where his own ministry was concentrated. It's a community-scale venue, not a grand civic house — local companies, visiting productions with a social conscience, events that serve the quartier as much as the wider city.

Look up when you leave. Notre-Dame de la Garde crowns the hill above — Mazenod commissioned it, and from almost anywhere in Marseille you can see exactly what he built.
