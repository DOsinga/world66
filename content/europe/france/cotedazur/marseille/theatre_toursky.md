In 1972, Richard Martin planted a theatre in the 13th arrondissement — a working-class district that had nothing of the kind. His principle was blunt: internationally recognised theatre shouldn't require a trip to the city centre or the right social background. He brought it here instead.

Under his long direction, Toursky became a Scène Nationale — France's official recognition that this was a venue of real artistic weight. The programming leaned hard into the Mediterranean, drawing on theatre, music, and performance from both shores of the sea.

It's one of the things that makes Marseille's cultural life different from Paris. The refusal to treat culture as something imported from the capital. The conviction that an audience in a port neighbourhood deserves the same quality as any other audience in France.
