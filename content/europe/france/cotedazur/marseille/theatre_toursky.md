Théâtre Toursky was established in 1972 by Richard Martin in the 13th arrondissement, a working-class neighbourhood that had no existing cultural venue of its own. Martin's founding principle was straightforward: internationally recognised theatre should not be something that requires a trip to the city centre or a particular social background to attend. He brought major productions to an audience that would not normally have encountered them, and did so without lowering the ambition of what was on stage.

The theatre takes its name from the Rue Toursky, the street on which it sits. Under Martin's long direction it became a Scène Nationale — a status conferred by the French state on venues of recognised artistic and civic importance — and developed a programme with a strong Mediterranean orientation, drawing on theatre, music, and performance traditions from across the sea's northern and southern shores.

Toursky is one of the institutions that has shaped what is distinctive about Marseille's cultural life: the combination of geographic reach and local rootedness, the refusal to treat culture as something imported from Paris, and the conviction that an audience in a port-city neighbourhood should have access to the same quality of work as any other audience in France.
