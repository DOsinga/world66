Tour Balthazar takes its name from Balthasar, one of the three Magi of Christian tradition, and belongs to a set of towers in the Euroméditerranée zone that have been given biblically resonant names — a quietly grand gesture for buildings in what was, not long ago, a working port district. The tower stands just metres from Tour La Marseillaise, forming part of a new skyline cluster that announces the scale of Marseille's waterfront ambitions to anyone arriving by motorway or sea.

Like its neighbours, Balthazar is a product of the Euroméditerranée agency's long-running effort to shift the city's economic centre of gravity northward from the Vieux-Port toward the Joliette and Arenc districts. The building combines residential apartments with ground-floor commercial space, part of a broader strategy to create a genuinely mixed neighbourhood rather than a monocultural office district. The streets around the tower are still finding their rhythm, with construction hoardings and freshly planted plane trees existing side by side.

From a distance — from the hilltop terraces of Notre-Dame-de-la-Garde or from the ferry approaching the Joliette terminal — Tour Balthazar reads as one element in a composition still being assembled. Up close, its vertical lines and glassy upper floors reflect the constantly changing Mediterranean light in ways that justify its prominent position on the regenerated quayside.
