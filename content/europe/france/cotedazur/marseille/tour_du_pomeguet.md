The Frioul islands sit four kilometres off the Vieux-Port in open water, and for centuries that distance was the point. Ships suspected of carrying plague were held here — passengers confined to the lazaret, one of the oldest quarantine stations in the Mediterranean, forbidden to set foot on the mainland until enough time had passed to be certain.

The Tour du Pomèguet is one of several fortified remnants on the southern island. Watchtowers, vaulted storage buildings, walled enclosures — the whole archipelago was designed to keep disease at arm's length from the city. Dumas used the quarantine fortress as inspiration for the Château d'If, though that island sits separately, visible from the shore.

Take the ferry from the Vieux-Port and walk it. The terrain is scrubby and windswept, the coves are excellent, and the city across the water looks completely different from out here — which is exactly how the people running the quarantine wanted it.
