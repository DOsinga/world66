La Marseillaise Tower rises 135 metres above the former port lands north of the city centre, making it the second-tallest building in Marseille after the CMA CGM Tower. Designed by architect Jean-Baptiste Lacoudre and completed in 2018, the building takes its name from the revolutionary anthem composed in the city — a pointed nod to its location in a district undergoing its own kind of radical transformation. Its curtain wall of blue-tinted glass reflects the sky above the Joliette waterfront, shifting in colour through the day.

The tower is the centrepiece of the Euroméditerranée project, France's largest urban renewal programme outside Paris, which since the mid-1990s has been converting the post-industrial port fringe into a mixed-use business and residential quarter. La Marseillaise provides roughly 33,000 square metres of office space and is certified HQE, France's high environmental quality standard. Its profile, slightly twisted along the vertical axis, gives it a sculptural quality that reads well from the Vieux-Port several kilometres to the south.

Standing at street level on the esplanade below, you get the full sense of scale: the tower dwarfs the nineteenth-century warehouses that survive nearby, and the contrast is precisely the point. Whether this kind of landmark development enriches or overwhelms a port city's character is a question Marseillais debate with characteristic passion — but from almost any hill in the city, La Marseillaise has already become part of the skyline.
