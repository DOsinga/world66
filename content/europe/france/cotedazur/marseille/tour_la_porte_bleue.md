Somewhere around here — in the zone between the city centre and the southern arrondissements — a blue gate once marked the entrance to a private estate. Maybe it still does. The tower element suggests something more than a simple gateway: a gatehouse with a turret above it, or a freestanding piece of architectural theatre borrowed from the language of fortification.

Blue was a practical choice for the Provençal sun — it fades slower than most colours, and it reads as cool in a city where heat presses in for six months of the year. The villa-building bourgeoisie of the 19th century understood this. They also understood that a gate with a tower above it said something unambiguous about who lived behind it.

The estate is likely subdivided now, the gate changed or gone. But the name stuck. In Marseille, street names outlast the things that gave them their names by a very long time.
