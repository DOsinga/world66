Victor Vasarely (1906–1997) invented Op Art — geometric patterns and contrasting colours calibrated to make a flat surface appear to move. He trained in Budapest under a Bauhaus follower, moved to Paris in 1930, and spent his later decades in the south of France. The Fondation Vasarely outside Aix-en-Provence, in a building he designed himself, holds the definitive collection.

His whole philosophy was that art should be democratic, reproducible, and environmental — integrated into architecture and public space, not locked in galleries. A Vasarely in the street was exactly what he intended. He wanted the experience of looking at one to be available to anyone who happened to walk past.

Stop in front of this one and let your eyes adjust. It's flat. Then it isn't.
