In 600 BCE, Phocaean Greeks from Anatolia sailed into this rectangular inlet and founded Massalia — and the city never left. The Vieux-Port has been a working harbour continuously for 2,600 years. Stand at the Quai des Belges at 7am and the fishermen are still selling their catch directly off the boats, same as always.

The 2013 Capital of Culture programme changed what surrounds the water. Norman Foster's vast polished-steel canopy went in, reflecting the sky and the sea below it. Car traffic got banned from the quays. Marseille got the largest pedestrian waterfront in France. The fish market stayed exactly where it was.

Ferries leave from here for the Château d'If and the Frioul islands all day. The restaurants along both quays serve bouillabaisse — invented by the fishermen here to use the catch nobody would buy. The Forts Saint-Jean and Saint-Nicolas guard the harbour entrance on either side, built in the 17th century to control the water and, some historians argue, to keep a watchful eye on the city itself.
