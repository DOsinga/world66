In the 19th century, Marseille's prosperous merchant families built summer villas along the coast and in the hills and gave them names like Villa Palestine, Villa Jérusalem, Villa Nazareth. This one is typical of the fashion.

It wasn't random exoticism. Marseille was the main port of departure for pilgrims travelling to the Holy Land. Its trading houses did business across the eastern Mediterranean. Some families had actually been there. When they put "Palestine" on the gate, they were making a statement about where they'd been and who they knew, as much as a religious one.

The northern arrondissements location marks this as an older piece of the city's residential geography. The hills above the docks were the desirable address before the southern coastline became fashionable. The name on the gatepost is a fossil of the cosmopolitan trading world that built this city.
