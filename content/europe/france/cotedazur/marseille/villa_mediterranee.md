The building is the first thing you notice — a white volume cantilevering 40 metres over a reflecting pool, right beside the MuCEM and Fort Saint-Jean. Stefano Boeri designed it for the 2013 Capital of Culture year. On a bright Provençal day the effect is genuinely arresting: it looks like it shouldn't hold, then it looks exactly right.

Its original purpose was to be an international forum for Mediterranean dialogue — migration, environment, culture. That didn't really work. By the late 2010s it was a beautiful white elephant, its events sparse and its identity unclear.

The turnaround came in 2022 with the Cosquer Méditerranée replica. Henri Cosquer found the original cave in 1985 — it's under the Calanques cliffs, completely flooded, inaccessible to anyone. The paintings inside are 27,000 years old: hand stencils, horses, auks. The replica recreates 2,300 square metres of it at full scale. You walk among Stone Age art inside a 21st-century cantilever. Best use anyone's found for the building.
