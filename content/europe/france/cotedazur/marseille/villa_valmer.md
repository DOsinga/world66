Every other villa on this stretch of the Corniche is gone — demolished for apartment blocks through the 20th century. Villa Valmer survived for one reason: the cliff descent made standard apartment development uneconomic. The terraced garden that runs down toward the water was, in effect, what saved it. Too awkward to build on, too beautiful to abandon.

The city bought the property. The main villa is now a conference centre, but the gardens are open to the public for free. Formal Provençal terraces — clipped hedges, gravel paths, Mediterranean plantings — descending in steps to within earshot of the sea. The views west to the Îles du Frioul are some of the finest on the Corniche.

The estate dates from the 1850s, when the Corniche road was being built as a public works promenade. The shipping and trading families who built here were made wealthy by North African commerce and the Suez Canal routes. All their villas are gone. This one survived because the cliff made it inconvenient. Go see it.
