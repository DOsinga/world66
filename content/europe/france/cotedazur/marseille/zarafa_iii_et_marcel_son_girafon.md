In 1827 the Egyptian viceroy Muhammad Ali sent a remarkable diplomatic gift to the courts of Europe: three giraffes, one each for the King of France, the King of England, and the Holy Roman Emperor. The French giraffe, a young female, was shipped to Marseille and given the name Zarafa — Arabic for giraffe. She spent the winter in Marseille recovering from the voyage, tended by her Sudanese keepers and two milch cows whose milk supplemented her diet, before making the journey north to Paris on foot the following spring. The walk took 41 days and drew enormous crowds at every town along the route.

Zarafa arrived in Paris in June 1827 and lived at the Jardin des Plantes for the next eighteen years, becoming the first giraffe most Parisians had ever seen and triggering a brief craze — giraffe-print fabrics, giraffe-shaped hat pins, giraffe dances. She outlived her royal patron Charles X, who was deposed in 1830, and remained a beloved fixture of the Parisian menagerie until her death in 1845. Her story has since been told in children's books, a French animated film, and a biography; she remains the most famous giraffe in French history.

Zarafa III and her calf Marcel at the Parc Zoologique de Marseille carry that name as a tribute to the original traveller who first made Marseille the gateway for a giraffe entering France. The zoo, situated in the Parc Borély in the south of the city, is a pleasant destination in itself; the giraffes, with their particular gift for making human visitors feel small and very slow, are among its most popular residents.
