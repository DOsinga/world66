In 1827 a young female giraffe arrived in Marseille by ship from Egypt — a diplomatic gift from the Egyptian viceroy to the King of France. Her name was Zarafa, Arabic for giraffe. She spent the winter here recovering from the voyage, tended by Sudanese keepers and two milch cows whose milk she drank, then walked north to Paris the following spring. The journey took 41 days. Crowds lined the route at every town along the way.

She arrived in Paris in June 1827 — the first giraffe most Parisians had ever seen — and triggered a craze: giraffe-print fabrics, giraffe-shaped hat pins, something called the giraffe dance. She outlived Charles X, who was deposed in 1830, and lived at the Jardin des Plantes until 1845. She remains the most famous giraffe in French history.

Zarafa III and her calf Marcel at the Parc Zoologique carry the name as a tribute to the original traveller. The zoo is in the Parc Borély in the south of the city — a pleasant destination in itself. And the giraffes are the right animals to make the point: Marseille has always been the city where remarkable things from elsewhere first arrived in France.
