Colmar sits in the heart of the Alsatian vineyards and is one of the most photogenic small cities in France. Its historic centre is a vast pedestrian zone of medieval half-timbered houses with carved gables, painted shutters, and doors adorned with lavish woodwork — the kind of place that looks almost too perfect to be real.

The old [Quartier des Tanneurs](/europe/france/east/alsace/colmar/quartier_des_tanneurs), where hides were once dried on the upper floors, and the [Petite Venise](/europe/france/east/alsace/colmar/petite_venise) canal district are the best areas for wandering. The [Unterlinden Museum](/europe/france/east/alsace/colmar/unterlinden_museum), housed in a former Dominican convent, is home to the Issenheim Altarpiece by Matthias Grünewald — one of the masterpieces of European art — alongside a strong collection of medieval sculpture and painting. Nearby, the [Dominican Church](/europe/france/east/alsace/colmar/dominican_church) contains Martin Schongauer's delicate "Virgin of the Rose Bush."

Colmar is also the birthplace of Frédéric Auguste Bartholdi, sculptor of the Statue of Liberty, and a [small museum in his family home](/europe/france/east/alsace/colmar/bartholdi_museum) documents the project. Other landmarks worth seeking out on a wander include [St. Martin's church](/europe/france/east/alsace/colmar/st_martins_church) in the central square, the painted Renaissance façade of the [Maison Pfister](/europe/france/east/alsace/colmar/maison_pfister), and the medieval customs hall, the [Ancienne Douane](/europe/france/east/alsace/colmar/ancienne_douane). The city makes an excellent base for exploring the Alsace Wine Route, which runs through the surrounding villages and vineyards.
