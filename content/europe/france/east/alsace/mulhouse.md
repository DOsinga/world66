Mulhouse is an industrial city in southern Alsace, near the Swiss and German borders, that has reinvented itself as a surprisingly compelling museum destination. The [Cité de l'Automobile](/europe/france/east/alsace/mulhouse/cite_de_lautomobile) houses the Schlumpf Collection — over 400 cars including the world's largest collection of Bugattis — and is one of the finest automobile museums in Europe. The [Cité du Train](/europe/france/east/alsace/mulhouse/cite_du_train), nearby, is the largest railway museum in France.

The city's industrial heritage runs deep. Mulhouse was a centre of textile manufacturing from the eighteenth century, and the [Musée de l'Impression sur Étoffes](/europe/france/east/alsace/mulhouse/musee_impression_etoffes) documents this history with an extraordinary collection of printed textiles. The old town is modest compared to [Colmar](/europe/france/east/alsace/colmar) or [Strasbourg](/europe/france/east/alsace/strasbourg), but the [Place de la Réunion](/europe/france/east/alsace/mulhouse/place_de_la_reunion) with its painted Renaissance town hall is worth a visit. Mulhouse also serves as a convenient base for the southern end of the Alsace Wine Route and the nearby Sundgau countryside.
