  Alsace and Moselle were annexed by Germany in 1871, returned to France in 1918, annexed again in 1940, and liberated in 1944 — a 73-year cycle of forced identity changes in which residents were alternately required to speak French or German, serve in the French or German army, and carry French or German papers. The memorial tells this story through first-person testimony: not political history, but the experience of ordinary people caught between two states.

The Mémorial d'Alsace-Moselle sits on a hillside above Schirmeck and is one of the most thoughtfully designed historical museums in France. It covers the period from 1870 to 1945, when the Alsace-Moselle region was passed back and forth between France and Germany — twice annexed, twice liberated — and the impact of that on the people who lived there. The exhibition is largely in French and German, reflecting the region's dual heritage, with some English material available.

The building itself is architecturally striking — a large, angular structure that sits partially into the hillside. Plan at least two to three hours; the permanent collection is extensive and the subject matter deserves time. The memorial also covers the Natzweiler-Struthof camp in some detail, which provides useful context before or after a visit to the camp itself, about 10 kilometres south.
