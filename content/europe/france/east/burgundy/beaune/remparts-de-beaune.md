Beaune's medieval ramparts survive in remarkably complete form, ringing the old town in a near-perfect circuit of walls, towers, and bastions. What makes them unusual is what's underneath: the town's négociants — the great wine merchants — have tunnelled vast cellars into the base of the fortifications over the centuries, and several of the most famous maisons de vin offer tastings and tours from entrances in the old walls.

The walk around the top of the ramparts takes about forty minutes at a gentle pace and gives good views across the rooftops of the old town and, on clear days, out toward the vineyards of the Côte de Beaune. It's a pleasant way to orient yourself when you first arrive, and almost entirely free.

If you have time for just one cellar visit, Patriarche Père et Fils occupies the largest network of wine cellars in Burgundy — five kilometres of tunnels beneath the old Carmelite convent on the rue du Collège. Entry includes a self-guided tasting of around a dozen wines, a silver tastevin cup to keep, and as much time as you want underground. It's touristy, but genuinely impressive.
