Notre-Dame, built between 1220 and 1250, is one of the purest examples of early Gothic in Burgundy. The west front is the eccentric part: three rows of slim columns and 51 grotesque gargoyles, most of them 19th-century replacements for those knocked off in the Revolution. The two tower clocks include a 14th-century Jacquemart figure that strikes the hours — looted from Courtrai by Philip the Bold in 1382, who brought it back to Dijon as a war trophy.

The interior is taller and lighter than the cramped exterior suggests. The 11th-century Black Virgin of Bon-Espoir behind the high altar is one of the oldest wooden statues in France. The Chouette — the city's symbolic owl — is on the north side of the church on Rue de la Chouette. Touch it with the left hand and make a wish.
