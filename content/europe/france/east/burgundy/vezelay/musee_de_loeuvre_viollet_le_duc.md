  Eugène Viollet-le-Duc arrived in Vézelay in 1840 to find the basilica in catastrophic condition — the roof had partially collapsed, the west facade was crumbling, and the building was at risk of total loss. His restoration, completed over the following decades, was one of the defining achievements of 19th-century French architectural conservation. Without it, there would be no Vézelay basilica to visit today. The museum displays his drawings, his tools, and the original carved capitals he replaced.
---

This small museum is housed in the former chapter house adjacent to the basilica and tells the story of Eugène Viollet-le-Duc's restoration of the Vézelay church in the mid-19th century. Viollet-le-Duc was the greatest French architectural restorer of his era — he also worked on Notre-Dame de Paris and the walled city of Carcassonne — and his intervention at Vézelay saved the building from collapse.

The museum displays original Romanesque capitals removed during the restoration alongside Viollet-le-Duc's architectural drawings and models. It is a small but rewarding stop that deepens your appreciation of what you see inside the basilica.
