The buildings of Clairvaux Abbey have been used as a high-security prison since Napoleon's reorganisation of the French penal system in 1808. The medieval and classical monastic structures sit inside a ring of much taller modern walls topped with razor wire and watchtowers. From the outside the contrast is unsettling: 12th-century stonework, blank steel doors, and the muffled sound of an institution.

Visitors are admitted only on the guided heritage tours run by the Renaissance de l'Abbaye association, and only on Saturday afternoons from May to October. You must register in advance and bring photo identification; phones and cameras are not permitted inside. What you see is carefully managed — the lay brothers' building, the great cloister, the surviving Romanesque fragments — but the working prison is never out of mind.
