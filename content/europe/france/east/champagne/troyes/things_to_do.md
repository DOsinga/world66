title: "Things to Do"
type: section
---

Troyes rewards slow wandering. The old town is shaped — genuinely — like a champagne cork, and its medieval street plan is extraordinarily intact. Start at the [Cathedral of Saint-Pierre-et-Saint-Paul](/europe/france/east/champagne/troyes/cathedral_saint_pierre), the city's headline sight, and allow a full morning for it — the stained glass alone justifies the trip. Then work outward through the [old town's half-timbered streets](/europe/france/east/champagne/troyes/old_town), ducking into the narrow bouchons (covered passageways between buildings) that link the main streets.

The [Musée d'Art Moderne](/europe/france/east/champagne/troyes/musee_art_moderne) holds one of the finest Fauvist collections in France, assembled by a local couple and donated to the city. Serious art lovers should not skip it. The [Cité du Vitrail](/europe/france/east/champagne/troyes/cite_du_vitrail), opened in 2021 in the former Hôtel-Dieu, takes a deep dive into the history and craft of stained glass — essential context if you want to understand what you're looking at in the cathedral and churches.

Beyond the Basilique Saint-Urbain — one of the purest examples of Rayonnant Gothic in France — there are eight more medieval churches in the old town, each with its own glass. The Église Sainte-Madeleine has the only surviving stone rood screen in France; the Église Saint-Pantaléon is essentially a storage museum of sculpture moved here from demolished churches. You could spend days just on the churches.
