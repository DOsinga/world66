  The cloister's carved tympanum above the north portal depicts the Last Supper — one of the better-preserved pieces of 14th-century ecclesiastical stonework in Lorraine. It survived both Richelieu's demolitions and the First World War largely intact, protected by the thickness of the stone and the village's relative obscurity.

The 14th-century cloister attached to the church of Saint-Maur is the oldest intact structure in Hattonchâtel, and the most architecturally significant. Its low arcades of limestone, worn smooth by seven centuries of Lorraine winters, enclose a small courtyard that feels genuinely medieval — a rarity in a region that was fought over so destructively in 1914–1918.

Access is through the village church. The cloister is modest in scale but well-proportioned, and the carved details on the capitals and doorways reward close attention. In summer the village association sometimes holds evening concerts here, the acoustics surprisingly good under the stone vaulting.
