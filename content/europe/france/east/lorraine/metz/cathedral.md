The Cathédrale Saint-Étienne dominates the skyline of Metz with its honey-gold limestone facade and one of the tallest Gothic naves in Europe. Locals call it the "Lantern of God" for the sheer area of its stained glass — over 6,500 square metres — which gives the interior an extraordinary luminous quality on a sunny day. Construction began in 1220 and continued for nearly three centuries.

Inside, the eight centuries of glasswork are the main attraction: Hermann von Münster's medieval rose window above the western portal, Renaissance panels in the choir, and 20th-century compositions by Chagall and Villon that bring the building's history into the modern era. Allow at least an hour. The cathedral is open daily; climbing the tower (when accessible) is one of the best ways to see the city from above.
