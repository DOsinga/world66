Saint-Étienne is the reason many people make the trip to Metz, and it earns the pilgrimage. Known as the 'Lanterne du Bon Dieu' — the Lantern of God — it holds the largest expanse of stained glass of any cathedral in the world: over 6,500 square metres, spanning seven centuries of glasswork. On a sunny afternoon, the nave floods with colour in a way that's genuinely hard to describe.

The windows range from medieval originals to a set designed by Marc Chagall in the 1960s, which you'll find in the ambulatory and the north transept. Chagall's panels — blues and greens and warm golds — feel remarkably at home in the Gothic stonework around them. You could spend an hour just on those.

Entry to the cathedral is free. Guided tours are available in French and occasionally English; check the schedule at the entrance. Arrive early in the morning or late afternoon for the best light, and to avoid school groups who tend to descend around midday.
