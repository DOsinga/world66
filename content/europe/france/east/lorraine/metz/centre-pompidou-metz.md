The Centre Pompidou-Metz opened in 2010 as the first decentralised branch of the Centre Pompidou in Paris, bringing one of the world's great modern and contemporary art collections out of the capital. The building, designed by Japanese architect Shigeru Ban with Jean de Gastines, is immediately striking: a sweeping white roof shaped like a traditional Chinese hat, supported by a lattice of hexagonal wooden beams made from laminated timber. The structure is both technically ambitious and visually light, and it has become an architectural landmark in its own right.

The museum draws on the Pompidou's vast permanent collection — one of the largest in the world for modern and contemporary art — to mount rotating exhibitions that change regularly. You will rarely see the same show twice, which makes it worth a return visit. The exhibitions span painting, sculpture, photography, video, and installation work, and are often thematic rather than chronological, placing pieces in unexpected conversation with each other.

Metz itself makes for a satisfying day trip from Luxembourg, Strasbourg, or Nancy, and the Centre Pompidou is the centrepiece. Allow two to three hours inside, then walk the short distance to the covered market and the cathedral. The museum has a good bookshop and the restaurants Umé and Yozora on site.
