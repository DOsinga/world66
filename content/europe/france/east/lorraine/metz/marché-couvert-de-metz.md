Metz's covered market occupies a magnificent neo-Gothic hall near the cathedral, built in the late 19th century when the city was under German administration — the architecture gives it a slightly Rhineland feel, all pointed arches and amber stonework. Inside, it's thoroughly French: stalls of Lorraine cheeses, mirabelle plum preserves, charcuterie, fresh fish from the river, and bakers selling pale de Lorraine and quiche.

Come on a Tuesday or Saturday morning when the market is at its fullest. The mirabelle plum is Lorraine's great local product — sweeter and more fragrant than any plum you'll find elsewhere — and you'll find it here in every form: fresh (in late summer), jammed, distilled into eau-de-vie, and baked into tarts. It makes an excellent edible souvenir.

The surrounding streets are good for a wander after: the old town around Place Saint-Louis has some of the best-preserved medieval arcade architecture in France, and it's rarely crowded even in high season.
