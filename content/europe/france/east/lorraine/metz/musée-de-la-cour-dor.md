Metz's main civic museum is the kind of place that surprises you — you wander in expecting a modest regional collection and find yourself lost for two hours among Roman mosaics, medieval sculpture, and Gallo-Roman thermal baths that you are literally walking through. The building itself is part of the attraction: a 17th-century convent built over a medieval granary built over Roman ruins, all of which you descend through floor by floor.

The collection spans two thousand years of Metz's history, from Gaulish artefacts through to 19th-century fine arts. The Roman rooms are particularly strong — Metz was the major city of Divodorum Mediomatricorum, and the archaeological finds reflect that significance. A replica of a Hieronymus Bosch painting draws admirers who weren't expecting to find him here.

Astonishingly, admission is free. The staff are friendly and there are some English-language explanations, though Google Translate will serve you well in the older galleries. Allow at least two hours; the layout is labyrinthine in the best possible way.
