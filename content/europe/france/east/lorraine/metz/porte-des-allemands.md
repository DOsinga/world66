One of the most striking pieces of medieval architecture in all of Lorraine, the Porte des Allemands straddles the Seille river on the eastern edge of the old city like a miniature castle that somehow wandered into the urban fabric and decided to stay. Its twin round towers, pointed turrets, and arched bridge date from the 13th and 15th centuries, and the whole ensemble is remarkably well preserved.

The name — Gate of the Germans — comes not from any wartime association but from the Teutonic Knights who once maintained a hospital nearby. Today it serves as an exhibition space, with a lift up to the first floor if you don't fancy the stairs. On a sunny day, the view from the bridge over the Seille is genuinely lovely.

You can walk here easily from the cathedral in about ten minutes. Entry is free, and even if the exhibition inside doesn't interest you, the exterior alone is worth the detour. It tends to be quieter than the central sights — a good place to catch your breath and take a few unhurried photos.
