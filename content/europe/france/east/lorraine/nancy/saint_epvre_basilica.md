A large neo-Gothic basilica that anchors the north end of the Vieille Ville, on the square that once served as the main marketplace of medieval Nancy. The current building dates from the 1860s — a major 19th-century rebuild that replaced an older church — but its lofty nave, elaborate facade, and setting in the old town make it one of the most impressive sights in Nancy.

The square in front, Place Saint-Epvre, is one of the pleasantest in the city — quieter than Place Stanislas, ringed by elegant 18th-century townhouses, and with a good view of the basilica's spires. The surrounding streets of the Vieille Ville have some of the best surviving medieval architecture in Nancy, including the Porte de la Craffe, a 14th-century gateway at the far end of the Grande Rue.
