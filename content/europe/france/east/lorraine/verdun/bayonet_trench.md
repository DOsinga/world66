The Bayonet Trench is one of Verdun's most-visited and most-misunderstood monuments. A long concrete canopy, funded by American banker George Rand, shelters a section of trench from which the rusted tips of rifle bayonets still protrude, marking the line where French soldiers of the 137th Infantry Regiment died in June 1916.

The site is short — fifteen minutes — and most powerful when combined with a walk in the surrounding forest, where shell craters and the outlines of communication trenches are still visible everywhere under the trees.
