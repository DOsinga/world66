Chateau de Vaux-le-Vicomte is one of the great French chateau visits south-east of Paris, close
enough to pair with Fontainebleau but distinct in character. Built for Nicolas Fouquet in the 17th
century, it brought together architect Louis Le Vau, painter Charles Le Brun, and landscape designer
Andre Le Notre in a composition that directly influenced Versailles.

The gardens are essential to the experience. Long axes, terraces, canals, and optical effects make
the estate feel theatrical, especially when viewed from the chateau steps or the far end of the
grounds. It is a full half-day trip rather than a minor side stop.
