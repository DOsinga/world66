The Maisons sur l'Agout are the signature view of Castres: a single row of crooked, brightly painted half-timbered houses standing directly on the river, their wooden balconies overhanging the water and their reflections doubling the view on a calm afternoon. They were built between the 13th and 17th centuries for the dyers, tanners, and weavers who needed the river to run their trades.

The best vantage point is from the Pont Neuf, looking north along the row, especially in the late afternoon when the western light catches the painted facades. Some of the ground-floor rooms are now restaurants and bars; an evening drink with a table over the water is one of the simple pleasures of Castres.
