  The core of the collection came from a single Castres-born bequest in 1894: Marcel Briguiboul, a local painter who had spent years in Spain, left three Goyas and a dozen other Spanish works to his hometown. The Musée Goya is housed in the former [Bishop's Palace](/europe/france/languedoc/castres/palais_eveche) and holds the most important collection of Spanish painting in France outside Paris. The three Francisco de Goya works at its centre are the headline — the *Portrait of Philip IV in Hunting Costume*, *The Self-Portrait with Spectacles*, and the engraved series of the *Caprichos* — but the rest is substantial: Velázquez, Murillo, Ribera, El Greco-school work, and a fine room of 17th-century Spanish still lifes.

The setting helps. The state apartments of the palace have been kept as 17th-century galleries, with the original stuccoed ceilings, and the windows on the upper floor look out over Le Nôtre's formal garden below. Allow two unhurried hours; the museum is closed on Mondays and admission is modest.
