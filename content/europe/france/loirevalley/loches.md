Loches is one of the finest medieval towns in the Loire Valley — and one of the least crowded. Perched on a ridge above the Indre river, its old quarter is dominated by a [royal citadel](/europe/france/loirevalley/loches/royal_citadel) that makes most other Loire chateaux look like country houses. This is a fortress in the proper sense: thick walls, towers, an 11th-century [keep](/europe/france/loirevalley/loches/donjon) that has stood for a thousand years, and dungeons where enemies of the French crown were quietly forgotten.

The town below the citadel is charming and very much lived-in. The [market square](/europe/france/loirevalley/loches/place_du_marche), the tangle of old streets, the [Tour Saint-Antoine](/europe/france/loirevalley/loches/tour_saint_antoine) Renaissance belfry, and the Romanesque [church of Saint-Ours](/europe/france/loirevalley/loches/collegiate_church_saint_ours) with its unusual hollow pyramidal spires — Loches rewards an afternoon of wandering. It makes an excellent base for the southern Loire, with [Chenonceau](/europe/france/loirevalley/chenonceau), [Amboise](/europe/france/loirevalley/amboise), and [Azay-le-Rideau](/europe/france/loirevalley/azaylerideau) all within easy reach.
