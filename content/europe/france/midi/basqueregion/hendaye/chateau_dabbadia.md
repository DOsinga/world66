The Château d'Abbadia occupies a spectacular promontory south of Hendaye beach, rising above the Atlantic on a rocky headland with views across the Basque coast and into Spain. The castle was built between 1864 and 1884 for Antoine d'Abbadie, a Franco-Irish nobleman and scientist, to the designs of the architect Eugène Viollet-le-Duc — the restorer of Notre-Dame and Mont Saint-Michel — in a flamboyant neo-Gothic style.

The interior is extraordinary: carved stone, pointed arches, and a riot of inscriptions and heraldic devices in multiple languages and scripts. An astronomical dome at the top of the main tower contains the original telescope, and the castle was actively used for serious astronomical observation until d'Abbadie's death in 1897. It is now managed by the Académie des Sciences and is open for guided tours. The surrounding estate — the Domaine d'Abbadia — is protected natural land with cliff walks and views across to Hondarribia on the Spanish side.
