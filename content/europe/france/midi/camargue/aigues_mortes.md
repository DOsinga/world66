Aigues-Mortes is the most dramatic medieval walled town in France — a perfect rectangle of ramparts, towers, and battlements rising straight out of the salt marshes on the western edge of the Camargue. Built by Louis IX in the 13th century as France's only Mediterranean port, it retains its entire circuit of walls virtually intact, which you can walk around the top in about an hour.

Inside, the town is small and well-preserved — a grid of narrow streets, a church, several cafes, and the square Tour de Constance, which served as both prison and lighthouse. The salt marshes around the town turn brilliant pink in summer from the halophytic algae. The Salins du Midi company, which still harvests salt here, runs tours of the salt pans that include jeep rides through the pink water. Aigues-Mortes is 30 kilometres west of Arles and makes a natural half-day excursion from the Camargue.
