---
title: "Beaches"
type: section
---

The Camargue coast is a long, largely undeveloped stretch of Mediterranean beach backed by dunes and scrub. The sand is pale and fine, the sea shallow and warm in summer, and the crowds are concentrated at the two accessible ends.

The beach at Saintes-Maries-de-la-Mer is the most accessible — a wide crescent immediately behind the town, with some facilities in summer. It is busy in July and August but manageable in June and September.

The Plage de Piémanson at the eastern end, near Salin-de-Giraud, is the most atmospheric — a long, flat strip of sand with no development, accessed by an unpaved road across the salt pans. It has a long tradition of naturist camping, though this is semi-tolerated rather than official. A few basic summer facilities appear in peak season but it remains essentially wild.

The beaches between the two access points are accessible only on foot or bicycle along the Digue à la Mer, which means they are quiet even in high summer.
