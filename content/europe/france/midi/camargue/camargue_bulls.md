The black Camargue bull is the other iconic animal of the delta, as much part of the landscape as the white horses and the flamingos. The herds are managed by gardians — traditional cowboys who work on horseback, using the same methods their predecessors used for centuries. The manades (herds) roam semi-wild across the marshes and are visible from the roads, especially in the early morning.

The relationship between the gardians and the animals — both the bulls and the horses they ride — is the heart of the Camarguais cultural identity. Several ranches offer introductions to this tradition, from visits to active manades to full-day rides accompanying a working gardian. The Musée de la Camargue near Albaron documents the history of the practice. In Saintes-Maries and the surrounding villages, the Course Camarguaise is played in arenas from April through September — the local sport, and worth watching.
