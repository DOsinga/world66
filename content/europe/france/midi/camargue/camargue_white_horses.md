The white horses of the Camargue are one of the most iconic sights in southern France — semi-wild herds grazing in the marshes, wading through shallow lagoons, and galloping across open ground with the kind of natural grace that makes you want to stop the car and stare. You can often spot them from the roads through the reserve, particularly around Méjanes and along the Route d'Arles as it crosses the delta.

The gardian tradition — herding the horses and black Camargue bulls on horseback — is protected as part of the regional heritage. Several ranches around Méjanes and Saintes-Maries offer guided rides into the marshes, ranging from one-hour introductory trips to full-day excursions. Riding through the lagoons at dawn, when the water reflects the sky and the flamingos are feeding, is the kind of experience that defines the Camargue.
