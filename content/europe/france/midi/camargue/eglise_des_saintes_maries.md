The fortified church in Saintes-Maries is one of the more unusual buildings in Provence — a Romanesque nave with battlements on top, built in the 9th century when the delta was regularly raided by Saracens from the sea. The rooftop, accessible by internal stairs, offers views across the surrounding marshland and the Mediterranean. The church has no steeple; instead the tower served as a watchtower.

In the crypt below the nave is the statue of Sara la Kali — Black Sara — whom the Roma world venerates as their patron. The crypt is draped in coloured cloths and ex-votos, and has an intensity that the main church above lacks. The annual pilgrimage in May brings tens of thousands of Roma from across Europe; the procession carries Sara's statue to the sea in an extraordinary scene of music, colour, and devotion.
