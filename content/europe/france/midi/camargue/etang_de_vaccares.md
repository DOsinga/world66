The largest lagoon in the Camargue stretches across 6,500 hectares at the heart of the regional nature park, separated from the Mediterranean by a narrow strip of land. It is strictly protected — public access to the water's edge is limited to designated observation points along the western shore — but the views across the shallow lagoon are dramatic and the birdlife extraordinary.

Flamingos are the signature sighting: up to 50,000 greater flamingos nest in the Camargue each year, and many feed in the saline shallows of Vaccarès. The pink haze of a feeding flock against the flat blue water is one of the defining images of the Camargue. Bring binoculars; the birds are often at distance. The best viewing spots are along the D37 road on the western edge of the lagoon, and from the Mas d'Agon observation tower on the eastern side.
