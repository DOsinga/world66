---
title: "Getting Around"
type: section
---

A car is the most flexible way to explore the Camargue — the reserve is spread over a large area and the distances between key sites are significant. The D570 runs north-south through the western part of the reserve; the D36 cuts east toward Salin-de-Giraud and the salt pans. Many of the interior tracks are unpaved and sometimes flooded; a regular car is fine for the main routes.

Bicycle is arguably the best way to see the region, especially in spring and autumn. The flat terrain, relatively light traffic on the minor roads, and the need to travel slowly to spot wildlife all favour cycling. Bikes can be rented in Saintes-Maries-de-la-Mer and in Arles. Several dedicated cycling routes cross the reserve, including the Digue à la Mer coastal path.

Horseback riding is the traditional way to travel in the Camargue and several ranches offer guided rides of varying lengths. The Mas Icard and Le Clos du Rhône, both near Saintes-Maries, are well-regarded operators.
