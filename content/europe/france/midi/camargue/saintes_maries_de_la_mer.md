The only real town in the Camargue proper, Saintes-Maries-de-la-Mer sits at the southern tip of the delta where the flat marshland meets the Mediterranean. It is a place of pilgrimage, beach holidays, and flamingo tours — an odd mixture that somehow works. The fortified Romanesque church of Notre-Dame-de-la-Mer, originally built as a defensive refuge in the 9th century, dominates the town and contains the relics said to be those of the Three Marys of the Gospels.

The town is the practical base for exploring the southern Camargue. You can rent bicycles here and ride north along the salt pans and lagoons to Pont de Gau, or west along the beach toward the mouth of the Petit Rhône. Jeep safaris, boat trips on the lagoons, and horseback rides with local gardians all depart from the town. The beach is wide, flat, and goes on for kilometres in both directions — good for walking out of season, crowded in July and August.
