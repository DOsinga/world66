Ceret is a small Catalan town in the foothills of the Pyrenees, close to the Spanish border but very much its own place. It has plane-shaded streets, a good Saturday market, red-tiled roofs, and one of the best modern art museums in southern France.

Artists came here before the First World War for the same reasons travellers still do: clear light, mountain edges, and a town that is lively without being frantic. Picasso, Braque, Gris, Soutine, and Chagall all passed through, and the [Musee d'Art Moderne](/europe/france/midi/ceret/musee_dart_moderne) gives Ceret far more cultural weight than its size suggests.

The rest of the town is best taken slowly. Walk from the [Place des Neuf Jets](/europe/france/midi/ceret/place_des_neuf_jets) to the [Church of Saint Peter](/europe/france/midi/ceret/eglise_saint_pierre), browse the [Saturday market](/europe/france/midi/ceret/saturday_market), then drop down toward the [Pont du Diable](/europe/france/midi/ceret/pont_du_diable) over the Tech. Ceret makes an easy half-day from the coast, but it is better with a night, especially if you like your art towns a little sun-baked and stubborn.
