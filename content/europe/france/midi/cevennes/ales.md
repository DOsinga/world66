Alès is the southern gateway to the Cévennes, a working town on the Gardon rather than a polished hill village. It grew on coal, silk, and industry, and that gives it a blunt, useful character: trains, markets, museums, and quick access to the valleys and ridges that make the Cévennes worth the trip.

The town's own sights are modest but genuine. The [Mine Temoin](/europe/france/midi/cevennes/ales/mine_temoin) tells the coal-mining story underground, [Fort Vauban](/europe/france/midi/cevennes/ales/fort_vauban) watches over the centre, and the [Cathedral of Saint John the Baptist](/europe/france/midi/cevennes/ales/cathedrale_saint_jean_baptiste) keeps the old core anchored. The [Musee Pierre-Andre Benoit](/europe/france/midi/cevennes/ales/musee_pierre_andre_benoit) adds a surprisingly strong modern-art note.

Come to Alès for practicality rather than romance. It is a good railhead for walkers finishing the Stevenson Trail, a base for [Cévennes](/europe/france/midi/cevennes) drives, and a place where the mountains' Protestant, mining, and rural histories meet without much tourist varnish.
