The medieval fortress that crowns Haut-de-Cagnes, built by Rainier I Grimaldi of Monaco in 1309 as a defensive watchtower and converted in the 17th century into a princely residence by Henri Grimaldi. The Grimaldis kept it until the French Revolution; today it is owned by the town and houses three museums in one — the **Olive Tree Museum** in the lower vaults (a serious history of olive cultivation in the region), the **Modern Mediterranean Art** collection in the upper rooms, and the famous **Suzy Solidor portraits** in one extraordinary room.

The 17th-century internal courtyard, with its loggia and original frescoes, is one of the most beautiful in the region. From the upper rooms and the rooftop terrace, the views run from the snow-capped Alps to the sea. Closed Tuesdays. The walk up to Haut-de-Cagnes from the lower town is steep — wear sensible shoes or take the free shuttle bus from the Square Bourdet.
