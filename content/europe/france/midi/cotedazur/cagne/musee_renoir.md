The country house where Pierre-Auguste Renoir spent the last twelve years of his life, surrounded by a four-hectare grove of ancient olive trees that Renoir himself bought to save from being cleared. The house is preserved as Renoir left it: his studio with the easel set up, the wheelchair in which he was carried out into the garden to paint, family photographs, his coat on a peg.

The collection includes about a dozen original Renoir paintings and sculptures, but the real attraction is the house itself — modest, lived-in, deeply personal — and the gardens, where the silver-green of the olive trees in afternoon light explains why so much of Renoir's late work has exactly that quality. About a 15-minute uphill walk from the centre of Cagnes-sur-Mer or take a taxi. Closed Tuesdays.
