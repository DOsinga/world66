A fourteenth-century fortress at the top of Haut-de-Cagnes, built by the Grimaldi family — the same dynasty that still rules Monaco. It anchors the skyline of the village and is visible from a long way down the coast.

Inside, the museum houses a permanent collection of Mediterranean olive tree paintings and the collection that makes Cagnes worth a detour: 224 portraits of Suzy Solidor, an interwar cabaret singer who had the inspired idea of commissioning paintings of herself from every artist who passed through the Riviera. Cocteau, Van Dongen, Foujita, and 221 others took her up on it. The result is an utterly eccentric room of one woman's face rendered in 224 different styles and visions, all hanging together — a collection that shouldn't work but absolutely does.
