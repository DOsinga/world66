Les Collettes is the house and studio where Renoir spent his final twelve years, from 1907 until his death in 1919. By the time he moved here he was already severely crippled by rheumatoid arthritis; he would eventually paint with brushes tied to his wrists. He kept working until almost the end.

The rooms are largely as he left them: canvases, easels, family photographs, personal effects, the specially adapted wheelchair. There is nothing reconstructed about it. The garden and the ancient olive trees he planted and painted are still there, gnarled and enormous now. It is one of the most quietly moving artist's houses on the Riviera — less famous than some, less visited, and all the better for it.
