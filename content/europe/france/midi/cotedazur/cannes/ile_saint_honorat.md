  Saint-Honorat was founded as a monastery around 410 AD, making it one of the oldest monastic sites in the Western world. Île Saint-Honorat is the smaller, quieter, and more spiritual of the two Lérins Islands. A Cistercian monastery has stood here since the 5th century and the monks still live and work on the island, cultivating vineyards and producing a honey liqueur called Lérins. You can buy their wine and liqueur at the island's shop — it is rather good.

The island is mostly off-limits to visitors except for the southern coastal path, the old fortified monastery on a rocky promontory (which is the oldest surviving building), and the working monastery church. There are no cars, no hotels, and the atmosphere is genuinely peaceful. Ferries from Cannes harbour take about 20 minutes.
