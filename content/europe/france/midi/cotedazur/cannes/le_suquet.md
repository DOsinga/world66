Le Suquet is the old quarter of Cannes, climbing steeply from the old port up through a tangle of cobbled lanes to a fortified hilltop with a 12th-century church and a watchtower with excellent views over the bay and the Lérins Islands. It is the most human-scaled part of Cannes and a welcome contrast to the grand hotels of the Croisette.

The winding Rue Saint-Antoine is the main artery, lined with restaurants serving Provençal food at prices lower than the waterfront. The Musée de la Castre sits in a former fortress at the summit and contains an impressive collection of Mediterranean and non-Western artefacts gathered by a 19th-century Dutch traveller. Even without entering the museum, the terraced gardens and the view from the battlements justify the climb.
