The Musée de la Castre occupies the rooms of a medieval fortress at the summit of Le Suquet, the old quarter of Cannes. The collection is genuinely eccentric and interesting: Lycklama à Nijeholt, the 19th-century Dutch collector who assembled it, gathered antiquities from across the Mediterranean, Persian artefacts, Himalayan objects, and pre-Columbian pieces during decades of travel. The result is an unusual window onto a Victorian gentleman's curiosity about the wider world.

The building itself is worth the visit — the fortress includes a Romanesque chapel and a watchtower that you can climb for panoramic views over Cannes, the Croisette, and the Lérins Islands. Admission is inexpensive and the museum is one of the more rewarding sights in the city for the effort required.
