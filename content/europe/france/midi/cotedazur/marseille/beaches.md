---
title: Beaches
type: section
---

Marseille is a big city with proper beaches inside its limits, which is unusual for a major European port. The water is clear but the beaches are not the white-sand pictures of the Côte d'Azur further east — most are pebbled or have small patches of imported sand.

The **Plages du Prado**, just south of the centre, are the obvious choice: a long crescent of small beaches created in the 1970s as a leisure area, with shaded grass behind, volleyball courts, lifeguards in summer, and easy bus or metro access. Crowded but reliable.

The **Catalans beach**, a short walk south of the Vieux-Port, is the closest sand to the centre — small but surrounded by cafés and within walking distance of dinner.

For something quieter, the rocky coves at the **Pointe Rouge** and **Les Goudes** on the far southern fringe of the city have clearer water and a more local feel; from Les Goudes a short walk leads into the first of the [Calanques](/europe/france/midi/cotedazur/marseille/calanques). Off Marseille altogether, the [Frioul islands](/europe/france/midi/cotedazur/marseille/frioulislands) have a series of small coves where you can swim from the rocks.

The water is generally swimmable from May through October; July and August are warmest but most crowded. Beaches inside city limits sometimes close briefly after heavy rain washes pollution into the bay — check the city's water-quality flags.
