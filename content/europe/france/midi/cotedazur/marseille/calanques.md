The Calanques are a 20-kilometre stretch of limestone cliffs and inlets running south-east from Marseille to Cassis, designated a national park in 2012. The name calanque refers to each steep-walled cove where the coastal cliffs drop into a turquoise wedge of sea. The most famous — Sormiou, Morgiou, Sugiton, En-Vau — are accessible by foot, boat or both, but reaching them on foot is the point: hot, rocky trails through pine and rosemary, then a sudden drop into a clear cove for a swim.

The classic walks start from the Luminy university campus in the south of Marseille and take in Sugiton, Morgiou or En-Vau. Routes vary from gentle to scrambling — for En-Vau you need decent shoes and a head for narrow paths. Bring more water than you think you need; there are no fountains in the park and summer temperatures push 35°C. Boat tours from the Vieux-Port offer an easier alternative, looping past the major calanques without disembarking. From late June to September, fire risk closes much of the park to walkers — check the daily access status before you set out.
