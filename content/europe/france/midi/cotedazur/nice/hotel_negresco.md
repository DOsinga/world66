story: >
  The Negresco was built by Romanian impresario Henri Negresco and opened in 1913 with a guest list that included Tsar Nicholas II. Negresco himself died penniless in Paris just four years later after the First World War ruined him financially. The hotel was saved by Jeanne Augier, who bought it in 1957 and filled it with her private art collection — including a Gustave Eiffel chandelier in the Salon Royal commissioned for the Tsar — turning it into the eccentric museum-hotel it remains today.
---

The Hôtel Negresco is the most famous building on the Promenade des Anglais, identifiable from a distance by its pink dome and Belle Époque silhouette. It is a listed historic monument and one of the last independently owned grand hotels on the French Riviera, run as a private fiefdom rather than a corporate property — which means it has accumulated an extraordinary collection of art and antiques in lieu of standardised furnishings.

Non-guests can visit the public areas: the Salon Royal beneath the Eiffel-engineered dome, the bar, and the Chantecler restaurant (one of the Riviera's finest, with two Michelin stars). The Negresco maintains its own staff in traditional uniforms and forbids swimwear in its corridors — a stance that feels either absurd or refreshing depending on your tolerance for hotel pomposity.
