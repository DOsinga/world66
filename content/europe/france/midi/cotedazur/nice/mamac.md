  MAMAC holds one of the most significant collections of Nouveau Réalisme in France — the movement founded in Nice by Yves Klein and Pierre Restany in 1960, which declared that reality itself was the raw material of art. Klein's International Klein Blue, a synthetic ultramarine he patented and used obsessively, was invented in his Nice studio. The museum's roof terrace, added during renovation, offers panoramic city views that Klein would have approved of.
---

MAMAC occupies a striking building of four marble towers on Place Yves Klein, overlooking Place Garibaldi at the edge of the old town. The permanent collection covers European and American art from the 1950s to the present, with particular strength in Nouveau Réalisme and the Nice School: works by Yves Klein (including monochrome canvases in his famous blue), Niki de Saint Phalle, Jean Tinguely, and Arman fill the upper galleries.

Pop Art, Minimalism, and later movements are well represented alongside the French material. The roof terrace with panoramic views over Nice and the sea is free to access and worth the trip alone. Note that the museum has undergone renovation; check current opening status before visiting.
