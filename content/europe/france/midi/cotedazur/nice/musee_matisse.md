story: >
  Henri Matisse came to Nice in 1917, initially for a short visit while recovering from illness, and never really left. He lived in the city until his death in 1954, finding in the Mediterranean light and the rhythms of the old town the colour and vitality that defined his mature work. His Nice period produced the famous Odalisques — women in North African dress painted in riotously patterned interiors — all suffused with the particular brilliance of this coast.
---

The Matisse Museum occupies a 17th-century Genoese villa in the Cimiez neighbourhood, set in an olive grove on a hill above the city. The collection is comprehensive: paintings, drawings, bronzes, paper cut-outs, and the bold decorative work from his later years when failing health meant he worked in bed with scissors. The famous blue-and-white paper cut-outs from his final decade are here, including preparatory work for the Vence Chapel.

The museum is a 20-minute bus ride (or a very uphill 30-minute walk) from the city centre. The grounds contain Roman ruins — Cimiez was the capital of the Roman province of Alpes Maritimae — and the nearby Franciscan monastery has a peaceful garden open to visitors. Combine the Matisse museum with the Chagall museum (15 minutes' walk downhill) for a full day of Riviera art.
