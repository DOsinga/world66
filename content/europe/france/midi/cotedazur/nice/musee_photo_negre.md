The photography museum in Vieux Nice is dedicated to Charles Nègre, a pioneering French photographer born in nearby Grasse who was among the first to photograph street scenes, workers, and ordinary Parisian life in the 1850s — work that anticipates the documentary tradition by several decades. The small museum occupies a historic building in the old town and shows rotating exhibitions drawn from historic and contemporary photography collections.

The permanent display of Nègre's work is worth the visit alone — his images of chimney sweeps, organ grinders, and street sellers have a clarity and humanity that feels distinctly modern. The museum is intimate and rarely crowded, and the building's stone vaulted interior makes an atmospheric setting for photography exhibitions.
