  The British burned Spanish galleys in this harbour in June 1742, a skirmish in the War of Austrian Succession barely remembered today. What is remembered is the postwar transformation: in the 1950s, a few film productions and one famous actress turned a working fishing harbour into the most photographed marina in Europe.
---

The Vieux Port is both the town's heart and its greatest spectacle. Painted houses in faded ochre, blue, and terracotta line the quay, and between them are moored some of the largest private yachts in the world — a display of wealth so extravagant it circles back to being entertaining. Walk the length of the harbour in the morning before the crowds arrive, when the fishermen are still unloading and the cafe terraces are setting up.

The quay itself is lined with cafes and restaurants that trade heavily on the view; the prices reflect it. The Sénéquier — red parasols, impossible to miss — has been here since 1887 and remains excellent for coffee and watching the harbour. The afternoon light on the painted facades is why the painters came; the evening light is better still.
