Bergerac contains two public statues of Cyrano de Bergerac, separated by a few hundred metres and about three decades of sculptural fashion. The older one, by Émilien Cabuchet, stands on the Place de la Myrpe — a stone figure in 17th-century dress, with the famously large nose and a sword at the hip. The newer, bronze Cyrano by Mauro Corda, installed in 2005, stands on the Place Pélissière in a more theatrical pose, cloak swirling.

Between them they make a small thematic walk through the old town. Both squares have a cafe or two within sight, so the tour can comfortably be made into a pause for a glass of Bergerac white.
