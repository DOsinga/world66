---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

Bergerac's climate is mild Atlantic, warmer and drier than further north in France but cooler than the Mediterranean coast. The shoulder seasons — May, June, and September — are the most rewarding times to visit: the days are long, the wine country is green or golden depending on the month, and the restaurants are still open without the high-summer crowds.

July and August are hot and busy, with the riverbank festivals and the open-air markets at their fullest, but also with high-season prices and accommodation tight in the surrounding chateaux country. The grape harvest, in late September and October, brings the cellars to life. Winter is quiet — many restaurants close, and the smaller museums shorten their hours — but the old town has its own appeal under a frost.
