The Pont Valentré is the landmark of Cahors and one of the great medieval bridges of Europe. Built between 1308 and 1378, it spans the River Lot west of the old town with six graceful arches and three square towers — the whole ensemble so well-preserved and so photogenic that it has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1998.

The bridge was built as a defensive structure, not just a river crossing — the towers had portcullises and could be sealed against attack. Walking across it takes five minutes; looking at it from the riverside path takes longer. The western approach offers the classic view with the bridge reflected in the Lot on calm mornings. The setting — the river looping through limestone cliffs, the towers rising from the water — is one of the finest in southwest France.
