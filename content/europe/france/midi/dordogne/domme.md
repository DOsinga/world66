Domme is a 13th-century bastide perched on a cliff 150 metres above the Dordogne river, and the views from its ramparts are among the finest in the Périgord. On a clear day you can see the river winding through a patchwork of walnut orchards, tobacco fields, and stone villages as far as the eye can follow.

The town itself is small but full of character — narrow streets lined with honey-coloured stone houses, a central square with cafes under the arcades, and a covered market hall built over the entrance to the [Grotte de Domme](/europe/france/midi/dordogne/domme/grotte_de_domme), a large cave system of stalactites and stalagnites. The fortified gateway, the [Porte des Tours](/europe/france/midi/dordogne/domme/porte_des_tours), still bears graffiti carved by Knights Templar who were imprisoned there in the early 14th century — crosses, symbols, and possibly portraits of the men themselves, scratched into the limestone over eleven years.

The [Belvédère](/europe/france/midi/dordogne/domme/belvedere) at the southern edge of town is widely considered the finest viewpoint in the entire Dordogne, with La Roque-Gageac, Beynac, and Castelnaud all visible from the same terrace. From Domme you can easily reach [Château de Castelnaud](/europe/france/midi/dordogne/domme/chateau_de_castelnaud), the best-preserved medieval fortress in the region, and [La Roque-Gageac](/europe/france/midi/dordogne/domme/la_roque_gageac), the cliff-side village visible directly below in the valley.

Domme is 8 kilometres south of Sarlat on the D46. The town itself takes half a day to explore; allow more if you're combining it with the valley sites.
