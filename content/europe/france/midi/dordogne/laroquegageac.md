La Roque-Gageac is regularly cited as one of the most beautiful villages in France, and it is hard to argue. The village is wedged between a towering limestone cliff and the Dordogne River, its golden stone houses and slate roofs reflected in the water below. A tropical garden clings to the south-facing cliff face, warmed by the microclimate the rock creates.

The [Château de la Malartrie](/europe/france/midi/dordogne/laroquegageac/chateau_de_la_malartrie), a 19th-century pastiche of a medieval castle, sits at one end of the village and adds to the fairy-tale quality of the scene. Above, troglodyte dwellings and a [12th-century fort](/europe/france/midi/dordogne/laroquegageac/fort_troglodyte) are carved into the cliff itself. [Gabares](/europe/france/midi/dordogne/laroquegageac/gabare_boat_trip) — traditional flat-bottomed boats — depart from the riverbank for gentle cruises along the Dordogne, passing walnut groves and other clifftop villages.

Within a few kilometres sit three exceptional châteaux: the [Château de Marqueyssac](/europe/france/midi/dordogne/laroquegageac/chateau_de_marqueyssac) with its 150,000 hand-trimmed box hedges and a spectacular belvedere over the valley, the medieval fortress of [Château de Castelnaud](/europe/france/midi/dordogne/laroquegageac/chateau_de_castelnaud) housing a fine museum of medieval warfare, and [Château de Beynac](/europe/france/midi/dordogne/laroquegageac/chateau_de_beynac) perched dramatically on the north bank cliff. Together they make this stretch of river one of the most concentrated medieval landscapes in France.

La Roque-Gageac is tiny and gets crowded in high summer, when the single road through the village can feel overwhelmed. Visit early in the morning or in the shoulder season for the best experience. The village sits between [Sarlat](/europe/france/midi/dordogne/sarlat) and [Domme](/europe/france/midi/dordogne/domme), making it an easy stop on a Dordogne tour.
