  The church is dedicated to two early Christian martyrs — Saint Cyr, a three-year-old child who refused to renounce his faith, and his mother Saint Julitta, both killed in Tarsus in 304 AD. The village name preserves the boy-saint twelve hundred years after his death and several thousand kilometres from where he died.
---

The fortified Gothic church at the top of the village dates from the early 16th century, replacing earlier sanctuaries dating back to the village's foundation. The single broad nave is built against the cliff itself — the rock forms part of the north wall — and the squat fortified bell tower was designed for defence as much as worship.

The interior is plain in the way most southern French village churches are, with a few late-medieval polychrome statues and a notably restored Christ figure above the altar. The real reward is the small terrace outside: it sits at the highest level of the village and looks out over the Lot, the cliffs opposite, and the russet rooftops cascading down toward the river. Sunset light makes it one of the photographer's spots in the village; arrive a little before to beat the small queue that forms.
