The Bastide Saint-Louis is the lower town on the left bank of the Aude, founded in 1247 by Louis IX on the standard southern French bastide plan: a perfect rectangular grid around a central arcaded square. The grid still defines the modern town centre and is one of the cleanest examples of medieval town planning anywhere in France.

The Cité gets the postcards but the Bastide is where things actually happen — markets, restaurants, bars, the cathedral. The seventeenth- and eighteenth-century merchant houses around place Carnot recall the cloth trade that made Carcassonne prosperous in those centuries.
