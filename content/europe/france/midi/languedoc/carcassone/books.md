title: Books
type: section
---

**Labyrinth** by Kate Mosse. A two-time-stream historical novel set partly in thirteenth-century Carcassonne and partly in the present — better written than most of its kind, and built around the Albigensian Crusade. The sequels (*Sepulchre*, *Citadel*) continue the south-French setting.

**The Yellow Cross** by René Weis. A serious history of the last Cathar communities in the Languedoc, drawn from the Inquisition records of Bishop Jacques Fournier of Pamiers. Slower going than *Labyrinth* but the real story.

**Montaillou** by Emmanuel Le Roy Ladurie. The classic — a microhistory of a single mountain village under Inquisition surveillance in the early fourteenth century, reconstructed from the same Fournier register. Reads like a novel; if you only read one book on medieval southern France, read this.

**Histoire des Cathares** by Michel Roquebert. The standard French history, in five volumes — but the one-volume *L'Épopée Cathare* covers the same ground without the bulk.

**The Perfect Heresy** by Stephen O'Shea. A readable English-language popular history of the Cathar wars, well suited as background reading before a trip to Carcassonne and the Cathar castles.
