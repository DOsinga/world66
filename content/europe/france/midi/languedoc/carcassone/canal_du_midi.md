The Canal du Midi runs along the north side of the modern town, just past the railway station — a 240-kilometre seventeenth-century waterway that links the Atlantic to the Mediterranean. A flight of locks at Carcassonne raises boats over the local high ground.

Walk or cycle the towpath in either direction for an afternoon — east toward Trèbes, west toward Bram, both pleasant. The plane trees that lined the canal are mostly gone, killed by a fungal disease since 2006, but new species are being replanted. Hire boats are available for a longer trip.
