title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

Cassoulet is the dish — a slow-cooked white-bean stew with confit duck or goose, pork, and Toulouse sausage — and Carcassonne is one of the places that claims it. Honest cassoulet is rib-stickingly heavy and best in winter, but you will find it on most menus year-round. Confit de canard and lamb from the Corbières are the other staples.

Prices in the Cité are inflated and quality is uneven. The better-value, more-local restaurants are down in the Bastide, particularly the side streets off place Carnot. Allow around €25–35 per person for a proper sit-down meal with a glass of wine; cassoulet served in a heavy ceramic cassole and a Corbières red is the order to make.
