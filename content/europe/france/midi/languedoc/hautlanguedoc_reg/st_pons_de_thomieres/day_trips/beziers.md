  Béziers is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in France, with origins in the pre-Roman Iberian settlement of Bèsiers. It is best known internationally for two events separated by more than 1,800 years: its destruction by Roman general Gnaeus Domitius Ahenobarbus in 120 BC during the conquest of Gaul, and the Cathar Crusade massacre of 1209, when crusaders under Simon de Montfort are said to have killed the entire population — Catholic and Cathar alike — on the instruction "Kill them all, God will know his own." Béziers is about 55 kilometres south of St-Pons — roughly an hour by car through the Orb gorge or a train ride on the scenic Transpyrénéen line. The city sits on a high bluff above the Orb river, and its silhouette — dominated by the Cathédrale Saint-Nazaire — is one of the great views of Languedoc when seen from the Canal du Midi below.

The old town has a good market, several museums, and a strong local identity built around rugby, bullfighting, and a fierce regional pride. The nine locks of Fonseranes (Écluses de Fonseranes) on the Canal du Midi just west of the city are a feat of seventeenth-century hydraulic engineering and worth an hour of anyone's time. The city is also a good place to stock up on wine from the appellations of the Languedoc Roussillon.
