---
title: When to Go
type: section
---

St-Pons-de-Thomières sits at around 400 metres in the Jaur valley, sheltered from the full force of both the Mediterranean summer heat and the Atlantic winter weather that batters the Espinouse plateau above. This makes it a comfortable destination in seasons when the coast or the high mountains would be less appealing.

**Spring (April–May)** is excellent: the chestnut forests are coming into leaf, the Gorges d'Héric are running full of snowmelt, and the wildflowers on the Espinouse moorland are at their peak. Temperatures are mild and the town is quiet.

**Summer (June–August)** is warm and busy, particularly in July and August when French families use the area as a cooler alternative to the coast. The swimming holes in the Gorges d'Héric attract crowds on hot weekends; arrive early or come midweek. The Saturday market is at its best for produce.

**Autumn (September–October)** is arguably the best time of all. The chestnut harvest draws people up from the lowlands, the market fills with mushrooms and dried chestnuts, and the hillsides turn amber. The Gorges d'Héric are quieter and the light is golden. Walking conditions are ideal.

**Winter (November–March)** is quiet to the point of sleepiness — some restaurants close, and the Grotte de la Devèze operates reduced hours. The Espinouse can have snow from December onwards. But the cathedral is undisturbed, the smaller roads through the forest are beautiful, and accommodation prices drop significantly.
