**The Mystery of Olivier Bécaille** by Émile Zola. A short story set partly in 19th-century Montpellier; Zola passed through several times and his eye for southern French detail is sharp.

**The Labyrinth** by Kate Mosse. A bestselling novel that weaves between modern-day Carcassonne and the Cathar wars of the 13th century. Languedoc is the real subject — read it before driving the Cathar country south of Montpellier.

**A Tale of Two Cities** by Charles Dickens. Not Montpellier itself, but Dickens spent time in the Languedoc and the south of France shapes much of his French settings. Less direct but still useful: Stevenson's **Travels with a Donkey in the Cévennes**, which describes the rugged country an hour north of Montpellier.

**Languedoc-Roussillon: The Wines and Winemakers** by Paul Strang. A serious working reference for anyone wanting to understand the vineyards around the city. Read this and you will drink better for the rest of your trip.
