Montpellier is well placed for day trips in every direction. **Nîmes**, half an hour east by train, has the best-preserved Roman amphitheatre in France and the Maison Carrée temple. **Sète**, twenty minutes south on the coast, is a working fishing port crisscrossed by canals — the Mont Saint-Clair viewpoint is worth the climb. **Aigues-Mortes**, a perfectly preserved 13th-century walled town surrounded by salt pans, sits at the edge of the Camargue with its pink flamingos and white horses.

Inland, **Saint-Guilhem-le-Désert** is a medieval village pinned to a rocky gorge with a UNESCO-listed abbey — combine it with a swim under the **Pont du Diable** in the Hérault river. **Pézenas** was Molière's old stamping ground and still has the air of a 17th-century theatre town. Wine drinkers should head north into the **Pic Saint-Loup** appellation; beach-seekers, south to **Palavas-les-Flots** or the giant 1960s pyramids of **La Grande-Motte**.
