Pézenas makes much of its Molière connection, and for once the pride is warranted. The playwright spent several seasons here in the 1650s as the guest of Armand de Bourbon, prince de Conti, who used the town as his administrative base and kept a theatre company as part of a flourishing court. For Molière it was a crucial period — he was in his late twenties and early thirties, still developing the satirical style that would make him famous, and Pézenas gave him both an audience and a cast of characters to observe.

The building known as the Maison Molière, on the Rue Conti, is the most direct memorial to his presence: a house in the old town where he is believed to have lodged. The connection runs through the broader old town as well — the barber's shop, the streets where he would have performed, the general civic life of a prosperous 17th-century Languedoc town. The tourist office runs a dedicated Molière walk that traces the relevant sites and puts the historical context in order; it is one of the better themed walks available in the region.
