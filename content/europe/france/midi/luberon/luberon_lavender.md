story: >
  The lavender that scents the Luberon hillsides is not wild but agricultural — it's grown primarily as lavandin, a hybrid developed in the 1920s that produces more oil but slightly less fragrance than true lavender. The real lavender (lavande fine) grows at higher altitudes, above 800 metres, and produces the perfume ingredient that Grasse has been processing for centuries. The Luberon's lavender is beautiful and fragrant enough that the distinction hardly matters to anyone but the perfumers.
---

The lavender fields of the Luberon are at their peak from late June through July, when the rows of blue-purple run along the hillsides between the limestone villages. The most famous views are around Gordes and the Sénanque Abbey to the north, but the fields scattered throughout the Luberon valley — particularly along the road between Bonnieux and Apt — are just as beautiful and considerably less crowded.

The harvest happens in July by machine or by hand depending on the terrain. The cut fields smell extraordinary for weeks afterward. The best time to visit for photography is early morning when the light is low and the insects are still.
