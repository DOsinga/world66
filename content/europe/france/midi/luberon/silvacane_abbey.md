  Silvacane is one of three Cistercian abbeys in Provence — the so-called "Three Sisters of Provence" alongside Sénanque and Le Thoronet. All three were built in the 12th century according to the same austere principles: no decoration, no stained glass, no elaboration of any kind. The Cistercian philosophy held that art was a distraction from God. The result, paradoxically, is three buildings of extraordinary beauty achieved through pure proportion and light.

Silvacane Abbey sits in a flat valley south of the Luberon ridge, its stone walls rising from the farmland without drama. It is the least visited of the three Cistercian abbeys in Provence, which means you often have it largely to yourself. The interior is spare Romanesque — plain arches, flagstone floors, and a cloister garden that manages to be both simple and profound.

The abbey church dates from the 12th century and has been in varying states of restoration since the Revolution, when it was turned into a farm. The current restoration is ongoing but the main church and cloister are fully accessible. The afternoon light through the plain windows of the nave is worth arranging your visit around.
