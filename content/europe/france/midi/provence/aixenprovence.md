Aix is the ancient capital of Provence, and it still carries itself that way. Elegant townhouses line plane-tree-shaded boulevards, fountains splash on every other corner, and cafe terraces fill with students from one of France's oldest universities.

The city revolves around the [Cours Mirabeau](/europe/france/midi/provence/aixenprovence/cours_mirabeau), a wide avenue separating the medieval [Vieil Aix](/europe/france/midi/provence/aixenprovence/vieil_aix) to the north from the 17th-century [Mazarin quarter](/europe/france/midi/provence/aixenprovence/mazarin_quarter) to the south. Paul Cezanne grew up here, painted the surrounding countryside obsessively, and his presence is everywhere: [his studio](/europe/france/midi/provence/aixenprovence/ateliercezanne) above town, the [Musee Granet](/europe/france/midi/provence/aixenprovence/museegranet) with its collection of his work, and the views of [Mont Sainte-Victoire](/europe/france/midi/provence/aixenprovence/mont_sainte_victoire) that he made famous.

Markets are central to life in Aix. Three times a week the squares fill with stalls selling Provencal olives, goat cheese, lavender honey, and whatever fruit is in season. Saturday morning is the big event. Come early, buy a bag of calissons — the almond-and-melon sweets that are the city's signature — and let the crowd carry you through the old town.
