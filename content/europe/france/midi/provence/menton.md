Menton sits on the last stretch of French coastline before [Italy](/europe/italy), squeezed between the mountains and the sea with [Monaco](/europe/monaco) just to the west. It has the mildest climate on the Riviera, which is why it became a winter retreat for European royalty and aristocracy in the 19th century — Queen Victoria, the Tsar, and the man who built the Suez Canal all kept houses here.

The [old town](/europe/france/midi/provence/menton/old_town) rises from the harbour like an amphitheatre, its pastel facades stacked up the hillside toward the baroque Saint-Michel church. The [Bastion Museum](/europe/france/midi/provence/menton/bastion_museum), a 17th-century fortress at the water's edge, was restored by Jean Cocteau in 1957 and now houses a museum of his work. The [Palais Carnolès](/europe/france/midi/provence/menton/palais_carnoles), once the summer residence of the Princes of Monaco, holds a decent collection of paintings and a magnificent citrus garden.

Menton is best known for its gardens. The [Val Rahmeh Botanical Garden](/europe/france/midi/provence/menton/val_rahmeh_botanical_garden), [La Serre de la Madone](/europe/france/midi/provence/menton/serre_de_la_madone), and the flamboyant [Fontana Rosa](/europe/france/midi/provence/menton/fontana_rosa) are all open to visitors and benefit from the unusually warm microclimate. The town hosts a [Lemon Festival](/europe/france/midi/provence/menton/lemon_festival) every February that fills the Bioves gardens with elaborate citrus sculptures. For modern art, the [Jean Cocteau Museum](/europe/france/midi/provence/menton/jean_cocteau_museum) holds the world's largest collection of his work, donated by an American collector who never visited the town.
