The Castellum Aquae is easy to miss, which is odd for one of the clearest Roman engineering survivals in Nîmes. This round stone basin was the arrival point for water carried from the Eure spring by the aqueduct that includes the Pont du Gard, before it was divided through lead pipes to fountains, baths, and houses across the ancient city.

Do not expect the drama of the arena or the polish of the Maison Carrée. The reward here is understanding how Roman Nîmes actually worked: the city as a system of gradients, channels, and public water. It is a quick stop, but a good one if you have already seen the Pont du Gard and want the story to land inside the city walls.
