Tarascon-sur-Ariège is a small town in the Ariège valley between Foix and the Spanish border. It is pleasant enough in itself — stone houses along the river, a [ruined castle](/europe/france/midi/pyrenees/tarasconsurari/chateau_de_tarascon) on the hill above — but the real draw is the prehistoric cave art in the surrounding valleys.

The [Grotte de Niaux](/europe/france/midi/pyrenees/tarasconsurari/grotte_de_niaux), a few kilometres south, contains some of the finest Magdalenian cave paintings in Europe: bison, horses, and ibex drawn with astonishing skill around 14,000 years ago. Visits are limited and must be booked in advance. The [Parc de la Préhistoire](/europe/france/midi/pyrenees/tarasconsurari/parc_de_la_prehistoire) nearby offers a broader introduction to the region's cave art heritage, and the vast [Grotte de Lombrives](/europe/france/midi/pyrenees/tarasconsurari/grotte_de_lombrives) and [Grotte de Bédeilhac](/europe/france/midi/pyrenees/tarasconsurari/grotte_de_bedeilhac) round out a serious circuit of Ice Age sites.

Tarascon makes a good base for exploring the upper Ariège, with hiking trails into the Pyrenees starting from the valley floor and the [old town](/europe/france/midi/pyrenees/tarasconsurari/old_town) along the river for evenings.
