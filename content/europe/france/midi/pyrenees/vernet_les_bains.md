Vernet-les-Bains sits on the eastern flank of the Pyrenees, in the Têt Valley just below the imposing peak of Canigou. It's a small spa town that most guidebooks underrate — the setting is beautiful, the walking is excellent, and it has a genuine unhurried character that makes it a much better base for exploring this corner of Catalonia than its modest profile suggests.

The town is most easily reached via Villefranche-de-Conflent, a short bus ride away on the Perpignan rail line and the starting point of the Petit Train Jaune narrow-gauge railway that winds up to Latour-de-Carol. If you're arriving without a car, this connection makes Vernet straightforward to reach.

The surroundings are the main draw. Hiking trails fan out in every direction, from gentle valley walks to the [Cascades des Anglais](/europe/france/midi/pyrenees/vernet_les_bains/cascades_des_anglais) to routes climbing toward the Canigou summit. The unmissable excursion is the [Abbey of Saint-Martin-du-Canigou](/europe/france/midi/pyrenees/vernet_les_bains/abbaye_saint_martin), a Romanesque monastery perched on a rocky spur an hour above the town. The town was also France's first [village arboretum](/europe/france/midi/pyrenees/vernet_les_bains/arboretum) — over 2,000 labelled trees of 320 species line the streets and paths. A [geological museum](/europe/france/midi/pyrenees/vernet_les_bains/musee_geologique) with an exceptional fossil collection rounds out the local sights; the curator is the collector himself and gives an enthusiastic tour in multiple languages. The [thermal baths](/europe/france/midi/pyrenees/vernet_les_bains/thermes), fed by springs at 65°C, are the town's original reason for being and still open for both curative courses and day visitors.

Late spring and summer are the best times to visit — some trails and facilities only open in June, which can be frustrating if you arrive in May with the sun blazing and the trees in full leaf.
