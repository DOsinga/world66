  Aiguines was once famous throughout France for its turned wooden bowls and boxes, made from boxwood grown on the surrounding plateau. The craft died out in the 20th century when cheaper imports arrived, but the village museum preserves the tools and products of an industry that once supplied Parisian markets. Aiguines occupies a bluff at the western end of the Verdon Gorge, above the point where the river spills into the Lac de Sainte-Croix. The village has a 17th-century château (private but visible from outside), a small museum of the boxwood-turning craft, and a terrace with views that justify the drive from anywhere in Provence.

The village is the quieter alternative to La Palud for visitors who want access to the gorge without the climbing-focused atmosphere. The Lac de Sainte-Croix is a short drive down the hill — a massive turquoise reservoir where canoes, kayaks, and paddleboards can be rented at the water's edge.
