  Lac de Sainte-Croix was created in 1973 by the completion of the Quinson dam on the Verdon River. Before the dam, the valley floor was farmland and the medieval village of Les Salles-sur-Verdon stood where the water now covers. The village was evacuated and demolished, and its inhabitants relocated to a new settlement on higher ground. On very low-water years, the ruins are occasionally visible below the surface.

Lac de Sainte-Croix is the great turquoise reservoir at the western end of the Verdon Gorge, and it's one of the most spectacular lakes in France. The water — that same impossible blue-green colour as the river — fills a drowned valley between limestone ridges, and the setting is dramatic in every direction.

The lake is a focus for water sports throughout the summer. Canoes, kayaks, pedal boats, and electric boats can be rented at the village of Sainte-Croix-de-Verdon on the north shore or at the mouth of the gorge, where the turquoise river water meets the still lake water in a visible boundary. Swimming is permitted throughout the lake, and the water quality is excellent.
