  The Verdon Gorge transformed rock climbing in Europe. When Parisian climbers arrived in the 1960s, they found a 300-metre limestone wall that could only be accessed by rappelling in from the top — unlike any cliff they had climbed before. The multi-pitch routes they established required overnight bivouacs on the wall and techniques borrowed from Yosemite. The gorge effectively introduced big-wall climbing to a generation of European climbers.

The Verdon Gorge is one of the most important rock climbing destinations in Europe. The main face — the Grande Face — is up to 700 metres tall and holds more than 300 bolted routes, ranging from accessible single-pitch climbs near the rim to serious five-day big-wall ascents on the deepest sections. The limestone is compact and featured, and the protection quality is high.

Routes are accessed either by rappelling in from the rim (for the deepest sections) or by hiking in from the Chalet de la Maline car park on the north rim. The sector near the Belvédère de l'Escalès has the most accessible routes for intermediate climbers. Guides and gear rental are available in La Palud. Climbing is possible year-round, but spring and autumn are the most comfortable seasons.
