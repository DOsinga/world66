The Basilique Notre-Dame dominates the Haute Ville skyline, its 101-metre dome visible from the Channel on clear days. The current basilica was built in the 19th century by the Abbé Haffreingue over the ruins of the medieval cathedral destroyed during the Revolution, and the result is a curious architectural hybrid — neo-classical exterior, baroque interior, and a crypt of extraordinary scale beneath.

The crypt is the oldest part of the complex and the reason serious pilgrims come: it houses a statue of the Virgin said to have arrived in a driverless boat in the 7th century, which has been a Marian pilgrimage site ever since. The interior is richly decorated with coloured marble and houses a collection of ex-votos from sailors who credited the Virgin with their survival. The altar, made from 147 different types of marble, is a remarkable piece of 19th-century craftsmanship.
