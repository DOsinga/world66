An estaminet is the Flemish equivalent of a brasserie — a warm, wood-panelled pub-restaurant where the menu runs entirely to local specialities, the beer list is long and serious, and the atmosphere is closer to Ghent than to Paris. Lille has a cluster of genuine estaminets in Vieux-Lille, and they are the best places in the city to eat the local cuisine.

The key dishes: **Welsh** (a generous rarebit of thick toast, ham, and aged Cheddar or Maroilles baked until bubbling), **carbonade flamande** (beef cheeks braised slowly in dark beer with a pain d'épice crust, intensely sweet-savoury), and **potjevleesch** (a cold terrine of chicken, rabbit, and pork in aspic — an acquired taste that rewards the effort). The beers to drink are the regional specialities from breweries like Trois Monts, Jenlain, and Leper.

**L'Estaminet du Vieux-Lille** on Rue de Gand is the most visited and deservedly so — it has been serving these dishes for decades and the quality is consistent. **Chez la Vieille** on Rue des Bouchers is quieter and slightly cheaper. Both are worth booking ahead on Friday and Saturday evenings.
