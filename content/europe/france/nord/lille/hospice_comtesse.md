The Hospice Comtesse is the oldest and best-preserved medieval building in Lille — a 17th-century complex of courtyards, chapels, and vaulted wards on the Rue de la Monnaie in the northern part of Vieux-Lille. Founded in 1237 by Joan, Countess of Flanders, it operated as a hospital for the poor for 700 years before becoming a museum.

The main hall — a long, barrel-vaulted ward where the sick were housed in rows of curtained beds — is intact and deeply atmospheric: Delftware tiles line the lower walls, a painted wooden ceiling covers the upper, and the whole space is lit by tall windows that let in grey northern light. The adjoining chapels and sisters' rooms contain period furniture, religious art, and objects relating to the history of charitable medicine in Flanders. The museum is quiet even in summer and rarely features on English-language tourist itineraries, making it one of the most pleasant stops in the city.
