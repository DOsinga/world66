---
title: When to Go
type: section
---

St-Valéry is a year-round destination, but it changes character sharply with the seasons. **Late spring** (May–June) is the best of the year: long days, mild weather, the bay alive with returning migrating birds, and the town quieter than in midsummer. **September** is the other sweet spot — warm enough for the steam railway and seal walks, with autumn light over the saltings.

**July and August** are the busiest months, when Parisian and Belgian families arrive en masse. The town is lively but cafés and restaurants need booking, and the upper-town lanes can feel crowded on weekends. Winter is quiet, often grey and windy, but it has its own appeal — the bay is at its most dramatic in stormy weather, and many of the heritage attractions close down or run a much reduced timetable.

Tides matter here more than weather. The tidal range in the Baie de Somme is among the largest in France, and the most striking sights — the seals on the sandbanks, the migratory birds feeding on the exposed mud — only happen at low water. Check the local tide tables (any tourist office or café has them posted) before planning a walk on the bay or a seal-watching trip.
