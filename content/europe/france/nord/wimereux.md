Wimereux was built as a bathing resort in the 1860s and never quite stopped looking like one. The seafront is a run of Belle Époque villas — turrets, painted balconies, tiled gables, no two the same — facing a beach that turns to rock and pool at low tide. It is four kilometres north of [Boulogne](/europe/france/nord/boulogne) and functions partly as its seaside suburb, which means it stays alive out of season in a way that the purpose-built resorts do not.

The Pointe aux Oies at the northern end is the best walk: dunes, a blockhouse or two left from the Atlantic Wall, and views back down the coast. The town is also the closest thing the Opal Coast has to a food destination — the *estaminets* along the Rue Carnot do moules, and the local mussel-and-cheese dish, the *welsh*, appears on most menus in a form that would puzzle anyone from Wales.

Water sports happen on the main beach and at the Pointe aux Oies; the surf and kitesurf schools that run sessions here are based up the coast at [Wissant](/europe/france/nord/wissant).
