A predominantly Gothic cathedral that dominates the skyline of Bayeux. The building dates mainly from the 11th to 13th centuries, with a Romanesque crypt and nave base surviving beneath the Gothic upper structure. The twin west towers are a local landmark, and the interior — long, light, and relatively uncluttered by French cathedral standards — is genuinely impressive.

The cathedral was where the Bayeux Tapestry was originally housed and displayed. Today it stands on its own as one of the finest Norman Gothic buildings in France, worth half an hour even for visitors more focused on the D-Day sites.
