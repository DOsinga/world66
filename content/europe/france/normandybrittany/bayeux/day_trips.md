---
title: "Day Trips"
type: section
---

Bayeux is the best base for visiting the D-Day landing beaches, which stretch along the Normandy coast starting about 15 km north of town.

**Omaha Beach and the American Cemetery at Colleville-sur-Mer** are the most visited sites — the cemetery holds 9,387 American graves on a bluff above the beach, and the view from the memorial down to the sand makes the landing's scale and cost immediately comprehensible. Allow two to three hours. **Pointe du Hoc**, 14 km west, is where US Rangers scaled a 30-metre cliff under fire on 6 June 1944; the German bunkers and shell craters are preserved exactly as the fighting left them.

**Arromanches** (20 km north) has the remarkable remains of Mulberry Harbour B, the prefabricated harbour that the British towed across the Channel after D-Day to supply their forces. Sections of the concrete caissons still stand in the bay. The D-Day museum in town provides context.

For the British and Canadian beaches — Gold, Juno, and Sword — the towns of **Courseulles-sur-Mer** and **Ouistreham** (30–40 km east) each have small museums and accessible beach access.
