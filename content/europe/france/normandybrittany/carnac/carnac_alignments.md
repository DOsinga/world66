The Carnac alignments are the largest megalithic alignment system in the world: over 3,000 standing stones arranged in roughly parallel rows that extend for more than three kilometres across the Breton interior. The rows are divided into three main groups — Menec, Kermario, and Kerlescan — each of which was once enclosed at one or both ends by a stone semicircle (cromlech). Several of these have been partially destroyed over the centuries.

Access has been restricted in recent decades to allow the vegetation to recover from decades of trampling. Guided visits are available in summer, allowing access to fenced sections. The best way to appreciate the full scale of the alignments is to walk the length of them along the perimeter, pausing at the gates and elevated viewpoints. The museum in town (Musée de Préhistoire) provides essential context; visit it first if you want to understand what you are looking at.
