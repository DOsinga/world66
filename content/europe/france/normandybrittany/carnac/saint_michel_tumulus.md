The Saint-Michel tumulus is a vast Neolithic burial mound on the eastern edge of Carnac town, built around 4500 BCE and one of the largest funerary mounds in Europe. It stretches roughly 125 metres in length, 60 metres in width, and rises to about 10 metres — a scale that makes it easy to mistake for a natural hill. On top stands a chapel dedicated to Saint Michael, a common Christianisation of ancient sacred high places.

The interior contains several stone-built burial chambers; these can be visited on guided tours run by the site in summer, though access is limited by conservation concerns and numbers are small. The exterior, freely accessible at all times, gives an excellent sense of the monument's scale and the commanding position it occupies over the surrounding landscape.
