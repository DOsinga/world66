  The original Château de Dinan appears in the Bayeux Tapestry, depicting the 1065 siege in which William the Conqueror attacked the castle of his rebellious vassal Conan II of Brittany. The current keep — 34 metres of circular stone towers — was commissioned in 1382 by John V, Duke of Brittany, who first had to seize the earlier fortress from its owners.
---

The most prominent landmark in Dinan, the castle's keep rises 34 metres above the old town and can be seen from across the river valley. The building is formed by two circular towers joined by a thick curtain wall, with machicolations running around the top — designed to allow defenders to drop things on attackers below. A moat and drawbridge separate the keep from the town's ramparts.

Inside, a local history museum covers Dinan from medieval times through to the Revolution. The collection is modest but well arranged, and the view from the top of the keep over the rooftops and down to the Rance is the real reward for climbing it.
