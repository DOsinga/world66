Honfleur is one of those Norman harbour towns that looks almost too picturesque to be real. The [Vieux Bassin](/europe/france/normandybrittany/honfleur/vieux_bassin) — a 17th-century enclosed harbour lined with tall, narrow slate-fronted houses — drew Impressionist painters like Boudin, Monet, and Jongkind, and it still draws crowds today.

Beyond the harbour, the town has genuine character. The wooden [church of Sainte-Catherine](/europe/france/normandybrittany/honfleur/ste_catherine), built by shipwrights after the Hundred Years' War, is unlike any other church in France. The [old salt warehouses](/europe/france/normandybrittany/honfleur/greniers_a_sel), the [Lieutenance](/europe/france/normandybrittany/honfleur/lieutenance) tower at the harbour mouth, and the [Eugène Boudin museum](/europe/france/normandybrittany/honfleur/musee_eugene_boudin) are all within walking distance. Honfleur is small enough to cover on foot in a day, but the restaurants and atmosphere reward a longer stay.
