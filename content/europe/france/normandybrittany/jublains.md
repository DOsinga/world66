Known in Roman times as **Noviodunum**, Jublains is a quiet agricultural town in the Mayenne département with an outsized archaeological heritage. The remains of a Roman fortress, theatre, and thermal baths are all within walking distance, making it one of the best-preserved Gallo-Roman sites in northwestern France.

The [Musée de Jublains](/europe/france/normandybrittany/jublains/musee_de_jublains), upgraded in 1995, houses finds from the site and explains the town's importance as a regional capital under Roman rule. The [Roman fortress](/europe/france/normandybrittany/jublains/roman_fortress) walls are the highlight — unusually intact and atmospheric. The [Roman theatre](/europe/france/normandybrittany/jublains/roman_theatre) and [thermal baths](/europe/france/normandybrittany/jublains/roman_baths) complete the picture of a properly substantial Gallo-Roman town.

Jublains is a short detour if you are driving through the [Loire Valley](/europe/france/centre/loirevalley) or heading toward Brittany, and worth the stop for anyone interested in Roman history.
