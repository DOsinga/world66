Les Andelys sits deep in one of the great loops of the Seine, backed by high chalk cliffs that give it a drama unusual for Normandy. The town is really two in one: Petit-Andely hugs the [riverbank below the ruins of Château Gaillard](/europe/france/normandybrittany/lesandelys/seine_riverside), while Grand-Andely spreads inland around the church of Notre-Dame.

[Château Gaillard](/europe/france/normandybrittany/lesandelys/chateau_gaillard) is the main draw. Richard the Lionheart built this fortress in a single year in 1196 to block the French advance on Rouen, and its ruined walls still command a spectacular view over the Seine valley. The climb up is short but steep, and the panorama from the top is one of the finest in the region.

In town, the [Collégiale Notre-Dame](/europe/france/normandybrittany/lesandelys/eglise_notre_dame) mixes Gothic and Renaissance styles, and the [Musée Nicolas Poussin](/europe/france/normandybrittany/lesandelys/musee_nicolas_poussin) honours the painter who was born here in 1594. Les Andelys also makes a good base for riverboat trips along the Seine, with several operators running scenic cruises through the surrounding loops and cliffs.
