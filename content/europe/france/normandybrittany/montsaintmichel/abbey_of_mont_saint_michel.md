The Abbey of Mont Saint Michel is the reason the island exists as travellers know it: a medieval monastery rising in stone terraces above the bay, with the village and fortifications wrapped around its lower slopes. The climb through the gate, lanes, and stairs builds the drama before you reach the abbey church and cloisters at the top.

The abbey has been a monastery, fortress, prison, and national monument. Its architecture is the reward, especially the high Gothic choir, the refectory, and the cloister suspended above the rock. Visit early or late if you can; midday crowds can make the narrow route feel like a queue rather than a pilgrimage.
