The Cairn de Barnenez is one of the oldest standing structures in Europe — a megalithic passage grave on a headland overlooking the Bay of Morlaix, built between 4500 and 4000 BCE. It looks from a distance like a terraced rocky hill, but what you're seeing is an enormous stepped cairn over 70 metres long, containing eleven separate burial chambers reached by individual stone-lined passages. The scale is overwhelming in proportion to what most people expect from Stone Age construction.

The site sits about 15 kilometres north of Morlaix on a promontory above the Baie de Morlaix, with views across the bay and out to sea. The setting alone justifies the visit: the sea-light on the stones, the silence, and the consciousness of being in the presence of something built 6,500 years ago creates a distinctive and unnerving kind of awe.

Barnenez requires a car or bicycle — there is no regular bus service to the headland. An hour to ninety minutes is comfortable for the visit. The site has a small museum and guided tours available in season; the guided tour gives access to the interior chambers, which you cannot enter independently.
