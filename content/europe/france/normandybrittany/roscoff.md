Roscoff is a small granite port town at the tip of northern Brittany, the French end of the ferry crossing from Plymouth. Most people arriving here drive straight on toward the rest of Brittany or south into France, which is a shame — the town is worth a few hours at least.

The old port is attractive, with 16th- and 17th-century stone houses built by wealthy merchants who traded in linen and onions (Roscoff's "Johnnies" were famous for cycling across Britain selling strings of pink onions door to door). The [Église Notre-Dame de Kroaz-Baz](/europe/france/normandybrittany/roscoff/eglise_notre_dame) has an ornate Renaissance bell tower, and the [Jardin Exotique de Roscoff](/europe/france/normandybrittany/roscoff/jardin_exotique), perched on a rocky outcrop, has a surprisingly rich collection of subtropical plants thriving in the mild Gulf Stream climate. The [Île de Batz](/europe/france/normandybrittany/roscoff/ile_de_batz), a low-lying island with sandy beaches and a remarkable exotic garden, is a short boat ride from the harbour.

The [Station Biologique de Roscoff](/europe/france/normandybrittany/roscoff/station_biologique), founded in 1872, is one of Europe's oldest marine research centres and has a small public aquarium. For most visitors, the town is a one- or two-night stop rather than a destination in its own right — but it's a very pleasant one.
