Fort de la Conchée sits on a wave-battered rock four kilometres off Saint-Malo, part of the defensive system built under Louis XIV and Vauban to protect the approaches to the corsair city. From shore it looks like a stone ship; from a boat you can see how completely it occupies the islet. Its isolation is the point, because this was a fort designed to command dangerous water rather than impress a town square.

The fort is not normally an everyday visitor attraction. Access is difficult and openings are rare, often tied to heritage events or organised boat excursions. Even if you only see it from the ramparts, the Sillon beach, or the water, it adds a useful piece to Saint-Malo's military geography.
