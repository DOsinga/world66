Trouville-sur-Mer would be considered one of Normandy's most elegant seaside resorts if it were not sitting right next to [Deauville](/europe/france/normandybrittany/deauville), which claims that title with more polish. But Trouville has the better character — it is a real fishing port as well as a resort, and the [waterfront fish market](/europe/france/normandybrittany/trouville/marche_aux_poissons) is one of the best in Normandy.

The beach is wide, the sand is golden, and the Belle Époque buildings along the [boardwalk](/europe/france/normandybrittany/trouville/les_planches) are beautiful. Trouville has attracted painters since the 19th century — Monet, Boudin, and Flaubert all spent time here; the small [Villa Montebello museum](/europe/france/normandybrittany/trouville/musee_villa_montebello) keeps a collection of their work. The [casino](/europe/france/normandybrittany/trouville/casino_barriere) and the grand [Cures Marines](/europe/france/normandybrittany/trouville/cures_marines) spa hotel anchor the seafront's wedding-cake architecture.

The town has a more relaxed, family-friendly feel than its glamorous neighbour across the river, and parking close to the beach can be tricky on summer weekends.
