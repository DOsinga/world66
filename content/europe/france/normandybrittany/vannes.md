Vannes is one of the best-preserved medieval towns in Brittany, ringed by [ramparts](/europe/france/normandybrittany/vannes/remparts) that drop down to formal gardens along the river Marle. The old town inside the walls is a tangle of half-timbered houses, cobbled lanes, and small squares, centred on the [Cathédrale Saint-Pierre](/europe/france/normandybrittany/vannes/cathedrale_saint_pierre) with its mix of Romanesque and Gothic stonework.

The town sits at the head of the Gulf of Morbihan, a vast inland sea scattered with islands — [boats leave from the port](/europe/france/normandybrittany/vannes/gulf_of_morbihan_boat) for day trips to the Île-aux-Moines and the Île d'Arz, or you can explore the gulf by kayak. The Wednesday and Saturday morning market on the [Place des Lices](/europe/france/normandybrittany/vannes/place_des_lices) is one of the liveliest in the region, with Breton produce, crêpes, cider, and local oysters from the gulf.

Vannes makes a good base for exploring southern Brittany: the standing stones at [Carnac](/europe/france/normandybrittany/carnac) are half an hour to the west, the walled citadel of Concarneau an hour further, and the Quiberon peninsula — with its wild coast and ferries to Belle-Île — is within easy reach.
