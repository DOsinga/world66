Hemingway's memoir of life in Paris in the 1920s among the expatriate writers and artists of the Lost Generation. Lyrically written and occasionally dishonest — he composed it from memory in his sixties and settled scores with old friends — but essential reading for anyone walking the Left Bank. The descriptions of Sylvia Beach's bookshop, Gertrude Stein's salon, and the cafes of Montparnasse are some of the finest writing about Paris ever produced.
