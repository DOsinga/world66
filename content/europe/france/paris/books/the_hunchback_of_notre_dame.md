Hugo's 1831 novel is set in medieval Paris, with the cathedral at its centre. Hugo was appalled by the state of Notre-Dame and wrote the novel partly to draw attention to the neglect of Gothic architecture in France — it worked. His descriptions of the cathedral's spires, gargoyles, and the Paris visible from its towers remain vivid even in translation. The city he describes is long gone, but Notre-Dame endures.
