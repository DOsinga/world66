The Grande Mosquée de Paris was opened in 1926 as a gesture of thanks to Muslim soldiers who fought for France during the First World War. Its courtyards, tilework, minaret, and garden bring North African and Andalusian influences into the Latin Quarter.

The mosque is both an active place of worship and a visitor sight, so dress and behave respectfully. The adjoining tea room and hammam make it a pleasant stop to combine with the Jardin des Plantes or the nearby museums.
