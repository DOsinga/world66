Hugo's 1831 novel about Quasimodo, Esmeralda, and the doomed priest Frollo is also a passionate argument for the preservation of Gothic architecture. Hugo was so alarmed by the decay and demolition of medieval Paris that he wrote this book partly as an act of cultural advocacy — and it worked. The novel's success drove public support for the restoration of Notre-Dame, beginning in 1844. His descriptions of medieval Paris, seen from the cathedral's towers, remain vivid even in translation.
