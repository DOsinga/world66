A small, perpetually booked bistro on a quiet South Pigalle corner, Le Pantruche is one of the addresses that turned the quarter into a dining destination. The cooking is modern French built on classic technique — a short, market-driven chalkboard menu, a famous Grand Marnier soufflé — served at close-set tables in a warm, slightly retro room named after old Paris slang for the city.

It is exactly the kind of place the neighbourhood is now known for: ambitious, unpretentious, and good value for the quality. Book ahead, as the dozen-odd tables fill fast; its sibling Caillebotte nearby is the fallback when it is full.
