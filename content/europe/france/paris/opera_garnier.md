Opéra Garnier is Paris at its most theatrical: a 19th-century opera house of marble staircases, gilded balconies, painted ceilings, and grand public rooms designed to be seen as much as used. Charles Garnier's building was inaugurated in 1875 and remains one of the city's great statements of Second Empire confidence.

You do not need a ticket to a performance to appreciate it. The daytime visit is enough to see the staircase, auditorium, and public foyers, though an evening inside gives the building its proper sense of occasion.
