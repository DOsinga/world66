title: "Things to Do"
type: section
---

Orange's two Roman monuments — the **Théâtre Antique** and the **Arc de Triomphe** — share the UNESCO inscription and can be seen in a half day. The theatre is the only Roman theatre in Europe with its original *scaenae frons* (stage wall) still standing, a 37-metre-high screen of stone that Louis XIV reportedly called "the finest wall in my kingdom." The arch, a kilometre north of the centre, is one of the oldest surviving triple-arched Roman triumphal arches anywhere, raised around 27 BC.

Beyond the antiquities, the old town is a small grid of Provençal streets centred on the **Place de la République** and the **Cathédrale Notre-Dame-de-Nazareth**. Climb **Colline Saint-Eutrope** behind the theatre for a view down into the cavea and across to Mont Ventoux. In July and August the theatre hosts the **Chorégies d'Orange** opera festival, France's oldest, founded in 1869.
