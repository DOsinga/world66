Sukhumi sits on a wide bay on the eastern Black Sea coast and is the capital of the breakaway region. Before the war it had close to 120,000 inhabitants, a mixed Georgian, Abkhaz, Armenian, Greek and Russian population, and a reputation as one of the most pleasant cities of the Caucasus. The war emptied it of most of its Georgian residents and the international isolation that followed left the centre frozen in time.

The waterfront, the Botanical Garden (one of the oldest in the Caucasus), and the abandoned monkey colony are the standard sights. The railway station — a vast stuccoed building that once linked Tbilisi to Sochi — is the most photographed ruin in the post-Soviet world. None of this is accessible to most foreign visitors under current conditions.
