On the seafront promenade north of the city centre, two enormous steel figures — a man and a woman — stand facing each other across about a metre of air. Every ten minutes, hidden motors begin to slide them together. They meet in the middle, briefly pass through each other, and then separate again. It is one of the most popular things to watch in Batumi, particularly at dusk when the figures are silhouetted against the Black Sea.

The work is by the Georgian sculptor Tamara Kvesitadze, and was originally created for the Venice Biennale in 2007. Locals know it as Ali and Nino after the famous Caucasus love story; officially the work is titled simply Man and Woman.
