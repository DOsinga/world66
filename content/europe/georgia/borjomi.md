You will hear the name Borjomi everywhere in Georgia — it is the brand that made the country's mineral water famous, and like Spa in Belgium, the town's name has become a generic term. The actual town sits in a narrow [gorge](/europe/georgia/borjomi/borjomi_gorge) in the Samtskhe-Javakheti region, surrounded by pine-forested hills and criss-crossed by the Mtkvari River.

The main draw is [Borjomi-Kharagauli National Park](/europe/georgia/borjomi/borjomi_kharagauli_national_park), one of the largest national parks in Europe, with hiking trails ranging from a half-day walk to a full week's traverse. In town, the [Central Park](/europe/georgia/borjomi/mineral_spring_park) contains a spring where you can drink the famous mineral water straight from the source — it tastes noticeably sulphurous and warm, nothing like the bottled version. The eccentric [Villa Firuze](/europe/georgia/borjomi/villa_firuze), with its Persian and Moorish facade, and the imperial [Likani Villa](/europe/georgia/borjomi/likani_villa), once the Romanov summer palace, are worth seeking out. Borjomi also serves as the natural base for visiting [Bakuriani](/europe/georgia/bakuriani), the nearby ski resort reached by the [charming Kukushka narrow-gauge railway](/europe/georgia/borjomi/bakuriani_railway).
