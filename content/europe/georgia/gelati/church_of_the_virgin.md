The Church of the Virgin is the main cathedral at Gelati Monastery, a large 12th-century Georgian Orthodox church that contains the finest medieval mosaics in Georgia. The central apse holds a mosaic of the Virgin with Archangels dating from the 12th century — gold-backed, remarkably intact, and considered one of the great works of Byzantine-influenced Georgian art.

The interior walls are covered in frescoes from several centuries, layered and overlapping in the way of a building that has been continuously used and continuously decorated since 1106. The effect is richly complex: images in different styles and states of preservation share the same walls, creating a visual record of Georgian religious art spanning nine centuries.
