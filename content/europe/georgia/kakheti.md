Kakheti is Georgia's most celebrated wine region, a landscape of ancient monasteries, vine-draped valleys, and fortified towns stretching east of Tbilisi toward the Alazani River. The region has been producing wine for at least eight thousand years — clay vessels called qvevri buried in the ground are still used for fermentation today — and a visit here is as much about culture and hospitality as it is about sightseeing.

The [Alazani Valley](kakheti/alazani_valley) is the heart of Kakheti: flat and fertile, backed by the Greater Caucasus to the north and the Tsiv-Gombori ridge to the south. The town of [Telavi](kakheti/telavi) serves as the regional capital and a practical base. From here you can reach [Tsinandali](kakheti/tsinandali), with its elegant estate and winery, and continue east to [Gremi](kakheti/gremi), [Alaverdi](kakheti/alaverdi_cathedral), and [Kvareli](kakheti/kvareli), each with its own cluster of monasteries and churches. The fortified town of [Sighnaghi](kakheti/sighnaghi), perched on a ridge with sweeping views over the valley, has been restored and is one of the most photogenic stops in the region.

If you know even one Kakhetian, expect to be invited home and fed a full supra — the traditional Georgian feast, presided over by a tamada (toastmaster) and accompanied by wine in quantities that require commitment. The region's hospitality is genuine and generous, and heading off the tourist trail, even briefly, pays dividends.
