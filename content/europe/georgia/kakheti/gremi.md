  Gremi was the royal capital of the Kingdom of Kakheti for most of the 16th and early 17th centuries, a prosperous city on the Silk Road with a large population and a thriving market. In 1615, Shah Abbas I of Persia sacked it so completely that it was never rebuilt. Historians estimate that the Persian raids of 1616–1617 killed or enslaved around 100,000 Georgians — roughly a quarter of the country's population at the time. The citadel on the hill is all that remains.

Gremi Citadel is the dramatic remnant of the former capital of the Kingdom of Kakheti, perched on a rocky outcrop above the valley floor west of Kvareli. The surviving complex includes the Church of the Archangels, built in 1565, and a tall defensive tower that offers a commanding view over the surrounding plain and vineyards.

The church interior preserves some of the best-surviving medieval frescoes in Kakheti. The tower serves as a small museum with archaeological finds from the site. The ruined palace walls below the church give a sense of how extensive the original city was before the Persian sacking made it a ghost site. The setting, with the snow peaks visible to the north and the flat valley stretching south, is one of the most evocative in the region.
