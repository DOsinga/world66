Kvariati's beach is the main reason people come to this small Adjaran resort village. It's a pebble beach, as is standard for the Georgian Black Sea coast, curving in a natural cove backed by the steeply rising green slopes of the Lesser Caucasus. The water is clean, noticeably less turbid than the sea near Batumi, and warm enough for swimming from June through September.

The beach is rarely overcrowded by the standards of Adjara's more famous resorts. The cove's sheltered position keeps the water calm — a contrast to the sometimes choppy conditions at Batumi's main beach. A handful of seasonal beach bars and simple restaurants set up along the waterfront in summer, serving grilled fish, khachapuri, and cold beers. In the evenings, when the day-trippers have gone, the beach is genuinely peaceful — just the sound of pebbles shifting in the gentle swell and the smell of salt and eucalyptus from the slopes above.
