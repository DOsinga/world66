**The Knight in the Panther's Skin** by Shota Rustaveli (12th century) — Georgia's national epic poem, written at the court of Queen Tamar. The story follows three knights in a quest of friendship and love across a mythologised medieval world. Georgians know it by heart the way others know Shakespeare. The Donald Rayfield translation (2012) is the best English version.

**Bread and Ashes: A Walk Through the Mountains of Georgia** by Tony Anderson (2003) — A travel account of a long walk through the Georgian mountains, Tblisi, and the Caucasus. Anderson captures the texture of early post-Soviet Georgia with unusual honesty about its poverty, corruption, and extraordinary beauty.

**Stalin: The Court of the Red Tsar** by Simon Sebag Montefiore — Stalin was born in Gori, 80 km west of Tbilisi, and spent his formative years in the city. Montefiore's comprehensive biography contextualises Georgia's most fateful export. His earlier **Young Stalin** focuses specifically on the underground years in Tiflis — the bank robberies, the printing presses, the revolutionary circle — and reads like a thriller.

**Edge of Empires: A History of Georgia** by Donald Rayfield (2012) — The comprehensive one-volume history of the country, covering everything from the ancient kingdoms of Colchis and Iberia through the Soviet period to independence. Rayfield is a Georgian literature scholar as well as a historian and writes with rare depth and affection for the subject. Essential reading before any serious visit.
