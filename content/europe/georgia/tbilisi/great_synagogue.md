The Great Synagogue of Tbilisi stands in the Abanotubani district of the old town, close to the sulphur baths. It was built in 1921 and serves the Georgian Jewish community — one of the oldest Jewish communities in the world, with a history stretching back perhaps 2,600 years. The synagogue is an active house of worship.

The building is notable for its Moorish-influenced architecture, with arched windows and a colourful interior. Visitors are welcome outside Shabbat and festivals; dress modestly. The proximity to the Juma Mosque (a few hundred metres away) and multiple Georgian Orthodox churches illustrates the old town's historic religious tolerance more vividly than any museum exhibit.
