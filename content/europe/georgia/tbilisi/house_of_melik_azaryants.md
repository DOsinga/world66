The House of Melik-Azaryants stands on Rustaveli Avenue with a facade that mixes Tbilisi grandeur, theatrical ornament, and the confidence of the city's early-20th-century commercial elite. Its balconies, arches, and sculptural details make it one of the avenue's most memorable private buildings.

It is best appreciated as part of a Rustaveli walk rather than as a destination with formal visiting hours. Pause outside, look up at the stonework, and use it as a cue to notice how Tbilisi's main boulevard layers imperial, Armenian merchant, Soviet, and modern Georgian stories in the same streetscape.
