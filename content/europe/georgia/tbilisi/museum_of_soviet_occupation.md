The Museum of Soviet Occupation occupies a hall in the main building of the Georgian National Museum on Rustaveli Avenue. It documents the seven decades of Soviet rule in Georgia from 1921 to 1991, with a particular emphasis on the human cost: the deportations, the executions, the suppression of the Georgian language and culture, and the names and faces of those who were killed.

The exhibits are emotionally powerful rather than architecturally impressive — photographs, personal documents, artefacts from the gulag, and explanatory panels in Georgian and English. It is a sobering counterpoint to the archaeological treasures on the other floors. Admission is included with the main museum ticket.
