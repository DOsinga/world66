---
title: "Shopping"
type: section
---

Tbilisi's most interesting shopping is for Georgian-specific things: wine, handicrafts, antiques, and spices.

**Wine:** The wine shops on and around Erekle II Street in the old town stock a good range of Georgian natural and traditional wines, including small producers not available outside the country. Georgian wine makes an excellent (if fragile) souvenir — buy it on your last day.

**Churchkhela and sweets:** Churchkhela — walnut chains dipped in grape juice and dried into long cylindrical sweets — are sold everywhere. The best come from Kakheti and are recognisable by their size and the fact that the grape coating is quite thick. The market at [Samgori](/europe/georgia/tbilisi/samgori_market) sells them cheaply; tourist shops in the old town charge more.

**Antiques and art:** The [Dry Bridge Market](/europe/georgia/tbilisi/dry_bridge_market) is the best place for Soviet antiques, old icons, paintings, and oddities. Prices are negotiable. The galleries around Shardeni Street and Gudiashvili Square sell contemporary Georgian art.

**Spices:** A bag of *khmeli-suneli* (the dried herb blend used in most Georgian cooking) and *adjika* paste from the Samgori market are cheap, pack flat, and transform your cooking at home.
