---
title: "Day Trips"
type: section
---

The **Neckar Valley** east of Heidelberg is one of the most scenic stretches of river in southern Germany. The valley road and the railway line pass through a succession of wine villages — Neckargemünd, Mosbach, Hirschhorn — each with a hilltop castle above the vine-terraced slopes. Eberbach, about 40km east, has a fine old town and a Cistercian monastery worth a stop. The valley is best explored by bicycle (the Neckartal-Radweg runs the full length) or by one of the historic steam trains that operate on summer weekends.

**Mannheim**, 20km north, is reachable in 15 minutes by S-Bahn and makes an interesting contrast: a Baroque planned city laid out on a strict grid, with the largest palace in Germany after Versailles. The Kunsthalle Mannheim has a strong collection of 19th and early 20th century German painting. The city is also the starting point for Rhine cruises.

**Stuttgart**, the state capital of Baden-Württemberg, is 80km south and about 40 minutes by ICE train. The Staatsgalerie is one of Germany's best art museums, the Porsche and Mercedes-Benz museums are just outside the centre, and the city's Württemberg wine country fills the surrounding hills. A day trip is easily done and well worth it, particularly if the Heidelberg crowds are getting heavy.

**Speyer**, 30km southwest across the Rhine, has one of the finest Romanesque cathedrals in Europe — the Kaiserdom, a UNESCO World Heritage Site — and the excellent Technik Museum with a real space shuttle on display. Worms, another 20km north along the Rhine, has the cathedral where Luther refused to recant in 1521.
