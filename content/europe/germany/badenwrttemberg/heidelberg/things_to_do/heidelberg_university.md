The Ruprecht-Karls-Universität Heidelberg, founded in 1386 by Elector Ruprecht I, is the oldest university in Germany and one of the oldest in the German-speaking world. With around 30,000 students it remains a large and active research university, and its presence shapes the character of the entire city — the density of bookshops, cafés, and late-night bars on the Hauptstrasse and the lanes behind it is a direct result. Among the alumni and faculty over the centuries are an extraordinary roll-call: Hegel, Jaspers, Gadamer, and over fifty Nobel laureates.

The main places to visit are in the old town. The **Alte Aula** (Old Auditorium) on Grabengasse is the historic great hall of the university, built in the 1880s in neo-Renaissance style, with a painted ceiling and a gallery of former rectors' portraits; it can be visited as part of a guided tour or during open days. Nearby is the **Universitätsmuseum**, which traces the 600-year history of the institution through documents, instruments, and curiosities.

Perhaps the most visited university-related sight is the **Studentenkarzer** (Student Prison), a few steps away on Augustinergasse. From 1712 to 1914, students who committed minor offences — duelling, rowdiness, disturbing the peace — were sentenced to time in this small lockup. Rather than regard it as a punishment, generations of students treated it as a rite of passage, covering every surface of the cells with drawings, poems, and graffiti. The accumulated layers of student art are genuinely remarkable. The prison is now a small museum, open most mornings; entry is inexpensive.
