Karlsruhe is unlike any other German city in its layout: 32 streets radiate outward from the [Schloss](/europe/germany/badenwrttemberg/karlsruhe/ducal_palace) like spokes from a hub, a Baroque urban plan that gives the city its nickname, "the fan city." At the centre of the market square, a [sandstone pyramid](/europe/germany/badenwrttemberg/karlsruhe/karlsruhe_pyramid) marks the tomb of the city's founder. Founded in 1715 as a grand-ducal residential city, it was built from scratch as an expression of Enlightenment rationalism — the geometry still legible today on any map.

The city is home to the [Karlsruhe Institute of Technology (KIT)](/europe/germany/badenwrttemberg/karlsruhe/kit_campus), one of Germany's oldest and most respected technical universities, giving it a large student population and a lively, unpretentious atmosphere. It is also Germany's "Residence of Law," housing both the Federal Constitutional Court and the Federal Court of Justice — their legal legacy made tangible at the [Platz der Grundrechte](/europe/germany/badenwrttemberg/karlsruhe/platz_der_grundrechte) outside the courts building.

For visitors, the highlights are cultural: the Badisches Landesmuseum inside the Schloss, the [Staatliche Kunsthalle](/europe/germany/badenwrttemberg/karlsruhe/staatliche_kunsthalle) with its Grünewald altarpiece, and the [ZKM Center for Art and Media](/europe/germany/badenwrttemberg/karlsruhe/zkm) — one of the most innovative media art museums in the world. The [Schlosspark and city gardens](/europe/germany/badenwrttemberg/karlsruhe/schlosspark) are genuinely impressive, stretching from the station to the city centre and beyond.

Karlsruhe also works well as a base for the [Black Forest](/europe/germany/badenwrttemberg/blackforest), reachable within an hour.
