  concept but also a piece of guerrilla marketing. Karl was sceptical the public was ready; Bertha

  with local resources: using a hairpin to unblock a fuel pipe, buying ligroin (a petroleum solvent)

In the courtyard of a building on Waldhofstraße, a simple plaque marks the workshop where Karl Benz developed and built the Patent Motorwagen between 1884 and 1886 — the world's first vehicle designed and built to be powered by a petrol engine. The invention happened here, in Mannheim, in this unremarkable industrial space.

The site itself is modest — this is not a museum but a working building with a memorial marker — but it gives a sense of scale: the breakthrough that reshaped the modern world happened in a small workshop in a medium-sized German city. The Technoseum carries the full story of Benz's invention and the family's role in the automobile industry with more detail and artefacts.

For those who want to see the physical location of automotive history rather than a museum reconstruction, this is the spot.
