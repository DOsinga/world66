Rottweil is the oldest town in Baden-Württemberg, founded by the Romans in AD 73, and the place that gave the Rottweiler dog its name — the breed descends from the mastiffs that Roman legions used to drive cattle over the Alps. A fountain on the Hauptstrasse commemorates the connection.

The old town is remarkably well preserved, with painted Renaissance and baroque facades lining the [Hauptstrasse](/europe/germany/badenwrttemberg/rottweil/hauptstrasse) and a pair of fortified gate towers — the [Schwarzes Tor](/europe/germany/badenwrttemberg/rottweil/schwarzes_tor) is the more striking — marking the old boundaries. The [Heilig-Kreuz-Münster](/europe/germany/badenwrttemberg/rottweil/heilig_kreuz_munster) holds an excellent group of late-medieval altarpieces, and the [Dominikanermuseum](/europe/germany/badenwrttemberg/rottweil/dominikanermuseum) houses Roman mosaics excavated nearby and a fine collection of medieval Swabian wood sculpture. The [Stadtmuseum](/europe/germany/badenwrttemberg/rottweil/stadtmuseum) on Hauptstrasse covers the town's two thousand years of history.

Rottweil is best known for its [Fasnet](/europe/germany/badenwrttemberg/rottweil/fasnet), the Swabian carnival held in the week before Lent. The "Narrensprung" — a procession of masked figures in elaborate carved wooden masks — is one of the oldest and most dramatic carnival traditions in Germany, drawing thousands of spectators. If you are in the region in February, it is not to be missed.

The [thyssenkrupp test tower](/europe/germany/badenwrttemberg/rottweil/test_tower), a 246-metre concrete cylinder on the outskirts, offers a viewing platform with panoramic views extending to the Alps on clear days.
