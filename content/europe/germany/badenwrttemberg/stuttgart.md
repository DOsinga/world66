Stuttgart is set in a bowl in the Swabian Hills — a city of vineyards, forested ridges, and steep valleys that give it a completely different topography from the flat industrial cities of the Rhine plain. It is the capital of Baden-Württemberg, the home of Mercedes-Benz and Porsche, and a city whose charms are genuine but take some finding.

The center pivots around [Schlossplatz](/europe/germany/badenwrttemberg/stuttgart/schlossplatz), the large square flanked by the [New Palace](/europe/germany/badenwrttemberg/stuttgart/new_palace) and the [Kunstmuseum Stuttgart](/europe/germany/badenwrttemberg/stuttgart/kunstmuseum_stuttgart) in its glowing glass cube. The [Staatsgalerie](/europe/germany/badenwrttemberg/stuttgart/stategallery), with works by Rembrandt, Picasso, and Joseph Beuys in a postmodern extension by James Stirling, is one of Germany's finest art museums. [Schillerplatz](/europe/germany/badenwrttemberg/stuttgart/schillerplatz) next to the [Old Palace](/europe/germany/badenwrttemberg/stuttgart/theoldpalace) and the [Stiftskirche](/europe/germany/badenwrttemberg/stuttgart/stiftskirche) is Stuttgart's oldest square and the best starting point for walking the historic center.

For automotive history, the [Mercedes-Benz Museum](/europe/germany/badenwrttemberg/stuttgart/mercedes_benz_museum) in Untertürkheim is a spectacular double-helix building holding 130 years of automobile history, while the [Porsche Museum](/europe/germany/badenwrttemberg/stuttgart/porschemuseum) in Zuffenhausen traces the sports car maker's history from the 356 onward. The [Stuttgart TV Tower](/europe/germany/badenwrttemberg/stuttgart/stuttgart_tv_tower) south of the city, completed in 1956 as the world's first concrete television tower, gives panoramic views over the Neckar valley and the Swabian Alps. The [Weissenhof Estate](/europe/germany/badenwrttemberg/stuttgart/weissenhof_estate) on a northern hillside is a UNESCO-listed housing exhibition from 1927 with buildings by Mies van der Rohe, Le Corbusier, and 15 other modernist masters.

Stuttgart sits on remarkable thermal springs. The [Mineral Bath Berg](/europe/germany/badenwrttemberg/stuttgart/mineralbathberg) is the oldest and quietest of the city's mineral baths. The [Wilhelma](/europe/germany/badenwrttemberg/stuttgart/wilhelma) zoo and botanical garden, set in a 19th-century Moorish palace complex in Bad Cannstatt, is one of Germany's most unusual attractions. The city also grows wine on urban hillsides — some vineyards are within walking distance of the center, and the local Trollinger and Lemberger wines are drunk almost entirely by locals. Stuttgart is about 100 miles south of [Frankfurt](/europe/germany/hessen/frankfurt).
