The New Palace is Stuttgart's most imposing building — a long baroque facade facing onto Schlossplatz, with wings extending back toward the royal gardens. Construction began in 1746 and took 61 years to complete, spanning several generations of Württemberg rulers and the shift from duchy to kingdom. The interior was destroyed in World War II bombing; reconstruction was completed in 1978.

Today the palace serves as the official seat of the Baden-Württemberg state government. The exterior, with its central pediment and symmetrical wings, is one of the finest examples of 18th-century baroque architecture in southwest Germany. The courtyard opens onto the Schlossgarten park, which runs north through the city to the main train station.

State government offices occupy most of the interior, and regular access is limited, but the exterior and courtyard are freely accessible at all times. Occasional open days allow visits to the state reception rooms.
