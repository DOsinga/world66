The Danube Swabian Museum tells a story that starts in Ulm and follows German-speaking settlers down the Danube into southeastern Europe. It is a museum about migration, frontier settlement, identity, and the upheavals that later scattered those communities again after the Second World War.

For visitors, it adds a wider historical layer to Ulm's river setting. The exhibits are not just local antiquarian material; they explain why this Swabian city looks east along the Danube, and how family histories can stretch across Hungary, Romania, Serbia, Croatia, and back to Germany.
