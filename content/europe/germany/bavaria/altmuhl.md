The Altmühl Valley (Altmühltal) is a natural park in central Bavaria, threading along the Altmühl River between Gunzenhausen and Kelheim. It is one of Germany's most popular cycling and hiking destinations, and it earns that reputation: limestone cliffs, medieval walled towns, Roman ruins, and a river wide enough to canoe make for a genuinely varied few days.

The main draw is the [Altmühltal Radweg](/europe/germany/bavaria/altmuhl/altmuhl_cycling) — a 160-kilometre cycling route along the river — and the parallel hiking trail. [Canoeing the Altmühl](/europe/germany/bavaria/altmuhl/canoeing) between the towns is another classic. The valley passes [prehistoric quarries at Solnhofen](/europe/germany/bavaria/altmuhl/solnhofen) (source of the Archaeopteryx fossil), [Roman Limes fortifications](/europe/germany/bavaria/altmuhl/roman_limes), and [Pappenheim](/europe/germany/bavaria/altmuhl/pappenheim) with its spectacular castle above the river. [Eichstätt](/europe/germany/bavaria/altmuhl/eichstaett), the valley's main town, is a baroque bishop's seat with a good fossil museum.

The Altmühl-Donau canal runs through the valley, connecting the Rhine, Main and Danube — a feat of engineering that is also pleasant to walk or cycle alongside. The river itself connects a string of calm lakes well suited to camping and swimming.
