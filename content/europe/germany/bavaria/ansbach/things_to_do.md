---
title: "Things to Do"
type: section
---

Ansbach rewards a day of easy wandering. The unmissable sight is the **Ansbach Residenz** — the lavish baroque palace of the Margraves of Brandenburg-Ansbach, with 27 state rooms decorated in stucco, tapestries, and delftware. Behind the palace, the **Hofgarten** (court garden) is one of the finest baroque gardens in Bavaria, arranged in formal parterres with clipped hedges and a long orangery wing. The garden is where **Kaspar Hauser** was stabbed in 1833, and a monument near the pavilion marks the spot.

The old town is compact and pleasant. The main square holds the **Markgraf-Georg-Brunnen** fountain and is ringed by half-timbered and baroque townhouses. The twin churches of **St. Gumbertus** and **St. Johannis** are both worth entering — St. Gumbertus contains the tombs of the Hohenzollern margraves and an outstanding late Gothic choir. The **Kaspar Hauser Monument** stands on the Platzl, a small square a few minutes' walk from the palace.

The **Gymnasium Carolinum**, founded in 1528, is one of Germany's oldest secondary schools and its beautiful courtyard can be visited. In July the **Rokoko-Festspiele** fill the palace gardens with costume concerts and baroque music performances.
