Bamberg is one of the best-preserved medieval towns in Germany and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Built on seven hills along the Regnitz River, it escaped major wartime damage and still looks remarkably as it did centuries ago — half-timbered houses, Baroque facades, and narrow lanes climbing up from the waterfront.

The [Cathedral](/europe/germany/bavaria/bamberg/bamberg_cathedral), a powerful mix of Romanesque and Gothic, dominates the hill town. Inside you will find the [Bamberger Reiter](/europe/germany/bavaria/bamberg/bamberg_horseman), a 13th-century equestrian statue whose rider has never been identified — one of the great mysteries of medieval art. The [Altes Rathaus](/europe/germany/bavaria/bamberg/altes_rathaus), perched improbably on a bridge in the middle of the river, is one of the most photographed buildings in Bavaria. Round the cathedral square are the two prince-bishops' palaces — the half-timbered [Alte Hofhaltung](/europe/germany/bavaria/bamberg/alte_hofhaltung) and the Baroque [Neue Residenz](/europe/germany/bavaria/bamberg/neue_residenz), whose rose garden gives the best view of the rooftops. Down by the water, [Klein Venedig](/europe/germany/bavaria/bamberg/klein_venedig) — the row of fishermen's houses standing directly in the Regnitz — is the city's other obvious photograph.

Bamberg is also a serious beer town. It has more breweries per capita than almost anywhere in Germany, and the local speciality is Rauchbier — a dark, smoky lager brewed with malt dried over beechwood fires. [Schlenkerla](/europe/germany/bavaria/bamberg/schlenkerla), in the old town, is the classic place to try it. You will either love it or think someone poured liquid bacon into your glass.
