The most photographed building in Bamberg: a half-timbered tower and Baroque core sitting on an artificial island in the middle of the Regnitz River, accessed by bridges on either side. Built in the fourteenth century after a property dispute with the prince-bishop made it impossible for the town to find dry ground for a town hall — so they made their own.

The exterior frescoes (Johann Anwander, 1755) include one famous detail: a putto whose stucco leg projects three-dimensionally out of the painted wall. Inside is the Sammlung Ludwig porcelain collection.
