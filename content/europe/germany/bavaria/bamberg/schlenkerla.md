story: "The malt is dried on the brewer's own beechwood fires — a method abandoned by almost every other brewery in Europe when industrial kilns arrived in the nineteenth century. Schlenkerla kept doing it, more or less by accident; today Rauchbier is what Bamberg is known for."
---

The classic Bamberg brewpub, in a Dominican monastery refectory from 1310, smoke-blackened beams, and a beer drawn directly from wooden barrels rolled out into the public room. The house Rauchbier — dark, intensely smoky from beechwood-fired malt — tastes for first-time drinkers like someone has dropped a slice of bacon into the glass. Stay for two. After a few sips it stops being shocking and starts being delicious.

Long communal tables, simple Franconian food (Schäuferla, sauerbraten), no reservations. Get there before seven on a weekend or you will queue.
