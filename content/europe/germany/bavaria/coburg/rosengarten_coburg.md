The Rosengarten — rose garden — occupies a pleasant green space on the northern edge of Coburg's old town, laid out as a formal park in the nineteenth century. It is the sort of civic garden that German towns do well: well-maintained, popular with local families, and at its best in June when the roses are in bloom. The park also contains the Convent of Our Lady (Kloster Unserer Lieben Frau), founded in the fourteenth century, whose Gothic church is still in use.

The garden makes a good fifteen-minute stroll from the market square, and the combination of formal rose beds, mature trees, and the convent buildings has a quiet charm. On warm summer afternoons there are outdoor concerts and events here. It is not a major sight in itself, but it provides a useful green pause between the Veste and the old town.
