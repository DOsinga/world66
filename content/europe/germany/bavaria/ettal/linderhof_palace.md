Linderhof Palace, about 10 km west of Ettal, is the smallest of Ludwig II's royal castles and the most intimate. Where Neuschwanstein was a grand theatrical statement and Herrenchiemsee an extravagant Versailles copy, Linderhof is a compact rococo gem hidden in a steep valley, surrounded by formal French gardens that feel surreal at this altitude.

The palace interior is extraordinary: every surface is gilded, mirrored, or encrusted with ornament. The dining room features the famous disappearing table. The bedroom is the largest room, where Ludwig slept under a canopy of embroidered silk. The Hall of Mirrors is a smaller but genuinely dazzling version of Versailles. The whole thing was built between 1869 and 1878 and Ludwig lived here regularly — unlike his other, more famous projects.

The gardens contain a Moorish Kiosk and a Venus Grotto — an artificial cave with a lake, stalactites, and coloured electric lighting installed to recreate scenes from Wagner's Tannhäuser. Ludwig regularly had himself rowed across the grotto lake by a servant while Wagner played. Entry to the gardens is free in winter when the palace itself is closed for parts of the season.
