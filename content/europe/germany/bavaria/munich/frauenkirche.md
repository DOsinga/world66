The twin-towered cathedral is Munich's most recognisable landmark, visible from almost everywhere in the city. Built between 1468 and 1488 in a late Gothic brick style that is unusual for Bavaria, the exterior is austere but commanding. The interior was largely rebuilt after wartime bomb damage gutted it in 1944 — the result is severely plain, almost unnerving in its blankness compared to the florid Baroque churches nearby.

Look for the Devil's Footprint near the entrance — a dark mark in the floor tile, accompanied by a legend about the architect tricking the devil into financing the construction. The south tower is open to visitors with good views across the old city. Entry to the cathedral is free; tower admission is modest.
