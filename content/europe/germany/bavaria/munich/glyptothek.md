A Neoclassical museum on Königsplatz dedicated entirely to ancient Greek and Roman sculpture, built by Leo von Klenze for King Ludwig I and opened in 1830. The colonnaded building is one of the finest examples of Greek Revival architecture in Germany. Inside, the collection is organised chronologically through a series of barrel-vaulted rooms — Greek archaic, classical, and Hellenistic work followed by Roman portraits and copies.

The Aphaia pediment sculptures are the centrepiece, but the Roman portrait collection is exceptional in its own right. The building was heavily damaged in 1944; the wall paintings were destroyed and the renovation focused on the architecture rather than restoration. The plain whitewashed walls give the sculptures unusual prominence. Free admission on Sundays.
