The neighbourhood around Munich's main train station is one of the city's most ethnically diverse and culturally interesting areas — the opposite of the Altstadt in character. The streets between the Hauptbahnhof and the Hackerbrücke bridge have been the landing point for successive waves of immigrants since the 19th century, and the result is a dense concentration of international restaurants, specialist groceries, and cheap eating.

The Bahnhofsviertel gets characterised as seedy (some sex industry, some drug activity near the station) but in daylight it's simply a busy urban neighbourhood. The Turkish market on Tumblingerstrasse in the Sendling area (Saturday mornings) is one of Munich's better weekly food markets. The Vietnamese restaurants on Schwanthalerstrasse are among the most authentic in Bavaria.
