A municipal museum on St. Jakobs-Platz covering Jewish history and culture in Munich, opened in 2007 alongside the rebuilt Ohel Jakob Synagogue and a community centre. The permanent exhibition takes an unconventional approach — asking questions rather than providing linear narratives — and covers Munich's Jewish community from the Middle Ages through the Nazi period to the present.

The building's transparent ground floor echoes the design principle of openness and accountability. The neighbourhood around St. Jakobs-Platz has become an important civic space: the Saturday market in Viktualienmarkt is a five-minute walk, and the combination of synagogue, museum, and community centre creates a living presence rather than a memorial-only site. Closed Mondays; modest admission fee.
