Munich's grand shopping boulevard runs east from the National Theatre on Max-Joseph-Platz to the Maximilianeum on the banks of the Isar. The street was laid out in the 1850s by King Maximilian II as a ceremonial promenade, and the architecture of the flanking buildings — a distinctive mix of Gothic and Romanesque elements developed specifically for the project — is unlike anything else in the city.

The Maximilianeum at the east end houses the Bavarian State Parliament; its exterior is freely viewable from the street and bridge. The street itself has become Munich's luxury shopping corridor, with international fashion houses occupying the historic facades. The ensemble is more interesting as urban planning than as a place to spend money.
