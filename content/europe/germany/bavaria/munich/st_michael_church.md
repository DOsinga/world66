A large Jesuit church on the Kaufingerstrasse pedestrian zone, built between 1583 and 1597 — one of the earliest and most important Renaissance churches in southern Germany. The nave is the widest barrel-vaulted space north of the Alps. The exterior has a striking gable decorated with a series of Holy Roman emperors and dukes in relief.

The church served as the spiritual centre of the Jesuit Counter-Reformation in Bavaria. The crypt houses Wittelsbach royal burials; a separate admission fee applies for the crypt visit but it's worth it. The church itself is free to enter and usually quiet enough to absorb the scale of the nave without difficulty.
