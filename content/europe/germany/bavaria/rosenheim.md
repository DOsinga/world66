Rosenheim is a Bavarian market town at the confluence of the Inn and Mangfall rivers, about an hour southeast of [Munich](/europe/germany/bavaria/munich) on the road toward Austria. It is a pleasant, unpretentious place with a handsome old town centre — colourful facades, arcaded streets, and a pedestrian zone around the [Max-Josefs-Platz](/europe/germany/bavaria/rosenheim/max_josefs_platz).

The town is popular with German visitors for its traditional Bavarian character without the tourist crush of the bigger Alpine towns. The [Herbstfest](/europe/germany/bavaria/rosenheim/herbstfest), Rosenheim's autumn beer festival, is one of the largest in Bavaria after Munich's Oktoberfest and has a more local feel. The [Inn-Museum](/europe/germany/bavaria/rosenheim/inn_museum), housed in a medieval toll house on the river, tells the story of salt transport along the Inn — a trade that made the town wealthy.

The Chiemsee, Bavaria's largest lake, is about 20 minutes east by train, and the mountains around Kufstein and the Inntal are close enough for [day trips](/europe/germany/bavaria/rosenheim/day_trips). Rosenheim works well as a base for exploring the Bavarian Alps without the higher prices of resort towns like Garmisch-Partenkirchen.
