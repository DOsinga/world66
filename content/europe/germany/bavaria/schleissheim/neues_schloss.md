The Neues Schloss Schleissheim is a palace on the scale of Versailles — begun in 1701 for Elector Max Emanuel of Bavaria, who intended it as an imperial residence and filled it with one of the finest collections of Baroque painting in Central Europe. The state rooms are a succession of gilded ceilings, painted walls, and Flemish and Dutch masterworks: Rubens, Titian, and Van Dyck all hang here.

Unlike the main Munich museums, the Neues Schloss rarely feels overcrowded. The formal gardens between the palace and Lustheim — designed with long water canals and geometric parterres — give the setting a grandeur that requires the space to appreciate. The combination of architectural ambition, superb paintings, and manageable visitor numbers makes this one of the better-value palace experiences in Bavaria. Admission is moderate; the Bavarian palace pass covers multiple sites.
