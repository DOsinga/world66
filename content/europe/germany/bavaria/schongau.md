Schongau is a small Bavarian town on the Lech river, about 60 kilometres southwest of Munich, with one of the best-preserved medieval town walls in southern Germany. The old town sits on a hill above the river bend, ringed by towers and a largely intact circuit of walls that you can walk around in under an hour. It's not a major tourist destination — most visitors to the area head straight to Neuschwanstein — but it rewards a quiet morning or afternoon.

The [Ballenhaus](/europe/germany/bavaria/schongau/ballenhaus), a 15th-century granary on the market square, is the most striking building in town, and the [parish church of Maria Himmelfahrt](/europe/germany/bavaria/schongau/maria_himmelfahrt) has fine Rococo frescoes by Matthäus Günther. The [Lech valley](/europe/germany/bavaria/schongau/lech_valley) below makes for good cycling and walking, and the town is on the Romantic Road cycling route, which brings a steady trickle of tourers through in summer. Three kilometres west, the [Romanesque basilica of St Michael in Altenstadt](/europe/germany/bavaria/schongau/altenstadt_basilica) is the finest Romanesque church in Upper Bavaria.

For a base in the Bavarian Alps foothills, Schongau is less crowded and cheaper than Füssen to the south, with access to the same mountains and to Neuschwanstein about 25 kilometres away.
