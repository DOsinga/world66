Tegernsee is a small resort town on the eastern shore of Lake Tegernsee, about an hour south of [Munich](/europe/germany/bavaria/munich) by train. It is polished, prosperous, and very Bavarian: lake steamers at the pier, onion-domed church towers, beer from the old monastery brewery, and the Alps rising just beyond the water.

The town grew around [Tegernsee Abbey](/europe/germany/bavaria/tegernsee/st_quirinus), a Benedictine monastery founded in the 8th century. After secularisation, the complex became a Wittelsbach summer residence, and that royal-lakeside air still shapes the waterfront. The old abbey church of St Quirinus is the main historic sight; nearby are the [Museum Tegernseer Tal](/europe/germany/bavaria/tegernsee/museum_tegernseer_tal), the [Olaf Gulbransson Museum](/europe/germany/bavaria/tegernsee/olaf_gulbransson_museum), and the famous Bräustüberl in the former monastery buildings.

The lake is the other reason to come. In summer, boats link Tegernsee with Gmund, Bad Wiessee, Rottach-Egern, and other landing stages around the shore. Swimming spots and lakeside walks are easy, and the [Wallberg](/europe/germany/bavaria/tegernsee/wallberg) above Rottach-Egern gives the classic high view back over the water. Tegernsee is not wild or cheap, but it is one of the easiest Alpine-lake days from Munich, and a good place to trade city noise for clear water, church bells, and mountain light.
