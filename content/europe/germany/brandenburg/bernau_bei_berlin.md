Bernau bei Berlin is a small historic town about 25km north of Berlin, easily reached by S-Bahn (line S2). It has a well-preserved medieval centre, including three of its original town gates — the [Steintor](bernau_bei_berlin/steintor), the [Werdertor](bernau_bei_berlin/werdertor), and the [Mühlentor](bernau_bei_berlin/muehlentor) — and a history dating back to the 13th century. It makes an excellent half-day or full-day trip from Berlin for anyone interested in Brandenburg's medieval past and in one of the finest Bauhaus buildings in Germany.

The [Heimatmuseum](bernau_bei_berlin/heimatmuseum) inside the Steintor covers 500 years of local history, from medieval brewing to local craft and daily life. The [Pulverturm](bernau_bei_berlin/pulverturm), a converted medieval gunpowder tower, now hosts the Wolf Kahlen Intermedia Arts Museum, an idiosyncratic space showing media art and video installation.

The biggest surprise in Bernau is the [ADGB Trade Union School](bernau_bei_berlin/adgb_trade_union_school) on the northern edge of town: a 1928 Bauhaus complex by Hannes Meyer, with its sinuous curved layout and radical social vision. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the most significant pieces of modern architecture in the Berlin region. Combine it with a walk into the forests of [Barnim Nature Park](bernau_bei_berlin/barnim_nature_park), which begins just beyond the town's edge, and you have a full day with genuine variety.
