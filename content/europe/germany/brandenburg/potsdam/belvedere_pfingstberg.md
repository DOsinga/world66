The Belvedere auf dem Pfingstberg is one of Potsdam's best-kept secrets — a pair of Italianate towers on a wooded hill north of the Dutch Quarter, offering the most panoramic views in the city. Frederick William IV designed the complex himself in the 1840s, modelling it on Italian Renaissance villas, but construction was never completed. The towers and terraced gardens were restored after reunification by a local citizen group.

The climb to the upper platform of the towers rewards with a 360-degree view that takes in the entire Potsdam palace landscape: Sanssouci to the west, Cecilienhof and the Heiliger See below, the Havel lakes stretching south, and the towers of the Old Market Square in the city centre. On a clear day, the Berlin television tower is visible in the distance. The belvedere is open on weekends and some weekdays in season — check the website before visiting, as hours are limited.
