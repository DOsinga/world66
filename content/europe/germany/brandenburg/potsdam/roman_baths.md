The Roman Baths (Römische Bäder) in Sanssouci Park are a picturesque ensemble of classical and Italian vernacular buildings grouped around a small courtyard with a goldfish pool and vine-covered pergola. Designed by Karl Friedrich Schinkel and Ludwig Persius in the 1830s for Crown Prince Frederick William, they were built as a romantic folly — a stage-set impression of a Roman country estate, never intended to function as actual baths.

The complex includes an Italianate garden house, a Roman villa facade with a loggia, and a small courtyard filled with ancient sculpture and plantings. It is a quiet, intimate corner of Sanssouci Park that most visitors walk past on their way to the larger palaces. In summer, the rose and vine covered pergolas are in full bloom and the courtyard feels genuinely Mediterranean. The Roman Baths are south of Charlottenhof, connected by a short path through the park.
