Bremen's Rathaus is one of the finest examples of Weser Renaissance architecture in northern Germany — a long, ornate façade of sandstone with Gothic arches, projecting bays, and detailed carved friezes. It was extended in the early 17th century and the Renaissance upper storeys added at that time give the building its distinctive horizontal emphasis. The interior, accessible only on guided tours, contains the Golden Chamber with an elaborate panelled ceiling, and the Great Hall with portraits of emperors and city councillors going back centuries.

Tours run Monday to Saturday (hourly from 11am to 4pm, fewer on Sundays) and include the historic rooms that are otherwise closed. The building has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2004. Entry to the forecourt and view of the façade is free at all times.
