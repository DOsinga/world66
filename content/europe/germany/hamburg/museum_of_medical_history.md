The Medizinhistorisches Museum, in a corner of the University Hospital Eppendorf, is a small but quietly disturbing collection of anatomical specimens, surgical instruments, wax moulages of diseases and a reconstructed early-20th-century operating theatre. It is not for queasy visitors.

The wax moulages, hand-painted with terrifying realism, are the highlight: a kind of analogue medical photography in three dimensions, used to teach doctors before colour film was available. The museum also covers the appalling history of the medical school during the Nazi period, including forced sterilisations carried out at the hospital. Combine with a walk around leafy Eppendorf if you need something calmer afterwards.
