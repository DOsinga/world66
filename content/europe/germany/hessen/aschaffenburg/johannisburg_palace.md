Johannisburg Palace (Schloss Johannisburg) is one of the most important Renaissance palaces in Germany, a monumental red sandstone structure rising above the River Main. Built in the early 17th century for the Archbishop-Elector of Mainz, it follows a regular courtyard plan with four corner towers that gives it an architectural clarity rare in German palatial architecture of the period.

The interior houses a gallery of Dutch and Flemish paintings from the 17th century — Rubens, Jan Brueghel, and others — which constitutes one of the better secondary art collections in Bavaria. The palace apartments can also be visited. But the most surprising element is the [Cork Model Collection](/europe/germany/hessen/aschaffenburg/cork_model_collection) in the south wing — a set of extraordinarily detailed models of ancient Roman monuments made entirely from bottle cork. The palace grounds offer good views over the Main river.
