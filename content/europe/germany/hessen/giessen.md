Giessen is a city of more than 850 years standing in the middle of Hesse, roughly halfway between [Frankfurt](/europe/germany/hessen/frankfurt) and [Marburg](/europe/germany/hessen/marburg). It sits on the River Lahn and has around 73,000 inhabitants. Though the inner city was largely destroyed in World War II, a few historic buildings survived: the [Old Castle](/europe/germany/hessen/giessen/old_castle) (Altes Schloss), the Burgmannenhaus, and the Zeughaus among them.

Giessen is defined by its university, the [Justus Liebig University](/europe/germany/hessen/giessen/justus_liebig_university), the second oldest and second largest in Hesse. With around 28,000 students the city has one of the highest student-to-population ratios in Germany, which gives it an energy well above what its size would suggest. Famous scientists worked here in the 19th century — Röntgen and Liebig among them. That tradition of scientific inquiry is still visible: the [Liebig Museum](/europe/germany/hessen/giessen/liebig_museum), housed in Liebig's original laboratory, is one of the most atmospheric small science museums in Germany, and the [Mathematikum](/europe/germany/hessen/giessen/mathematikum) is a world-first hands-on mathematics museum that draws visitors from across the country.

Outdoors, the [River Lahn](/europe/germany/hessen/giessen/lahn_river) is the main attraction — popular for canoeing, cycling, and swimming in summer. The surrounding Middle Hessian highlands offer hiking through green countryside dotted with small castles. The [Botanical Garden](/europe/germany/hessen/giessen/botanical_garden), dating to 1609, is one of the oldest in Germany and worth an afternoon.
