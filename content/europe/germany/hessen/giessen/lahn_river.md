  The Lahn flows for 245 kilometres from its source in the Rothaar Mountains to
  join the Rhine at Lahnstein. For most of its length it passes through
  landscapes barely changed since the 19th century — the Lahn Valley railway
  follows the river but rarely intrudes, and the path along the banks is one of
  the quieter long-distance cycle routes in Germany.

The River Lahn is one of Giessen's best assets. A well-marked cycle path runs along both banks and connects the city to Marburg to the north and Wetzlar to the south-west — both reachable within a half-day's cycling. In summer the riverbanks fill with students; there are several spots where locals swim, particularly downstream from the city centre.

Canoes and kayaks can be rented from operators in and around Giessen, and paddling the Lahn through the valley is a popular day trip. The river is gentle enough for beginners but varied enough to stay interesting. If you prefer two wheels, the Lahn cycle path (Lahntal-Radweg) is well-signposted and can be done as a multi-day tour all the way to the Rhine, with accommodation and bike repair stations along the route.
