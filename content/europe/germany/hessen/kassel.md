Kassel is best known for two things: the [documenta](/europe/germany/hessen/kassel/documenta), one of the world's most important contemporary art exhibitions held every five years, and the [Bergpark Wilhelmshöhe](/europe/germany/hessen/kassel/bergpark_wilhelmshohe), a hillside park crowned by the colossal [Herkules](/europe/germany/hessen/kassel/herkules) statue that is the city's landmark. The park is a UNESCO World Heritage Site — its cascading water features, running down the hillside from the Herkules to the palace lake below, are a spectacle that has drawn crowds for centuries.

The city sits on the Fulda River and about 63 percent of its area is woodland, parks, and gardens with public access. The [Wilhelmshöhe Palace](/europe/germany/hessen/kassel/wilhelmshohe_palace) houses an outstanding collection of Old Masters, and the [Löwenburg](/europe/germany/hessen/kassel/lowenburg), a faux-medieval castle in the park, adds to the romantic atmosphere. Kassel is also the focal point of the German Fairy Tale Road — the Brothers Grimm lived and worked here in the early 19th century, writing much of their famous collection. The story is told at [GRIMMWELT](/europe/germany/hessen/kassel/grimmwelt) on a hillside terrace above the city.

With 200,000 inhabitants and a university of some 18,000 students, Kassel has a youthful edge. The documenta has left permanent marks on the cityscape — notably Joseph Beuys's [7000 Oaks](/europe/germany/hessen/kassel/7000_oaks), each paired with a basalt block scattered throughout the city, and Walter De Maria's [Vertical Earth Kilometer](/europe/germany/hessen/kassel/vertical_earth_kilometer) under the Friedrichsplatz. Local specialities include Ahle Worscht (dry-cured sausage) and beer from the local breweries.
