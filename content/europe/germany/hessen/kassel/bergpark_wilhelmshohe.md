The **Bergpark Wilhelmshöhe** is Kassel's biggest claim to fame — a 240-hectare landscape park rising up the slope of the Habichtswald, declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2013. At the top stands the colossal **Herkules statue** (1717), an eight-metre copper figure on top of a stone pyramid on top of an octagonal pavilion, the whole composition visible from anywhere in the city.

The park's signature event is the **Wasserspiele** (water features), released on summer Wednesdays, Sundays, and holidays. Water flows down a series of cascades, channels, an aqueduct, and a series of grottoes before erupting in a 50-metre fountain in front of Wilhelmshöhe Palace at the foot of the hill. It is the largest hillside hydraulic system in Europe and the engineering — gravity-driven, no pumps — is older than the United States.
