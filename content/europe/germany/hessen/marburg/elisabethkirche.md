The Elisabethkirche is the oldest purely Gothic church in German-speaking lands, begun in 1235 in the year of St Elisabeth's canonisation and completed around 1290. It is remarkable inside: slim columns, pale stone, and unusually large windows for its era that flood the nave with light. The building was revolutionary for its time and influenced church construction across central Europe.

The church is still a pilgrimage site. Elisabeth's golden reliquary shrine — the Goldene Madonna — is one of the masterpieces of medieval German goldsmithing. In the northern transept you'll find the memorial to the Landgraves of Hesse, and scattered through the church are the tombs of various figures from the region's history. After World War II, the remains of Hindenburg and the Hohenzollern kings Frederick William I and Frederick the Great were transferred here from Potsdam and kept until 1991, when they were reburied in their original resting places.
