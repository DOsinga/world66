The Büchnerhaus in Goddelau is a modest but thoughtfully presented museum in the house where Georg Büchner was born on 17 October 1813. Büchner is one of the most remarkable figures in German literary and political history: in just 23 years he wrote Danton's Death, Woyzeck, and Leonce and Lena, founded a radical political movement, fled prosecution for sedition, and died in exile in Zurich. The Woyzeck fragment alone secured his place in the canon of European literature, and the Hessische Landbote — the revolutionary pamphlet he co-wrote in 1834 — remains a landmark document of early German radicalism.

The museum occupies the ground floor of the birth house, a typical Ried farmhouse dating from the early 19th century. The permanent exhibition covers Büchner's life chronologically, with particular attention to his time in Darmstadt and Gießen, his political activities, and the literary works. First editions, manuscript facsimiles, and contemporary documents are displayed alongside contextual material about the turbulent political climate of the 1830s. The exhibition is in German, but the subject matter is clear enough that visitors with some background knowledge will find it rewarding. The Büchner Prize — Germany's most prestigious literary award — is named in his honour and has been awarded annually since 1923; the museum marks recent recipients.
