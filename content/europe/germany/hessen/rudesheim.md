Rüdesheim am Rhein is one of Germany's most visited small towns — remarkable given its modest population — due to its position at the southern end of the Rheingau wine region and its dramatic Rhine Valley setting. It has been a stop for travellers since at least the early 19th century, when English and German Romantic writers made the Rhine valley fashionable.

The narrow [Drosselgasse](/europe/germany/hessen/rudesheim/drosselgasse) pedestrian lane is the tourist epicentre: wine taverns and restaurants packed together, inevitably crowded in summer. Beyond this, the town has real substance. The [Klunkhardshof](/europe/germany/hessen/rudesheim/klunkhardshof), the [Adlerturm](/europe/germany/hessen/rudesheim/adlerturm) fortified tower, and the [Brömserburg](/europe/germany/hessen/rudesheim/bromserburg) — a thousand-year-old castle now housing the Rheingau Wine Museum — are all worth seeing. The [Church of St. Jakobus](/europe/germany/hessen/rudesheim/churchstjakobus) on the market square, rebuilt after wartime destruction, retains medieval tombstones of the town's old nobility.

The [Niederwald Monument](/europe/germany/hessen/rudesheim/niederwald_monument) above the town, reachable by gondola, gives spectacular views over the narrow Rhine valley, the Nahe estuary, the nature park Rhein-Taunus, and the vine-covered Rheingau slopes. Up the hill, the [Abbey of St. Hildegard in Eibingen](/europe/germany/hessen/rudesheim/eibingen_abbey) — founded by Hildegard of Bingen in the 12th century — is worth the short detour for the church mosaics and the abbey shop's wine. The Rüdesheimer Riesling grown here is world-famous.
