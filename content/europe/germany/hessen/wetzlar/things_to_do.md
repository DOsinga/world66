---

Wetzlar's Altstadt is one of the most photogenic in central Germany and an unusually intact piece of urban history. The town was the seat of the **Reichskammergericht** — the supreme imperial court of the Holy Roman Empire — from 1693 to 1806, which is why the old centre has so many handsome lawyers' houses and so few of the usual industrial-era replacements.

The young Goethe spent the summer of 1772 here as a legal trainee at the imperial court, fell in love with Charlotte Buff (engaged to a colleague), and turned the experience into *The Sorrows of Young Werther*. The **Lottehaus** and **Jerusalemhaus** trace this episode in detail. The unfinished **Dom** is the architectural anchor, the riverside walks along the Lahn are an easy hour's stroll, and the modern story of Wetzlar is the **optical industry** — Leica still makes its cameras here and runs a small museum and visitor centre at Leitz-Park.
