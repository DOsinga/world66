Bad Gandersheim is a small spa town in southern [Lower Saxony](/europe/germany/lowersaxony), just west of the Harz. It looks modest at first: half-timbered houses, a market square, a slow river valley, and wooded hills close enough for an afternoon walk. Then the abbey church comes into view, and the town's weight becomes clearer.

[Gandersheim Abbey](/europe/germany/lowersaxony/bad_gandersheim/gandersheim_abbey) was founded in the 9th century by the Liudolfing family, ancestors of the Ottonian emperors, and for centuries was one of Saxony's important religious and political houses. Its most famous canoness was Hrotsvitha, usually called Roswitha in German, the 10th-century writer often described as the first known female dramatist in the Latin West.

The town still leans into that theatrical inheritance. Each summer the [Gandersheimer Domfestspiele](/europe/germany/lowersaxony/bad_gandersheim/gandersheimer_domfestspiele) turns the square in front of the abbey church into a large open-air stage. Outside festival season, the pleasure is quieter: the Romanesque church, the old market, the town museum, and Brunshausen monastery on the edge of town. Bad Gandersheim is not as grand as [Goslar](/europe/germany/lowersaxony/goslar) or [Hildesheim](/europe/germany/lowersaxony/hildesheim), but it gives a spare day a real shape.
