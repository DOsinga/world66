---
title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

Braunschweig's local specialities are modest but distinctive. **Braunschweiger Mumme** is a thick, syrup-like malt extract (originally a strong dark beer from the 15th century, now non-alcoholic by law after export regulations changed in the 19th century) — it turns up in sauces and as a flavouring in local cooking. The city is also in asparagus country: in May and June, restaurants around the region serve asparagus with ham and potatoes in a dozen variations.

The **Magniviertel** has the best concentration of restaurants and traditional German pubs (*Kneipen*). The lanes around the Magnitorwall and Alte Knochenhauerstraße are where locals eat and drink in the evening. The market square (**Altstadtmarkt**) and its surrounding streets have more tourist-oriented cafés, but the quality is generally fine.
