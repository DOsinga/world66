---
title: "Things to Do"
type: section
---

Braunschweig's historic core is compact and almost entirely walkable. The **Burgplatz** is the centrepiece — a medieval square containing the cathedral, Dankwarderode Castle, and the famous bronze lion (the original, cast in 1166, is inside the castle; the outdoor one is a 19th-century replica). The atmosphere in the evening, especially when lit, is among the finest in Lower Saxony.

The **Herzog Anton Ulrich Museum** (closed Mondays) holds one of Europe's oldest public art collections: Vermeer's *Girl with a Wine Glass*, Rembrandts, Cranach, Holbein, and an exceptional applied arts collection. The city's other museum worth serious time is the **Braunschweigisches Landesmuseum**, which covers the regional history from the Welf duchy to the present.

For a different angle on the medieval period, walk north from Burgplatz through the lanes of the **Magniviertel** — the oldest surviving quarter of the city, its half-timbered houses largely intact despite the wartime bombing of the wider city. The **Altstadtmarkt** square, five minutes west, has the Gothic town hall and the church of St Martin opposite.
