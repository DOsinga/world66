Norderney is the most visited of the East Frisian Islands and the one with the most to offer beyond beaches and fresh air. The western end of the island has a proper town — the oldest North Sea resort in Germany, established in 1797 — with restaurants, shops, a theatre, and a [thalasso spa](/europe/germany/lowersaxony/east_frisian_islands/norderney/thalasso_spa). The eastern two-thirds are largely undeveloped dune landscape within the Wadden Sea National Park.

The beaches here are outstanding. The [north beach](/europe/germany/lowersaxony/east_frisian_islands/norderney/north_beach) stretches for kilometres, wide and sandy, with sections designated for surfing, swimming, and nudism (this is Germany, after all). The [*Weiße Düne* (White Dune)](/europe/germany/lowersaxony/east_frisian_islands/norderney/weisse_dune) area on the eastern end is particularly beautiful and less crowded. On the south side, the Wadden Sea mudflats are exposed at low tide — guided [mudflat walks](/europe/germany/lowersaxony/east_frisian_islands/norderney/wadden_sea_walks) (*Wattwanderungen*) are one of the island's signature experiences.

The town itself is pleasant for an evening stroll. The [Conversationshaus](/europe/germany/lowersaxony/east_frisian_islands/norderney/conversationshaus), a grand nineteenth-century assembly hall, hosts concerts and exhibitions. The streets behind the promenade have a good selection of fish restaurants and cafes. Norderney has more nightlife than any other East Frisian Island, though expectations should be calibrated accordingly — this is a family resort, not Ibiza.

Cars are allowed in the town area but most of the island is car-free. [Bicycles](/europe/germany/lowersaxony/east_frisian_islands/norderney/cycling) are the standard transport and can be rented everywhere. Ferries from Norddeich run roughly hourly and take about an hour.
