**The Eyes of My Princess** by Carlos Cuauhtémoc Sánchez. Not strictly Hanoverian, but Sophie of Hanover and her family loom large in any popular history of the 17th-century English succession. For the real thing, **Sophie's World of Letters**, edited by Ringmar, gathers her remarkable correspondence with Leibniz.

**Leibniz: An Intellectual Biography** by Maria Rosa Antognazza. The standard modern life of the philosopher who spent his last 40 years in Hanover as Welf court librarian. Slow but rewarding.

**The Tin Drum** by Günter Grass. Set mostly in Danzig but with a long chapter in post-war Lower Saxony — useful for the texture of life in the British Zone of occupation, of which Hanover was a key city.

**Berlin Alexanderplatz** by Alfred Döblin. Not about Hanover, but the kind of modernist German urban novel that the Kestnergesellschaft's interwar exhibitions sat alongside. A useful piece of cultural context for the Anzeiger-Hochhaus and the city's brief 1920s flowering.
