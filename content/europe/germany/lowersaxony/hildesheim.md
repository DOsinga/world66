Hildesheim is a mid-sized city in [Lower Saxony](/europe/germany/lowersaxony) whose two Romanesque churches — [St. Michael's](/europe/germany/lowersaxony/hildesheim/st_michaels_church) and the [Cathedral of the Assumption](/europe/germany/lowersaxony/hildesheim/hildesheim_cathedral) — are UNESCO World Heritage Sites. Both date to around 1000 AD and contain remarkable art, including the famous [Bernward Doors](/europe/germany/lowersaxony/hildesheim/bernward_doors) and the Bernward Column in the cathedral.

The city centre was heavily bombed in 1945 but the [historic market square](/europe/germany/lowersaxony/hildesheim/market_square) has been faithfully reconstructed, with its distinctive half-timbered Knochenhaueramtshaus (Butchers' Guild Hall). In the cathedral cloister, a thousand-year-old rose bush — said to be the oldest in the world — still blooms each May. The [Roemer- und Pelizaeus-Museum](/europe/germany/lowersaxony/hildesheim/roemer_und_pelizaeus_museum) holds one of Germany's finest Egyptian antiquities collections, a further reason to spend more than a passing hour in the city.
