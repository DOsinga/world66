Oldenburg is a relaxed city in northwestern Lower Saxony, close enough to [Bremen](/europe/germany/bremen) for an easy side trip but with a quieter identity of its own. It was once the seat of the Grand Duchy of Oldenburg, and that courtly past still shows in the [castle](/europe/germany/lowersaxony/oldenburg/oldenburg_castle), the [Schlossgarten](/europe/germany/lowersaxony/oldenburg/schlossgarten), and the formal buildings around the market square.

The centre is compact, flat, and largely pleasant on foot or by bike. Brick churches, the old town hall, the Lappan tower, and pedestrian shopping streets sit within a short walk of the museums. The [Horst Janssen Museum](/europe/germany/lowersaxony/oldenburg/horst_janssen_museum) is the most distinctive cultural stop, dedicated to the sharp, restless graphic work of one of northern Germany's great draughtsmen.

Oldenburg is not a blockbuster city, and it should not be sold as one. Its appeal is the combination of good museums, green space, student life, and a handsome old core that survived well enough to feel coherent. If you have a spare day in northwestern Germany and want somewhere easier than Bremen or Hamburg, it earns the stop.
