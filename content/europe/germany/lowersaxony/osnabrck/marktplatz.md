The Marktplatz is the heart of Osnabrück and one of the best-preserved medieval market squares in northern Germany. It is surrounded by a sequence of significant buildings — the Gothic Rathaus to the north, the Marienkirche to the east, the Ledenhof to the south — that make it feel like a coherent historical ensemble rather than a collection of survivors. The square itself is large enough to feel open and has been the commercial and civic centre of the city since the Middle Ages.

The Saturday market fills the space with stalls selling local produce, bread, cheese, flowers, and seasonal specialities from farms in the surrounding Westphalian countryside. It is a working market rather than a tourist attraction, which makes it more interesting. At Christmas, the market takes over the square with stalls and mulled wine, and the Gothic and Renaissance buildings around it provide a backdrop that suits the occasion. At other times of year, the square is pleasant to cross on the way between the major sights without requiring any particular effort to appreciate.
