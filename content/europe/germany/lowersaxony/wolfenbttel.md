Wolfenbüttel is a small town just south of [Braunschweig](/europe/germany/lowersaxony/braunschweig) in Lower Saxony, with one of the best-preserved timber-frame old towns in northern Germany. Over 600 half-timbered houses line the streets of the centre, many of them dating from the 16th and 17th centuries when Wolfenbüttel served as the residence of the Dukes of Brunswick-Lüneburg. The [Stadtmarkt](/europe/germany/lowersaxony/wolfenbttel/stadtmarkt) is the showpiece, and the canal-side lanes of [Klein Venedig](/europe/germany/lowersaxony/wolfenbttel/klein_venedig) are the most photogenic.

The town's greatest claim to fame is the [Herzog August Bibliothek](/europe/germany/lowersaxony/wolfenbttel/herzog_august_bibliothek), which in the 17th century was the largest library in Europe. Gotthold Ephraim Lessing served as its librarian from 1770 until his death in 1781, and the [Lessing House](/europe/germany/lowersaxony/wolfenbttel/lessinghaus) next door is now a museum. The ducal palace, [Schloss Wolfenbüttel](/europe/germany/lowersaxony/wolfenbttel/schloss_wolfenbuettel), houses a local history museum, and two great Protestant churches — [Marienkirche](/europe/germany/lowersaxony/wolfenbttel/marienkirche) and [St Trinitatis](/europe/germany/lowersaxony/wolfenbttel/st_trinitatis) — anchor the centre. [Jägermeister](/europe/germany/lowersaxony/wolfenbttel/jaegermeister_headquarters), the herbal liqueur, was invented here in 1934 — the company's headquarters are still in town, though there is no distillery tour.
