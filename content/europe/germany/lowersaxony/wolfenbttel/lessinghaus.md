Across the road from the library stands the small Baroque house where Gotthold Ephraim Lessing lived as ducal librarian from 1777 until his death in 1781. The building has been preserved as a museum of his life and work, with original manuscripts, his furniture, and a permanent exhibition on the German Enlightenment.

The collection is in German, but the museum provides English booklets. For anyone interested in 18th-century thought, theatre, or the early history of religious tolerance, it is unmissable. About an hour is enough.
