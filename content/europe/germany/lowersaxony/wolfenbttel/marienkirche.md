The Marienkirche dominates the central market square with its tall stone tower and slate spire. Construction ran from 1608 to 1626, and the building is one of the most important examples of early Protestant church architecture anywhere in Germany.

The interior is bright and uncluttered, in keeping with Reformation theology, but the tombs of the Wolfenbüttel dukes in the choir are richly carved. The Renaissance pulpit and the painted ducal box are highlights. Open daily; free entry.
