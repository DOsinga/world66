Neubrandenburg is a small city in the lake district of Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania, best known for its remarkably complete [medieval city wall](/europe/germany/mecklenburgwesternpomerania/neubrandenburg/stadtmauer). The brick wall, dating from the thirteenth century, still encircles the old town almost entirely, punctuated by four elaborately decorated Gothic gates — the [Treptower Tor](/europe/germany/mecklenburgwesternpomerania/neubrandenburg/treptower_tor), Stargarder Tor, Friedländer Tor, and Neues Tor — that are among the finest of their kind in northern Germany.

The city was heavily damaged in 1945 and much of the centre was rebuilt in GDR-era concrete blocks, giving it a stark contrast between the medieval fortifications and the socialist architecture within. The [Konzertkirche](/europe/germany/mecklenburgwesternpomerania/neubrandenburg/konzertkirche), a ruined Gothic church rebuilt as a modern concert hall, is a striking example of this layered history. [Lake Tollensesee](/europe/germany/mecklenburgwesternpomerania/neubrandenburg/tollensesee), just south of the city, is a long glacial lake with beaches, sailing, and a cycling path around its shore. Neubrandenburg makes a useful stop when exploring the Mecklenburg Lake District.
