Rostock is the largest city in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania, a former Hanseatic League trading port on the Baltic coast with a population of around 200,000. The old town retains much of its medieval character — gabled merchant houses, brick Gothic churches, and stretches of the old city wall, all rebuilt after heavy wartime damage.

The [St Mary's Church (Marienkirche)](/europe/germany/mecklenburgwesternpomerania/rostock/marienkirche) is the architectural highlight, a vast Gothic basilica with a magnificent astronomical clock dating from 1472. The [Abbey of the Holy Cross](/europe/germany/mecklenburgwesternpomerania/rostock/abbey_holy_cross), a 13th-century Cistercian nunnery, now houses the Cultural History Museum. [St Petri Church](/europe/germany/mecklenburgwesternpomerania/rostock/st_petri_church), with its 117-metre spire, offers panoramic views over the city and the harbour.

[Warnemünde](/europe/germany/mecklenburgwesternpomerania/warnemunde), Rostock's seaside district at the mouth of the Warnow River, has a broad white sand beach, a lighthouse, and a strip of fish restaurants along the Alter Strom harbour. It is one of the most popular beach destinations on the German Baltic coast.

Rostock also has one of the oldest [universities](/europe/germany/mecklenburgwesternpomerania/rostock/university_of_rostock) in northern Europe, founded in 1419, which gives the city a youthful energy. The [Rostock Zoo](/europe/germany/mecklenburgwesternpomerania/rostock/rostock_zoo) is one of the best in northern Germany, and the [Kunsthalle Rostock](/europe/germany/mecklenburgwesternpomerania/rostock/rostock_art_gallery) has a strong collection of 20th-century German art. Day trips from Rostock include the Molli narrow-gauge railway to Bad Doberan and Heiligendamm (Germany's oldest seaside resort), and the Renaissance town of Güstrow, about 50 kilometres south.
