Rügen is [Germany's](/europe/germany) largest island, connected to the mainland by a bridge near Stralsund. Its dramatic white chalk cliffs — the Kreidefelsen, most famously the [Königsstuhl](/europe/germany/mecklenburgwesternpomerania/rugen/koenigsstuhl) — have been famous since Caspar David Friedrich painted them in the early nineteenth century, and they remain the island's most striking sight. [Jasmund National Park](/europe/germany/mecklenburgwesternpomerania/rugen/jasmund_national_park) protects the cliffs and the ancient beech forest behind them, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Beyond the cliffs, Rügen has long sandy beaches, particularly along the east coast at [Binz](/europe/germany/mecklenburgwesternpomerania/rugen/binz), Sellin, and [Göhren](/europe/germany/mecklenburgwesternpomerania/rugen/goehren) — elegant Baltic seaside resorts with restored Wilhelmine-era pier architecture (Seebrücken) in the distinctive [Bäderarchitektur](/europe/germany/mecklenburgwesternpomerania/rugen/baederarchitektur) style. The [Prora](/europe/germany/mecklenburgwesternpomerania/rugen/prora) complex, a vast Nazi-era holiday resort on the coast, is a sobering historical curiosity that has been partly converted into museums and apartments.

The island is easy to explore by car or bicycle. The [Rasender Roland](/europe/germany/mecklenburgwesternpomerania/rugen/rasender_roland), a narrow-gauge steam railway, runs between several of the resort towns and is a charming way to travel. [Cape Arkona](/europe/germany/mecklenburgwesternpomerania/rugen/cape_arkona) at the northern tip has two lighthouses and the remains of a Slavic fort, while the car-free island of [Hiddensee](/europe/germany/mecklenburgwesternpomerania/rugen/hiddensee) just off the western coast offers an even quieter escape.
