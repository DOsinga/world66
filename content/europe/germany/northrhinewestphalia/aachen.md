Aachen is a small city in the northwest corner of Germany, touching the borders of Belgium and the Netherlands at the same time. For centuries its identity was defined by two things: the hot springs that rise through the rock below the old town, and Charlemagne, who built his palace here in the 790s and turned a thermal resort into the capital of an empire that stretched from the Pyrenees to the Elbe.

The [Aachener Dom](/europe/germany/northrhinewestphalia/aachen/aachener_dom) is the city’s undisputed centrepiece — a UNESCO World Heritage site and one of the great medieval buildings of Europe, where thirty Holy Roman Emperors were crowned between 936 and 1531. The [Cathedral Treasury](/europe/germany/northrhinewestphalia/aachen/aachen_treasury) next door holds Charlemagne’s relics and some of the finest medieval goldwork in the world. The [Rathaus](/europe/germany/northrhinewestphalia/aachen/rathaus), built on the foundations of the Carolingian palace, faces the Dom across Am Markt and gives the old town its second great medieval building.

Beyond the historic core, Aachen is a university city — RWTH Aachen is one of Germany’s top technical universities, and 45,000 students give the place an energy that older German cities sometimes lack. The [Pontstrasse](/europe/germany/northrhinewestphalia/aachen/pontstrasse) runs north from the old town through the student quarter and has the best concentration of bars and cheap restaurants. The [Carolus Thermen](/europe/germany/northrhinewestphalia/aachen/carolus_thermen) lets you bathe in the same sulphurous springs that Charlemagne used.

The [Three Countries Triangle](/europe/germany/northrhinewestphalia/aachen/dreilaendereck) is 8 km to the west — the hilltop point where Germany, Belgium, and the Netherlands meet — and a popular excursion. Cologne is 60 km east and reachable in 45 minutes by fast train.
