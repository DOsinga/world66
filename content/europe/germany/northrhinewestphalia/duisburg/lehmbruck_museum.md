The Lehmbruck Museum is dedicated to the work of Wilhelm Lehmbruck, the Duisburg-born sculptor who was one of the major figures of German Expressionism. Lehmbruck's elongated, mournful figures — The Kneeling Woman, The Fallen Man — are among the most affecting sculptures of the early 20th century, and this collection represents them in depth.

The museum building itself is notable: a purpose-built concrete structure from 1964 by Manfred Lehmbruck (the sculptor's son), designed around the needs of sculpture exhibition, with top-lit spaces and a garden that extends the collection outside. The permanent collection goes beyond Lehmbruck to include a broader survey of 20th-century sculpture, with works by Rodin, Brancusi, Henry Moore, Joseph Beuys, and others. A thoughtful and undervisited museum that merits a longer stay than most people give it.
