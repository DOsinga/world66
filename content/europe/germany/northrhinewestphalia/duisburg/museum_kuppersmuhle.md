The Museum Küppersmühle (MKM) occupies a converted grain mill and silo on the edge of Duisburg's Inner Harbour. The conversion was carried out by Herzog & de Meuron — the architects responsible for London's Tate Modern — who added a distinctive red staircase tower to the solid brick industrial shell while leaving the basic structure intact.

The collection focuses on post-war and contemporary German art, with particular depth in the work of Gerhard Richter, Georg Baselitz, Anselm Kiefer, and Jörg Immendorff. It is a serious collection, assembled over decades by Sylvia and Ulrich Ströher, and represents some of the most significant German art of the second half of the 20th century. The combination of industrial architecture and heavyweight painting feels right: the scale of the building matches the ambition of the work.
