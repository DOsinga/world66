The Soul of Africa Museum is an unlikely institution: a private collection of several hundred West African vodun and traditional religious objects displayed in a small apartment building in central Essen. The collection focuses on Benin, Togo, and Nigeria, and includes carved figures, brass work, textiles, and ritual objects assembled over four decades by the collector Walther Lensing.

The museum is not large — a visit takes about an hour — but the collection is seriously good and the presentation is more contextually aware than many ethnographic collections in major German museums. The emphasis is on the sacred and ritual function of the objects rather than their aesthetic qualities as art objects, which is an unusual and more honest approach. Opening times are limited; check the website before visiting.
