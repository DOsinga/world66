story: >
  The Glaselefant was designed by the artist Horst Rellecke and built for the 1984 Federal Horticultural Show on the grounds of the former Maximilian coal mine. It weighs around 700 tonnes, stands nearly 30 metres high, and contains about 30,000 glass bricks. The original plan was to remove it after the show; local opposition was strong enough that it stayed, and it has been a Hamm landmark ever since.
---

The Glaselefant is Hamm's most iconic structure — a 30-metre glass elephant that sits in the middle of the Maximilianpark on the site of a former coal mine. You can ride an elevator up through the elephant's body to reach a viewing platform in the torso, with views over the park and the surrounding city. There is also a restaurant inside the elephant at ground level.

It sounds absurd, and it is — in a completely charming way. The Glaselefant has none of the pretension of landmark architecture and all of the appeal of something built for pure delight. The Maximilianpark surrounding it is a well-maintained garden landscape with a butterfly house, tropical greenhouses in the old mine buildings, and pleasant walks through former industrial structures colonised by plants.
