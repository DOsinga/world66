---
title: "Things to Do"
type: section
---

Münster's centre is compact and very walkable — or cyclable, which is how most people here move. The [Dom](/europe/germany/northrhinewestphalia/mnster/munster_cathedral) (St Paulus Cathedral) dominates the old town and houses a notable treasury and the extraordinary 1540 astronomical clock, which performs at noon each day. The [Rathaus](/europe/germany/northrhinewestphalia/mnster/historical_city_hall) on the Prinzipalmarkt is one of the finest civic buildings in Westphalia — it was here that the Peace of Westphalia was signed in 1648, ending the Thirty Years' War, and the Friedenssaal where it happened is open to visitors. The nearby **Schloss** is worth a look from the outside for its Baroque façade.

[St Lambert's Church](/europe/germany/northrhinewestphalia/mnster/st_lamberti_church), a short walk from the Rathaus, is unmistakable: three iron cages hang from its tower, placed there in 1536 to display the corpses of the executed Anabaptist leaders who had seized the city a year earlier. The cages are still there.

The [LWL-Museum für Kunst und Kultur](/europe/germany/northrhinewestphalia/mnster/lwl_museum_fur_kunst_und_kultur) holds the city's main art collection, with fine late Gothic Westphalian works alongside 20th-century Expressionism. The **Stadtmuseum** covers local history and is free to enter, as is the **LWL-Museum für Naturkunde** (natural history, with a mammoth skeleton). The [Pablo Picasso Museum](/europe/germany/northrhinewestphalia/mnster/picasso_museum) specialises in graphic art. For outdoor time, the [Aasee](/europe/germany/northrhinewestphalia/mnster/aasee) lake southwest of the centre is where Münster relaxes.

For a change of pace, rent a bicycle and do a circuit of the [Promenade ring](/europe/germany/northrhinewestphalia/mnster/cycling_and_the_promenade) — the tree-lined boulevard on the old city walls that encircles the entire old town — then head out into the flat Münsterland countryside, one of the best cycling regions in Germany.
