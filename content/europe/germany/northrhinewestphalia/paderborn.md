Paderborn is a small city of about 100,000 in the Ostwestfalen-Lippe region of [North Rhine-Westphalia](/europe/germany/northrhinewestphalia). People have settled here since pre-Roman times, drawn by the [springs of the Pader](/europe/germany/northrhinewestphalia/paderborn/pader_springs) — one of the shortest rivers in Germany, rising in over 200 springs right in the city centre.

The [cathedral](/europe/germany/northrhinewestphalia/paderborn/paderborn_cathedral) is the centrepiece, and built into one of its windows is the city's main landmark: the Drei-Hasen-Fenster (Three Hares Window), showing three rabbits arranged in a circle, each sharing an ear with its neighbour — an optical illusion that has fascinated visitors for centuries. The old town around the cathedral is compact and walkable, and the [Chapel of St. Bartholomäus](/europe/germany/northrhinewestphalia/paderborn/bartholomaeus_church) nearby is the oldest hall church north of the Alps, built in 1017 by Byzantine craftsmen.

Once a year in summer Paderborn celebrates [Libori](/europe/germany/northrhinewestphalia/paderborn/libori_festival), a week-long festival that combines a church procession, a funfair, and a great deal of beer. It has been held since 836 and draws visitors from across the region. The [Heinz Nixdorf MuseumsForum](/europe/germany/northrhinewestphalia/paderborn/heinz_nixdorf_museum), one of the world's largest computer museums, is a significant draw for technology enthusiasts.
