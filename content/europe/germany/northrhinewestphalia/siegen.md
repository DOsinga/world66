Siegen's main claim to fame is a big one: Peter Paul Rubens was born here in 1577. The city has leaned into that connection, and the Siegerlandmuseum in the [Oberes Schloss](/europe/germany/northrhinewestphalia/siegen/oberes_schloss) (Upper Castle) holds a notable collection of Rubens paintings alongside exhibits on the region's iron-mining heritage. The hilltop castle itself, with its views over the wooded Siegerland hills, is worth the walk up.

The city sits in a valley surrounded by the densely forested hills of the Rothaargebirge, making it a good base for hiking along the [Rothaarsteig](/europe/germany/northrhinewestphalia/siegen/rothaarsteig) trail. Below the Oberes Schloss, the [Unteres Schloss](/europe/germany/northrhinewestphalia/siegen/unteres_schloss) (Lower Castle) anchors the old town centre with its distinctive octagonal tower. The [Nikolaikirche](/europe/germany/northrhinewestphalia/siegen/nikolaikirche) nearby — a round church built on a Crusader model — features the golden Kroenchen crown visible from across the city.

Siegen is not a tourist town — it is a mid-sized university city with a workaday feel and limited nightlife. But the combination of the Rubens connection, two proper castles, the [Hainer Stollen](/europe/germany/northrhinewestphalia/siegen/hainer_stollen) mine where Nazis hid artworks during WWII, and easy access to the surrounding forests gives it enough to fill a day or an overnight stop.
