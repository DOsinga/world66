The Hainer Stollen is an abandoned mine shaft in the hills above Siegen, connected to the Siegerland's centuries-old iron-ore mining tradition. It gained its place in history during the Second World War, when Nazi cultural authorities used it to hide artworks evacuated from Westphalian museums to protect them from Allied bombing. The collection stored here included significant paintings and sculptures, recovered by American forces in 1945.

Today the Hainer Stollen is accessible for guided tours arranged through local historical associations and the Siegerlandmuseum. The mine itself gives a vivid impression of the Siegerland's industrial past quite apart from its wartime role. The combination of industrial heritage and wartime history makes it one of the more unusual sites in the region. Tours are not daily and typically need to be arranged in advance; the Siegen tourist office can provide current contact details and availability.
