  Coal was mined in the Muttental valley as early as the 14th century, making it one of the oldest documented mining sites in the Ruhr region. The small-scale "Stollenbergbau" — drift mining into the valley walls rather than sinking deep shafts — continued here long after the big industrial operations further north had made it obsolete. The trail passes sites that span six centuries of continuous extraction.

The best reason to come to Witten. This walking trail runs through the Muttental valley south of the city, passing a series of preserved and reconstructed mine sites spanning the region's coal-mining history from medieval drift mines to 19th-century pit-head buildings. The trail is clearly waymarked, about 6km in length, and can be walked in two to three hours at a comfortable pace.

The open-air museum aspect works well: the mine shafts and buildings are set in a wooded valley with the Muttental stream running alongside, making it a genuinely pleasant walk rather than a trudge through industrial heritage. The oldest surviving mine on the trail dates to the early 19th century; the geological outcrops along the way show the coal seams that made the Ruhr the industrial heartland it became.
