  The garden was created in 1991 as a gift from Kaiserslautern's Japanese sister city of Tokushima on the island of Shikoku. The collaboration brought Japanese landscape architects to Kaiserslautern and sent German craftsmen to Japan for training. The result is not a decorative pastiche but a garden that follows authentic Japanese principles — it was designed by Masao Hayakawa, one of Japan's leading garden designers, and is considered one of the finest Japanese gardens in Europe.
---

The Japanischer Garten in the Stadtpark is one of the more unexpected discoveries in Kaiserslautern — a large, carefully maintained Japanese garden that genuinely merits the descriptions lavished on it. Covering about 1.7 hectares, it includes a tea house, two large ponds, stone lanterns, and a dry rock garden, all designed to Japanese principles of landscape composition.

The garden is at its best in May, when the azaleas and cherry trees are in flower, and in autumn, when the maples turn. The setting within the broader Stadtpark — a public green space popular with Kaiserslautern residents — means the garden feels part of a living city rather than a museum exhibit.
