  The Pfälzerwald is the largest contiguous forest in Germany — larger than the Black Forest, less famous. It merges seamlessly across the French border with the Vosges du Nord to form a biosphere reserve designated by UNESCO in 1998. The forest has been managed for timber production for centuries, but the combination of sandstone geology, varied tree species, and a network of small streams gives it a biodiversity that surprises visitors expecting a monoculture.
---

The Palatinate Forest begins almost immediately at the city limits of Kaiserslautern and extends south and west through one of Germany's most varied natural landscapes. The forest covers 1,800 square kilometres of sandstone hills, river valleys, and mixed woodland — it feels genuinely wild in its deeper sections, despite the proximity of several towns.

For walkers, the forest offers hundreds of kilometres of marked trails. The sandstone rock formations around Dahn — about 40 minutes south by car — are particularly spectacular: towers and faces of red sandstone rising through the forest canopy, excellent for scrambling and bouldering. The Deutsche Weinstrasse wine villages along the eastern edge of the forest (Neustadt, Deidesheim, Bad Dürkheim) are easily combined with a forest walk for a classic Palatinate day out.
