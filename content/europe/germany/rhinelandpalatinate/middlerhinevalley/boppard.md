Boppard is the largest town on the Middle Rhine and has a more lived-in quality than the smaller tourist villages downstream. Its Roman walls — some of the best-preserved in Germany — can still be traced around the old town, and the Romanesque church of St. Severus has carved capitals worth studying closely. The waterfront promenade is unusually long and pleasant.

The main natural spectacle is the Bopparder Hamm, the dramatic river bend visible from the Vierseenblick lookout reached by chairlift from the town. From the top, the bend in the river creates an illusion of four separate bodies of water — one of the classic Rhine views. The steep vineyards on the slopes of the Hamm produce Riesling from old slate soils. Boppard is on the main Koblenz-Mainz rail line with frequent connections.
