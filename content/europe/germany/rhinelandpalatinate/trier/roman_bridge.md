The Römerbrücke (Roman Bridge) crosses the Moselle near the old city centre and is one of the oldest bridges still in use in Germany. The massive stone piers date from the 2nd century AD, making them authentic Roman engineering; the arches above were rebuilt in the 12th and 17th centuries after flood damage. The bridge is still open to vehicles and pedestrians.

Walking across or along the riverbank gives you a close view of the Roman stonework at water level — rough-cut basalt piers that have withstood nearly two millennia of Moselle floods. The view from the bridge back towards the city, especially in the early morning, is one of the better prospects in Trier.

The riverside path south from the bridge leads eventually to the Zurlauben dock, where boat trips on the Moselle depart during the summer season.
