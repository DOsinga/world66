A sloping field of around 2,000 medieval and modern Jewish gravestones, just outside the old city
wall. Many of the oldest stones lean into each other, their inscriptions worn but legible. The
grave of the Maharil — Rabbi Jacob ben Moses Moelin, who codified the customs of Ashkenazi Jewry
in the 14th century — is a particular focus of pilgrimage, often piled with small stones left by
visitors.

UNESCO inscribed the cemetery as part of the ShUM Sites of Speyer, Worms and Mainz in 2021. The
gates are usually open; a small panel near the entrance explains the layout. Visit quietly,
respectfully, and ideally with a kippah or covered head — it is still an active religious site.
