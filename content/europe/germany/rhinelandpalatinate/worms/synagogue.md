The oldest synagogue site in Germany — first built around 1034, rebuilt and extended through the
medieval period, burned by the Nazis on Kristallnacht in 1938, and reconstructed in 1959 using the
surviving original stones. The current building feels small and quiet; the men's hall preserves
the Romanesque pillars and stone benches.

Behind the synagogue, a stone staircase leads down into the 1186 **mikveh**, a deep ritual bath
cut into the rock to reach the groundwater. The cool, dim shaft is one of the most atmospheric
spaces in Worms. The site sits on the UNESCO-listed Judengasse, alongside the Levy Synagogue and
the Rashi House — visit them all in one circuit of about an hour.
