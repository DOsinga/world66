  The St Johanner Markt was laid out by Friedrich Joachim Stengel in the 1760s
  as a showcase for the court of Nassau-Saarbrücken. The baroque architect is
  said to have conceived of the square, the Ludwigskirche, and the castle as a
  unified ensemble — a miniature Versailles for a minor German prince who
  happened to be very close to France.

The St Johanner Markt is the heart of Saarbrücken — a cobbled baroque square surrounded by low historic buildings that now house cafes, bars, and restaurants. On warm evenings the square fills with people at outdoor tables, and the atmosphere is more French than German: lingering, convivial, and unconcerned with getting anywhere quickly.

It is the most pleasant place in the city to sit and watch life go by. A weekly market operates on the square, selling fresh produce, cheese, and bread. In December it becomes a Christmas market. The square is also the starting point for exploring the rest of the historic city: the Ludwigskirche is a few minutes' walk to the west, and the castle is visible from the southern edge of the square.
