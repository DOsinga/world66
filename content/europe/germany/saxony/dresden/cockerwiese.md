The Cockerwiese — Cocker Meadow — is a patch of parkland near the Großer Garten that has no formal significance and looks like any other Dresden green space. Its importance is entirely in one night: 16 July 1988, when Joe Cocker played here to 160,000 people in a concert that became one of the defining events in the final chapter of East German history.

The GDR authorities had been allowing Western rock concerts through the mid-1980s as a way of managing youth discontent — a safety valve. Bob Dylan, Bruce Springsteen, and Bryan Adams all played in East Germany in the years leading up to 1989. The Cocker concert was the largest, drawing people from across Saxony and beyond. The atmosphere was described by those who were there as extraordinary — not just a concert but a collective experience of a world they could see but not reach.

The Berlin Wall fell sixteen months later. Many people who were at the concert consider it, in retrospect, the moment they understood that the system was ending.

The meadow today is unmarked except in local memory. Dresdners use it as a shortcut and a place to walk dogs. The name has stuck informally, passed on from the generation that was there. It is about ten minutes on foot from the southern entrance of the Großer Garten.
