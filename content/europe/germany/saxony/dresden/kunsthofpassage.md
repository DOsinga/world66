The Kunsthofpassage is a series of connected courtyards in Dresden's Äußere Neustadt — the alternative quarter north of the Elbe — where local artists transformed a row of neglected 19th-century apartment blocks into one of the most inventive public art spaces in eastern Germany. Each courtyard has a different theme and was designed by a different group of artists.

The most famous is the Hof der Elemente (Court of the Elements), whose back wall is covered in an intricate arrangement of funnels, spouts, and drainpipes that channel rainwater into a musical system — different pipe diameters produce different tones, and when it rains the whole wall plays. Locals come here specifically when the weather is bad.

The other courtyards are worth exploring too: there's one covered in animal-themed tilework, another with mythological creatures, and a quieter one with a metalwork fountain. Small independent cafes, boutiques, and studios have opened in the ground floors of the surrounding buildings. The whole passage runs between Görlitzer Straße and Alaunstraße — you can enter from either end.

The Neustadt is most interesting in the evening, when the bars and restaurants along Louisenstraße and Alaunstraße fill up. The Kunsthofpassage is a good reason to come during the day; the neighbourhood rewards a longer walk.
