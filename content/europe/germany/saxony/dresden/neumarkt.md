The Neumarkt is the historic market square at the heart of Dresden's old town, and its reconstruction over the past two decades is one of the most ambitious urban rebuilding projects in Germany. The focal point is the Frauenkirche, which dominates the square from the south, but the square itself — ringed by restored Baroque and Neoclassical facades — tells a story about how Dresden has chosen to remember and rebuild.

After 1945, the Neumarkt was a field of rubble and then, under the GDR, a grey open space surrounded by reconstructed socialist-era housing. Since reunification, the city has systematically rebuilt the surrounding blocks according to their pre-war appearance, using the same sandstone and the same proportions, though the interiors are modern. The effect is striking: a coherent urban square that reads as historic but is almost entirely new.

Archaeologists worked through each demolition before reconstruction began and found layers going back to the medieval market. Finds are displayed in the Stadtmuseum nearby.

The square is lively on market days and in the evenings when the outdoor cafes fill up. It is the best place in Dresden to sit and watch the Frauenkirche change colour as the light shifts through the day — the pale sandstone goes from cream to amber to pink in the late afternoon.
