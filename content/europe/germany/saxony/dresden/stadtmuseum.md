The Dresden City Museum occupies the Landhaus, an eighteenth-century administrative building on Landhausstrasse, and covers the full arc of Dresden's history from its medieval origins through the GDR period and reunification. For visitors who have spent time with the Baroque art and the reconstructed palaces, the Stadtmuseum provides essential context: where did all this wealth come from, who lived here, and what happened to the city across the twentieth century?

The GDR section is particularly strong — daily life in East Dresden is documented through objects, photographs, and oral histories in a way that avoids both nostalgia and demonisation. The collection also covers the 1945 bombing and its aftermath, including the drawn-out political dispute over casualty numbers that became a Cold War propaganda battleground. Admission is cheaper than the major state museums, and the building itself — with its elegant courtyard — is worth seeing. Less crowded than the Altstadt's prestige institutions.
