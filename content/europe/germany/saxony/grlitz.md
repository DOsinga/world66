Görlitz is Germany's easternmost city, sitting right on the Polish border — the Neisse River separates it from its Polish twin, Zgorzelec. What makes Görlitz extraordinary is that it survived the Second World War almost entirely intact. The result is a town with over 4,000 listed monuments spanning Gothic, Renaissance, Baroque, and Art Nouveau, all in remarkable condition.

Hollywood has noticed: the [Art Nouveau department store](/europe/germany/saxony/grlitz/gorlitz_department_store) doubled as the lobby of Wes Anderson's Grand Budapest Hotel, and the town has served as a backdrop for various other productions. But Görlitz is worth visiting on its own merits. The [Untermarkt](/europe/germany/saxony/grlitz/untermarkt) and [Obermarkt](/europe/germany/saxony/grlitz/obermarkt) squares are beautiful, the [Heiliges Grab](/europe/germany/saxony/grlitz/heiliges_grab) is a unique medieval replica of the Jerusalem Church of the Holy Sepulchre, and the [Görlitz Synagogue](/europe/germany/saxony/grlitz/gorlitz_synagogue) — one of the few in Germany to survive Kristallnacht — has been beautifully restored.

The [Church of Saints Peter and Paul](/europe/germany/saxony/grlitz/st_peter_and_paul) contains one of Germany's finest Baroque organs, the [Senckenberg Museum of Natural History](/europe/germany/saxony/grlitz/senckenberg_museum) occupies a handsome Old Town building, and the [walk down to the Neisse](/europe/germany/saxony/grlitz/neisse_river_walk) and across into Poland is a pleasure. The [Görlitz Zoo](/europe/germany/saxony/grlitz/gorlitz_zoo) specialises in European wildlife including wisent (European bison). The general atmosphere is of a place that time forgot in the best possible way.
