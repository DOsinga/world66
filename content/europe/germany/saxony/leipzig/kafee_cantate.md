The Zum Arabischen Coffe Baum (At the Arabian Coffee Tree) has been serving drinks since 1694, making it one of the oldest coffee houses in Europe. The building's entrance is topped by a carved relief of an Arab offering coffee to a European cherub — the original sign carved when coffee was still an exotic Turkish import. Over the centuries the café hosted a notable roster of regulars: Schumann composed at the tables here, Wagner discussed opera plans, Liszt stopped in during his Leipzig period.

The coffee house now contains a small museum on its upper floors tracing the history of coffee in Saxony, with original furniture, cups, and equipment from different periods. The ground floor and cellar operate as a restaurant and bar, serving coffee and Saxon food. It is the right place to have a coffee after visiting the nearby Thomaskirche and Bach Museum.
