  Bernardo Bellotto was a Venetian painter and the nephew of the more famous
  Giovanni Antonio Canal — the original Canaletto. Bellotto was highly skilled
  and knew it, and throughout his career he signed his own works "Canaletto,"
  deliberately trading on his uncle's name. This caused centuries of confusion
  among collectors and art historians. His Pirna paintings were long
  mis-attributed or wrongly dated as a result. It was only careful archival
  research in the 19th and 20th centuries that untangled which "Canaletto" made
  which painting. Bellotto spent time at the Dresden court of Augustus III in
  the 1750s; his views of Pirna and the Elbe valley were commissioned as part
  of that engagement, and are considered among the finest topographical

The Stadtmuseum Pirna is housed in the former Dominican monastery, a 15th-century building in the old town a short walk from the market square. It holds the collection of paintings by Bernardo Bellotto known informally as the Canaletto Museum — a series of extraordinarily detailed views of Pirna and the Elbe valley made in the 1750s. Bellotto painted the market square, the church, the Elbe waterfront, and the surrounding landscape with the precision of a surveyor. His paintings are some of the most accurate pre-photographic records of any German town, and historians have used them to reconstruct the appearance of buildings destroyed in subsequent centuries.

The museum also covers Pirna's broader history — the Elbe sandstone trade that built much of Dresden, the town's role in the Reformation, and the darker 20th-century chapter at Sonnenstein. But it is the Bellotto paintings that most visitors come for. They are hung in a dedicated room and can be compared with photographs of the same views taken from the same angles today; remarkably little has changed in 270 years. The museum is small enough to see comfortably in an hour.
