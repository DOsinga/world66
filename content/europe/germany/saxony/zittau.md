Zittau sits in Saxony's far southeast, close enough to Poland and the Czech Republic that the borderland feeling is part of the visit. It was once a wealthy Upper Lusatian trading town, and the old centre still has enough churches, squares, fountains, and civic buildings to make a good day on foot.

The reason Zittau stands out from other small Saxon towns is its pair of medieval Lenten cloths. The larger one, displayed in the [Holy Cross Church Museum](/europe/germany/saxony/zittau/holy_cross_church_museum), is a rare survival of the cloths once hung before church altars during Lent. Add the [St Johannis Church](/europe/germany/saxony/zittau/st_johannis_church) tower, the old salt house and market square, and the town has a compact but real cultural core.

Zittau is also the starting point for the [Zittau Narrow-Gauge Railway](/europe/germany/saxony/zittau/zittau_narrow_gauge_railway), a steam line into the Zittau Mountains and the villages of Oybin and Jonsdorf. That makes it more than a museum town: come for the old streets in the morning, then ride into the hills in the afternoon.
