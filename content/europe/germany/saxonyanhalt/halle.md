Halle an der Saale is Saxony-Anhalt's largest city, built on salt. For over a millennium, "white gold" mined from the surrounding brine springs made this one of the wealthiest towns in central Germany, and that prosperity left its mark in grand churches, merchant houses, and a skyline dominated by five famous towers. Stand on the [Marktplatz](/europe/germany/saxonyanhalt/halle/marktplatz) and you see them all at once: the twin Hausmannstürme and twin blue towers of the [Marktkirche Unser Lieben Frauen](/europe/germany/saxonyanhalt/halle/marktkirche), and the free-standing [red tower](/europe/germany/saxonyanhalt/halle/roter_turm) beside them — one of the most striking cityscapes in eastern Germany.

The city sits on the river Saale, a major tributary of the Elbe, and has a compact, walkable old town centred on the market square. It's a university city with a lively student scene, and birthplace of the composer George Frideric Handel, whose [house](/europe/germany/saxonyanhalt/halle/handel_haus) is now a museum. The [Moritzburg](/europe/germany/saxonyanhalt/halle/moritzburg), with its modernist roof over a medieval shell, holds the regional art collection, while the [State Museum of Prehistory](/europe/germany/saxonyanhalt/halle/landesmuseum_vorgeschichte) houses the Nebra Sky Disc — the oldest known depiction of the cosmos. Despite being largely overlooked by international tourism, Halle rewards those who make the detour: the old town is well-preserved, the restaurants are unpretentious and good value, and the pace is refreshingly unhurried.
