story: >
  Friedrich Gottlieb Klopstock was born in Quedlinburg in 1724 and became one of the most celebrated German writers of the 18th century. His epic poem Der Messias, begun in 1748, was a deliberate attempt to do for German what Milton's Paradise Lost had done for English — to show that the language could carry serious epic verse. It made him famous across Europe. Goethe, Schiller, and Herder all acknowledged his influence. The birthplace museum is modest but places Klopstock in the literary context he earned.
---

The museum occupies the half-timbered house where Klopstock was born, which is fitting given that his work helped define a sense of German cultural identity at a moment when that identity was still being invented. The exhibits cover his life, his correspondence with major figures of the German Enlightenment, and the reception of his work.

Klopstock is not well-known outside Germany today, and even within it he is more respected than read. But the museum makes a reasonable case for his importance. It is also a good excuse to enter one of the old timber-frame houses and see what they look like inside. The rooms are small, low-ceilinged, and surprisingly atmospheric.
