story: >
  Quedlinburg Abbey was founded in 936 by Queen Matilda as a memorial institution after the death of her husband Henry I. It became one of the most powerful religious houses in the Holy Roman Empire, with its abbesses holding the rank of imperial princess. The abbey was suppressed during the Reformation in 1539 and secularised, but the Schlossberg complex — church, palace, and grounds — survived largely intact and is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
---

The Stiftsberg is the sandstone bluff on which Quedlinburg was founded, and the abbey complex spread across its summit is what the town exists to serve. Walking up from the lower town, through the gate and into the cobbled courtyard, you feel the shift in atmosphere immediately — quieter, higher, with views over the rooftiles stretching toward the flat Harz foothills.

The complex includes the Collegiate Church, the Romanesque palace remains, a small but worthwhile museum in the former abbey buildings, and gardens along the southern edge of the hill. It takes a good half-day to see the whole thing properly. The UNESCO designation covers both the Stiftsberg and the old town below, recognising Quedlinburg as one of the best-preserved medieval townscapes in Central Europe.
