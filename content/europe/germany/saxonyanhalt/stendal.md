Stendal is the main town of the Altmark, the quiet northern part of Saxony-Anhalt where brick Gothic churches and old Hanseatic towns sit between the Elbe and the flat North German plain. It is not a grand German showpiece, but it has enough real substance for a slow day: two surviving medieval gates, a handsome market square, serious churches, and a museum devoted to Johann Joachim Winckelmann, the Stendal-born scholar who helped shape European Neoclassicism.

The old town is compact. Start at the [Uenglinger Gate](/europe/germany/saxonyanhalt/stendal/uenglinger_gate), walk toward St. Mary's Church and the market square, then continue south to [St. Nicholas' Cathedral](/europe/germany/saxonyanhalt/stendal/st_nicholas_cathedral). The best part of Stendal is how much of its medieval status still reads in brick: tall gables, defensive gates, and churches built for a town that once belonged to the Hanseatic League despite sitting far inland.

Stendal is strongest as an Altmark stop rather than a destination for a whole trip. If you are already moving between Magdeburg, Wittenberge, or the Elbe towns, it is a worthwhile day of old-town walking and northern German history.
