Schleswig-Holstein is Germany's northernmost state, pressed between Denmark to the north, the North Sea to the west, and the Baltic Sea to the east. It is a flat, windswept land of fields, heathland, thatched farmhouses, and long coastlines. The landscape is not dramatic, but the light is extraordinary — the big skies and double coastline give it a quality that has drawn painters since the 19th century.

[Lübeck](/europe/germany/schleswigholstein/lubeck) is the most visited city, a medieval Hanseatic trading city whose old town, packed with red-brick Gothic churches and patrician houses, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. [Kiel](/europe/germany/schleswigholstein/kiel) is the state capital and home to Germany's main naval base and the annual Kieler Woche sailing regatta, one of the largest in the world. [Flensburg](/europe/germany/schleswigholstein/flensburg) sits right on the Danish border and has a distinctly hybrid character, with a strong rum-trading history and a cheerful harbour quarter. [Schleswig](/europe/germany/schleswigholstein/schleswig), the ancient capital, is quieter and worth a visit for its cathedral and the Viking ship collection at Schloss Gottorf.

The North Frisian Islands — [Sylt](/europe/germany/schleswigholstein/sylt), Föhr, Amrum, and others — lie off the North Sea coast, connected by causeway or ferry. Sylt in particular has become fashionable as a German beach resort, with expensive restaurants and wide tidal beaches. The island has a distinct flavour: sand dunes, beach clubs, and Frisian thatched houses in almost equal measure.
