story: >
  The sheltered bay here was the scene of an unexpected naval ambush on 5 April 1849. During the First Schleswig War, the Danish warships Christian VIII and Gefion sailed into the bay expecting little resistance. Schleswig-Holstein shore batteries opened fire, and within hours the Gefion had struck her colours and the Christian VIII had caught fire and exploded in a catastrophe that killed over 200 Danish sailors. The battle made Eckernförde briefly famous across Europe.

The main beach stretches for about three kilometres along the western shore of Eckernförder Bucht, the sheltered bay that gives the town its mild summer character. The water is calm and relatively shallow, warming up reliably in July and August — for the Baltic, it can feel almost Mediterranean. Rows of Strandkörbe, the traditional wicker beach chairs with retractable hoods, are rented out in summer and occupied from morning to evening.

The beach is backed by a tree-lined promenade popular with walkers and cyclists, and the transition from town to shore is unusually easy — no main road to cross, just the promenade path. At the northern end, the beach becomes quieter and the dunes are lightly wooded. Families with small children tend to cluster at the central section, where the water is shallowest and the lifeguard post is most visible.
