Heligoland is Germany's odd island out: a tiny offshore archipelago far out in the North Sea, with red sandstone cliffs, car-free lanes, duty-free shops, and a history that jumps between Denmark, Britain, Germany, and the Second World War.

The island is small enough for a day trip, but it does not feel like a simple beach resort. Walk the cliff path to [Lange Anna](/europe/germany/schleswigholstein/heligoland/lange_anna), watch gannets and guillemots at the [Lummenfelsen](/europe/germany/schleswigholstein/heligoland/lummenfelsen), and take the small boat across to [Düne](/europe/germany/schleswigholstein/heligoland/dune) for sand, clear water, and seals.

Heligoland's military history gives the scenery a harder edge. Bunkers, bomb craters, and museum displays recall the island's years as a fortress and the enormous postwar British explosion that failed to erase it. Stay overnight if you can; once the day boats leave, the cliffs and harbour become much quieter.
