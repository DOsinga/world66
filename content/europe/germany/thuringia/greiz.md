Greiz calls itself the Pearl of the Vogtland, and for a small Thuringian town it has some substance behind the claim. The [Sommerpalais](/europe/germany/thuringia/greiz/sommerpalais), set in the [Fürstlich Greizer Park](/europe/germany/thuringia/greiz/fuerstlich_park) along the White Elster river, is considered the finest late-Baroque palace in Thuringia and houses a notable collection of satirical art and caricatures — one of the largest in Germany.

Above the town, the [Oberes Schloss](/europe/germany/thuringia/greiz/oberes_schloss) perches on a rocky spur with views over the valley and the surrounding forests. The lower town has a quiet, well-kept centre with a handful of cafes around the market square and several streets of [Jugendstil houses](/europe/germany/thuringia/greiz/jugendstil_streets). Greiz was the capital of the tiny principality of Reuss Elder Line until 1918, and you can still sense that this was once a place that considered itself rather important.

It is not a destination in its own right, but if you are passing through the Vogtland or exploring eastern Thuringia, Greiz rewards a half-day stop.
