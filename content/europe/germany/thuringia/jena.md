Jena occupies a dramatic position in the valley of the Saale river, where limestone cliffs and wooded hillsides press close on both sides of the water. The city has the feel of a place shaped by ideas as much as by industry, and this is historically accurate: the Friedrich Schiller University, founded in 1558, attracted a concentration of philosophical and literary talent in the 1790s and 1800s that remains almost unparalleled in German cultural history. Schiller himself lived and wrote here, Goethe visited frequently from nearby [Weimar](/europe/germany/thuringia/weimar), Hegel wrote the Phenomenology of Spirit in the city, and Fichte and Schlegel held chairs here simultaneously.

The Carl Zeiss optical works, founded here in 1846, gave the city a second identity as a centre of precision optics and scientific instrumentation. The [Zeiss Planetarium](/europe/germany/thuringia/jena/zeiss_planetarium), built in 1926, was the world's first public planetarium and is still operational. The [Deutsches Optisches Museum](/europe/germany/thuringia/jena/optical_museum) traces eight centuries of instrument-making with an outstanding Carl Zeiss collection. The [Botanischer Garten](/europe/germany/thuringia/jena/botanischer_garten), attached to the university, is one of the oldest university botanical gardens in Germany — Goethe used it as a laboratory for his plant metamorphosis theory. The [Paradies park](/europe/germany/thuringia/jena/paradies_park) along the Saale provides the city's main green lung, and the river walks are excellent in spring when the limestone cliffs turn white with cherry blossom. The [Jenzig](/europe/germany/thuringia/jena/jenzig) — the steep hill above the eastern bank — provides a view over the valley that was painted extensively by Romantic artists; the traditional approach crosses the [Camsdorf Bridge](/europe/germany/thuringia/jena/camsdorf_bridge) and ends at the [Grüne Tanne](/europe/germany/thuringia/jena/grune_tanne) inn.

The student population makes Jena feel younger and more animated than its Thuringian neighbours, and the city centre around the Marktplatz has a lively bar and café culture larger than you'd expect from a city of its size. The [Friedrich Schiller University](/europe/germany/thuringia/jena/university_of_jena) buildings and the [Johannistor](/europe/germany/thuringia/jena/johannistor) — the only surviving medieval city gate — anchor the central pedestrian area. [Weimar](/europe/germany/thuringia/weimar) is thirty kilometres west, making the two cities a natural double visit for anyone following the Thuringian cultural route.
