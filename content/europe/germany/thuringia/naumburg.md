Naumburg an der Saale is an old cathedral city on the fast rail line between [Leipzig](/europe/germany/saxony/leipzig) and [Weimar](/europe/germany/thuringia/weimar), reachable from both in under an hour. Rather neglected for decades, the 30,000-inhabitant city has made giant steps in restoring its historic buildings since reunification.

The old town, set on heights overlooking the Saale valley, is dominated by the [Naumburg Cathedral](/europe/germany/thuringia/naumburg/naumburg_cathedral) — a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2018 that shows medieval German architecture and sculpture at its best. The west choir is world-famous for its lifelike donor portraits by the anonymous "Master of Naumburg," particularly the figures of Uta and Ekkehard, and Hermann and Reglindis. The figures have a psychological realism that nothing else in medieval Europe quite matches.

The [Marktplatz](/europe/germany/thuringia/naumburg/marktplatz) is handsome and lived-in, lined with restored gabled houses and the late-Gothic town hall. [Stadtkirche St Wenzel](/europe/germany/thuringia/naumburg/stadtkirche_st_wenzel) on the market square holds a Bach-approved organ and a Cranach altarpiece. The [Nietzsche-Haus](/europe/germany/thuringia/naumburg/nietzsche_haus) commemorates the philosopher's formative years here. Naumburg also makes a good base for exploring the [Saale-Unstrut wine region](/europe/germany/thuringia/naumburg/saale_unstrut_wine), Germany's most northerly, with terraced vineyards along two river valleys.
