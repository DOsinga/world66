Sonneberg sits on the southern edge of [Thuringia](/europe/germany/thuringia), close to the Bavarian border, and built its reputation on toys. For generations this was one of Europe's great toy-making towns, a place of dolls, model railways, wooden figures and export catalogues. The nickname "world toy city" sounds inflated until you step into the [German Toy Museum](/europe/germany/thuringia/sonneberg/german_toy_museum) and realise how industrial, skilled and international the trade became.

The town itself is modest and workaday, with more interest in its story than in its skyline. Around the station and centre, the [Sonneberg Play Mile](/europe/germany/thuringia/sonneberg/sonneberg_play_mile) links toy-themed stops and public art, while [Juttaplatz](/europe/germany/thuringia/sonneberg/juttaplatz) gives the old toy quarter a small focal point. Up on the hills, the [Sonneberg Observatory](/europe/germany/thuringia/sonneberg/sonneberg_observatory) adds a completely different note.

Sonneberg is best as a half-day or overnight stop for travellers who like industrial history, family museums, or the lesser-known edges of the Thuringian Forest. It will not compete with Weimar for beauty, but it has a niche and knows it.
