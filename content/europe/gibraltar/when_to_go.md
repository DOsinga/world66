---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

Gibraltar's Mediterranean climate makes it a year-round destination, but the timing of your visit makes a real difference.

**Spring (April–June)** is arguably the best time. Temperatures are warm (20–25°C), the crowds are manageable, and the Rock is at its most scenic. The Calentita food festival in June is a good reason to visit in early summer.

**Summer (July–August)** is hot and busy. Day-trippers pour in from the Costa del Sol and cruise ships dock almost daily. July and August temperatures regularly hit 30°C. If you're planning to explore the Upper Rock on foot, go early in the morning. The **Gibraltar Fair** at the end of August is a week-long fairground in the Alameda area — rides, food stalls, and live entertainment, more local than tourist.

**Autumn (September–October)** is the best time to visit. **National Day (10 September)** is the most important date in the Gibraltarian calendar — the entire territory turns out in red and white, marching from the frontier to Casemates Square, with music, food stalls, and an enormous balloon release. The atmosphere is genuinely celebratory; book accommodation well in advance. The **Gibraltar International Jazz Festival** in October draws international and local artists across multiple venues. Temperatures drop to a comfortable 20–25°C and crowds thin out after the summer.

**Winter (November–March)** is quiet and mild — rarely below 13°C, and snow essentially unheard of. Rainfall picks up, particularly December through February, and some tourist operations run on reduced schedules. That said, a winter visit has a charm of its own, with short queues and a chance to see everyday Gibraltar without the summer rush.

One weather note: the Levanter wind pushes a distinctive cloud cap over the top of the Rock, visible from Spain. It doesn't affect the town much but can reduce the views from the Upper Rock.
