Amorgos is the easternmost island of the Cyclades, a long narrow ridge of rock and scrub rising steeply from the Aegean. It is quieter and less developed than the big-name Greek islands, and it has stayed that way partly because it takes a while to get here — ferries from [Naxos](/europe/greece/naxos) or Piraeus are not quick.

The island became famous as the setting for Luc Besson's *Le Grand Bleu* (The Big Blue), and the deep blue water that attracted the filmmaker is still the main draw. The [monastery of Panagia Hozoviotissa](/europe/greece/amorgos/hozoviotissa_monastery), a white slash of a building clinging to a 300-metre cliff above the sea, is one of the most dramatic sights in the Cyclades. Inside it is tiny — just a few dark, incense-scented rooms — but the setting is unforgettable.

[Chora](/europe/greece/amorgos/chora_amorgos), the main village, is a classic Cycladic hilltop settlement: whitewashed houses, winding lanes, a handful of tavernas where you eat on terraces overlooking the sea. The south coast has the best swimming at [Agia Anna beach](/europe/greece/amorgos/agia_anna_beach), directly below the monastery. [Hiking trails](/europe/greece/amorgos/hiking_trail) connect the villages across the island's spine, and the walking is excellent if you can handle the heat. Most visitors arrive by ferry at [Katapola](/europe/greece/amorgos/katapola), the main port on the west coast.
