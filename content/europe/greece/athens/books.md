---
title: "Books"
type: section
---

Athens has produced more memorable books than almost any other city in Europe, and a few are essential reading for a visitor.

**Henry Miller, *The Colossus of Maroussi* (1941)** — Miller's account of his pre-war months in Greece is the great modern foreigner's book about the country. The "Colossus" is the poet George Katsimbalis, but Athens emerges as the real protagonist: the heat, the light, the cafés of pre-war Plaka.

**Patrick Leigh Fermor, *Mani* (1958) and *Roumeli* (1966)** — Fermor's two travel books on Greece are loosely about the rural Peloponnese and central Greece, but the Athens of the 1950s drifts through both — a city of refugees and ouzo cafés on the edge of modernity.

**Apostolos Doxiadis, *Uncle Petros and Goldbach's Conjecture* (1992)** — a short modern novel set in Athens about mathematics, family, and obsession. The Athenian background is sharply observed.

**Petros Markaris, the Inspector Costas Charitos detective novels** — Markaris's series of crime novels is the best fictional guide to contemporary Athens, particularly the books written before and during the financial crisis of the 2010s. **Deadline in Athens** is the easiest place to start.

**Eleni Sikelianos, *The Book of Jon* (2004)** — for a Greek-American voice in the city.
