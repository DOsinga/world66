Fiskardo sits on the northernmost tip of Cephalonia, curled around a horseshoe bay. It's the island's most beautiful village, and the only settlement that came through the 1953 earthquake largely intact — its limestone bedrock absorbed the shock that destroyed everywhere else. The old stone buildings that line the [Venetian harbour](/europe/greece/cephalonia/fiskardo/venetian_harbour) have been carefully restored and converted into taverns, cafes, boutiques, and small hotels.

In summer the quay fills with yachts; August brings serious money and serious crowds. The rest of the year it's quieter and more itself. From here you can arrange [boat trips across to Ithaca](/europe/greece/cephalonia/fiskardo/boat_trip_ithaca), visible across the strait. A coastal path leads north from the harbour to the [Norman lighthouse ruins](/europe/greece/cephalonia/fiskardo/norman_lighthouse) — the village itself is named after Robert Guiscard, the Norman duke who died here in 1085. The small [Byzantine Museum](/europe/greece/cephalonia/fiskardo/byzantine_museum) near the harbour is worth an hour for its ceramics and shipwreck finds. For food, [Tassia](/europe/greece/cephalonia/fiskardo/tassia) is the most established name on the waterfront, serving good fish and the local Robola white wine.
