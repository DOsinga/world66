Corfu Town, or Kerkyra, is the handsome capital of [Corfu](/europe/greece/corfu) and the island's strongest argument for leaving the beach. Four centuries of Venetian rule shaped the old town more deeply than almost anywhere else in Greece: tall ochre houses, laundry-strung lanes, arched passageways, little squares, and fortifications built to keep the Ottomans out of the Adriatic.

The old town sits between the [Old Fortress](/europe/greece/corfu/corfu_town/old_fortress) on its eastern promontory and the [New Fortress](/europe/greece/corfu/corfu_town/new_fortress) above the port. Between them is the [Campiello](/europe/greece/corfu/corfu_town/old_town), a compact maze that feels closer to Venice than to the Cyclades. The [Liston and Esplanade](/europe/greece/corfu/corfu_town/liston_esplanade) add a French and British layer: arcaded cafes, a cricket ground, and the kind of public square that makes no sense until you remember how many powers ruled here.

Corfu Town is not just pretty. The [Church of Saint Spyridon](/europe/greece/corfu/corfu_town/church_of_st_spyridon) is the island's devotional heart, and the [Museum of Asian Art](/europe/greece/corfu/corfu_town/museum_of_asian_art) is one of the more unexpected museums in Greece, housed in a British neoclassical palace on the Esplanade. South of the centre, [Mon Repos](/europe/greece/corfu/corfu_town/mon_repos) gives a quieter walk through royal, British, and ancient Corcyra.

It is easy to see Corfu Town in a long day, especially if you are staying elsewhere on the island. Stay overnight if you can: the cruise crowds thin, the lanes cool down, and the town becomes a place to wander rather than a checklist.
