The New Fortress of Corfu stands on a high hill overlooking the new port and the western approach to Corfu Town. Less visited than the Old Fortress, it is in many ways more interesting: the Venetian defensive architecture is better preserved, the underground passages and cisterns can be explored, and the view from the summit takes in the whole of the town and the harbour. The Venetian winged lion carved above the main gate is one of the finest examples on the island.

Inside the walls, the British added barracks and administrative buildings during their protectorate (1814–1864) — you can still see the British garrison's infrastructure overlaid on the Venetian fortifications. The fortress is well-signposted from the new port. A small café operates inside during summer.
