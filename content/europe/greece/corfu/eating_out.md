---
title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

Corfiot cuisine differs noticeably from mainland Greek food, shaped by four centuries of Venetian rule and the island's unusual position on medieval trade routes. The characteristic dishes are slow-cooked meat stews: **sofrito** (veal braised in white wine with garlic and parsley), **pastitsada** (rooster or beef with a spiced tomato sauce on pasta, originally a Venetian dish), **bourdeto** (fish stewed with red pepper and onion), and **stifado** (beef with pearl onions). Olive oil production is also more important on Corfu than most Greek islands — the olive groves are extensive and the oil is good.

For traditional Corfiot cooking, stick to the older restaurants in the Campiello district and away from the Esplanade's tourist-facing cafés. **Hrysomallis** (Nikiforou Theotoki 6) is the most consistent option for classic dishes. The market street Nikiforou Theotoki has good produce shops for self-catering.
