The Museum of Asian Art occupies the Palace of Saint Michael and Saint George, a neoclassical building on the north side of the Esplanade built by the British in 1819 as the residence of the Lord High Commissioner. The museum houses one of the most important collections of Asian art in Europe: over 15,000 objects from China, Japan, Korea, Cambodia, India, Tibet, Nepal, and Thailand.

The quality of individual pieces is remarkable — Tang dynasty pottery, Song porcelain, Japanese lacquerware, Tibetan thangkas, Indian bronzes. The palace itself is worth seeing: the entrance hall with its classical colonnade, the decorated state rooms, and the views over the Esplanade from the upper floors. The combination of a neoclassical British colonial building with a world-class Asian art collection is very Corfu.
