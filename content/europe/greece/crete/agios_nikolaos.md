Agios Nikolaos is the capital of eastern [Crete](/europe/greece/crete)'s Lassithi region, a pleasant town of about 10,000 people built around a small lake and a Venetian harbour. It became Crete's first real tourist resort in the early 1970s and was once known for noisy nightlife, but has since settled into a more relaxed, upmarket identity. The recent addition of a large marina has added sailing regattas to the summer calendar.

The town takes its name from the [small Byzantine Church of Saint Nicholas](/europe/greece/crete/agios_nikolaos/church_of_saint_nicholas) on a peninsula near the old harbour. Built between the 7th and 9th centuries during the Iconoclastic period, it retains some of its original frescoes and is open to visitors year-round.

[Lake Voulismeni](/europe/greece/crete/agios_nikolaos/lake_voulismeni) is the focal point — legend says Athena once bathed here. Today it is ringed with tavernas and cafes, and brightly painted fishing caiques bob around the edges. The [Archaeological Museum](/europe/greece/crete/agios_nikolaos/archaeological_museum) holds fine Minoan material from sites across eastern Crete, including the remarkable Goddess of Myrtos.

The surrounding coastline has more Blue Flag beaches than any other area in Greece. [Almyros](/europe/greece/crete/agios_nikolaos/almyros_beach) is a long sandy stretch just north of town, while smaller rocky coves to the south offer solitude. The clear water is excellent for snorkelling. The ancient Dorian city of [Lato](/europe/greece/crete/agios_nikolaos/lato), 6 km to the west, makes an excellent short excursion with spectacular views over the Mirabello Bay.
