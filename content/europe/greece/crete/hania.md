Hania (Chania) is widely considered the most beautiful city in Crete, and it's hard to argue otherwise. The old town is a near-perfect example of Venetian colonial architecture, with an entire [Jewish quarter](/europe/greece/crete/hania/evraiki), a Turkish quarter, and Venetian mansions all compressed into a labyrinth of narrow streets around an old [harbour](/europe/greece/crete/hania/venetian_harbour). The harbour itself — with its lighthouse, Venetian shipyards, and the old mosque converted to a market — is where everyone ends up in the evenings.

The [Agora municipal market](/europe/greece/crete/hania/agora_municipal_market), built in 1911 in the shape of a cross, is the best place to stock up on Cretan olive oil, honey, and herbs. The [Archaeological Museum](/europe/greece/crete/hania/archaeological_museum) has a good collection of Minoan artefacts from western Crete. The [Maritime Museum](/europe/greece/crete/hania/maritime_museum) at the Firkas fortress at the entrance to the harbour covers Cretan and Greek naval history. The [Etz Hayyim Synagogue](/europe/greece/crete/hania/etz_hayyim_synagogue) in the old Jewish quarter is one of the most historically significant sites in western Crete.

Hania is also an excellent base for western Crete. The [Samaria Gorge](/europe/greece/crete/hania/samaria_gorge) — a 16-km hike through Europe's longest limestone gorge, from the Omalos plateau down to the Libyan Sea — is one of the great walks in Greece, open from May to early October. The village of [Paleochora](/europe/greece/crete/hania/paleochora) on the southwestern coast and the beaches of the [Akrotiri peninsula](/europe/greece/crete/hania/akrotiri) are both easily reached by car or bus.
