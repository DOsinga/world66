The Sanctuary of Demeter and Persephone is one of the most important religious sites of the ancient Greek world. For nearly two thousand years — from Mycenaean times until the sanctuary was sacked by Alaric and his Visigoths in 396 CE — pilgrims came here to be initiated into the Eleusinian Mysteries, secret rites that promised initiates a blessed afterlife and a different relationship to death and rebirth.

The site is compact but layered. You enter through the outer court and pass through the Greater Propylaea, a monumental Roman-era gateway modeled on the Propylaea on the Athenian Acropolis. Beyond it, the inner sanctuary opens up, with the foundations of temples, altars, and processional ways visible at various levels of excavation. At the center stands the ruined Telesterion, the vast initiation hall where the Mysteries culminated.

The contrast with the surroundings is striking. Refineries and container yards press in on all sides. Far from diminishing the experience, the industrial backdrop throws the antiquity of the ruins into sharp relief — this place was old before the industrial age was imaginable.

The site is a strong candidate for UNESCO World Heritage status. An entrance fee covers both the archaeological site and the museum.
