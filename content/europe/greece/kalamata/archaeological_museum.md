The Archaeological Museum of Messenia is housed in a purpose-built modern building on the north side of the city and covers the region's history from prehistoric times through the Byzantine period. The permanent collection is well organized and includes significant finds from excavations across Messenia, including Mycenaean gold, Roman mosaics, and Byzantine sculpture.

The Mycenaean section is the highlight — objects from the area around Nestor's Palace at Pylos demonstrate the wealth and sophistication of the Bronze Age civilization that once dominated this corner of Greece. There are also exhibits on the ancient city of Messene (not to be confused with Messenia, the region), which has some of the best-preserved ancient walls in Greece and is about 25 km north of Kalamata.

Opening hours vary seasonally; closed Mondays. Admission is modest. The museum is an essential stop before visiting any of the ancient sites in the region.
