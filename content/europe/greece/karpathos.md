Karpathos is the second-largest of the Dodecanese islands, lying midway between [Rhodes](/europe/greece/rhodes) and [Crete](/europe/greece/crete) in the southeastern Aegean. It is mountainous, rugged, and far less developed than its famous neighbours — which is exactly its appeal.

The island splits into two distinct halves. The southern end around [Pigadia](/europe/greece/karpathos/pigadia) (the capital and port) has most of the tourist infrastructure, good beaches at [Afiartis](/europe/greece/karpathos/afiartis_windsurfing) and [Kyra Panagia](/europe/greece/karpathos/kyra_panagia_beach), and the dramatic [Apella beach](/europe/greece/karpathos/apella_beach) wedged beneath cliffs on the east coast. The northern end is wild and roadless, dominated by the village of [Olympos](/europe/greece/karpathos/olympos_village) — a mountaintop settlement where women still wear traditional embroidered dress and the local dialect preserves ancient Doric Greek forms. Getting to Olympos requires a boat or a rough mountain track, and the isolation is part of the experience.

Karpathos has strong winds, especially in summer, making it one of the best windsurfing spots in Greece. [Afiartis bay](/europe/greece/karpathos/afiartis_windsurfing) is the main launch point. Ferries connect the island to Rhodes and Piraeus, and there is a small airport with seasonal flights.
