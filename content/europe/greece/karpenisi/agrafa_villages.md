  The Agrafa, whose name literally means "the unwritten" or "the unregistered," earned the name during Ottoman rule because the villages were so remote and inaccessible that Ottoman census-takers never reached them. The area was never properly taxed or administered, and the Agrafa klephts became legends of Greek resistance long before the 1821 uprising.

The Agrafa is the collective name for the wild mountain range northwest of Karpenisi, a tangle of high ridges, deep valleys, and stone villages that remains one of the least visited parts of mainland Greece. The landscape is spectacular and largely unspoiled: beech and fir forests, rivers that run clear through limestone gorges, and villages where traditional stone architecture has survived because there was never enough wealth or ambition to replace it.

Driving into the Agrafa from Karpenisi is an adventure in itself — roads narrow to single tracks, hairpin bends give way to cliff-edge traverses, and the views open across a wilderness of ridges with no visible development. Villages like Agios Nikolaos, Granitsas, and Voutyro reward the drive with excellent tavernas serving local meat and cheese and the particular hospitality of people who know their guests have made an effort to arrive. Allow a full day and a vehicle with reasonable ground clearance.
