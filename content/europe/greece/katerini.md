Katerini is the unassuming capital of Pieria, a coastal plain in northern Greece roughly halfway between Thessaloniki and Mount Olympus. The town itself has around 60,000 people and little in the way of tourist sights — it is not a place people fly in for — but its setting is genuinely extraordinary: the snowy summits of **Mount Olympus**, the mythological home of Zeus and the Greek gods, rise 15 km to the west, and the long sandy beaches of the **Olympian Riviera** start just a few kilometres to the east.

That pairing of mountain and sea makes Katerini a useful base for three days of easy exploring. [Ancient Dion](/europe/greece/katerini/dion), the Macedonians' great sanctuary at the foot of Mount Olympus and the place from which Alexander the Great set out to conquer the Persian Empire, lies 17 km south — the [Archaeological Museum of Dion](/europe/greece/katerini/archaeological_museum_dion) next to the site holds the world's only hydraulis water organ found in archaeological context. The trailheads for the hike up [Mount Olympus](/europe/greece/katerini/mount_olympus) — an overnight summit via the Spilios Agapitos refuge is within reach of any reasonably fit walker — start at [Litochoro](/europe/greece/katerini/litochoro), another 15 minutes down the road. And in between, the beach villages of [Paralia](/europe/greece/katerini/paralia), [Olympiaki Akti](/europe/greece/katerini/olympiaki_akti) (Olympic Beach) and Korinos provide Blue Flag-certified sand, tavernas on the waterfront, and a cheap summer holiday atmosphere aimed mainly at Greek and Balkan visitors. Further south, [Platamon Castle](/europe/greece/katerini/platamon_castle) — a Crusader fortress where Olympus meets the sea — is one of the best-preserved medieval castles in northern Greece.
