Larissa is the capital of Thessaly and Greece's largest inland city, yet it barely registers on most travellers' itineraries. That is precisely its appeal. This is a working Greek city with a lively university population, excellent tavernas, and none of the tourist infrastructure — or prices — of the islands.

The city sits on the [Pineios River](/europe/greece/larissa/pineios_river_walk) with the bulk of Mount Olympus visible to the northeast. Two ancient theatres survive on [Frourio Hill](/europe/greece/larissa/frourio_hill) in the centre: the [First Ancient Theatre](/europe/greece/larissa/first_ancient_theatre), a large Hellenistic structure seating 10,000, and the [Second Ancient Theatre](/europe/greece/larissa/second_ancient_theatre), both free to visit and often empty. Climbing the hill also reveals the remains of the Ottoman city that once occupied Thessaly — the [Bayrakli Mosque](/europe/greece/larissa/bayrakli_mosque), the [Ottoman Baths](/europe/greece/larissa/ottoman_baths), and the ruins of the [Bedesten](/europe/greece/larissa/bedesten) covered market are all within a short walk.

The [Diachronic Museum of Larissa](/europe/greece/larissa/diachronic_museum) is the most complete archaeological museum in Thessaly, covering seven thousand years of settlement in the plain. The main square, [Plateia Laou](/europe/greece/larissa/plateia_laou), is ringed by cafes and comes alive in the evenings when half the city seems to be out for a stroll. For food, head to the side streets around the old Turkish bazaar area — the mezedes here are some of the best in central Greece, and prices are a fraction of what you would pay in Athens.

Larissa works well as a base for reaching [Meteora](/europe/greece/meteora), which is about an hour west by car or train. Mount Olympus and the [Pelion](/europe/greece/pilion) peninsula are both within easy striking distance.
