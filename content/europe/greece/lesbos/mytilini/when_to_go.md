---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

**Spring (April–May)** is excellent. The island is green, the wildflowers are out, and the birdwatching is at its peak — Lesbos lies on a major migration flyway and draws twitchers from across Europe, particularly for the spring passage of raptors and waders. The days are warm enough for cafe-sitting and sightseeing without the summer heat.

**Summer (June–August)** is hot, crowded, and expensive by Greek island standards. Temperatures regularly reach 35°C and the ferry traffic from Athens means Mytilini fills up in July and August. The compensations are a lively outdoor scene and long warm evenings. The Lesbos International Documentary Festival takes place in late July.

**Autumn (September–October)** rivals spring. The sea stays warm for swimming well into October, the olive harvest begins in October, crowds thin dramatically after mid-September, and prices drop. The landscape turns golden and the light is softer.

**Winter (November–March)** is mild by northern European standards but damp. The island gets proper rain in winter. Most tourist infrastructure closes, but the city itself carries on — the cafes fill up, the ferries run, and you'll experience Mytilini as a real Aegean town rather than a visitor destination.
