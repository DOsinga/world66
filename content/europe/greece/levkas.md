Lefkada (also known as Levkas) is an Ionian island connected to the Greek mainland by a [floating bridge](/europe/greece/levkas/floating_bridge) — making it one of the few Greek islands you can drive to. The west coast has some of the most spectacular beaches in all of Greece: [Porto Katsiki](/europe/greece/levkas/porto_katsiki), with its towering white cliffs; [Egremni](/europe/greece/levkas/egremni_beach), reached by hundreds of steep steps; and [Kathisma](/europe/greece/levkas/kathisma_beach), the most accessible and popular.

The island's capital, [Lefkada Town](/europe/greece/levkas/lefkada_town), was rebuilt after a 1948 earthquake in a distinctive style — the upper floors of many buildings are clad in corrugated metal painted in bright colours, a practical response to seismic risk that gives the town an oddly cheerful appearance. Just north of the town the long Venetian [Castle of Santa Maura](/europe/greece/levkas/santa_maura_castle) guards the lagoon. [Nydri](/europe/greece/levkas/nydri), on the sheltered east coast, is the main resort and the departure point for boat trips to the tiny islands of [Meganisi](/europe/greece/levkas/meganisi), Skorpios (once owned by Aristotle Onassis), and Madouri.

Inland, Lefkada is mountainous and surprisingly green for a Greek island. The hill villages of [Karya](/europe/greece/levkas/karya) and [Englouvi](/europe/greece/levkas/englouvi) are barely touched by tourism, and the local lentils from Englouvi are considered the best in Greece. At the southwestern tip, the dramatic white cliff of [Cape Doukato](/europe/greece/levkas/cape_doukato) gave the island its name and was, in classical legend, where the poet Sappho took her life.
