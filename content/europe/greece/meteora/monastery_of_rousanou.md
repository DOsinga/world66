The Monastery of Rousanou is the most dramatically positioned of all the Meteora monasteries: the entire structure sits on a narrow rock pinnacle with walls falling sheer in every direction. It looks from certain angles like the rock itself has been transformed into a building. Founded in the 16th century, it was abandoned for long periods and restored as a convent in 1988. It is now home to a small community of Greek Orthodox nuns.

The monastery church contains beautiful 16th-century frescoes depicting scenes from the life of Christ and the lives of the martyrs. Rousanou is one of the most photographed of the Meteora monasteries because of its extraordinary setting — the standard postcard view shows it surrounded by rocky pinnacles with the sky above and the plain far below. A bridge connects it to an adjacent rock, from which the approach stairs begin.
