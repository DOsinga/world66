Milos is a volcanic island in the Cyclades, famous for its extraordinary variety of beach landscapes — coloured rock formations, sea caves, white sand coves, and the extraordinary lunar-looking coastline around [Sarakiniko](/europe/greece/milos/sarakiniko), where wind-eroded white pumice looks like a moonscape dropped into the Aegean. The [Venus de Milo](/europe/greece/milos/venus_de_milo_site), now in the Louvre, was found here in 1820.

The island's volcanic origins mean the landscape is far more dramatic than most Cycladic islands: multi-coloured cliffs, hot springs, and a deeply indented harbour at Adamas. The village of [Plaka](/europe/greece/milos/plaka) perches above the harbour with sweeping views and good tavernas. Milos is quieter than Mykonos or Santorini but has been getting busier — still, outside July and August you will find it without the crowds of its more famous neighbours.
