The Ancient Theatre of Milos is cut into the hillside near Trypiti, using the natural slope of the volcanic rock to support the seating tiers in the typical Hellenistic fashion. Dating to the Hellenistic period with later Roman modifications, the theatre is in good enough condition that the basic form is immediately legible — the curved orchestra, the remains of the stage building, and the arc of stone seating rising up the hill. It was here, near this theatre, that the Venus de Milo was unearthed in the agricultural fields just below.

What makes the theatre exceptional is not its archaeological completeness but its setting. From the upper rows the view extends over the bay of Milos, the harbour at Adamas, and on clear days across to the island of Kimolos floating in the middle distance — the same view that audiences would have had two thousand years ago. The site is freely accessible and rarely crowded even in peak season. It is most rewarding in the early morning or late afternoon, when the light is low and the stone takes on a warm colour against the deep blue of the sea below.
