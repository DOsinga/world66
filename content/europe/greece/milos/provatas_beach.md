Provatas is the most accessible of the south coast beaches and the one best suited to a relaxed, uncomplicated day at the sea. The beach is wide and sandy, the water shelves gently — making it good for children — and the surrounding cliffs provide natural shelter from the meltemi winds that can make the northern shores choppy in July and August. There are sunbeds for hire, a beach bar, and a taverna nearby, which means you can arrive empty-handed and spend the day comfortably.

The trade-off for this accessibility is that Provatas gets noticeably busier than the harder-to-reach beaches. In the height of summer the car park fills by mid-morning and the sunbed lines extend across most of the usable sand. Even so, it is considerably less crowded than the equivalent beach on a more tourist-saturated island. The beach is reached by a surfaced road from the main circuit, branching south from near the village of Zefyria. The cliffs above the beach are made of the same coloured volcanic rock that appears throughout the southern coast of Milos — ochre, red, and grey-green in the afternoon light.
