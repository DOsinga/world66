The Archaeological Museum occupies a beautiful eighteenth-century Venetian arsenal on the north side of Syntagma Square. It is a small museum but a focused one: the collection covers the prehistoric and Mycenaean periods of the Argolid, and most of what you see was excavated within an hour's drive of Nafplio.

The standout exhibit is the late Bronze Age Dendra panoply — a complete suit of Mycenaean bronze armour, roughly 3,500 years old, almost identical to the kind described by Homer. Other highlights include Neolithic pottery from the Franchthi Cave, Mycenaean frescoes from Tiryns, and a striking Geometric clay mask. Allow an hour to ninety minutes; it pairs well with a visit to Mycenae or Tiryns the same day.
