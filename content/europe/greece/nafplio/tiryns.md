Tiryns is a Mycenaean acropolis four kilometres north of Nafplio on the road to Argos. It is much smaller and less spectacular than [Mycenae](/europe/greece/mycenae), but it has the most impressive surviving cyclopean walls anywhere in Greece — courses of unworked limestone blocks so massive they look prehistoric even now.

The site became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1999 (jointly with Mycenae). The highlight is the corbelled gallery on the south side of the acropolis, a tunnel of cyclopean stone built into the wall thickness with no mortar at all. The site is small — an hour is enough — and quiet compared to Mycenae. The Nafplio–Argos buses stop on the highway opposite the entrance.
