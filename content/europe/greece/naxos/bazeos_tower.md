Bazeos Tower is a 17th-century fortified manor house standing alone on a plain in the centre of Naxos, about 15 kilometres from Hora on the road to Filoti. It is one of the best-preserved Venetian-era pyrgoi on the island and one of the most unusual: where most tower houses are embedded in villages, Bazeos sits in open agricultural land, a compact defensive complex of house, tower, chapel, and outbuildings that once served as a monastery and later passed to the Bazeos family, who still own it.

The building is beautifully proportioned in the way that Cycladic Venetian architecture tends to be — austere from a distance, with considerable refinement up close in the carved stonework around windows and doorways and in the small chapel at the complex's heart. The interior, when open, shows how Naxian merchant-landowners actually lived: cool rooms with thick walls, wooden beamed ceilings, and furniture that is both practical and quietly elegant.

What makes Bazeos particularly worth visiting in summer is the cultural programme. The Bazeos Tower Festival runs through July and August, bringing theatre productions, art exhibitions, and concerts to the courtyard and outbuildings. The setting is exceptional: performances at night in the illuminated courtyard, with the plain stretching dark in every direction, achieve an atmosphere that no purpose-built venue could replicate. Check the festival programme before your visit; events are typically listed on the tower's website and posted in Hora.

Outside the festival season, the tower can be visited on a self-guided basis. The drive there on the inland road takes you through the Tragea plain, passing olive groves and the occasional Byzantine chapel, which is itself a reason to make the trip.
