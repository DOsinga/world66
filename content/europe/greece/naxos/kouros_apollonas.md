The Kouros of Apollonas lies in an ancient marble quarry in the hills above the village of Apollonas on Naxos's northern coast. It is the largest of the island's several abandoned kouros statues, measuring 10.7 metres from feet to crown — taller than a three-storey building — and still attached at its back to the living rock where it was being carved around 600 BC. It is one of the most remarkable archaeological sights in Greece and almost entirely uncrowded.

The site sits above the main road; a short path leads up from a small car park near the village. The quarry setting gives the statue a context that museum pieces lack: you can see the cut marks in the surrounding stone, the rough faces where earlier blocks were extracted, the precise line where the sculptors stopped working. The figure is bearded — most kouros are clean-shaven youth — which has led some scholars to identify it as Dionysus rather than Apollo, since Apollonas was a centre of Dionysian cult. Others argue it represents a divine figure, possibly from the sanctuary that stood nearby. The question remains open.

The drive to Apollonas from Hora takes about an hour on the road through the island's interior, passing through Filoti and Koronos before descending to the north coast. The village of Apollonas itself is a working fishing village with a small beach and a couple of tavernas — a good place for lunch before or after the quarry visit. The road is serviceable but narrow and winding over the mountain section; take it slowly. The kouros is freely accessible and lit for evening visits if you time your drive to arrive at dusk.
