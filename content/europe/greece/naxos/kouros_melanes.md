The Melanes kouros — or more precisely the two related kouros statues in the Flerio area near Melanes village — are the most accessible of Naxos's abandoned ancient sculptures and, in some ways, the most evocative. Where the Apollonas kouros lies in an obvious quarry setting, the Melanes figures rest in a small garden of olive trees and ferns, tended by a local family, with water running nearby from an ancient spring. The setting is deeply peaceful and slightly unreal: enormous ancient marble men lying under gnarled olive trees, birds singing, a stream threading past.

The larger of the two figures is around 5.5 metres and dates to approximately 600–580 BC. It lies on its back, its face turned slightly to one side, with the finely carved curls of its archaic hair still sharp around the crown. The smaller figure nearby is incomplete and partially buried. Both show the formal conventions of the kouros type — arms at sides, left foot advanced, stiff frontality — but close up the carving has a delicacy that photographs don't convey. The knees, the musculature of the torso, the elaborate treatment of the hair: this was skilled work by a master sculptor, abandoned suddenly for reasons that, as with all the Naxian kouros, remain unclear.

The site is reached via a signed path from the village of Melanes, a short drive or manageable walk from Hora's outskirts. A local family tends the garden and has done so for generations; there is a small honesty donation box. The informality of the site is part of its charm — no ticket booth, no guided tour, just the statues in their olive grove, available to anyone who makes the short detour. Visit in the morning before the sun is fully overhead, when the light in the garden is still cool and dappled.
