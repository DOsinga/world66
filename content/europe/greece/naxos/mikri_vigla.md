Mikri Vigla is where the wind is a feature rather than a nuisance. The beach sits on a slightly exposed section of the western coast that catches the summer meltemi reliably from late morning, producing consistent conditions that attract windsurfers and kitesurfers from across Europe. Several surf schools and equipment rental operations are based here, and on a good August afternoon the bay is full of coloured sails and kites in a way that's impressive to watch even from a beach towel.

The beach itself divides naturally into two sections around a rocky headland: the northern bay faces northwest into the full force of the wind, which is what the surfers want, while the southern cove is more sheltered and calmer for ordinary swimming. Both have fine sand. The northern section gets crowded with equipment; the southern cove is quieter. If you're not surfing, go south — the water is clear and the beach less chaotic.

For those who do windsurf or kitesurf, Mikri Vigla is one of the better spots in the Mediterranean. The meltemi runs July through August with unusual reliability here, generating flat-water conditions on some days and waves on others depending on the swell direction. Equipment rental and lessons are available from the beach schools, which cater to all levels. Enquire about lessons well ahead in peak season as the schools fill their beginner slots quickly.

The drive from Hora takes about 25 minutes. There is a small beach bar and a couple of tavernas at the northern end.
