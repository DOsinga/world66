  The central gallery of the museum is arranged to reproduce the exact viewing
  angle of the Temple of Zeus pediments as they would have appeared to a
  worshipper standing before the temple. The east pediment shows the moment
  before the chariot race begins — athletes and horses frozen in terrible
  anticipation — while the west pediment captures the savage chaos of the
  Centauromachy at its peak. The sculptors achieved this by making every
  figure a psychologically precise portrait rather than a type.

One of the most important archaeological museums in Greece, holding the finest collection of material from the sanctuary. The centrepiece is the set of sculptures from the Temple of Zeus: the two pediment groups and the twelve metopes showing the Labours of Hercules. These are among the earliest masterpieces of Classical Greek sculpture, carved around 460 BC, and seeing them in person rather than in reproduction is a different experience entirely.

The museum also contains the Nike of Paionios (a winged victory originally mounted on a tall triangular column outside the temple), the Hermes of Praxiteles (one of very few indisputably original works by a named ancient Greek sculptor), a large collection of bronze votive offerings, and the iron scraper and clay jug believed to have belonged to the workshop of Pheidias himself. Allow at least two hours.
