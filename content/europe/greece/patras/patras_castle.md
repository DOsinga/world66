Patras Castle commands the hill above the city and earns the climb. The 6th-century Byzantine fortification was built by Justinian on an ancient acropolis site and subsequently remodelled by every power that occupied Patras — Frankish crusaders, Venetians, and Ottomans all left their mark on the walls and towers. The resulting structure is a layered history of western Greece's medieval occupation, readable in the shifting styles of stonework.

Inside the walls, the castle grounds are large and surprisingly green, with good shaded areas for walking. The main reason to climb, beyond the architectural interest, is the view: the Gulf of Patras spreads out below, with the Ionian islands visible on a clear day and the Rio-Antirrio suspension bridge stretching across the narrows to the north. The Roman odeon is visible on the slope below. Entry is free, hours are generous, and the castle is rarely crowded even in summer. The walk up from the city centre takes about 20 minutes and passes through the old upper town.
