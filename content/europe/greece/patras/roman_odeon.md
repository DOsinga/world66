The Roman Odeon of Patras is one of the best-preserved small Roman theatres in Greece, sitting on the southern slope of the castle hill in a position that gives it a natural backdrop of medieval walls above and the city spreading below. Built in the 2nd century AD, the semi-circular cavea was originally roofed — an odeon is a covered theatre, built for musical performances and recitals rather than open-air drama — though the roof is long gone and performances today take place under the sky.

The stone seating is original Roman work, solidly restored, and the theatre holds around 2,200 people comfortably. During the Patras International Festival (typically July and August) it hosts performances of ancient Greek drama, classical music concerts, and dance that make the historical setting feel entirely natural. Outside the festival season the site is open to visitors during daylight hours, and walking down through the stone seats to the stage is one of the more transporting experiences in Patras. The combination of Roman, Byzantine, and medieval layers visible on the hillside above makes the castle-to-odeon walk one of the best itineraries the city offers.
