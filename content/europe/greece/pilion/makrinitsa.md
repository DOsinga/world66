Makrinitsa sits at 600 metres on the northwestern shoulder of the Pelion range, 6 kilometres from Volos, and the view from its main square justifies the climb entirely: the city below, the Pagasitic Gulf, and on clear days the mountains of central Greece beyond. Greeks call it the balcony of Pelion, and the description is exact.

The village is one of the best-preserved traditional settlements on the peninsula. The cobbled lanes are narrow, the houses are the classic Pelion style — thick stone walls, slate roofs, painted wooden balconies — and the square has a working fountain and a massive old plane tree. The Museum of Folk Art and History of Pelion, housed in the Topali Mansion, holds over 1,500 objects documenting local crafts, clothing, and daily life from the 18th and 19th centuries.
