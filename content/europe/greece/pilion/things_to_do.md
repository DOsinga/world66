title: "Things to Do"
type: section
---

Walking the old kalderimi mule paths between villages is the best way to experience Pelion — many are well-marked and pass through orchards and dense forest. The peninsula has two personalities: the western shore facing the calm Pagasitic Gulf is the more accessible side, with the classic villages of [Makrinitsa](/europe/greece/pilion/makrinitsa) and [Portaria](/europe/greece/pilion/portaria) close to Volos; the eastern Aegean coast is wilder, with dramatic clifftop views, pebble beaches accessible by foot, and the more remote villages of [Tsagarada](/europe/greece/pilion/tsagarada) and [Damouchari](/europe/greece/pilion/damouchari).

The historic [Pelion Railway](/europe/greece/pilion/pelion_railway) runs from Ano Lechonia on the coast up through chestnut forest to the village of Milies on weekends. For beaches, [Agios Ioannis](/europe/greece/pilion/agios_ioannis_beach) is the most developed option on the eastern coast; [Mylopotamos](/europe/greece/pilion/mylopotamos_beach) is more beautiful and accessible only on foot.
