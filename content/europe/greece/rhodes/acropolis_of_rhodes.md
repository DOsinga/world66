The Acropolis of Rhodes sits on Monte Smith, a hill about three kilometres southwest of the city centre named after the British admiral Sir Sidney Smith, who used it as an observation post during the Napoleonic Wars. The ancient city of Rhodes spread across this hillside, and the remains include a partially reconstructed Temple of Apollo Pythios, a restored Hellenistic stadium, and a small odeon (covered theatre) used today for concerts.

The setting rewards the walk up regardless of the ruins. The hill gives a sweeping view over modern Rhodes, the medieval Old Town, and on clear days across to the Turkish coast. The site is open and unwalled, and free to enter. Go in the late afternoon when the light is soft and the worst of the heat has passed — the western slopes catch the last sun beautifully. The Temple of Apollo columns are floodlit at night and visible from much of the city.
