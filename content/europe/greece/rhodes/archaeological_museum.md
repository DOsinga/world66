The Archaeological Museum occupies the former Hospital of the Knights, a handsome Gothic building completed in 1489 around a large central courtyard. The collection covers the prehistoric, ancient, and early medieval periods on Rhodes and the surrounding Dodecanese islands, and it is one of the strongest regional collections in Greece.

The highlights are in the sculpture galleries. Two marble Aphrodites from the third and second centuries BC dominate the collection — the larger Aphrodite of Rhodes, depicted crouching and wringing water from her hair, is among the finest Hellenistic sculptures in Greece. There are also good Mycenaean finds from the eastern part of the island, a substantial collection of archaic and classical grave stelae, and a room of Roman-era portrait heads. The medieval exhibition covering the Knights period is thorough. Closed Mondays.
