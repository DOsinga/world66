The walls enclosing the Old Town of Rhodes are among the best-preserved medieval fortifications in Europe. Running for four kilometres around the entire Old Town, they average ten metres in height and up to twelve metres in thickness — the bulk designed not to withstand scaling but to absorb cannon fire. The outer face is a continuous embankment of cut stone; behind it runs a dry moat fifteen metres wide with a glacis on the far side.

The most rewarding way to see the walls is to walk the top, which is possible on a guided tour departing from the Palace of the Grand Master on Tuesday and Saturday mornings. From the battlements you see the full geometry of the fortifications — the D'Amboise Gate to the west, the Tower of Spain at the southwest corner, and the long eastern curtain wall facing the harbour. From ground level, the moat walk (accessible from the Amboise Gate) gives a different perspective on the sheer mass of the stonework.
