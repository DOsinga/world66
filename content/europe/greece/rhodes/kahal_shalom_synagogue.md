The Kahal Shalom Synagogue in the Juderia (the old Jewish quarter) is the oldest synagogue in Greece and one of the oldest in Europe, founded in 1577. The building has been in continuous use ever since, though today it functions primarily as a memorial and heritage site. The interior preserves its original Sephardic layout with a central bimah and women's gallery above, and the blue and white tiles are original.

The Juderia itself is worth exploring beyond the synagogue. The neighbourhood occupies the southeastern corner of the Old Town, its streets narrower and quieter than the tourist corridor along Sokratous Street. Plaques mark former Jewish homes and businesses, and the Jewish Museum of Rhodes — housed in a medieval building around the corner — gives a full account of the community's history on the island from the Byzantine period through the Holocaust.
