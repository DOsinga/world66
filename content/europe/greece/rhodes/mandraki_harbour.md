Mandraki is the oldest and most picturesque of the three harbours of Rhodes City, used continuously since ancient times. The mole is lined with windmills — three remain of the original fourteen that once stood there, used to grind grain unloaded from ships. The tower of St. Nicholas stands at the far end of the mole, and the bronze deer atop columns at the harbour entrance mark the traditional (if unverifiable) site of the Colossus.

The harbourfront is a good place to be in the morning, when the excursion boats are loading for day trips to Lindos and the small islands, and again in the evening when the light catches the fortifications and the windmills. The waterfront buildings behind the harbour include the Governor's Palace and the new market, both built by the Italians in the early twentieth century in a Venetian Gothic style that looks oddly appropriate beside the medieval walls. The new market building houses a small bazaar and a cafe worth stopping at for coffee with a view of the harbour.
