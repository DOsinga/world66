The Old Town of Rhodes is a functioning neighbourhood — roughly 6,000 people live inside the walls — that happens to be one of the most intact medieval urban environments in Europe. UNESCO has listed it as a World Heritage Site since 1988, recognising a place that has been continuously inhabited for over two thousand years and that preserves visible layers of ancient, Byzantine, Latin, Ottoman, and modern Greek history within a single square kilometre.

The walls divide the interior into the Castello (the northern quarter where the Knights governed and lived) and the Chora (the larger southern area where Greeks, Jews, and merchants lived). The clearest way to understand the Old Town is to walk it systematically: start at the Palace of the Grand Master, follow the Street of the Knights down to the Archaeological Museum, then turn south into the Chora along any of the narrower streets toward the Juderia. You will pass mosques converted to churches converted back to mosques, Byzantine chapels with Ottoman minarets still attached, and Ottoman fountains in squares that were Byzantine plazas that were Greek agoras before them.
