Sokratous Street is the commercial spine of the Old Town, running east from the Palace of the Grand Master toward the harbour gates. During the medieval period it marked the rough boundary between the Latin Castello to the north and the Greek and Jewish Chora to the south. Today it is the main tourist bazaar, lined with jewellery shops, leather goods, ceramics, and the inevitable souvenir stalls, but the buildings are genuine medieval structures adapted over centuries of use.

The street is at its best at either end of the day. In the morning before ten, the deliveries are still arriving and the shopkeepers are setting up, and you can walk the length of it without the afternoon crowds. In the evenings the tourist shops close and the street takes on a different character — locals cutting through, the sound of conversations from windows above, cats claiming the steps. The Mehmet Agha Mosque at the eastern end dates from the Ottoman period and is one of seven mosques built in the Old Town after 1522; only a few remain standing, and none are in active religious use.
