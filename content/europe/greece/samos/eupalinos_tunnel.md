story: "Eupalinos of Megara dug his 1,036-metre aqueduct in the 530s BC, starting from both ends of the mountain simultaneously. The two teams met in the middle with an error of only a few centimetres — at a time when the Greeks had no compasses, no theodolites, and no working geometry of trigonometry. It is the second-known tunnel in history to use this method, and the first that we know was a deliberate engineering feat."
---

The tunnel runs under Mount Kastro for a kilometre, cut through limestone by hand in the sixth century BC to bring water from a spring on the north side of the mountain to the ancient capital on the south. It served the city for a thousand years and remained one of the great underground works of the ancient world.

Visitors can walk a long section of it on a wooden walkway, ducking under the rock ceiling and seeing the original aqueduct channel three metres below — an extraordinary thing to be inside. Tickets are limited; come early or book in advance during the high season. The tunnel is north of Pythagoreio and a short drive from the town.
