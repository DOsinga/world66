  The square was named after Admiral Andreas Miaoulis, the hero of the Greek War of Independence. Plateia Miaouli is the beating heart of Ermoupoli and one of the most elegant public squares in the Greek islands. Ringed with palm trees and flanked by marble-paved cafes, the square draws a mixed crowd of islanders and visitors every evening for the ritual of the volta — the slow, sociable promenade that Greeks have perfected over centuries.

The neoclassical town hall closes one end of the square, its imposing facade giving a sense of how seriously the merchants of 19th-century Syros took civic architecture. They were building a capital city, and they wanted it to look like one. Sit at one of the kafeneions under the palms with a frappe and watch the scene — children chasing pigeons, old men playing backgammon, the ferries moving silently across the harbour below.
