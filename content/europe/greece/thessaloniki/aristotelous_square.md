The main square of Thessaloniki, designed in the 1950s by Ernesto Hébrard as a formal open space linking the city to the waterfront. The surrounding arcaded buildings have a uniform neo-Byzantine style; the square itself has cafés spilling out of every ground floor. Thessaloniki's café culture is taken more seriously than almost anywhere in Greece — sitting here with a freddo espresso watching the city pass is an activity in itself. The seafront promenade stretches in both directions at the foot of the square.
