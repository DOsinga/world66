The Monastir Synagogue, tucked into a courtyard off Syngrou Street near the Modiano market, is the only surviving active synagogue in a city that once had 40. Built in 1927 by Jews who had emigrated from Monastir (now Bitola in North Macedonia), it survived the Nazi occupation when most of Thessaloniki's Jewish community did not.

Inside, the synagogue follows the Sephardic layout with the *bimah* (reading platform) in the centre and the ark at the far end. Visiting is possible but not always straightforward — check with the Jewish community of Thessaloniki for current access arrangements. The Jewish Museum nearby on Agiou Mina Street is an excellent complement, with detailed documentation of the city's Sephardic history and culture.
