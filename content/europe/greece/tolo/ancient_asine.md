story: >
  Asine is mentioned in Homer's catalogue of ships in the *Iliad* — one of the towns that sent men to Troy. Around 700 BC the Argives sacked the city for siding with Sparta and forbade its rebuilding. The site remained empty until the Venetians fortified it 2,500 years later. The Swedish archaeologist Axel Persson excavated here in the 1920s, attended in the last season by the young crown prince Gustaf VI Adolf — a king who dug his own trenches.
---

A short walk or quick drive east of the village brings you to a steep limestone headland with the layered remains of one of the oldest settlements in the Argolid. Mycenaean walls at the base, classical Greek courses above, and on top a Venetian fortress that incorporated the older stone.

It is not a manicured archaeological park — paths are rough, signage minimal, and you can essentially wander where you like. The views over the gulf and back to Tolo's beach are among the best in the area. Open during daylight, free entry.
