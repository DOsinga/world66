Agios Sostis is a small beachfront village on the south coast of Zakynthos, separated from its inland twin Lithakia by a kilometre of olive groves and a single road. Together they form one of the quieter parts of the Laganas Bay coastline — much smaller and less developed than [Laganas](/europe/greece/zakinthos/agiossostislithakia/laganas) proper a couple of kilometres east, with most of the action concentrated on the short stretch of seafront and the wooden bridge across to the bar-island of [Cameo](/europe/greece/zakinthos/agiossostislithakia/cameo_island).

The setting is the eastern edge of the [National Marine Park of Zakynthos](/europe/greece/zakinthos/agiossostislithakia/national_marine_park), the protected nesting area for loggerhead sea turtles in Laganas Bay. From the harbour at Agios Sostis, small boats run trips out to [Marathonisi](/europe/greece/zakinthos/agiossostislithakia/marathonisi) — Turtle Island — a tied uninhabited islet with the bay's main nesting beach. Swimming there is restricted to the cooler hours and away from the nesting strip; on most trips you will see at least one turtle in the water during the crossing.

Lithakia itself is a working inland village, with a few traditional tavernas, an old church and the ruins of a small Venetian-era olive press. Most visitors stay in Agios Sostis on the beach side; Lithakia is where you go for dinner away from the resort menus.

This is not somewhere you fly to Zakynthos for, but it is a calmer base than the louder resorts on the same island. Two or three days fits.
