Balassagyarmat sits on the Ipoly River, with Slovakia just across the water and the low hills of Nógrád around it. It is not grand Hungary, but it has a strong local grain: county-town architecture, Palóc folk culture, old churches, and a civic pride summed up in its nickname, Civitas Fortissima.

The best reason to stop is the cluster around the centre. The [Palóc Museum and Skansen](/europe/hungary/balassagyarmat/paloc_museum_and_skansen) gives the town its cultural anchor, with village buildings and exhibits on the folk life of Nógrád. Nearby, the [Civitas Fortissima Museum](/europe/hungary/balassagyarmat/civitas_fortissima_museum) explains the 1919 border fighting that still shapes the town's self-image.

Balassagyarmat also rewards a slow walk. The [Former County Hall](/europe/hungary/balassagyarmat/former_county_hall) sets the tone on the main square, while the [Balassagyarmat Prison](/europe/hungary/balassagyarmat/balassagyarmat_prison) is an unusually serious piece of 19th-century institutional architecture. Add the baroque Catholic church, the Lutheran church, and the old Jewish prayer house if you have time.
