Erzsébetváros — the seventh district — is Budapest's Jewish Quarter and the home of the ruin bar scene. Before the Second World War it was the heart of a substantial Jewish community; the Great Synagogue on Dohány Street, the largest in Europe, anchors the district's southern end. The neighbourhood suffered catastrophically during the war, and the vacant lots and crumbling courtyards left behind became the raw material for a new kind of venue when entrepreneurs started opening improvised bars in the ruins during the 2000s.

Szimpla Kert was the first and remains the most famous of the ruin bars, but the district now contains dozens of variations on the theme, from intimate wine bars to massive multi-room clubs like Instant-Fogas. The streets around Kazinczy utca are the centre of it all. By day the area is relatively quiet; by Friday and Saturday evening it is one of the most animated places in Central Europe. The Jewish heritage layer runs alongside the nightlife — the Dohány Street Synagogue, the Jewish Museum, and the Memorial of the Hungarian Jewish Martyrs are all within a few minutes' walk of Szimpla.
