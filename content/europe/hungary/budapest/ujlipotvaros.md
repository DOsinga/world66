Újlipótváros — "New Leopold Town", the northern half of District XIII — is one of Budapest's most quietly desirable neighbourhoods: leafy, modern, and resolutely local. Laid out in the early 20th century and filled in during the 1930s with elegant Bauhaus and modernist apartment blocks, it has none of the tourist churn of the inner city and all of the everyday texture that comes with it.

The spine is **Pozsonyi út**, a tree-lined street of independent boutiques, bookshops, and unhurried cafes where residents linger over coffee well into the afternoon. It opens onto **Szent István Park**, a green rectangle along the Danube embankment with views across to the Buda hills. From here the bridge to **Margaret Island** — the city's great car-free park — is a short walk north.

It is an easy neighbourhood to slow down in: come for an afternoon of browsing and people-watching rather than ticking off monuments. The riverside is especially good at golden hour.
