Eger is one of Hungary's most rewarding provincial cities, a handsome Baroque town in the foothills of the Mátra and Bükk mountains in northeastern Hungary. It is famous for two things: [Eger Castle](/europe/hungary/eger/eger_castle), where in 1552 a small Hungarian garrison held off a vastly larger Ottoman army in one of the most celebrated defensive stands in Hungarian history; and [Egri Bikavér](/europe/hungary/eger/bikaver_wine) — "Bull's Blood of Eger" — the robust red wine blended from Kékfrankos, Kadarka, and other local grapes.

The old town is compact and walkable, anchored by the monumental [Cathedral Basilica](/europe/hungary/eger/cathedral_basilica) and the [Archbishop's Lyceum](/europe/hungary/eger/archbishop_lyceum) — whose eighteenth-century library and working camera obscura are among the finest in Hungary. The [Eger Minaret](/europe/hungary/eger/eger_minaret), one of the northernmost surviving Ottoman minarets in Europe, rises incongruously from the Baroque streetscape. Thermal baths, and [Dobó Square](/europe/hungary/eger/dobó_square) lined with Baroque architecture round out the old town. The [Valley of the Beautiful Woman](/europe/hungary/eger/valley_of_beautiful_woman), a short walk from the centre, offers wine tasting directly from the barrel. Eger is two hours from Budapest by train and makes an excellent overnight trip.
