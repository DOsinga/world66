  The equestrian statue in the centre of the square depicts István Dobó, the captain who led the defence of Eger Castle in 1552. It was erected in 1907 during a wave of Hungarian nationalist sentiment at the end of the Austro-Hungarian period. The sculptors included bronze reliefs on the plinth depicting women of the castle throwing boiling water and wax onto the Ottoman attackers — one of the more vivid pieces of patriotic statuary in central Europe.

Dobó Square (Dobó István tér) is the heart of Eger's old town, a handsome cobbled square enclosed by Baroque buildings and centred on a bronze equestrian statue of the city's defender. The square has the unhurried, well-maintained air of a prosperous Hungarian provincial city — outdoor cafes, ice cream sellers in summer, a Christmas market in December — and the surrounding streets contain some of Eger's best Baroque architecture.

The Minorite Church on the north side of the square, built in the 1730s, has one of the finest Baroque facades in Hungary. From the square, the castle hill rises steeply to the east. Most of Eger's restaurants and cafes are within a short walk.
