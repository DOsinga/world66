Gödöllő is a small town about 30 kilometres northeast of Budapest, easily reached by suburban railway (HÉV) in around 40 minutes. Its main attraction is the [Royal Palace of Gödöllő](/europe/hungary/godollo/royal_palace), the largest Baroque palace in Hungary and the favourite country residence of Emperor Franz Joseph I and his wife, Empress Elisabeth — the beloved "Sisi" whose portrait is known across Central Europe. The palace dominates the town and draws the majority of its visitors.

Beyond the palace, Gödöllő is a pleasant small town with a distinct character. The 19th century brought it close connections with Hungary's cultural elite — writers, artists, and intellectuals settled here, and at the turn of the 20th century it hosted the Gödöllő Artists' Colony, a Hungarian Arts and Crafts movement that drew on the ideals of William Morris and produced some distinctive textile and graphic work. The town also has a significant agricultural university and a somewhat academic atmosphere that sets it apart from a typical tourist town.

The Queen Elisabeth Park adjacent to the palace is well maintained and makes for a pleasant walk. A short distance out of town, the pilgrimage church at [Máriabesnyő](/europe/hungary/godollo/mariabesnyő) is an interesting detour for those with more time — a Baroque pilgrimage church surrounded by monastic buildings in a wooded setting, still actively used by pilgrims and with a long local tradition.
