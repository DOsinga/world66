The most striking object in Széchenyi Square is this tall Baroque column, topped by a gilded ark and flanked by elaborate bronze figures of angels. Erected in 1731, it stands at the centre of the city's main pedestrian square and gives the square its distinctive character.

The column is in the tradition of the plague columns found across Central European Baroque cities — erected as thanksgivings after the city survived disease or disaster. This one is particularly elaborate and well-preserved. The surrounding square is pleasant for sitting, with café terraces and the long facades of the old Jesuit church and former Carmelite monastery filling the sides.
