  The cathedral's most venerated possession is the Weeping Madonna of Győr — an Irish painting of the Virgin Mary that, on St. Patrick's Day in 1697, was witnessed by the entire congregation weeping tears of blood. The bishop of Győr certified the miracle, and the painting has drawn pilgrims ever since. It now rests in a gilded altar in the Héderváry Chapel.
---

Győr's cathedral stands at the highest point of Káptalandomb (Chapter Hill), and the view from its steps over the confluence of the Rába and Rábca rivers is reason enough to climb up. The building itself is a mix of Romanesque, Gothic, and Baroque — the result of repeated construction, damage, and rebuilding over nine centuries.

The interior is grand without being overwhelming. The nave is long and cool, the altars are exuberantly Baroque, and the side chapels hold genuine treasures. The Héderváry Chapel in the north aisle contains the 15th-century reliquary bust of King Ladislaus I, one of the finest surviving medieval goldsmith works in Hungary. Mass is held regularly; the cathedral is open to visitors outside service times.
