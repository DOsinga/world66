The former Carmelite monastery occupies the south side of Széchenyi Square and is one of Győr's finest Baroque buildings. The church was built in the 1720s and its facade — all movement and drama in the Central European Baroque manner — is particularly good. The interior has an elaborate frescoed ceiling and gilded altarpieces.

The monastery complex behind the church now serves as a cultural centre and hosts concerts and events. The church itself is open for services and visits. It's worth stepping inside for the ceiling fresco alone, which fills the nave with a swirling scene of the Carmelite order in heaven — ambitious in scale and well-preserved despite the building's conversion over the years.
