Hollókő is a small village in the Cserhát hills of the Northern Uplands, about 100 km northeast of [Budapest](/europe/hungary/budapest), and one of the most carefully preserved examples of traditional rural Hungary. The Old Village — a single curving street of around sixty whitewashed houses with carved wooden balconies and a shingled wooden church — was the first village anywhere to be inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage list, in 1987, recognised not for any single monument but for the way the whole settlement preserves the vernacular architecture and land-use patterns of the Palóc people who have lived here for centuries.

Above the village stand the ruins of the 13th-century Hollókő Castle, built after the Mongol invasion and later contested between rival noble families. The climb up through the woods takes twenty minutes or so and rewards you with sweeping views over the wooded hills and the red roofs of the village below. The name means "raven stone," and local legend ties the castle's founding to ravens that carried stones to build its walls.

What makes Hollókő more than an open-air museum is that people still live here and the Palóc folk traditions are still practised. The village is busiest and most atmospheric at Easter, when residents dress in elaborately embroidered traditional costume for the festival, and you can watch demonstrations of weaving, pottery, and embroidery in the small craft houses. It is a longer day trip from Budapest than the Danube Bend towns and best reached by car, but if you want to see something of Hungary beyond the capital, it is well worth the drive. Come out of season or midweek to avoid the holiday crowds that can overwhelm so small a place.
