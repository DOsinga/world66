Miskolc is Hungary's third-largest city, tucked into the foothills of the Bükk Mountains in the northeast of the country. It is an industrial city that does not pretend to be a tourist magnet, but the surrounding area has genuine appeal — most notably Miskolctapolca, a suburb where you can swim through a natural cave system fed by thermal springs at the extraordinary [Cave Bath](/europe/hungary/miskolc/cave_bath).

The [Bükk National Park](/europe/hungary/miskolc/bukk_national_park) starts practically at the city's edge, with hiking trails through beech and oak forests, limestone karst formations, and lookout towers with views across the Northern Hungarian hills. The village of [Lillafüred](/europe/hungary/miskolc/lillafüred), accessible by narrow-gauge forest railway from the city, has a dramatic waterfall, two caves open for tours, and a grand lakeside hotel from the 1920s.

[Diósgyőr Castle](/europe/hungary/miskolc/diósgyőr_castle), on the western side of the city, is a 13th-century royal fortress with four round towers that now houses a medieval museum and hosts summer jousting tournaments. The city centre itself has an attractive pedestrian main street — [Széchenyi Street](/europe/hungary/miskolc/széchenyi_street) — lined with cafes and 19th-century buildings, the [Herman Ottó Museum](/europe/hungary/miskolc/otto_herman_museum) for Hungarian archaeology and natural history, and the [Avas Hill](/europe/hungary/miskolc/avas_lookout_tower) with its unusual wine cellars carved into the volcanic tuff.

Miskolc is also a convenient base for visiting the wine region of [Eger](/europe/hungary/eger) and the Tokaj vineyards to the east.
