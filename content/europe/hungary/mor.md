Mór is a small Hungarian wine town in the Fejér county foothills, about 70 km southwest of Budapest. It has been producing white wines — particularly from the Ezerjó grape — for centuries, and the local cellars (pincék) are the main reason to visit. The pace is slow and genuinely local; you'll encounter few tourists.

Wandering the streets leads you to the [pincék](/europe/hungary/mor/wine_cellar_row) scattered across the hillsides. Each cellar has its own character, and most welcome visitors for a tasting. A favourite experience is sipping wine inside a barrel room — some cellars have repurposed giant casks as seating. Climb to the top of the vineyarded hill for a view over the whole town. The baroque [Lamberg Castle](/europe/hungary/mor/lamberg_castle) anchors the small main square, and the wooded [park behind it](/europe/hungary/mor/lamberg_park) is the obvious place to recover between tastings.

The local cemetery is worth a visit: the care given to individual plots, with raised flower gardens at each one, says a lot about the town's character. North of town, an obelisk marks the [Battle of Mór](/europe/hungary/mor/battle_of_mor_memorial), where the Hungarian army was routed by Austrian forces in 1848.
