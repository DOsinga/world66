The Early Christian Necropolis of Pécs is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, comprising a group of 4th-century painted burial chambers that survive under the Cathedral Quarter of the city. The site was designated by UNESCO in 2000 as "an outstanding example of early Christian architecture" — the frescoed tombs are among the best-preserved of their kind in Central Europe and compare favourably with catacombs found in Rome itself.

The burial chambers are accessible through the Cella Septichora Visitor Centre below Cathedral Square. The exhibition displays several of the tomb chambers with their original painted decoration intact, including biblical scenes, decorative geometric patterns, and portrait busts. A separate complex, the Cella Trichora, preserves additional chambers a short walk away. Both are included in a combined ticket. The underground spaces are cool and well-lit, and the frescoes are clearly visible. The contrast between these ancient spaces and the cathedral directly above them is one of the most striking historical juxtapositions in Hungary.
