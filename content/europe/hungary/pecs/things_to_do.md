title: Things to Do
type: section
---

Pécs packs an unusual density of good sights into its compact old town. The Ottoman legacy is the most visually distinctive: the [Mosque of Pasha Qasim](/europe/hungary/pecs/mosque_of_pasha_qasim) on [Széchenyi Square](/europe/hungary/pecs/szechenyi_square) is the largest surviving Ottoman building in Hungary, and the [Pasha Yakovalı Hasan Mosque](/europe/hungary/pecs/pasha_yakovaly_hasan_mosque) south of the square is the only intact example with its minaret. Together they are unlike anything else in Central Europe.

The Cathedral Quarter above the square contains the [Pécs Cathedral](/europe/hungary/pecs/pecs_cathedral), the [Early Christian Mausoleum](/europe/hungary/pecs/early_christian_mausoleum) with its remarkable 4th-century frescoes (UNESCO World Heritage), and several museums clustered in the surrounding streets. The [Csontváry Museum](/europe/hungary/pecs/csontvary_museum) and the [Vasarely Museum](/europe/hungary/pecs/vasarely_museum) — showing two very different Hungarian artists born generations apart — are both essential stops for anyone interested in art. The [Zsolnay Cultural Quarter](/europe/hungary/pecs/zsolnay_cultural_quarter) occupies the famous porcelain factory complex on the edge of the centre.

For a view over the whole city and the plain stretching south toward Croatia, take the chairlift up to the [Pécs TV Tower](/europe/hungary/pecs/pecs_tv_tower) on the Mecsek ridge, with hiking trails through the forested hills beyond.
