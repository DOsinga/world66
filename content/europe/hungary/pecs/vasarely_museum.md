  Victor Vasarely was born Vásárhelyi Győző in Pécs in 1906 and emigrated to
  Paris in 1930. He spent the following decades developing what would become
  Op Art — abstract geometric works that create powerful illusions of movement
  and depth through carefully calculated patterns and colour contrasts. When
  the style became internationally famous in the 1960s, Vasarely donated works
  to his home city, and the museum opened in 1976 in a historic building he
  helped restore. He said his work belonged to Pécs as much as to Paris.

The Vasarely Museum occupies a Baroque building near the Cathedral Quarter and displays a substantial collection of works by Victor Vasarely, the founder of Op Art and one of the most internationally recognized Hungarian artists of the 20th century. The collection includes early graphic works, tapestries, and the geometric paintings that made him famous, displayed across several rooms of the restored building.

Vasarely's works are highly reproducible — prints and posters are ubiquitous — but seeing the originals reveals the precision and scale that separates the real thing from a copy. The illusions of depth and movement in the larger pieces are considerably more compelling at full size. The museum is located steps from the Csontváry Museum, and visiting both together offers a striking contrast: the visionary turbulence of Csontváry against the cool geometric precision of Vasarely, two very different modes of Hungarian modernism from the same city.
