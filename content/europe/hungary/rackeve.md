Ráckeve is a small town on the southern end of Csepel Island, about 40 kilometres south of [Budapest](/europe/hungary/budapest). It sits by a quieter branch of the Danube, with a modest centre, a Serb minority history, and enough architecture to make a good slow day from the capital.

The town's best sight is the [Our Lady Serbian Orthodox Church](/europe/hungary/rackeve/our_lady_serbian_orthodox_church), a rare Gothic Serbian Orthodox church with medieval frescoes and a Baroque bell tower. Ráckeve's other strong landmark is [Savoyai Castle](/europe/hungary/rackeve/savoyai_castle), an early Baroque palace by the Danube associated with Prince Eugene of Savoy. Between them are small squares, churches, galleries, and the reconstructed [water mill](/europe/hungary/rackeve/water_mill) on the riverbank.

Ráckeve is not a big-ticket Hungarian destination, and that is part of its use. Come for a half day if you are already in Budapest and want a small Danube town without the crowds of Szentendre or Visegrád. The HÉV suburban train makes it easy, though the trip is slower than the distance suggests.
