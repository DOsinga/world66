Sopron is a historic city in northwestern Hungary, just a few kilometres from the Austrian border. Its old town is remarkably intact — one of the best-preserved medieval city centres in Hungary — with a horseshoe of medieval and Baroque streets circling the old Roman settlement of Scarbantia. The [Firewatch Tower](/europe/hungary/sopron/firewatch_tower), rising from the Roman city gate, has become the symbol of the city.

Sopron's Austrian proximity has given it a particular character: the town voted in a 1921 referendum to remain part of Hungary rather than join Austria, earning it the title "Most Loyal Town." The medieval [Fő Tér](/europe/hungary/sopron/fo_ter) (Main Square), flanked by the [Goat Church](/europe/hungary/sopron/goat_church) and surrounded by Gothic and Baroque burgher houses, is the heart of any visit. The [Roman Remains of Scarbantia](/europe/hungary/sopron/roman_remains) are visible under the square itself. The cross-border shopping and wine trade have historically made Sopron prosperous. The surrounding hills produce excellent wines, particularly Kékfrankos (Blaufränkisch) red wines from the Sopron wine region. Nearby, [Fertő Lake](/europe/hungary/sopron/ferto_lake) — shared with Austria as the Neusiedlersee — is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of Central Europe's great birdwatching destinations. The medieval core is compact and easily explored on foot.
