Dóm Square is one of the largest and most impressive urban squares in Hungary. Laid out after the 1879 flood reconstruction, it is enclosed on three sides by the arcaded National Pantheon colonnade and anchored on the fourth by the twin towers of the Votive Church. The overall composition — symmetrical, monumental, intentional — feels more like an Italian piazza than a typical Central European city square.

In summer the square transforms into the venue for the Open-Air Festival, with a permanent stone stage against the church façade capable of holding large theatrical and operatic productions. Outside festival season it is the kind of square that rewards sitting on a bench: there is nearly always something to look at, the proportions are generous without being overwhelming, and the Dömötör Tower in one corner provides a reminder that not everything in Szeged was wiped out in 1879.
