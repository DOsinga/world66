story: >
  Pick Salami was founded in 1869 by Márk Pick, a Jewish entrepreneur who recognised that the combination of Szeged's cold, damp winters — ideal for curing — and the locally abundant paprika could produce a distinctive product. The salami became Hungary's first internationally marketed food export and is still produced to the original recipe. The curious phrasing on the label — "Winter Salami" — preserves the memory of those seasonal curing conditions even though modern production is no longer weather-dependent.
---

The Pick Salami and Szeged Paprika Museum occupies a building near the original Pick factory on the Tisza riverbank and tells the story of two of Hungary's most famous food exports from a city that claims ownership of both. The salami exhibits trace the production process from the nineteenth century to the present, including the distinctive red mould-ripening method that gives Pick salami its characteristic flavour and appearance. The paprika section covers the spice's history in the region, its role in Hungarian cuisine, and the development of the milder varieties that made it commercially viable in international markets.

The museum is compact and can be visited in under an hour. The adjacent factory shop sells the full range of Pick products, including vacuum-packed salami suitable for travelling and paprika in grades from sweet to genuinely hot. It is one of the more enjoyable food-focused museums in Hungary — well-curated, not too earnest, and staffed by people who can tell you which salami to buy.
