Székesfehérvár was the coronation city of medieval Hungary — for five centuries, kings were crowned and buried here. That history is still visible in the compact old town, where baroque façades line pedestrianised streets and the [ruins of the royal basilica](/europe/hungary/szekesfehervar/medieval_basilica_ruins) sit in an open-air archaeological garden right in the centre.

Today it is a mid-sized city about an hour southwest of [Budapest](/europe/hungary/budapest) by train, prosperous and well-kept but largely overlooked by tourists. That is part of its appeal. The main square, [Városház tér](/europe/hungary/szekesfehervar/varoshaz_ter), and the surrounding streets are handsome and walkable, with enough museums, churches, and café terraces to fill a good half-day. The [Hiemer House](/europe/hungary/szekesfehervar/hiemer_house) and the [Cathedral of Saint Stephen](/europe/hungary/szekesfehervar/st_stephen_cathedral) are worth seeking out, and on the eastern edge of town the eccentric [Bory Castle](/europe/hungary/szekesfehervar/bory_castle) is one of the strangest pieces of architecture in Hungary.

The city makes an easy day trip from Budapest, or a quiet overnight stop if you are heading toward [Lake Balaton](/europe/hungary/lakebalaton).
