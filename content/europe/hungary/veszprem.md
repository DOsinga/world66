Veszprém is one of the oldest towns in [Hungary](/europe/hungary), perched on a series of hills above the Séd valley, about 15 kilometres north of [Lake Balaton](/europe/hungary/balaton). The [Castle District](/europe/hungary/veszprem/castle_district) along the hilltop ridge is the main attraction — a single atmospheric street lined with baroque palaces, churches, and the foundations of a medieval royal palace. The views from the castle walls over the surrounding hills are excellent, with the best of them from the [Elizabeth Look-out](/europe/hungary/veszprem/erzsebet_lookout) at the end of the ridge.

The town was the seat of Hungary's first bishopric, and [St. Michael's Cathedral](/europe/hungary/veszprem/st_michaels_cathedral) and the [Bishop's Palace](/europe/hungary/veszprem/bishops_palace) still dominate the skyline. The [Saint Stephen Viaduct](/europe/hungary/veszprem/viaduct), a graceful multi-arched bridge spanning the valley, connects the old town to the modern centre below. Veszprém makes a good base for exploring the northern shore of Lake Balaton and the Bakony hills behind it — the Herend porcelain factory, one of Europe's finest, is just 15 minutes away.
