The headline day trip from Dalvík is [Grímsey](/europe/iceland/grimsey), Iceland's northernmost inhabited island and the only part of the country to sit on the Arctic Circle. The Sæfari ferry sails from Dalvík harbour three times a week, taking about three hours each way; some sailings allow a day return, while others mean an overnight in the island's guesthouse. The Arctic Circle monument is the obvious draw, but the seabird cliffs — packed with puffins, razorbills, and guillemots in summer — are the better reason to go.

Closer to hand, [Hrísey](/europe/iceland/hrisey) lies in the middle of Eyjafjörður, reached by a 15-minute ferry from Árskógssandur about 20 km south of Dalvík. It is a quiet, low island of heathland trails and tame ptarmigan, with a mild microclimate and fjord views toward the mountains. A few hours covers the marked walks.

Inland and to the northwest rises [Tröllaskagi](/europe/iceland/trollaskagi), the rugged peninsula whose mountain passes and coastal roads connect Dalvík to the fishing towns of Ólafsfjörður and Siglufjörður. Allow a full day to drive it, stopping for the old mountain viewpoints above the tunnels and, in Siglufjörður, the excellent Herring Era Museum.
