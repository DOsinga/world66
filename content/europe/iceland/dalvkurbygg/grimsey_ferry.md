  Grímsey is the only part of Iceland that lies on the Arctic Circle — a line that doesn't sit still but drifts northward by about 15 metres each year due to the Earth's axial tilt. The ferry from Dalvík takes about three hours each way, and arriving on the island means you are, by definition, north of the Arctic Circle for the duration of your visit. The island's 80-odd permanent residents are famously unimpressed by this geographical distinction.

The ferry to Grímsey departs from Dalvík three times a week and takes about three hours to reach the island. Grímsey is the northernmost inhabited island of Iceland, and its main claim is the Arctic Circle monument — a concrete globe on a post that gets moved each year to track the actual position of the circle.

The island is more interesting for its seabird colonies than its geographical quirk — the cliffs are packed with puffins, guillemots, and razorbills in summer. Day trips are possible on some sailings but check the schedule carefully. The crossing can be rough in bad weather. Dalvík ferry terminal.
