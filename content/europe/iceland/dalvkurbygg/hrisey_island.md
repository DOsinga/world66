  Hrísey was once the site of Iceland's largest shark-processing plant — Greenlandic shark, the slow-growing deep-sea fish that is fermented for months to make hákarl, the traditional Icelandic delicacy with a powerful ammonia smell. The processing plant is long gone, but the island's position in the middle of Eyjafjörður, sheltered from Atlantic storms, gives it an unusual mild microclimate compared to the mainland coast nearby.

Hrísey is a small island in the middle of Eyjafjörður, accessible by a short ferry crossing from Árskógssandur, about 20 km south of Dalvík. The island is known for its ptarmigan — there are reportedly more per square kilometre here than almost anywhere else in Iceland — and for its tranquility, which draws Icelanders as much as foreign visitors.

Walking trails cross the island's heathland and give views over the fjord and the mountains of the Tröllaskagi peninsula. The crossing from Árskógssandur takes about 15 minutes; ferries run frequently in summer.
