story: >
  Tröllaskagi — "Troll's Peninsula" — is the dramatic wedge of land between Skagafjörður and Eyjafjörður that contains some of the highest and most rugged mountains in northern Iceland. The mountain roads across the peninsula, including the Fljót and Skíðadalur valleys, pass through landscapes that were essentially inaccessible by road until the late 20th century. Several of the passes are only open in summer and require caution even then.
---

The Tröllaskagi peninsula is the reason to linger in Dalvík rather than pushing straight to Akureyri. The roads west and northwest from Dalvík wind through fjords and over mountain passes — the Ólafsfjörður tunnel opened in 1991, but the old mountain road above it still gives access to the kind of dramatic viewpoints that Iceland's tunnels now bypass.

The towns of Ólafsfjörður and Siglufjörður on the north coast of the peninsula are both worth visiting for their fishing heritage and, in Siglufjörður's case, an excellent herring era museum. Both are accessible from Dalvík by a combination of tunnel and coastal road.
