Holmavik is a small fishing village on the shores of Steingrímsfjörður in the Westfjords of Iceland, about 274 kilometres from Reykjavík via the Hvalfjörður tunnel. The village serves as the commercial hub for the surrounding Strandir coast — a remote and seldom-visited stretch of Iceland that sees far fewer travellers than Snæfellsnes or the main Westfjords towns.

The reason most people come here is the [Museum of Sorcery and Witchcraft](/europe/iceland/holmavik/museum_of_sorcery_and_witchcraft), one of Iceland’s most unusual and compelling small museums. It covers the 17th-century witch trials that gripped the Westfjords — Iceland burned 21 people for sorcery, and the Strandir coast was at the epicentre — and displays the grimoires, magical staves, and folk sorcery that ran through rural Icelandic life for centuries. A related exhibit, the Sorcerer’s Cottage, sits 27 kilometres north in Bjarnarfjörður.

Beyond the museum, Holmavik is a base for fishing, hiking, and experiencing the raw, unpopulated character of the Strandir coast. Trout and char fishing in nearby lakes and rivers is good in summer; the surrounding fjords offer sea cruises and whale watching. The road north along the Strandir coast toward Djúpavík is one of Iceland’s finest drives — increasingly rough, increasingly empty, increasingly beautiful.
