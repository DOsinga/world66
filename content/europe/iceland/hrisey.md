Hrísey sits in the middle of Eyjafjörður, sheltered from Atlantic storms by the mainland on either side, which gives it an unusually mild microclimate for this latitude. The island is reached by a short ferry from Árskógssandur, about 20 km south of Dalvík; the crossing takes around 15 minutes and runs frequently through the summer. It is a quiet, low-lying place — Iceland's second-largest island, but with only a small village clustered near the harbour.

The island was once home to Iceland's largest shark-processing plant, where slow-growing Greenlandic shark was fermented for months to make hákarl, the pungent, ammonia-heavy traditional delicacy. That industry is long gone, and what remains is tranquility. Hrísey is known for its ptarmigan — reportedly more per square kilometre here than almost anywhere else in Iceland — and the birds are tame enough to wander the village.

Walking trails cross the island's heathland, opening up views over the fjord and the mountains of the Tröllaskagi peninsula. There is little to do beyond walking, watching birds, and slowing down, which is precisely why Icelanders come as much as foreign visitors do. A couple of hours is enough to see the marked trails; a full day lets you settle into the island's pace.
