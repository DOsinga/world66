Hveragerði is a small geothermal town about 45 km east of Reykjavík, sitting directly on top of one of Iceland's most active hot-spring fields. Steam rises from vents throughout the town, and the ground is warm enough to bake bread in a pot buried in the earth — locals actually do this, and the resulting rúgbrauð (hot-spring bread) has a texture and character you will not find in a conventional oven.

The thermal energy powers a network of [geothermal greenhouses](/europe/iceland/hveragerdi/greenhouse_horticulture) where flowers, tomatoes, cucumbers, and even bananas grow year-round despite the northern latitude. The town has a long tradition of horticultural education, and trainee gardeners from across Iceland come here to learn. Walk through the [Hveragerði Geothermal Park](/europe/iceland/hveragerdi/hveragerdi_geothermal_park) in the town centre to see hot springs and steam vents without leaving the pavement.

For hikers, a well-marked trail leads up the [Reykjadalur valley](/europe/iceland/hveragerdi/reykjadalur_hot_spring_river) to a geothermally heated river where you can bathe outdoors — one of the better free experiences in Iceland. The town also has the [NLFI Rehabilitation Centre](/europe/iceland/hveragerdi/nlfi_rehabilitation_centre) with thermal pools that draw visitors seeking geothermal therapy and are open to the public on a day-visitor basis.
