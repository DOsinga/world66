Jökulsárlón is a glacial lagoon in southeastern Iceland, fed by icebergs calving from the Breiðamerkurjökull glacier — an outlet of the vast Vatnajökull ice cap. The lagoon has formed within living memory: in 1935 it barely existed; today it is Iceland's deepest lake at over 200 metres and still growing as the glacier retreats. The floating icebergs range from house-sized chunks to smaller pieces worn smooth by waves, and they range in colour from brilliant white to deep blue, with occasional streaks of ash-black from volcanic layers trapped in the ice.

This is one of the most photographed places in Iceland, and for good reason. The lagoon sits directly beside the Ring Road (Route 1) in the south of the country, making it accessible to everyone doing the ring route. Diamond Beach — where smaller ice chunks wash up on the black sand after the melt — lies just across the road bridge, and the contrast of crystal ice on volcanic black sand has become something of an Icelandic cliché. It is, however, exactly as striking in person as in photographs.

Seals are common here: they haul out on the ice floes and swim among the boats. Arctic terns nest on the lagoon edges in summer and dive aggressively if you approach too closely. Boat tours operate from May to October — both traditional inflatable Zodiac tours and amphibious duck boat tours — and are the best way to see the lagoon's interior and get close to the larger icebergs. The Ring Road bridge over the outlet channel offers good views without a tour.

Jökulsárlón is roughly 370 kilometres from Reykjavik on the Ring Road, about a four-hour drive. Most visitors stop here as part of a south coast day trip from Reykjavik or on the way around the ring route. The nearest town is Höfn, about an hour to the east.
