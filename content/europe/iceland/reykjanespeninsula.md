The Reykjanes Peninsula is the first piece of Iceland most international travellers touch, but too many hurry through it between Keflavík Airport and [Reykjavik](/europe/iceland/reykjavik). That is a mistake. This is one of the most geologically blunt landscapes in the country: black lava, steaming vents, fissures, lighthouses, power plants, and rough Atlantic edges packed into a compact half-day loop.

The peninsula sits on the Mid-Atlantic Ridge, and in recent years that has stopped being an abstract fact. Eruptions and earthquakes around Fagradalsfjall, Svartsengi, and Grindavík have repeatedly changed access to parts of the western peninsula. Check Icelandic authorities before hiking near new lava fields or planning anything around Grindavík; conditions can change faster than guidebooks.

For most visitors, Reykjanes works best as a first or last day in Iceland. You can soak at the [Blue Lagoon](/europe/iceland/reykjanespeninsula/blue_lagoon), walk the fissure at the [Bridge Between Continents](/europe/iceland/reykjanespeninsula/bridge_between_continents), see the mud and steam at [Gunnuhver](/europe/iceland/reykjanespeninsula/gunnuhver), and still reach the airport without crossing half the country. It is not the prettiest Iceland, but it is among the most honest.
