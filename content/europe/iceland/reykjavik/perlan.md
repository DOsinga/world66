A glass dome sits on top of six cylindrical tanks that hold most of Reykjavik's hot-water supply, on a low hill on the south side of town. Inside is an excellent nature museum — a real ice cave that runs through the underbelly of the building, full-scale displays on glaciers, volcanoes, geothermal energy and Icelandic wildlife, and a planetarium that screens a Northern Lights show.

The top floor is an outdoor observation deck with a 360-degree view: the city below, Mount Esja across the bay, the Reykjanes peninsula to the south, and Snæfellsjökull (the glacier of Jules Verne's *Journey to the Centre of the Earth*) faintly visible 120 km away on a clear day.
