Skógar is a small settlement on Iceland's south coast, best known as the site of [Skógafoss](/europe/iceland/skogar/skogafoss), one of the biggest and most spectacular waterfalls in Iceland. The falls plunge 60 metres from the edge of the former sea cliffs, and in good light a rainbow is almost always visible in the mist at the base. You can walk right up to the foot of the falls and, via a steep staircase, to the top for views across the coastal plain to the sea. Ten minutes' walk east is [Kvernufoss](/europe/iceland/skogar/kvernufoss), a smaller waterfall that you can walk behind — known to almost no one and almost always empty.

The [Skogar Folk Museum](/europe/iceland/skogar/skogar_folk_museum) beside the falls is one of the best of its kind in Iceland, with an open-air collection of traditional turf houses and over 18,000 historical artefacts assembled by a single collector over sixty years. The [Fimmvörðuháls](/europe/iceland/skogar/fimmvorduhals) hiking trail begins here, crossing the pass between Eyjafjallajökull and Mýrdalsjökull glaciers to Þórsmörk — a demanding two-day walk across an active volcanic landscape. Within easy reach are [Seljavallalaug](/europe/iceland/skogar/seljavallalaug), Iceland's oldest outdoor swimming pool built into a mountain valley in 1923, and [Sólheimajökull](/europe/iceland/skogar/solheimajokull), an accessible glacier with guided walking tours available. Skógar is on the Ring Road about 150 kilometres east of Reykjavik.
