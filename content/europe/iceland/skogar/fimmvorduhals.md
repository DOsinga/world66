The Fimmvörðuháls trail is a 25-kilometre route connecting Skógar with Þórsmörk, crossing the high pass between Eyjafjallajökull and Mýrdalsjökull glaciers at an elevation of around 1000 metres. Most hikers do it as a two-day route, staying in a hut at the midpoint on the pass. The upper section passes through one of the most dramatic landscapes in Iceland: glaciers on both sides, lava fields from the 2010 eruption still partly bare and new-looking, and views in clear weather that extend across the entire south coast.

The trail is well-marked but requires appropriate equipment — waterproof layers, good boots, and some basic navigation ability. Weather on the pass can change from clear to whiteout in an hour, even in summer. The lower sections on both the Skógar and Þórsmörk sides involve river crossings that can be difficult in early summer snowmelt. Check conditions before setting out and consider booking the mountain hut in advance for the midsummer period.
