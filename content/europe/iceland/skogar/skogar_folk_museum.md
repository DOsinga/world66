The Skogar Folk Museum is one of the finest rural heritage museums in Iceland, and deserves more time than most visitors give it. The outdoor section contains a collection of restored traditional turf houses — some fully furnished with original tools, furniture, and domestic objects — that give a concrete sense of the extraordinary difficulty of everyday life in Iceland before the 20th century. The grass-roofed houses are genuine historical structures, reassembled here from across the district.

The indoor exhibition building holds a vast collection of boats, fishing equipment, agricultural tools, household objects, and clothing, all assembled by a single collector over sixty years. The selection is remarkable in its completeness: this is not a curated highlights show but the actual accumulation of everything that one obsessive man could save from the farms of his district. Allow two hours minimum; the museum is far larger than it looks from outside.
