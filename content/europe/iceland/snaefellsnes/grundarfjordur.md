Grundarfjörður is a small fishing town on the north coast of Snæfellsnes, sitting at the base of Kirkjufell mountain beside a wide fjord. It has the functional character of a real fishing community rather than a tourist village — fish processing, boats, and a harbour alongside a growing layer of visitor infrastructure drawn by the Kirkjufell phenomenon. The town has a couple of guesthouses, a campsite, and a restaurant.

Whale-watching tours operate from Grundarfjörður, and this section of the north coast has a good reputation for orca sightings in winter when herring shoals concentrate in the fjords. Snorkelling tours in the Breiðafjörður bay are also available. The road from Grundarfjörður continues west along the north coast to the smaller settlements of Ólafsvík and Rif, and eventually to the glacier area — this north coast route is slightly less travelled than the southern coast road and offers a different perspective on the peninsula.
