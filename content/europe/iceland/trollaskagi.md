Tröllaskagi — "Troll's Peninsula" — is the dramatic wedge of land between Skagafjörður and Eyjafjörður, holding some of the highest and most rugged mountains in northern Iceland. The peaks rise straight from the sea, and the valleys between them, including Fljót and Skíðadalur, stayed essentially inaccessible by road until the late 20th century. It is the reason to linger in Dalvík rather than push straight on to Akureyri.

The roads west and northwest from Dalvík wind through fjords and over mountain passes. The Ólafsfjörður tunnel opened in 1991, but the old mountain road above it still climbs to the kind of dramatic viewpoints that Iceland's newer tunnels now bypass — worth the detour in good summer weather, though several passes are open only in summer and demand caution even then.

The fishing towns of Ólafsfjörður and Siglufjörður sit on the peninsula's north coast, both reachable from Dalvík by a combination of tunnel and coastal road. Both reward a visit for their fishing heritage, and Siglufjörður in particular for its excellent Herring Era Museum, which recreates the boom years when this remote town was one of the busiest herring ports in the North Atlantic. Allow a full day to drive the peninsula and stop along the way.
