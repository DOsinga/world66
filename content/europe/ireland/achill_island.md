Achill Island is the largest of the Irish islands, about 148 square kilometres of bog, heath and mountain off the coast of County Mayo. It doesn't feel like a day trip from anywhere. Since 1887 it has been joined to the mainland by a swing bridge at Achill Sound, and the road runs on across the island through Keel and Dooagh until it gives out above [Keem Bay](/europe/ireland/achill_island/keem_bay), where a sandy cove sits under the slopes of [Croaghaun](/europe/ireland/achill_island/croaghaun). On the far side of that mountain are the highest sea cliffs in Ireland.

About 2,300 people live here, in villages strung along the road: Achill Sound by the bridge, Keel and Dooagh in the west, Dugort on the north coast, Dooega in the south. Roughly half the island is Gaeltacht, though English is what you will mostly hear. The island was settled at least from the Neolithic, and the stone walls of prehistoric fields still run up the side of Slievemore under the bog. The most haunting site is more recent: the [Deserted Village](/europe/ireland/achill_island/deserted_village), a long line of roofless stone cottages on the same mountain, abandoned in the years of the Famine. Megalithic tombs and old forts also survive along the Atlantic Drive, the island's coastal road, and [Kildavnet Castle](/europe/ireland/achill_island/kildavnet_castle) is the tower house associated with the pirate queen Grace O'Malley.

Achill has drawn artists and writers for two centuries. The painter Paul Henry lived here for almost a decade, Heinrich Böll spent his summers in Dugort and wrote about the island in his *Irish Journal*, and Graham Greene stayed in the late 1940s. More recently, Keem Bay, Cloughmore and Purteen Pier stood in for the fictional island in *The Banshees of Inisherin*. The west-facing beaches are exposed and good for surfing, with a surf school in Keel, and Slievemore, Croaghaun and Minaun all make fine hill walks.

You need your own wheels. Bus 450 runs from [Westport](/europe/ireland/westport) a few times a day, but the best way to arrive is along the [Great Western Greenway](/europe/ireland/achill_island/great_western_greenway), the 42-kilometre walking and cycling path that follows the old railway from Westport via Newport and Mulranny to Achill Sound. Bikes can be hired at both ends.
