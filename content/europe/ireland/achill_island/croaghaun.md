Croaghaun is the highest mountain on Achill at 688 metres, and its northern face falls straight into the Atlantic as the highest sea cliffs in Ireland, and the third-highest in Europe. You can't reach those cliffs by road, which is part of their power: from the top you look down on a wall of rock and sea birds with nothing beyond it but ocean.

The usual approach is from Keem Bay. Most walkers aim for the southwest top at 664 metres, which has the best views down the cliffs, rather than the true summit. It's a proper hill walk over rough ground, and the mountain gets plenty of cloud, so pick your day and bring a map.
