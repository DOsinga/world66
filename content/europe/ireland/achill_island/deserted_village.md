On the southern slope of Slievemore, near Dugort, lie the remains of between 80 and 100 single-room stone cottages, unmortared and roofless, strung out for about 1.5 kilometres along the mountainside. Around them you can still make out the field strips and potato "lazy beds" of the rundale system, where families farmed scattered plots and shared the grazing. The settlement dates from no earlier than around 1750, and the Ordnance Survey recorded 137 houses here in 1838.

The village was abandoned in the 1850s, after the Famine, years of failed harvests and rising rents. Most families moved down to Dooagh on the coast and took up fishing. Entry is free: park by the modern graveyard and walk up the track. Slievemore (671 m) can be climbed from above the village, and a megalithic tomb lies on the hillside about a kilometre to the east.
