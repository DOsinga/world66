Adare is a village on the River Maigue, 15 kilometres south-west of [Limerick](/europe/ireland/limerick), and it's often called one of the prettiest in Ireland. Its Irish name, Áth Dara, means "ford of the oak": the village grew up where the river could be crossed, and the Normans built a castle to guard the crossing. Much of the main street was built by the Earls of Dunraven as an estate village for their nearby mansion, and the thatched cottages along Main Street near the gates of their house are the image everyone takes home.

For a village of 1,200 people, Adare is unusually rich in medieval buildings. In the Middle Ages it had three monasteries, and all three survive in some form: the [Trinitarian Abbey](/europe/ireland/adare/trinitarian_abbey), now the Catholic parish church; the [Augustinian Friary](/europe/ireland/adare/augustinian_friary) by the river bridge, now the Church of Ireland church; and the roofless [Franciscan Friary](/europe/ireland/adare/franciscan_friary) on the golf course. Across the river stands [Desmond Castle](/europe/ireland/adare/desmond_castle), a 13th-century fortress visited by guided tour in summer.

The Dunravens' Tudor-revival mansion, Adare Manor, is now a luxury hotel and golf resort, and it will host the Ryder Cup in September 2027. The less glamorous side of Adare is the traffic: the main Limerick to Tralee road runs straight down Main Street, and it's usually jammed. A bypass is due to open in 2027. Until then, park in the free car park behind the Heritage Centre and walk.

The Heritage Centre on Main Street is the place to start. It has a small exhibition on the village's history, the tourist office, and the tickets for Desmond Castle. Buses 13 and 14 run hourly from Limerick and take about 20 minutes, so Adare makes an easy half-day trip from the city or a stop on the way to Kerry.
