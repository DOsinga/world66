Blarney Castle is a 15th-century tower house, built in 1446 by Dermot McCarthy, King of Munster, and one of Ireland's best-preserved medieval fortifications. The keep stands about 26 metres tall and the climb to the top — up worn spiral stairs — is steep enough to require some care.

The **Blarney Stone** sits in the southern parapet, just below the top of the tower. The stone is large, dark, and embedded in the castle wall; it has been kissed by millions of people over the centuries, a fact that occasionally prompts visitors to reconsider. But the ritual continues and the queue at the top of the tower is testimony to the stone's pull.

Beyond the stone, the **castle grounds** are the real surprise. The formal gardens are extensive and well maintained: the **Poison Garden** is planted with clearly labelled toxic plants (wolfsbane, deadly nightshade, mandrake); the **Fern Garden** is a quiet Victorian creation in a damp hollow; the **Rock Close** is a strange landscape of large boulders said to have druidic associations, with an artificial cave, a witch's stone, and a wishing steps.

The grounds also contain the ruins of a later mansion and assorted estate buildings. Allow at least two hours; the gardens alone take an hour if you wander properly.
