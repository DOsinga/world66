story: >
  Raheen-a-Cluig means "little fort of the bell" in Irish, a name that suggests an earlier bell tower or monastic bell house on the site. The church is thought to date from the twelfth century and was associated with the Archbishop of Dublin. It was already in ruins by the seventeenth century, having fallen out of use as the population shifted and the medieval parish reorganised. It is now a protected National Monument.
---

Raheen-a-Cluig is a small but atmospheric medieval ruin sitting near the base of Bray Head, largely unknown to casual visitors. The roofless nave and chancel survive to wall height, with the characteristic rounded arches and simple stonework of Romanesque-influenced Irish church architecture. The site is quiet and slightly overgrown, which adds to rather than detracts from its character — it feels genuinely old rather than managed.

The church is accessible on foot from the town, making it a worthwhile addition to any walk towards Bray Head. It is the kind of place that rewards visitors who take five minutes to stop and look closely rather than walking straight past. As a National Monument it is protected and maintained, though there is no formal visitor infrastructure. Worth finding if you are already heading for the cliff walk — it lies roughly on the way.
