Brú na Bóinne, the "palace of the Boyne", is the most important prehistoric landscape in Ireland and one of the great Neolithic sites of Europe. In a loop of the River Boyne, 8 kilometres west of [Drogheda](/europe/ireland/drogheda) and about an hour north of [Dublin](/europe/ireland/dublin), farmers built three huge passage tombs around 3200 BC: [Newgrange](/europe/ireland/bru_na_boinne/newgrange), [Knowth](/europe/ireland/bru_na_boinne/knowth) and [Dowth](/europe/ireland/bru_na_boinne/dowth). That makes them older than Stonehenge and the pyramids of Giza. Around them lie dozens of smaller tombs, henges and standing stones, and between them the three big mounds hold the largest collection of megalithic art in Europe. The whole bend of the river has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1993.

The tombs were built with a precise eye on the sky. At Newgrange the rising sun on the winter solstice shines through a "roof box" above the entrance and lights the inner chamber for about 17 minutes. Knowth's two passages face the equinoxes, and Dowth catches the low winter sun in the afternoon. Later people kept coming back: Knowth became a royal seat in the early Middle Ages, the Normans used it as a motte, and the Cistercian monks of [Mellifont](/europe/ireland/drogheda/old_mellifont_abbey) farmed the land. The name Newgrange comes from their "new grange", or farm.

You cannot simply drive up to the monuments. Newgrange and Knowth are reached only on guided tours from the [visitor centre](/europe/ireland/bru_na_boinne/bru_na_boinne_visitor_centre) on the south bank, which takes you over a footbridge and onto a shuttle bus. Tickets are limited and booking ahead is essential, especially in summer. Newgrange is the only tomb you can go inside, and photography is not allowed in the passage. Dowth is not part of the tours, but you can see the grassy mound from the road.

Be honest with yourself about what you will get. Brú na Bóinne is busy and well organised, and the reconstructed white quartz front of Newgrange divides archaeologists. But standing in that dark chamber when the guide switches on the "solstice" light is still a moment few places can match. Combine it with the battlefield of the Boyne, [Monasterboice](/europe/ireland/drogheda/monasterboice) and the old town of Drogheda, or with the [Hill of Tara](/europe/ireland/trim/hill_of_tara) and [Trim](/europe/ireland/trim) further up the river.
