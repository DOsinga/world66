Knowth is the largest of the Brú na Bóinne tombs: a mound about 67 metres across, built around 3200 BC, with two separate passages running in from east and west and 17 smaller tombs clustered around it. It holds more than a third of all the megalithic art in Western Europe, mostly spirals, lozenges and wavy lines cut into the 127 kerbstones. Later it became a hill fort and the seat of the kings of Northern Brega, and the Normans used the top as a motte.

Access is by guided tour from the visitor centre only. You cannot enter the chambers, but you can look down the eastern passage, walk around the kerbstones and climb onto the mound for views across the Boyne valley. For many visitors the carved kerb at Knowth is more memorable than the queue at Newgrange.
