The Rock of Cashel is a 60-metre limestone outcrop rising dramatically from the surrounding Tipperary plain, topped by one of the most impressive groupings of medieval buildings in Ireland: a 12th-century round tower, the Romanesque Cormac's Chapel, a ruined 13th-century Gothic cathedral, and the 15th-century Hall of the Vicars Choral. The complex is enclosed by a circuit wall and has been a place of royal and ecclesiastical power for over a thousand years.

**Cormac's Chapel** (1127–1134) is the most important building on the Rock — the first and finest Romanesque church in Ireland, commissioned by King-Bishop Cormac Mac Carthaigh. Inside, traces of the original 12th-century frescoes survive on the walls: a Norman knight, the Magi, symbols of the evangelists. The larger **cathedral** next to it was built in the 13th century and is now roofless after being burned in 1495 by Gerald Fitzgerald, Earl of Kildare — who, when asked by Henry VII why he had done it, reportedly answered "because I thought the archbishop was inside." The **Round Tower**, 28 metres high and the oldest building on the site, is an 11th-century landmark. Entry is charged; admission includes access to all buildings and a small exhibition. Opening hours: daily from 9am.
