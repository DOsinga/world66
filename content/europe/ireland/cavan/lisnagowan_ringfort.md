Lisnagowan is one of County Cavan's best-preserved ringforts — the circular earthwork enclosures that served as farmsteads for Irish families throughout the early medieval period. The rath consists of a raised earthen bank enclosing a roughly circular interior, and it remains remarkably intact for a structure of this age. It is designated a National Monument.

Ringforts like Lisnagowan are scattered across the Irish countryside in enormous numbers — an estimated 45,000 survive — but few are as well-preserved or as easily visited as this one. The surrounding fields and drumlin hills give it context: you can understand exactly why a farmer would have chosen this gently elevated spot, with sight lines in every direction and the boggy lowlands keeping unwelcome visitors at a distance.
