Clonmel is the largest town in County Tipperary, set in the Suir Valley with the [Comeragh Mountains](/europe/ireland/clonmel/comeragh_mountains) rising to the south. The name comes from the Irish "Cluain Meala" — meadow of honey — and the setting lives up to it: if you arrive from the direction of Cashel, the town rolls out below in one of the most panoramic views of green Irish landscape you will find.

The town has a long history as a market centre and was the last place in [Ireland](/europe/ireland) to hold out against Cromwell in 1650. The [Main Guard](/europe/ireland/clonmel/main_guard), a seventeenth-century courthouse on the main street, is one of the few surviving public buildings from that era. Clonmel is also the [birthplace of Laurence Sterne](/europe/ireland/clonmel/laurence_sterne_birthplace), author of Tristram Shandy, and the place where the Italian entrepreneur Charles Bianconi launched Ireland's first public transport system — a network of horse-drawn coaches — in 1815.

The surrounding countryside is excellent for walking, particularly in the Comeraghs and the Nire Valley to the south. The [Tipperary Museum of Hidden History](/europe/ireland/clonmel/tipperary_museum_of_hidden_history) opened in 2020 and gives good context for the region's layered past.
