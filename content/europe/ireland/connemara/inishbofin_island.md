Inishbofin Island sits off the Connemara coast, far enough out to feel like a real crossing but close enough for a long day from Cleggan. It is a low, wind-bright island of beaches, stone walls, old monastic traces, and a small harbour village shaped by ferry times.

The island works because its pleasures are simple. Walk to the ruins of [Cromwell's Barracks](/europe/ireland/connemara/inishbofin_island/cromwells_barracks), swim or stroll at [East End Bay](/europe/ireland/connemara/inishbofin_island/east_end_bay), and look into the [Inishbofin Heritage Museum](/europe/ireland/connemara/inishbofin_island/inishbofin_heritage_museum) near the pier before the ferry back.

It is quieter than the Aran Islands and less dramatic than the mainland mountains, but that is not a weakness. Inishbofin is for a slow day of sea air, walking, and small discoveries.
