Omey Island is one of Connemara's oddest small detours: an island you reach by walking or driving across the seabed. At low tide, the strand opens from Claddaghduff and a marked route leads over the sand. A few hours later the Atlantic covers the road again, and the island feels far more remote than its 600 metres from the mainland suggest.

There is no village to wander and almost nothing to buy. That is the point. Omey is a place for timing the tide, crossing slowly, and spending a few quiet hours among dunes, grazing land, old stones, and wide sea views. Bring food, waterproof shoes, and a proper tide check; the visit only works when you have enough low-water time to cross, explore, and return without rushing.

The human history is older than the empty fields first suggest. [Teampall Feichin](/europe/ireland/connemara/omey_island/teampall_feichin) and [St Feichin's Well](/europe/ireland/connemara/omey_island/st_feichins_well) keep the memory of an early Christian settlement alive, while the island's graveyard and scattered archaeological remains give the walk a slightly haunted edge. The other anchor is the landscape itself: [Omey Strand](/europe/ireland/connemara/omey_island/omey_strand), Fahy Lough, rabbit-cropped grass, and the changing line between beach and sea.

Omey is best treated as a half-day from Clifden or a slow stop on the Sky Road side of Connemara. It is not a conventional sightseeing stop, but if you like places where the tide sets the schedule, it is worth the effort.
