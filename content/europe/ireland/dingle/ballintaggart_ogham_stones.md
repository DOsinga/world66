Ballintaggart is a circle of nine ogham stones in a small enclosure on a low ridge a couple of kilometres east of Dingle town. Ogham is the early medieval Irish alphabet, written as a sequence of notches cut along the edge of a standing stone, and the Dingle Peninsula has more ogham stones than anywhere else in Ireland. The Ballintaggart stones date from the 5th to 7th centuries AD and bear personal names — the standard ogham inscription is "*X son of Y*", carved as a memorial.

The site is signposted from the N86 east of Dingle. The track up is short but rough, and there is no visitor centre or signage at the site itself. Bring a torch in the late afternoon — the cuts are shallow and easier to read in raking light.
