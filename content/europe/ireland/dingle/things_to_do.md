---
title: "Things to Do"
type: section
---

Dingle's appeal divides cleanly into the town and the peninsula around it. The town is a small, walkable harbour with painted shopfronts, traditional music, and a remarkable density of good pubs and restaurants for its size. The peninsula is the real prize — a 50-kilometre loop of cliffs, beaches, beehive huts, ogham stones, and early Christian sites, much of it along the **Slea Head Drive**.

Allow at least two days. One for **Slea Head Drive** and the western end of the peninsula, with stops at **Dunbeg Fort**, **Dunmore Head**, and the Blasket Islands viewpoint. One for the **Conor Pass** north over the spine of the peninsula and the early Christian sites at **Gallarus Oratory** and **Kilmalkedar**. The Irish-language coast — known as the **Corca Dhuibhne Gaeltacht** — is one of the strongholds of spoken Irish; you will hear it in shops, schools, and pubs.
