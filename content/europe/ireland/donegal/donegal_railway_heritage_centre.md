  The County Donegal Railways were a 3-foot narrow-gauge network that threaded through the county's rugged terrain from 1863 to 1959. At their peak the system ran 220 kilometres through landscape considered too difficult for standard-gauge track. When the system closed in 1960, it was one of the largest narrow-gauge railway closures in the British Isles, ending a form of transport that had connected remote Donegal communities for nearly a century.
---

The Donegal Railway Heritage Centre occupies the former railway station building on the edge of Donegal town and commemorates the County Donegal Railways, a narrow-gauge network that operated from 1863 until 1959. The museum contains restored locomotives, rolling stock, photographs, and exhibits on the history of a railway system that was remarkable for its scale and ingenuity in a county with no easy terrain.

The collection is enthusiastically maintained by local volunteers and is better than its modest size would suggest. The narrow-gauge locomotives are handsome objects and the photographic archive captures a way of life in rural Donegal that has entirely disappeared. For anyone with an interest in railways or in Irish social history, the centre is worth an hour. Children tend to like the locomotives.
