Tory Island, officially Toraigh, lies off the northwest coast of [Donegal](/europe/ireland/donegal), far enough out that the crossing feels like a real departure. It is small, Irish-speaking, weather-beaten, and still inhabited. That last point matters: Tory is not an empty scenic island but a living community that fought hard in the 1970s to avoid evacuation.

The island's identity is unusually strong. Medieval monastic remains stand near the pier, including the [Tau Cross](/europe/ireland/donegal/tory_island/tau_cross) and [round tower](/europe/ireland/donegal/tory_island/round_tower). At the eastern end, [Dun Bhaloir](/europe/ireland/donegal/tory_island/dun_bhaloir) puts myth and cliff scenery in the same place: local tradition links the headland to Balor of the Evil Eye, while the Atlantic does the rest.

Tory also has a modern cultural story. Since the mid-20th century it has been associated with a community of painters, and for decades visitors were famously greeted by Patsy Dan Rodgers, the island's elected "King of Tory", until his death in 2018. The ferry is weather-dependent, the facilities are few, and that is part of the point. On a clear day, it is one of Donegal's best trips.
