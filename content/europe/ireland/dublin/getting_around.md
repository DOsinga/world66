---
title: "Getting Around"
type: section
---

The old centre is compact — you can walk from Trinity College to Dublin Castle in ten minutes. Most major sights are within a 30-minute walk of each other. Walking is almost always the best option for the historic core.

**Buses** cover the wider city, operated by Dublin Bus. The network is extensive but can be slow in traffic; the Leap Card (a reloadable smart card) is the most convenient way to pay. **Luas** trams run on two cross-city lines connecting the Docklands, the city centre, and suburbs to the southwest. The **DART** (Dublin Area Rapid Transit) is the suburban rail line running along the coast from Malahide in the north to Greystones in the south — invaluable for reaching Howth, Dún Laoghaire, Dalkey, and Sandycove.

Taxis are plentiful and can be booked via the **Free Now** app. Cycling is possible but the city is not particularly cycle-friendly in the centre. **Dublinbikes**, the city's public bike scheme, has stands across the inner city and is cheap for short journeys.
