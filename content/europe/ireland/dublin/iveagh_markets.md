The Iveagh Markets on Francis Street are a handsome red-brick and granite covered market hall, built by the Guinness magnate Lord Iveagh in 1906 to rehouse the street traders displaced by his clearance of the nearby slums. Carved stone heads above the entrance represent the trading nations of the world, a confident flourish of Edwardian civic ambition.

The markets have stood empty and increasingly derelict for many years, the subject of a long-running tug-of-war over their restoration. For now they can only be admired from outside, but the facade is a striking reminder of the Liberties' history as Dublin's market and trading quarter, in the heart of its antiques district along Francis Street.
