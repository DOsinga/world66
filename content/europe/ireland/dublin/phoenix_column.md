The Phoenix Column is a 1747 column topped by a flaming phoenix that stands near the centre of Phoenix Park. The column was erected by the Earl of Chesterfield, who also laid out the park's main avenue, and it gave the park its English name — though the park's real name comes from the Irish 'fionn uisce', meaning clear water, which the English approximated as 'phoenix'.

The column makes a useful meeting point and orientation landmark within the large park. It stands near the Papal Cross, erected for John Paul II's 1979 visit, and both are within walking distance of the main car parks.
