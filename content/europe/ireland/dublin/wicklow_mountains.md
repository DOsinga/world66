The Wicklow Mountains begin barely 20 kilometres south of Dublin city centre and feel like a different world. The national park covers 200 square kilometres of bog, heather moorland, granite peaks, and wooded glens. The Military Road — built after the 1798 rebellion to flush out insurgents hiding in the hills — runs south through the heart of the park and makes for a dramatic drive.

The main destination is **Glendalough** (Irish: *Gleann dá Loch*, Valley of the Two Lakes), a glacial valley containing the ruins of a 6th-century monastic city founded by St Kevin. The round tower, cathedral, and surrounding hermit cells make it one of the finest early Christian sites in Ireland. The two lakes and the wooded valley are beautiful in themselves.

Bus tours run from Dublin city centre daily. By car, Glendalough is about 1 hour 15 minutes from the city; the last bus from Glendalough returns in the late afternoon, so check times. Combine with Powerscourt Estate (about 30 km north of Glendalough) for a full day in Wicklow.
