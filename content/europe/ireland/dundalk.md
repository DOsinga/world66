Dundalk sits roughly halfway between [Dublin](/europe/ireland/dublin) and [Belfast](/europe/unitedkingdom/northernireland/belfast), making it a natural stopping point on the Dublin–Belfast corridor. The town has a handsome 19th-century cathedral, [St Patrick's Cathedral](/europe/ireland/dundalk/st_patricks_cathedral) — its Gothic spires modelled on King's College Chapel, Cambridge, and completed during the darkest year of the Great Famine — and a compact centre with good pubs and a [County Museum](/europe/ireland/dundalk/county_museum) in a former distillery building.

The real draw is the surrounding landscape. The [Cooley Peninsula](/europe/ireland/dundalk/cooley_peninsula) to the northeast has excellent hill walking — the Táin Way loops through mountains that inspired the great Irish cattle-raid epic, the Táin Bó Cúailnge. The coast road out to Carlingford is one of the prettiest short drives in the northeast, ending at a perfectly preserved medieval village with Norman castle ruins and a reputation for oysters. [Proleek Dolmen](/europe/ireland/dundalk/proleek_dolmen), 4 km from the town, is one of the finest Neolithic portal tombs in Ireland, its 47-tonne capstone balanced on three upright stones in an open field. And [Dún Dealgan Motte](/europe/ireland/dundalk/dun_dealgan_motte), traditionally the birthplace of the mythological hero Cú Chulainn, rises from a drumlin hill west of town with fine views over the bay.
