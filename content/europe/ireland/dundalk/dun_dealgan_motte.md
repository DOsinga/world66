Dún Dealgan Motte is a well-preserved Norman motte — the earthwork base of a timber castle — sitting on a drumlin hill 2 km west of Dundalk town centre. The motte dates from the 12th century and is a National Monument, but the site itself carries much older significance: the name Dún Dealgan predates the Norman arrival and is associated in Irish mythology with Cú Chulainn.

The motte rises steeply from the surrounding farmland and offers good views over Dundalk Bay and toward the Cooley and Mourne Mountains. It's a short walk from the road and takes about 15 minutes to climb. The site is freely accessible and unguarded; it's maintained but not developed as a formal visitor attraction. Worth combining with the County Museum for context on both the Norman and the mythological layers of Dundalk's history.
