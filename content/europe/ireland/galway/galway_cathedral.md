Galway Cathedral — officially the Cathedral of Our Lady Assumed into Heaven and St Nicholas — is an imposing limestone building on an island in the River Corrib, accessible from the city centre by the Salmon Weir Bridge. It was completed in 1965 and has the scale of a medieval cathedral despite being twentieth-century: a large dome, Renaissance exterior, and an interior with high arched ceilings and striking modern mosaic work.

The mosaics are the most interesting thing inside — geometric patterns and figurative scenes throughout the nave and transepts, with a particularly vivid image of John F. Kennedy alongside religious figures in one chapel, a tribute to Kennedy's visit to Galway just before his assassination. The Salmon Weir Bridge alongside the cathedral is a good place to watch Atlantic salmon leaping up the weir during migration season, typically April to June. The cathedral grounds are open daily and the building can be visited outside of services.
