  The visitor centre was deliberately built below the treeline and partly into the hillside to minimise its visual impact on the valley. The Office of Public Works, which manages Ireland's national heritage sites, insisted on this after the controversy over earlier visitor infrastructure at sensitive sites. The restraint is worth noting — most heritage sites of this scale would have something much more intrusive.

The Office of Public Works visitor centre at Glendalough is the sensible starting point for a visit — it has a small museum covering the history of the monastic settlement from St Kevin's founding in the 6th century through the medieval period, with original stonework and reconstructed artifacts. The introductory film is worth watching before you walk the site.

The centre also sells the walking maps you need for the longer routes in the national park. Admission to the visitor centre is charged; the monastic site itself is free to walk around. The centre's car park fills early on summer weekends and bank holidays — aim to arrive before 9am if you can.
