---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

Glendalough is busy from April to October, with peak crowds in July and August and on Irish bank holiday weekends. The site is at its best in spring (April–May), when the oak woodland is coming into leaf, the car parks are manageable, and the light in the valley is exceptional. Autumn (September–October) is similarly good — the woodland turns gold and the midges that plague summer visitors have gone.

Winter visits have their own appeal: the valley is quiet, the bare trees open up views that are hidden in summer, and frost on the ruins is genuinely beautiful. The main hazard is slippery paths on wet or icy days. Glendalough is in Ireland, so assume it might rain at any time of year and dress accordingly.

The Writers' Room at The Wicklow Heather restaurant in Laragh village — mentioned in Atlas Obscura — is worth knowing about if you want somewhere decent to eat after a walk. It has a good selection of Irish whiskeys and keeps a collection of first editions by Irish authors.
