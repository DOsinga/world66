Lahinch is a small seaside town on Liscannor Bay, on the north-west coast of County Clare. It was a fishing hamlet until the West Clare Railway arrived in 1887 and turned it into a resort, with a promenade, a long sandy strand and, from 1892, a golf club. The railway closed in 1961, but by then a new kind of visitor was turning up: surfers. Today the promenade is lined with surf schools and board-hire shacks, and on a good swell the water off [Lahinch Beach](/europe/ireland/lahinch/lahinch_beach) is full of wetsuits, even in winter.

The other pilgrims come for the golf. [Lahinch Golf Club](/europe/ireland/lahinch/lahinch_golf_club) is one of the great links courses, laid out over the dunes at the north end of town, with the stump of [Dough Castle](/europe/ireland/lahinch/dough_castle) standing on the course. The town's Irish name, Leacht Uí Chonchubhair, "O'Connor's memorial cairn", recalls the local chieftains who held that castle.

Lahinch has only about a thousand residents, and it's a relaxed, unpretentious place, with cafés, a handful of pubs and a leisure centre on the promenade. It makes a good base for this stretch of coast. The [Cliffs of Moher](/europe/ireland/doolin/cliffs_of_moher) are about 10 kilometres north, and you can walk to them from the south along the clifftops from [Moher Tower](/europe/ireland/lahinch/moher_tower). Liscannor, a small harbour village, is 4 kilometres west, Ennistymon with its river cascades is 4 kilometres inland, and [the Burren](/europe/ireland/the_burren) begins just to the north.

Bus 350 runs along the coast between Galway and [Ennis](/europe/ireland/ennis) several times a day, stopping at Lahinch, the Cliffs of Moher and [Doolin](/europe/ireland/doolin). The nearest railway station is Ennis.
