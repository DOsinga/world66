title: "Books"
type: section
---

**Angela's Ashes** by Frank McCourt (1996) — the Pulitzer-winning memoir of growing up Catholic and poor in 1930s and 1940s Limerick. The book has the rain, the lanes, the rosaries, and the drink; it also has McCourt's wit. The city's relationship with the book has warmed over time, and reading it before walking the south-eastern lanes adds an extra dimension to a visit.

**'Tis** by Frank McCourt (1999) — the sequel, set largely in New York after the family emigrated, but still rooted in the Limerick the narrator left. Useful if you want the full arc.

**The Country Girls** by Edna O'Brien (1960) — not set in Limerick, but the great novel of Catholic rural Ireland in the 1950s; the small-town Munster world that drove characters like the McCourts to the city makes more sense after reading it.

**Spill Simmer Falter Wither** by Sara Baume (2015) — set on the wild south-west coast of Ireland rather than Limerick itself, but written by a contemporary Irish novelist whose work captures the Atlantic west in spare, vivid prose; an antidote to the heritage-Ireland version of Munster.

**The Crooked Cross** by Michael Curtin (1988) — a comic novel set in 1980s Limerick that captures the city in the years between the McCourt era and the contemporary revival. Wry, character-driven, and easy to find in local bookshops.
