Macroom is a small market town in County Cork, set where the fertile Lee Valley begins to give way to rougher uplands on the road toward Kerry. It is not a grand destination, but it has a good one-day shape: a castle gate in town, a strange drowned wetland nearby, and several hard-edged pieces of Irish history in the surrounding countryside.

The strongest reason to stop is [The Gearagh](/europe/ireland/macroom/the_gearagh), a former oak forest now flooded into a wetland of water, reeds, and dead tree stumps. It is eerie, quiet, and unlike the usual Irish ruin or scenic overlook.

Macroom also carries the marks of conflict. [Macroom Castle](/europe/ireland/macroom/macroom_castle) anchors the town centre, while the [Michael Collins Monument](/europe/ireland/macroom/michael_collins_monument) lies to the south at the site of the 1922 ambush that killed one of modern Ireland's central figures. With a car, the town makes a worthwhile Cork-Kerry stop rather than just somewhere the bus passes through.
