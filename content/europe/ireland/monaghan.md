Monaghan is the county town of County Monaghan in the Republic of Ireland, sitting close to the Northern Ireland border in the drumlin-scattered landscape of the Ulster midlands. It is a compact market town built around [The Diamond](/europe/ireland/monaghan/the_diamond), a diamond-shaped central square — a layout common to Ulster plantation towns — with the 19th-century Gothic Revival [St Macartan's Cathedral](/europe/ireland/monaghan/st_macartans_cathedral) dominating the skyline from its hill. The town itself is quiet and unpretentious, the kind of place where the locals still do their shopping in the old streets.

The [Monaghan County Museum](/europe/ireland/monaghan/monaghan_county_museum) on Hill Street is genuinely worth a visit: it holds the Cross of Clogher, an early medieval processional cross, and a good collection tracing the region's history from prehistory through the linen trade. The surrounding county is better known to anglers than tourists — it is riddled with small glacial lakes that hold good coarse fishing. [Rossmore Forest Park](/europe/ireland/monaghan/rossmore_forest_park), 3 km south, is the easiest place to step into the drumlin landscape that defines this corner of Ireland. Monaghan makes a useful base for anyone travelling between Dublin and Donegal, or for exploring the border country that bears the complicated marks of partition.
