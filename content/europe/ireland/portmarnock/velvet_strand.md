The Velvet Strand is the long, broad sweep of sand that gives Portmarnock its character, running for several kilometres along the coast with views across to Ireland's Eye and Lambay Island. Its firm, level surface made it an ideal early airstrip, and on a June morning in 1930 the aviator Charles Kingsford Smith took off from here in the Southern Cross to make the first eastbound transatlantic flight, reaching America by the Fourth of July.

Today it is a popular spot for walking, swimming, and watching kitesurfers ride the breeze off the Irish Sea. A globe-shaped monument on the seafront marks the historic flight. The beach is an easy day trip from the city, around 25 minutes by DART.
