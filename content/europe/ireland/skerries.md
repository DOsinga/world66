Skerries is a pleasant surprise — a genuine Victorian fishing village that has somehow survived the ham-fisted urban planning that plagues the Dublin commuter belt. It's on the train line from the city, 30km north of Dublin, and the coastline is good for walking. Three offshore islands are visible from the harbour: Shenick's, St. Patrick's, and Colt, giving the view a proper maritime depth.

[Skerries Mills](/europe/ireland/skerries/skerries_mills) is the main sight: the only place in Europe with two windmills and a watermill on a single site, all restored to working order. The [harbour](/europe/ireland/skerries/skerries_harbour) is a small working fishing port with a handful of seafood restaurants and kayak tours that paddle out to the islands. [Shenick Island](/europe/ireland/skerries/shenick_island) can be walked to at low tide and hosts a Napoleonic Martello tower and a colony of harbour seals. [Red Island](/europe/ireland/skerries/red_island) is a headland park connected by a causeway, with good views across the Irish Sea.

There are plenty of pubs. The Stoop Your Head is a reliable gastro-pub. The chip shops, on the other hand, are a low point: dreadful food at inexplicable prices.
