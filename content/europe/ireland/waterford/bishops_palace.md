The Bishop's Palace was designed in 1741 by Richard Castle, the most prolific architect of Georgian Ireland, and it served as the residence of the Church of Ireland bishops of Waterford and Lismore until the early twentieth century. It is the oldest complete Georgian townhouse in Ireland and is now one of the Waterford Treasures museums, covering the city's social, commercial, and cultural history from about 1700 to 1970.

The collection takes you from Waterford's eighteenth-century prosperity — fine glass, silver, furniture, and portraits — through the nineteenth century and into the mid-twentieth, with particular attention to Waterford Crystal, the city's defining export industry. The house itself, with its fine plasterwork and elegant proportions, is as interesting as the exhibits.

The Bishop's Palace is on The Mall, Waterford, adjacent to the Medieval Museum.
