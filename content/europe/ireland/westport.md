Westport is one of the few planned towns in Ireland, laid out in the 18th century by architect James Wyatt. The result is a pleasing Georgian grid of streets centered on The Mall, a tree-lined boulevard running alongside the Carrowbeg River. It sits on the eastern shore of Clew Bay, in the shadow of Croagh Patrick, and it's a genuinely good base for exploring the west of Mayo.

[Croagh Patrick](/europe/ireland/westport/croagh_patrick) dominates the skyline to the southwest. On the last Sunday of July, tens of thousands of pilgrims climb it — some barefoot — to honor St. Patrick, who is said to have fasted on the summit for forty days. The rest of the year you can walk up without ceremony, and on a clear day the view of [Clew Bay](/europe/ireland/westport/clew_bay), with its scatter of islands, is one of the best in Connacht.

[Westport House](/europe/ireland/westport/westport_house), built partly on the foundations of Grace O'Malley's castle, is the town's most formal attraction and opens to visitors from spring through early autumn. The coastline around Clew Bay offers a string of sandy beaches — Bertra, Old Head, Carramore near Louisburgh — and the bay eventually gives way to Killary Harbour, Ireland's only true fjord, tucked into the hills to the south. The town's Georgian heart centres on [The Mall](/europe/ireland/westport/the_mall), a lime-tree-lined boulevard alongside the Carrowbeg River.

The town itself has a lively pub scene and a good concentration of restaurants for its size. It works well as a jumping-off point for Connemara to the south or Achill Island to the north.
