Inishbofin Island lies off the Connemara coast, far enough from Westport to feel like a deliberate escape rather than a quick side stop. The island is small, open, and very Atlantic: white-sand coves, low hills, stone walls, and a harbour village where ferry rhythms shape the day.

Walking is the best way to see it. Looped routes lead past beaches, lake edges, ruined fortifications, and sea views toward the mainland mountains. It works especially well for travelers who want a quieter island than the Aran Islands and are willing to build a day around ferry times.
