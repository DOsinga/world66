Tynwald Hill is one of the Isle of Man's most meaningful historic places: a stepped grass mound at St John's where the island's parliament gathers each summer to proclaim new laws. The ceremony gives the place its weight, but even on an ordinary day it explains a lot about Manx identity and self-government.

The site is small and open, so it works best as a deliberate stop between Peel and the island's interior rather than as a long visit. Read the markers, look across to the chapel, and you get a compact lesson in how old institutions can survive in plain sight.
