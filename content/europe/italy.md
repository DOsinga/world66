Italy rewards return visits in a way that few countries can match. The landscapes alone span an extraordinary range — the glaciated peaks of the [Dolomites](/europe/italy/dolomites), the terraced vineyards of [Cinque Terre](/europe/italy/liguria/cinque_terre), the cypress-lined hills of the [Val d'Orcia](/europe/italy/tuscany/val_dorcia), the volcanic drama of [Mount Etna](/europe/italy/sicily/mount_etna_park) — and behind each landscape is a distinct culture, cuisine, and dialect that feels nothing like the one two hours away. This is a country that was unified only in 1861, and it shows, in the best possible way.

The north divides neatly between the lakes, the Alps, and the great Renaissance cities. [Lake Como](/europe/italy/lombardia/lake_como) sits in a ring of mountains and holds the most concentrated collection of grand lakeside villas in Europe. [Lake Garda](/europe/italy/lake_garda) is bigger and more varied, with Sirmione's Roman ruins at its southern tip and the Dolomite foothills rising from the northern shore. [Venice](/europe/italy/veneto/venice) needs no introduction, but [Verona](/europe/italy/veneto/verona), [Bergamo](/europe/italy/lombardia/bergamo), and [Mantua](/europe/italy/lombardia/mantua) reward the traveller who ventures beyond the obvious. For wine, [Piemonte](/europe/italy/piemonte) is the destination — Barolo and Barbaresco come from the Langhe hills around [Alba](/europe/italy/piemonte/alba), and the Nebbiolo grape here produces some of the world's most serious reds. The [Ligurian](/europe/italy/liguria) coast is different again: [Genoa](/europe/italy/liguria/genoa) is a magnificently complex port city of medieval caruggi and Baroque palaces, and the [Cinque Terre](/europe/italy/liguria/cinque_terre) — five fishing villages clinging to terraced cliffs above the Ligurian Sea — is one of the most photographed stretches of coastline in the world.

[Tuscany](/europe/italy/tuscany) is where most first-time visitors head and for good reason. [Florence](/europe/italy/tuscany/florence) holds a concentration of Renaissance art that is still staggering after repeated visits. [Siena](/europe/italy/tuscany/siena), [Lucca](/europe/italy/tuscany/lucca), and [San Gimignano](/europe/italy/tuscany/sangimignano) each offer something different. But the landscapes can be as compelling as the cities: the [Val d'Orcia](/europe/italy/tuscany/val_dorcia), a UNESCO World Heritage landscape of rolling hills, lone cypresses, and medieval hill towns — [Pienza](/europe/italy/tuscany/pienza), [Montalcino](/europe/italy/tuscany/montalcino), [Bagno Vignoni](/europe/italy/tuscany/bagno_vignoni) — is the Italy of the postcards made real. The [Chianti](/europe/italy/tuscany/chianti) hills between Florence and Siena are classically beautiful and heavily visited; the quieter corners of [Mugello](/europe/italy/tuscany/mugello) and [Lunigiana](/europe/italy/tuscany/lunigiana) are not.

[Rome](/europe/italy/lazio/rome) requires several days and still leaves things undone. The Colosseum, the Forum, the Vatican — these are the obvious draws — but the Trastevere neighbourhood, the Capitoline Museums, and the ordinary pleasure of walking between piazze and stopping for coffee are what linger. Day trips to [Ostia Antica](/europe/italy/lazio/ostiaantica) give a sense of Roman urban life that the Forum alone cannot.

The south is less visited and more surprising. [Naples](/europe/italy/campania/naples) is chaotic and magnificent, with the best pizza on earth and a archaeological museum of unrivalled depth. The [Amalfi Coast](/europe/italy/campania/amalfi_coast) — dramatic cliffs, pastel towns stacked above the Tyrrhenian, lemons and fishing boats — is among the most beautiful coastlines in Europe and justifiably one of Italy's top destinations. [Pompeii](/europe/italy/campania/pompeii) lies nearby, with streets, homes, and bakeries frozen in 79 AD. Further south, [Puglia](/europe/italy/puglia) offers whitewashed towns, trulli houses, and a long Adriatic coast that feels nothing like northern Italy. [Matera](/europe/italy/basilicata/matera), with its ancient cave dwellings, was a European Capital of Culture and is one of the most extraordinary towns in Italy.

[Sicily](/europe/italy/sicily) is a world apart — Greek temples at [Agrigento](/europe/italy/sicily/agrigento) and [Selinunte](/europe/italy/sicily/selinunte), the Arab-Norman cathedral at [Monreale](/europe/italy/sicily/monreale), the baroque towns of the Val di Noto, and always the presence of [Mount Etna](/europe/italy/sicily/mount_etna_park) on the horizon. The [Aeolian Islands](/europe/italy/sicily/aeolianislands) off the north coast — seven volcanic islands including still-erupting Stromboli — are among the most spectacular places in the Mediterranean.

Sardinia ([Costa Smeralda](/europe/italy/sardinia/costasmeralda) aside) remains comparatively quiet: long beaches, nuraghe ruins, and a cooking tradition based on lamb, pecorino, and flatbread that owes more to its pastoral interior than the sea around it.
