L'Aquila sits at the foot of some of the most dramatic country in the Apennines, and a car opens up a string of rewarding excursions within an hour's drive. The big one is the [Gran Sasso](/europe/italy/abruzzo/gransasso), the highest massif in the range: ride the funivia up to the Campo Imperatore plateau, a high, treeless expanse that locals call "Little Tibet," and walk as far as the weather allows.

To the southeast, the restored hill village of [Santo Stefano di Sessanio](/europe/italy/abruzzo/santo_stefano_di_sessanio) is a near-perfect medieval ensemble of pale stone houses, well worth a half-day on its own. From there it is a short drive to the ruined fortress of Rocca Calascio, perched on its ridge high above the plateau — stark walls, an octagonal chapel below, and views that go on for miles. Go late in the day, when the low sun warms the stone.
