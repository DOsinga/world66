Chieti sits on a ridge above the Pescara River in [Abruzzo](/europe/italy/abruzzo), with the Maiella and Gran Sasso mountains as a backdrop and the Adriatic Sea just a few kilometres to the east. The panorama alone is worth a stop.

As Theate, the city was founded roughly 500 years before [Rome](/europe/italy/lazio/rome) and grew to some 60,000 inhabitants under the Roman Empire. The remains of that era are scattered through the modern city: the [Roman theatre](/europe/italy/abruzzo/chieti/roman_theatre), the amphitheatre at [La Civitella](/europe/italy/abruzzo/chieti/la_civitella), the well-preserved [Roman temples](/europe/italy/abruzzo/chieti/roman_temples), and the thermal baths. Later centuries added Gothic and Baroque layers — [Chieti Cathedral](/europe/italy/abruzzo/chieti/chieti_cathedral), dedicated to San Giustino, and the churches along [Corso Marrucino](/europe/italy/abruzzo/chieti/corso_marrucino) are the highlights.

For most visitors the single best reason to come is the [Museo Archeologico Nazionale d'Abruzzo](/europe/italy/abruzzo/chieti/museo_archeologico), which houses the **Warrior of Capestrano** — a sixth-century BC stone soldier in an enormous disc-shaped helmet, one of the great early relics of Italian art.
