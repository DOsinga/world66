Lanciano is one of those Abruzzo towns that rewards slowing down. It sits inland from the Adriatic, close enough to [Pescara](/europe/italy/abruzzo/pescara) for an easy trip but with a much older feel: brick churches, narrow lanes, sudden views over the Sangro valley, and a centre that still moves at market-town pace.

The town is best known for the [Eucharistic Miracle](/europe/italy/abruzzo/lanciano/eucharistic_miracle), kept in the sanctuary of San Francesco and visited by pilgrims from well beyond Abruzzo. Even if you are not travelling for religious reasons, the complex anchors the old quarter and gives Lanciano a different atmosphere from the beach towns on the coast.

The historic centre is compact. Start around [Lanciano Cathedral](/europe/italy/abruzzo/lanciano/lanciano_cathedral), built over the old bridge that linked parts of the medieval town, then wander to [Santa Maria Maggiore](/europe/italy/abruzzo/lanciano/santa_maria_maggiore) and the [Diocesan Museum](/europe/italy/abruzzo/lanciano/museo_diocesano). Lanciano is not a place of blockbuster sightseeing; it is better as a half day of churches, views, coffee, and the feeling of Abruzzo away from the seafront.
