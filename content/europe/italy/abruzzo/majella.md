The Majella, often written Maiella in Italian, is the wilder, quieter mountain mass south of the Gran Sasso. From [Pescara](/europe/italy/abruzzo/pescara) it looks close enough to touch on a clear day, but the range quickly becomes serious: high limestone ridges, beech forest, wolf country, deep gorges, and villages that feel very far from the Adriatic beach resorts.

Most travellers come here for [Majella National Park](/europe/italy/abruzzo/majella/majella_national_park), hiking, caves, and the strange line of hermitages tucked into cliffs and ravines. The high point is [Monte Amaro](/europe/italy/abruzzo/majella/monte_amaro), a broad Apennine summit that is best left to experienced walkers in good weather.

The range is also one of Abruzzo's best places for mixing nature with small-town travel. [Fara San Martino](/europe/italy/abruzzo/majella/fara_san_martino) is famous for pasta and a dramatic gorge; [Caramanico Terme](/europe/italy/abruzzo/majella/caramanico_terme) is a spa town and walking base; [Grotta del Cavallone](/europe/italy/abruzzo/majella/grotta_del_cavallone) gives the mountains a theatrical underground side. Come with a car, sturdy shoes, and a willingness to let the weather decide your day.
