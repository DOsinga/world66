Maratea is the best [Basilicata](/europe/italy/basilicata) has to offer coastal travellers. Squeezed onto a narrow strip between mountains and the Tyrrhenian Sea, it is the region's only stretch of coastline — and it makes the most of it.

The [old town](/europe/italy/basilicata/maratea/maratea_old_town) is built on the slopes of Monte San Biagio, its narrow streets threading between 17th- and 18th-century buildings. The main sight is the church of Santa Maria Maggiore in the centre of the village, but the real pleasure is in wandering. Above it all, on the summit, stands an enormous statue of [Christ the Redeemer](/europe/italy/basilicata/maratea/cristo_redentore) — smaller than Rio's, but dramatically sited beside the ancient [Basilica of San Biagio](/europe/italy/basilicata/maratea/basilica_of_san_biagio).

The [port](/europe/italy/basilicata/maratea/porto_di_maratea) is the liveliest area, with shops, cafes and some good restaurants. Boat excursions run to the caves along the coast, including the small but spectacular [Grotta delle Meraviglie](/europe/italy/basilicata/maratea/grotta_delle_meraviglie). The best beaches — rocky, clear-watered — are found at [Acquafredda](/europe/italy/basilicata/costa_di_maratea/acquafredda), about 10 km north, and Marina di Maratea, 5 km south along the coastal road.
