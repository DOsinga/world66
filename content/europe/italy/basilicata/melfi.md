Melfi is a medieval town in northern [Basilicata](/europe/italy/basilicata), set at the foot of the extinct volcano Monte Vulture. It was a Lombard stronghold in the early Middle Ages and later became the first Norman capital in southern Italy — the impressive [Norman castle](/europe/italy/basilicata/melfi/castello_normano) above the town was where Robert Guiscard was proclaimed Duke of Apulia in 1059 and where Frederick II promulgated the Constitutions of Melfi in 1231, one of the most important legal codes of the medieval world.

The castle now houses the Museo Nazionale del Melfese, with finds from the region including a fine Roman sarcophagus. The old town below is compact and atmospheric, with narrow streets and a [cathedral](/europe/italy/basilicata/melfi/cathedral) rebuilt after the 1694 earthquake — though the medieval Romanesque bell tower survived. Melfi is also the gateway to the [Aglianico del Vulture](/europe/italy/basilicata/melfi/aglianico_del_vulture) wine district, one of southern Italy's best reds, produced in villages scattered across the volcanic slopes of Monte Vulture.
