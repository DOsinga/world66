Metaponto is a small coastal town in [Basilicata](/europe/italy/basilicata) where the Ionian Sea meets one of the richest Greek colonial landscapes in Italy. Founded in the 7th century BC by Greek settlers, it was here that Pythagoras spent the last years of his life after fleeing Croton. The town's archaeological legacy is exceptional — layers of Magna Graecia, Carthaginian, and Roman occupation are all visible.

The [Parco Archeologico](/europe/italy/basilicata/metaponto/parco_archeologico) preserves the remains of temples, a theatre, and the grid plan of the ancient city. The [Museo Archeologico Nazionale](/europe/italy/basilicata/metaponto/museo_archeologico) next door holds the finds from decades of excavation, including pottery, coins, and votive offerings. Both are about 3 km from the modern train station. A few kilometres further north, the [Tavole Palatine](/europe/italy/basilicata/metaponto/tavole_palatine) — fifteen columns of a 6th-century BC temple of Hera — stand in an open clearing and are the single most striking thing to see in the area. The [Lido di Metaponto](/europe/italy/basilicata/metaponto/lido_di_metaponto), the beach resort strip, is a completely separate settlement — pleasant enough in summer but with nothing of historical interest.
