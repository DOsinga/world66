Pietrapertosa is one of Basilicata's most dramatic villages, built directly under the jagged sandstone towers of the Lucanian Dolomites. From below, the houses seem to climb into the rock; from the top of the village, the view opens across a rough mountain landscape that feels much larger than this small corner of southern Italy.

The old Arabata quarter preserves the memory of Saracen settlement, with tight lanes and stone houses pressed under the castle rock. Above it are the remains of a fortress used by Saracens, Normans, and later feudal lords. The whole place is steep, stony, and slightly theatrical.

Pietrapertosa is often paired with nearby Castelmezzano, its equally striking neighbour across the valley. The two villages are linked in summer by the Volo dell'Angelo zipline, but walking the lanes and climbing to the castle are enough to justify the trip even if you keep both feet on the ground.
