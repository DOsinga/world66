Cosenza sits at the confluence of the Busento and Crati rivers, the largest city in northern Calabria and capital of its province. It was the chief city of the ancient Brutii before the Romans took it in 204 BC.

According to tradition, the Visigothic king Alaric I was buried in the bed of the Busento here around 410 AD, his tomb hidden by diverting the river. The [Castello Normanno-Svevo](/europe/italy/calabria/cosenza/castello_normanno_svevo), built by Emperor Frederick II, dominates the old town from the hilltop above — and the [Cathedral](/europe/italy/calabria/cosenza/cattedrale_di_cosenza) he attended at its 1222 consecration still holds the Byzantine Stauroteca he gifted it.

Modern Cosenza has a lively [centro storico](/europe/italy/calabria/cosenza/centro_storico) with good restaurants and bars, and a pedestrianised [Corso Mazzini lined with outdoor sculptures](/europe/italy/calabria/cosenza/corso_mazzini_sculpture) by Dalí, de Chirico, and other major 20th-century artists. Santiago Calatrava's [footbridge](/europe/italy/calabria/cosenza/calatrava_bridge) over the Busento is one of the most striking pieces of architecture in the region. The city is a natural gateway to the Sila mountains and the Pollino National Park.
