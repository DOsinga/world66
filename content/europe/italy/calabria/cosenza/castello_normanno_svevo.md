The Castello Normanno-Svevo dominates the hilltop above the old town, a Norman fortress substantially rebuilt by Emperor Frederick II in the 13th century. The views over the confluence of the Busento and Crati rivers — and the city spread across the valley below — justify the climb alone.

The castle is open to visitors and has been partially restored. The interior spaces include exhibition rooms and the old royal chambers, though much of the structure retains its spare medieval character. The surrounding area of the old town, with its steep lanes and medieval churches, is the most atmospheric part of Cosenza and deserves an unhurried hour or two.
