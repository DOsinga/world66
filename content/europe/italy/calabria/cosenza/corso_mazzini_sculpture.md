Corso Mazzini, Cosenza's main pedestrianised thoroughfare, doubles as an open-air sculpture museum — one of the largest in Italy. Works by Salvador Dalí, Giorgio de Chirico, Giacomo Manzù, and many other major 20th-century artists are placed along the full length of the corso, creating what amounts to a free-admission gallery in the open air.

The collection began in the late 1960s and has grown incrementally through acquisitions and donations. It now spans the full length of the pedestrian zone and includes bronze and stone works in a wide range of styles. It's an unexpectedly rich cultural resource for a city that receives few international visitors, and the contrast between the major artworks and the everyday life of the street — shoppers, schoolchildren, coffee drinkers — gives it a character no purpose-built museum could replicate.
