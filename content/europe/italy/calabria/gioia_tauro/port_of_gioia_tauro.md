  Gioia Tauro's port was originally planned as a steel mill in the 1970s as part of a 
  government development scheme for the Mezzogiorno. The steel plant was never completed —
  it fell victim to the economic crisis and the long shadow of the 'Ndrangheta, Calabria's
  organised crime network. The half-built industrial infrastructure was eventually repurposed
  as a container terminal, which opened in 1995 and grew into one of Europe's busiest transshipment 
  hubs, handling cargo for dozens of countries without it ever touching Italian roads.

The Port of Gioia Tauro is one of the largest container transshipment terminals in the Mediterranean, sitting on the Tyrrhenian coast just below the city. Container ships from Asia, the Americas, and Northern Europe exchange cargo here before it continues to its final destinations, making it one of the most strategically important ports in southern Europe.

The port itself is a working industrial facility, not a visitor attraction, but its sheer scale is impressive from the lungomare — cranes rise fifty metres above the waterline, and the constant movement of containerships against the backdrop of the Aeolian Islands and Sicily on clear days makes for a striking scene. The port's origins are deeply tied to postwar southern Italian politics: what is now a successful commercial operation began as an ill-fated industrial development scheme that never reached its original purpose.
