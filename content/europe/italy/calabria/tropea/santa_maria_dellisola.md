Santa Maria dell'Isola is the image that defines Tropea: a medieval church clinging to a tufa rock that juts into the Tyrrhenian Sea, connected to the main cliffs by a stone staircase cut into the hillside. The approach is part of the experience — you climb from the beach, ascending past flower-covered walls, emerging at a small terrace with one of the finest views on the Calabrian coast.

The interior of the church is modest — whitewashed walls, a simple nave, a few votive offerings — but it is the exterior and the setting that matter here. Come at sunset, when the light on the rock and the sea below is at its most dramatic, and the Aeolian Islands appear as blue shapes on the horizon. In summer this is crowded; arrive early in the morning or late afternoon to avoid the worst of it.
