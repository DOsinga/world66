Positano is the most photographed town on the Amalfi Coast — a cascade of pink, white, and terracotta houses tumbling down a steep hillside to a small beach and a waterfront where the church of Santa Maria Assunta stands with its majolica-tiled dome. The architecture photographs magnificently; the experience of being there in high summer, when the lanes are jammed with tourists, is less so.

The main beach (Spiaggia Grande) has sunbeds for rent and is always busy in summer. A ferry service connects Positano to Amalfi, Ravello, and the island of Capri. The town is built almost entirely on steps — there are no flat streets, only lanes and stairways descending to the sea. Boutiques selling the resort's famously coloured clothing line the Via Positanesi d'America. Positano is expensive; accommodation, food, and sunbeds all command a premium. Come in early May or late September for the best balance of weather and manageable crowds.
