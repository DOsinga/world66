Ravello perches 350 metres above the sea on a ridge between the valleys of the Dragone and Regina rivers, with views that drop straight down to the coastline and out across the Gulf of Salerno. It is the quietest and most aristocratic of the Amalfi Coast towns — too high for the day-trippers to bother with when there's a beach to reach — and it has attracted writers, composers, and wealthy visitors since the 19th century. D.H. Lawrence wrote part of Lady Chatterley's Lover here; Gore Vidal lived here for decades.

The two great gardens are the main sights. **Villa Rufolo** (13th century) has a Moorish cloister and terraced gardens that make up the stage for the summer music festival; the tower is open and the views from the upper terrace are exceptional. **Villa Cimbrone**, a 20-minute walk through the old town, has a longer garden walk that ends at the Terrazza dell'Infinito (Terrace of Infinity) — a balustrade lined with busts looking straight out over the sea at a height that makes the view genuinely vertiginous. The town has a good cathedral with a beautiful 12th-century ambo (pulpit).
