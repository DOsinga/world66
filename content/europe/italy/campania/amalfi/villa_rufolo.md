Villa Rufolo in Ravello is one of the oldest surviving private gardens in Italy, occupying a promontory with views straight down to the Gulf of Salerno. The complex was built in the 13th century by the Rufolo family and was later used by various kings and popes; by the 19th century it had fallen into near-ruin and was purchased by a Scottish businessman, Francis Neville Reid, who restored the gardens in 1853.

The main attraction is the terraced garden — wide lawns planted with palms, Dragon Blood trees, and roses — that cascades down to a platform with an uninterrupted view of the coastline. The platform forms the stage for the summer Ravello Festival concerts, when orchestras play against the backdrop of the sea. The Moorish cloister inside the villa is the most architecturally remarkable piece, with pointed arches and geometric inlay work that would not look out of place in Andalucia.
