The Amalfi Coast stretches about 50 kilometres along the southern side of the Sorrentine Peninsula, from [Positano](/europe/italy/campania/positano) in the west to Vietri sul Mare in the east. The coastal road, the SS163, is an experience in itself — carved into sheer cliffs with hairpin turns and views that make passengers forget all about the driving. It earned UNESCO World Heritage status not just for its beauty but as a cultural landscape, a place where terraced lemon groves, fishing villages, and Saracen watchtowers have grown into the rock over centuries.

[Positano](/europe/italy/campania/positano) is the most photogenic stop, its pastel houses tumbling steeply down to a grey-sand beach, all boutiques and bougainvillea. [Amalfi](/europe/italy/campania/amalfi) itself, once a powerful maritime republic that rivalled Venice and Genoa, has the grand striped cathedral of Sant'Andrea and a less frenetic pace once the day boats leave. [Ravello](/europe/italy/campania/ravello), perched 350 metres above the sea, is quieter still, with the gardens of Villa Rufolo and Villa Cimbrone offering some of the finest views on the coast — the kind that drew Wagner, Greta Garbo, and Gore Vidal to linger for years.

Beyond the famous three, smaller villages reward anyone willing to stop. Praiano keeps its fishing-village calm, Atrani sits in a fold of the cliffs barely a kilometre from Amalfi yet feels a world apart, and Furore hides a fjord-like inlet beneath its bridge. Walkers should make time for the Path of the Gods (Sentiero degli Dei), a high mountain trail between Bomerano and Nocelle with the entire coastline unfurling beneath your feet — arguably the best half-day this stretch of Italy offers.

Getting around takes patience. SITA buses run the full route and are cheap but packed in summer; ferries connect Positano, Amalfi, and [Salerno](/europe/italy/campania/salerno) from April to October and spare you the worst of the traffic. Driving is gorgeous but stressful — the road is narrow and tour buses claim most of it. A day trip from [Naples](/europe/italy/campania/naples) or [Sorrento](/europe/italy/campania/sorrento) is manageable, but staying a night lets you have the coast to yourself once the crowds wash back out in the evening. Come in May, June, or September if you can; July and August are beautiful but brutal.
