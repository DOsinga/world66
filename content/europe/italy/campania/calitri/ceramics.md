Calitri has been producing distinctive pottery for centuries — recognisable by its use of red and green glazes on a white slip, with geometric and floral patterns that draw on both Italian and Moorish decorative traditions. The forms are functional: plates, bowls, jugs, and storage vessels that were made to be used as well as displayed.

A handful of working ceramics studios in and around the borgo still make traditional pieces, and most will let visitors watch the production process. The quality varies — some workshops produce work of genuine craft distinction, while others have moved towards souvenir production. The best pieces are hand-thrown and hand-painted; look for the weight and irregularity that distinguish them from machine-made imitations.

Ceramics courses are available for visitors who want to try the craft. The local tourist office (or a hotel in town) can put you in contact with workshops that offer hands-on sessions, which typically run a few hours.
