Santa Maria del Soccorso stands on a promontory at the northern end of Forio's seafront, its whitewashed walls glowing against the blue of the sea. The church dates to the 14th century but has been rebuilt and extended several times since. The location alone would make it worth visiting: the terrace in front drops sharply to the sea on three sides, and on a clear day you can see all the way to the islands of Ponza and Ventotene on the horizon.

The interior is modest — a single nave with a painted wooden ceiling and walls covered in votive offerings left by sailors over six centuries. The real experience, though, is the terrace at dusk. A crowd gathers every evening in summer without any announcement or organisation, drawn by the same instinct that has been drawing people to this headland for generations. The bell rings, the sun drops, and for a few minutes everyone on the terrace is doing the same thing.
