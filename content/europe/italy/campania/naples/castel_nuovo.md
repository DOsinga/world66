story: >
  The white triumphal arch squeezed between the castle's two massive towers was added in 1453 to commemorate Alfonso I of Aragon's entry into Naples — the first Renaissance triumphal arch to be built since antiquity. The sculptors included Francesco Laurana, and the reliefs show Alfonso's procession through the city in extraordinary detail. It was inserted directly into an existing medieval gateway, the old arch literally framed by the new, as if Alfonso was marching through history itself.

The great medieval castle at the harbour — "New Castle" to distinguish it from the older Castel dell'Ovo — was built by Charles I of Anjou from 1279 and has been expanded, rebuilt, and modified by every subsequent dynasty. The current structure, with its five round towers and their black tufa stone, is largely 15th-century Aragonese. It anchors the waterfront between the ferry port and Piazza del Plebiscito and is one of the defining landmarks of Naples.

The interior contains the Civic Museum, with collections of bronzes, ivories, and paintings from the medieval and Renaissance periods. The Palatine Chapel retains original frescoes, and the tower rooms offer some of the best views over the Castel dell'Ovo and the bay. The triumphal arch of Alfonso I, inserted between the two towers facing the harbour, is one of the finest pieces of early Renaissance sculpture in Italy and repays close inspection. Open daily except Monday.
