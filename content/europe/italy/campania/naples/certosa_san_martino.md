  The certosa's collection includes the most important collection of Neapolitan presepi (nativity scenes) in the world — not the simple crib scenes of popular imagination but elaborate theatrical constructions with casts of hundreds, some of them set in detailed recreations of 18th-century Neapolitan streets, with miniature food vendors, soldiers, beggars, and the occasional devil lurking in a corner. The tradition of the Neapolitan presepe is recognised by UNESCO, and this museum is where it reaches its highest artistic expression.

A Carthusian monastery on the Vomero hill above Naples, founded in 1325, rebuilt in the 17th century, and converted into a museum in 1866 after Unification. The Certosa di San Martino now houses the Museo Nazionale di San Martino, with collections covering the history of Naples from the medieval period to the 19th century. The church interior is one of the most extreme examples of Neapolitan baroque in the city — every surface covered with marble, fresco, gilding, and sculpture.

The views from the monastery terrace are among the best in Naples — the full sweep of the bay from Vesuvius to Capo Posillipo. The cloister, designed by Cosimo Fanzago with skulls carved into the balustrade railing, is particularly striking. The museum is reached by the Funicolare Centrale from Via Toledo or by the Montesanto funicular from Piazza Montesanto.
