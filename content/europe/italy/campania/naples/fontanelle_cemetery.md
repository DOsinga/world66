A vast cave cemetery carved into the Capodimonte tuff rock in the Sanità district, containing the skeletal remains of an estimated 40,000 people — plague victims, cholera dead, and casualties from the various centuries of epidemic disease that struck Naples. The ossuarium was formed when bodies were tipped wholesale into the caves during the 17th and 18th centuries, and the bones remain largely as they were, in massive disordered piles filling the chambers.

The caves were used as an air-raid shelter during World War II and then sealed in the 1960s, reopening as a cultural site in 2010. They are now a recognised museum, entered from Via Fontanelle in the Sanità. The guided tour covers not just the bones themselves but the history of the extraordinary "cult of the dead" — the Neapolitan tradition in which ordinary families adopted individual skulls from the cave, gave them names, tended them, and prayed to them in exchange for supernatural assistance.
