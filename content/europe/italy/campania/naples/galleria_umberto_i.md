story: >
  The Galleria was built between 1887 and 1891 as part of the "risanamento" — the great urban renewal project that demolished the medieval slum quarters of Naples after a devastating cholera epidemic in 1884. Over 40,000 people were displaced. The glass-and-iron arcade replaced the worst of the alleys, and its grandeur was partly meant to announce that modern Naples had arrived. Within a generation, the surrounding streets were in much the same condition as the ones demolished.

A spectacular 19th-century shopping arcade a few steps from the Teatro San Carlo and Piazza del Plebiscito. Built from 1887 to 1891, the Galleria Umberto I has an octagonal central space beneath a soaring iron-and-glass dome — the floor is tiled in geometric patterns, the facades above are elaborate stucco and ironwork, and the scale is deliberately overwhelming. It is smaller but architecturally comparable to the Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II in Milan.

Today the galleria contains a mix of shops, cafes, and a bank. Many of the original commercial uses have changed but the space remains in daily use and is freely accessible. Come in the early evening when the light through the dome is at its most dramatic and Neapolitans are walking through on their passeggiata.
