title: "Books"

---

**My Brilliant Friend** by Elena Ferrante (2011) — the first of the four Neapolitan novels, following two girls growing up in a poor neighbourhood on the outskirts of Naples in the 1950s. The city is as much a character as the people, and Ferrante's Naples — violent, claustrophobic, occasionally ecstatic — is more real than most travel writing. The entire tetralogy rewards reading before a visit.

**The Volcano Lover** by Susan Sontag (1992) — a historical novel set in late-18th-century Naples, centred on Sir William Hamilton (the British envoy who collected volcanoes and Roman antiquities), his wife Emma, and Admiral Nelson. A serious meditation on aesthetics, power, and the culture of Bourbon Naples at its peak.

**Naples '44** by Norman Lewis (1978) — a British intelligence officer's diary of occupied Naples in 1943-44. The most unsentimental account of the city written in English and the best single book for understanding what the war did to Naples and what Naples did with the war.

**Christ Stopped at Eboli** by Carlo Levi (1945) — technically about Basilicata to the south, but Levi's account of the Italian south as a place abandoned by modernity is indispensable context for understanding Naples and the Mezzogiorno. The title's idea — that civilization ends south of Eboli — is the prejudice that Neapolitans have been fighting against for centuries.
