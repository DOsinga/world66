Naples' cathedral, properly called the Cathedral of the Assumption of Mary but universally known as the Duomo or the Cathedral of San Gennaro, stands on Via del Duomo in the heart of the historic centre. The building you see today is mostly 13th-14th century Gothic with a 19th-century neo-Gothic facade, but the site has been sacred since before the Christian era — excavations beneath the cathedral have revealed Greek and Roman structures going back to the 4th century BC.

The interior is enormous and contains several distinct chapels worth exploring. The **Cappella di San Gennaro** (Chapel of the Treasury), built from 1608 to 1637, is one of the finest examples of Neapolitan baroque anywhere, richly decorated with silver busts of saints and paintings by Domenichino and Jusepe de Ribera. The crypt beneath contains San Gennaro's relics. The adjacent Basilica di Santa Restituta is older than the main cathedral and preserves 4th-century baptismal fonts.
