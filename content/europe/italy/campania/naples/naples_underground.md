  The tunnels served as an air-raid shelter during World War II, when up to 4,000 people sheltered in them during Allied bombing raids. When the war ended, residents simply left — furniture, tools, toys, and household objects were abandoned in place and remain there today, buried in the rubble and debris that gradually filtered in over the following decades. Parts of the wartime shelter have been excavated and you can see the personal effects of the families who sheltered there.

Below the streets of the historic centre runs a labyrinth of tunnels, cisterns, and chambers that dates back to the Greek city of Neapolis in the 4th century BC. The Greeks quarried tufa stone from beneath the city to build it above, leaving an enormous network of voids that were later converted into a water cistern system serving most of Naples until the cholera epidemic of 1884. That same year the city council ordered the water system permanently sealed — and the underground was forgotten for nearly a century.

The main public access is through the Napoli Sotterranea tour, entering near Piazza San Gaetano on Via dei Tribunali. Guided tours of about 80 minutes take you through Greek cisterns, Roman drainage channels, sections of the wartime shelter, and passages narrow enough to require squeezing through sideways. The tours are in Italian and English and run several times daily. The temperature underground is a steady 14°C — bring a layer regardless of the season above.
