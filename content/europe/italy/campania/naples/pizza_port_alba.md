  Port'Alba is claimed by some as the site of the world's first pizzeria: Antica Pizzeria Port'Alba, which opened around 1738 as a street stall for the lazzaroni (the Neapolitan urban poor) and moved indoors with tables and chairs in 1830. The claim is debated — other establishments make similar ones — but it is old enough to make the argument plausible, and the current restaurant still operates from the same address.

The 17th-century gateway and the short street behind it, connecting Piazza Dante to the bookshops of the old university quarter. Via Port'Alba is one of the best-known streets in Naples for secondhand and antiquarian books — small shops spill their stock onto the pavement, and the atmosphere of dusty shelves and casual browsing has survived the digital age more completely here than almost anywhere in Italy.

The arch of Port'Alba itself was built in 1625, one of the original gateways in the old city walls. Through the arch and into Via Port'Alba the character changes from the market street of Piazza Dante to a quieter, more reflective atmosphere. The legendary pizzeria is at number 18; the bookshops begin almost immediately after.
