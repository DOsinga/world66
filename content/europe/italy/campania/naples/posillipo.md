The promontory of Posillipo stretches west from the city into the bay, lined with aristocratic villas, rock-cut Roman remains, and some of the most beautiful sea views in Naples. The name comes from the Greek Pausylypon — "relief from care" — and the area has been sought out by the wealthy as a retreat from the city since Roman times. Virgil is said to have owned a villa here, and there is a tomb on the promontory traditionally (if incorrectly) attributed to him.

The road along Posillipo, past villas and small harbours, leads to the descent into the Phlegrean Fields and the beaches of Bagnoli. In the evening the seafront restaurants and bars at the water's edge offer some of the best sunset views in the city. Funicolare connections from Piazza Amedeo in Chiaia reach the Vomero ridge above.
