The Spanish Quarter is a grid of steep narrow streets climbing from Via Toledo into the Vomero hill, built by the Spanish viceroy Don Pedro de Toledo in the 16th century to house his troops. Five centuries later it remains one of the most densely inhabited and intensely alive neighbourhoods in Naples — washing lines stretched between buildings four storeys up, shrines to Diego Maradona and San Gennaro in the street-corners, the smell of frying dough from basement kitchens, and the noise of everything compressed into streets barely wide enough for two people to pass.

The quarter has a reputation that is not entirely undeserved — it is one of the poorer parts of the city — but visitors on foot in daylight are extremely unlikely to have any trouble. The main reason to come is the atmosphere. Walk north from Via Toledo up any of the parallel alleys and follow your nose. The best street food in Naples is often found here: friggitorie (fry shops) selling cuoppi, pizza fritta, and crocchè from open windows.
