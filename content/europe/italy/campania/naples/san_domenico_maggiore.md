  Thomas Aquinas taught at the attached priory of San Domenico Maggiore for several years in the late 13th century, and a crucifix in the sacristy is said to have spoken to him during his studies here: "You have written well of me, Thomas. What reward would you have?" Aquinas answered: "None other than yourself, Lord." The sacristy also contains the caskets of thirty-five members of the Aragonese royal family, laid on shelves along the walls — a royal ossuary that is one of the most macabre and fascinating rooms in Naples.

The most important Gothic church in Naples, founded in 1283 and rebuilt in the 15th century on the square of the same name — Piazza San Domenico Maggiore, one of the most atmospheric squares in the historic centre. The church was the preferred place of worship of the Aragonese court and consequently filled with their tombs, monuments, and commissioned works.

The interior is a single enormous nave, 54 metres long, lined with funerary monuments and paintings by Giovanni da Nola, Caravaggio, and Titian. The sacristy contains the hanging coffins of the Aragonese royal family and an extraordinary collection of relics and vestments. The square outside, with its Obelisk of San Domenico (1737), is one of the social hubs of the centro storico — students from the nearby university fill the bars and the steps of the church.
