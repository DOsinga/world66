story: >
  Ferdinand I of the Two Sicilies built this church in fulfilment of a vow made in exile during the Napoleonic period: if he regained his throne, he would build a church to Saint Francis of Paola. He regained it in 1815 and had the church constructed between 1817 and 1846 — deliberately modelled on the Pantheon in Rome, to make the point that Bourbon Naples could match anything in the capital of a unified Italy that he opposed.
---

A massive neoclassical church closing the western side of Piazza del Plebiscito, modelled almost exactly on the Pantheon in Rome. The circular porticoed interior, with its coffered dome and marble floor, is one of the great interior spaces of Naples — especially impressive given how little-visited it is compared to the square in front of it. The colonnaded wings that curve out from the church to embrace the piazza were built at the same time and are part of the same architectural composition.

The church is still in active use and entry is free. Sunday morning mass draws a genuine congregation. The interior acoustics — as in the Pantheon — are extraordinary; any sound made in one part of the building travels intact across the full diameter.
