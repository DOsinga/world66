The Sanità is the working-class valley district north of the centro storico, separated from it by the Via Foria and the Capodimonte hill. For much of the 20th century it was considered one of the most dangerous parts of Naples; in the past decade it has undergone a remarkable, largely self-organised regeneration. Young people from the neighbourhood have restored churches, developed tourism around the catacombs and baroque palaces, and created a cooperative economy that has reduced the grip of the Camorra.

The district holds some of Naples' most extraordinary baroque architecture: the Palazzo dello Spagnolo with its famous double-ramp staircase, the Palazzo Sanfelice with another, and the Catacombs of San Gennaro entered from the Basilica di Madre del Buon Consiglio. The Sunday market on Via Arena alla Sanità is one of the least touristy markets in the city. Come in the morning when the light hits the stucco facades and the neighbourhood is about its daily business.
