story: >
  The star-shaped plan of Castel Sant'Elmo — six points radiating from a central core, designed so that every face could be defended from the ones adjacent to it — was drawn by the Spanish engineer Pedro Luis Escrivá in 1537. It is one of the first applications of the "trace italienne" defensive system in southern Italy, the revolutionary bastion fortification that made medieval curtain walls obsolete and changed the face of European warfare. The Spanish were simultaneously building similar forts across their American empire.

The star-shaped fortress dominating the Vomero hill above Naples, built by the Spanish in 1537 and used ever since as a military stronghold, prison, and now cultural centre. The views from the ramparts are the most panoramic in Naples — the entire city is laid out below, with Vesuvius rising behind it and the islands of the bay visible on clear days. The fortress is reached from the Certosa di San Martino next door, or from the Montesanto or Centrale funicolare.

The interior now houses contemporary art exhibitions and hosts events. The permanent collection includes historical maps and views of Naples. The best reason to come is simply the terrace and the prospect it offers — particularly impressive in the late afternoon when the light flattens across the bay.
