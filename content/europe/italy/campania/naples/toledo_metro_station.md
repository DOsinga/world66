  The Toledo station was designed by Óscar Tusquets Blanca and took over a decade to complete. The main feature — a deep shaft lined in mosaic tiles that shift from deep ocean blue at the bottom to bright white at the top, lit by a single oculus of natural light — was inspired by light diffusing through water. The artist William Kentridge contributed the war and peace mosaics on the walls. Euronews in 2013 named it Europe's most beautiful metro station.
---

The Toledo metro station on Line 1 descends 50 metres into the earth and is one of the most extraordinary pieces of civic architecture in Italy. The main escalator shaft is tiled entirely in blue and white mosaic work that gradually lightens as you ascend — an effect that CNN once called "the most beautiful metro station in Europe." It took 15 years to build and the art commissions drew on Spanish and Italian artists.

The experience of arriving at Toledo station — stepping off the train and looking up at the glowing shaft — is unlike anything else in the underground systems of Italy. The station is on the main shopping street of Toledo, the central artery of the city, so the contrast between the everyday commercial street above and the luminous space below is part of the point. Take the escalator slowly.
