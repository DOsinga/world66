  Via dei Tribunali is one of the two main east-west streets of the Greek city of Neapolis, laid out around 470 BC. The modern street follows the ancient decumanus exactly — the same width, the same alignment, the same line through the city. Two and a half thousand years of unbroken urban use, and the street is still doing what it was built to do.

The central artery of the historic centro storico, running from Piazza Bellini in the west to Piazza Garibaldi in the east along the course of the ancient Greek street grid. Via dei Tribunali is dense with churches, pizza shops, street food vendors, and the everyday life of a working Neapolitan neighbourhood that has never fully gentrified. The street takes its name from the medieval law courts (tribunali) that once lined it.

Walking the full length is one of the better ways to understand Naples. You pass the Duomo, several important baroque churches, the best pizza in the city (Sorbillo is here, at number 32), vendors selling fried pizza from carts, a nuns' convent whose turnstile dispenses pastries, and dozens of side alleys leading to churches, palaces, and courtyards. Allow at least two hours and don't hurry — the things worth seeing are often down the alleys rather than on the main street.
