The long public garden running along the seafront in Chiaia, laid out by Ferdinand IV in 1781 as a royal pleasure garden and opened to the public in 1888. The Villa Comunale stretches for nearly a kilometre between Piazza Vittoria and the Riviera di Chiaia, with a central avenue of palms, umbrella pines, and fountains. In the 19th century it was the setting for the passeggiata of fashionable Naples; today it is somewhat faded but still in daily use by dog-walkers, runners, and families.

Within the park sits the Stazione Zoologica Anton Dohrn, founded in 1872 as one of the world's first marine biology research stations and still in operation. Its public aquarium, the oldest in Europe, has tanks of Mediterranean sea life that date from the original 1874 installation. The combination of Victorian scientific ambition and the somewhat shabby grandeur of the park around it captures something essential about Naples.
