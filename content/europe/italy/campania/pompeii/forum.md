story: "When Vesuvius erupted on 24 August 79 AD, the Forum was the first place most Pompeians would have run to in panic. Archaeological evidence shows that many people were killed in the Forum colonnades trying to shelter from the initial shower of pumice. A second wave of pyroclastic surge killed those who had survived the first hours — the plaster casts on display nearby preserve the positions of people who died huddled against walls or with arms raised against the heat."

The civic and commercial heart of ancient Pompeii, surrounded by the remains of temples, market halls, and the basilica (law courts). The Temple of Jupiter dominates the north end with Vesuvius framed directly behind it — when you stand at the south end of the Forum and look north, the volcano rises behind the temple columns in a composition that still feels arranged. The Forum's paving stones show the ruts worn by generations of carts and wagons.

Plaster casts of eruption victims are displayed in a small storage building on the western side of the Forum — among the most affecting things at the site. Giuseppe Fiorelli developed the technique in the 1860s: volcanic ash hardened around the decomposed bodies, creating voids that Fiorelli filled with plaster to preserve the exact shapes of the dead. More than 100 casts have been made. The forum is usually the first major area reached from the Porta Marina entrance.
