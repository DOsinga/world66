Pozzuoli is a port city just west of Naples, and one of the most geologically strange places in Italy. The entire town sits within the Campi Flegrei — the Phlegraean Fields — a vast volcanic caldera that has been slowly rising and sinking for millennia. The process, called bradyseism, has lifted and submerged the ancient Roman city of Puteoli multiple times; the [Macellum of Pozzuoli](/europe/italy/campania/pozzuoli/macellum_of_pozzuoli), the old Roman market building, shows clear marine bore marks halfway up its columns from periods when the floor was completely underwater. Pozzuoli is built on a live volcano, and everyone knows it.

The Roman past is everywhere. The [Anfiteatro Flavio](/europe/italy/campania/pozzuoli/anfiteatro_flavio) is the third-largest Roman amphitheater in Italy after the Colosseum and the one in Capua, with unusually well-preserved underground passages. The [Rione Terra](/europe/italy/campania/pozzuoli/rione_terra) is the old rocky promontory where the ancient city was founded, now being slowly excavated to reveal Roman streets beneath the medieval and modern buildings. The [Pozzuoli Cathedral](/europe/italy/campania/pozzuoli/pozzuoli_cathedral), apparently a baroque church, is built directly over the remains of a Roman temple to Augustus.

To the northwest, the volcanic landscape opens up into some of the most dramatic terrain in the region. The [Solfatara](/europe/italy/campania/pozzuoli/solfatara) crater steams and hisses with sulphurous gases. [Lake Avernus](/europe/italy/campania/pozzuoli/lake_avernus), a perfectly round volcanic lake, was considered by the Romans to be the entrance to the underworld. [Cumae](/europe/italy/campania/pozzuoli/cumae), the first Greek colony in mainland Italy, sits on a cliff above the sea a few kilometres to the west. The whole area can be explored in a full day from Naples, but Pozzuoli also works well as a base in itself — it is less crowded and less expensive than central Naples, with a good fish market and a distinctly local character.
