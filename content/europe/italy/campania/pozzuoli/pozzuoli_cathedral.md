Pozzuoli Cathedral, officially the Basilica of San Procolo Martire, stands on the promontory of Rione Terra in the old heart of the city. The building visible today is largely the result of 17th-century reconstruction, but the interior preserves the structure of a 1st-century Roman temple to Augustus — the original six columns of the temple pronaos are still standing, incorporated into the nave walls, their fluted marble shafts rising to the full height of the interior.

The temple was converted to Christian use in late antiquity and has remained a place of worship continuously for sixteen centuries. The fire of 1964 and subsequent restoration made the Roman origins far more visible than they had been — years of plasterwork and baroque decoration were stripped away, revealing the ancient structure that had been hidden inside the church. It is one of the clearest examples in Italy of the physical continuity between Roman and Christian religious space.
