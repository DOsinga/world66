The Dolomites are among the most dramatic mountain landscapes in Europe — a compact section of the Eastern Alps where pale limestone towers, needles, and plateaux rise from green valleys in shapes that look more like science fiction than geology. UNESCO inscribed the Dolomites as a World Heritage Site in 2009, recognising not only their extraordinary scenery but the exceptional biodiversity and geological record they preserve. The core of the range spans roughly a hundred kilometres across the provinces of Bolzano, Trento, and Belluno, straddling three Italian regions: South Tyrol, Trentino, and the Veneto.

What makes the Dolomites distinctive is the rock itself: dolomia, a pale grey-white stone that has eroded over millions of years into forms found nowhere else. At sunrise and sunset, this rock glows orange, pink, and deep red in the phenomenon locals call *enrosadira* — a Ladin word that translates roughly as "turning rose." The best places to see it are the great plateau massifs — the Sella, the Marmolada, the Pale di San Martino — and the isolated towers of [Tre Cime di Lavaredo](/europe/italy/dolomites/tre_cime_di_lavaredo), the range's most photographed landmark. The valleys are equally beautiful: [Val Gardena](/europe/italy/dolomites/val_gardena) is Ladin-speaking and filled with woodcarvers; [Val di Fassa](/europe/italy/trentinoaltoadige/val_di_fassa) runs beneath the Catinaccio group; and the broad plateau of [Alpe di Siusi](/europe/italy/dolomites/alpe_di_siusi) is the largest high-altitude meadow in Europe, green in summer and white in winter.

The range has two main seasons. In winter, the Dolomiti Superski network connects twelve ski areas on a single lift pass — roughly 1,200 kilometres of piste across the central massif — making it one of the largest and most varied ski circuits in the world. [Cortina d'Ampezzo](/europe/italy/veneto/cortina_dampezzo), [Selva di Val Gardena](/europe/italy/dolomites/val_gardena), Canazei, and Arabba are the main resort towns. In summer, the same mountains become hiking, via ferrata, and cycling territory: the high passes — [Passo Giau](/europe/italy/dolomites/passo_giau), Passo Falzarego, Passo Sella, Passo Pordoi — are all passable by road and surrounded by trails. The WWI history adds another layer: the front passed directly through these mountains in 1915–18, and the tunnels, trenches, and galleries blasted into the rock at 2,000 metres are still accessible on hiking routes above Cortina and in the Cinque Torri area.

The Dolomites are reachable by train to Bolzano, Trento, or Belluno, with buses continuing into the valleys; or by car, which gives far more flexibility in the mountains. Accommodation ranges from glamorous hotels in Cortina to simple rifugi (mountain huts) at high altitude. The food follows local Alpine traditions — dumplings, speck, polenta, game, and strong cheeses — with regional variations between the Tyrolean north and the Venetian south.
