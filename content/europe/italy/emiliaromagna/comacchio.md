Comacchio is like a small [Venice](/europe/italy/veneto/venice), built on islands in a lagoon and connected by canals and bridges, roughly 30 km north of [Ravenna](/europe/italy/emiliaromagna/ravenna) at the southern edge of the Po Delta. The comparison to Venice is obviously hyperbolic — Comacchio is a small town of 20,000, not a city — but the visual logic holds: pale facades reflected in still water, boats moored at doorsteps, and the [Ponte dei Trepponti](/europe/italy/emiliaromagna/comacchio/ponte_dei_trepponti) at the northern entrance, a 17th-century bridge of extraordinary design with five stairways ascending from five different directions to a single central arch.

The town is built on the [Valle di Comacchio](/europe/italy/emiliaromagna/comacchio/valle_di_comacchio), one of the largest brackish lagoon systems in Europe and a habitat for eels since Roman times. Comacchio's defining product is the eel, caught in the surrounding wetlands and preserved for centuries using a marinating technique developed here: slow-cooked in vinegar and spices, then stored in terracotta vessels. The [Manifattura dei Marinati](/europe/italy/emiliaromagna/comacchio/manifattura_dei_marinati) — the old eel processing factory, now a working museum — is the most atmospheric building in town. The [Sagra dell'Anguilla](/europe/italy/emiliaromagna/comacchio/when_to_go) (Eel Festival) in October draws visitors from across Emilia-Romagna.

Beyond the lagoon and the eel, there are good Baroque churches — the [Cattedrale di San Cassiano](/europe/italy/emiliaromagna/comacchio/comacchio_cathedral) on the main square, the Church of Santo Rosario on Piazza del Popolo — and a genuinely unhurried atmosphere that makes Comacchio a pleasant base for exploring the Po Delta. The [Lidi di Comacchio](/europe/italy/emiliaromagna/comacchio/lidi_di_comacchio), 5 km to the east, offer wide sandy Adriatic beaches for a day's swimming.
