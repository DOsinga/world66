The Valle di Comacchio is a vast system of brackish lagoons, marshes, and reed beds stretching north and east of the town across the Po Delta, covering roughly 12,000 hectares. It is one of the most important wetland habitats in the Mediterranean, supporting enormous colonies of waterbirds — flamingos, herons, egrets, spoonbills, avocets — as well as the eels that have defined Comacchio's economy for centuries.

Boat tours of the lagoon run from the town landing in spring and summer, lasting one to two hours and passing through channels bordered by rushes and the traditional wooden fishing structures (lavorieri) that have caught eels here since medieval times. Cyclists can follow dedicated paths through the valley on flat, well-maintained tracks. The spectacle of flamingos feeding in the shallows with Comacchio's bell tower visible on the horizon is one of the most distinctive sights in Emilia-Romagna. Visit in spring for migrating birds or in autumn for the traditional eel season.
