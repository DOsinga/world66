Modena is best known for its industrial prowess, particularly in the Italian motor industry — Ferrari, Maserati, and Lamborghini all have roots here. But the old city in the centre is truly monumental, with one of Italy's finest Romanesque cathedrals and its impressive 88-metre [Ghirlandina bell tower](/europe/italy/emiliaromagna/modena/duomo_di_modena), both UNESCO World Heritage Sites. Modena is also the birthplace of balsamic vinegar and the late tenor Luciano Pavarotti.

This is Emilia-Romagna at its most prosperous and self-confident. The [Mercato Albinelli](/europe/italy/emiliaromagna/modena/mercato_albinelli), the city's magnificent covered food market, sells some of the finest produce in Italy. [Piazza Grande](/europe/italy/emiliaromagna/modena/piazza_grande) is the ceremonial heart of the city, flanked by the cathedral and the medieval Palazzo Comunale. The [Galleria Estense](/europe/italy/emiliaromagna/modena/galleria_estense) holds one of the finest Renaissance art collections in northern Italy, while the [Museo Enzo Ferrari](/europe/italy/emiliaromagna/modena/museo_enzo_ferrari) tells the story of Modena's most famous industrial son.

The local obsession with both cars and cuisine is entirely genuine. Massimo Bottura's [Osteria Francescana](/europe/italy/emiliaromagna/modena/osteria_francescana) has held three Michelin stars and topped the World's 50 Best list — and Modena's culinary ambition doesn't stop there. A day here barely scratches the surface.
