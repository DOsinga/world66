Parma is famous worldwide for two things: Parmigiano-Reggiano cheese and Prosciutto di Parma. But this elegant city in Emilia-Romagna has much more to offer than its culinary reputation suggests.

The old city centre is genuinely monumental, with one of Italy's finest Romanesque cathedrals and a baptistery covered in pink Verona marble that catches the light beautifully. Inside [the cathedral](/europe/italy/emiliaromagna/parma/parma_cathedral), Correggio's frescoed dome is a masterpiece of Renaissance illusionism, and the [octagonal baptistery](/europe/italy/emiliaromagna/parma/parma_baptistery) next door is one of the great transitional buildings between Romanesque and Gothic. The nearby [Palazzo della Pilotta](/europe/italy/emiliaromagna/parma/palazzo_della_pilotta) houses the [Galleria Nazionale](/europe/italy/emiliaromagna/parma/galleria_nazionale), with works by Correggio, Parmigianino, and Leonardo da Vinci, as well as the [Teatro Farnese](/europe/italy/emiliaromagna/parma/teatro_farnese), a stunning wooden theatre built in 1618. Don't miss the tiny [Camera di San Paolo](/europe/italy/emiliaromagna/parma/camera_di_san_paolo), whose ceiling Correggio decorated as an illusionistic pergola.

Parma is also known for its musical heritage. Giuseppe Verdi was born just outside the city, and the [Teatro Regio](/europe/italy/emiliaromagna/parma/teatro_regio) is one of Italy's most important opera houses. The city takes its music seriously -- attending a performance here, surrounded by a knowledgeable and demanding audience, is an experience in itself. Niccolò Paganini, the most famous violinist of his age, is buried [in the Villetta Cemetery](/europe/italy/emiliaromagna/parma/paganini_tomb) on the southern edge of town.

For food lovers, the area around Parma is pilgrimage territory. You can visit the dairies where Parmigiano-Reggiano is made and the curing houses where prosciutto hangs for months in the Apennine air. In the city, even a simple lunch of tortelli d'erbetta (pasta stuffed with ricotta and Swiss chard) will remind you why this region is considered Italy's gastronomic heartland.
