The Camera di San Paolo is a small square chamber inside the former convent of San Paolo, painted by Correggio in 1519 as the private dining room of the abbess Giovanna da Piacenza. The vaulted ceiling is a single illusionistic pergola — sixteen oval openings ringed with vine leaves, through which putti lean to peer down at the visitor.

It is one of Correggio's most charming works and, because the room is small and rarely full, easily one of the most rewarding 20-minute stops in Parma. Tickets are sold at the entrance to the cloister; combined visits with the adjacent Camera di Aracne are sometimes possible.
