Located on the Adriatic midway between [Venice](/europe/italy/veneto/venice) and [Florence](/europe/italy/tuscany/florence), Ravenna (pop. 136,000) is best known for the wonderful mosaic decoration of its churches and tombs — relics from its Byzantine past that have no equal in Western Europe.

Eight monuments built over a period of 1,500 years have been added to the UNESCO World Heritage List. The [Basilica of San Vitale](/europe/italy/emiliaromagna/ravenna/basilica_san_vitale) and the [Mausoleum of Galla Placidia](/europe/italy/emiliaromagna/ravenna/mausoleum_galla_placidia) contain the finest early Byzantine mosaics outside Constantinople itself — extraordinary gold and blue compositions, 1,500 years old and still vivid. The [Mausoleum of Theodoric](/europe/italy/emiliaromagna/ravenna/mausoleum_theodoric), built from a single 230-ton limestone slab, is an engineering marvel of a different kind. The [Baptistery of Neon](/europe/italy/emiliaromagna/ravenna/baptistery_of_neon) and the smaller [Arian Baptistry](/europe/italy/emiliaromagna/ravenna/arian_baptistry) round out the mosaic circuit.

Beyond the mosaics, [Dante's tomb](/europe/italy/emiliaromagna/ravenna/dantes_tomb) marks where the exiled poet died in 1321, and the [Basilica of San Francesco](/europe/italy/emiliaromagna/ravenna/basilica_san_francesco) contains a flooded crypt where goldfish swim over ancient mosaic floors. The city itself is charming, with a lively evening atmosphere around Piazza del Popolo, good restaurants drawing on Emilia-Romagna's celebrated food culture, and easy access to Adriatic beaches.
