title: "Things to Do"
type: section
---

Ravenna's eight UNESCO World Heritage monuments are the main draw. The mosaics in the [Basilica of San Vitale](/europe/italy/emiliaromagna/ravenna/basilica_san_vitale) and the [Mausoleum of Galla Placidia](/europe/italy/emiliaromagna/ravenna/mausoleum_galla_placidia) are among the finest in the world — shimmering gold and blue compositions that have survived largely intact since the 5th and 6th centuries. The [Mausoleum of Theodoric](/europe/italy/emiliaromagna/ravenna/mausoleum_theodoric), built entirely of Istrian stone and capped by a single 230-ton monolith, is striking in a completely different way.

The [Baptistery of Neon](/europe/italy/emiliaromagna/ravenna/baptistery_of_neon) and the smaller [Arian Baptistry](/europe/italy/emiliaromagna/ravenna/arian_baptistry) provide contrasting perspectives on early Christian art — the orthodox and the heretical — while the [National Museum](/europe/italy/emiliaromagna/ravenna/national_museum_ravenna), housed in the ex-Benedictine Monastery next to San Vitale, has an excellent collection of ivories, textiles, and archaeological fragments.

[Dante's tomb](/europe/italy/emiliaromagna/ravenna/dantes_tomb), a small neoclassical temple in the centre of town, marks where the poet was buried after dying in Ravenna in 1321. Next to it, the [Basilica of San Francesco](/europe/italy/emiliaromagna/ravenna/basilica_san_francesco) contains one of Ravenna's strangest sights: a flooded crypt where goldfish now swim over ancient mosaic floors.
