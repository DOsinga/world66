The oldest public library in Italy is housed in the Palazzo Gambalunga off Corso d'Augusto, and the eighteenth-century reading room is open to anyone who walks in. The collection runs to more than 200,000 volumes including illuminated manuscripts, sixteenth-century printed editions, and the city's main collection of Fellini's personal papers.

Free admission. Open Monday to Friday and Saturday morning. The atmosphere of the main hall — gilded wood, painted ceiling, library ladders — is reason enough for a quick visit.
