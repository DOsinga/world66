The Carso (Kras in Slovenian) is the limestone plateau that rises sharply behind Trieste, visible from most of the city and only 20 minutes by bus. It is a strange, dramatic landscape of sinkholes, caves, and scrubby Mediterranean vegetation — geologically distinct from the coastal plain and historically Slovenian rather than Italian in character.

Trieste is where speleology (cave science) was born. The Grotta Gigante, a short bus ride north of the city, is one of the world's largest tourist caves — a single chamber 100 metres high — and worth visiting for the sheer scale. Guided tours run regularly.

The Carso is also home to the *osmize* tradition: temporary farmhouse wine bars that open for a few days at a time, typically announced by a frond of fir attached to a post at the roadside. Families sell their own wine, prosciutto, and cheese at long shared tables on their terraces. The atmosphere is convivial and entirely local. Listings change constantly, but websites like osterie.net track current openings.
