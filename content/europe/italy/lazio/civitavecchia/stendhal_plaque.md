  Stendhal — Henri-Marie Beyle — was French consul in Civitavecchia from 1831
  until his death in 1842 and disliked the place intensely, calling it "the
  loneliest hole on earth". He nonetheless wrote *La Chartreuse de Parme* here
  in just 52 days in 1838, dictated in a tearing hurry. He died in Paris on a
  visit and is buried in Montmartre, but the plaque on this building is the
  only marker of the time he spent here.
---

A modest plaque on Via Aurelia Antica in the old quarter marks the house where the French novelist Stendhal lived and wrote *The Charterhouse of Parma* during his years as French consul in Civitavecchia. The building itself is unremarkable — just a small townhouse, not a museum — and there is nothing to see beyond the plaque.

But Civitavecchia is light on literary connections, and Stendhal is a serious one. A two-minute stop, in passing, en route between the cathedral and Piazza Leandra.
