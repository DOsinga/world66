Frascati is the most visited of the Castelli Romani hill towns, perched in the Alban Hills about 20 kilometres south-east of Rome. It has been a retreat from the city heat since ancient times — the Romans called the area *Tusculanum* — and the legacy is a string of Renaissance and Baroque villas with extraordinary gardens. Today it is best known for its white wine, [Frascati DOC](/europe/italy/lazio/frascati/frascati_doc_wine), which you can drink directly from the source.

The heart of town is [Piazza Marconi](/europe/italy/lazio/frascati/piazza_marconi_terrace), with long views down across the Roman plain. From here it is a short walk to the [Duomo](/europe/italy/lazio/frascati/frascati_cathedral), to [Villa Aldobrandini](/europe/italy/lazio/frascati/villa_aldobrandini) (the most spectacular of the hillside villas, designed by Giacomo della Porta in 1598), and to the backstreet [fraschette](/europe/italy/lazio/frascati/fraschette) where locals still fill demijohns from the barrel. The Porta Porticella market is a good spot to pick up bread, cheese, and cured meats for a picnic.

The best time to visit is during the Sagra dell'Uva in October, when the town celebrates the grape harvest with processions, free wine flowing through a fountain on the piazza, and general good cheer. But Frascati is worth a trip any time — it is an easy and very pleasant half-day from Rome by train.
