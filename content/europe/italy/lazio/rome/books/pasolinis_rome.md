Pasolini's first novel, published in 1955, follows a group of teenagers surviving on the margins of postwar Rome — in the shanty towns and borgate on the city's outskirts, worlds away from the Forum and the Colosseum. Written in Rome's street dialect, it was prosecuted for obscenity and made Pasolini's name simultaneously. It remains the most vivid portrait of the city's underside, and the neighbourhoods Pasolini described — Testaccio, Pietralata — still exist.
