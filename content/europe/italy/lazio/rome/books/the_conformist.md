Moravia's 1951 novel follows a man who becomes a fascist informer and assassin in an attempt to be normal — to conform to the society that his troubled childhood has left him outside. Set partly in Rome and partly in Paris, it is one of the most penetrating accounts of fascism as a psychological state rather than an ideology. Bernardo Bertolucci's 1970 film adaptation, shot in Rome, is equally essential.
