Robert Graves's 1934 novel, narrated by the Emperor Claudius, brings the first century of imperial Rome to ferocious life: the murderous politics of Augustus's court, the madness of Caligula, the scheming of Livia, and the corruption that ran through every institution of the Roman state. It reads as vividly as any thriller and the research behind it is serious. Walking through the Forum or the Palatine Hill after reading it, you will see the stones differently — not as ruins but as the setting for genuinely extraordinary events.
