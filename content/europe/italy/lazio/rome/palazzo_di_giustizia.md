The Palazzo di Giustizia is hard to miss on the Tiber: a huge neoclassical courthouse whose bulk earned it the Roman nickname *Il Palazzaccio*, the Ugly Palace. It was built on difficult alluvial ground in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, and stories about sinking foundations and construction scandals have followed it ever since.

You come here more for the outside than the legal business inside. Walk past it on the way between Prati, Castel Sant'Angelo, and Piazza Navona, and give yourself a moment to take in the overblown carvings, columns, and sheer weight of the thing.
