Pasolini's 1955 debut novel is set in the borgate — the shantytown suburbs of postwar Rome where the poor from the south had been dumped during the Fascist period. His portrait of street children, small-time crime, and casual violence in the rubble-strewn outskirts is as far from the Eternal City of monuments and piazzas as it is possible to get. It is also one of the most vivid accounts of any 20th-century European city by someone who genuinely knew it. Pasolini was later murdered in Rome; the circumstances remain disputed.
