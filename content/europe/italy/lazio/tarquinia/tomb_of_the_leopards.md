  The Tomb of the Leopards dates to around 480–450 BC and is named for the two confronted leopards painted above the main banquet scene. The three reclining banqueters below them — shown drinking, eating, and being attended by servants — are depicted with a vivacity and naturalism unusual for the period. The reds, blacks, and ochres of the original pigment have survived remarkably well for 2,500 years.
---

The Tomb of the Leopards is one of the most celebrated of Tarquinia's painted tombs, and with good reason: the main chamber preserves a complete cycle of Etruscan banqueting frescoes in almost perfect condition. The painting style is confident and expressive, showing banqueters, musicians, and dancers against a background of stylised trees, all observed through glass panels set into the tomb entrance.

Like all the accessible tombs in the Monterozzi Necropolis, visits are brief — the chambers are small and the viewing is through glass — but the quality of the painting makes a strong impression. The tomb is usually included in the rotation of open tombs and is one of the highlights of the necropolis visit.
