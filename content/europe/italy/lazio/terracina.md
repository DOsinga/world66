Terracina is a coastal town in southern Lazio, halfway between Rome and Naples along the old Via Appia. It splits into two parts: a modern seaside town along the beach, and the atmospheric upper town (centro storico) built over ancient Roman ruins on the hillside above.

The highlight is the [Temple of Jupiter Anxur](/europe/italy/lazio/terracina/temple_of_jupiter_anxur) on Monte Sant'Angelo, above the old town. The terrace of the ruined temple has sweeping views over the Tyrrhenian Sea and the Pontine Plain — on a clear day you can see the islands of Ponza. Down in the old town, the main piazza sits directly on top of the ancient Roman forum, the stonework still visible below; the [Terracina Cathedral](/europe/italy/lazio/terracina/terracina_cathedral) incorporates ancient Roman columns into its portico.

The beach below is long and sandy, popular with Roman day-trippers in summer. Terracina is less touristy than the Amalfi Coast and makes a pleasant stop on the coastal route between Rome and Naples.
