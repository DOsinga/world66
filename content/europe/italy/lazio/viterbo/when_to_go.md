---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

Viterbo sits at 300–400 metres above sea level on a volcanic plateau, which makes it noticeably cooler and less humid than Rome in summer — a genuine relief. Spring (April–June) and autumn (September–October) are the best times to visit: mild temperatures, clear light on the stone, and the surrounding countryside at its most beautiful.

July and August are warm but manageable; the city is far less crowded than Rome or the Tuscan towns at the same latitude, and the nearby lakes provide relief from the heat. Winter is cold and quiet; the medieval streets can be atmospheric in low season but some restaurants and attractions reduce their hours.

**Macchina di Santa Rosa** (3 September): the most important event in Viterbo's calendar and one of the great popular spectacles of central Italy. Each year a massive illuminated tower (the *macchina*), now standing over 30 metres tall, is carried through the streets on the shoulders of about 100 men (*facchini*) in a procession celebrating the city's patron saint. The event takes place at night, with the tower lit by thousands of candles and electric lights. It is genuinely spectacular and worth planning a trip around; book accommodation weeks in advance.

**Easter and Holy Week** draw significant numbers of pilgrims to the cathedral and the Papal Palace. The **Feast of Corpus Christi** (June) sees elaborate floral carpets laid through the streets of the old town.
