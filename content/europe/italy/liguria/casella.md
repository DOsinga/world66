Casella is a small hill town in the Scrivia Valley above Genoa, known mainly as the terminus of the Genova-Casella railway — a narrow-gauge line that winds up through chestnut woods and small valleys from Genoa's Piazza Manin, a 50-minute ride that feels entirely out of step with the modern city it departs from. The railway has been running since 1929 and the rolling stock still has wooden interiors. It is a weekend excursion for Genoese, particularly families in summer, and the combination of the train ride and a long lunch at one of the hilltop restaurants makes for one of the best days out from Genoa.

The town's food is the main draw: wild mushrooms from the surrounding woods in autumn, hand-made ravioli stuffed with borage and ricotta, and the local Polcevera white wine. Several trattorias on the edge of the village have been feeding Genoese Sunday excursionists for generations. From Casella the road continues up the valley to Montoggio and Torriglia, making it a good base for a half-day exploration of the upper valley.
