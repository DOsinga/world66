The Church of San Pietro is the centrepiece of Corniglia's main square and the finest piece of architecture in the village. Built in 1334 in the Genoese Gothic style, it is modest in scale but precise in detail. The facade is faced with white and dark green marble in alternating bands — a Genoese signature seen in cathedrals across Liguria. The rose window above the portal is particularly beautiful: an intricate wheel of carved marble tracery that survived centuries of salt air and coastal earthquakes.

Inside, the church is simple and cool. There are a few old paintings, some decorated tiles, and the calm atmosphere of a building that has served the same small community for nearly seven centuries. Mass is still held here. The square outside, Largo Taragio, is the natural gathering place of the village — a good spot to sit with a glass of local wine and watch life go by.
