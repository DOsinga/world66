Genoa is Italy's most underrated city. While tourists crowd Florence and Venice, Genoa goes quietly about its business — a working port that also happens to have the largest medieval city centre in Europe, a cluster of Renaissance palaces that can match any in Italy, and a food culture built around pesto, focaccia, and fresh fish that the rest of the world has been trying to replicate ever since.

The city was born as a sea republic. From the 11th to the 18th centuries, Genoese merchants and admirals operated across the known world — from the Black Sea to the Atlantic — and the wealth they brought back is written into the city's stones. The [caruggi](/europe/italy/liguria/genoa/caruggi) — those impossibly narrow lanes between tower-tall houses — lead to sudden piazzas fronted by grand palaces. The [Strade Nuove (Via Garibaldi)](/europe/italy/liguria/genoa/via_garibaldi), lined with the homes of the old banking families, are now a UNESCO World Heritage site. [Palazzo Bianco](/europe/italy/liguria/genoa/palazzo_bianco), [Palazzo Rosso](/europe/italy/liguria/genoa/palazzo_rosso), and [Palazzo Tursi](/europe/italy/liguria/genoa/palazzo_tursi) together hold one of the great painting collections of northern Italy.

Christopher Columbus was born here, probably around 1451 — [Porta Soprana](/europe/italy/liguria/genoa/porta_soprana), the twin-towered medieval gate, stands near the house traditionally associated with his boyhood. Niccolò Paganini learned his violin in the [Pré](/europe/italy/liguria/genoa/pre_neighbourhood) neighbourhood — his Guarneri violin is preserved at [Palazzo Tursi](/europe/italy/liguria/genoa/palazzo_tursi). Renzo Piano, who redesigned the waterfront for the 1992 Expo, still considers it home. The [Acquario di Genova](/europe/italy/liguria/genoa/acquario_di_genova) — Europe's largest aquarium — is the centrepiece of that [Porto Antico](/europe/italy/liguria/genoa/porto_antico) waterfront, alongside the [Neptune Galleon](/europe/italy/liguria/genoa/neptune_galleon) and the [Galata Museo del Mare](/europe/italy/liguria/genoa/galata_museo_del_mare).

Don't miss the [Cimitero Monumentale di Staglieno](/europe/italy/liguria/genoa/staglieno_cemetery) — one of the greatest cemeteries in the world, with funerary sculptures of uncanny realism. And [Piazza De Ferrari](/europe/italy/liguria/genoa/piazza_de_ferrari), the monumental heart of the city, is the natural meeting place and starting point for any visit.

Genoa is not a polished tourist destination. Parts of it are rough. The caruggi at night feel genuinely medieval. But that rawness is exactly the point: this is a city that has been living its own life for a thousand years and doesn't particularly need your approval. Come here instead of heading straight down the coast — you'll thank yourself.
