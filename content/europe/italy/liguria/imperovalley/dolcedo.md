Dolcedo sits on a tributary valley south of the main Impero route, a well-preserved medieval village known for its ancient bridges — seven of them span the Prino stream within or near the village, the oldest dating to the 13th century. The village itself is a tight cluster of ochre and terracotta buildings connected by narrow covered passages and external staircases, with the church of San Tommaso at its centre.

The surrounding countryside is classic western Ligurian olive territory: terraced hillsides, dry-stone walls, and old mills that once pressed the local oil. Dolcedo's oil has a long reputation and the village still has working producers. A small market takes place periodically in the village square, and the walk along the stream to the medieval bridges is pleasant in any season.

Dolcedo makes a good detour on the way up toward Lucinasco and the upper valley; it adds perhaps 30 minutes to the drive but has more architectural character than some of the larger villages.
