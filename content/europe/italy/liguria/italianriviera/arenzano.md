Arenzano is a small seaside town on the Ligurian coast west of Genoa, squeezed between the mountains and the sea. Together with neighbouring Cogoleto, it stretches along the coast between Cape Invrea and the cliffs of the Lastroni, where a distinctive rock called the "Aguggia" marks the landscape. [Punta San Martino](/europe/italy/liguria/italianriviera/arenzano/cape_san_martino) divides the two towns, with the small valley of the Lerone stream running behind it.

The steep hillsides rising directly from the water give the coastline a dramatic feel. Historically the area was quarried — copper and stone at Arenzano, lime at Cogoleto — though today the town is better known as a quiet beach getaway for Genoese looking to escape the city for a day. The beach at Vesima, just to the east, is a popular spot framed by Cape Passera and the Aguggia rock.

In the centre, the [Santuario di Gesù Bambino di Praga](/europe/italy/liguria/italianriviera/arenzano/santuario_gesu_bambino) draws pilgrims year-round, and the wooded grounds of [Villa Negrotto Cambiaso](/europe/italy/liguria/italianriviera/arenzano/villa_negrotto_cambiaso) make a pleasant break from the seafront. Behind the town the [Parco del Beigua](/europe/italy/liguria/italianriviera/arenzano/beigua_park), a UNESCO Geopark, climbs sharply into mountain country with serious hiking and views as far as Corsica on a clear day.
