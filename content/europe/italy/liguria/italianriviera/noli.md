Noli is one of the Italian Riviera's best-kept secrets — a medieval fishing village tucked east of the dramatic [Capo Noli](/europe/italy/liguria/italianriviera/noli/capo_noli), where the Ligurian coastline is at its most rugged. Once a powerful maritime republic (one of only five in Italian history, alongside Venice, Genoa, Amalfi, and Pisa), it still carries that history in its towers, narrow lanes, and the remarkable [Church of San Paragorio](/europe/italy/liguria/italianriviera/noli/san_paragorio).

The [old town](/europe/italy/liguria/italianriviera/noli/noli_old_town) is a compact maze of caruggi (narrow alleys) lined with stone houses, many still bearing the red-brick towers that were status symbols in the Middle Ages. The [beach](/europe/italy/liguria/italianriviera/noli/noli_beach) is small but pretty, and the water quality has earned Noli the European Blue Flag year after year. Above the town, the ruins of [Monte Ursino castle](/europe/italy/liguria/italianriviera/noli/monte_ursino_castle) offer sweeping views along the coast.

The wooded hills behind Noli make for excellent walking, and the GeoTrek Noli trail connects geological points of interest with sea views. Just offshore, [Nemo's Garden](/europe/italy/liguria/italianriviera/noli/nemos_garden) — an underwater farm in transparent biospheres — is one of the most genuinely surprising sights on the Riviera. It is a quieter, more authentic alternative to the better-known Cinque Terre — the kind of place where fishermen still mend nets on the beach and the restaurants serve whatever came in that morning.
