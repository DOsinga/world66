The Riviera delle Palme — the Riviera of Palms — stretches along the Ligurian coast southwest of Savona, running from Varazze down toward the border with the Riviera dei Fiori. It takes its name from the palm-lined promenades that front the beach towns, a feature shared with the French Côte d'Azur just over the hills. The climate is mild year-round, the hills immediately behind the coast are steep and wooded, and the narrow coastal plain is packed with holiday towns, fishing villages, and market-garden terraces.

[Alassio](/europe/italy/liguria/italianriviera/rivieradellepalme/alassio) and [Albenga](/europe/italy/liguria/italianriviera/rivieradellepalme/albenga) are the main centres. Alassio is the classic resort, with a long sandy beach and a lively waterfront; Albenga is older and more serious, with a well-preserved medieval core and Roman origins — the cathedral baptistery dates to the 5th century. Inland, the valleys push up into the Ligurian Apennines, where villages like [Toirano](/europe/italy/liguria/italianriviera/rivieradellepalme/toirano) sit above cave systems, and [Castelbianco](/europe/italy/liguria/italianriviera/rivieradellepalme/castelbianco) produces a local DOC wine.

The region is known for its food. [Farinata](/europe/italy/liguria/italianriviera/rivieradellepalme/farinata) — the chickpea flatbread baked in a wood-fired oven — is the quintessential Ligurian street food, and the area's artichokes and truffles are prized by cooks throughout northwest Italy.
