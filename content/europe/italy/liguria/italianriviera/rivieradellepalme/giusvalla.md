Giusvalla is a small village on the northern slopes of the Ligurian Apennines, perched above the right bank of the Giusvalletta, a tributary of the Valla. At around 500 metres above sea level, it sits in a quiet fold of the hills where farmland gives way to chestnut and oak forest — the kind of inland Ligurian landscape that most visitors to the coast never reach. The village is compact and unhurried, with the feel of a place that has changed slowly over many centuries.

The history here runs deep. A fortified settlement existed on the heights above the village as early as the tenth century, and by 1142 the castle had become a fief of the Del Carretto, one of the dominant noble families of the Ligurian hinterland. The [castle ruins](/europe/italy/liguria/italianriviera/rivieradellepalme/giusvalla/castle_ruins) that survive today — thick stone walls above the village — are a reminder of the long tug of feudal power that shaped this corner of the Apennines, passing through the hands of the marquises of Ponzone and Monferrato before the territory became part of the Kingdom of Sardinia in 1735.

The [church of San Matteo](/europe/italy/liguria/italianriviera/rivieradellepalme/giusvalla/chiesa_san_matteo) is the other focal point, a modest stone parish church whose thirteenth-century holy-water stoup is the oldest dateable object in the village. Giusvalla is not a destination for sightseeing in the conventional sense — it rewards those who simply want to walk the surrounding hills, breathe the Apennine air, and find a corner of Liguria that tourism has largely passed by.
