The parish church of San Nicolò, built in 1570, stands at the centre of the village and serves as its civic anchor. The building is a plain counter-Reformation structure, typical of the small Ligurian hill towns that could not afford the decorative excesses of coastal Baroque — which is part of its austere appeal.

The church's dedication to Saint Nicholas, patron of sailors and children, reflects the broader Ligurian tradition that extended even this far inland, where the sea was two valleys away but its influence shaped religious life. The interior contains modest furnishings from the 17th and 18th centuries, and the bell tower is visible from the upper paths approaching the village.
