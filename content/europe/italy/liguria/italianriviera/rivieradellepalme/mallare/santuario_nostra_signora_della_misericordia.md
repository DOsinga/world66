At the hamlet of Eremita, a short walk below the village, the Baroque shrine of Nostra Signora della Misericordia occupies a site with far older religious significance. The sanctuary was built on the foundations of a pagan temple — the exact nature of the earlier cult is unknown — and was later enlarged and remodelled during the Baroque period, probably in the 17th or 18th century.

The resulting building mixes the original medieval core with later Baroque decoration in a way that is neither fully one thing nor the other, which gives it a slightly odd but genuinely atmospheric quality. It is a place of local pilgrimage, particularly on the feast day of Our Lady of Mercy. The hamlet of Eremita itself — the name means "hermitage" — is little more than a cluster of stone houses, and the walk out from Mallare takes about twenty minutes along the valley road.
