Rocca di Perti is a long limestone ridge that rises behind Feglino, the principal climbing wall of the Finalese and one of the busiest crags in Italy. A summit cross marks the high point at about 480 metres, reached by a marked path in roughly an hour from the village — a fine walk in its own right, with views across the Riviera and inland to the Alps on a clear day.

For climbers it is a sport-climbing playground: hundreds of bolted single-pitch routes from easy slabs to overhanging hard lines, all on grippy grey and orange limestone. Spring and autumn are the seasons; summer is too hot on the south-facing sectors. The cliffs are owned by the comune and access is free.
