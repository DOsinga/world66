Sassello sits in a green valley on the northern slope of the Ligurian Apennines, surrounded by woods and pastures and partly within the [Parco del Beigua](/europe/italy/liguria/italianriviera/rivieradellepalme/sassello/parco_del_beigua). It is a quiet, attractive hill town well off the coastal tourist trail, known above all for its [amaretti](/europe/italy/liguria/italianriviera/rivieradellepalme/sassello/amaretti) — soft macaroons made to a centuries-old recipe that remain the town's most famous product.

The town has a turbulent past, having been fought over by Genoa, Savoy, Spain, and Austria. Ruins of the [Doria fortifications](/europe/italy/liguria/italianriviera/rivieradellepalme/sassello/fortifications) — the Bastia Inferiore on a hilltop and the Bastia Superiore near town — survive from the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries. The [church of Santa Trinità](/europe/italy/liguria/italianriviera/rivieradellepalme/sassello/santa_trinita) (1654) has frescoes by Bruschetto, and the oratory of the Disciplinati contains a wooden sculpture group by the Genoese master Maragliano.

The [Museo Perrando](/europe/italy/liguria/italianriviera/rivieradellepalme/sassello/museo_perrando) houses archaeological finds from the area alongside a good collection of seventeenth- and eighteenth-century pottery from Savona and a gallery of paintings.
