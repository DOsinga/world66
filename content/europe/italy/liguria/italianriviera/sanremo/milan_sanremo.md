Milan–Sanremo, held each year in mid-March, is the earliest Monument of professional cycling and one of the sport's most beloved one-day classics. The race starts in Milan and finishes in Sanremo after roughly 300 kilometres — the longest one-day race on the calendar. It is nicknamed "La Classicissima" and has been won by Eddy Merckx seven times.

The decisive moment almost always comes on the Poggio di San Remo, a short but steep climb just outside town that the race crosses in the final five kilometres. You can ride the Poggio yourself on any day of the year — it is a 4-kilometre ascent from the coastal road, with the finish in Sanremo visible below. On race day in March, the route through town is lined by large crowds and the atmosphere is among the best in Italian sport. The finish is on Via Roma, a short walk from the town centre.
