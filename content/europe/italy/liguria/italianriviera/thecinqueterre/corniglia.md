Corniglia is the oddity of the Cinque Terre: it perches on a 100-metre-high cliff above the sea rather than sitting at the water's edge. To reach it from the train station you must climb the Lardarina, a zigzagging staircase of 377 steps cut into the hillside (or take the bus that sometimes runs). The effort is rewarded with a village that sees fewer visitors than the others, narrow lanes without souvenir shops, and terrific views from the belvedere at the cliff's edge.

The village has only a few hundred residents and a handful of bars and trattorie. There is no harbour and no swimming beach directly below (though rocky coves are accessible by a path down the cliff). What Corniglia has is atmosphere: church bells, cats on windowsills, and the quiet that the more accessible villages have largely lost. The gelato bar at the end of the main street is legendary.
