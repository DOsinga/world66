Manarola is one of the oldest of the Cinque Terre villages and one of the most photographed, particularly at sunrise when the pastel-coloured houses reflect in the harbour water. The village clusters up a narrow rocky inlet; the houses are tall and tightly packed, and a single main street runs down to the small boat harbour. The Church of San Lorenzo, dating from 1338, stands at the top of the village.

Swimming is good at Manarola: the rocks on either side of the harbour entrance are popular, and the water is clear and deep. The Via dell'Amore path runs north from here to Riomaggiore, reopened in 2024 after years of closure following landslide damage. The hillside vineyards above the village produce Sciacchetrà, the sweet dessert wine that is the Cinque Terre's most distinctive product, made in such small quantities that much of it is consumed locally.
