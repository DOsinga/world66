Lavagna sits on the eastern bank of the Entella river, directly across from [Chiavari](/europe/italy/liguria/italianriviera/tigullio/chiavari) and effectively its quieter twin. The town developed along a main street running perpendicular to the shore — typical of Ligurian coastal settlements that grew to service a harbour rather than a beach.

The [harbour](/europe/italy/liguria/italianriviera/tigullio/lavagna/porto_di_lavagna) is Lavagna's chief asset today: a large, well-equipped marina popular with sailors working the Ligurian coast. The town's economic life revolves around tourism and the sea, and it has a relaxed, unpretentious feel compared to the busier resorts along the gulf.

The main historic monument is the [Chiesa di Santo Stefano](/europe/italy/liguria/italianriviera/tigullio/lavagna/chiesa_di_santo_stefano), originally medieval but rebuilt in 1650 by the architect Gio Batta Ghiso. In the Piazza della Libertà, the Baroque Palazzo Franzone — now the town hall — is worth a look for its ornate facade. Up in the hills above town, the [Basilica dei Fieschi](/europe/italy/liguria/italianriviera/tigullio/lavagna/basilica_dei_fieschi) is the most rewarding historical detour in the area — a beautifully preserved 13th-century Romanesque-Gothic church built by a pope's family.
