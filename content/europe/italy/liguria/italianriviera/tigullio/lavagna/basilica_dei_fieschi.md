The Basilica di San Salvatore dei Fieschi stands in the hamlet of San Salvatore, a short drive up into the hills above Lavagna in the neighbouring commune of Cogorno. It is a Romanesque-Gothic church built in the 13th century on the orders of the Fieschi family, one of the most powerful noble families in medieval Liguria — they produced two popes (Innocent IV and Adrian V) and numerous cardinals and bishops.

The church itself is built of alternating black and white stone in the Ligurian Romanesque style, and it is considerably better preserved than most medieval churches of its age. The interior retains original stonework and some early frescoes. Adjacent to the church stands the Palazzo dei Fieschi, a surviving piece of the medieval complex. The whole ensemble sits in a small piazza that feels genuinely unchanged — a rare thing in Liguria, where coastal development has transformed most of the towns. The basilica is about 4 km from central Lavagna by road and well worth the detour.
