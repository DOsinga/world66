La Spezia is the main transport hub for the Cinque Terre, the string of five cliff-hugging villages that draw visitors from across the world. The city itself is a working port with a gritty, unpretentious character that most travellers overlook in their rush to catch the train north. That's their loss — the old centre around [Via del Prione](/europe/italy/liguria/laspezia/via_del_prione) has good restaurants, a lively market, and a relaxed evening passeggiata.

The [Naval Museum](/europe/italy/liguria/laspezia/naval_museum) is exceptional — one of the best in Italy and the main reason to budget more than a transit stop here. The [Castello di San Giorgio](/europe/italy/liguria/laspezia/castello_san_giorgio) on the hill above town has good views and an archaeological museum with a portrait that locals insist is Julius Caesar. The [Mercato Centrale](/europe/italy/liguria/laspezia/mercato_centrale) on Piazza Cavour is the best place to eat cheaply and well: grab farinata or a cinque e cinque sandwich from a focacceria.

The [Cinque Terre access](/europe/italy/liguria/laspezia/cinque_terre_access) couldn't be easier — trains from La Spezia Centrale run every 30 minutes to all five villages. Buy a Cinque Terre Card at the station if you plan to hike or take multiple trains. Ferries from [Porto Mirabello](/europe/italy/liguria/laspezia/porto_mirabello) also run to the villages and to [Porto Venere](/europe/italy/liguria/laspezia/portovenere) at the southern tip of the gulf.
