The Riviera dei Fiori — the Riviera of Flowers — is the westernmost stretch of the Italian Riviera, running from [Ventimiglia](/europe/italy/liguria/ventimiglia) on the French border to roughly Andora in the east. The name comes from the carnations, roses, and mimosa grown on the terraced hillsides above the coast, which have supplied the flower markets of northern Europe since the nineteenth century. Drive inland from any of the coastal towns in spring and the terraces are bright with colour.

[Sanremo](/europe/italy/liguria/sanremo) is the undisputed capital of the western Riviera — a proper resort city with a grand casino, a hillside old town (La Pigna) of steep lanes and medieval arches, and a seafront promenade that still has something of its Belle Époque glamour. It is best known internationally for the Sanremo Music Festival, the Italian pop competition held here each February whose winners go on to represent Italy at Eurovision. The Russian Orthodox church on the Corso Imperatrice is a reminder of the Russian aristocracy who spent winters here before 1917. [Imperia](/europe/italy/liguria/imperia) is a quieter provincial capital made up of two towns — Oneglia, built around olive oil, and Porto Maurizio, built on a headland with a large Baroque cathedral. The olive oil produced from Taggiasca olives in the Imperia hinterland is among the finest in Italy: pale, delicate, and best drizzled rather than cooked.

The hinterland rewards exploration far more than the coast. [Dolceacqua](/europe/italy/liguria/dolceacqua), in the Nervia valley above Ventimiglia, is one of the most beautiful medieval villages in Liguria — a single-arch medieval bridge over the Nervia, a ruined Doria castle on the hill, and the local Rossese red wine, one of the few great reds produced on the Ligurian coast. Monet painted the bridge here in 1884. [Diano Marina](/europe/italy/liguria/dianomarina) has a long sandy beach and is a popular family resort with a gentler character than Sanremo. Inland from Diano, the hilltop village of Cervo — with its Baroque church standing above the sea — is one of the most photogenic on the entire Riviera.
