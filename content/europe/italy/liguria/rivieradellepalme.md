The Riviera delle Palme — the Riviera of the Palms — stretches along the Ligurian coast from Varazze in the east to Andora in the west, taking in Savona, Finale Ligure, Loano, Albenga, and Alassio along the way. The name comes from the palm-lined promenades that the towns planted in the nineteenth century to attract northern European visitors, and the palms are still there, lining the seafronts between stretches of sandy and pebble beach.

This is working Liguria as much as resort Liguria. Savona has a real port and a serious historical centre — the Priamàr fortress, the Sistine Chapel connection (Pope Julius II was born here), the ceramics tradition that runs through Savona and Albisola that has attracted artists since the seventeenth century. Finale Ligure has medieval ruins above a handsome old town. Albenga has a Roman grid plan intact, with a fifth-century baptistery that is one of the most important early Christian monuments in northern Italy. Inland, the valleys of the Lerrone, Merula, and Neva rivers contain castles, frescoed churches, and villages that see almost no tourist traffic. The [Riviera delle Palme cooking](/europe/italy/liguria/rivieradellepalme/gastronomy) draws on Genoese traditions but has its own character: lighter, greener, with vegetables playing a bigger role than in the city, and excellent local olive oil from the taggiasca olive.

The coast towns worth visiting in detail include [Borgio Verezzi](/europe/italy/liguria/rivieradellepalme/borgioverezzi), [Laigueglia](/europe/italy/liguria/rivieradellepalme/laigueglia), and several inland villages. [Casanova Lerrone](/europe/italy/liguria/rivieradellepalme/casanovalerrone) and [Pontinvrea](/europe/italy/liguria/rivieradellepalme/pontinvrea) are typical of the quiet hill communes that repay a detour.
