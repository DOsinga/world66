Torriglia sits at around 800 metres in the upper Scrivia Valley, far enough above the coast to be genuinely cool in summer — which is why it was nicknamed the "Switzerland of Liguria" and has been a summer retreat for Genoese families since the 19th century. The medieval village is dominated by the ruins of the Fieschi castle, a 13th-century stronghold of the powerful Fieschi family whose descendants famously organised a failed conspiracy against the Doria family in 1547, an episode that made it into Schiller's play and Verdi's opera.

The surrounding hills are the start of the Ligurian Apennine trails system. Paths lead into chestnut and beech forest toward the Alta Via dei Monti Liguri, the long-distance ridge-walk that runs the length of the Apennine backbone above Liguria. The Brugneto reservoir nearby, set among forested hills, supplies drinking water to Genoa and is the centre of a quiet nature reserve with walking and picnic areas. In autumn the town fills with Genoese foraging for porcini mushrooms in the woods above the village; the local restaurants shift entirely to mushroom-based menus.
