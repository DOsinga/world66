Bellagio sits on the tip of the promontory that divides Lake Como into its two southern arms, and no other position on the lake matches it for views: from the waterfront promenade you can see both branches of the lake, the Alps rising steeply on all sides, and the opposite shores. The town has been drawing wealthy visitors since Roman times — Pliny the Younger had a villa here — and the combination of elegance, setting, and scale (it is still small enough to walk in an hour) keeps it the most visited town on the lake.

The [Villa Serbelloni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Serbelloni) gardens, perched on the headland above the town, are among the finest lakeside gardens in Italy — terraced hillside walks through azaleas, camellias, and woodland with views down both arms of the lake. The [Villa Melzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Melzi_d%27Eril) on the southern waterfront has a neoclassical villa and English-style lakeside garden with Japanese elements. The Salita Serbelloni — a steep stepped alley climbing from the waterfront through the old town — is lined with silk shops, restaurants, and bars.

The ferry from Bellagio is the most practical way to move around the central lake: the small car ferry to Varenna on the eastern shore and Menaggio on the western takes about 15 minutes and runs throughout the day. The short crossing is one of the finest boat rides in Europe — very little distance, very large scenery.
