title: Day Trips
type: section
---

The valleys and lakes around Bergamo give you a string of easy escapes, most of them an hour or less from the city.

Head north up the [Brembana Valley](/europe/italy/lombardia/brembana_valley) for Alpine scenery, mountain cheeses, and walking trails. Partway up, [San Pellegrino Terme](/europe/italy/lombardia/san_pellegrino_terme) is a faded Belle Époque spa town with a casino, a river beach, and the famous mineral water that bears its name — about 40 minutes by bus. Deeper into the valley lie the car-free medieval hamlets of [Cornello dei Tasso and Oneta](/europe/italy/lombardia/cornello_dei_tasso_and_oneta), tied to the origins of both the European postal system and the Harlequin character. At the head of the valley, the [Passo di San Marco](/europe/italy/lombardia/san_marco_pass) climbs to nearly 2,000 metres before dropping toward the Valtellina — a classic mountain drive, open in summer only.

For water, [Lake Iseo](/europe/italy/lombardia/lake_iseo) is about an hour away by car and far less crowded than Como or Garda, with car-free Monte Isola at its centre. Smaller and quieter still, [Lake Endine](/europe/italy/lombardia/lake_endine) in the Val Cavallina is the place to go when you want a lake to yourself.

Further afield, the shores of [Lago di Garda](/europe/italy/lake_garda), about an hour to the east, hold the highest concentration of theme parks in Italy — Gardaland chief among them — alongside the lake's own resort towns. And Milan is just 50 minutes by train if you need a proper city fix.
