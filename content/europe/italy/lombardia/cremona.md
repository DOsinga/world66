Cremona is a city on the south bank of the Po in Lombardy, and it has one of the most remarkable concentrations of medieval architecture in northern Italy — the main piazza alone contains a cathedral, a baptistery, the tallest bell tower in Italy, and a series of civic loggie that together constitute a visual feast largely free of the tourist crowds that overwhelm Verona or Siena. But the city is most famous for something more intimate: violins. Cremona was the city of Stradivari, Guarneri, and Amati, and it remains the world capital of violin-making to this day.

The [Piazza del Comune](/europe/italy/lombardia/cremona/piazza_del_comune) is the starting point for any visit — a medieval civic composition anchored by the [Cathedral (Duomo)](/europe/italy/lombardia/cremona/cremona_cathedral) and its adjacent [Torrazzo](/europe/italy/lombardia/cremona/torrazzo), which at 112 metres is the tallest medieval tower in Italy. The [Baptistery](/europe/italy/lombardia/cremona/cremona_baptistery) and [Loggia dei Militi](/europe/italy/lombardia/cremona/loggia_dei_militi) complete the ensemble. A few minutes' walk away, the [Museo del Violino](/europe/italy/lombardia/cremona/museo_del_violino) in the renovated Palazzo dell'Arte is an extraordinarily well-conceived museum of luthiery, with actual Stradivari instruments you can hear in concert.

The city's connection to music runs deeper than tourism. The violin-making workshops (liuterie) are still active — several hundred craftsmen work in Cremona today, continuing a tradition of approximately 500 years. You can visit some workshops by appointment and watch the process of shaping and finishing a violin using tools and techniques Stradivari would have recognized. There are few other places in the world where a medieval craft tradition continues at this level.

Cremona is also known for its food: mostarda di Cremona (fruit preserve in a spiced mustard syrup, used as an accompaniment to meats and cheese), salame cremonese, and tortelli cremaschi (a sweet filled pasta that dates from the Renaissance). The city has a solid restaurant scene and makes a worthwhile overnight stop on a journey between Milan and Venice.
