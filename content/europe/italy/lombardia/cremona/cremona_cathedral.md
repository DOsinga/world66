Cremona Cathedral (Cattedrale di Santa Maria Assunta) was begun in 1107 and consecrated in 1190, though construction and modifications continued for centuries. The facade combines Romanesque structure with Gothic ornamental additions — the large rose window was added in the 14th century — and it is one of the finest facades in Lombardy. The octagonal baptistery and the Torrazzo bell tower stand beside it, together forming the compositional heart of the Piazza del Comune.

The interior is remarkable for its fresco cycle, which covers the nave from floor to ceiling in scenes from the life of Christ and the Virgin. The quality is uneven — several artists worked on it over decades — but the cumulative effect is overwhelming. The transepts contain good works by Pordenone, and the marble chancel screen is a refined piece of late medieval craftsmanship. The cathedral is large and usually uncrowded, making it easier to look at than most of the famous churches in Milan or Verona.
