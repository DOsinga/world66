Lazise is a small walled town on the eastern shore of Lake Garda. It was once the Venetians' most important outpost on the lake — a naval base and customs checkpoint — and the medieval fortifications still stand in remarkably good shape.

The main sights are the [Castello Scaligero](/europe/italy/lombardia/lagodigarda/lazise/castello_scaligero) and the [Dogana Veneta](/europe/italy/lombardia/lagodigarda/lazise/dogana_veneta) at the harbour edge, a rare surviving example of Venetian administrative architecture outside Venice itself. The town walls are well-preserved and pleasant to walk along. The small Romanesque [Chiesa di San Nicolò](/europe/italy/lombardia/lagodigarda/lazise/chiesa_san_nicolo) at the old port has medieval frescoes and an atmospheric setting between the water and the castle walls.

Lazise is an easy-going place to eat and drink. Local bars pour wines from the Bardolino DOC, which begins practically at the town limits, and it's easy to find a table with lake views and a plate of salami to go with a glass.
