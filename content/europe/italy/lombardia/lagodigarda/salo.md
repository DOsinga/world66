Salò sits in a sheltered gulf on the western shore of Lake Garda, its mild microclimate nurturing lemon trees and olives in a landscape that feels more Mediterranean than Lombard. Despite its modest size, the town carries enormous historical weight: for nineteen months from 1943 to 1945, it lent its name to Mussolini's last, German-backed republic — though the actual seat of power was scattered among the villas further up the lake.

The town was badly damaged by earthquake in 1901 and rebuilt in an elegant Liberty style, giving the lakefront piazza and promenade much of their current character. The [Duomo di Salò](/europe/italy/lombardia/lagodigarda/salo/duomo) on the main square is a late-Gothic cathedral with a notably incomplete façade and a rich interior of Renaissance paintings. The [Hotel Laurin](/europe/italy/lombardia/lagodigarda/salo/hotel_laurin) — the only Fascist ministry actually located in the town — stands on the lakefront with its original Liberty architecture intact. The [Lungolago](/europe/italy/lombardia/lagodigarda/salo/lungolago), the long waterfront promenade, is the place for an evening passeggiata with views across the sheltered gulf.

The hilly hinterland behind the town is good for hiking. Salò is part of the Alto Garda Bresciano Park, which extends north to Limone and covers a large area of forested ridges above the lake.
