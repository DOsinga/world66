Lake Como is Italy's third-largest lake and its deepest — 410 metres at the bottom — set in a ring of steep Alpine foothills that keep the climate mild enough for palm trees and citrus on the shores while snow sits on the peaks above. The lake has attracted the wealthy and the famous since Roman times: Pliny the Younger had villas here, and the tradition of grand lakeside residences has continued without interruption. The concentration of 18th- and 19th-century villas with terraced gardens cascading down to the water is unlike anything else in Italy.

The city of [Como](/europe/italy/lombardia/como) at the southern tip is the largest settlement — a compact old town with a cathedral, Roman-era walls, and a working lakefront. But most visitors come for the smaller towns on the shore. Bellagio occupies the promontory where the two southern arms of the lake divide, with views in three directions and a stepped medieval centre. Varenna on the eastern shore is quieter and possibly prettier, with the Villa Monastero and its botanical garden right on the waterfront. Tremezzo on the western shore holds the Villa Carlotta, whose gardens are at their best in spring when the azaleas and rhododendrons are in bloom. On the western shore south of Tremezzo, the Villa del Balbianello — set on a wooded headland, with loggia open to the lake on three sides — is one of the most beautiful buildings in Italy and has appeared in several films.

The lake is busiest and most beautiful in May and June, before the summer crowds arrive and while the gardens are still flowering. September and October are also excellent. In winter the lakeside towns are quiet, some restaurants close, and the light is often extraordinary. Getting between the towns is half the experience: the ferries that cross between Bellagio, Varenna, and Menaggio take about 15 minutes and offer views of the villas, terraces, and mountains that no road can match.
