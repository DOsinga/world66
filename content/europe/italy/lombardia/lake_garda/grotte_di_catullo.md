The Grotte di Catullo at the tip of the Sirmione peninsula are the largest Roman villa ruins in northern Italy — and among the most atmospheric anywhere, with their olive trees, lake views, and crumbling arcades. The name is a misnomer (the Romans didn't call them "grottoes"), but it has stuck for centuries.

The ruins cover several hectares and include substantial fragments of the cisterns, corridors, and halls of what was clearly an enormous and luxurious villa. A small museum at the site houses finds from the excavations. The setting — elevated above the lake, with views east to the Veronese shore and south to the plain — is as compelling as the archaeology. Open year-round; closed Mondays.
