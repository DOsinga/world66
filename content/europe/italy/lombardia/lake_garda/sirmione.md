Sirmione occupies a narrow peninsula jutting 4 km into the southern end of Lake Garda — an absurdly dramatic location that has made it one of the most visited spots in northern Italy. The medieval old town at the base of the peninsula is protected by the Scaligero Castle; the tip holds the Roman villa ruins of the Grotte di Catullo and a thermal spa complex.

The peninsula is closed to through traffic during peak hours; most visitors park outside the gate and walk in. The old lanes are lined with restaurants and shops. The lake on both sides of the peninsula is visible from almost everywhere, and the water is unusually clear and warm by lake standards. Sirmione is genuinely beautiful but genuinely crowded in July and August — September and October are a better bet.
