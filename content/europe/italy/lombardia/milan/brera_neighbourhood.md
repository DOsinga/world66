The Brera is Milan's art district — a neighbourhood of cobbled streets, independent galleries, antique shops, and upscale restaurants in the city's historic core. The Pinacoteca di Brera anchors it, but the neighbourhood itself, with its 18th-century Palazzo Brera complex housing a botanical garden and astronomical observatory, is worth exploring beyond the museum.

Via Fiori Chiari and Via Madonnina are the main streets for galleries and boutiques; Piazza del Carmine is good for a coffee. On the third Sunday of each month, the streets fill with an antiques market. The Brera has become somewhat tourist-polished over the past two decades, but it retains more local character than many comparable neighbourhoods in other Italian cities.
