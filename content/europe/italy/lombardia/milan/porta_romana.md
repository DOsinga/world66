The southeastern approach to the old city, named for the Roman road that ran from here toward the capital, and now one of Milan's most quietly fashionable residential districts. Corso di Porta Romana drives straight from the centre out to the surviving 17th-century gate, lined with bourgeois apartment blocks, and beyond it the neighbourhood loosens into leafy streets, former industrial yards, and a growing cluster of contemporary art and design.

The pull for visitors is the Fondazione Prada, the art foundation Rem Koolhaas built into a converted 1910s distillery, complete with a tower clad in gold leaf and a bar designed by film director Wes Anderson. Around it the old workshops of the Scalo Romano railway yard are being redeveloped, and the area's restaurants and bars have a more local, less touristed feel than the centre. The gate itself, the Arco di Porta Romana, marks the historic boundary.

This is a neighbourhood for an afternoon at the Fondazione followed by dinner where actual Milanese eat. It is less about monuments than atmosphere — the texture of a working city quarter turning slowly creative.
