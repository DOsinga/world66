Varenna on the eastern shore of Lake Como is often described as the more peaceful alternative to Bellagio — smaller, less photographed, and reached by fewer tourists, though the direct train from Milan (50 minutes to Varenna-Esino station) makes it easily accessible. The village climbs steeply from a small ferry landing, with coloured houses, narrow alleys, and a waterfront walkway partly carved into the rock face.

The [Villa Monastero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Monastero) was built on the foundations of a 13th-century Cistercian convent; its lakeside garden extends for 2km along the shore, with terraces of camellias, agapanthus, lemon trees, and rare botanical specimens. The [Villa Cipressi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Cipressi) next door has been converted into a hotel but its terraced gardens are open to visitors. The castle ruins above the village — the Castello di Vezio — give the best elevated view of the central lake.

Varenna is the main ferry hub for the eastern shore: the triangle service linking Varenna, Bellagio, and Menaggio runs throughout the day and is the most practical way to explore the central lake without a car. The village itself is small enough that most visitors cover it in a few hours, but the combination of gardens, views, and relative quiet makes it worth an overnight stay.
