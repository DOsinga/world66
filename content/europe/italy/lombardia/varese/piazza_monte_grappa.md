Piazza Monte Grappa is the civic heart of Varese, a large rationalist square built under Fascism in the 1930s and dominated by the Torre Civica — the town's monumental clock tower. The architecture is imposing rather than charming, but the square functions as a genuine gathering point: market stalls, cafe terraces, and the daily rhythm of a provincial Italian city.

The pedestrianised streets leading off the square — particularly Corso Matteotti — are good for a stroll through the historic centre. Liberty-era shop fronts and ironwork balconies hint at the prosperous early-twentieth-century city that Varese once was, before the postwar sprawl that now surrounds it.
