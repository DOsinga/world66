For Recanati there is really only one writer to read, and you should read him. Everything by **Giacomo Leopardi** is set against or born from this town, and the more you know of his work the more the small streets and the hill behind the house mean.

Start with the **Canti** — Leopardi's collected lyric poems, including *L'Infinito*, *Il Sabato del Villaggio*, *A Silvia*, *Le Ricordanze* and *Il Passero Solitario*, all written here. The standard English translation is Jonathan Galassi's (Farrar, Straus and Giroux, 2010), which prints the Italian and English on facing pages.

For prose, the **Zibaldone** — the gigantic philosophical notebook Leopardi kept between 1817 and 1832 — is the great unfinished masterpiece. The complete English edition (Farrar, Straus and Giroux, 2013) runs to over two thousand pages; dip in rather than read through. The **Operette Morali** (Penguin Classics has a good translation) is a shorter and easier way into his thought: a series of philosophical dialogues, often darkly funny, on death, illusion and the futility of human hope.
