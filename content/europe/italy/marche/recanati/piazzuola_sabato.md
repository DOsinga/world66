The Piazzuola del Sabato del Villaggio is the small square outside the Casa Leopardi, the courtyard the poet looked out onto from his bedroom window. It is the setting of *Il Sabato del Villaggio*, one of his most loved poems — a portrait of late-Saturday-afternoon village life with all its small expectancy and quiet sadness.

The square is paved in dark stone, ringed by a few low houses and the wall of the Leopardi palazzo, and is normally empty of more than a handful of visitors. The well at the centre and the simple facades along the south side are exactly as they appear in the poem. A small plaque carries the relevant lines. Come at dusk, when the long lines of *Il Sabato del Villaggio* fit the light best.
