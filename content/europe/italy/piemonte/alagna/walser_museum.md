  The Walser people were Germanic-speaking settlers from the Upper Valais in Switzerland who crossed the Alpine passes in the 13th century and established communities in remote high valleys across what is now Italy, France, and Liechtenstein. Alagna's Walser community has preserved its dialect, architecture, and traditions more completely than almost any other Italian Walser settlement. The characteristic Walser farmhouse — with its stone base, overhanging wooden upper storeys, and distinctive "stadel" granary structure on stone mushroom-shaped supports — was designed to prevent hay from spoiling in the damp Alpine climate.

The Walser Museum in Alagna occupies one of the village's preserved Walser farmhouses and documents the history and material culture of the Germanic-speaking community that settled Valsesia in the Middle Ages. The museum covers the migration of the Walser people from the Swiss Valais, their traditional way of life (cattle farming, cheese-making, limited grain cultivation at high altitude), their distinctive architecture, and their slow integration with the surrounding Piedmontese culture over the centuries.

The building itself — a traditional Walser casa — is the best exhibit: the construction techniques, the division between animal and human quarters, the granary, and the hay storage all reflect solutions evolved for life at 1,200 metres in a long, cold valley. The Walser linguistic heritage is still alive in Alagna: some older villagers still speak the local Titsch dialect, a variant of the German spoken in 13th-century Valais. The museum is open seasonally; check locally for hours.
