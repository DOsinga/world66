Barolo is tiny, steep, and famous far beyond its size. The village sits among the Langhe vineyards south of Alba, giving its name to the Nebbiolo wine that Piedmont likes to call "the king of wines and the wine of kings."

The centre is no more than a few lanes around [Falletti Castle](/europe/italy/piemonte/barolo/falletti_castle), but that is enough. The castle now holds [WiMu](/europe/italy/piemonte/barolo/wimu), a playful wine museum, and its cellars shelter the [Enoteca Regionale del Barolo](/europe/italy/piemonte/barolo/enoteca_regionale_del_barolo), where you can taste across the appellation without driving from estate to estate.

Barolo is not a general sightseeing town. If wine leaves you cold, the stop will feel short. If it matters to you, this is one of the clearest places in Italy to connect a bottle with its landscape: Cannubi vines on the slope, La Morra on the ridge, Monforte and Serralunga beyond the folds of hills.
