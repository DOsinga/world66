Bari is the capital of Puglia, a port city on the Adriatic coast that has been a trading hub between southern Europe and the eastern Mediterranean for centuries. It is not the prettiest city in the south, but it has genuine character — particularly in the old town, Bari Vecchia, a labyrinth of narrow streets on a headland between the old and new harbours.

The [old town (Bari Vecchia)](/europe/italy/puglia/bari/bari_vecchia) contains the [Basilica di San Nicola](/europe/italy/puglia/bari/basilica_san_nicola), built to house the relics of St Nicholas (yes, that one) brought from Turkey by local sailors in 1087, and the imposing [Swabian Castle](/europe/italy/puglia/bari/swabian_castle). Don't miss [Strada delle Orecchiette](/europe/italy/puglia/bari/strada_orecchiette) where local women still hand-shape pasta in front of their homes. The [Lungomare Nazario Sauro](/europe/italy/puglia/bari/lungomare) along the seafront is one of Italy's finest waterfront promenades. The new town has the [Teatro Petruzzelli](/europe/italy/puglia/bari/teatro_petruzzelli), Italy's fourth-largest opera house, rebuilt after a notorious 1991 arson. Bari is also a useful base for exploring the rest of Puglia — [Alberobello](/europe/italy/puglia/alberobello), [Lecce](/europe/italy/puglia/lecce) and [Castellana Grotte](/europe/italy/puglia/castellana_grotte) are all within day-trip distance.
