Palazzo Granafei-Nervegna is a 16th-century palazzo in Brindisi's old centre, close to the cathedral and the harbour. It is not as essential as the Roman column or the port itself, but it gives the city another layer: aristocratic stonework, civic rooms, exhibition spaces, and fragments of ancient Brindisi gathered into one building.

The best-known object here is the large marble capital associated with the Roman column, a reminder that Brindisi's classical remains are not confined to the waterfront steps. Drop in if you are already walking the historic centre; the palace works well as a short architectural and archaeological pause between the Duomo, the harbour, and the city's smaller churches.
