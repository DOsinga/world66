Pantagruele is a long-established trattoria in the heart of the old town, known for cooking that takes Puglian classics seriously without being precious about them. The kitchen does a good fave e cicoria — the bitter greens with pureed fava beans that is as much a Puglian staple as orecchiette — alongside more substantial meat and fish dishes.

The setting is pleasant: a couple of rooms in an old building with stone arches and white walls, modestly decorated. The pasta is made in-house and the portions are substantial. Book ahead on weekend evenings; at lunch it's usually possible to walk in. The restaurant has been run by the same family for decades, which shows in the confidence of the cooking.
