Torre Vado is a tiny seaside frazione of Morciano di Leuca on Puglia's Ionian coast, just north of the Salento tip. For most of the year it is a quiet place with fewer than a hundred permanent residents. In summer it swells into a beach and holiday village, with apartments, family resorts, boat trips, and evening markets along the promenade.

The village grew around the 16th-century [Torre Vado](/europe/italy/puglia/torre_vado/torre_vado_tower), one of the coastal watchtowers built to warn inland towns of raids from the sea. The tower is still the landmark, though the modern reason to come is simpler: warm Ionian water, low-key summer life, and easy access to the caves and cliffs near Santa Maria di Leuca.

Torre Vado works best as a small beach base if you have a car. [Otranto](/europe/italy/puglia/otranto) and [Lecce](/europe/italy/puglia/lecce) have the history; Torre Vado has the sea, boat excursions, and the feeling of being near the end of Italy's heel.
