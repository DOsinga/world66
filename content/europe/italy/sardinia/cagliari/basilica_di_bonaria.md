story: >
  According to legend, a Catalan ship caught in a violent storm off Cagliari in 1370 was saved when the sailors jettisoned a heavy wooden crate that had been weighing it down. The crate washed ashore at Bonaria and was found to contain a large statue of the Madonna holding a candle that was still lit despite the sea crossing. The Aragonese took this as a divine sign, dedicated the hill to Our Lady of Bonaria ("good winds"), and built the first church shortly afterward. Sailors from across the Mediterranean subsequently came to venerate the image, and in 1764 it was declared patron of all Sardinia.
---

The Basilica of Our Lady of Bonaria stands on a low hill south of the port, and its bulk — a large Gothic church flanked by a later Baroque basilica — dominates the skyline of the southern city. The complex is one of the most important Marian shrines in Sardinia, venerated by sailors for centuries as the patron of seafarers. Christopher Columbus visited the shrine before his first voyage and named his first landfall in the New World after it — Buenos Aires, Argentina.

The Gothic church (14th century) is the older structure; the adjoining baroque basilica was added in the 18th century when the original proved too small for the crowds. The statue of Our Lady of Bonaria, a Catalan Gothic figure in polychromed wood, is displayed above the high altar. The museum attached to the shrine displays votive offerings from grateful sailors going back five centuries — model ships, oil paintings of storms survived, sailors' tools.
