story: >
  San Saturnino was a Roman soldier martyred in Cagliari in 304 CE during the Diocletianic persecution — one of the earliest recorded Christian martyrdoms in Sardinia. The basilica built over his tomb by the 5th century became the principal church of the island for several centuries, predating Cagliari Cathedral by roughly eight hundred years. Its architectural form — a Greek cross plan topped by a tall cylindrical drum — is directly derived from early Byzantine models, making it one of the most significant pre-Romanesque buildings in the western Mediterranean.

The Basilica di San Saturnino is the oldest surviving Christian building in Sardinia, its foundations dating to the late 4th or early 5th century. It stands outside the old city walls to the east, now incongruously located near a main road but enclosed in a pleasant walled garden. The building — a pale stone church of restrained but powerful form — has survived the centuries remarkably intact, given its age and the turbulent history of the city.

The interior is plain by any standard: rough stone walls, minimal decoration, the essential geometry of the early Christian basilica. This plainness reads differently from the stripped interiors of post-Reformation churches; here it reflects the simplicity of the earliest Christian architecture, before the accumulated wealth of the medieval church transformed the style. The adjacent garden has some Roman-period fragments and sarcophagi from the surrounding necropolis.
