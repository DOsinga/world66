  The bastione was built between 1896 and 1902 on the site of an older Spanish fortification, as part of a programme to modernise and beautify the approach to the Castello quarter. The architect Dionigi Scano designed the sweeping staircase and the upper terrace specifically to create a dramatic promenade — Cagliari's answer to the Spanish Steps in Rome. The panoramic terrace at the top quickly became the social centre of the city and remains so, especially at sunset.

The Bastione di Saint Remy is the grand 19th-century staircase and terrace that connects the lower city to the Castello quarter, and it functions as Cagliari's great public outdoor room. The approach via the sweeping double staircase past triumphal arches is theatrical by design; the upper terrace, reached by this route or by a lift from the street below, offers one of the best views in the city — south over the rooftops to the bay, north toward the pale limestone walls of the citadel.

The terrace is at its best in the early evening when Cagliari comes out for the passeggiata, and it's lined with benches and the occasional food stall. In summer, temporary exhibitions and outdoor events use the space. Even without a specific event, the view and the architecture make it an essential stop on any walk through the old city.
