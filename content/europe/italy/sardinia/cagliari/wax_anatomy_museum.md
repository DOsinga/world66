The Wax Anatomy Museum at the University of Cagliari holds one of the most significant collections of anatomical wax models in Europe — 23 full-body figures and dozens of detail studies, all created in the early 19th century. The figures are displayed in their original wooden cabinets in a frescoed hall, and the combination of scientific precision and 18th-century aesthetics produces an effect that is simultaneously beautiful and deeply strange.

The models were used to teach anatomy to medical students when dissection of human bodies was restricted, and their extraordinary detail reflects the ambition of this educational project. Today the collection is a museum of the history of medicine as much as an art gallery. It's not widely advertised and many visitors to Cagliari miss it entirely — which is a pity, as it is among the most unusual things you can see in Sardinia.
