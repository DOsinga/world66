Porto Rotondo is a planned resort village just north of the **Costa Smeralda** on Sardinia's north-eastern coast, wrapped around a perfectly circular natural harbour that gave it its name ("round port"). Unlike most Sardinian settlements, it has no real history: the village was built from scratch in the 1960s by two Venetian brothers as a discreet rival to the Aga Khan's nearby Costa Smeralda, and designed in the same toned-down Mediterranean style — low pastel-washed houses, narrow lanes, umbrella pines, and a central piazza lined with cafés and designer boutiques. It is small, unashamedly upmarket, and at the height of summer is one of the most expensive villages in Italy.

The [marina](/europe/italy/sardinia/costasmeralda/portorotondo/marina) is the heart of the place — rows of gleaming yachts and a seafront promenade of bars, restaurants and gelato stands. The surrounding coves have some of the best beaches on this stretch of coast: [Spiaggia di Ira](/europe/italy/sardinia/costasmeralda/portorotondo/spiaggia_ira), [Punta Volpe](/europe/italy/sardinia/costasmeralda/portorotondo/punta_volpe), and further out the long sweeping arc of **Marinella** and the **Spiaggia Bianca** of Golfo Aranci. Inland, the hill above the port is crowned by the [Church of San Lorenzo](/europe/italy/sardinia/costasmeralda/portorotondo/san_lorenzo_church), an unusual modern building filled with sculpted olive wood by Mario Ceroli. Porto Rotondo is a place for sitting by the water, eating well, and yacht-watching; for more atmosphere and cheaper prices, the old port town of **Olbia** is 15 minutes to the south.
