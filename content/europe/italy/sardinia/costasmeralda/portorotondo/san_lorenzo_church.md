The Church of San Lorenzo crowns the hill above Porto Rotondo's marina, a contemporary building of warm stone and timber that manages to feel genuinely spiritual without copying any historical form. It was designed as part of the original village plan in the 1960s and is unusual among resort churches for the ambition of its art programme.

Inside, the large wooden sculptures by Mario Ceroli dominate the space — figures carved from unpolished olive wood with a rough, organic quality that contrasts with the smooth white walls. The effect is striking, and the church has attracted considerable attention from students of modern sacred architecture. It is open daily and worth visiting even if you have limited interest in churches — it is one of the few things in Porto Rotondo that isn't primarily about the sea.
