Dorgali sits on the slopes of Monte Bardia in the heart of the Barbagia region, about 15 km south of [Nuoro](/europe/italy/sardinia/nuoro). It's one of the best places in Sardinia to find traditional crafts — the town is known for its leather work, ceramics, and woven textiles, which you can buy directly from artisans' workshops.

The town has a good archaeological museum ([Museo Civico](/europe/italy/sardinia/dorg/museo_civico_dorgali)) with finds from local nuraghi and the nearby Tiscali site. [Tiscali](/europe/italy/sardinia/dorg/villaggio_nuragico_tiscali) itself — an ancient Nuragic village hidden inside a collapsed cave on the flanks of Monte Tiscali — is one of the most atmospheric archaeological sites on the island, reached by a demanding two-hour hike through the Lanaittu valley.

Dorgali is also the gateway to the [Gola Su Gorropu](/europe/italy/sardinia/dorg/gola_su_gorropu), a dramatic limestone canyon sometimes called Europe's deepest. The [Grotta di Ispinigoli](/europe/italy/sardinia/dorg/grotta_di_ispinigoli) to the north is worth an hour of your time for its extraordinary 38-metre stalactite column alone.

The coast below Dorgali — the Gulf of Orosei — is spectacular. Cala Goloritzé, Cala Biriola, and Cala Sisine are among the finest coves on the island, all accessible by boat from [Cala Gonone](/europe/italy/sardinia/dorg/cala_gonone) (8 km downhill from Dorgali).
