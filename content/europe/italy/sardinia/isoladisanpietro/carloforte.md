Carloforte is the only town on Isola di San Pietro, a small island off the southwest coast of Sardinia. Despite being Italian, the place has a distinct identity: it was founded in the 18th century by Genoese-origin settlers from the island of Tabarka off Tunisia, and their descendants still speak a Ligurian dialect called Tabarchino. Carlofortini don't consider themselves Sardi, and if you pay attention you'll hear it.

The town has a distinctly un-Sardinian look — pastel-coloured [harbour buildings](/europe/italy/sardinia/isoladisanpietro/carloforte/carloforte_harbour), a relaxed piazza shaded by tall palms, and narrow lanes climbing the hillside toward the remains of old fortifications. The sea here is excellent for swimming and snorkelling, and the waters around the island are a marine reserve. The best beaches are on the southwest coast: [La Bobba](/europe/italy/sardinia/isoladisanpietro/carloforte/la_bobba_beach) is the largest, and [Punta delle Colonne](/europe/italy/sardinia/isoladisanpietro/carloforte/punta_delle_colonne) at the island's tip has dramatic basalt column formations rising from the sea.

The island's economy runs on tuna fishing and tourism. The annual [Girotonno festival](/europe/italy/sardinia/isoladisanpietro/carloforte/girotonno_festival) in late May-June celebrates bluefin tuna with food, music, and cooking competitions from tuna-fishing cultures around the world. Outside peak summer, Carloforte is quiet, unhurried, and genuinely pleasant.
