The La Maddalena Archipelago is a national park comprising around 60 islands and islets off the northeastern tip of Sardinia, a short ferry crossing from Palau. The two largest islands are La Maddalena (where the main town is) and Caprera, connected by a causeway.

The archipelago's beaches and coves are stunning — some of the clearest water anywhere in the Mediterranean. The most famous beaches include [Cala Coticcio](/europe/italy/sardinia/lamaddelena/cala_coticcio) on [Caprera](/europe/italy/sardinia/lamaddelena/caprera) (nicknamed "the Tahiti of the Mediterranean"), [Budelli with its protected pink-sand beach](/europe/italy/sardinia/lamaddelena/budelli_pink_beach), and the beaches of [Spargi](/europe/italy/sardinia/lamaddelena/cala_granara) and Santa Maria. Day-trip boats from La Maddalena town visit the smaller islands; in high season these fill up, so book ahead.

On Caprera, the [Casa Museo di Garibaldi](/europe/italy/sardinia/lamaddelena/casa_museo_garibaldi) preserves the home where Giuseppe Garibaldi spent his later years and died in 1882. His tomb is in the garden. It's a moving and historically significant place, and less visited than you'd expect given its importance to Italian national history.

[La Maddalena town](/europe/italy/sardinia/lamaddelena/maddalena_town) itself is pleasant and unpretentious — a working port with good seafood restaurants and the feel of a place that hasn't been entirely swallowed by tourism. Ferries run frequently from Palau on the mainland.
