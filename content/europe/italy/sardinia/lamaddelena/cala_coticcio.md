  Cala Coticcio has been nicknamed "the Tahiti of the Mediterranean" — a marketing
  epithet that would normally invite scepticism, but in this case the water genuinely
  is that improbable shade of turquoise. The colour comes from the bright granite

  act as natural windbreaks and concentrate the reflected light.

Cala Coticcio is the most celebrated cove in the Maddalena archipelago — a narrow inlet on the southeastern tip of Caprera sheltered by wind-sculpted granite boulders, with water so clear and brilliantly coloured that photographs look like they have been digitally enhanced. It is not easily reached: you need either a 45-minute walk from the nearest road (a rough track through maquis), or a boat. Both are worth it.

The cove is small, which means it gets crowded in July and August. The best strategy is to arrive by water early in the morning before the day-trip boats arrive, or in the late afternoon after they leave. Snorkelling is excellent around the granite outcrops. There are no facilities — bring water and food.
