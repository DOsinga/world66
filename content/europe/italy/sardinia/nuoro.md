Nuoro is the real Sardinia. Tucked into the mountainous heartland of the island, far from the coastal resorts, it is the capital of the Barbagia — a rugged interior region where Sardo is still spoken, traditional festivals survive intact, and the landscape has a spare, almost austere beauty.

The old quarter spreads around the [Corso Garibaldi](/europe/italy/sardinia/nuoro/corso_garibaldi), a broad pedestrian street lined with old palazzi and small bars. This is where Nuoro's literary legacy lives: Grazia Deledda, the only Italian woman to win the Nobel Prize in Literature, was born here in 1871, and her [birthplace](/europe/italy/sardinia/nuoro/casa_di_grazia_deledda) is preserved as a small house museum. Her novel *Canne al Vento* captures the mountain villages of Barbagia with unsparing honesty.

The [Ethnographic Museum](/europe/italy/sardinia/nuoro/museo_della_vita_e_delle_tradizioni_popolari_sarde) on Via Antonio Mereu is outstanding — one of the best in Sardinia, with an extraordinary collection of traditional jewellery, carnival masks, embroidered costumes, and handwoven carpets. For modern and contemporary art, the [MAN museum](/europe/italy/sardinia/nuoro/man_art_museum) near the Corso is considerably stronger than you might expect. The [National Archaeological Museum](/europe/italy/sardinia/nuoro/national_archaeological_museum) beside the [cathedral](/europe/italy/sardinia/nuoro/nuoro_cathedral) covers the Nuragic civilisation with a fine collection of bronze figurines.

Above the town, [Monte Ortobene](/europe/italy/sardinia/nuoro/monte_ortobene) rises to almost 1,000 metres. A winding road or a good hour's walk on foot takes you to the summit, where a large bronze statue of the Redeemer looks out over the town and the surrounding valleys. The views are sweeping on a clear day.

Nuoro is an excellent base for the villages of the Barbagia. [Orgosolo](/europe/italy/sardinia) is famous for its political murals, painted across the walls of the village since the 1960s. Fonni, the highest village in Sardinia, and Mamoiada — home of the Mamuthones carnival masks — are both within easy reach.
