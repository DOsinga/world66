The Giorgio Asproni National Archaeological Museum sits in the historic centre beside the Cathedral of Our Lady of the Snows. Its collection covers the Nuragic civilisation — the Bronze Age culture that covered Sardinia in thousands of round stone towers (nuraghi) between roughly 1800 and 500 BCE — as well as finds from the subsequent Phoenician trading settlements and Roman occupation of the island.

The Nuragic collection is the highlight. The bronze figurines known as bronzetti are particularly striking: small stylised representations of warriors, shamans, archers, and mythological animals that were deposited as votive offerings. These figures, found at sacred wells and nuraghi across the island, represent one of the most distinctive Bronze Age art traditions in the Mediterranean.

The museum provides essential context for visiting the archaeological sites around Nuoro. The Nuraghe Mereu, just outside town, and the Santa Barbara nuraghe at Macomer are within reasonable reach; the famous Su Nuraxi nuraghe at Barumini, one of the best-preserved on the island and a UNESCO World Heritage Site, is about 90km to the southwest.
