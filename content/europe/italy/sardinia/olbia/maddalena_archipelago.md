The Maddalena Archipelago is a national park of seven main islands and dozens of smaller islets scattered across the Strait of Bonifacio between Sardinia and Corsica. The granite landscapes are among the most beautiful in the Mediterranean — the combination of pink and grey rock, silver sand, and the particular shade of turquoise-blue water that comes from the clean Atlantic currents sweeping through the strait is unlike anywhere else.

Ferries depart from Palau, about 40km north of Olbia (an hour by car or bus), and take 15 minutes to reach La Maddalena town. From there, a bridge connects to Caprera. For reaching the smaller outer islands and the best beaches — Spiaggia Rosa, now closed to protect its unusual pink sand colouration — boat tours depart from La Maddalena. The archipelago is busiest in July and August but the national park status keeps development limited.
