Porto Ferro is a small, sheltered bay on Sardinia's northwest coast, about 10 km north of [Alghero](/europe/italy/sardinia/alghero). The beach has a distinctive reddish tinge from the iron-rich sand. At each end, two old Spanish watchtowers — [Torre Negra](/europe/italy/sardinia/portoferro/torre_negra) and [Torre di Bantine Sale](/europe/italy/sardinia/portoferro/torre_di_bantine_sale) — frame the cove. The northern tower, Torre Negra, is one of the best-preserved on the island.

The bay is backed by dunes and Mediterranean scrub and has no commercial development to speak of — no bars, no sun-lounger hire. That's the attraction: it stays quieter than most northwest coast beaches even in peak summer. Swimming is good but currents can be strong when the mistral blows.

About 2 km inland, [Lake Baratz](/europe/italy/sardinia/portoferro/lake_baratz) is Sardinia's only natural freshwater lake. The walk from beach to lake through cork oak forest is one of the better short hikes in this corner of the island.
