Sassari is Sardinia's second city and the main centre of the island's north. Its history is layered — Genoese, Pisan, Aragonese, and Spanish rulers each left their mark, and the old town still shows it. The architecture mixes Spanish Gothic townhouses with Italian Baroque churches, all crammed into a maze of narrow alleys that are worth wandering for hours.

The [Duomo](/europe/italy/sardinia/sassari/sassari_cathedral) has a lavish Baroque facade that looks more Spanish than Italian. The [Fontana del Rosello](/europe/italy/sardinia/sassari/fontana_del_rosello) at the bottom of the steps on Corso Trinità is a fine piece of late Renaissance sculpture, fed by natural springs. The [Museo Nazionale Sanna](/europe/italy/sardinia/sassari/museo_nazionale_sanna) in the newer part of town has a strong archaeological collection, including Nuragic bronzes and Roman artefacts from across northern Sardinia.

The city is not a tourist destination in the usual sense — most visitors to Sardinia head for the coast — but Sassari has a liveliness and self-sufficiency that the resort towns lack. It is a university city, and the evening passeggiata along Corso Vittorio Emanuele II is one of the better people-watching experiences on the island. The [Cavalcata Sarda](/europe/italy/sardinia/sassari/cavalcata_sarda) in May and the Discesa dei Candelieri in August are among the most spectacular folk events in Sardinia.
