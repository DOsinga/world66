Acireale is a handsome Baroque town on Sicily's eastern coast, perched on a lava cliff above the Ionian Sea between [Catania](/europe/italy/sicily/catania) and [Taormina](/europe/italy/sicily/taormina). Often overlooked by visitors rushing between those two more famous destinations, it rewards a stop with its ornate churches, its lively piazza, and a genuine small-city atmosphere that feels distinctly un-touristy.

The town centre is dominated by the [Piazza Duomo](/europe/italy/sicily/acireale/piazza_duomo), flanked by the cathedral and the Basilica dei Santi Pietro e Paolo, both dripping with the elaborate stonework that characterises Sicilian Baroque. The [Collegiate Basilica of San Sebastiano](/europe/italy/sicily/acireale/san_sebastiano) on the adjacent piazza is considered the finest Baroque facade in town. Corso Umberto, the main street, is a good place for an evening passeggiata alongside locals. The [Terme di Santa Venera](/europe/italy/sicily/acireale/terme_santa_venera), thermal baths fed by sulphurous springs, have been in use since Roman times. Below the cliff, the fishing hamlet of [Santa Maria la Scala](/europe/italy/sicily/acireale/santa_maria_la_scala) offers fresh seafood and sea views.

Acireale is best known for its [carnival](/europe/italy/sicily/acireale/carnival), one of the oldest and most spectacular in Sicily. Every February, enormous papier-mache floats — satirical, colourful, and often politically pointed — parade through the streets, accompanied by days of celebration. If you are in eastern Sicily in carnival season, it is well worth timing your visit to catch it. The [Pinacoteca Zelantea](/europe/italy/sicily/acireale/pinacoteca_zelantea) is the town's main art gallery for a quieter afternoon. Outside of carnival, Acireale makes a pleasant base for exploring the Riviera dei Ciclopi coastline, with its dramatic lava formations and fishing villages like nearby [Acitrezza](/europe/italy/sicily/acitrezza).
