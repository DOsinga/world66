The Hellenistic-Roman Quarter shows Agrigento from a different angle than the temple ridge. Instead of columns and sanctuaries, you get the lived-in city: streets, house plans, drains, courtyards, and traces of domestic rooms laid out near the archaeological museum. It makes ancient Akragas feel less like a postcard and more like a working town.

The site is best paired with the Valley of the Temples and the Regional Archaeological Museum, because the three explain one another. The ruins are low and can look cryptic at first, but the grid is still legible, and the scale of the quarter gives a useful sense of how large and organised the classical city was beyond its ceremonial monuments.
