Calatafimi Segesta is a small inland town in western Sicily whose importance comes from what surrounds it. Just outside town, the ruins of ancient Segesta rise in open hills: a near-complete Doric temple on one slope and a Greek theatre on another, with wide views over the countryside.

The modern town itself is modest, but it gives the archaeological park a human setting rather than leaving it as a coach-tour stop between Palermo and Trapani. The old centre has churches, steep lanes and the slow feel of rural Sicily; nearby [Pianto Romano](/europe/italy/sicily/calatafimi_segesta/pianto_romano) marks Garibaldi's 1860 victory in the campaign that helped create modern Italy.

Come for Segesta first. The [Doric temple](/europe/italy/sicily/calatafimi_segesta/segesta_temple) and [Greek theatre](/europe/italy/sicily/calatafimi_segesta/segesta_theatre) are strong enough to justify a detour on their own, and the combination of classical ruins, Sicilian hills and Risorgimento memory gives Calatafimi Segesta more identity than its size suggests.
