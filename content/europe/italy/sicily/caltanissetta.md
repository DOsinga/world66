Caltanissetta sits in the middle of Sicily, away from the island's easier coastal pleasures. It is a working inland city with steep streets, a handsome old centre, and a history tied to sulphur mining, noble families, and archaeological sites in the dry hills around town.

The centre is best seen on foot. Piazza Garibaldi, Corso Umberto I, [Palazzo Moncada](/europe/italy/sicily/caltanissetta/palazzo_moncada), the cathedral, and the churches around them give the city its Baroque face. A short walk east, the broken towers of [Pietrarossa Castle](/europe/italy/sicily/caltanissetta/pietrarossa_castle) stand above the Angeli district with a view over the Salso valley.

Caltanissetta's stronger identity is just outside the postcard frame. The [Mineralogical, Paleontological and Sulphur Mines Museum](/europe/italy/sicily/caltanissetta/mineralogical_paleontological_and_sulphur_mines_museum) explains the industry that once made central Sicily rich and brutal. Nearby archaeological sites such as [Sabucina](/europe/italy/sicily/caltanissetta/sabucina_excavations) pull the story much further back, before the city became the inland capital of the sulphur belt.

It is not a soft resort town, and that is the point. Caltanissetta works for travellers who want a Sicilian city with fewer tour buses, more local life, and enough serious sights to fill a full day between [Agrigento](/europe/italy/sicily/agrigento), [Piazza Armerina](/europe/italy/sicily/piazza_armerina), and the Madonie.
