Siracusa sits sixty kilometres south of Catania and is the most rewarding single-day excursion from the city. The Greek city was once a rival of Athens, and the surviving evidence — the Greek theatre, the limestone quarries used as prisons during the Peloponnesian War, the temples — is exceptional.

The island of Ortigia, the medieval core of the modern town, is a separate experience: tight streets, baroque churches built directly on Greek temples, a long sea-facing promenade and an excellent food market. A full day is comfortably enough; two days lets you do both the archaeological park and Ortigia properly.
