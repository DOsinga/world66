The Madonie are the mountains that rise behind Cefalu and Palermo's eastern coast, a protected pocket of Sicily where the island suddenly feels alpine. Limestone peaks, beech woods, old mule tracks, and high villages replace the beaches and fishing ports below.

This is not wilderness in the North American sense. The park is stitched together by villages such as [Castelbuono](/europe/italy/sicily/madonie/castelbuono), [Petralia Sottana](/europe/italy/sicily/madonie/petralia_sottana), [Petralia Soprana](/europe/italy/sicily/madonie/petralia_soprana), and [Geraci Siculo](/europe/italy/sicily/madonie/geraci_siculo), with churches, stone lanes, and bakeries never very far from the trails. That is the charm: you can climb [Pizzo Carbonara](/europe/italy/sicily/madonie/pizzo_carbonara) in the morning and eat mountain cheese or badda beans in a village by late afternoon.

The Madonie make an excellent inland break from [Cefalu](/europe/italy/sicily/cefalu). Go with a car if you can; buses exist, but they are built for residents rather than walkers with a tight itinerary. The roads are slow, winding, and beautiful, and the weather can change fast once you leave the coast.
