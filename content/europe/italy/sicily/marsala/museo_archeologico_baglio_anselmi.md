The archaeological museum occupies a converted **baglio** — a fortified wine warehouse — on the seafront at the western edge of the old town. Its centrepiece is the **Punic ship**: roughly half the hull of a 35-metre Carthaginian warship, raised from the seabed off Punta Scario and painstakingly conserved.

The other rooms hold finds from Lilybaeum and the surrounding necropoleis: Phoenician anchors, terracotta votive masks, Roman glass, mosaic pavements, and a clean display of grave goods that traces the sequence of cultures along this coast. It is the obvious counterpart to a visit to Motya, and the two together give a coherent picture of Phoenician Sicily.
