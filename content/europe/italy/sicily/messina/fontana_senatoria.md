Fontana Senatoria stands beside Palazzo Zanca, Messina's city hall, a compact marble fountain that rewards a slower look rather than a special detour. It was made in the early 17th century to mark the work of the city's senators on the water supply, and their names are still tied to the fountain's identity.

The fountain is quieter than the grander Neptune and Orion fountains, but it fits well into a walk around the Duomo, the harbourfront, and the civic buildings. Look for the circular basin, shell-like decoration, and the pine-cone finial in the centre. It is the sort of small civic monument that helps Messina feel layered rather than merely rebuilt.
