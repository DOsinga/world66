Palazzo del Monte di Pieta is one of the more graceful fragments of old Messina, a city that lost much of its historic fabric to earthquakes and wartime bombing. The palace began as a charitable pawn institution in the early 17th century, but the part that catches the eye now is its grand Baroque stairway and the theatrical courtyard behind the street facade.

It sits a short walk uphill from the cathedral, so it works well as a quiet detour after seeing the astronomical clock. Come for the architecture rather than a big museum visit: the pleasure is in the carved stone, the sense of urban theatre, and the reminder that Messina once had a far richer architectural landscape than the rebuilt city first suggests.
