  The castle was built by Roger I of Sicily after the Norman conquest of the island in the 11th century. Roger's campaign unified Sicily under Christian rule after more than two centuries of Arab control, and the string of fortified towers he planted on volcanic plugs across the Etna plain — Motta Sant'Anastasia, Paternò, Adrano — formed a defensive network that his heirs would use for centuries.
---

The Norman tower at Motta Sant'Anastasia rises from a sheer basalt plug in a way that makes it immediately apparent why this rock was fortified in the first place — the view from the base encompasses the entire Simeto plain, the approaches from Catania, and on clear days the summit of Etna. It is one of the most photogenic Norman towers in Sicily.

The tower itself dates from the late 11th century and is largely intact, though not always open for interior visits. The approach on foot from the town centre passes through narrow medieval streets. The basalt rock formation on which it sits is itself remarkable — a volcanic remnant that protrudes from the flat agricultural plain in a way that would have made it unmistakably valuable as a military position.
