Noto is the jewel of Sicilian Baroque. Rebuilt entirely after the 1693 earthquake, the town is a masterpiece of unified urban design, all honey-coloured limestone, theatrical facades, and ornate balconies. It has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2002.

The main street, [Corso Vittorio Emanuele](/europe/italy/sicily/noto/corso_vittorio_emanuele), is the showcase, lined with churches and palaces that glow golden in the late afternoon light. The [Cattedrale di San Nicolò](/europe/italy/sicily/noto/cattedrale_di_san_nicolo) at the top of its grand staircase, [Palazzo Villadorata](/europe/italy/sicily/noto/palazzo_villadorata) with its extraordinary carved balcony corbels, and the convex Baroque facade of [Chiesa di San Domenico](/europe/italy/sicily/noto/chiesa_di_san_domenico) are the architectural highlights. Noto is small enough to see in a few hours, but the atmosphere and the excellent granita and pastries from [Caffè Sicilia](/europe/italy/sicily/noto/caffe_sicilia) make it worth lingering.

Beyond the town itself, the [Vendicari Nature Reserve](/europe/italy/sicily/noto/riserva_naturale_oasi_faunistica_di_vendicari) 12 km to the south offers a complete contrast: coastal wetlands, migratory birds, and uncrowded beaches. The ruins of [Noto Antica](/europe/italy/sicily/noto/noto_antica) — the original hilltop city destroyed in 1693 — are 8 km north and reward a detour for those interested in what was lost.
