story: >
  The fountain was commissioned by a Florentine nobleman for his own garden in Tuscany, then sold unfinished to the Palermo Senate in 1574 and brought to Sicily in pieces. To install it the senate had to demolish several existing buildings. The display of naked nymphs and satyrs scandalised the city — Piazza Pretoria has been known as the Piazza della Vergogna, "Square of Shame", ever since.
---

The Fontana Pretoria fills the centre of Piazza Pretoria, a sequence of concentric basins crowded with marble figures of river gods, allegorical women, satyrs, putti, and animals. Sixteen statues stand on the lowest level, with smaller groups rising in tiers up the central column. By Francesco Camilliani and Michelangelo Naccherino, finished 1554.

The piazza is closed to traffic and the fountain is illuminated at night. The neighbouring church of Santa Caterina d'Alessandria, with one of the most ornate Baroque interiors in the city, is worth combining with the fountain visit.
