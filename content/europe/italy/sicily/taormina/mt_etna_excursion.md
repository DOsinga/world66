  Etna is the largest active volcano in Europe and has been erupting almost continuously for the past 500,000 years. It produces more lava than any other European volcano, and new craters open and close with unnerving regularity. The fertile volcanic soil on its lower slopes supports some of Sicily's best vineyards — growing wine in the shadow of an active volcano that may bury your fields is either reckless farming or perfect terroir, depending on whom you ask.

Mt Etna (3,357 m) is visible from Taormina on clear days, its summit trailing steam or, in eruption, a column of smoke visible from hundreds of kilometres away. Day trips from Taormina to the volcano take about an hour by car or organised tour to reach the upper cable car station at 2,500 m. From there, guided walks reach the summit craters (when access is permitted) through a lunar landscape of black lava and ash.

Tours from Taormina can be booked through local agencies and typically run half-day to full-day. The volcano is active and access to the summit craters is restricted when eruption risk is high — the guides know the current status. The north face of Etna around Linguaglossa has excellent hiking through beech and birch forests at lower altitudes if the summit is closed.
