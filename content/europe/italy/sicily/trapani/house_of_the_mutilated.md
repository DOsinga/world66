The House of the Mutilated is an uncomfortable but revealing piece of Trapani's 20th-century architecture. Built in the Fascist period for the association of war-wounded veterans, it belongs to a network of Italian buildings where commemoration, propaganda, and modernist design overlap.

It is mainly an exterior stop for travellers interested in architecture and political memory. Trapani's churches and waterfront are easier to love, but this building adds a sharper historical layer to the city and rewards a slow look at its symbols and proportions.
