Palazzo della Giudecca, also called Palazzo Ciambra, stands in Trapani's former Jewish quarter and is one of the city's most distinctive historic houses. Its pointed details, fortified tower, and Spanish-influenced decoration mark the transition from late medieval Sicily into the Renaissance.

The building is privately held and usually admired from outside, so do not expect a museum visit. It is still worth seeking out while walking the old centre, because it gives the Giudecca district a physical anchor and hints at the Jewish community that shaped this part of Trapani before the expulsions of the late 15th century.
