Bolzano (Bozen in German) sits at the confluence of the Talvera and Isarco rivers, in a broad valley surrounded by the Dolomites. The city has a distinctly Austrian character — German is co-official with Italian, the architecture is central European, and the food leans toward dumplings and apple strudel rather than pasta. It is the capital of the South Tyrol, a region that only became part of Italy after the First World War.

The old town centres on [Piazza Walther](/europe/italy/trentinoaltoadige/bolzano/piazza_walther), named for the medieval German poet Walther von der Vogelweide. The [Gothic Duomo](/europe/italy/trentinoaltoadige/bolzano/duomo) is nearby, and the medieval arcades of [Via dei Portici](/europe/italy/trentinoaltoadige/bolzano/via_dei_portici) lead north to the [fruit market square](/europe/italy/trentinoaltoadige/bolzano/piazza_delle_erbe). The city's undisputed star attraction is [Ötzi the Iceman at the South Tyrol Museum of Archaeology](/europe/italy/trentinoaltoadige/bolzano/otzi_museum) — a 5,300-year-old mummified man discovered in 1991 in the glacial ice of the Alps, displayed remarkably well preserved.

The surrounding mountains are the main draw for outdoor enthusiasts. The [Kohlerer Bahn](/europe/italy/trentinoaltoadige/bolzano/kohlerer_cable_car) — the world's first passenger cable car, still running — connects the city to the forested hills above, while a modern cable car reaches the [Renon plateau](/europe/italy/trentinoaltoadige/bolzano/renon_plateau) with its panoramic views. The [Rosengarten](/europe/italy/trentinoaltoadige/bolzano/rosengarten) and Schlern massifs of the Dolomites are accessible as day trips. The mountain huts (rifugi) are generally open from late June through September.
