Folgaria sits on a sunny plateau at 1,168 metres above sea level, looking out over the Adige Valley toward the Lavarone mountain range in the southern Dolomites. With its onion-domed [Chiesa di San Lorenzo](/europe/italy/trentinoaltoadige/folgaria/chiesa_di_san_lorenzo), coffee houses, and Austrian-flavoured architecture, it is a reminder that this part of Trentino was Habsburg territory until 1918.

The town is primarily a winter destination — the [Folgaria–Lavarone–Luserna ski area](/europe/italy/trentinoaltoadige/folgaria/ski_area_folgaria) offers around 100 km of runs across the plateau — but the terrain is equally good in summer for hiking and mountain biking. The [Passo Coe trails](/europe/italy/trentinoaltoadige/folgaria/passo_coe_hiking) fan out across open highland meadows, and the [alpine botanical garden](/europe/italy/trentinoaltoadige/folgaria/giardino_botanico_alpino) at 1,612 metres is spectacular in early July. History buffs should seek out [Forte di Sommo Alto](/europe/italy/trentinoaltoadige/folgaria/forte_di_sommo), one of the best-preserved Austro-Hungarian fortifications from the First World War fighting on this plateau.

The atmosphere stays relaxed and unhurried year-round, more village than resort, and the surrounding landscape is genuinely beautiful.
