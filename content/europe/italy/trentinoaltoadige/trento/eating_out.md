---
title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

Trentino cooking is mountain food with strong Austrian influences — *canederli* (bread dumplings) in broth or with butter and sage, *spätzle* with smoked speck, *strangolapreti* (spinach gnocchi), polenta with mushrooms, and game in the autumn. The local wines are excellent and underrated: Teroldego (a dark, structured red), Marzemino, Müller-Thurgau, and the Trentodoc sparkling wines that rival the better Franciacortas.

**Scrigno del Duomo**, on the Piazza Duomo, is the city's reliable upmarket choice — refined Trentino cooking in a vaulted medieval cellar with a long wine list. **Osteria a Le Due Spade**, in a 16th-century inn near the cathedral, is the place for a slow tasting menu. For something simpler, **Antica Trattoria Due Mori** has been serving canederli and polenta in roughly the same form since 1882, and the cafés around Piazza Cesare Battisti do a brisk lunchtime trade in plates of the day at reasonable prices.
