Forster's 1908 novel follows Lucy Honeychurch, a young Englishwoman whose visit to Florence disrupts her carefully ordered world in ways she doesn't immediately understand. The opening chapters set in the Pensione Bertolini and the Piazza Santa Croce are as good a portrait of early-20th-century English tourism in Italy as exists, and the murder she witnesses in the piazza — with Michelangelo's statue visible through the colonnade — is one of the great opening shocks in Edwardian fiction.
