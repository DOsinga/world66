George Eliot's most demanding novel is set in 15th-century Florence, during the era of Lorenzo the Magnificent and the monk Savonarola. It follows the daughter of a blind classical scholar through the political and religious tumult of a city at the height of its power and at the edge of catastrophe. The research is meticulous, the portrait of Renaissance Florence — its intellectual culture, its street life, its violence — is extraordinary, and it is considerably underread.
