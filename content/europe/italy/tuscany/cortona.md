Cortona is a compact medieval hill town in eastern [Tuscany](/europe/italy/tuscany), perched on a steep slope with sweeping views over the Val di Chiana to Lake Trasimeno. It became internationally famous as the setting for Frances Mayes' memoir *Under the Tuscan Sun*, but the town's appeal long predates the book — the [Etruscans built walls here](/europe/italy/tuscany/cortona/etruscan_walls) that still stand.

The [Piazza della Repubblica](/europe/italy/tuscany/cortona/piazza_della_repubblica) and its surrounding streets are full of small restaurants, wine bars and artisan shops. The [Museo dell'Accademia Etrusca](/europe/italy/tuscany/cortona/museo_accademia_etrusca) on Piazza Signorelli houses an impressive collection of Etruscan artefacts, and the [Museo Diocesano](/europe/italy/tuscany/cortona/museo_diocesano) across the cathedral square holds Fra Angelico's Annunciation. The quiet lanes above the centre lead to the [Basilica di Santa Margherita](/europe/italy/tuscany/cortona/basilica_santa_margherita) and the [Medici fortress](/europe/italy/tuscany/cortona/fortezza_medicea), both offering panoramic views. Outside the walls, the Renaissance [Santa Maria delle Grazie al Calcinaio](/europe/italy/tuscany/cortona/santa_maria_delle_grazie) and the Etruscan tombs at [Il Sodo](/europe/italy/tuscany/cortona/melone_del_sodo) reward a half-day's exploration in the surrounding countryside.

Cortona is small enough to explore in half a day, but the atmosphere — and the wine — tend to make people stay longer.
