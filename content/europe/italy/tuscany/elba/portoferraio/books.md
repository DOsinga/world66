title: "Books"
type: section
---

Napoleon's nine and a half months on Elba have generated a small library of their own; a couple of the most accessible accounts are worth packing.

**Vincent Cronin, *Napoleon* (1971)** — a balanced biography that handles the Elba interlude with particular care, drawing on Napoleon's own letters from the Palazzina dei Mulini. Read alongside a visit to the villa.

**Norman Mackenzie, *The Escape from Elba* (1982)** — a detailed account of Napoleon's escape on 26 February 1815 and the Hundred Days that followed, written like a thriller. The opening chapters are set entirely on Elba and are the best portrait of his exile court at Portoferraio.

**Alexandre Dumas, *The Count of Monte Cristo* (1844)** — the great revenge novel opens with Dantès being put ashore on the island of Monte Cristo, the small Tuscan archipelago island visible on a clear day from Elba's south coast. The novel gives a vivid Romantic-era picture of the region's smugglers and Napoleonic exiles.

**Patrick O'Brian, *The Mauritius Command* (1977)** — not set on Elba but a beautifully written naval-era novel that captures the Mediterranean of Napoleon's exile. O'Brian's twenty-volume Aubrey-Maturin series remains the best maritime fiction of its era, and several volumes pass through these waters.
