Firenzuola is a small Apennine town on the northern edge of Tuscany, closer in spirit to mountain passes and stone quarries than to postcard Florence. The name means "little Florence", but do not come expecting Renaissance polish. Come for pietra serena, chestnut woods, wartime scars, and a colder, rougher Tuscan landscape.

The town was badly damaged during the Second World War because the Gothic Line ran through this part of the Apennines. That history still shapes visits to the surrounding passes, especially the [Futa Pass Cemetery](/europe/italy/tuscany/fiorenzuola/futa_pass_cemetery) and [Passo del Giogo](/europe/italy/tuscany/fiorenzuola/passo_del_giogo). In town, the [Museo della Pietra Serena](/europe/italy/tuscany/fiorenzuola/museo_della_pietra_serena) explains the local stone tradition better than any souvenir shop could.

Firenzuola works best as part of an Alto Mugello loop rather than a stand-alone destination. Pair it with [Palazzuolo sul Senio](/europe/italy/tuscany/palazzuolo_sul_senio), [Badia di Moscheta](/europe/italy/tuscany/fiorenzuola/badia_di_moscheta), or a slow drive over the passes toward Emilia-Romagna.
