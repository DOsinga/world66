Florence is a small city with an outsized claim on Western civilization. In the 15th and 16th centuries, the Medici-funded Renaissance concentrated more art and architecture here than almost anywhere else on earth, and the results are still on view in a compact historic centre that you can walk across in twenty minutes. The [Uffizi Gallery](/europe/italy/tuscany/florence/uffizi_gallery) holds the world's greatest collection of Renaissance painting. The [Galleria dell'Accademia](/europe/italy/tuscany/florence/galleria_dellaccademia) has Michelangelo's David. The [Duomo](/europe/italy/tuscany/florence/duomo_florence), designed by Brunelleschi with an engineering audacity that was unmatched for a century, still dominates the skyline. The [Baptistery](/europe/italy/tuscany/florence/baptistery) opposite it has Ghiberti's Gates of Paradise and a mosaic ceiling of ten million tesserae.

But Florence rewards those who go beyond the famous. The [Bargello](/europe/italy/tuscany/florence/bargello) is less crowded than the big names and contains Donatello's bronze David and the competition panels by Ghiberti and Brunelleschi. [San Miniato al Monte](/europe/italy/tuscany/florence/san_miniato_al_monte) has Benedictine monks singing Gregorian vespers at 17:30 most evenings, free and extraordinary. [Santa Croce](/europe/italy/tuscany/florence/santacroce) has Giotto and the tombs of Michelangelo, Galileo, and Machiavelli. [Santa Maria Novella](/europe/italy/tuscany/florence/santa_maria_novella) near the station has Masaccio's Holy Trinity — the first painting to use mathematical perspective — and Ghirlandaio's frescoes. [Museo di San Marco](/europe/italy/tuscany/florence/museo_san_marco) has Fra Angelico's extraordinary cell frescoes, one per monk, each a meditation on the life of Christ. [Museo Galileo](/europe/italy/tuscany/florence/museo_galileo) has two of Galileo's original telescopes and his preserved middle finger.

The [Oltrarno](/europe/italy/tuscany/florence/oltrarno), on the south bank of the Arno, is a different city. The [Palazzo Pitti](/europe/italy/tuscany/florence/palazzo_pitti_boboli) and [Boboli Gardens](/europe/italy/tuscany/florence/bobolipark) are here, as is Pontormo's Deposition in [Santa Felicita](/europe/italy/tuscany/florence/santafelicita), and the neighbourhood around [Piazza Santo Spirito](/europe/italy/tuscany/florence/piazza_santo_spirito) has a local character that the north bank has largely lost to tourism.

Florence's food is straightforward and excellent. Bistecca alla Fiorentina — thick-cut T-bone, grilled rare over charcoal — is the signature. The [Mercato Centrale](/europe/italy/tuscany/florence/mercatocentrale) has some of the best ingredients in the city. For the purest version of Florentine lunch, [Trattoria Mario](/europe/italy/tuscany/florence/trattoria_mario) has operated on communal benches steps from the market since 1953.
