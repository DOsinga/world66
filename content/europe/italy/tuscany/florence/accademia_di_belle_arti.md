The Accademia di Belle Arti di Firenze is the teaching institution that gives the adjacent Galleria dell'Accademia its name — not the museum itself, but the art school founded by Cosimo I de' Medici, Giorgio Vasari, and Baccio Bandinelli in 1563, making it the oldest academy of fine arts in Europe. The school continues to train painters, sculptors, and designers, and its student degree exhibitions (held annually in June) are open to the public.

The building on Via Ricasoli is entered separately from the Galleria. The school's own collection, which includes drawings, prints, casts, and historical teaching materials, is occasionally open for visits and exhibitions. For travellers interested in contemporary Florentine art production rather than Renaissance masterpieces, a degree show here provides a useful counterpoint to the museum circuit. The school is also a good source of artist-run studios and events in the surrounding university neighbourhood.
