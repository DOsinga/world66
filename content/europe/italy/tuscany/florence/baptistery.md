Florence's octagonal baptistery, dedicated to John the Baptist (patron of the city), is older than the cathedral it faces. The interior is covered in Byzantine-style mosaics on a vast scale — some ten million tesserae covering the ceiling with a Last Judgment, paradise, hell, and biblical scenes completed between 1240 and 1300. The effect, looking up into the dome, is overwhelming.

The exterior bronze doors are equally extraordinary. The east doors — Ghiberti's "Gates of Paradise," completed 1447 — depict ten Old Testament scenes in gold relief, each a masterpiece of Renaissance perspective and naturalism. The originals are in the Museo dell'Opera del Duomo; the doors on the building are replicas, but well worth studying in detail. The competition panels for the north doors commission (1401) — submitted by Ghiberti and Brunelleschi — are often cited as the moment Renaissance art was born.
