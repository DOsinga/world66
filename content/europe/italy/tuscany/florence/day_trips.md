Tuscany is the ideal territory for day trips from Florence. Trains and buses connect the main towns efficiently, and the landscape between them — cypress avenues, olive groves, vine-covered hills — rewards a slower journey by car or even bicycle.

**Siena** (1.5 hours by bus or train) is the most compelling day trip — a medieval city frozen in time since the 14th century, with the Campo as one of the great urban spaces in Europe and a civic museum containing Sienese painting at its peak. Go by bus via the Chiana valley for views.

**Pisa** (1 hour by train) is underrated beyond the Leaning Tower. The Campo dei Miracoli — cathedral, baptistery, tower, and cemetery together — is a genuinely extraordinary ensemble.

**San Gimignano** (1.5 hours by bus) has its medieval towers intact, tremendous Vernaccia white wine, and the extraordinary Collegiata church. Worth a half-day.

The Chianti wine road south toward Siena is the classic Tuscan drive — book a car and spend a day at cantinas in Greve or Radda. Fiesole, 20 minutes by bus above Florence, has Etruscan and Roman remains and fine views back over the city.
