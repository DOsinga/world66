The streets around San Frediano and the broader Oltrarno — particularly the lanes off Borgo San Frediano, Via Santo Spirito, and Via Maggio — contain a concentration of working artisan workshops that has diminished sharply over the past two decades but still survives. Gilders, picture restorers, furniture makers, hand-bookbinders, ceramicists, and marble workers occupy ground-floor spaces that would long since have become apartments or boutiques in other parts of the city.

Many workshops are open to the street or have glass doors through which the work is visible. Some welcome visitors who knock and express genuine interest. The Florence Craftsmen trail (organised by the city) maps the remaining workshops, but simply wandering the side streets between Porta San Frediano and Piazza Santo Spirito on a weekday morning will yield more encounters with active craft production than any museum of decorative arts. This is particularly worthwhile for anyone interested in traditional woodworking, gilding, or bookbinding techniques that have been practised in this neighbourhood continuously since the Renaissance.
