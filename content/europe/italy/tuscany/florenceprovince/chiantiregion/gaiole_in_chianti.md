Gaiole in Chianti sits on a hill near the northern course of the Arbia river, deep in the wine country between Florence and Siena. This is the quieter, more rural side of Chianti — fewer tour buses, more working vineyards, and a surprising concentration of medieval castles and fortified farmhouses that recall the centuries when this borderland was fought over by the two rival cities.

The [Castello di Brolio](/europe/italy/tuscany/florenceprovince/chiantiregion/gaiole_in_chianti/castello_di_brolio), seat of the Ricasoli family since 1141, dominates the landscape south of town and is open to visitors — the views from its walls stretch across an ocean of vines and olive groves. Closer to Gaiole, the [Badia a Coltibuono](/europe/italy/tuscany/florenceprovince/chiantiregion/gaiole_in_chianti/badia_a_coltibuono) produces excellent wine and olive oil from an 11th-century monastic estate. The comune has mapped out six itineraries forming a "Chianti castles route" that threads through towers, fortified churches, and Romanesque pievi — including the [Pieve di Spaltenna](/europe/italy/tuscany/florenceprovince/chiantiregion/gaiole_in_chianti/pieve_di_spaltenna) and the [Castello di Meleto](/europe/italy/tuscany/florenceprovince/chiantiregion/gaiole_in_chianti/castello_di_meleto) — in the surrounding hills.

Viticulture and olive growing remain the lifeblood of the area, as they have been for centuries. Gaiole is a good base for exploring Chianti on foot or by car, with far fewer crowds than Greve or Radda.
