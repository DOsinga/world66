Impruneta is a small town in the Chianti hills, just 13 km south of [Florence](/europe/italy/tuscany/florence). It has been famous for centuries for its [terracotta](/europe/italy/tuscany/florenceprovince/chiantiregion/impruneta/terracotta_workshops) — the distinctive red clay tiles and pots that you see across Tuscany are often made here. The town's kilns have been firing since at least the Renaissance, when Brunelleschi reportedly sourced tiles from Impruneta for the dome of Florence cathedral.

The main square, Piazza Buondelmonti, is dominated by the [Basilica of Santa Maria dell'Impruneta](/europe/italy/tuscany/florenceprovince/chiantiregion/impruneta/basilica), which houses a venerated icon of the Virgin Mary attributed to St Luke. In October, the [Fiera di San Luca](/europe/italy/tuscany/florenceprovince/chiantiregion/impruneta/fiera_di_san_luca) is one of the oldest agricultural fairs in Tuscany — livestock, food, and of course terracotta.

The surrounding countryside is classic Chianti: olive groves, vineyards, and cypress-lined roads. Just west of the town, the [Florence American Cemetery](/europe/italy/tuscany/florenceprovince/chiantiregion/impruneta/florence_american_cemetery) is a sober counterpoint to the surrounding vineyards. Impruneta makes a good base for visiting Florence without staying in the city.
