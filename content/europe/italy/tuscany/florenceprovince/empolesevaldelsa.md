The Empolese Valdelsa is the southwestern corner of the Florentine province, running along the Arno valley and the Elsa tributary toward Siena. It is not the most famous part of Tuscany — it lacks the concentrated tourist infrastructure of Chianti or the Valdichiana — but it has a quiet character that rewards travelers who take time to explore it. The towns are genuinely Tuscan rather than tourist-managed, the landscape is excellent, and there are real historical claims to anyone's attention.

The most significant of those claims belongs to [Vinci](/europe/italy/tuscany/florenceprovince/empolesevaldelsa/vinci), birthplace of Leonardo da Vinci, whose two dedicated museums draw visitors from across the province. The biggest town is [Empoli](/europe/italy/tuscany/florenceprovince/empolesevaldelsa/empoli), a working city on the Arno rail line with a Romanesque church and a Renaissance art collection that punches well above its weight. [Certaldo](/europe/italy/tuscany/florenceprovince/empolesevaldelsa/certaldo) is a walled medieval hill town associated with Boccaccio, the author of the Decameron; [Montaione](/europe/italy/tuscany/florenceprovince/empolesevaldelsa/montaione) and [Gambassi Terme](/europe/italy/tuscany/florenceprovince/empolesevaldelsa/gambassiterme) are small spa towns in the hills.

The zone is a practical base for day trips: Florence and Siena are both within an hour, San Gimignano is very close, and the route south along the Elsa valley follows the ancient Via Francigena pilgrim road. The olive oil and Chianti produced in the Montespertoli hills, southwest of Empoli, are good enough to be the focus of their own festivals in late autumn.
