Cerreto Guidi is a quiet Tuscan hill town with an outsized historical legacy: its Medici hunting villa, commissioned by Cosimo I in the sixteenth century, is one of the UNESCO-listed Medici Villas and now houses a museum dedicated to Isabella de' Medici, the brilliant and tragic daughter who died here in 1576. That story alone makes Cerreto Guidi worth visiting, but the town offers more than a single landmark. Perched between the Montalbano hills to the east and the vast Padule di Fucecchio wetlands to the west, it sits in a landscape of vineyards and olive groves that has changed remarkably little over the centuries.

The town itself is small and unhurried — a central piazza, a handful of churches, and the distinctive paired ramps of the Medici Villa rising above it all. The surrounding municipality produces Chianti wine under the Empolese DOC, and the country roads threading through the hills lead past farmhouses and cypress-lined lanes that embody everything people mean when they picture Tuscany. Vinci, birthplace of Leonardo da Vinci, is just six kilometres away, making Cerreto Guidi an excellent and less-crowded base for exploring both Medici and Leonardo country.

What draws people here is the combination of history and calm. There are no tour-bus crowds, no queues, and no souvenir shops blocking the view. In July the town stages La Notte d'Isabella, a two-day festival celebrating the life and cultural legacy of Isabella de' Medici with period music, costumes, and evening events in the piazza.
