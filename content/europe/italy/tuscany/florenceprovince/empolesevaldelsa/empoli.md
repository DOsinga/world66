Empoli is a working town in the Arno valley about 30 km west of Florence. It is not a tourist destination in the usual sense, but it has a compact historic centre organised around Piazza Farinata degli Uberti that rewards a short visit.

The most important building on the piazza is the [Collegiata di Sant'Andrea](/europe/italy/tuscany/florenceprovince/empolesevaldelsa/empoli/collegiata_santandrea), a Romanesque church with a striking green-and-white marble facade. Inside, the adjacent museum houses a collection of Renaissance art that would be the pride of a much larger town, including works by Masolino da Panicale and Filippino Lippi. The [Palazzo Ghibellino](/europe/italy/tuscany/florenceprovince/empolesevaldelsa/empoli/palazzo_ghibellino), also on the square, houses the Museo Paleontologico and the town's historical archive.

Empoli appears in documents from the 8th century as a castle, but the town as a civic entity dates from 1119. Today it is a practical place — a transport hub on the Florence-Pisa rail line, handy for day trips to the smaller Tuscan towns of the Valdelsa.
