Vinci is a small hilltop town in the Empolese Valdelsa, about 40 kilometres west of Florence, and it owes its fame entirely to one man: Leonardo da Vinci was born here on 15 April 1452. The town has embraced this legacy with two dedicated museums — the [Museo Leonardiano](/europe/italy/tuscany/florenceprovince/empolesevaldelsa/vinci/museo_leonardiano) in the medieval castle and the [Casa Natale di Leonardo](/europe/italy/tuscany/florenceprovince/empolesevaldelsa/vinci/casa_natale_di_leonardo) at the actual farmhouse where he was born — and together they make a compelling half-day stop for anyone traveling between Florence and Pisa or Lucca. A third option, the private [Museo Ideale Leonardo da Vinci](/europe/italy/tuscany/florenceprovince/empolesevaldelsa/vinci/museo_ideale_leonardo), approaches him more broadly as thinker and artist and is worth combining with the others.

Beyond Leonardo, Vinci sits on the southern slopes of Montalbano, a forested ridge that separates the Arno valley from the Valdinievole. The agricultural landscape around the town has changed little in centuries: terraced olive groves, Chianti vineyards, and small hilltop villages connected by a network of footpaths and cycle tracks. [Path 14 (the Strada Verde)](/europe/italy/tuscany/florenceprovince/empolesevaldelsa/vinci/strada_verde) runs from the town center up to Leonardo's birthplace in about an hour's walk through the olive trees — a genuinely pleasant way to link the two museums.

The town itself is compact and easy to explore on foot. The [Castello dei Conti Guidi](/europe/italy/tuscany/florenceprovince/empolesevaldelsa/vinci/castello_dei_conti_guidi) dominates the center, its medieval tower visible from the valley below. Around it, the streets are quiet and unpretentious — this is not a tourist trap, and most visitors come for the morning, see the museums, eat lunch, and leave. That's a reasonable plan. If you want to stay longer, the footpath network gives you reason to.
