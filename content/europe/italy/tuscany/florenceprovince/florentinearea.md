The Florentine area is the cluster of towns and villages immediately surrounding [Florence](/europe/italy/tuscany/florence), forming the innermost ring of the Florentine province. Where the Chianti and Mugello regions have their own distinct characters, the Florentine area proper is made up of places that are effectively in dialogue with the city — visible from it, accessible by bus or bicycle, and shaped by centuries of Florentine patronage and building.

[Fiesole](/europe/italy/tuscany/florenceprovince/florentinearea/fiesole) is the most visited, sitting on a hill above Florence with Etruscan walls, a Roman theatre, and some of the finest views in Tuscany. [Bagno a Ripoli](/europe/italy/tuscany/florenceprovince/florentinearea/bagnoaripoli) spreads across the southeastern hills along the right bank of the Arno — quieter and more residential, dotted with Romanesque churches and olive groves, with wine produced on small family farms. [Pratolino](/europe/italy/tuscany/florenceprovince/florentinearea/pratolino) to the north is worth the trip almost entirely for the Parco Demidoff, the former Medici park whose centrepiece is Giambologna's colossal stone Apennine figure crouched over a pool.

All three are reachable from central Florence in under an hour by bus or car. The area rewards the traveller willing to step off the main tourist circuit: the landscape is classic Florentine countryside, and the crowds are a fraction of what you'll find inside the city walls.
