  Fiesole predates Rome itself. The Etruscans established a settlement here in the 7th century BC — centuries before the Romans arrived and built the theatre you see today. When the Romans founded Florence (Florentia) in 59 BC in the valley below, Fiesole was already an old city looking down on them.
---

The Zona Archeologica is the best reason to come to Fiesole. Tucked just behind the main piazza, the site contains a Roman theatre built in the 1st century BC and expanded under Augustus, with the original stone seating still largely intact. In summer, the theatre hosts outdoor performances; in any season it's a remarkable thing to stand in — a functioning performance space built two thousand years ago on a Tuscan hilltop.

The archaeological area extends beyond the theatre to include Roman baths, an Etruscan temple terrace with fragmentary walls, and a stretch of city walls showing both Etruscan and Roman construction phases. The attached Museo Civico Archeologico holds the finds from the excavations — bronze objects, funerary urns, coins, and votive statues that trace Fiesole from its Etruscan origins through Roman settlement. Allow at least ninety minutes for the full site and museum. The ticket covers both.
