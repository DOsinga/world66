The Valdarno Superiore — Upper Arno Valley — is the stretch of the Arno between Florence and Arezzo, a corridor of farmland, quarries, and small industrial towns set between gentle hills. It lacks the immediate glamour of Chianti or the Mugello, but it has its rewards: early Renaissance connections, the birthplace of some remarkable thinkers, and good access to southern Tuscany.

The main town is [Figline Valdarno](/europe/italy/tuscany/florenceprovince/valdarnosuperiore/figline), birthplace of Marsilio Ficino (1433–1499), the philosopher who translated all of Plato's works into Latin for Cosimo de' Medici and shaped the course of Renaissance thought. To the north, [Incisa in Valdarno](/europe/italy/tuscany/florenceprovince/valdarnosuperiore/incisainvaldarno) was the birthplace of Petrarch. Both towns merged into a single comune in 2014, though they remain distinct places. The valley's sandstone cliffs, the Balze del Valdarno near Castelfiorentino, are a dramatic erosion landscape and an unlikely source of prehistoric fossils — the Arno valley has produced an unusual number of Pliocene finds.

The main rail line through the valley (Florence–Arezzo) puts the area within easy reach of both cities. It makes a sensible stop on the road south if you want to leave the main tourist flow behind for a day.
