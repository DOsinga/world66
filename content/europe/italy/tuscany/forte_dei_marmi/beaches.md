---
title: "Beaches"
type: section
---

The beach is the reason Forte dei Marmi exists as a resort, and it is a very particular kind of beach. Almost the entire seafront is divided into numbered *bagni* — private beach clubs — where rows of matching striped parasols and wooden cabanas are rented by the season or by the day. Families have held the same spots in the same *bagno* for three or four generations. Day visitors can almost always find a sunlounger for hire; the clubs also provide showers, beach bars, and waiters who bring cold drinks to your chair. A narrow strip of free public beach (*spiaggia libera*) exists at the northern and southern ends of the waterfront.

The sand is fine and pale — unusually white for the Tyrrhenian coast — and the water is clean and shallow close to shore, shelving gently enough to be safe for children. The backdrop of the Apuan Alps is exceptional: on clear days the snow-capped marble peaks are visible directly behind the town, a striking counterpoint to the blue sea.
