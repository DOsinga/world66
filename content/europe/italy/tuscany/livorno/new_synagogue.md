The New Synagogue of Livorno (Tempio Maggiore) was built in 1962 to replace the 17th-century original, which was destroyed in World War II Allied bombing raids. The current building is a striking example of mid-century sacred architecture — concrete and marble, with a large dome and a light-filled interior designed to evoke the grandeur of the lost original.

The synagogue stands as a reminder of Livorno's unique history as a city of tolerance. The *Leggi Livornine* of 1593 made Livorno one of the most welcoming cities in Europe for Jews, conversos, Greeks, and Armenians — at a time when most Italian cities had strict restrictions on non-Christian communities. The adjacent Piazza Benamozegh is named for the 19th-century Livornese rabbi and philosopher Elijah Benamozegh, one of the most important Jewish thinkers of the modern era.
