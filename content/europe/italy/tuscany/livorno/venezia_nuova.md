The Venezia Nuova district is Livorno's most photogenic neighbourhood, a 17th-century grid of canals, bridges, and warehouses that was designed to give merchants direct water access to the port. Four canals cut through the quarter, lined with ochre and terracotta buildings and crossed by small stone bridges. It is not Venice — it is rougher, quieter, less polished — but that is precisely the appeal.

The quarter was built during Livorno's free-port era and was originally the Jewish and merchant district. The synagogue (both the old and new versions) is nearby, along with the Dutch-German church that reflects the remarkable diversity of this trading city. Walking the fondamenta — the canalside paths — is the best way to see it. Early evening, when residents are out and the light is good, is ideal.
