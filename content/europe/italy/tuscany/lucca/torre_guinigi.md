Torre Guinigi is the most distinctive tower in Lucca — a tall medieval tower house with a clump of holm oak trees growing incongruously from a garden on its summit, visible from all over the city. The tower was built by the wealthy Guinigi family in the late 14th century as a combination of defensive fortification and status symbol, in the Lucchese tradition of tower-building as conspicuous display of civic power.

The 230 steps to the top are worth climbing for the view over Lucca's terracotta rooftops and the surrounding Tuscan plain, and for the close-up spectacle of the trees themselves — ancient holm oaks whose thick trunks and canopy provide shade for the handful of people who make the climb. The best view of the tower from outside is from Via Sant'Andrea, looking south.
