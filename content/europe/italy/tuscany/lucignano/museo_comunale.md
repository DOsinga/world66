  The Albero d'Oro — the Golden Tree — is one of the great treasures of Sienese Gothic goldsmithing. Dating from the 1350s and worked on by at least three generations of craftsmen, it stands nearly two metres tall and holds the relics of the Holy Thorn and the True Cross inside its gilded silver branches. It was made as a devotional object for the church of San Francesco and stayed in Lucignano even as armies and rulers came and went around it.

The Palazzo Comunale houses Lucignano's small but excellent civic museum, whose centrepiece is the Albero d'Oro (Golden Tree), a masterpiece of medieval goldsmithing that ranks among the finest reliquaries in Italy. The tree, dating from the mid-fourteenth century, is a dazzling construction of gilded silver, coral, enamel, and precious stones fashioned in the shape of a branching tree. It is the kind of object that justifies a detour on its own.

The museum also holds a collection of Sienese and Aretine paintings from the thirteenth to the sixteenth centuries, including works attributed to Bartolo di Fredi, Luca Signorelli, and the workshop of Lippo Vanni. For a small-town museum in a town of fewer than four thousand people, the quality is genuinely surprising.
