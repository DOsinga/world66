  San Francesco was the original home of the Golden Tree reliquary before it was moved to the Palazzo Comunale museum. The church was built in the thirteenth and fourteenth centuries, and its simple Romanesque-Gothic exterior belies a surprisingly rich interior: the nave walls retain extensive fresco cycles, including fourteenth-century scenes from the lives of Saints Francis and Anthony. The church of San Francesco sits near the top of the town, just below the Collegiata, and is one of Lucignano's two major medieval churches. Its Romanesque-Gothic facade is understated — plain stone and a pointed portal — but the interior holds genuine treasures.

The fresco cycle on the nave walls dates from the fourteenth century and depicts scenes from the lives of several saints. The quality is uneven, but the best fragments show the assured hand of painters working in the Sienese tradition. The church also preserves a carved stone sacristy doorway and several wooden choir stalls of considerable age.
