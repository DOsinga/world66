Montecatini Terme is Tuscany's famous spa town, sitting on the plain between [Pistoia](/europe/italy/tuscany/pistoia) and [Lucca](/europe/italy/tuscany/lucca) at the foot of the Apennine foothills. It became fashionable in the late nineteenth century, and the Belle Époque thermal establishments — grand, ornate, and unhurried — are still the reason most people come. The grande dame of the batch is the Terme Tettuccio, a neoclassical pavilion of marble colonnades and painted ceilings where taking the waters remains a pleasantly theatrical ritual.

The town divides into two: Montecatini Terme itself on the valley floor, busy with hotels and trattorias, and Montecatini Alto perched on a hilltop above, reached by a century-old funicular. The Alto is far quieter — a medieval village with a central piazza, a small archaeological museum, and views across the whole plain towards Pistoia and Firenze. If you want to understand why the Medici and later the European aristocracy came here to recover, the Alto gives you the answer more clearly than any spa.

The surrounding area is good walking and cycling country, and day trips to [Florence](/europe/italy/tuscany/florence), [Pisa](/europe/italy/tuscany/pisa), and [Lucca](/europe/italy/tuscany/lucca) are all within an hour by train or car. Montecatini is also useful as a base: hotels are plentiful and generally less expensive than in Florence, and the railway station puts you on the main Viareggio–Florence line.
