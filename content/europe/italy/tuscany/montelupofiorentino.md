Montelupo Fiorentino is a small Tuscan town on the Arno river between Florence and Empoli, best known for a centuries-old tradition of ceramic production that once supplied the major courts and markets of Renaissance Italy. The town's position at the confluence of the Arno and Pesa rivers made it a natural trading point, and by the 15th century its workshops were producing maiolica of exceptional quality — exported across Europe and collected by the Medici.

The Museo Archeologico e della Ceramica di Montelupo tells this story in considerable depth across two sections: one devoted to prehistoric and Roman-era excavations of the Arno valley, the other to the town's ceramic history from its beginnings in the late 13th century through the Renaissance and beyond. A Roman villa dated to 80-60 BC, excavated in an adjacent park, can be visited by guided arrangement. The museum is modest in scale but serious in scholarship, and contains pieces of genuine beauty from Montelupo's period of greatest production.

The town's ceramic identity remains alive. A pottery market takes place on the third of the month at the exposition centre on Corso Garibaldi, and the high point of the calendar is the International Pottery Fair in the last week of June, with exhibitions, historical re-enactments, and market stalls. Several of the town's ceramic factories welcome visitors by appointment. Montelupo is easily reached by train from Florence on the Empoli line and makes a satisfying half-day excursion for anyone with an interest in craft history.
