Orbetello sits on a narrow causeway in the middle of a lagoon on the southern Tuscan coast, connected to the [Monte Argentario](/europe/italy/tuscany/orbetello/monte_argentario) peninsula by two sand tomboli that enclose the water on either side. The setting is extraordinary — a town floating between sea and lagoon, with flamingos wading in the shallows.

The town itself is a pleasant, working Italian place rather than a polished tourist centre. The [Spanish fortifications](/europe/italy/tuscany/orbetello/spanish_walls) date from the 16th century, when Orbetello was the capital of the tiny Stato dei Presidi. The [cathedral](/europe/italy/tuscany/orbetello/cattedrale) on the main piazza is handsome, the evening passeggiata along the lungomare is lively, and the seafood — particularly bottarga and eel from the lagoon — is excellent.

The [lagoon](/europe/italy/tuscany/orbetello/laguna_orbetello) is a WWF nature reserve and an important wetland habitat. Birdwatching is good year-round, with flamingos, herons, and osprey. [Monte Argentario](/europe/italy/tuscany/orbetello/monte_argentario), reached by either causeway, has rocky coves, good diving, and the harbour towns of Porto Santo Stefano and Porto Ercole. The [Giannella tombolo](/europe/italy/tuscany/orbetello/giannella) has a long sandy beach, and the wilder [Feniglia](/europe/italy/tuscany/orbetello/feniglia) on the southern side has a long pine forest path closed to cars. Orbetello is one of those places where the landscape does most of the work — you could spend a week here swimming, eating fish, and watching the light change over the lagoon.
