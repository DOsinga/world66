Pistoia is a handsome Tuscan city that sits in the shadow of its famous neighbours — [Florence](/europe/italy/tuscany/florence) is 35 km east, [Lucca](/europe/italy/tuscany/lucca) 45 km west — and benefits from the relative neglect. The historic centre is compact and almost entirely medieval, built around the [Piazza del Duomo](/europe/italy/tuscany/pistoia/piazza_del_duomo), one of the finest squares in Tuscany.

The [Cathedral of San Zeno](/europe/italy/tuscany/pistoia/cathedral_of_san_zeno) holds a silver altar of Saint James that took two centuries to complete, with contributions attributed to Brunelleschi. The octagonal [Baptistery](/europe/italy/tuscany/pistoia/baptistery) opposite is striped in green and white marble. Wandering the streets around the piazza you find Romanesque churches at every turn — [San Giovanni Fuoricivitas](/europe/italy/tuscany/pistoia/san_giovanni_fuoricivitas) with its spectacular striped marble flank, [Sant'Andrea](/europe/italy/tuscany/pistoia/santandrea) with its extraordinary pulpit by Giovanni Pisano, and the [Ospedale del Ceppo](/europe/italy/tuscany/pistoia/ospedale_del_ceppo) with its remarkable Della Robbia frieze running the length of the portico.

Pistoia feels like a lived-in Italian city rather than a museum piece. The Wednesday and Saturday market fills the piazza, and the bar scene along Via del Lastrone is lively without being touristy. In July, the Giostra dell'Orso — a jousting festival dating to the 13th century — takes over the old town.
