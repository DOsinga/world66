Punta Ala is one of Tuscany's most exclusive seaside resorts, a manicured promontory jutting into the Tyrrhenian Sea with views toward the Island of Elba. Unlike the crowded Versilia coast further north, Punta Ala feels contained and unhurried — a place of pine-shaded paths, a well-kept marina, and long sandy beaches backed by Mediterranean scrub.

The name means "Wing Point," after the shape of the headland. Two sixteenth-century watchtowers, [Torre Nuova and Torre Hidalgo](/europe/italy/tuscany/punta_ala/torre_nuova), still stand on the hills above, built by Eleonora of Toledo to guard against Barbary pirates. The modern resort grew up in the twentieth century around a golf course and sailing club, and it retains that quietly upmarket character — polo matches, regattas, and long lunches by the water.

What makes Punta Ala worth a stop is the coastline. The beaches south of the [marina](/europe/italy/tuscany/punta_ala/punta_ala_marina) stretch for kilometres, alternating between broad sandy stretches and rocky coves accessible only by boat. [Cala Violina](/europe/italy/tuscany/punta_ala/cala_violina), a short drive or hike away through pine forest, is one of the most beautiful beaches in Tuscany — the quartz sand squeaks underfoot when you walk on it. The surrounding Maremma countryside offers Etruscan ruins, thermal springs, and hill towns within easy day-trip distance.
