San Quirico d'Orcia is a small walled town in the [Val d'Orcia](/europe/italy/tuscany/san_quirico_dorcia/val_dorcia_landscape), the stretch of southern Tuscany that appears on every other postcard of Italy — rolling hills, lone cypress trees, golden light. The town sits on the old [Via Francigena](/europe/italy/tuscany/san_quirico_dorcia/via_francigena) pilgrimage route and has an elegant Romanesque collegiate church, the [Collegiata dei Santi Quirico e Giulitta](/europe/italy/tuscany/san_quirico_dorcia/collegiata_dei_santi_quirico_e_giulitta), with three ornate carved portals that are worth a stop on their own.

The [Horti Leonini](/europe/italy/tuscany/san_quirico_dorcia/horti_leonini), a formal Italian garden laid out in the 16th century, sits within the town walls and is a peaceful spot with clipped box hedges and views over the valley. San Quirico is a good base for exploring the Val d'Orcia: [Pienza](/europe/italy/tuscany/pienza) and [Montalcino](/europe/italy/tuscany/montalcino) are both a short drive away, and [Bagno Vignoni](/europe/italy/tuscany/san_quirico_dorcia/bagno_vignoni_day_trip) — with its extraordinary thermal pool for a town square — is just 4 kilometres south. The landscape between them — the famous winding road lined with cypresses near Monticchiello — is one of the most photographed in Italy.
