The Val d'Orcia is a UNESCO World Heritage Cultural Landscape — not just a valley but a complete picture of how medieval and Renaissance Tuscany shaped the land to match a vision of beauty and productivity. The rolling clay hills, the lone farmhouses on ridgelines, the avenues of cypress trees along white gravel roads — all of it has been tended and maintained through centuries of deliberate cultivation.

The most photographed view is the winding road near Monticchiello and Pienza, with its famous double row of cypresses. You will have seen it on travel posters. It is, if anything, more beautiful in real life, especially in early morning light or after harvest when the fields turn amber. Drive the back roads between San Quirico, [Pienza](/europe/italy/tuscany/pienza), and Montalcino at walking pace — the landscape unfolds differently around every bend.
