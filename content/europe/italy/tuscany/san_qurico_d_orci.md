San Quirico d'Orcia sits at the heart of the Val d'Orcia, the stretch of southern Tuscany that appears on every postcard and desktop wallpaper you have ever seen of Italy. The rolling hills, lone cypress trees, and golden light are all real, and this small medieval town is one of the best places from which to take them in.

The town itself is a single main street lined with stone buildings, opening onto a couple of quiet piazzas. The [Collegiata](/europe/italy/tuscany/san_qurico_d_orci/collegiata), a Romanesque church with an elaborately carved portal dating from the 12th century, is the standout sight. Nearby, the [Horti Leonini](/europe/italy/tuscany/san_qurico_d_orci/horti_leonini) are a rare example of a Renaissance Italian garden, laid out in geometric box hedges and offering views over the surrounding countryside.

San Quirico has been a stop on the [Via Francigena](/europe/italy/tuscany/san_qurico_d_orci/via_francigena), the medieval pilgrimage route from Canterbury to Rome, for over a thousand years — Archbishop Sigeric of Canterbury recorded it by name in 994. Today it makes an excellent base for [driving the Val d'Orcia](/europe/italy/tuscany/san_qurico_d_orci/val_dorcia_drives) by car or bicycle. The famous cypress-lined road near the Podere Belvedere is a short drive south, and the thermal baths at [Bagno Vignoni](/europe/italy/tuscany/san_qurico_d_orci/bagno_vignoni) are just five kilometres away. [Pienza](/europe/italy/tuscany/pienza) and [Montalcino](/europe/italy/tuscany/montalcino), two of Tuscany's most celebrated small towns, are both within fifteen minutes.
