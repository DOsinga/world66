Bagno Vignoni is a tiny hamlet 5 kilometres south of San Quirico with a central square unlike any other in Italy. Where most piazzas have a fountain or a well, Bagno Vignoni has a large rectangular thermal pool, built during the Renaissance and still fed by hot volcanic springs. On cool mornings it steams, giving the village an otherworldly atmosphere.

The thermal waters flow from the pool down through channels to a spa complex at the edge of the village, where modern bathing facilities are open to the public. The historic pool itself is now preserved as a monument rather than a working bath. Walking around it and then down through the old tanning mills below the village — where the thermal water once powered a leather industry — is the most interesting way to spend an hour here.

The drive or walk from San Quirico along the Via Francigena to Bagno Vignoni takes about 90 minutes on foot, or 10 minutes by car. It is an easy and rewarding excursion.
