  The Horti Leonini were laid out in 1540 for Diomede Leoni, a local nobleman, as a resting place for pilgrims
  travelling the Via Francigena. The geometric box hedges were intended to give the garden a formal character
  appropriate to its civic purpose — it was, from the start, a public space as much as a private one, which
  explains why it has survived in something close to its original form while so many Renaissance gardens have
  been altered or destroyed.

The Horti Leonini are a Renaissance garden in the lower part of San Quirico, laid out in the 16th century and remarkably well preserved. The garden is arranged in geometric patterns of box hedge that have been maintained in their original form for nearly five centuries. Beyond the formal parterre section, there is a wilder wooded area with holm oaks that provides shade in summer.

The views from the garden over the surrounding Val d'Orcia countryside are excellent: the rolling hills, the scattered farmhouses, and the cypress-lined roads that make this one of the most photographed landscapes in Italy. In late afternoon, when the light turns golden, the view from the garden's upper terrace is particularly good.

Entry is free and the garden is open during daylight hours. It is a pleasant place to sit for 20 minutes before walking the main street of the town. In summer it hosts open-air events.
