The Via Francigena, the medieval pilgrim road from Canterbury to Rome, passes directly through San Quirico d'Orcia. The town has been a waypoint on this route for over a thousand years, and it retains some of the character of a pilgrim station — the Collegiata faces the main street along which travellers would have walked, and the Horti Leonini were explicitly created as a resting place for those passing through.

Today the Via Francigena is a marked walking and cycling route, and the stretch through the Val d'Orcia is among the most beautiful sections of the entire pilgrimage from Canterbury to Rome. Walking a section of it — from San Quirico to Bagno Vignoni (about 5 km) or continuing further south toward Radicofani — is the best way to experience the Val d'Orcia landscape at close range rather than through a car window.

Waymarkers show the Francigena route through town and out into the countryside. The walk to Bagno Vignoni is easy and takes about 90 minutes.
