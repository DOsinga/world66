Magione is a quiet Umbrian town on the eastern shore of [Lake Trasimeno](/europe/italy/umbria/magione/lago_trasimeno), about 20 kilometres west of Perugia. It is not a major destination, but it earns its place on the map: the lakeside setting is lovely, and the town has genuine medieval bones.

The name comes directly from its most interesting building — the [Castello dei Cavalieri di Malta](/europe/italy/umbria/magione/castello_dei_cavalieri_di_malta), locally called the Magione, which means "mansion" or "lodge" in medieval Italian. First documented in 1075, the structure started life as a Benedictine abbey and was later taken over by the Knights Hospitaller. What stands today is a compact fortress with a square plan, circular corner towers, and an interior courtyard lined on three sides with stacked loggias. It is one of the better-preserved Hospitaller buildings in central Italy, and worth seeking out.

The town also has a parish church and the church of the Madonna delle Grazie, dated to 1209. Neither is exceptional, but both are worth a quick look if you are already here.

The best short excursion from Magione is to [Montecolognola](/europe/italy/umbria/magione/montecolognola), a medieval hilltop village a few kilometres away with a small castle and views across Lake Trasimeno and Monte Melino. On a clear day the panorama is genuinely good.

Magione is primarily useful as a base for exploring Lake Trasimeno — the lake itself offers beaches, boat trips to the islands ([Isola Maggiore](/europe/italy/umbria/magione/isola_maggiore) has a small fishing community and a lace-making tradition), and a cycle path that circles most of the shore. If you are travelling by car through Umbria and want somewhere unhurried near the lake, Magione is a sensible stop.
