  In November 1502, a gathering known as the Magione Congress took place in this very castle. Cesare Borgia's enemies — including the Orsini family and several condottieri he had deceived — met here to plot his downfall. The castle that gives the town its name is one of the most complete surviving Hospitaller fortifications in central Italy. First documented in 1075 as a Benedictine hospice, it passed to the Knights of St John in the 12th century, who rebuilt it into the compact fortress visible today: a square plan with circular towers at the corners, thick stone walls, and an interior courtyard enclosed by two-storey loggias.

The structure is well-preserved and the interior courtyard has an austere, functional beauty. The loggia arcades give the fortress an unexpectedly elegant character for a military building. The castle can be visited on guided tours — check locally for current opening hours, as they vary with the season. It is the primary reason to come to Magione rather than simply drive through.
