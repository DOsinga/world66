The Basilica of San Salvatore stands in Spoleto's monumental cemetery on Ciciano hill, outside the medieval walls. This location makes it easy to overlook, but it deserves a detour: the church is one of the most important examples of early Christian architecture in Italy, built between the 4th and 8th centuries using columns salvaged from Roman temples. The carved decorations on the facade blend classical motifs with early Christian symbolism in a way that is historically extraordinary.

Inside, the church is stripped bare — no decoration, just columns, arches, and the silence of a very old building. The Roman columns lining the nave come from at least two different temples, visible in the varying heights and styles. UNESCO inscribed it in 2011 as part of a group of seven sites marking the Lombard presence in Italy.
