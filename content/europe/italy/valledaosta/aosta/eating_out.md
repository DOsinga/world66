---
title: Eating Out
type: section
---

Valle d'Aosta cuisine is mountain food built for cold winters and hard work. Fontina — the valley's semi-soft, nutty cow's milk cheese — appears in fonduta (the local version of fondue), melted over polenta, and layered into pasta. Carbonade is a slow-braised beef stew cooked in red wine, served with polenta or rye bread. Seuppa valpellinentze layers cabbage, Fontina, and bread in beef broth and bakes until golden — simple and deeply satisfying.

Lard d'Arnad (a cured fatback seasoned with rosemary and herbs, DOP protected) and Jambon de Bosses (a cured ham from the high Saint-Rhémy valley) are the charcuterie of the region and appear on antipasto boards across the city. Meals in Aosta tend toward the generous; the cooking tradition is more Piedmontese-alpine than Italian-Mediterranean, and portions reflect that. The local red wine, Torrette, made from Petit Rouge grapes grown on steep terraced vineyards, is the natural pairing.
