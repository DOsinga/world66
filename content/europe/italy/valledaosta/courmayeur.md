Courmayeur sits at the foot of Mont Blanc on the Italian side, a compact and attractive Alpine resort that draws weekend crowds from [Milan](/europe/italy/lombardia/milan) and [Turin](/europe/italy/piemonte/turin). It is one of the most fashionable ski towns in the Val d'Aosta, though its appeal has at least as much to do with the setting, the restaurants, and the nightlife as with the skiing itself.

The slopes are limited in size and difficulty — a keen skier will cover them in a day. But the [off-piste terrain and glacier descents](/europe/italy/valledaosta/courmayeur/skiing) on the Mont Blanc massif are spectacular, and the [Skyway Monte Bianco](/europe/italy/valledaosta/courmayeur/skyway_monte_bianco) cable car rises to 3,466 metres at Punta Helbronner with views that take in the full western Alpine arc. The Mont Blanc Tunnel connects Courmayeur directly to [Chamonix](/europe/france/alpes/chamonix) on the French side, making it easy to ski or explore both resorts.

In summer, Courmayeur is a base for hiking and climbing in the Mont Blanc range. The [Tour du Mont Blanc](/europe/italy/valledaosta/courmayeur/tour_du_mont_blanc), one of Europe's great long-distance trails, passes through, and the two lateral valleys — [Val Veny](/europe/italy/valledaosta/courmayeur/val_veny) to the south-west and [Val Ferret](/europe/italy/valledaosta/courmayeur/val_ferret) to the north-east — offer outstanding day hikes. The [Pré-Saint-Didier thermal baths](/europe/italy/valledaosta/courmayeur/pre_saint_didier_spa), five kilometres down the valley, are a favourite way to end a day in the mountains.

The town centre on [Via Roma](/europe/italy/valledaosta/courmayeur/via_roma) is pedestrianised, lined with cafes and boutiques, and genuinely pleasant for an evening passeggiata. The [Museo Alpino Duca degli Abruzzi](/europe/italy/valledaosta/courmayeur/museo_alpino_duca_degli_abruzzi) in the centre covers the mountaineering history of the massif from the earliest ascents onwards.
