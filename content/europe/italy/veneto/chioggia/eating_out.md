---
title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

Chioggia eats fish. The boats come in nightly, the [Pescheria](/europe/italy/veneto/chioggia/fish_market) opens before dawn, and most restaurants buy their seafood within sight of the kitchen. The classic Chioggia menu leans on lagoon and Adriatic specialities: *moeche* (soft-shell crabs, in season briefly in spring and autumn), *sarde in saor* (sardines marinated with onions, raisins, pine nuts, and vinegar), *bigoli in salsa* (thick wholemeal spaghetti with an onion and anchovy sauce), and *risotto di gò* (risotto with the small lagoon goby).

**Trattoria El Gato** on Campo Sant'Andrea is the long-standing favourite — Adriatic seafood, white tablecloths, sensible prices. **Osteria Penzo** on Calle Larga Bersaglio is tiny, family-run, and brilliant for sarde in saor and grilled cuttlefish. For something more contemporary, **Restaurant L'Ostricaro** specialises in raw seafood — oysters, carpaccios, and the famous Chioggia *canocchie*. On the seafront in Sottomarina, **Restaurant Vittore Pisani** has a long terrace facing the Adriatic and a proper fritto misto.

Finish with a glass of fragolino, the local strawberry-scented red, or a Spritz with Cynar instead of Aperol — a Veneto variation a few cafes still serve.
