The Ponte Vigo is the photogenic arched footbridge at the southern end of the old town, linking the Piazzetta Vigo with the eastern quayside. It was built in stone in the 17th century, replacing a series of wooden predecessors that had stood here since the Middle Ages.

The bridge is often compared to the more famous arched bridges of Venice — a comparison Chioggia people are happy to draw, since you can stand on this one without anyone trying to sell you a selfie stick. The view east over the canal, with the moored fishing boats and the pastel facades of the houses, is one of the iconic images of the town.
