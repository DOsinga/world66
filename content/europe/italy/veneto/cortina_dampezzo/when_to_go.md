---
title: When to Go
type: section
---

Cortina has two distinct seasons, with a quieter shoulder in between.

**Winter (December to March)** is when Cortina is at its most famous and most expensive. Christmas and New Year bring Italian high society; February is peak ski season. Snow conditions are generally reliable above 1,800 metres. Book accommodation months in advance for peak weeks. The 2026 Winter Olympics (co-hosted with Milan) will bring particular attention to the resort in February 2026.

**Summer (late June to September)** is excellent and increasingly popular. The hiking season peaks in July and August; wildflowers are at their best in late June and early July. Temperatures are mild — daytime highs of 20–25°C, cool at altitude — and the trails are in good condition. August can be busy but nowhere near as crowded as winter. Many visitors find summer the better time to come: the scenery is just as spectacular, the prices are lower, and you can actually see the mountains without ski goggles.

**Shoulder seasons (April–May, October–November)** are genuinely quiet. Many hotels and restaurants close. The mountains are less accessible and the weather unpredictable, though early May brings the first wildflowers to the valley floor. Avoid unless you specifically want solitude.

The Cortina Outdoor Festival in late August is worth timing a visit around if you are interested in trail running, mountain biking, and climbing events. The Christmas market in December is smaller and more authentic than those in South Tyrol but still pleasant.
