The Lido is the long, narrow barrier island that separates the Venice lagoon from the Adriatic — eleven kilometres of sand, pine trees, and Liberty-style architecture, reachable in fifteen minutes by vaporetto from San Marco. It feels like a different world from Venice: wide streets where cars actually drive, garden hedges hiding elegant villas, and a proper sandy beach facing the open sea.

The beach is the main draw in summer. It divides between free public sections — most easily reached near the vaporetto stops — and the grand stabilimenti, private beach clubs with striped cabanas and sun loungers that have drawn well-heeled visitors since the Belle Époque. The Excelsior and the Hotel des Bains were both immortalised in Thomas Mann's *Death in Venice*, and the seafront retains a faded grandeur that is quite distinct from Venice itself.

Every September the island transforms into the centre of global cinema for the Venice International Film Festival, the world's oldest. The Palazzo del Cinema fills with film crews and critics, red-carpet arrivals play out along the Lungomare Marconi, and even ordinary visitors catch the electric atmosphere. Outside festival season, the quieter eastern shore facing the lagoon is rewarding for a walk or a cycle: flat paths run along the waterfront with long views back toward Venice's skyline.

Take vaporetto lines 1, 5.1, or 6 from the Riva degli Schiavoni. Bicycles are available to rent near the ferry terminal and are the sensible way to cover the island's length.
