Padova — Padua in English — is one of northern Italy's most rewarding cities, often overlooked by travellers heading straight to [Venice](/europe/italy/veneto/venice) just 40 minutes away by train. That is a mistake. The city has one of the oldest universities in the world (founded 1222, where Galileo taught), the extraordinary [Scrovegni Chapel](/europe/italy/veneto/padova/scrovegni_chapel) with Giotto's frescoes — widely considered the starting point of Western painting — and the [oldest botanical garden](/europe/italy/veneto/padova/orto_botanico) in Europe.

The city centre is a pleasure to walk: arcaded streets, three linked piazzas that form the commercial and social heart of the city, and the vast [Prato della Valle](/europe/italy/veneto/padova/prato_della_valle), one of the largest squares in Europe, ringed by a canal and statues. The [Basilica of Sant'Antonio](/europe/italy/veneto/padova/basilica_santantonio) draws pilgrims from across the Catholic world, and the daily markets in the [Piazza delle Erbe](/europe/italy/veneto/padova/piazza_delle_erbe) are lively and affordable.

Padova is a university town first and foremost, which keeps it young, affordable, and full of good bars and cheap restaurants. The [Anatomical Theatre](/europe/italy/veneto/padova/anatomical_theatre) inside the university's Palazzo del Bo and the [Church of the Eremitani](/europe/italy/veneto/padova/eremitani_church) next to the Scrovegni Chapel are often skipped and should not be. It makes an excellent base for exploring the Veneto, or a rewarding day trip from Venice.
