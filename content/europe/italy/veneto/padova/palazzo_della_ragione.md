A fire in 1420 destroyed many of those paintings; what you see today is a later cycle. In the corner stands a wooden horse made for a 1466 joust, enormous and slightly threatening.

The Palazzo della Ragione is the medieval market hall and courthouse that divides the Piazza delle Erbe (herb market) from the Piazza della Frutta (fruit market) — the two main squares that form the social heart of Padova. The ground floor is still lined with market stalls selling food and produce, as it has been for 700 years.

The upper floor is a single vast hall — the Salone — which at 2,350 square metres is one of the largest roofed spaces in medieval Europe. The frescoes covering every wall depict an elaborate astrological cycle, the months, the planets, and the trades associated with each sign. The ceiling is ribbed into a kind of keel shape, like the inside of an upturned ship, and is supported only at the external walls.

The hall is used for exhibitions; the permanent fresco cycle is reason enough to visit. The view from the external loggia over the two markets is among the best in the city.
