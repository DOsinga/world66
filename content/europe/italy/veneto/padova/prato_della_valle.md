  Prato della Valle was a swamp and flood plain through most of Padova's history, used for markets, executions, and horse races but never successfully drained. In 1775, Venetian governor Andrea Memmo commissioned a radical redesign — an elliptical island surrounded by a canal, with 78 statues of the great men associated with Padova arranged along the canal's edge. Memmo oversaw the project personally, ran over budget, and was eventually recalled to Venice in some disgrace. He died believing the project had ruined him. The square is now one of the largest in Europe and Padovans consider it the finest thing in the city.

Prato della Valle is one of the largest urban squares in Europe — an elliptical lawn of about 90,000 square metres, surrounded by a canal crossed by four bridges and lined with 78 statues. The statues are of Padovans and people associated with the university: saints, doges, medieval scholars, condottieri. They range in quality from fine to mediocre but the collective effect, ranged along the canal on a clear evening, is genuinely imposing.

The square is used as a market space on Saturdays (antiques and collectables on alternate Saturdays), and as a venue for outdoor concerts and events in summer. The Abbey of Santa Giustina closes off the southern end with its large Baroque facade. At its best in the early morning or late afternoon when the light catches the statues and the canal, and the foot traffic is manageable.
