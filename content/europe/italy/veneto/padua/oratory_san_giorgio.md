Two small buildings beside the Basilica di Sant'Antonio are almost always overlooked by visitors hurrying into the main basilica, and each is a small treasure.

The **Oratory of San Giorgio**, a fourteenth-century funerary chapel for the Lupi family, is covered with a fresco cycle by **Altichiero da Zevio** — one of the masters of the late Trecento, working in a style that is more naturalistic and dramatic than anything else in northern Italy of his moment.

Next door, the **Scoletta del Santo** is the headquarters of the lay confraternity of St Anthony, decorated by an unusually starry roster of painters including **Titian** in his early twenties (three frescoes of the miracles of the saint, painted around 1511) and Domenico Campagnola. Combined ticket with the basilica; allow half an hour for both.
