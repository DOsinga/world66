Quarto d'Altino is a small town in the Veneto, about 15 minutes by train from Venice and a few kilometres from Marco Polo International Airport. For most visitors it functions purely as a transit point, but the town has an ancient history worth knowing.

Modern Quarto d'Altino sits near the site of Roman **Altinum**, one of the most important cities of northeast Italy before the barbarian invasions of the 5th century forced its population to flee to the lagoon islands — including the island that would become Venice. The [Museo Nazionale di Altino](/europe/italy/veneto/quarto_daltino/museo_nazionale_di_altino) holds finds from the Roman and pre-Roman settlement, including mosaics, inscribed funerary monuments, and everyday objects from a prosperous Roman city that otherwise left no standing ruins. Aerial photographs displayed in the museum reveal the ghostly street plan of Altinum still visible as crop marks in the surrounding farmland.

The town itself is unremarkable, but useful as a base if Venice accommodation is full or overpriced — trains run frequently and the journey takes about 15 minutes.
