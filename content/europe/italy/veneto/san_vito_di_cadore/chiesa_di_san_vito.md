The parish church of San Vito stands at the centre of the village, a compact baroque building whose tall campanile is the main vertical landmark in the townscape. The church dates in its current form from the 18th century, though a place of worship has stood on the site since medieval times. The interior holds carved wooden altarpieces and painted panels typical of the Cadore region, where a tradition of skilled woodcarving and ecclesiastical art flourished from the Renaissance onward.

The square in front of the church is the social heart of the village — a small piazza lined with benches and overlooked by a handful of bars, where locals gather in the early evening and visitors pause between activities. The village's traditional stone houses cluster tightly around it, giving San Vito a sense of place that has survived the ski resort development on its edges.
