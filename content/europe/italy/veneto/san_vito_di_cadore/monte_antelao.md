Monte Antelao, at 3,264 metres the highest peak in the eastern Dolomites, dominates the skyline above San Vito di Cadore from almost every point in the village. The great bulk of pale limestone rises sharply from the Boite valley, its profile unmistakable — a broad pyramidal summit sitting above a chaos of ridges, couloirs, and steep scree slopes. Locals call it the "King of Cadore," a title earned less by diplomacy than by sheer mass.

The standard ascent from San Vito or neighbouring [Borca di Cadore](/europe/italy/veneto/borca_di_cadore) is a serious alpine undertaking, typically done in two days via the Galassi refuge at 2,018 metres. The final section involves fixed ropes and demands a head for heights. Easier walks on the lower flanks — through the Val d'Oten or toward the Malga Tudaio — give access to the mountain's character without the commitment of a summit bid. In winter the Antelao flanks collect deep snow and should be treated with avalanche awareness.
