Treviso is a walled medieval city about 30 kilometres north of [Venice](/europe/italy/veneto/venice) that most visitors pass through on the way to somewhere else. That's their loss. The historic centre has canals, arcaded streets, towers, and a [cathedral with frescoes by Titian](/europe/italy/veneto/treviso/duomo_di_treviso) — all on a much more human scale than Venice, without the crowds or the prices.

The city is also the birthplace of tiramisu (still served at [Le Beccherie](/europe/italy/veneto/treviso/ristorante_beccherie), where it was reportedly invented in 1972), and the local food scene is quietly excellent. Radicchio di Treviso — the bitter red chicory grown in the surrounding hills — appears on every menu in autumn and winter. The covered [Pescheria](/europe/italy/veneto/treviso/pescheria) fish market on a canal island is one of the most atmospheric in the region.

Easy day trip from Venice (30 minutes by train) but comfortable enough as a base for exploring the Prosecco hills to the north.
