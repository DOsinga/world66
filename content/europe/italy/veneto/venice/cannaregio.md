Cannaregio is the largest of Venice's six sestieri, stretching from the train station to the northern edge of the old city. It is the most residential part of central Venice — the sestiere where Venetians actually live, shop, and eat, mostly out of earshot of the tourist circuit. The Strada Nova, running east from the station, is one of the few straight streets in Venice, a 19th-century urban cut through the medieval fabric.

The neighbourhood contains the Jewish Ghetto, the Ca' d'Oro, and the church of Madonna dell'Orto, which has major Tintorettos in a quiet setting. The fondamenta along the northern edge of the sestiere — particularly Fondamenta della Misericordia and Fondamenta degli Ormesini — are lined with small bars and restaurants where the clientele is mostly local. Come here for the feeling of a Venetian neighbourhood that has not been entirely given over to tourism.
