The Jewish Museum of Venice sits on the Campo di Ghetto Nuovo, at the heart of the quarter. Its collection traces five centuries of Venetian Jewish life through ritual silver, embroidered textiles, illuminated manuscripts, and everyday objects, displayed across a couple of compact floors above the campo.

The museum's real value is as a gateway to the synagogues. Four of the Ghetto's five scuole are hidden in the upper storeys of the surrounding houses, unmarked from the street, and the museum's guided tours are the only way to see them. Book a tour rather than just the museum — it is the difference between a small collection and an hour inside some of the most remarkable interiors in Venice.
