---
title: "Things to Do"
type: section
---

Vicenza is essential for anyone interested in architecture. Andrea Palladio (1508–1580) moved here as a young man and used the city as his laboratory — his work here influenced everything from English country houses to the US Capitol. The main showcase is the [Basilica Palladiana](/europe/italy/veneto/vicenza/basilica_palladiana), but the [Teatro Olimpico](/europe/italy/veneto/vicenza/teatro_olimpico) — the oldest surviving indoor theatre in the world, still with its original stage set — is equally extraordinary. Walking the city's streets is itself the experience; Palladian buildings appear around every corner, and [Contra Porti](/europe/italy/veneto/vicenza/contra_porti) alone concentrates more significant architecture per metre than most cities manage in an entire historic centre.

[Villa La Rotonda](/europe/italy/veneto/vicenza/villa_la_rotonda), two kilometres south of centre, is the most imitated private building in history. [Palazzo Chiericati](/europe/italy/veneto/vicenza/palazzo_chiericati) houses the civic museum and is itself worth visiting for the architecture alone. The [Loggia del Capitaniato](/europe/italy/veneto/vicenza/loggia_del_capitaniato) and [Torre Bissara](/europe/italy/veneto/vicenza/torre_bissara) anchor the main piazza. [Parco Querini](/europe/italy/veneto/vicenza/parco_querini) provides a green respite from marble and stone.

Beyond the Palladio story, [Antonio Pigafetta](/europe/italy/veneto/vicenza/antonio_pigafetta), the Vicentine who sailed with Magellan and recorded the first circumnavigation, deserves recognition that the city gives him only modestly.
