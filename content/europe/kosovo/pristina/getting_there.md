---
title: "Getting There"
type: section
---

Pristina Adem Jashari International Airport (PRN) is about 15km west of the city centre and handles flights from much of Western Europe — particularly from cities with large Kosovar diaspora populations such as Zurich, Vienna, Stuttgart, and London. Wizz Air, easyJet, and Turkish Airlines are among the carriers that serve it. There are no direct transatlantic flights.

From the airport, taxis to the city centre take around 20 minutes and cost roughly 20–25 euros; agree on a fare before you get in. A public bus (line 2A) also runs between the airport and the city, much cheaper but with less frequent service.

Overland, Pristina is connected by bus to Skopje (about 1.5 hours), Belgrade (about 6 hours, political complexities aside), Tirana (about 5 hours), and most other Balkan capitals. The Pristina bus station is central and functional. There is no passenger rail service to speak of — Kosovo's train network is minimal and slow.

Crossing from Serbia into Kosovo by land is technically possible but can be complicated for travellers entering from the Serbian side if they did not previously enter Kosovo from a third country — check current rules before attempting it.
