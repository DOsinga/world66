---
title: "Things to Do"
type: section
---

Pristina's attractions are concentrated in the compact city centre, easily covered on foot in a day. The mix of Ottoman, Yugoslav, and post-independence architecture tells the story of a city that has reinvented itself several times over.

Start with [Mother Teresa Boulevard](/europe/kosovo/pristina/mother_teresa_boulevard), the pedestrian axis where all of Pristina seems to pass at some point in the day. From there, the [Newborn Monument](/europe/kosovo/pristina/newborn_monument) and [National Library of Kosovo](/europe/kosovo/pristina/national_library_of_kosovo) — Pristina's most famous and most debated building — are a short walk toward the university. The [Bazaar of Pristina](/europe/kosovo/pristina/bazaar_of_pristina) and its surrounding Ottoman structures, including the [Great Hammam](/europe/kosovo/pristina/great_hammam_of_pristina), [Jashar Pasha Mosque](/europe/kosovo/pristina/jashar_pasha_mosque), and [Clock Tower](/europe/kosovo/pristina/clock_tower_of_pristina), are north of the boulevard and give a sense of the city before Yugoslavia.

The two American presidents have their own pilgrimage circuit: [Bill Clinton](/europe/kosovo/pristina/bill_clinton_statue) and [George W. Bush](/europe/kosovo/pristina/george_bush_statue) both have statues and streets named after them, testaments to a geopolitical gratitude rarely seen in Europe. The [Kosovo Museum](/europe/kosovo/pristina/kosovo_museum) is worth an afternoon for the Neolithic figurine collection alone.

Day trips: the [Gračanica Monastery](/europe/kosovo/pristina/gracanica_monastery) is 10km south and one of the Balkans' finest Byzantine buildings. The [Gazimestan](/europe/kosovo/pristina/gazimestan) monument on the Kosovo Polje plain, site of the 1389 battle and the 1989 Milošević speech, is 10km north.
