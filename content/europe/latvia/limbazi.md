Limbaži is a small town in Latvia's Vidzeme region, north of [Riga](/europe/latvia/riga) and west of [Cesis](/europe/latvia/cesis). It is not as immediately dramatic as the Gauja Valley towns, but it has enough history and local character to reward a slow half-day or day if you are travelling through northern Latvia.

The old centre grew around a medieval castle of the Archbishops of Riga. Today the [Limbaži Castle Ruins and Observation Tower](/europe/latvia/limbazi/limbazi_castle_ruins) are the main reason to stop, with the [Limbaži Museum](/europe/latvia/limbazi/limbazi_museum) next door adding context on local history and Baumaņu Kārlis, who wrote the Latvian national anthem.

The surprise is the [Live Silver Museum](/europe/latvia/limbazi/live_silver_museum), a small specialist collection built around large silver artworks. Add a walk to [Lielezers](/europe/latvia/limbazi/lielezers), the lake just south of town, and Limbaži becomes more than a quick castle stop.

It is a modest place, and that should be part of the plan. Come for ruins, a museum or two, and a Latvian small-town rhythm rather than nightlife or grand architecture.
