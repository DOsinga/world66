Ventspils is a port city on Latvia's northwest coast, about 190 kilometres from Riga. It has a reputation as one of Latvia's cleanest and best-kept cities — the municipality has drawn steady income from its busy commercial harbour and, to its credit, has spent much of it on parks, cycle paths, and a well-maintained [old town](/europe/latvia/ventspils/old_town).

At the heart of the centre stands the [Livonian Order Castle](/europe/latvia/ventspils/livonian_castle), dating to 1290 and among the oldest structures in Latvia. It now houses the Ventspils Museum, with exhibits covering the city's maritime and cultural history. The [riverside promenade](/europe/latvia/ventspils/promenade) along the Venta is a pleasant place to walk, with the [Boats of the Sun](/europe/latvia/ventspils/boats_of_the_sun) fountain and the painted [Cows of Ventspils](/europe/latvia/ventspils/cows_of_ventspils) — left-overs of a 2002 art exhibition that have become an unofficial civic mascot.

The [city beach](/europe/latvia/ventspils/city_beach) is broad and Blue Flag certified, backed by dunes and equipped with summer facilities. It draws Latvian families in good numbers but sees far fewer foreign visitors than Jūrmala — which is part of the appeal. Behind the beach, the [Seaside Open-Air Museum](/europe/latvia/ventspils/seaside_park) preserves a village of coastal Latvian buildings and runs a working narrow-gauge railway through the grounds in summer.

Stena Line ferries run from Ventspils to Nynäshamn in Sweden and to Travemünde in Germany, making the city a practical entry or exit point if you are travelling the Baltic by sea.
