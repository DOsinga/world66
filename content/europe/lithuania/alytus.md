Alytus straddles the Nemunas, Lithuania's largest river, in the southern part of the country. The city is divided into two halves by the river valley, connected by the [Bridge of White Rose](/europe/lithuania/alytus/bridge_of_white_rose) — one of Lithuania's longest pedestrian bridges — which also serves as the main promenade for evening walks above the forested banks below. It is not a destination that many international travellers seek out, but Alytus has a quiet appeal rooted in its natural setting and its sobering 20th-century history.

The city's WWII past weighs heavily. The [Vidzgiris Forest memorial](/europe/lithuania/alytus/vidzgiris_forest_memorial) marks the site where thousands of Jewish residents were murdered during the Holocaust, and the local museum addresses this history directly. On a different note, Alytus has reinvented itself as something of a beer town — it hosts an annual beer festival and is home to several craft breweries that have put Lithuanian brewing on the map.

The [Nemunas riverbanks](/europe/lithuania/alytus/nemunas_riverbanks) and surrounding pine forests offer good walking and cycling, and the [Dzukija National Park](/europe/lithuania/dzkija_national) is within easy reach to the east. If you are travelling through southern Lithuania between Vilnius and the coast, or heading toward the Polish border, Alytus provides a worthwhile pause with more character than the highway rest stops suggest.
