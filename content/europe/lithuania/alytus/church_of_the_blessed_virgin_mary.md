This Catholic church on the western side of Alytus is the most architecturally notable of the city's several churches. Built in the early 20th century, it stands on a rise above the surrounding neighbourhood and its twin towers are visible from much of the western part of the city.

The interior is worth a visit for its painted vaulting and the quality of the stained glass. Services are still held regularly. The church represents the particular texture of Lithuanian Catholicism — deeply rooted, sustained through Soviet-era suppression, and part of what makes cities like Alytus feel distinctly Lithuanian rather than simply post-Soviet.
