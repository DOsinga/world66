Grūtas Park, sometimes called "Stalin's World", is an outdoor Soviet sculpture park about 8 kilometres from Druskininkai. It brings together the statues of Lenin, Stalin, Dzerzhinsky, and other Soviet figures that were removed from Lithuanian public spaces after independence in 1990 — assembled in a pine forest, flanked by reconstructed camp watchtowers, barbed wire, and exhibition halls full of Soviet-era memorabilia.

The effect is genuinely strange: these enormous, ideologically charged monuments, which once commanded public squares and inspired either reverence or terror, now stand in a forest like embarrassed relatives at a party. The park is educational, slightly absurd, and oddly affecting. The indoor exhibitions — photographs, propaganda posters, party documents — add historical context. A playground shaped like a Soviet tank completes the surreal atmosphere. Allow one to two hours. The park is open year-round; direct minibuses run from the Druskininkai bus station.
