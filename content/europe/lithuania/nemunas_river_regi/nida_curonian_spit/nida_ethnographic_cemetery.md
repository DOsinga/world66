The little ethnographic cemetery on the northern edge of Nida holds one of the most complete surviving collections of krikštai — the unique wooden grave markers carved by the Kursenieki and Lithuanian fishermen of the spit. Each marker is carved from a single piece of wood in the shape of a bird, horse, plant or human figure, and was originally placed at the foot rather than the head of the grave.

The cemetery itself is small and quiet, set among pines on a sandy slope. A handful of headstones with German and Lithuanian inscriptions sit alongside the older krikštai. Free and open at any time.
