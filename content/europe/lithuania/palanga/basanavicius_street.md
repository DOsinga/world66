story: >
  Jonas Basanavičius, for whom the street is named, was the founding figure of the Lithuanian national movement — a doctor and folklorist who published the first Lithuanian-language newspaper (from Ragainė, then in East Prussia) and led the assembly that declared Lithuanian independence in 1918. His connection to Palanga was personal: he died here in 1927, and a plaque on one of the boulevard's buildings marks the house where he spent his last years.
---

Basanavicius Street is the pedestrian boulevard running from the town centre toward the pier, and in summer it is the main artery of Palanga's social life. The street is lined with restaurants, bars, amber jewellery shops, ice cream stands, and souvenir stalls — it can feel relentlessly commercial in July, but the sheer energy of Lithuanian summer holidaymakers makes it entertaining rather than oppressive.

In the evening the boulevard fills completely. Families with children, couples, groups of friends — everyone comes through here on the way to or from the pier. Music drifts from the bars and the smell of grilling meats and waffle cones fills the air. It's chaotic and rather charming.
