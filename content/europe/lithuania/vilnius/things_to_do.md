---
title: Things to Do
type: section
---

Vilnius has one of the largest surviving medieval old towns in Europe, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1994. Over 40 churches in styles from Gothic to Baroque to Neoclassical fill the skyline with spires. The range is extraordinary: the delicate Gothic tracery of [St. Anne's Church](/europe/lithuania/vilnius/stanneschurch), the overwhelming Baroque stucco of [SS. Peter and Paul](/europe/lithuania/vilnius/church_of_st_peter_and_paul), the neoclassical grandeur of [Vilnius Cathedral](/europe/lithuania/vilnius/cathedral).

Beyond the churches, [Gediminas Castle](/europe/lithuania/vilnius/gediminascastle) on its hilltop offers the best panorama of the city. [Vilnius University](/europe/lithuania/vilnius/vilnius_university) has 13 interconnected courtyards open to walk through. The [Palace of the Grand Dukes](/europe/lithuania/vilnius/palace_of_grand_dukes) tells the story of the Grand Duchy that once stretched from the Baltic to the Black Sea.

The [Museum of Occupations and Freedom Fights](/europe/lithuania/vilnius/museum_of_genocide_victims) in the former KGB headquarters — with its preserved prison cells and execution chamber — is one of the most honest accounts of Soviet terror anywhere in the former Soviet sphere. The [Jewish Quarter](/europe/lithuania/vilnius/jewish_quarter) tells the story of a community that once made Vilnius one of the great centres of Jewish intellectual life, before the Holocaust almost entirely destroyed it.

Across the river, the bohemian [Užupis](/europe/lithuania/vilnius/uzupis) district — self-declared independent republic since 1997 — rewards an afternoon of wandering. The [Hill of Three Crosses](/europe/lithuania/vilnius/hill_of_three_crosses) gives another elevated perspective and carries its own powerful story of cultural survival.
