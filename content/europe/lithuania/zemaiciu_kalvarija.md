Žemaičių Kalvarija is a small Samogitian village with an outsized place in Lithuanian Catholic life. Its name means Samogitian Calvary, and the village is built around a basilica, a hilly Way of the Cross, and one of the country's great pilgrimage traditions.

The centre is the [Basilica of the Visitation of the Blessed Virgin Mary](/europe/lithuania/zemaiciu_kalvarija/basilica_of_the_visitation), but the place only makes full sense on foot. The [Way of the Cross Chapels](/europe/lithuania/zemaiciu_kalvarija/way_of_the_cross_chapels) lead through lanes, slopes, and churchyard spaces, turning the whole village into a devotional landscape rather than a single monument.

Žemaičių Kalvarija is quiet for much of the year. In early July, during the Great Žemaičių Kalvarija Festival, it changes completely as pilgrims arrive for processions, Masses, and the old calvary route. Even if you are not religious, it is one of the clearest places in Lithuania to see how Catholic ritual, Samogitian identity, and village topography are tied together.
