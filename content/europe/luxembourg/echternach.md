Echternach is the oldest town in Luxembourg, founded around a Benedictine abbey established by the Anglo-Saxon missionary St Willibrord in 698 AD. The town was badly damaged during the Second World War — the [Basilica of St Willibrord](/europe/luxembourg/echternach/basilica_of_st_willibrord) and much of the centre were rebuilt after 1944 — but reconstruction has been successful and the historic core has recovered well. The narrow streets, [medieval ramparts](/europe/luxembourg/echternach/medieval_ramparts), and the arcaded [Place du Marché](/europe/luxembourg/echternach/place_du_marche) under the late-Gothic Denzelt town hall have considerable charm.

The main attractions are the [Basilica](/europe/luxembourg/echternach/basilica_of_st_willibrord) with its surviving early-medieval crypt, the surrounding [abbey complex](/europe/luxembourg/echternach/abbey_of_echternach), the [Prehistory Museum](/europe/luxembourg/echternach/prehistory_museum), and the [Roman Villa](/europe/luxembourg/echternach/roman_villa) on the edge of town — one of the largest north of the Alps. The real draw, though, is the landscape: the sandstone gorges and beech forests of the [Mullerthal Trail](/europe/luxembourg/echternach/mullerthal_trail), often called Luxembourg's "Little Switzerland". The most photographed feature is the [Wolf Gorge](/europe/luxembourg/echternach/wolf_gorge), a slot canyon a short walk north of the town.

Echternach is about 30 km northeast of [Luxembourg City](/europe/luxembourg/luxembourgcity), and immediately across the Sauer river from Germany — the bridge into Echternacherbrück makes a casual border crossing on foot.
