title: "Books"
type: section
---

Luxembourg has a small literary output disproportionate to its size. A few books that help a visitor understand the place:

**Anise Koltz, *At the Edge of Night*** — the grand old lady of Luxembourg poetry, writing in French after the Nazi occupation killed her German-language husband. The slim selected poems, translated into English by John Taylor, get to the heart of the layered, multilingual sensibility of Luxembourgish writers.

**Guy Helminger, *Etwas fehlt immer*** — short stories by a contemporary Luxembourgish writer (in German). His evocations of the city's gritty edges and EU expat absurdities are sharp and funny.

**Jean Portante, *Mrs. Haroy or the Memory of the Whale*** — a novel built around the Italian immigrant experience in Luxembourg, weaving Portante's family arrival from Abruzzo with the steel industry that brought them.

**Norbert Jacques, *Dr. Mabuse, der Spieler*** — the pulpy 1921 novel by a Luxembourgish author that inspired Fritz Lang's German Expressionist films. A reminder that the country has produced more than its image suggests.

For history, James Newcomer's *The Grand Duchy of Luxembourg* remains the best one-volume English-language account.
