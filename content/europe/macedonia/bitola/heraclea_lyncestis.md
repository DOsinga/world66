Heraclea Lyncestis is the most important archaeological site in North Macedonia outside Skopje, and it is unreasonably overlooked by visitors who pass through Bitola. Founded by Philip II of Macedon in the 4th century BC, the city became a significant Roman administrative centre after 167 BC, and the ruins that survive — particularly the mosaics — date mostly to this Roman and early Byzantine period.

The site, about 2.5 kilometres south of the city centre, is open-air and includes a porticoed street, a theatre, baths, and two large basilicas with exceptional floor mosaics. The Small Basilica mosaic in particular is remarkable: a continuous carpet of hunting animals, trees, and birds executed in minute tesserae, covering the entire floor area. The Great Basilica mosaic is more fragmentary but equally fine. Both have protective canopy structures over them.

The small on-site museum houses finds from the excavations, including Roman portraits, inscriptions, and decorative objects. Admission is inexpensive. The site is walkable from central Bitola but most visitors take a taxi. Plan a minimum of 90 minutes.
