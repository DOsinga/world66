The Jewish cemetery on the northern edge of Bitola is the most significant surviving trace of what was once a large and prosperous Sephardic Jewish community. The community dated back to the late 15th century when Spanish Jews were welcomed by the Ottoman Empire; at its peak in the 19th century, the Bitola Jewish community numbered several thousand and played a significant role in the city's commercial life.

The cemetery contains hundreds of graves with inscriptions in Ladino (Judeo-Spanish), Hebrew, and occasionally French, spanning several centuries. The oldest legible stones date to the 18th century. The cemetery has been restored with support from international Jewish organisations and is now a heritage site with interpretive panels explaining the community's history. It is accessible during daylight hours.
