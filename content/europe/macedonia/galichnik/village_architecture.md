Galichnik is one of the finest surviving examples of Mijak vernacular architecture in the Balkans. The village sits at around 1,400 metres on the slopes of Mount Bistra, and its stone houses with their distinctive wooden balconies and overhanging upper floors were built in the 18th and 19th centuries by the village's master builders — the Mijak people were renowned throughout the Ottoman Empire as skilled craftsmen.

Most of the houses have been empty for decades as younger generations moved to Skopje or abroad, but the absence of modern intrusions has preserved the village's character almost intact. Walking the stone lanes between these semi-abandoned mansions, many with locked gates and overgrown gardens, gives a vivid sense of how prosperous this remote community once was.
