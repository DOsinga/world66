The Čaršija, Skopje's Old Bazaar, is the largest and best-preserved Ottoman bazaar in the Balkans outside Istanbul. It dates back to the 12th century and expanded significantly under Ottoman rule from the 14th century onward. Today it is a dense quarter of stone-paved lanes lined with craft workshops, goldsmiths, tailors, tea houses, restaurants, mosques, and caravanserais — not a tourist reconstruction but a living neighbourhood where people work.

The main axis runs roughly east-west from the Stone Bridge across the Vardar River to the old commercial heart. Within the bazaar you'll find the Mustafa Pasha Mosque (1492), the Daut Pasha Baths (now the National Gallery), and the Kursumli An — a 16th-century caravanserai now used for exhibitions. The Church of the Holy Saviour just at the bazaar's edge contains the finest Macedonian woodcarving in existence: a 10-metre iconostasis carved by the Mijak school in the early 19th century. Spend at least a morning here; it is the reason to come to Skopje.
