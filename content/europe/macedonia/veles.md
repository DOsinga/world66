Veles sits in the middle of North Macedonia on the Vardar, a river city built into hills and long shaped by the road and rail corridor between Skopje, the south, and the Aegean. It is not a polished showpiece, but it has a strong local identity: writers, revolutionaries, pottery, old churches, and a steep urban landscape that feels very different from lakeside [Ohrid](/europe/macedonia/ohrid) or monumental [Skopje](/europe/macedonia/skopje).

The best introduction is the hill above town, where the [Kosturnica Memorial](/europe/macedonia/veles/kosturnica_memorial) looks over the Vardar valley. In the old quarters, the [Clock Tower](/europe/macedonia/veles/clock_tower) and [Saint Panteleimon](/europe/macedonia/veles/saint_panteleimon) show two different sides of Veles: Ottoman urban memory and Macedonian Orthodox craft.

Veles also rewards slow wandering. The theatre named for Jordan Hadzi Konstantinov-Dzinot points to the city's literary self-image, while the churches and monastery south of the centre make a good rough-edged walk if you do not mind uneven streets. For a break from town, [Lake Mladost](/europe/macedonia/veles/lake_mladost) is the easy local escape.

Most travellers pass Veles by train or highway. That is a shame if you enjoy second-tier Balkan cities where daily life is more visible than tourism.
