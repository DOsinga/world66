Bormla, officially Cospicua, is the oldest inhabited of the Three Cities on the southern shore of the Grand Harbour in [Malta](/europe/malta). The town nestles behind the historic [No. 1 Dock](/europe/malta/cottonera_or_three/cospicua_or_bormla/no_1_dock), built in the 1840s in what was once the Porto delle Galere. The dockyard here has been in use since Phoenician times, expanded by every successive ruler — the Knights of St John, the French, and the British all used it as their Mediterranean base for ship repair.

The town was laid out on a grid pattern in 1717, though some older streets survive from earlier periods. The [Church of the Immaculate Conception](/europe/malta/cottonera_or_three/cospicua_or_bormla/immaculate_conception), built in the Doric style, dominates the skyline and contains centuries of craftsmanship. The 17th-century [Cottonera Lines](/europe/malta/cottonera_or_three/cospicua_or_bormla/cottonera_lines) of the Knights wrap the town in a great arc of bastions, pierced by the baroque Notre Dame Gate; the inner [St Helen's Gate](/europe/malta/cottonera_or_three/cospicua_or_bormla/st_helens_gate) carries you through the Margherita Lines. The [Bir Mula Heritage museum](/europe/malta/cottonera_or_three/cospicua_or_bormla/bir_mula_heritage), a social history collection housed in a small palazzino on St Margaret's Hill, and the smaller [St Paul's Church](/europe/malta/cottonera_or_three/cospicua_or_bormla/st_pauls_church) round out the central sights.

Cospicua suffered badly in the Second World War — the dockyard was a prime target — and much of the town was rebuilt afterwards. Today it is quieter and less visited than neighbouring Vittoriosa (Birgu), but the waterfront regeneration around the docks is gradually changing that.
