Vittoriosa, known locally as Birgu, sits on one of the two promontories extending toward [Valletta](/europe/malta/valletta) across the Grand Harbour. It is the second oldest town in Malta after Mdina, and the first seat of the Knights of St. John when they arrived in 1530. [Fort St. Angelo](/europe/malta/cottonera_or_three/vittoriosa/fort_st_angelo), at the tip of the peninsula, was the Knights' headquarters during the Great Siege of 1565 and remains the town's most commanding landmark.

The old town is entered through [the Three Gates](/europe/malta/cottonera_or_three/vittoriosa/the_three_gates) — a sequence of fortifications that feels like stepping back centuries. Inside, narrow streets lead to the [Parish Church of St. Lawrence](/europe/malta/cottonera_or_three/vittoriosa/the_parish_church) (rebuilt by Lorenzo Gafa in the seventeenth century), the [Malta Maritime Museum](/europe/malta/cottonera_or_three/vittoriosa/malta_maritime_museum) in the old naval bakery, and the town square with its Victory Monument commemorating the Great Siege. The [Inquisitor's Palace](/europe/malta/cottonera_or_three/vittoriosa/inquisitors_palace) — the only intact Inquisition palace open to the public anywhere in the world — fills a medieval building a few alleys from the waterfront. Look also for [the Executioner's House](/europe/malta/cottonera_or_three/vittoriosa/executioners_house), whose medieval stonework still carries the eye-and-axes symbol of its original occupant's trade.
