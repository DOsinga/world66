Marsaxlokk is Malta's most photogenic fishing village — a sheltered bay on the southeast coast lined with [brightly painted *luzzu*](/europe/malta/marsaxlokk/luzzu_harbour), the traditional wooden boats with Eyes of Osiris on their prows. The harbour has been in use since Phoenician times, and fishing is still the main business here.

The [Sunday fish market](/europe/malta/marsaxlokk/sunday_market) draws half of Malta to the waterfront, where fishermen sell the morning catch alongside stalls of honey, capers, and sun-dried tomatoes. On any other day the quayside is quieter, and you can sit at one of the harbourfront restaurants eating grilled *lampuki* or octopus stew while watching the boats bob. The village is small enough to see in an hour, but the combination of colour, fresh seafood, and working-harbour atmosphere makes it one of Malta's essential stops. Combine it with a swim at [St Peter's Pool](/europe/malta/marsaxlokk/st_peters_pool) on the Delimara peninsula and a stop at the Ice Age caves of [Għar Dalam](/europe/malta/marsaxlokk/ghar_dalam) for a full south-coast day.
