Mdina is Malta's ancient capital, a fortified hilltop city in the centre of the island that the locals still call "the Silent City." The name fits. Behind the [baroque main gate](/europe/malta/mdina/mdina_gate), the narrow limestone streets are almost empty — no cars, very few residents, and a stillness that feels centuries old. The Knights of St John moved the capital to [Valletta](/europe/malta/valletta) in the 1570s, and Mdina has been quietly dignified ever since.

[St Paul's Cathedral](/europe/malta/mdina/st_pauls_cathedral) dominates the small central square, its interior floor paved with the tombstones of three hundred Knights and its vaulted ceiling covered in paintings by Mattia Preti. The attached [Cathedral Museum](/europe/malta/mdina/cathedral_museum) holds religious art, Dürer engravings, and a collection of Baroque music manuscripts unrivalled south of Naples. [Palazzo Falson](/europe/malta/mdina/palazzo_falson), a medieval nobleman's house, is worth a visit for its eclectic collection and cool stone rooms — seventeen rooms left exactly as their last owner arranged them in 1962.

From [Bastion Square](/europe/malta/mdina/bastion_square) at the city's highest point, you can see from the Grand Harbour to the northern coast. The [Palazzo Vilhena](/europe/malta/mdina/palazzo_vilhena) houses the National Museum of Natural History in one of the island's finest Baroque courtyards. Come early or late in the day — by midmorning the tour groups arrive and the silence is harder to find.
