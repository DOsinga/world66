Palazzo Falson is the finest medieval secular building in Mdina, and one of the best historic house museums in the Mediterranean. The structure dates from the Norman period — the ground floor is believed to be twelfth century — and was expanded over subsequent centuries while retaining its distinctive double-light windows and stone loggia.

The house is displayed as its last private owner, Captain Olof Frederick Gollcher, left it in 1962: seventeen rooms crammed with an extraordinary collection assembled over a lifetime. There are paintings attributed to Murillo and Nicolas Poussin, an Edward Lear watercolour painted during his Malta visit, over 4,500 books and manuscripts, silverware, carpets, armour, and a remarkable array of antique timepieces. The atmosphere is of a cultured man's home rather than a formal museum, which makes it unusually engaging.

Admission includes a guided audio tour. The rooftop terrace offers good views over the old city walls.
