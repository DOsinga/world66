---
title: "Books"
type: section
---

Moldova sits at the neglected edge of European literature, but a handful of books cast real light on what it means to live in one of the continent's most overlooked corners.

**The Good Life Elsewhere** by Vladimir Lorchenkov (translated by Ross Ufberg) is the place to start. This darkly comic Moldovan novel follows a group of villagers desperate to migrate to Italy by any means necessary — on tractors, in lorries, in improbable schemes. It is savage, funny, and tells you more about the desperation and humour of rural Moldova than any guidebook.

**The Transnistria Travelogue** — there is no single canonical book on this region, but Charles King's academic history **The Moldovans: Romania, Russia, and the Politics of Culture** provides essential context on the identity tug-of-war that shaped the country and still defines Soroca's border-town atmosphere. It is dense but rewarding.

**Isabel Fonseca's Bury Me Standing: The Gypsies and Their Journey** is not about Moldova specifically, but it is the best English-language account of Roma culture in Eastern Europe. Reading it before wandering through Ţigănia gives the neighbourhood's eccentric mansions a far richer meaning.

**Dracula** by Bram Stoker is set in the Carpathian region just across the border and is worth a reread for atmosphere. Soroca's limestone bluffs, its fortress, and the mist that rises off the Dniester in the morning have a gothic quality that Stoker would have recognised immediately.
