  King Nikola I built this palace in 1867 and it served as the official royal residence until Montenegro was absorbed into the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes in 1918. King Nikola was known to visitors as the "peasant king" and the simplicity of his palace was frequently remarked upon by European diplomats accustomed to Versailles-scale grandeur.

The Royal Palace of Cetinje houses the history collection of the National Museum of Montenegro. The building is a neoclassical structure from 1867, modest in scale and simple in decoration — exactly the right size for a mountain principality that ruled a territory smaller than some European counties.

The interior has been preserved with period furnishings, photographs, weapons, and diplomatic gifts that collectively tell the story of the Petrović-Njegoš dynasty and the 35-year reign of King Nikola I (1860–1918). The throne room, the library, and the private apartments are all accessible. The palace faces the monastery across a small park, which was historically the main public space of the capital.
