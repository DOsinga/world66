Herceg Novi sits at the entrance to the Bay of Kotor, just a few kilometres from the Croatian border. It's the first town you'll reach coming from Dubrovnik, and it makes for a pleasant introduction to Montenegro — less touristed than Kotor, more authentic than Budva.

The old town climbs steeply up the hillside, with a mix of architectural layers reflecting centuries of different rulers: the [Kanli Kula](/europe/montenegro/hercegnovi/kanli_kula) (Bloody Tower) fortress at the top, the [Sahat Kula](/europe/montenegro/hercegnovi/sahat_kula) (Clock Tower) in the main square, Ottoman mosques, and a baroque seafront. The [Forte Mare](/europe/montenegro/hercegnovi/forte_mare) castle sits right on the water. There are good views of the bay from the upper town.

Herceg Novi has a reputation among Montenegrins for its mild climate — mimosas bloom in winter, and it's noticeably warmer than inland areas. The [Pet Danica promenade](/europe/montenegro/hercegnovi/pet_danica_promenade) runs along the seafront below the old town, with restaurants and cafes that get lively on summer evenings.

The town is a good base for exploring the northern part of the bay, and a starting point for the ferry shortcut across the bay to Lepetane (saving the long drive around). It's about 40km by road to [Kotor](/europe/montenegro/kotor), or 15 minutes by ferry across the narrowest part of the bay.
