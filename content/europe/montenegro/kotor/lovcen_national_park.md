story: "When Petar II Petrović-Njegoš — Montenegro's prince-bishop, poet, and national hero — died of tuberculosis in 1851, he asked to be buried at the top of Mount Lovćen, the highest peak of Montenegro. His remains were placed there in 1855, moved during the Italian occupation in World War I, and finally laid in the present mausoleum at 1,657 metres in 1974."

Lovćen rises directly behind Kotor — a dramatic limestone massif that the serpentine road climbs in 25 hairpin turns, every one of them with a view back over the bay. The park itself is a high karst plateau of dark beech forest and grey limestone, with the Mausoleum of Njegoš at its summit reached by a tunnel and a final stair of 461 steps.

Cetinje, the old royal capital, sits on the eastern side of the massif. The whole drive from Kotor to Cetinje takes about an hour and a half; the *Kotor Serpentine* alone is one of the great mountain roads of Europe. Allow most of a day for a proper visit, including a stop at the mausoleum and lunch in one of the Njeguši villages on the way.
