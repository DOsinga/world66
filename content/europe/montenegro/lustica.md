Luštica is the long, hilly peninsula that closes the mouth of the [Bay of Kotor](/europe/montenegro/bayofkotor), separating the sheltered inner bays from the open Adriatic. It is one of the least developed stretches of the Montenegrin coast: a ridge of grey limestone planted with olive groves, scattered stone hamlets, and a string of coves on the seaward side that you mostly reach by boat. From the [Herceg Novi](/europe/montenegro/hercegnovi) promenade it sits directly across the water, low and green on the horizon.

For most visitors the peninsula is a day on the water rather than a place to drive around. The standard boat tour leaves the Herceg Novi seafront in the morning and works its way along the coast: the electric-blue interior of the [Blue Cave](/europe/montenegro/hercegnovi/blue_cave), the white pebbles and clear water of [Žanjic](/europe/montenegro/hercegnovi/zanjic_beach), and the perfectly circular Austro-Hungarian fortress on [Mamula Island](/europe/montenegro/hercegnovi/mamula_island) at the very mouth of the bay. The little fishing village of Rose, with its abandoned wartime submarine pens, is usually part of the same loop.

You can also reach Luštica by road, looping around the bay through [Tivat](/europe/montenegro/tivat) and across the Verige strait, but it is a long way round for what is a short hop by sea. Bring water and food if you are heading to the open-coast beaches; facilities are minimal once you leave the main coves, and the seaward side can turn rough quickly when the afternoon wind gets up. Go in the morning while the water is calm and the light in the cave is at its best.
