Podgorica is Montenegro's capital and largest city, with about 200,000 people — roughly a third of the country's population in one place. It's the administrative and economic centre, and despite its modest size, it has a genuine city feel: universities, embassies, a reasonable café culture, and some decent restaurants.

The city was heavily bombed in World War II and was rebuilt in a fairly utilitarian Yugoslav fashion. As a tourist destination it's not compelling — most travellers pass through rather than stay. But if you have a day, the old Turkish quarter of [Stara Varoš](/europe/montenegro/podgorica/stara_varos) — centred on the [Clock Tower](/europe/montenegro/podgorica/clock_tower) and the old mosque — gives some texture. The [Morača Canyon](/europe/montenegro/podgorica/day_trips) on the edge of town is worth a detour: a short drive north, the river cuts a dramatic gorge through limestone, and the 13th-century Morača Monastery sits inside it.

The [Cathedral of the Resurrection of Christ](/europe/montenegro/podgorica/cathedral_resurrection) is the most striking modern building in the city — a Neo-Byzantine structure completed in 2013, famous for its interior frescoes that include Tito, Marx, and Engels depicted among the damned. The [Millennium Bridge](/europe/montenegro/podgorica/millenium_bridge) over the Morača is the city's most photogenic modern landmark, especially at night.

The city sits in a valley and gets very hot in summer — temperatures of 40°C or above in July are not unusual, making Podgorica regularly one of the hottest cities in Europe during heatwaves. The coast is only an hour away if you need relief. [Gorica Hill](/europe/montenegro/podgorica/gorica_hill) provides forested walking just minutes from the centre.

Podgorica Airport (TGD) handles year-round international flights into Montenegro and is 15–20 minutes from the city centre by taxi. The main bus and train stations are adjacent to each other and central. Podgorica is a useful transit hub — buses and trains to [Kotor](/europe/montenegro/kotor), Budva, Bar, and Belgrade all originate here. Skadar Lake National Park is about 30km south, easily reached by car or taxi.
