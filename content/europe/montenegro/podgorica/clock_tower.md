The Clock Tower stands in the heart of Stara Varoš, Podgorica's Ottoman quarter, on Bećir-Beg Osmanagić Square. It's a simple square stone structure, 12 metres high, with a clock face added later — originally it would have been a minaret-style tower used to announce prayer times. The tower survived both World War II bombing and the Yugoslav-era reconstruction that demolished much of the old city, making it one of the more tangible links to Podgorica's Ottoman past.

You cannot enter the tower, but the square around it is the social heart of Stara Varoš — there are a few cafes nearby, and the neighbourhood's low stone buildings give the area a texture completely different from the concrete boulevards of the modern city. Worth combining with a walk through the rest of the quarter and a look at the Osmanagić Mosque a few minutes away.
