Lake Skadar is Montenegro's great inland water landscape, a broad, reed-fringed lake shared with Albania and backed by low mountains, fishing villages, vineyards, and Orthodox island monasteries. It is the country's softer counterweight to the Bay of Kotor and [Durmitor](/europe/montenegro/dormitor): less dramatic at first glance, but wonderfully rich if you slow down.

Most visitors enter through [Virpazar](/europe/montenegro/skadarlake/virpazar), where boat trips head into channels of lilies, reeds, and open water. From there you can reach [Besac Fortress](/europe/montenegro/skadarlake/besac_fortress), the ruined island forts of [Lesendro](/europe/montenegro/skadarlake/lesendro) and [Grmozur](/europe/montenegro/skadarlake/grmozur), and the birding areas that make the lake one of the Balkans' great wetland habitats.

The northern side has a different feel. [Rijeka Crnojevica](/europe/montenegro/skadarlake/rijeka_crnojevica) is a sleepy riverside town with an old stone bridge, while [Pavlova Strana](/europe/montenegro/skadarlake/pavlova_strana) gives the famous horseshoe view over the river arm leading toward the lake. On the water, [Kom Monastery](/europe/montenegro/skadarlake/kom_monastery), [Beska Monastery](/europe/montenegro/skadarlake/beska_monastery), and [Moracnik Monastery](/europe/montenegro/skadarlake/moracnik_monastery) make the lake feel as historical as it is natural.

Come in spring or early summer for birds, green hills, and water lilies; come in autumn for softer light and fewer boats. In high summer the lake can be hot and busy around Virpazar, but it is still easy to find quiet if you kayak, take a longer boat route, or drive the narrow back roads with patience.
