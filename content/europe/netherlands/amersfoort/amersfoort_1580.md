Mounted on a wall near the Koppelpoort area, this large relief reconstruction shows Amersfoort as it would have appeared around 1580 — a moment when the city had reached its late-medieval peak but before the upheavals of the Dutch Revolt, the subsequent economic shifts, and centuries of urban change had altered its character. The detail is remarkable: walls, towers, waterways, the major churches, the layout of streets that in some cases still exist beneath your feet.

Sixteen hundred years of city-building compressed into a single image. The Eem river that still flows through the city appears in its original, unmodified course. The city walls that made Amersfoort defensible — and of which several stretches and gates survive — can be traced in their entirety. The Sint Joriskerk and its tower dominate the skyline then as they do now.

What makes this kind of reconstruction valuable is not just the historical information but the perceptual shift it produces. Standing in front of it, you see the modern city differently — the surviving medieval layer becomes legible, the changes become visible, and the distance between 1580 and today collapses a little. The cobblestones underfoot suddenly feel much older.
