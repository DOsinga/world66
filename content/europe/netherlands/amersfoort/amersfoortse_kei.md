In 1661, a group of Amersfoort citizens accepted a bet from a local landowner: if they could drag a massive glacial boulder from the countryside into the city centre, he would pay for a barrel of beer. Word spread, enthusiasm ran away with it, and somewhere around 400 people turned up to haul a 9-tonne stone some 20 kilometres into town. They succeeded — and were immediately mocked across the Dutch Republic as "Keienstrekkers," boulder pullers.

The joke had legs. Amersfoort became synonymous with collective foolishness, and satirical prints circulated for years. But the citizens turned the ridicule around: they adopted the nickname with pride, and today "Keienstrekker" is a badge of honour rather than an insult.

The boulder's story didn't end there. In 1672, the French invasion brought panic, and the Kei was hastily buried to hide it. It stayed underground for over two centuries until a chance discovery in 1903 led to its excavation and reinstatement. The same stone that caused the original embarrassment now stands as one of the most beloved landmarks in the city — a monument to the peculiar Dutch habit of laughing at yourself.
