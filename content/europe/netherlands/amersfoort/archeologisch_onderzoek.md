In 1991, excavations on the Hof — Amersfoort's central square — uncovered the foundations of a medieval bishop's residence and evidence of occupation stretching back to the 13th century. The Hof had been a central gathering place for centuries, but the excavation revealed just how much history was compressed into the ground beneath it: fragments of pottery, building foundations, and material that rewrote the timeline of the square's development.

The plaque marks the dig site and records what was found. It is the kind of archaeological memorial that most cities have but few people read. The Hof looks as if it has always been a pleasant open square; the plaque reminds you that it has been many other things first.
