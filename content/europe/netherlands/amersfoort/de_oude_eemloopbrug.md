The Eem was straightened in the 19th century to improve drainage and navigation, which left behind an oxbow remnant — the old loop of the river that once curved through this part of town. The bridge marks where the original meander ran, now a green corridor rather than a waterway.

River straightening was one of the defining engineering projects of 19th-century Dutch water management, and it fundamentally changed the appearance of towns along the river valleys. What you see here is the ghost of the older, wilder Eem.
