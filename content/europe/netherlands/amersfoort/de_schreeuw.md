Positioned near the old city walls, this bronze sculpture shows a human figure with its head thrown back and mouth open in a wordless cry. The work is known simply as De Schreeuw — The Scream — and it makes no attempt to explain itself. The anguish is universal.

The sculpture is widely read as a memorial to the suffering of the Second World War, a conflict that left particular marks on Amersfoort. The city housed a German transit and punishment camp, Kamp Amersfoort, through which thousands of Dutch prisoners passed between 1941 and 1945. Many were worked, starved, or shot there; survivors carried the experience for the rest of their lives. De Schreeuw gives that grief a face.

Standing in front of it, the scale surprises you. The figure is life-sized but the emotion is outsized — larger than any single person, reaching for something collective. It is the kind of public art that does not ask for your attention so much as claim it.
