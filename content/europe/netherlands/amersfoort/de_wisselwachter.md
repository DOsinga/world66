Close to Amersfoort Centraal station, a bronze figure stands in the posture of a working man mid-task — De Wisselwachter, the Switchman. He represents the railway workers who operated the points and signals at one of the most strategically important rail junctions in the country. Amersfoort sits at the crossing of several major lines and has been a key node in the Dutch network since the nineteenth century.

The switchman's job was to physically throw the levers that directed trains from one track to another — a role that required precision, timing, and a calm head when hundreds of tonnes of steel were bearing down at speed. It was unglamorous work, often done in all weather, and largely invisible to passengers sitting warm in their carriages. The statue makes it visible.

There is a straightforward dignity to the sculpture. It does not reach for grandeur; it simply shows a man doing his job carefully. That restraint feels appropriate for a city that has always been more of a crossroads than a destination — a place people pass through, and where the people who keep things moving have always mattered.
