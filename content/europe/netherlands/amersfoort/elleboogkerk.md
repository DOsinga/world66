The Elleboogkerk — Elbow Church — takes its name from its floor plan: the nave bends at an angle, like an elbow, which is highly unusual in Gothic church architecture where the long straight axis is almost universal. The bend was almost certainly forced by the street layout, with the church fitted into an awkward plot between existing buildings.

The church dates originally from the 15th century, though the current building has been significantly altered over the centuries. It became a Reformed church after the Reformation and has been used for various purposes since. The elbow floor plan is still visible from above or when you walk the interior — the space turns in a way that disorients you slightly, breaking the expected axial perspective.

There are only a handful of bent-nave churches in the Netherlands. Amersfoort has one because someone needed to fit a church into a tight corner, and the resulting geometry outlasted the original constraint by six centuries.
