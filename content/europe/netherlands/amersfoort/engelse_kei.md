The English stone sits among a cluster of European Keien near the Hof, each donated by a partner city or region. England's relationship with the Netherlands runs deeper than most: William of Orange became King William III of England in 1689, Dutch water engineers shaped the English Fens, and the Dutch and English were competitors, allies, and enemies in roughly alternating decades from the 1600s through the Napoleonic Wars.

The stone itself is just a stone. But as a gesture from a country with that particular history, it carries more weight than the more neutral European partnerships.
