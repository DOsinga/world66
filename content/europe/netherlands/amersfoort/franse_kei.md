French forces occupied Amersfoort in 1672 (the Rampjaar — disaster year), again briefly in the 1790s, and then as part of Napoleon's annexation of the Netherlands from 1810 to 1813. The city spent several years under direct French administration, with Dutch replaced by French in official documents and the city's fortifications partially dismantled.

The French partner-city stone is, in this context, a remarkably generous gesture. The Keienstrekkers tradition turns the whole thing into a joke — the city that once dragged a boulder now collects stones from its former occupiers. There is something very Dutch about that.
