Galgenberg — gallows hill — is where Amersfoort's public executions took place from the medieval period until the early 19th century. The location was deliberate: visible from the road into the city, positioned so that travellers approaching from the south would see it before they saw anything else. The gallows was a statement about what happened to criminals here, addressed at everyone who passed.

Public execution as civic spectacle ended in the Netherlands in the 19th century, and the hill has been absorbed into the urban fabric without much ceremony. The name remains; the function is centuries gone. Standing here knowing what this place was used for gives the unremarkable landscape an entirely different quality.
