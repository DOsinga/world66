At one of the railway underpasses near Amersfoort station, a steel ant the size of a small car emerges from the shadows. GiAnt is not subtle. The sculpture is large enough that you see it before you register what it is, and then — once you do — the scale hits you properly. An insect rendered at a scale that reverses the usual power relationship: suddenly you are the small creature walking past something enormous.

The work is part of Amersfoort's commitment to public art spread across the city rather than concentrated in galleries. Several sculptures occupy unexpected positions around town — underpasses, squares, traffic islands — creating the occasional jolt of the surreal in an otherwise orderly Dutch urban landscape.

GiAnt works particularly well in its location. The low ceiling of the underpass compresses the space, the ant seems to fill it, and the combination of industrial materials (steel, concrete) with a natural form (insect) produces a productive unease. Whether the ant is arriving or departing, threatening or industrious, depends entirely on your mood when you encounter it.
