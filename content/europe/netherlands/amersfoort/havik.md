Havik means hawk, and this canal-side street takes its name from the birds that were kept here in the medieval period. Falconry was the prestige sport of the medieval aristocracy and the upper merchant class, and maintaining hawks required space, trained falconers, and enough open water nearby to hunt over. The Eem and its surrounding wetlands made Amersfoort good falconry country.

The canal that runs alongside the Havik is one of the more attractive stretches of Amersfoort's inner water system. The combination of the name, the water, and the medieval house façades on the opposite bank makes this one of the stops a walking tour guide wouldn't skip — though you could walk the length of the street in two minutes and never think about why it's called what it is.
