Het Secretarishuisje — the secretary's little house — is exactly what it says: a small residential building attached to the Kamperbinnenpoort, built in 1630 to house the official responsible for recording traffic through the gate. The secretary was a bureaucratic functionary who maintained registers of merchants, travellers, and goods entering and leaving the city. In an era before formal customs infrastructure, the gate secretary was the city's primary data-collection point.

The house is remarkably small, which tells you something about what civic officials could expect as accommodation in 1630. The attachment to the gate structure means it was essentially part of the city's administrative apparatus, indistinguishable from the wall itself.
