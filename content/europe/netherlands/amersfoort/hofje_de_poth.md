Hofjes are a distinctively Dutch urban form: enclosed courtyard complexes originally built as charitable housing for the elderly poor, widows, or specific guild members. De Poth is one of the oldest surviving hofjes in Amersfoort, with origins in the 16th century. The name comes from the De Poth family, who endowed the foundation.

The entrance from the street is deliberately modest — a small gate in a wall gives almost no hint of the courtyard beyond. This was intentional: hofjes were private charitable institutions, not public amenities, and the architecture reflected the distinction between the donor's quiet piety and any desire for public display. The residents had a garden, a pump, a chapel, and each other. They did not have much else.

Push open the gate if it is unlocked (hofjes are often accessible during daytime) and you step into a space that reads as completely separate from the street outside — lower noise, different scale, different light. That transition is the whole point of the hofje as a building type.
