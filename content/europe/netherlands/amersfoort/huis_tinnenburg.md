Huis Tinnenburg dates from 1414, making it one of the oldest surviving residential buildings in Amersfoort. The name derives from "tin" — the house was associated with a tin merchant or tin trade in the medieval period, when metal goods were a significant part of the local economy. The building survived the cycles of fire, flood, and rebuilding that destroyed most comparable structures in Dutch provincial towns.

Standing here, you are looking at a building that was already old when the Amersfoortse Kei was dragged into the city in 1661, old when William of Orange sailed for England in 1688, old when Napoleon's troops marched through in 1811. Most of what surrounds it has been replaced several times over.
