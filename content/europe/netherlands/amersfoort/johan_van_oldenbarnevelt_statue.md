Johan van Oldenbarnevelt was born in Amersfoort in 1547 and became one of the most powerful political figures in the Dutch Republic — the Advocate of Holland who effectively ran the Republic's domestic policy for decades and negotiated the Twelve Years' Truce with Spain in 1609. He was executed in 1619 on charges of treason, at the age of 71, after losing a power struggle with Prince Maurice of Orange. The charges were largely political.

This full standing statue is separate from the portrait bust near the station. The park setting gives it a quieter, more reflective quality than the municipal dignity the bust conveys. Oldenbarnevelt was a man who built the Republic's institutions and was then consumed by them — a fate the Dutch have spent four centuries arguing about.
