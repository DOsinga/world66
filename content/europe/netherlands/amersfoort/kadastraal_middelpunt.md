Set into the pavement of the Hof, Amersfoort's old town square, is a modest bronze disc that carries enormous practical significance: it marks the cadastral centroid of the Netherlands, the geometric heart of the country as calculated by the land registry service. Stand here and you are, mathematically speaking, in the middle of everything.

But the disc points to something deeper than a curiosity. Amersfoort is also the origin point of the Rijksdriehoeksmeting (RD-stelsel), the national coordinate grid that underpins every Dutch map, GPS reading, and land registration. When Dutch engineers or surveyors specify a location anywhere in the country, they express it as a distance from this city. The coordinates (0, 0) of the Dutch grid do not actually fall on the disc itself — the true zero point lies underground near the church — but the disc commemorates the system and its historical anchor in Amersfoort.

For anyone interested in cartography, surveying, or the invisible infrastructure of a nation, this is hallowed ground. For everyone else, it is a good excuse to stand in the middle of a beautiful square and feel briefly central to something.
