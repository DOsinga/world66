Klein Groenesteijn — Little Green Stone — is a 1912 villa in the Berg neighbourhood south of the old city, part of the wave of prosperous residential development that extended Amersfoort southward in the early 20th century. The name references the green-tinged stone or render of the original building, and the "klein" (little) suggests there was or was imagined to be a larger Groenesteijn nearby.

The Berg neighbourhood attracted Amersfoort's professional and business class from the late 19th century onwards — doctors, lawyers, managers, and the owners of the small industries that were growing up around the station. The garden houses they built here were expressions of Arts and Crafts and early Art Nouveau influence, designed to signal prosperity without the ostentation of aristocratic country houses.

Klein Groenesteijn is one of the better-preserved examples of that early 20th-century residential character — a house built by someone who had made it, in a neighbourhood that was itself being made.
