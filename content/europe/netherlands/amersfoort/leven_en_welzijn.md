"Leven en Welzijn" — Life and Welfare — was carved in 1962, in the middle of the Dutch post-war economic miracle. The Netherlands had rebuilt faster than almost any other country in Europe after the war, and by the early 1960s the welfare state and rising living standards were the central ambition of Dutch political life. The title is a mission statement for its era.

The sculpture itself is in the figurative tradition that was still dominant in Dutch public art in the early 1960s before abstraction took over. Standing in front of it now, the title feels like a message from a more optimistic moment.
