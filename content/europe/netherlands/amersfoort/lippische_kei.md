This stone carries several names: the Lippische Kei (from Lippe, a former German principality now in North Rhine-Westphalia), the Soestdijkse Kei (referencing Soestdijk Palace, the residence of Queen Juliana), and the Bernhard Kei (after Prince Bernhard of Lippe-Biesterfeld, who married Princess Juliana in 1937 and became prince consort).

Prince Bernhard was born in the House of Lippe — a minor German noble family — and the stone connects Amersfoort's collection to the Dutch royal family through his German origins. It is one of the most politically layered Keien in the collection: a German stone, a royal connection, and a reminder that the Dutch House of Orange has deep German roots.
