The Dutch Republic was a Calvinist state, but it was never only Calvinist. Lutherans — often merchants and craftsmen with roots in the German lands or Scandinavia — were tolerated, if kept at arm's length from public life. In town after town they eventually got a church of their own, usually tucked discreetly onto a side street rather than the main square.

That's what you're looking at on the Langestraat. The Lutheran congregation of Amersfoort is small — barely a hundred souls today, drawn in from the surrounding Eemland and the edge of the Veluwe — but it has held on to its own building and its own way of doing things for generations. A minority church that simply refused to disappear.
