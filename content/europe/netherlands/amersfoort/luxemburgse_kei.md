Luxembourg and the Netherlands are linked by the House of Nassau-Weilburg and a shared history that runs from the Belgian revolution of 1830 (which separated both countries from the United Kingdom of the Netherlands) through to the modern Benelux structure. The Luxembourg royal family took refuge in Britain during the German occupation of WWII, just as the Dutch royal family sheltered in London.

A stone from Luxembourg to Amersfoort is, in European terms, a gesture between near-relatives — small countries with entangled histories, both having survived much larger neighbours trying to absorb or control them.
