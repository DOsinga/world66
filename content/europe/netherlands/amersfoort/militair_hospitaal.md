Amersfoort was a garrison town for most of its modern history — the central position on the Utrecht-Zwolle axis and the proximity to the eastern border made it a logical place to station troops. A military hospital was a functional necessity: soldiers got sick and injured, and the cost of transporting them to civilian facilities was prohibitive.

The building on the Stadsring dates from the 19th century, when the Dutch army reorganised its medical facilities on the new model influenced by Florence Nightingale's reforms during the Crimean War (1853–1856). Clean air, space between beds, and dedicated sanitation were the principles; the building's layout reflects them.

The hospital closed as a military facility and has since been converted. What remains is the institutional scale of the building — the long corridors, the ward dimensions, the functional severity — which is visible even when the function has changed. Amersfoort's military history is more present in its streetscapes than most visitors register.
