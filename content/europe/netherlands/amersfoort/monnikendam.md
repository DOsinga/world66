Monnikendam — Monks' Dam — takes its name from the religious community that controlled the water management at this point in the inner moat. The gate dates from around 1400 and marks where the Sint-Pieters and Bloklands monastic complex bordered the city's water defences. Monasteries in medieval Dutch towns often took responsibility for sections of the water infrastructure — they had the organisational capacity and the labour to maintain the sluices.

The building is now a cultural venue, but the water gate structure remains, and you can still see how it connected the moat system to the streets beyond. Amersfoort's water management — the moats, sluices, and canals that defined its medieval topography — is largely invisible now, but the Monnikendam is one of the places where the system surfaces.
