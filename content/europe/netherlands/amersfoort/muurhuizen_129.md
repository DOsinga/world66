Number 129 on the Muurhuizen circuit is generally considered the oldest continuously inhabited domestic building in Amersfoort, dating from around 1450. It is built directly against the 14th-century city wall — the wall is its back wall — which is why it survived when so many other medieval houses in the city were demolished or rebuilt. The wall is structurally embedded in every house on this street, and that structural dependency is what preserved them.

The house looks modest from the street: a narrow façade, low ceilings, small windows that reflect the scale of domestic life in the mid-15th century. Inside, the rooms would have been dark and low. The people who first lived here were not wealthy; the wall-side plots were cheap precisely because they backed onto military infrastructure rather than garden space.

The Muurhuizen as a whole is one of the better arguments for adaptive reuse: a fortification that became a neighbourhood, and that neighbourhood is still lived in six hundred years later. Number 129 is the oldest node in that continuous chain of habitation.
