The Netherlands was part of the Habsburg inheritance — first under the Austrian Habsburgs, then (from 1556) under the Spanish branch. Emperor Charles V, who was born in Ghent and spoke Dutch as his first language, ruled what is now the Netherlands, Belgium, and Luxembourg as the "Low Countries" from his seat in Brussels and then Madrid.

The Austrian stone acknowledges this deep historical link. The Dutch Revolt against Habsburg (and specifically Spanish) rule defined the founding of the Dutch Republic in the late 16th century. A stone from Austria to Amersfoort is, in some ways, a gift from the successor state of the empire the city spent eighty years fighting to leave.
