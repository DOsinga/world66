Amersfoort had a significant weaving industry from the medieval period through to the 18th century — wool cloth produced here was sold across the Netherlands and exported internationally. The weavers were one of the organised trade guilds in the city, with their own regulations, quality standards, and institutional presence.

't Oude Wevershuis is a physical survival of that guild economy. Weaving was skilled domestic labour, often organised around guild-regulated workshops where multiple looms operated under one roof. The building served a community of craftspeople whose trade has entirely vanished from the city — the industrial revolution moved textile production to mills, and Dutch artisanal weaving largely ended in the 19th century.

The fulling mill on the Eem (now the Voormalige Volmolen) was part of the same economy: weavers produced the cloth, the mill finished it. What remains is a building and a name where the industry used to be.
