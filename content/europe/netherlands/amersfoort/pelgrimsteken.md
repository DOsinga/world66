From the late medieval period until the Reformation, Amersfoort was one of the most visited pilgrimage sites in the northern Netherlands. The destination was a small stone image of the Virgin Mary — Our Lady of Amersfoort — which had been found in the River Eem in 1444 and credited with miracles. Pilgrims came from across the region; the inflow of visitors was a significant economic driver for the town.

The Reformation ended the cult. The image was destroyed or removed in the 1580s when Amersfoort became Protestant. This pilgrim's sign is a modern reinstatement of the old waymarking system, reminding walkers — and anyone standing here — that this route once had a spiritual destination that most people have now entirely forgotten.
