This big brick church went up in 1908 to serve the Soesterkwartier, the neighbourhood that grew up around Amersfoort's railway yards to house the workers. The local architect Herman Kroes designed it; the tower followed in 1934. For most of a century it was the beating heart of a Catholic working-class parish.

Then the congregation simply melted away. As Amersfoort's Catholic parishes merged and the pews emptied, the church was declared surplus. The last Mass was said here on 5 January 2014.

It didn't fall to the wrecking ball, though. The nave was shrunk to 450 seats and the rest of the shell filled with offices and a neighbourhood centre — the door even moved to a different street. It's one of the most honest monuments in the city to what happened to Dutch churchgoing in a single lifetime.
