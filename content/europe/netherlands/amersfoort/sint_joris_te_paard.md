Near the Sint Joriskerk, Amersfoort's great Gothic church, stands a bronze equestrian statue of Saint George in the moment after the kill — lance down, dragon presumably dispatched, horse alert beneath him. Sint Joris te paard (Saint George on Horseback) has been the city's patron for centuries, and his image appears everywhere in Amersfoort's civic symbolism, from the coat of arms downward.

The Sint Joriskerk itself, with its tall freestanding tower, defines the skyline of the old city. The church was begun in the fifteenth century and expanded over the following hundred years; the tower, known as the Lange Jan, reaches 98 metres and can be seen from well outside the city. The statue stands as an extension of this medieval civic identity — the knight as guardian, the city as something worth protecting.

There is also something gently theatrical about the placement. The dragon-slaying gesture is caught mid-story, like a scene from an illuminated manuscript translated into three dimensions and dropped into a cobblestone square. Amersfoort wears its medieval history lightly but consistently.
