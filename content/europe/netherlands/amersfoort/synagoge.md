There has been a synagogue on this spot since 1727, which makes it one of the oldest still in use anywhere in the Netherlands. When it opened, the tobacco merchant Abraham Italiaander did something unusual: he got the town's Sephardic Jews — descendants of refugees from Spain and Portugal — and its Ashkenazi Jews from the German lands to worship as a single congregation. Two very different traditions under one roof.

By 1941 the Jewish community here numbered around 700. Roughly half of them were murdered in the Shoah. Walk the surrounding lanes and you may spot the small brass *Stolpersteine* set into the pavement outside their former homes.

And yet the building is not a memorial or a museum — it is a working synagogue again. Services are held at least weekly, and the Chief Rabbi of the region, Binyomin Jacobs, is based in Amersfoort. Nearly three centuries on, the lights are still on.
