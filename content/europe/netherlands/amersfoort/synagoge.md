The Netherlands Israelite Congregation (NIG) synagogue on the Drieringensteeg is Amersfoort's active Jewish house of worship, serving a community whose history in the city goes back to the 17th century. The building dates from the 19th century, when emancipation legislation allowed Dutch Jews to build openly in city centres rather than in back alleys and courtyards.

During the German occupation (1940–1945), the Amersfoort Jewish community was almost entirely deported. The transit camp at Amersfoort, north of the city, processed thousands of Jewish prisoners. The synagogue survived the war; most of the congregation did not. The community that meets here today is small, sustained partly by a broader regional membership, and the building carries the weight of that rupture.

The Drieringensteeg — Three Rings Alley — gets its name from the ironwork rings set into the walls where merchants and travellers once tied their horses. The synagogue and the alley name together are a compressed piece of urban history in one narrow street.
