Tolhuisje means "little toll house" — the building where a toll collector sat and extracted payment from people entering the city, merchants bringing goods to market, farmers bringing produce, and travellers on their way through. Every gate in a medieval Dutch city was a revenue opportunity, and the tolls were a significant part of municipal income.

The Tolhuisje sat on the northern approach, monitoring traffic before it reached the Kamperbinnenpoort. The toll collector's job was simultaneously a city job and a deeply unpopular one: he was the face of a system that made every commercial transaction more expensive and every journey into town slightly resented.

Dutch cities abolished most gate tolls in the 19th century as part of national economic liberalisation. The tolhuisje became a redundant building — and then, in the way of redundant buildings, it either disappeared or got repurposed. This one survived, its name still telling you what it once was, in a street where no one pays to pass anymore.
