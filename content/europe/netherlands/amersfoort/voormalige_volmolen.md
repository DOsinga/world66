A volmolen is a fulling mill — a water-powered machine that beat wet wool cloth to shrink and felt it, finishing the material after weaving. Amersfoort had a significant textile industry in the 16th and 17th centuries, producing woollen cloth that was traded across the Netherlands and exported. The River Eem provided the water power; the surrounding countryside provided the wool.

The industry declined in the 18th century as Dutch textile production moved to other cities and regions. This building, dating from 1645, is one of the last physical traces of what was once a major part of the local economy. It has long since been converted to housing, and nothing in its current appearance suggests what it once contained.
