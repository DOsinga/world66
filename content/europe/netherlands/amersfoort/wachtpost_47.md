Wachtpost 47 is one of a series of numbered brick sentry shelters built along the Dutch railway network in 1886, when the military became concerned about the vulnerability of railway infrastructure to sabotage or enemy action. Each post housed a guard who could raise the alarm and slow enemy access to a critical section of line.

The numbering is still visible on the building. Post 47 and its companion Post 48 both survive near Amersfoort, making this stretch of the old Utrecht line unusually well-preserved in terms of its military heritage infrastructure. The posts are small — barely large enough for one person — and built in a functional brick style that was replicated identically across the whole network.

The guards who staffed these posts in the late 19th century were watching for threats that never materialised on Dutch soil during their lifetimes. The Netherlands remained neutral in World War I, and the posts were quietly decommissioned. They survived because they were solid, cheap to maintain, and too small to be worth demolishing.
