Swiss mercenaries were a fixture of European armies from the 15th through the 18th century, and the Dutch Republic was one of their major employers. The Swiss Guards that still protect the Pope are a survival of this tradition; the Dutch States Army drew on Swiss cantons for infantry throughout the Eighty Years' War and beyond.

Switzerland also provided the Dutch Republic with banking connections, Alpine passes for trade, and Protestant solidarity — both were Reformed Protestant states in a largely Catholic continent. A Swiss stone in Amersfoort is a gesture between countries that have been doing business, literal and metaphorical, for five hundred years.
