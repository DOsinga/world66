story: The Koepelgevangenis in Arnhem was designed in the panopticon style — a circular plan in which a single guard in the central tower could theoretically observe all cells simultaneously, without the prisoners knowing whether or not they were being watched. Bentham proposed the panopticon as a model of social control in 1787; Arnhem's version was built in 1886 and used as an actual working prison until 2016. It is one of only a handful of panopticon prisons still standing in the world.

The Arnhem Koepelgevangenis is a 19th-century panopticon-design prison — a circular building with tiers of cells arranged around a central observation point — that operated as a working prison from its construction in 1886 until 2016. The building is a striking piece of Victorian-era institutional architecture: the central dome, the radiating wings, and the sense of enforced visibility built into the design are immediately apparent.

Since closing as a prison, the building has been converted to a multi-purpose cultural venue, hosting concerts, theatre events, and temporary exhibitions. Tours of the building are available and cover the architecture, the history of the panopticon design concept, and the prison's actual history over 130 years of use. Located on the western edge of the city centre, about 10 minutes' walk from the Korenmarkt.
