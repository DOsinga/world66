St Eusebius' Church is a 15th-century Gothic church in the heart of Arnhem, notable for its tower (which offers panoramic views from an observation platform accessible by lift), its turbulent wartime history, and its extraordinary interior decoration. The church was heavily damaged in 1944 during the Battle of Arnhem and substantially rebuilt in the post-war decades.

The interior contains a remarkable series of artworks: the floor mosaics and wall decorations by Herman Makkink depict magnified images of micro-organisms — cells, bacteria, diatoms — creating a surreal but somehow appropriate environment in a medieval Gothic church. The tower elevator takes visitors up to a viewing platform with excellent views over the Rhine and the surrounding landscape. Guided tours are available and the church doubles as a venue for concerts and events.
