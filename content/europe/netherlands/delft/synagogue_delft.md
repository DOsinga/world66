Synagogue Delft is a small but meaningful heritage site, built in the 19th century and tied to the city’s Jewish community. It adds a quieter religious and social-history layer to a town better known for canals, Vermeer, and blue pottery.

Access can be limited, so check opening arrangements rather than assuming a casual visit. Even from the outside, it is a worthwhile marker of a community history that is easy to miss in Delft’s postcard centre.
