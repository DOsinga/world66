  Vermeer painted almost exclusively in one room of his house in Delft, using a camera obscura to project and trace images — a technique that explains the extraordinary precision of his light and perspective. The Vermeer Centrum recreates that room at actual scale and demonstrates how the technique worked. It fundamentally changes how you look at paintings like Girl with a Pearl Earring and The Milkmaid.
---

Not a gallery — none of Vermeer's actual paintings are here — but an information centre devoted to understanding how he worked and why his paintings look the way they do. The centre occupies a canal house on the Voldersgracht and recreates Vermeer's studio at 1:1 scale, with high-quality reproductions of all 36 known works displayed in context.

The experience is educational in the best sense: you leave understanding the specific light of Delft's old city, the way Vermeer arranged his subjects, and why his paintings are so hard to replicate. A short film explains the camera obscura hypothesis. Combined with a visit to the [Oude Kerk](/europe/netherlands/delft/oude_kerk) — where Vermeer is buried — and a walk along the Voldersgracht, this makes a satisfying morning of Vermeer's Delft.
