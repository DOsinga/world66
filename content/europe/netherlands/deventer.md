Deventer is one of the oldest cities in the Netherlands, sitting on the banks of the IJssel river in the province of Overijssel. It has a beautifully preserved medieval centre, a strong literary tradition, and one of the best-known food specialities in the country: the Deventer Koek, a spiced honey cake that has been baked here since the fifteenth century. You can buy it at [Bussink](/europe/netherlands/deventer/bussink_koek) on the Brink, which has been selling it since 1593.

The old town is compact and rewarding to explore on foot. [The Brink](/europe/netherlands/deventer/de_brink), the main square, is one of the largest and most handsome in the Netherlands, lined with medieval and Renaissance buildings and anchored by [the Waag](/europe/netherlands/deventer/de_waag) (weigh house), which dates from 1528. [The Bergkerk](/europe/netherlands/deventer/bergkerk), up on the hill above the river, offers fine views. Deventer's literary credentials are strong -- it was a centre of the Brethren of the Common Life, the religious movement that educated Erasmus, and today it hosts one of the largest [outdoor book markets](/europe/netherlands/deventer/book_market) in Europe each August.

The city has a lively cultural scene for its size, with good restaurants and cafes along the riverfront and in the old centre. The [Speelgoedmuseum](/europe/netherlands/deventer/speelgoedmuseum) (toy museum) and the [Museum de Waag](/europe/netherlands/deventer/de_waag) are both worth a visit. Deventer is easily reached by train from Amsterdam, Utrecht, or Zwolle, and makes a fine day trip or a pleasant overnight stop on a tour of the eastern Netherlands.
