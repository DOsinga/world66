The Great Church of Saint Lebuinus is the largest building in Deventer's old town, a tall Gothic basilica rising above the Brink. The interior, painted a startling whitewashed cream after the Reformation, is light-flooded and unusually spacious for a Dutch town church. Highlights include the Romanesque crypt (the oldest part of the building, where the eighth-century missionary Lebuinus is buried), the elaborate seventeenth-century organ, and the painted vault decorations rediscovered under whitewash in the nineteenth century.

In summer the tower is open and gives the best long view in the city — out over the Brink, the IJssel and the green flood-meadows of the Worp on the far bank. The church doubles as a concert venue, with a regular programme of choral and organ recitals.
