---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

Ede and the Hoge Veluwe are worth visiting year-round, but **late August to mid-September** is the outstanding time to come. That is when the heather on the Veluwe blooms — the moorland turns a deep purple-pink and the light in the early morning and late afternoon is remarkable. The park draws big crowds at weekends during the bloom, but weekday visits are manageable.

The **September commemorations** around the Airborne Monument on the Ginkelseheide fall in the same window — usually the third weekend of the month — and combine well with a heather walk. The anniversary drop draws thousands and the atmosphere is genuinely moving.

**Spring** (April–May) is excellent for cycling and walking; the forest is fresh and the park is not yet crowded. **Autumn** (October–November) brings colour to the beeches and oaks and is the best season for spotting red deer, which rut in October and are often visible at dusk on the heathland edges. **Winter** is cold and grey, but the park stays open and the Kröller-Müller Museum is a reason to visit in any weather.

Avoid the Kröller-Müller on summer Sunday afternoons — it fills up quickly and the car parks overflow.
