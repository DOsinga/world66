story: >
  For most of the 20th century, Strijp-S was a closed city within Eindhoven — a vast Philips factory complex that workers could enter only with an identity pass, surrounded by walls and inaccessible to the public. When Philips finally vacated the site in 2002, the city acquired one of the largest brownfield redevelopments in the Netherlands: 27 hectares of industrial buildings in the middle of the urban fabric. The transformation into a creative and residential district took fifteen years.
---

The former Philips factory complex in northwest Eindhoven, now the city's most interesting neighbourhood: a mix of converted industrial buildings housing creative studios, independent shops, bars, restaurants, and new residential development. The buildings themselves — some listed, some repurposed with glassy additions — give the area a character that the rest of Eindhoven, rebuilt quickly after wartime bombing, largely lacks.

The Strijp-S railway station (on the Eindhoven–Den Bosch line) serves the neighbourhood and makes it easy to reach from the city centre without a bike. The area is at its most interesting during Dutch Design Week in October, when dozens of studios open and the factories fill with exhibitions. Year-round, it has enough cafes, street art, and independent business to fill an afternoon.
