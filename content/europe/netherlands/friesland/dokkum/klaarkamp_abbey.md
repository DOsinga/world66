Klaarkamp was one of the most important Cistercian monasteries in the northern Netherlands, founded in 1163 and operating for nearly four centuries before its dissolution during the Reformation in the 1580s. At its height, it was a major landowner and cultural centre for the region.

What remains is fragmentary — the abbey was systematically demolished after dissolution, and the stones were reused in other buildings throughout the area. The site is now farmland, with archaeological earthworks marking the outline of the original buildings. A small interpretive installation near the site explains the monastery's history and layout.

The ruins are worth the 4 km drive or cycle from Dokkum if you're interested in medieval monastic history. The flat Frisian landscape around the site is typical of the province at its most atmospheric — wide skies, low horizons, and the sense that this land has been inhabited and worked for a very long time.
