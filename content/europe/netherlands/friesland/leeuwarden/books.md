title: "Books"

---

**Simon Vestdijk, *De koperen tuin* (1950).** The great Dutch novel set in Leeuwarden. A young pianist comes of age in a thinly disguised pre-war "H." — instantly recognisable as Leeuwarden — and the city's gardens, canals, and bourgeois society are drawn with affection and a sharp ironic eye. The Koperen Tuin restaurant in the Prinsentuin takes its name from this book.

**Pat Shipman, *Femme Fatale: Love, Lies, and the Unknown Life of Mata Hari* (2007).** Closely researched biography of Leeuwarden's most famous daughter, with substantial early chapters on her Frisian childhood. Useful background before visiting the Fries Museum's Mata Hari collection.

**Hylke Speerstra, *It wrede paradys* (*The Cruel Paradise*, 1999).** A celebrated Frisian collection of oral histories from emigrants who left Friesland for Canada, the US, and Australia. Translated into Dutch and English. A vivid sense of the Frisian temperament and the rural world that surrounds Leeuwarden.

**Theun de Vries, *Het rad der fortuin* (1938) and other novels.** De Vries was a Frisian-born Dutch communist novelist whose long historical fiction sequences are full of the region's character. Look for the early Friesland-set novels.

**Cathie Dunsford, ed., *The Mata Hari Story* (1998).** Lighter and more popular than Shipman; available in English translation. Good for a flight in.
