story: "The Lady of Stavoren is the heroine of a medieval tale: a vain, wealthy widow who sent a captain to fetch her the noblest cargo in the world. He came back with wheat, which she ordered dumped overboard. A beggar warned her that wealth could turn to nothing — and so it did. The dumped grain sprouted into a sandbank that silted up the harbour, the trade moved elsewhere, and Stavoren shrank to the village it is today. The statue commemorates the legend rather than the woman."

The bronze statue stands at the end of the harbour pier, facing out over the IJsselmeer, with her hand raised as if waiting for the ship's return. It is the most photographed object in town and the obvious place to begin a visit — the legend is told on a plaque beside the statue, and the location at the harbour mouth gives the story its setting.

In good weather the pier is also a fine vantage point. To the south the long shore of the IJsselmeer disappears into the haze; to the west, on a clear day, you can see the silhouettes of windmills and the line of the Houtribdijk towards Enkhuizen. The ferry to Enkhuizen sets out from the basin just behind the statue.
