Workum is one of the eleven historic Frisian cities, a quiet town on the shore of the IJsselmeer with a handsome church tower and a traditional harbour. It has city rights since 1399 and the stepped gables along its streets tell the story of a prosperous trading port on what was then the Zuiderzee. Today it is calm and unhurried, which is precisely why the Dutch come here.

The [Jopie Huisman Museum](/europe/netherlands/friesland/workum/jopie_huisman_museum) is the main draw: Huisman was a scrap dealer and self-taught painter who became one of the most popular Dutch artists of the 20th century, known for hyperrealistic still lifes of worn-out objects — bent cutlery, frayed rope, old shoes — that made his subjects feel dignified and important. The [Museum Warkums Erfskip](/europe/netherlands/friesland/workum/warkums_erfskip) occupies the historic Waag and former town hall on the market square and covers the town's history from its trading heyday through the Second World War. The [Sint-Gertrudiskerk](/europe/netherlands/friesland/workum/sint_gertrudiskerk) dominates the square — the largest late-Gothic church in Friesland, with a collection of unusual funeral biers and an unfinished tower that ended up with an onion-shaped cap.

The town is also a good base for watersports. The [IJsselmeer waterfront](/europe/netherlands/friesland/workum/ijsselmeer_waterfront) is five minutes' walk from the centre, with a beach popular for kitesurfing and a harbour busy with sailing boats in summer. The Frisian lake district begins immediately to the east, and the Elfstedenroute cycling path passes through town.
