---
title: "Things to Do"
type: section
---

Hire a whisper boat and the village does the rest. The small electric punts are nearly silent, and within minutes of leaving the rental dock you are threading through a canal system that passes under 52 wooden bridges, alongside thatched farmhouses, and through gardens that reach down to the water's edge. There is no better way to understand Giethoorn than from the water — it is, after all, a place that was built for boats, not roads. A couple of hours on the canal is the essential Giethoorn experience, and even on a busy day it manages to feel peaceful once you are out of the village centre.

The peatland landscape around the village is just as rewarding as the canals. Weerribben-Wieden National Park begins at the edge of the village and extends north and west across a flat, watery wilderness of reed beds, oxbow lakes, and narrow channels. This is one of the most biodiverse wetland habitats in the Netherlands, with otters, marsh harriers, bitterns, and rare dragonfly species for those who look carefully. Rent a bike at the village entrance and explore the polder roads beyond the tourist zone. The village museums — 't Olde Maat Uus especially — give useful context for the peat-cutting history that shaped everything here. A note on timing: summer weekends are genuinely crowded. Weekdays and the shoulder seasons (April-May and September-October) are noticeably quieter.
