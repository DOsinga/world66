Groningen's Jewish Memorial is a sober stop rather than a showpiece, but it adds an important layer to the city. It recalls the Jewish residents who were deported and murdered during the Second World War, grounding that history in a specific neighbourhood rather than leaving it abstract.

Visit it with Folkingestraat and the surrounding streets in mind, since this part of Groningen still carries traces of the former Jewish quarter. The memorial works best as a pause during a walk through the centre, a reminder that the city's lively student character sits beside a much more difficult 20th-century history.
