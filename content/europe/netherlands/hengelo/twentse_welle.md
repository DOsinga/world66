  The museum is housed in a converted cotton mill — the Stork factory complex that once made Hengelo one of the Netherlands' industrial centres. The building itself is part of the exhibit: the bare brick walls and iron columns of the spinning hall now contain displays on weaving, metalworking, and the region's natural history, including the Twente landscape as it existed before industrialisation changed everything.

Twentse Welle is the main museum of the Twente region, covering both industrial heritage and natural history. It's one of those regional museums that exceeds expectations — the collection is well-curated and the industrial machinery on display gives a visceral sense of how textile production actually worked.

The museum is particularly good on the social history of the Twente workers, with reconstructed domestic interiors and photographs of factory life from the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Worth two hours of your time. Industrieplein 4, Hengelo.
