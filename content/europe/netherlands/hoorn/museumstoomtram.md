  The steam tram line was originally built in 1887 to connect the smaller towns of West Friesland with the main railway network, carrying passengers, agricultural produce, and goods across the flat polder landscape. It was replaced by buses in the 1970s but preserved by volunteers who have been running steam-hauled trains on the restored track ever since. On summer Sundays you can ride the full 22 kilometres to Medemblik, then return to Hoorn by boat across the Markermeer — one of the more satisfying half-day loops in the Netherlands.

The Museumstoomtram is a heritage steam tram that runs between Hoorn and the small port of Medemblik along a 22-kilometre narrow-gauge line through the flat West Frisian polder landscape. Trains run on selected days from spring to autumn, with the summer schedule the most frequent.

The tram itself is the attraction: steam locomotives pulling vintage tram carriages through a landscape of fields, dykes, and small farms. In Medemblik, the medieval castle of Radboud is worth a visit before you take the return boat or train. Check the timetable in advance and book if travelling with a group — the trams are popular with families and cycling tourists.
