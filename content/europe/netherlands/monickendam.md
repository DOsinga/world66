Monnickendam (often misspelled Monickendam) is a quiet historic town just north of Amsterdam on the shores of the Markermeer. It received its city rights around 650 years ago and has retained much of its old character — canal houses, narrow streets, and a relaxed pace that is increasingly rare in the towns of the Waterland region.

Where nearby [Volendam](/europe/netherlands/volendam) is overrun with tourists, Monnickendam is comparably calm and at least as pretty. It makes a pleasant half-day trip from Amsterdam. The [old harbour](/europe/netherlands/monickendam/old_harbour) is the most atmospheric corner, with traditional Dutch fishing boats still moored alongside a working smokehouse. The [Speeltoren](/europe/netherlands/monickendam/speeltoren) — a 15th-century tower housing a museum of carillons — is the town's centrepiece, and its 16th-century bell mechanism is one of the oldest of its kind still functioning.

[Marken](/europe/netherlands/monickendam/marken), a former island village 7 km across the water, is easily combined with a visit — a boat runs in summer, and the two together make a full day of it.
