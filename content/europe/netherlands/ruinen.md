Ruinen is a traditional brink village in Drenthe, one of those quiet Dutch settlements built around a village green where sheep once grazed. The surrounding landscape is all heathland, forest and cycling paths — this is the kind of place where you come to slow down.

The [**brink and St. Mary's Church**](/europe/netherlands/ruinen/brink_and_church) at the village centre are the historic heart of the settlement — the church stands where a Benedictine monastery was founded in 1140, and the green is one of the finest surviving examples of a traditional Drenthe brink. [**Dwingelderveld National Park**](/europe/netherlands/ruinen/dwingelderveld), just north of the village, is the largest continuous heathland in the Netherlands and spectacular in late summer when the heather blooms purple. The [**hunebedden**](/europe/netherlands/ruinen/hunebedden) — prehistoric megalithic tombs built around 3000 BCE — are scattered across the surrounding countryside and easily reached by bicycle.

There are a handful of restaurants, including a pannenkoekenboerderij (pancake farm) popular with families, and a couple of bungalow parks for longer stays. The village makes a good base for Drenthe's prehistoric and natural highlights.
