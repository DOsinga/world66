's-Hertogenbosch — Den Bosch to everyone who lives there — is the capital of North Brabant, a southern Dutch city with a Catholic soul that sets it firmly apart from the Protestant cities of the north. It was the birthplace of Hieronymus Bosch, the 15th-century painter whose bizarre visions of Hell and human folly hang in Madrid, Vienna, and Venice, and the city has made the most of this connection: the [Jheronimus Bosch Art Center](/europe/netherlands/shertogenbosch/jheronimus_bosch_art_center) is a major reason to visit, and the Gothic [St. John's Cathedral](/europe/netherlands/shertogenbosch/st_johns_cathedral) — one of the finest Gothic churches in the Netherlands — towers over the medieval centre.

The old town is compact and genuinely charming, with canals running under the streets (the city has an underground canal system you can take a boat through), a lively market square, and a density of cafes and restaurants that reflects a confident, prosperous regional capital. The [Noordbrabants Museum](/europe/netherlands/shertogenbosch/noordbrabants_museum) is excellent, with a collection spanning art and history from prehistoric times through the present. A small Hieronymus Bosch collection here includes works the art centre doesn't have.

Den Bosch also comes alive in February during its famous Carnival, which is celebrated here with an intensity unusual even for the Netherlands. The city transforms completely, adopts a mock identity called Oeteldonk, and parties for three days — a legacy of the southern Catholic tradition that northern Dutch cities largely lack. If you happen to be in the area in late February, make the effort.
