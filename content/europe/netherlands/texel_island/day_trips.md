The most practical day trip from Texel carries you back across the Marsdiep strait to [Den Helder](/europe/netherlands/denhelder), the navy city at the tip of the North Holland peninsula. The TESO ferry takes around twenty minutes and runs roughly every hour, so you can easily spend a morning in Den Helder and return on an afternoon crossing. The main draw is the [Marinemuseum](/europe/netherlands/denhelder/marinemuseum) at the historic Willemsoord naval dockyard — one of the best maritime museums in the Netherlands, where you can board a Second World War submarine and walk the deck of the 1860s ironclad Schorpioen. The [Fort Kijkduin](/europe/netherlands/denhelder/fort_kijkduin) fortification and the surrounding dune coast make a good second stop; grey seals often haul out on the sandbanks below.

Within the island itself, two excursions make for a rewarding full day on foot or by bike. [De Slufter](/europe/netherlands/texel_island/de_koog/de_slufter), the dramatic tidal inlet north of De Koog, is unlike anything else on the Dutch coast: a wide salt-marsh valley where the North Sea floods directly through the dunes at high tide, turning pink with sea lavender in late summer. The full walking circuit takes about two hours. The southern beach plains — De Geul and De Hors — are best reached from Den Hoorn, where a five-hour round trip along the tideline brings you to the island's southernmost tip. Both areas have safety signs posted when tides are running fast; follow them.
