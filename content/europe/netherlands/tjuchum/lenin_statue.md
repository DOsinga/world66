Standing incongruously among the flat fields of Groningen, this nine-metre bronze Lenin is one of the more unlikely sights in the Netherlands. The statue was originally erected in Eisleben, East Germany, and was saved from destruction after reunification by a Dutch collector who transported it across the border to his garden in Tjuchem.

The statue is on private property but is easily visible from the road. It has become something of a minor pilgrimage site for fans of Cold War history and lovers of the absurd — few things are stranger than encountering a monumental piece of Soviet propaganda standing serenely in a Dutch polder, watched over by cows.
