The Twente landscape is unusual for the Netherlands — gently hilly rather than flat, with Saxon farmsteads, heath, and woodland instead of the polders that define the western part of the country. The region was shaped by glacial action rather than by drainage, and cycling through it you encounter a countryside that feels closer to the German Münsterland across the border than to Amsterdam. It is some of the best easy cycling in the Netherlands, and the cities of Twente make natural bases for it.

[Hengelo](/europe/netherlands/hengelo) sits at the heart of the Twente cycling network, a dense grid of numbered junction points (knooppunten) that lets you plan routes of any length without route-finding complications. You ride from one numbered post to the next, noting the sequence before you set off, and the network does the navigation for you. From the city you can reach the Hoge Boekel estate, the village of Delden with its castle, and the small heath reserves that dot the countryside between the Twente towns.

Bike rental is available at and near Hengelo station, as well as in neighbouring Enschede and Almelo. The terrain is mild enough for casual cyclists but varied enough to be genuinely interesting — short climbs, shaded lanes through old estates, and long open stretches across farmland. A half-day loop through the countryside southeast of Hengelo is the most rewarding option, but you can just as easily string together a full day linking several of the Twente cities and their surrounding estates.

Spring and early autumn are the best times to ride, when the heath is in colour and the woodland tracks are dry. Bring a junction-point map or use one of the local cycling apps, and allow time to stop at the farmhouse cafes that sit along many of the routes.
