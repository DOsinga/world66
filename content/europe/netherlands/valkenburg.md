Valkenburg aan de Geul is a small tourist town in the hilly south of the Netherlands (Limburg province), 10 km east of Maastricht. It is the only town in the Netherlands with a significant hill — the [Cauberg](/europe/netherlands/valkenburg/cauberg) — which has made it a regular finish in the Tour de France and Amstel Gold Race. Cycling fans come specifically to ride the climb.

The town is centred on a medieval [castle ruin](/europe/netherlands/valkenburg/valkenburg_castle) on the hill, partially destroyed on the orders of William III in 1672; the caves beneath it were used during World War II as bomb shelters — and as storage for evacuated Rijksmuseum masterpieces — and can be toured today. The [Fluweelengrot](/europe/netherlands/valkenburg/fluweelengrot) and the unusual [Roman Catacombs](/europe/netherlands/valkenburg/roman_catacombs) are two other cave systems worth visiting. The town centre has a pedestrian shopping street, restaurants, and bars. It is popular with Dutch and German holiday-makers in summer and known for its underground Christmas market. [Maastricht](/europe/netherlands/maastricht) is 10 km west and easily visited as a day trip from Valkenburg, or vice versa.
