Vlissingen (known in English as Flushing) is a port city on the southwestern tip of the province of Zeeland, where the Western Scheldt estuary meets the North Sea. It has a long maritime history — the sea here is serious, with strong tides and a busy shipping channel carrying container ships and tankers in and out of Antwerp.

The city centre sits behind a long sea boulevard, the [Boulevard de Ruyter](/europe/netherlands/vlissingen/boulevard_de_ruyter), which runs along the top of the dike above a [wide sandy beach](/europe/netherlands/vlissingen/badstrand). The historic core retains some 17th- and 18th-century architecture around the [Koopmanshaven](/europe/netherlands/vlissingen/koopmanshaven) and the [Sint-Jacobskerk](/europe/netherlands/vlissingen/st_james_church), and the town's connections to [Admiral Michiel de Ruyter](/europe/netherlands/vlissingen/michiel_de_ruyter_statue), born here in 1607, are celebrated throughout. The [muZEEum](/europe/netherlands/vlissingen/musealia) tells the city's maritime story, and on a windy stretch of the boulevard the [Wind Organ](/europe/netherlands/vlissingen/vlissingen_wind_organ) plays itself. Vlissingen is also the gateway to the island of Walcheren, with good cycling routes through the dune landscape and the surrounding polders.
