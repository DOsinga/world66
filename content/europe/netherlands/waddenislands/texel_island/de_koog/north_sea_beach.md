  The Texel beach is marked not by place names but by numbered poles — paaltjes —
  spaced every kilometre along its 30 km length. The system dates to the nineteenth
  century when Dutch coast guards used them to coordinate rescue operations for
  ships wrecked on the treacherous offshore sandbanks.

The North Sea beach stretching west of De Koog is one of the finest in the Netherlands — thirty kilometres of unbroken, wide, clean sand backed by high dunes. The access points from De Koog are well-marked and it's a ten-minute bike ride through the dunes to the shore.

The surf here is genuinely lively, making it a favourite with kitesurfers and windsurfers, particularly around the beach access points where a few schools operate in summer. But the beach is wide enough that you can walk ten minutes in either direction from the car park and find a patch of sand that feels deserted even in August. The water is cold by Mediterranean standards — expect 14–18°C in summer — but plenty of people swim. The tideline is rewarding for beach-combers: amber, shells, and occasionally pieces of historic ship wreckage surface after winter storms.
