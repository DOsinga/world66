Museum Kaap Skil is the finest museum on Texel and one of the most distinctive maritime museums in the Netherlands. It is built around a simple but compelling concept: Texel lies at the junction of the North Sea and the Wadden Sea, which made it one of the most dangerous stretches of water on the Dutch shipping routes for centuries. Thousands of ships have wrecked on its shores, and the beachcombers who worked these shores accumulated extraordinary finds.

The museum displays the results of that history — coins, ceramics, personal effects, navigational tools, and cargo recovered from wrecks across the centuries. The centrepiece is material from the Palmwood Wreck, a VOC ship from 1640 found in 1994 with its contents still largely intact. The museum also covers the lives of the Texel fishermen, the pilots who guided ships through the treacherous waters, and the Waddenzee ecology. It is well-designed, informative, and genuinely moving in places. Allow at least two hours.
