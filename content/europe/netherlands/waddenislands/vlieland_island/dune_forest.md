The forest south and west of Oost-Vlieland is the largest stand of woodland on the Dutch Wadden islands — Austrian and Scots pine planted in the 19th century to stabilise the dunes after centuries of livestock grazing left them bare. Today it is a tranquil, sandy-floored wood threaded with marked walking trails of varying lengths.

The shortest loops are 2–3 km and start from the village edge; longer routes head out into the dunes proper and connect with the cycle path network. The forest is a refuge in bad weather — you get more shelter than on the open beach — and birdlife is good, especially in spring. Several pull-outs along the trails have benches with views across to the Wadden Sea.
