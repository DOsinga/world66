Tromp's Huys is the oldest surviving building in Oost-Vlieland, a captain's house from 1576 that was later associated (though the link is more legend than fact) with the family of Admiral Maarten Tromp. The interior is preserved in 17th-century style with original beams, tiled stoves, and Delft Blue tile work, and serves as the village museum.

Exhibits cover the maritime history of Vlieland — shipwrecks, the Dutch East India Company trade, the lost village of West-Vlieland — alongside a permanent collection of paintings by the 19th-century artist Betzy Akersloot-Berg, who lived in the house. It is a small, well-curated museum that you can do in 45 minutes; useful on a windy day when the beach is unappealing.
