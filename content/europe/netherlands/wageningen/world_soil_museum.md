Part of Wageningen University & Research, the **World Soil Museum** (Wereldbodemmuseum) is the only museum in the world dedicated entirely to soils, and it is stranger and more absorbing than it sounds. The collection consists of physical soil monoliths — intact cross-section slabs cut from the ground at sites across more than 70 countries — displayed alongside maps, instruments, and explanatory panels about how soils form and why they matter.

The museum makes a compelling case that soil is not just dirt. The exhibits connect soil types to civilisation, famine, agriculture, and climate in ways that linger after you leave. It is small enough to do in 90 minutes and works well for both children (who tend to find the giant earth-slabs fascinating) and adults interested in food systems or earth science. Entry is free for WUR students and low-cost for visitors. Worth combining with a walk around the campus.
