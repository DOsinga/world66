Arendal is a small coastal town on [Norway's](/europe/norway) southern shore, the kind of place Norwegians head to in summer for wooden boats, white-painted houses, and island-hopping in the Raet archipelago. The old town, [Tyholmen](/europe/norway/arendal/tyholmen), sits on what was once a separate island, its 17th- and 18th-century timber buildings packed tightly together along narrow lanes — one of the most intact assemblages of old wooden urban architecture in Norway.

The [harbour](/europe/norway/arendal/arendal_harbour) is the centre of life. In summer it fills with boats and the waterfront cafes stay open late in the long Scandinavian evenings. [Merdø Island](/europe/norway/arendal/merdo_island), a short ferry ride from the harbour, has a preserved sea captain's house and quiet beaches. [Raet National Park](/europe/norway/arendal/raet_national_park), Norway's first marine national park, protects the coast and seabed east of town. The large island of [Tromøya](/europe/norway/arendal/tromoya), connected by bridge, offers cycling, beaches, and open views toward the Skagerrak. The [Aust-Agder Museum](/europe/norway/arendal/aust_agder_museum) covers the maritime and social history of the region.

Arendal is quieter and less touristed than [Kristiansand](/europe/norway/kristiansand) to the west, and that is part of its appeal. It makes a good stop on a drive along the southern coast.
