The two obvious day trips from Bodø are Saltstraumen and Lofoten, at opposite ends of the scale: one is a 30-minute drive to a natural phenomenon, the other is a full day (or longer) to an archipelago that deserves a week.

**Saltstraumen** (30 km southeast) is the world's strongest tidal current and one of the most dramatic natural sights in Norway. Drive or take the local bus along the Rv17; check tidal times before you go. See the separate Saltstraumen entry under Things to Do.

**Lofoten** is technically a day trip if you take the first express catamaran and return on the last, but it is cramped. The ferry from Bodø to Svolvær takes 3.5 hours each way; you get perhaps four hours in the archipelago, which is enough to understand why you should come back and stay longer. If your schedule allows only a day, consider an organised boat trip instead — several operators run day excursions from Bodø harbour that include some of the Lofoten coastline without requiring you to rush the ferry schedule.

**Svartisen glacier** is further afield (about 150 km south) but accessible by ferry across Holandsfjorden. Nordland Turselskap runs guided glacier walks from the visitor centre. Combine it with a leg of the scenic Kystriksveien coastal route if you have a car.
