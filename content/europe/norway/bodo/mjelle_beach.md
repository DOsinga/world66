Mjelle is a secluded beach on the western tip of the Bodøhalvøya peninsula, reached by a 30-minute drive from the city centre or a longer hike along the coastal path. The beach is narrow but sheltered, with a reddish tinge to the sand caused by mineral deposits — local guides like to call them gemstone particles, which is an embellishment, but the colour is distinctive and the setting is fine.

This part of the peninsula sees little tourist traffic. The surrounding coastline is rocky and exposed, with views across open water toward the outer islands. On calm summer days the water temperature is survivable for a swim; in winter the beach is wild and windswept but still worth the walk for the light and the solitude. Bring everything you need — there are no facilities. Access is via the Rv834 road toward Tverlandet, then a short path down to the water.
