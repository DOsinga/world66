  Hardangervidda is the largest mountain plateau in Europe — 8,000 square
  kilometres of treeless tundra at 1,000 to 1,400 metres, home to the largest
  herd of wild reindeer on the continent. The plateau was the staging ground
  for the 1943 SOE raid on the Norwegian heavy-water plant at Vemork; the
  saboteurs crossed it on skis through a winter storm and the trail they took
  is still marked.

Hardangervidda begins immediately south and west of Geilo, and the village is one of the main trailheads onto the plateau. In summer the landscape is open tundra: lichen-coloured rocks, shallow lakes, wide skies, and very long views. The Norwegian Trekking Association (DNT) runs a network of huts a day's walk apart, so multi-day crossings are well organised and don't require a tent.

In winter the same terrain is skiable on cross-country gear, and the same hut network operates, though the weather can be ferocious. The Norwegian Wild Reindeer Centre Pavilion at Tverrfjellhytta on the northern edge of the plateau is one of the more interesting modern Norwegian buildings; the southern Hardangervidda Natursenter at Eidfjord (on the Hardanger fjord) is the easiest visitor centre to reach from Geilo by road.
