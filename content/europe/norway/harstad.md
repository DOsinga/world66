Harstad is the largest town on the island of Hinnøya in Troms, northern [Norway](/europe/norway), sitting well above the Arctic Circle at 68°N. This means [midnight sun](/europe/norway/harstad/midnight_sun) from late May to mid-July and a good chance of [northern lights](/europe/norway/harstad/northern_lights) from autumn through spring. The combination of extreme light conditions and dramatic coastal scenery — fjords, mountains, and open sea — makes it one of the more compelling small cities in the Arctic.

The Trondenes peninsula just north of the town holds an exceptional concentration of history. [Trondenes Church](/europe/norway/harstad/trondenes_church), built in the 13th century, is the northernmost medieval stone church in the world. The [Trondenes Fort](/europe/norway/harstad/trondenes_fort) next door holds the massive WWII-era "Adolf Gun" — one of the largest artillery pieces ever mounted — left in place because it proved too difficult to demolish. The [Trondenes Historical Center](/europe/norway/harstad/trondenes_historical_center) ties these threads together.

The town is a stop on the [Hurtigruten](/europe/norway/harstad/hurtigruten) coastal ferry and makes a practical base for exploring the Vesterålen islands to the south. [Whale watching](/europe/norway/harstad/whale_watching) trips run from the area in season — orcas follow herring into the fjords in winter, sperm whales feed off the Vesterålen coast year-round. The [Arctic Arts Festival](/europe/norway/harstad/arctic_arts_festival) in June is Norway's largest cultural event north of Bergen.
