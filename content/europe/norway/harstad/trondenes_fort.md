  The Adolf Gun at Trondenes was the largest land-based artillery gun ever used in combat. It had a calibre of 40.6 cm (roughly 16 inches) and could fire a 1,030 kg shell over 56 km. Hitler reportedly visited the gun on a tour of the Norwegian coast in 1942. After the war, Norwegian authorities wanted to demolish it but it proved too difficult — the gun is simply too massive and too deeply embedded in concrete to remove economically, so it remains where the Germans left it.

Trondenes Fort was built by German forces during the WWII occupation of Norway as part of the Atlantic Wall — the system of coastal defences stretching from Norway to France. The fort's centrepiece is one of four massive naval guns emplaced on the Trondenes peninsula: the 40.6 cm SK C/34 naval gun, known as the "Adolf Gun," is one of the largest guns ever mounted in a land fortification and remains largely intact.

The gun and its concrete emplacement are open to visitors, and the scale of the installation is startling up close — the barrel alone is over 20 metres long. The bunker complex surrounding it has been preserved as a WWII heritage site. The walk from the parking area passes good views over the strait and the outer islands.
