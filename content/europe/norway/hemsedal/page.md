Hemsedal calls itself the Scandinavian Alps, which is marketing, but not entirely wrong. The [ski area](/europe/norway/hemsedal/hemsedal_ski_area) is one of Norway's largest and most reliable for snow, with terrain that ranges from gentle family slopes to serious off-piste above the treeline. It draws skiers from [Oslo](/europe/norway/oslo) (three hours by car) and increasingly from abroad, though it remains far less crowded than the big Alpine resorts.

The village itself is small — a cluster of hotels, rental cabins, and a few restaurants along the valley floor. Hemsedal is not a charming old mountain village; it is a purpose-built ski destination, and it does not pretend otherwise. What it does well is skiing: 53 kilometres of groomed runs served by modern lifts, a good [terrain park](/europe/norway/hemsedal/hemsedal_terrain_park), and backcountry touring in the [Hemsedalsfjella mountains](/europe/norway/hemsedal/hemsedalsfjella_hiking) above the resort for those with the skills and equipment.

In summer the valley transforms into hiking and mountain biking country. The [Hemsedalsfjella](/europe/norway/hemsedal/hemsedalsfjella_hiking) peaks top out above 1,800 metres, and the trails through alpine meadows are genuinely beautiful. The [Rjukandefossen](/europe/norway/hemsedal/rjukandefossen) waterfall near Tuv is worth visiting in snowmelt season. [Fishing for trout](/europe/norway/hemsedal/hemsila_fishing) in the Hemsila river is popular throughout summer. But winter is the main season, and if you are looking for Norway's best downhill skiing without the crowds of a French or Austrian resort, Hemsedal is a strong choice.
