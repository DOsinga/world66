John Andreas Savio (1902–1938) is the most important Sami visual artist of the twentieth century. Born in Sør-Varanger of mixed Sami and Norwegian heritage, he worked mainly as a printmaker, producing stark woodcuts of reindeer herding, Arctic landscapes, fishing, and the daily life of Sami communities in Finnmark. He died of tuberculosis at 36, but left behind a body of work of extraordinary clarity and power.

The permanent Savio collection at Grenselandmuseet is the best place to see his prints. The works are modest in scale but striking in their economy of line — the black-and-white woodcuts capture the cold light and open horizons of Finnmark with a precision that no photograph quite matches. For anyone interested in Sami culture, this collection is worth more than any amount of staged reindeer experiences. The museum holds the main archive of his work; a smaller selection is also displayed in the town library.
