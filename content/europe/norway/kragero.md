Kragerø is a small coastal town on Norway's southern shore, tucked into a maze of islands, skerries, and sheltered inlets. Edvard Munch called it "the pearl of the coastal towns," and in summer it is easy to see why — white wooden houses climb the hillsides above a harbour full of boats, and the surrounding [archipelago](/europe/norway/kragero/kragero_archipelago) offers some of the warmest swimming water in Norway.

The town comes alive between June and August, when Norwegians descend on the islands for sailing, kayaking, and long evenings on the rocks. The rest of the year it is quiet, almost sleepy, but the light is beautiful and the walking trails along the coast are empty. The [Berg-Kragerø Museum](/europe/norway/kragero/berg_kragero_museum) covers the town's seafaring past and its connection to Edvard Munch, who spent two recuperative summers here in 1909–10. The [Kragerø Church](/europe/norway/kragero/kragero_church) above the harbour is worth a visit for the view from the churchyard alone.

The real draw for visitors is [Jomfruland National Park](/europe/norway/kragero/jomfruland_national_park), a flat island just offshore reached by a short ferry from the harbour. With sandy beaches, cycling tracks, a 19th-century lighthouse, and exceptional birdwatching during migration season, it offers a kind of calm Norwegian coastal experience that is increasingly rare. The seafood from the harbour restaurants is as fresh as it gets anywhere in Telemark.
