Kristiansund (sometimes written Kristiansund N, to distinguish it from Kristiansand in the south) is a small coastal city in Møre og Romsdal, built across four islands connected by bridges. It has been a fishing and trading town for centuries, and the production of klippfisk (dried and salted cod) was once the economic backbone — the [Klippfiskmuseet](/europe/norway/kristiansund/klippfiskmuseet) in the old town tells that story.

The city was heavily bombed in 1940 and rebuilt in a functionalist style, so it lacks the old wooden architecture of neighbouring towns like Ålesund. But the harbour setting is attractive, and the [Kirkelandet Church](/europe/norway/kristiansund/kirkelandet_church) — a 1964 modernist building — is one of the best pieces of post-war architecture in western Norway. The island of [Innlandet](/europe/norway/kristiansund/innlandet) preserves a handful of pre-war merchant buildings and is where the Klippfiskmuseet warehouses stand.

Kristiansund is the departure point for the [Atlantic Ocean Road](/europe/norway/kristiansund/atlanterhavsveien) (Atlanterhavsveien), one of Norway's most famous scenic drives — a series of bridges leaping from island to island across the open sea, reached via the [Atlantic Ocean Tunnel](/europe/norway/kristiansund/atlantic_ocean_tunnel) under the fjord. The [sundbåt harbour ferry](/europe/norway/kristiansund/sundbaten), running since 1876, is the best way to move between the islands and gives the town its distinctive rhythm. Climb [Kvernberget](/europe/norway/kristiansund/kvernberget) for the panoramic view over the whole island city and out to sea.
