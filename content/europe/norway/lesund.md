Ålesund is an anomaly on the Norwegian coast — an entire town built in Art Nouveau style. In January 1904, a catastrophic fire destroyed almost the whole town centre overnight, leaving 10,000 people homeless. International aid poured in (Kaiser Wilhelm II, who loved the area, was a major contributor), and the rebuilding happened fast, in the fashionable Jugendstil of the time. The result is a remarkably coherent townscape of turrets, spires and ornamental facades in a setting of islands and channels. The [Jugendstilsenteret](/europe/norway/lesund/jugendstilsenteret) museum tells the full story of the fire and rebuilding, and the best overview of the whole town is from the [Aksla viewpoint](/europe/norway/lesund/aksla_viewpoint) — 418 steps above the main street.

The inner channel of [Brosundet](/europe/norway/lesund/brosundet) cuts through the city centre and is the most photogenic stretch of the waterfront, lined with converted warehouses and Art Nouveau facades. The [Sunnmøre Museum](/europe/norway/lesund/sunnmore_museum), on the southern edge of town, has over 55 historic buildings and replica Viking ships. For marine life, [Atlanterhavsparken](/europe/norway/lesund/atlanterhavsparken) on the western tip of the island is one of Europe's larger saltwater aquariums.

The town is also the main gateway to the [Geirangerfjord](/europe/norway/lesund/geirangerfjord_day_trip), a UNESCO World Heritage Site reachable by a spectacular mountain drive. The island of [Runde](/europe/norway/lesund/runde_island), the southernmost seabird cliff in Norway, is an easy day trip — hundreds of thousands of puffins, gannets and other seabirds nest here between April and August.
