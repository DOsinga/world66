title: Things to Do in Lillehammer
type: section

Lillehammer punches well above its weight for a town of 30,000. The two unmissable experiences are [Maihaugen](maihaugen), one of northern Europe's finest open-air museums — including the [Garmo Stave Church](garmo_stave_church) and the [Norwegian Olympic Museum](norwegian_olympic_museum) — and the 1994 Olympic venues clustered on the hillside above town, where you can take the chairlift to the top of the [Lysgårdsbakken](lysgardsbakken) ski jumps or ride a wheeled bobsled down the Olympic track.

The [Lillehammer Art Museum](lillehammer_art_museum), with its Snøhetta building and strong collection of Norwegian 19th-century painting, is worth an hour or two. [Storgata](storgata), the curving main street of 19th-century wooden buildings, connects the town's various points and is one of the best-preserved wooden streets in Norway.

In summer, the [Skibladner](skibladner) paddle steamer — the world's oldest paddle steamer in regular service — crosses [Lake Mjøsa](lake_mjosa) to Hamar and back. In winter, [Hafjell](hafjell) alpine centre (15 km north) offers downhill skiing, and the town fills up in March for the [Birkebeiner](birkebeiner) cross-country ski race. [Håkons Hall](hakons_hall), the Viking-roofed Olympic ice arena, hosts ice hockey matches through the winter. The adjacent [Kanthaugen Freestyle Arena](kanthaugen) hosted the 1994 aerials and moguls events.

The Nobel laureate Sigrid Undset lived in Lillehammer for over two decades; her home [Bjerkebæk](sigrid_undset) is now a museum, and the Norwegian Festival of Literature it hosts each spring is the town's biggest cultural event of the year.
