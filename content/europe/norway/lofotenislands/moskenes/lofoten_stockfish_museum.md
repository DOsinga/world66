The Lofoten Tørrfiskmuseum in Å tells the story of stockfish — cod hung and dried on outdoor wooden racks (hjell) in the cold, steady Arctic wind — from the Viking age to the present day. The process sounds simple but it takes months and requires the right combination of temperature and air movement that only the Lofoten winter reliably provides.

Exhibits cover the fishing methods, the drying and grading process, the trade routes that carried tørrfisk to Portugal and Italy, and the role of the Hanseatic merchants who dominated Lofoten commerce for centuries. Norway still exports tens of thousands of tonnes of stockfish annually; the racks you see on hillsides across the archipelago are not a heritage attraction but a working industry.
