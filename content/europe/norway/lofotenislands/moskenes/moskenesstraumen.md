  Edgar Allan Poe's 1841 story "A Descent into the Maelström" and Jules Verne's 1869 novel "20,000 Leagues Under the Sea" both draw on historical accounts of the Moskenesstraumen. Earlier descriptions exaggerated its power to the point of fantasy — claiming it could swallow ships whole — but the reality, while less apocalyptic, is genuinely impressive at peak tide.

The Moskenesstraumen is one of the strongest tidal currents in the world — a maelstrom off the southern tip of Moskenesøya where the tidal flow between the island and the small island of Mosken creates violent whirlpools and standing waves several metres high when conditions peak. The current runs at up to six knots at spring tide.

Organised boat trips from Sørvågen and Reine venture out to experience the Moskenesstraumen at close range. The same boats also reach the Kollhellaren cave at Refsvika on the western coast, which contains cave paintings approximately 3,000 years old and is accessible only by sea.
