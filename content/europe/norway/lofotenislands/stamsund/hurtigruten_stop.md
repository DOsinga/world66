  The Hurtigruten route was established in 1893 as a mail and passenger service connecting isolated Norwegian coastal communities that had no road access. Stamsund was added to the route early on. For decades, the Hurtigruten was the only regular connection these villages had to the outside world. Today it operates as both a ferry service and a cruise, though the ships still carry cargo and local passengers on every voyage.
---

The Hurtigruten coastal express makes a regular stop at Stamsund dock, both southbound and northbound. Even if you're not sailing on it, the arrival of the ship — particularly in winter, when the mountains are snow-covered and the light is low — is one of the more atmospheric moments in a Norwegian coastal village. Travellers heading from Bergen to Kirkenes or back will pass through here as part of the classic Norwegian coastal voyage.

Booking a cabin on the Hurtigruten for the Lofoten stretch (say, from Bodø to Svolvær, or the reverse) is one of the finest ways to experience the archipelago. The ship passes through the narrow sounds between the islands at close range, and the mountains rise immediately from the water.
