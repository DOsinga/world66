The mountain rising directly above Svolvær, Fløya offers the best panoramic view of the town, the surrounding islands, and — on a clear day — the full sweep of the Lofoten Wall to the southwest. The summit stands at 610 metres and is reachable in 1.5 to 2 hours from the town centre on a marked trail that starts near the top of Blåbærlia street.

The trail is steep but well-maintained, with a via ferrata section near the top that adds a degree of exposure for those who want it. Most walkers go in summer and autumn, but the winter ascent in crampons is popular with those wanting a view over the snowy archipelago. In good conditions you can see the Svolværgeita's twin horns from the side on the way up, giving a better sense of just how vertical the drop between them is.

At the summit there is a small shelter and a visitor book. Look back down to the harbour and the Svinøya peninsula for the classic view of Svolvær that appears in most photographs of the town.
