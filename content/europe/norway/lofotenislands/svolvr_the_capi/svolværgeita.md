The Svolværgeita — the Svolvær Goat — is the most recognisable feature in all of Lofoten: a twin-pronged rock pinnacle rising above the town, visible from the harbour and from far out to sea. The two horns stand close enough together that a jump between them is possible and terrifying in equal measure. For most visitors it is simply a landmark; for climbers it is one of Norway's most famous objectives.

The approach to the base of the Goat is a demanding scramble up loose rock and steep terrain, requiring good mountain experience. The technical climbing up the pinnacle itself is serious, and the jump between the horns is something only expert mountaineers attempt. Guided ascents are available from operators in Svolvær for those with the appropriate skill level.

For non-climbers, the view of the Goat from the harbour and from the Fløya mountain path is striking enough. At dawn and dusk the twin horns catch the light and turn pink above the town in a way that explains why this particular formation ended up on every postcard of Lofoten.
