Vågan municipality encompasses most of the islands of Austvågøy and Gimsøy in the northern Lofoten archipelago, along with a scattering of smaller islands including Henningsvær and Skrova. Its main town is [Svolvær](/europe/norway/lofotenislands/vagan/svolvar), the de facto capital of Lofoten and the busiest entry point for the islands by ferry, express boat, and air.

Svolvær (population around 4,500) has a working-harbour atmosphere and is well set up for visitors — several good fish restaurants, gear shops, and the starting point for most boat tours into the surrounding fjords and sea stacks. The famous [Svolværgeita](/europe/norway/lofotenislands/vagan/svolvargeita), a pair of rocky peaks above the town, is a classic mountaineering challenge; the jump between the two summits has claimed a number of would-be daredevils. The [Lofoten War Memorial Museum](/europe/norway/lofotenislands/vagan/lofoten_war_memorial_museum) covers the German occupation during World War II in detail.

[Kabelvåg](/europe/norway/lofotenislands/vagan/kabelvag), 5 km west of Svolvær, is the oldest fishing village in Lofoten and feels more peaceful and historic. It's home to the [Lofoten Regional Museum](/europe/norway/lofotenislands/vagan/lofoten_museum) at Storvågan, which runs ongoing archaeological excavations on the site of the medieval trading centre of Vågar, and the [Espolin Gallery](/europe/norway/lofotenislands/vagan/espolin_gallery) showing works by the much-loved northern Norwegian artist Kaare Espolin Johnson. The [Lofoten Aquarium](/europe/norway/lofotenislands/vagan/lofoten_aquarium) is also here.

[Henningsvær](/europe/norway/lofotenislands/vagan/henningsvar), accessible by a dramatic causeway across rocky islets, is the most picturesque of the fishing villages — a tight cluster of painted wooden buildings around a small harbour, with a handful of cafes and galleries that make it a popular stop on any Lofoten itinerary.
