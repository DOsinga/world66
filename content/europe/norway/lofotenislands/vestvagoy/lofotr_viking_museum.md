  In 1981, a farmer ploughing a field at Borg noticed his tractor rocking unusually. Archaeologists were called, and they uncovered the largest Viking chieftain's longhouse ever found — 83 metres long, with walls still standing to shoulder height. The site had been occupied from roughly 500 to 950 AD, and the finds included fine Byzantine glassware, Roman vessels, and gold jewellery, pointing to a chieftain of extraordinary wealth and international connections.

The Lofotr Viking Museum at Borg is the undisputed highlight of Vestvagoy and one of the finest Viking Age sites in Scandinavia. A full-scale reconstruction of the longhouse — 83 metres from end to end — stands on the original foundations, giving a visceral sense of how enormous and sophisticated these buildings actually were. Inside, the re-created great hall, chieftain's quarters, and animal stalls bring the Iron Age to life without resorting to kitsch.

The museum sits on the edge of a long lake, and the approach from the car park, with mountains behind and water in front, sets the scene well. Alongside the longhouse, there's a replica Viking ship moored in the adjacent boathouse — visitors can join sailing trips during summer. Costumed interpreters demonstrate crafts including iron-working, weaving, and bread-baking; the quality of the demonstration varies by season, but at peak summer it's genuinely absorbing. Plan for at least two hours.
