Kjeragbolten is a single boulder, about the size of a small car, wedged in a crevasse near the summit of Kjerag, roughly 1,000 metres above Lysefjord. Standing on it — with nothing but a sheer drop to the fjord on either side — has become one of the defining photographs of Norwegian tourism, and on a clear summer day there's usually a queue of hikers waiting their turn.

The hike up from the Øygardstøl car park near Lysebotn is about 11 km round trip and genuinely strenuous: several sections use fixed chains to help climb steep rock faces, and the route crosses three separate valleys before reaching the plateau. Budget six to eight hours and go only in good weather — the chains and rock get dangerous when wet. The same cliffs draw BASE jumpers, who launch from ledges near the boulder; watching them go is, for most visitors, plenty of vertigo for one day.
