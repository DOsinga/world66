Molde bills itself as the "City of Roses," though the reason for the name is not immediately obvious. What is obvious is the setting: the town sits on the north shore of the broad Romsdalsfjorden, and from [Varden park](/europe/norway/molde/varden_viewpoint) you can count over 200 mountain peaks on both sides of the fjord. It is one of the most panoramic locations on the Norwegian coast.

Molde is a stop on the Hurtigruten coastal route and has ferry connections south toward [Ålesund](/europe/norway/alesund). The town itself is small and quiet for most of the year, but it comes alive during the [Moldejazz festival](/europe/norway/molde/moldejazz_festival) in July — one of Europe's oldest jazz festivals, drawing international acts and filling the town to capacity.

Outside festival season, the main reason to linger is the surrounding landscape. The [Atlantic Road](/europe/norway/molde/atlantic_road) (Atlanterhavsveien), one of Norway's most spectacular drives, is about 70 km to the west. The [Hjertøya island fishing museum](/europe/norway/molde/hjertoya_island), reached by a short boat ride from the centre, is an unexpectedly rewarding visit — it tells the story of a fisherman's life rather than just the mechanics of fishing, with well-preserved living quarters, sheds, and boats set among pines and birches. The [Romsdal Museum](/europe/norway/molde/romsdal_museum) in town covers regional history and explains why almost no historic buildings remain in Molde's centre.
