Moss is a small industrial city on the eastern shore of the Oslofjord, about an hour south of [Oslo](/europe/norway/oslo) by train. It is not a typical tourist destination, but it has a few things worth stopping for if you are passing through or exploring the Østfold coast.

The main cultural draw is [Gallery F15](/europe/norway/moss/gallery_f15) at Alby Manor, set in a handsome estate overlooking the fjord on the [Jeløya peninsula](/europe/norway/moss/jeloya_peninsula). The gallery hosts rotating contemporary art exhibitions and the grounds are lovely for a walk. Jeløya itself is surprisingly green and scenic for being so close to town, with beaches and gentle coastal paths.

Moss also has a place in Norwegian history: the [Convention of Moss](/europe/norway/moss/convention_of_moss) was signed here in 1814, paving the way for Norway's union with Sweden while preserving the Norwegian constitution. The convention building still stands in the town centre. The [Moss Jernverk](/europe/norway/moss/moss_jernverk) and the old industrial quarter along the Mosselva river round out a pleasant half-day visit, and the ferry to Horten across the fjord connects you to the Vestfold side if you are driving south. [Vansjø](/europe/norway/moss/vansjo_lake) lake to the east is popular for summer swimming and kayaking.
