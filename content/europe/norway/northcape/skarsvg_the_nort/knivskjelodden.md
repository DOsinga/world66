Knivskjelodden is the actual northernmost point of Europe that is accessible on foot — a narrow, rocky peninsula that juts into the Arctic Ocean at 71°11' north, about 1,500 metres further north than the famous globe on the North Cape plateau. It is reached by a 9-kilometre hiking trail from the North Cape car park, passing through open tundra with views down to the sea on both sides of the ridge.

The trail takes three to four hours each way and is unmarked beyond the trailhead sign. The terrain is tundra — mostly firm but boggy in places — and the weather can change fast at this latitude. At the tip there is no monument, no café, no visitors centre: just a cairn, the Arctic Ocean, and the knowledge that there is nothing but water between you and the polar ice cap. Most people who do the walk bring a small stone to add to the cairn.

The contrast with the North Cape plateau is the point. Where the plateau has a steel globe and a gift shop, Knivskjelodden has nothing but wind and the genuine end of Europe. For many visitors this is the more powerful experience, and the empty trail in both directions tends to confirm it. Go in summer for the midnight sun; in late autumn the trail can be icy and navigation becomes harder.
