The Aurlandsdalen is an uninhabited valley running from the high Aurland plateau down to Flåm, traversed by a hiking route known as Rallarvegen or the "Sheep Road." The walk from the plateau to Flåm — about 20 kilometres, mostly downhill — is one of the classic day hikes in western Norway, passing through steep birch and pine forest, alongside the Aurlandselvi river, and through narrow gorges with dramatic waterfalls.

The standard approach is to take the Flåmsbana railway up to Myrdal and then walk a section of the Rallarvegen (the construction road for the Bergen-Oslo railway) east before descending into the Aurlandsdalen. This makes a full day of approximately 20 kilometres with 500 metres of descent. The valley is genuinely wild; there are no facilities below the plateau. The trail is well marked and most fit walkers can complete it; the terrain is rough in places. September is a good month — cooler than summer and the birch trees begin to turn.
