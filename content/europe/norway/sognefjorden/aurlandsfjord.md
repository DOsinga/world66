The Aurlandsfjord is the branch of the Sognefjorden running south from its junction to the village of Flåm. It is about 29 kilometres long, narrowing as it goes, with steep mountain walls that rise directly from the water. Flåm, at the very head of the fjord, is the main base for the inner Sognefjorden — a small village that receives an extraordinary number of visitors given its size, principally because the Flåmsbana railway terminates here and the fjord cruise boats call in.

The Stegastein viewpoint above Aurland village — a cantilevered steel platform projecting 30 metres out over a 650-metre drop — is reached by a toll road from the village and is one of the most vertiginous viewing platforms in Norway. The Aurlandsvegen (Snow Road) continues from Stegastein across the plateau above the fjord to Laerdal — a spectacular high mountain road that is only open in summer. The [Aurlandsdalen](/europe/norway/sognefjorden/aurlandsdalen) valley above Flåm provides a classic multi-day hiking route through an uninhabited highland valley.
