Borgund Stave Church is the best-preserved medieval stave church in Norway — a UNESCO World Heritage candidate structure that has stood since around 1180 CE, virtually unchanged. It rises from the floor of the Lærdal valley on a series of staves (vertical wooden posts) above a stone foundation, the roof layers rising in tiers decorated with carved dragon heads and shingled surfaces. The entire structure is preserved in an extraordinary condition: original carved portals, original iron fittings, original construction.

The stave church stands beside a 17th-century log church that replaced it for regular worship; the medieval church was preserved as a monument rather than demolished. A small visitor centre explains the construction technique — the way the staves, sills, and rafters interlock without nails or significant metal hardware. The valley setting is dramatic: the surrounding mountains rise steeply on all sides, and the church looks exactly as it would have appeared to travellers on the historic mountain crossing between eastern and western Norway.
