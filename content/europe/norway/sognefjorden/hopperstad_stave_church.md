Hopperstad Stave Church is one of the oldest stave churches in Norway, dating from approximately 1130 CE, in the village of Vikøyri on the southern shore of the Sognefjorden. It was rescued from dereliction in 1880 and carefully restored; the exterior is largely original but the interior shows 19th-century restoration work. The carved baldachin (canopy) above the altar space is original medieval woodcarving of high quality.

The village of Vik, which includes Vikøyri, also has the Romanesque stone Hove Church nearby — one of the few medieval stone churches in this part of Norway. The combination of Hopperstad's wooden stave architecture and Hove's stone Romanesque construction, both from the same era, makes Vik worth the detour from the main fjord route. The ferry connection from Vik across the fjord to Vangsnes on the north shore gives a view of the fjord from the water.
