title: "Books"

---

**Egil's Saga** (anonymous, 13th century) — The Old Norse sagas are the primary literary record of Viking-age Vestfold, and Egil's Saga is among the most gripping: a poet-warrior's life spanning raids, feuds, and the political turbulence of early Norway. Several scenes involve the coast around Tønsberg. Reading it before visiting sharpens the landscape considerably.

**The King's Mirror** (*Konungs skuggsjá*, anonymous, c. 1250) — A Norwegian didactic text structured as a father's advice to his son, covering commerce, the sea, court life, and the natural world. It was likely composed in the Vestfold region and gives a vivid picture of the concerns and worldview of medieval Norway at its peak.

**Knut Hamsun, *Hunger*** (1890) — Hamsun is Norway's great difficult genius — Nobel laureate and later Nazi collaborator — and *Hunger* is his most urgent book: a starving writer adrift in a Norwegian city, half-mad with pride and desperation. It is not set in Tønsberg, but it captures something true about the pressures of small-country Norwegian life that formed the world around this coast.

**Edvard Munch: Behind the Scream** by Sue Prideaux (2005) — Munch lived and worked for years at Åsgårdstrand, a short drive from Tønsberg, and this biography is the best in English: rigorous, readable, and genuinely illuminating about how the fjord landscapes around Vestfold fed into his painting. Worth reading before a visit to his house at Åsgårdstrand.
