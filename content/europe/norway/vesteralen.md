Vesterålen is the archipelago immediately north of Lofoten, sharing the same jagged peaks and Arctic light but drawing a fraction of the visitors — most travellers pour their whole trip into Lofoten and never cross the strait. That's a mistake, or at least an opportunity: the scenery is nearly as dramatic, the roads are quieter, and the islands hold two wildlife draws Lofoten can't match.

Sperm whales feed year-round in a deep submarine canyon just off [Andenes](/europe/norway/andenes), at the archipelago's northern tip, making it one of the most reliable whale-watching bases in Norway without needing to go as far as Svalbard. The same stretch of coast holds the seabird cliffs of Bleiksøya, an offshore islet packed with Atlantic puffins through the summer breeding season.

[Stokmarknes](/europe/norway/stokmarknes), on the island of Hadseløya, is Vesterålen's other main stop and the birthplace of the Hurtigruten coastal steamer service — most visitors see the archipelago as a stretch of the Hurtigruten route between Harstad and Lofoten, or arrive by road via the bridges and tunnels that link the islands to the mainland.
