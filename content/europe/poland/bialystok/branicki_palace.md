  Jan Klemens Branicki, the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth's Grand Hetman of the Crown, rebuilt his family estate in the 1750s on a scale calculated to outshine the royal palaces of Warsaw — partly in the hope of being elected king himself. He failed, but the palace stuck, and the formal gardens behind it are still the finest example of baroque landscape design in northeastern Poland.

A long pale-yellow façade with a green copper roof, a forecourt of paired pavilions, and a perfectly axial baroque garden behind — Branicki Palace dominates the southern edge of central Białystok and is the city's defining sight. The palace itself houses the Medical University of Białystok, so you can only see a few representative rooms; the real attraction is the gardens, which are free, open daily, and one of the best places in the city for a slow walk.

Look for the upper terrace with its statues, the sculpted parterres, and the large pond at the far end. The vegetable garden — the *Ogród Włoski* — is laid out as it was in the 18th century and still produces. A small chapel and pavilion sit at the corners. Allow an hour or two.
