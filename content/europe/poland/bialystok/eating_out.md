---
title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

Białystok eats well, with a regional cuisine shaped by Polish, Lithuanian, Belarusian, Tatar and Jewish influences. The local specialty is *kartacze* — large potato dumplings stuffed with seasoned minced meat, sometimes called *cepeliny*. Hearty and filling, found at every regional restaurant. Other Podlasie staples: *babka ziemniaczana* (a baked potato cake), *kiszka ziemniaczana* (a potato sausage), and the rye-beer soup *kwas chlebowy*.

Tatar villages east of the city contribute *pierekaczewnik* — a multi-layered baked savoury pastry with meat or cheese — which appears on better menus and at specialist places like Carska in Białowieża. For Jewish food, look for *bagieliki* (the local bagel, baked from the same dough that produced the cousin known as the New York bagel — Białystok was the original "bialy" town) at one or two surviving bakeries.

The strip along Lipowa and Sienkiewicza streets has the densest concentration of restaurants and cafés. **Esperanto** and **Restauracja Branicki** are reliable regional choices; **Kawiarnia Wszystko Gra** is a good central café for breakfast.
