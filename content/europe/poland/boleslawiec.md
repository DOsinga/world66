Boleslawiec is a small town in southwestern Poland, close to the German border, and the centre of the Polish pottery industry. The town's [hand-stamped ceramics](/europe/poland/boleslawiec/pottery_factories) — distinctive blue, white, and green patterns applied to stoneware — are famous well beyond Poland, and dozens of factories operate here, most with shop floors where you can buy directly at factory prices.

Beyond the pottery, Boleslawiec has a handsome [market square](/europe/poland/boleslawiec/market_square) surrounded by pastel-coloured buildings and the remains of a medieval wall that can be traced on a short walk around the centre. The [rail viaduct](/europe/poland/boleslawiec/rail_viaduct) over the Bóbr River, built in 1845, is one of the oldest railway bridges in Poland still in use. The atmosphere is relaxed and unhurried, and the [Ceramics Museum](/europe/poland/boleslawiec/ceramics_museum) gives good context before you dive into the factory showrooms.
