Jasna Góra — Luminous Mountain — is Poland's most important pilgrimage site and one of the most visited religious destinations in the world. The Pauline monks established the monastery here in 1382, and it has been continuously occupied ever since. The fortified hilltop complex includes a basilica, a chapel housing the Black Madonna, a treasury, a library, a system of baroque bastions, and a bell tower visible from across the city.

The centrepiece is the Chapel of Our Lady, where the icon of the Black Madonna hangs behind a silver-and-gold cover that is ceremonially lifted several times a day to revealed sounds. Pilgrims kneel on the stone floor, many having walked hundreds of kilometres to reach this spot. The icon itself dates to at least the 14th century, though its origins are debated — tradition attributes it to Luke the Evangelist, scholars to medieval Byzantium. The cuts on the Madonna's cheek are said to date from a 1430 robbery attempt. The treasury and armoury display extraordinary gifts brought by centuries of pilgrims, including John Paul II's papal vestments.
