The Emigration Museum opened in 2015 in the restored modernist marine station at the entrance to the port — the same building that received and dispatched Polish emigrants on the trans-Atlantic liners *Batory* and *Piłsudski* in the 1930s. The permanent exhibition traces 200 years of Polish emigration: to the Americas, to Australia, to Britain after the Second World War, and into the EU after 2004.

It is one of the best museums in northern Poland and an unusual subject for one — the displays manage to be moving rather than nostalgic, and they take in the emigration that broke up families and the emigration that founded Chicago's Polonia. Two to three hours. The café in the old waiting hall is a good lunch stop.
