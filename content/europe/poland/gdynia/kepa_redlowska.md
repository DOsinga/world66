Kępa Redłowska is the headland of beech-forested cliffs that separates central Gdynia from the seaside neighbourhood of Orłowo. The eastern slope, facing the sea, drops sheer to the beach in a series of bluffs reaching 90 metres — among the tallest sea cliffs on the Polish Baltic.

A network of marked walking paths threads the woods on top; the main trail, the **red trail**, runs from the centre of Gdynia south to Orłowo Pier in about 2 hours of easy walking. Two viewpoints (Plażowa and the abandoned **Torpedownia** lookout) give the best photo angles. The headland is also a small nature reserve protecting old-growth beech and a colony of woodpeckers.
