The Silesian Insurgents Memorial stands in the central roundabout near the Spodek arena, a massive sculpture of three stylised eagles rising from a common base. It was erected in 1967 to commemorate the three Silesian Uprisings of 1919, 1920, and 1921, when Polish-speaking Silesians fought to break away from German administration and join the newly reconstituted Polish state.

The monument's scale is imposing — you see it from some distance along the main boulevard. It works both as a piece of monumental socialist-era sculpture and as a genuine marker of Silesian identity, which has always been distinct from both German and Polish national narratives. For anyone interested in the complex history of this region, it is worth pausing here before walking into the city centre.
