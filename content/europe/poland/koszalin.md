Koszalin is a mid-sized city of around 105,000 in Western Pomerania, set a few kilometres inland from the Baltic coast. It is not a beach destination itself, but it serves as a practical base for the sandy coastline around [Mielno and Kołobrzeg](/europe/poland/koszalin/mielno_beach), both within easy reach. The city was a prosperous Hanseatic seaport in the medieval period — its 14th-century [St Mary's Cathedral](/europe/poland/koszalin/st_marys_cathedral) survives from that era — but the harbour silted up and Koszalin's maritime trade ended before the modern era.

The town centre, rebuilt after heavy wartime damage, is modest but workable. [Chełmska Hill](/europe/poland/koszalin/chelmska_hill) — a wooded summit with a pilgrimage sanctuary consecrated by Pope John Paul II in 1991 and a viewing tower — offers the best overview of city and landscape. The city also has an unexpected cultural distinction: it hosts one of Poland's oldest and most respected film festivals, the [Youth and Film Festival](/europe/poland/koszalin/youth_and_film_festival), which launched the careers of Kieślowski and Zanussi among others.

The real draw is the surrounding landscape: lakes, forests, and the coastal dunes of Słowiński National Park to the east, where shifting sand buries entire trees and the beach stretches for kilometres.
