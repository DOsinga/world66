A wooded hill southeast of the city centre, Chełmska Hill carries a long history of religious significance. A medieval chapel was built on the summit to Christianise what had been a Slavic pagan worship site, and the hill has been a place of pilgrimage ever since. The current sanctuary was consecrated by Pope John Paul II during his 1991 visit to Poland, and the site draws Catholic pilgrims particularly around the feast of the Assumption in August.

An observation tower on the hilltop offers views over the city and, on clear days, toward the Baltic coast to the north. The forested slopes make for a pleasant walk from the city centre. The Museum of Vladimir Vysotsky — an incongruous but genuine institution dedicated to the Russian singer-songwriter — is housed nearby and worth a look if you have an interest in Soviet-era popular culture.
