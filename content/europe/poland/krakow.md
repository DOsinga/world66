Krakow was Poland's royal capital for five centuries, and walking its Old Town feels like the intervening years never happened. The vast [Main Market Square](/europe/poland/krakow/rynek_glowny) — one of Europe's largest medieval plazas — is ringed by townhouses and churches that survived the Second World War intact, which puts Krakow in rare company on the continent. [Wawel Castle](/europe/poland/krakow/wawel_castle) looms over the river at the Old Town's southern edge, and the former Jewish quarter of [Kazimierz](/europe/poland/krakow/kazimierz), just a short walk away, has become one of the most atmospheric neighbourhoods in Central Europe.

Beyond the history, Krakow has a lively university-town energy — the cellars below the Old Town are full of bars and jazz clubs, the food scene is genuinely good, and the city is compact enough to explore entirely on foot. [St. Mary's Basilica](/europe/poland/krakow/st_marys_basilica) anchors the square with its hourly trumpet fanfare; [Collegium Maius](/europe/poland/krakow/collegium_maius), Poland's oldest surviving university building, is a short walk away; and the [Czartoryski Museum](/europe/poland/krakow/czartoryski_museum) holds Leonardo da Vinci's Lady with an Ermine — one of only four paintings by Leonardo in existence. The [Schindler's Factory Museum](/europe/poland/krakow/schindlers_factory) in Kazimierz is one of the finest historical museums in Poland, and the [Wieliczka Salt Mine](/europe/poland/krakow/wieliczka_salt_mine) and [Auschwitz-Birkenau](/europe/poland/krakow/auschwitz_birkenau) are both within easy day-trip distance.
