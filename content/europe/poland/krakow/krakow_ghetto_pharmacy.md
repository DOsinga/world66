The Eagle Pharmacy (Apteka pod Orłem) on Plac Bohaterów Getta in Podgórze is the only remaining original structure from the Krakow Ghetto, which the Germans established in this district across the Vistula from Kazimierz in 1941. The pharmacy operated throughout the ghetto's existence, its Polish pharmacist Tadeusz Pankiewicz choosing to stay inside the walls rather than retreat to safety outside.

The museum occupies the original pharmacy premises, preserved much as they were during the occupation, and the exhibition — "The Krakow Ghetto. Inside the Walls" — tells the story of the ghetto from its creation to its liquidation in 1943, through documents, photographs, and personal accounts. The pharmacy itself serves as an anchor for the story: its original counters, scales, and cabinets are intact. The museum is quieter and more reflective than Schindler's Factory a short walk away; the two are best visited together. The square outside has a memorial of empty metal chairs representing the ghetto's Jewish residents.
