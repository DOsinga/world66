Fifteen kilometres southeast of Krakow, the Wieliczka Salt Mine has been extracting salt from beneath the Carpathian foothills since the 13th century and is one of the oldest industrial enterprises in Europe. The mine was a crucial source of Poland's royal revenue for centuries — "white gold" — and the inscribed UNESCO World Heritage Site status reflects both its historical significance and the extraordinary spaces the miners created underground.

The standard tourist route descends 135 metres through nine levels, passing through chambers carved into pure salt formations, underground lakes with crystalline salt water, and the miners' chapels that are the mine's most famous feature. The highlight is the Chapel of St. Kinga — a full-size underground church with a nave, altar, and side chapels, every surface carved from salt, which has served as a place of worship for miners since the 17th century. The tour takes about two and a half hours. The mine can be reached by public bus from Krakow's main bus station in about 30 minutes; organized tours from the city are also widely available and marginally more convenient.
