The resort town of Zakopane sits in a mountain valley at the foot of the Tatra range, about 100 kilometres south of Krakow. It is the most famous mountain destination in Poland, a place where Polish writers and painters went in the late 19th century to discover the Goral (highland) culture and breathe mountain air, and where every ski-capable Pole still goes in winter. The Tatras behind the town, forming the border with Slovakia, rise to nearly 2,500 metres and contain some of the most dramatic mountain scenery in Central Europe.

In summer, Zakopane is the starting point for hikes into the Tatras — the trail to Morskie Oko lake (a 9-kilometre walk each way) is the most popular route, but the ranges offer a full spectrum from easy forest walks to serious technical climbs. In winter it becomes a ski resort, crowded on weekends with visitors from Krakow and Warsaw. The town itself is interesting for its wooden Zakopane-style architecture (a distinctive mountain vernacular developed by architect Stanisław Witkiewicz in the 1890s) and for Krupówki, the pedestrianized main street that is either lively or garish depending on your tolerance for tourist commerce.
