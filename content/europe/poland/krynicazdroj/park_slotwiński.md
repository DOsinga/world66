Park Słotwiński is the heart of Krynica-Zdrój's spa life. The long, tree-lined promenade runs through the park and is flanked by the 19th-century pump room (pijalnia wód), a concert hall, and the various pavilions and buildings that characterise a Central European spa town of the Belle Époque period. The setting is gracious and slightly old-fashioned, which is precisely the point.

The mineral waters available at the pump rooms include several distinct springs, each with different mineral content and claimed therapeutic properties. Zuber water — named after the geologist who identified the spring in 1911 — is exceptionally high in mineral salts and carbon dioxide, and is dispensed in small doses. Jan, Mieczysław, and Słotwinka waters are milder. You can buy a glass or a mug at the pump rooms for a nominal sum and join the locals doing slow laps of the promenade. The ritual is deliberately unhurried.
