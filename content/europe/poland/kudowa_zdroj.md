Kudowa-Zdrój is a small spa town in the Kłodzko region of southwestern [Poland](/europe/poland), tucked into a valley of the Table Mountains right on the Czech border, about 10 km from [Náchod](/europe/czechrepublic/nachod). It has been taking the waters since the eighteenth century, and the bones of that genteel past are still visible — a leafy spa park, a pump room and colonnade where mineral springs are still drunk, and the slightly worn grand hotels of a resort that once drew Prussian and Austrian visitors in equal number.

The spa park is the heart of the town: ponds, clipped lawns, a bandstand, and shaded walks that make a pleasant hour or two between border and lunch. The colonnade dispenses several iron- and carbon-rich waters, an acquired taste worth trying once. Around the park the town is quiet and a little melancholy, in the way of spa towns everywhere out of season.

The reason most day-trippers cross over, though, is in the suburb of Czermna, where the Baroque Skull Chapel lines its walls and ceiling with thousands of human skulls and bones — one of the strangest interiors in central Europe, and an easy add-on to a stop in the park.

Kudowa-Zdrój is straightforward to reach from Náchod by car or bus, and the border crossing is open and unceremonious. Combine it with a meal on the Polish side and a stroll through the spa gardens for a gentle half-day across the frontier.
