Łańcut Castle is one of the great aristocratic palaces of Central Europe, and the miracle is that so much of it survived. Built in the early 17th century by the Lubomirski family and expanded through the 18th and 19th centuries by the Potockis, the castle passed through three centuries of Polish noble life — wars, partitions, and two world wars — and came out the other end with a substantial part of its original furnishings and decorations intact.

The interiors are room after room of accumulated splendour: Rococo ballrooms, a Turkish apartment decorated with 18th-century orientalist fantasy, a chinoiserie dining room, a theatre where the Potockis staged private opera performances, and a long series of drawing rooms filled with porcelain, tapestries, clocks, and paintings. The impression is less of a museum than of a house whose owners simply stepped out.

The carriage house, separate from the palace building, holds one of the largest collections of horse-drawn carriages and coaches in Europe — over 60 vehicles, from gilded state coaches to humble farm carts, all in remarkable condition. The English-style landscape park surrounding the estate has an orangery, a rose garden, and the remains of the original 17th-century fortifications. An annual classical music festival in May fills the castle's salons and theatre with chamber concerts.

Allow at least three hours for the palace and carriage museum together; serious visitors spend a full day. Guided tours of the interiors are required and run in Polish, with English audio guides available.
