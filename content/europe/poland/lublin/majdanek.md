The Majdanek concentration and extermination camp is among the most sobering sites in Poland, and for a specific reason: unlike Auschwitz, Treblinka, or Sobibor, it was not significantly demolished before liberation. The Soviet advance in July 1944 was so fast that the SS had time to burn only some of the records. The gas chambers, the crematoria, the wooden barracks where prisoners slept on three-tier bunks, the watchtowers, and the wire remain largely as they were.

The camp sits about 4 kilometres from central Lublin, on the edge of the city. A visit takes 2–3 hours and includes the museum, the preserved camp structures, and the mausoleum containing the ashes of victims. The experience is appropriately difficult. Photography is permitted but should be treated with restraint. It is one of the most important Holocaust sites in Europe and should not be skipped by anyone visiting Lublin.
