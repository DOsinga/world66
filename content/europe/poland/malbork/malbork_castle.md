Malbork Castle is the largest Gothic brick castle in the world and one of the finest medieval fortresses in Europe. Built from 1274 onwards by the Teutonic Knights as the headquarters of their monastic-military order, it grew over the centuries into a vast complex of three interconnected castles — the High Castle, the Middle Castle, and the Low Castle — set within walls, moats and towers along the Nogat River. The sheer scale of the place is staggering: the complex covers 21 hectares and contains more brick than any other Gothic building on earth.

The interior matches the exterior for ambition. The Grand Master's Palace in the Middle Castle is decorated with the richest surviving examples of Teutonic craftsmanship: vaulted ceilings in brilliant reds, greens and gold, carved stonework, and enormous windows that flood the rooms with northern light. The castle museum houses an outstanding amber collection — amber was the economic foundation of the entire Teutonic state, and the displays trace the art of amber carving from the medieval period to the present. The armoury and the reconstructed chapter house are also highlights.

Plan at least half a day, ideally a full day. The castle is best visited with a guided tour, which brings the rooms and their history to life in a way that wandering alone cannot. In summer, a spectacular sound-and-light show is staged on the castle walls after dark — worth staying for.

Admission includes access to all three castle sections. The castle is open year-round, though summer brings crowds and advance booking is advisable for tours.
