The Mazurian Lakes — *Pojezierze Mazurskie* — are the largest lake district in Europe: more than 2,000 lakes connected by rivers and canals in the northeastern corner of Poland, covering an area larger than Luxembourg. The landscape was carved by retreating glaciers at the end of the last ice age, leaving a mosaic of water, forest, and meadow that stretches from Olsztyn in the west to the Belarusian border in the east. In summer, when the water is warm and the forests are green, the region is as close to paradise as Poland offers.

The centre of Mazuria — as it is commonly called — is the chain of large lakes running from [Giżycko](/europe/poland/mazurianlakes/gizycko) in the north through [Mikołajki](/europe/poland/mazurianlakes/mikolajki) to [Lake Śniardwy](/europe/poland/mazurianlakes/lake_sniardwy), Poland's largest lake at 110 square kilometres. This chain is the main sailing and motorboat corridor, served by marinas, yacht clubs, and hire facilities at every town. [Giżycko](/europe/poland/mazurianlakes/gizycko) is the largest town in the region and the sailing capital; [Mikołajki](/europe/poland/mazurianlakes/mikolajki), smaller and more attractive, is where the sailboats actually converge on summer weekends until the town quay is so packed you can barely see the water.

Beyond sailing, Mazuria offers kayaking on the [Krutynia River](/europe/poland/mazurianlakes/krutynia_river) — a classic multi-day river journey through forest and quiet lakes — birdwatching at the [Łuknajno Reserve](/europe/poland/mazurianlakes/luknajno_reserve) (one of Europe's most important mute swan colonies), and cycling on the flat roads between lakes. In winter the frozen lakes support ice fishing, ice yachting, and cross-country skiing. The region was also the setting for some of the most intense fighting on the Eastern Front in 1944–45; [Wolf's Lair](/europe/poland/mazurianlakes/wolfs_lair), Hitler's main wartime headquarters, lies in the forests near Kętrzyn.

The Mazurian Lakes are enormously popular with Polish holidaymakers and far less known to foreign visitors, which means a more genuine experience in most places. The main season is June to August; the rest of the year is quiet and the smaller towns essentially shut down.
