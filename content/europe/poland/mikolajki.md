Mikołajki is a small resort town in the heart of Poland's Masurian Lake District, the region of forested hills and interconnected lakes that stretches across the northeast of the country. The town sits on a narrow isthmus between [Lake Mikołajskie](/europe/poland/mikolajki/lake_mikolajskie) and Lake Tałty, and in summer its waterfront fills with sailboats, kayaks, and the occasional excursion steamer. Lake Śniardwy, Poland's largest lake, is just a short sail to the south.

Tourism drives the local economy almost entirely. From June through August the population swells with Polish and German visitors who come for sailing, windsurfing, fishing, and [cycling along the lake trails](/europe/poland/mikolajki/cycling_trails). Outside of summer the town is very quiet — most restaurants and rental outfits close for the season. If you are looking for a base for exploring the Masurian lakes, Mikołajki is one of the most convenient starting points, with [kayak routes](/europe/poland/mikolajki/kayaking_lakes) and boat rental widely available.

Four kilometres east of town, the [Łuknajno Lake Nature Reserve](/europe/poland/mikolajki/luknajno_reserve) protects one of Europe's largest mute swan colonies in a UNESCO biosphere reserve accessible on foot or by bicycle. The [Holy Trinity Church](/europe/poland/mikolajki/holy_trinity_church), the oldest building in town, reflects Mikołajki's history as part of the predominantly Lutheran Masurian region before 1945.
