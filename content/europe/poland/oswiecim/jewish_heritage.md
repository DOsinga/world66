The Oswiecim Synagogue (Chevra Lomdei Mishnayot Synagogue) on Plac Ksiedza Jana Skarbka is the only surviving Jewish house of worship in Oswiecim. Built in 1913, it was used as a warehouse during the Nazi occupation, which is what saved it from destruction when all other synagogues in town were burned. The building has been restored and now houses a small museum documenting the Jewish community of Oswiecim before the war.

Nearby, the Great Synagogue Memorial Park marks the site where the town's main synagogue — a large 19th-century building — once stood. The memorial is a quiet garden with explanatory panels. A few minutes' walk from the main synagogue, the Jewish Cemetery contains graves dating back several centuries, though much of it was destroyed during the occupation. These sites together offer a counterpoint to the camp complex — a reminder that Oswiecim had a thriving, diverse community before 1939, not just a history of destruction.
