Sandomierz is one of Poland's oldest and best-preserved towns, perched on a loess bluff above the Vistula river. The Old Town survived both the Mongol invasions and the Second World War largely intact, leaving a compact medieval centre that feels far removed from the busier tourist circuits of [Kraków](/europe/poland/krakow) or [Warsaw](/europe/poland/warsaw).

The [market square](/europe/poland/sandomierz/town_hall) is ringed by Renaissance townhouses and dominated by a 14th-century Gothic town hall. Below the streets runs a network of [underground tourist routes](/europe/poland/sandomierz/underground_route) through old merchant cellars. The [cathedral](/europe/poland/sandomierz/sandomierz_cathedral), dating from the 1360s, holds a set of remarkable Ruthenian-Byzantine frescoes. Walk up to the [Opatów Gate](/europe/poland/sandomierz/opatow_gate) for sweeping views over the Vistula valley. The [Royal Castle](/europe/poland/sandomierz/sandomierz_castle) at the southern edge of the hilltop houses a regional museum.

Sandomierz gained a second life as a film location for the hugely popular Polish TV crime series *Father Mateusz*, and fans of the show will recognise its streets immediately. It is also the heart of a small but growing wine region — the surrounding loess hills produce some of Poland's most interesting wines, and several vineyards welcome visitors.
