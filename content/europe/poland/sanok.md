Sanok sits on the San River in southeastern Poland, where the country starts to lift toward the Bieszczady Mountains. It is a small town, but not a thin one: castles, churches, wooden village architecture, and the strange, unsettling art of Zdzislaw Beksinski all sit within an easy day on foot.

The best reason to come is the [Museum of Folk Architecture](/europe/poland/sanok/museum_of_folk_architecture), one of Poland's strongest open-air museums. It gathers wooden churches, cottages, workshops, and a reconstructed Galician market square from the cultures that once met in this corner of the Carpathians. Across the river, [Sanok Castle](/europe/poland/sanok/sanok_castle) holds the Historical Museum and the largest collection of Beksinski works.

Sanok works well as a cultural stop before or after the Bieszczady. It is not a resort town, and that helps. Spend the morning in the skansen, cross back for the castle and old centre, then keep an evening free for the river paths or a simple meal around the rynek.
