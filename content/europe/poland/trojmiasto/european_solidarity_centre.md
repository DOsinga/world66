The European Solidarity Centre opened in 2014 on the grounds of the Gdansk Shipyard where the Solidarity trade union movement was born in August 1980. The building itself is extraordinary — a rusted, steel-clad structure whose exterior evokes the shipyard's industrial past while the interior is a floating, light-filled museum that tells the story of how dock workers helped bring down the Soviet bloc.

The permanent exhibition traces the history of Solidarity from its founding through martial law, the underground years, and the eventual Round Table negotiations that led to Poland's first free elections in 1989. The material is intelligent and well-presented, avoiding the triumphalism you might expect and taking seriously both the courage of the movement and the complexity of what came after. Allow two hours minimum; the exhibition is substantial.

The shipyard neighbourhood around the centre is undergoing gradual transformation and has an interesting scrappy quality — cranes still visible, old workers' halls being converted, new cultural venues opening. The contrast with the polished Old Town a few minutes away is striking.
