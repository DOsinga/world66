Śródmieście Południowe is the southern half of Warsaw's city centre, running from Aleje Jerozolimskie down to Łazienki Park. It has two distinct characters: the broad commercial axis along [Nowy Świat](/europe/poland/warsaw/nowy_swiat) and the dense residential grid to the west, where the streets around Zbawiciela Square and Mokotowska constitute Warsaw's most concentrated bar, restaurant, and independent shop scene.

The [Palace of Culture and Science](/europe/poland/warsaw/palace_of_culture) dominates the northern edge — a Stalinist gift from the Soviet Union that remains Warsaw's tallest building and most contested landmark. The [Museum of Modern Art](/europe/poland/warsaw/modern_art_museum) now stands beside it on Plac Defilad. Further south, the neighbourhood loosens into embassy terraces, tree-lined streets, and a café culture that feels closer to Berlin or Amsterdam than to anything in central-eastern Europe.
