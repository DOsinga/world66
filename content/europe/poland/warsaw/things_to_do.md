Warsaw was 90% destroyed in World War II and its reconstruction is itself one of the most remarkable stories in European history. The [Old Town](/europe/poland/warsaw/old_town) — lovingly rebuilt from photographs, paintings, and memory after the war — is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The [Warsaw Rising Museum](/europe/poland/warsaw/warsaw_rising_museum) is essential viewing for understanding what happened here, and [POLIN](/europe/poland/warsaw/polin_museum) tells the story of Poland's Jewish community with exceptional depth.

The Royal Route running south from the Old Town passes [Sigismund's Column](/europe/poland/warsaw/sigismunds_column), the [Copernicus Monument](/europe/poland/warsaw/copernicus_monument), and [Krakowskie Przedmieście](/europe/poland/warsaw/krakowskie_przedmiescie) before continuing along [Nowy Świat](/europe/poland/warsaw/nowy_swiat) to [Łazienki Park](/europe/poland/warsaw/lazienki_park) with its [lakeside palace](/europe/poland/warsaw/lazienki_palace) and Sunday Chopin concerts.

Further afield, [Wilanów Palace](/europe/poland/warsaw/wilanow_palace) is Warsaw's Versailles; the [Praga district](/europe/poland/warsaw/praga_district) across the Vistula is the authentic, un-rebuilt side of the city; and the [Neon Museum](/europe/poland/warsaw/neon_museum) in Praga is one of the most distinctive museums in Poland.
