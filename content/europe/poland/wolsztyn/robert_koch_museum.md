Robert Koch, later famous for identifying the bacteria behind tuberculosis and cholera, worked in Wolsztyn as a district physician in the 1870s. The small museum keeps that scientific thread visible in a town otherwise known for steam engines and lake walks.

It is a niche stop, but a good one: medical instruments, biographical material, and the reminder that major scientific careers often begin in ordinary provincial rooms.
