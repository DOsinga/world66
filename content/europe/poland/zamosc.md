Zamość is one of Poland's most satisfying small-city surprises: a late-Renaissance town planned almost from scratch as an ideal city. Jan Zamoyski hired the Italian architect Bernardo Morando in the 16th century, and the result still reads clearly today: arcaded merchant houses, ordered streets, a grand square, and fortifications wrapped around the whole composition.

The [Great Market Square](/europe/poland/zamosc/great_market_square) is the stage set, with pastel facades and a town hall staircase that seems made for civic theatre. A few minutes away, the [Zamość Synagogue](/europe/poland/zamosc/zamosc_synagogue) tells the story of a once-large Jewish community in one of Poland's finest Renaissance synagogues. The [cathedral](/europe/poland/zamosc/zamosc_cathedral) and the [Bastion VII route](/europe/poland/zamosc/bastion_vii) round out the picture: faith, trade, defence, and ambition packed into a walkable old town.

This is not a place that needs a long checklist. Zamość works because the whole old city hangs together. Come for half a day if you are crossing eastern Poland; stay overnight if you like quiet squares after the day-trippers leave.
