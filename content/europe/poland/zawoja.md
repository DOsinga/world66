Zawoja is a long mountain village in southern Poland, stretched for many kilometres below Babia Gora in the Beskids. It is not a tidy postcard town. Its appeal is practical and physical: trailheads, forested slopes, weather that changes fast, and the feeling that the mountain is always just above the roofs.

The reason to come is [Babia Gora National Park](/europe/poland/zawoja/babia_gora_national_park), whose main peak rises to 1,725 metres and has the strongest alpine feel in this part of the Beskids. The park is a UNESCO biosphere reserve, with spruce forest, subalpine vegetation, and wildlife that includes lynx, wolves, and bears. On clear days the summit views run across the Tatras and the Polish-Slovak border country.

Zawoja also has quieter anchors: the wooden [Church of St Clement](/europe/poland/zawoja/church_of_st_clement), valley hamlets, and the [Mosorny Gron](/europe/poland/zawoja/mosorny_gron) chairlift area for views, skiing, and mountain biking. It is a good spare-day choice from Krakow only if you want a real mountain day rather than another cafe-and-old-town afternoon.
