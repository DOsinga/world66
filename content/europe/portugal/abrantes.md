Abrantes sits high above the Tagus, close to the point where Portugal's middle becomes flatter, warmer, and more open. It is not a grand monument town like nearby [Tomar](/europe/portugal/tomar), but it has enough shape for a spare day: a castle ridge, a compact old centre, river views, and churches that show its long military and religious history.

The [Castle of Abrantes](/europe/portugal/abrantes/castle_of_abrantes) is the reason to climb. From its walls and gardens, the Tagus valley spreads out below in a way that explains why this hill mattered. The old town falls away from there in narrow streets and small squares, with the [Church of Santa Maria do Castelo](/europe/portugal/abrantes/church_of_santa_maria_do_castelo) and [Church of São Vicente](/europe/portugal/abrantes/church_of_sao_vicente) adding the best architectural stops.

Abrantes works best as a quiet counterpoint to the better-known Templar towns around the Médio Tejo. Come for a half-day if you are nearby, or use it as a slower stop between river country and the Beira Baixa line.
