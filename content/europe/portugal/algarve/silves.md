Silves is the most historically significant town in the [Algarve](/europe/portugal/algarve), though you would not know it from its quiet current state. For several centuries during the Moorish period it was the capital of al-Gharb, a city of scholars, poets, and traders whose population and wealth exceeded that of Lisbon. The Crusaders took it in 1189 and the Portuguese recovered it definitively in 1242, after which the town gradually declined in importance, and now it sits peacefully on a hill above the orange groves of the Arade river valley, more pleasant than impressive.

The [Moorish castle](/europe/portugal/algarve/silves/silves_castle) at the top of town is the best-preserved Moorish fortification in Portugal — massive red sandstone walls enclosing an interior where you can walk the battlements and look out over the patchwork of citrus and cork oak that stretches to the coast. Below the castle, the Cathedral of Silves (Sé de Silves) occupies the site of an earlier mosque, a Gothic building rebuilt several times that still holds the tombs of some Algarve crusader kings.

The [Museu Arqueológico](/europe/portugal/algarve/silves/museu_arqueologico) on Rua das Portas de Loulé is worth a visit for its Arabic water cistern alone — a deep circular well left in situ beneath the museum building, its vaulted stonework intact from the Islamic period. The collection of local finds extends from the Chalcolithic through to the medieval era.

Silves is about 15 kilometres from the coast, far enough to be largely ignored by beach tourists, which keeps it pleasantly uncrowded most of the year. The annual Medieval Fair in August draws large crowds to a surprisingly well-mounted recreation of the Moorish-era market. The rest of the year, an afternoon is enough to see the main sights, with a stop at the riverside cafés before heading back.
