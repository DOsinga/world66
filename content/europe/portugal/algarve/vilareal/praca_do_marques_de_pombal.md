The central square is the reason most people stop in Vila Real de Santo António. It follows the Pombaline grid that the Marquês de Pombal imposed on the town after the 1755 Lisbon earthquake, with radiating streets and a large paved plaza at the centre. The pattern is unmistakable: black-and-white cobblestone radiating from an obelisk in the middle, flanked by identical whitewashed buildings with orange trim.

It is a formal, almost theatrical space — designed to project authority and symmetry rather than organic charm. But it works. Sitting at one of the café tables under the orange trees and looking out at the geometry of it is genuinely pleasant. The surrounding streets are lined with pastelarias, restaurants, and small shops. The square is liveliest in the early evening when local families come out for the passear — the Portuguese strolling tradition that fills town centres at dusk.
