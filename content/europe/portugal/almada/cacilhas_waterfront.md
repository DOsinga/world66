  Cacilhas has been a ferry crossing point for centuries, and its name may derive
  from the Latin "calcilia" — a type of small boat. In the nineteenth century,
  Cacilhas was a holiday escape for wealthier Lisboetas who came by ferry to eat
  fish and breathe river air. The pattern has not changed much: Lisbon office workers
  still pile off the 1pm ferry and head straight to the same riverside restaurants
  their grandparents knew.

Cacilhas is the natural arrival point for visitors crossing from Lisbon, and the waterfront immediately rewards the twelve-minute ferry ride. The Tagus here is wide enough to feel like an inland sea, and the view back to Lisbon — the old city climbing its hills, the dome of the Basilica da Estrela visible in the middle distance, the Ponte 25 de Abril to the west — is one of the best of the capital you will find anywhere.

The waterfront itself has the Fragata Dom Fernando II e Glória moored to one side and a row of seafood restaurants on the other. A short walk brings you to the base of the Elevador Panorâmico da Boca do Vento, while buses to Cristo Rei depart from a stop just outside the terminal. The whole area is quiet compared to Lisbon across the water — smaller streets, fewer tourists, a slower pace — and that contrast is a large part of its appeal.
