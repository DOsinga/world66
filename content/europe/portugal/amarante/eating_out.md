Amarante is in the heart of the Vinho Verde region, and any restaurant in town will pour the local white — light, slightly fizzy, low in alcohol — by the carafe. The best restaurants cluster on the south bank near the monastery and the river. Restaurante Largo do Paço, in a 16th-century palace just up the hill, is the high-end option (Michelin-listed). Simpler choices on the riverside serve grilled bacalhau, roasted kid (*cabrito*), and the local *vitela* veal.

For something sweet, the famous *doces de São Gonçalo* — the phallic almond pastries — are sold at the confeitarias along the main street; Confeitaria da Ponte is the best-known. The town is also justly proud of its *foguetes* and *São Gonçalos* (cinnamon-and-egg specialities).
