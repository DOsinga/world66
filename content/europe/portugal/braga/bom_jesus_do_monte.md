Bom Jesus do Monte is one of Portugal's great set pieces — a baroque sanctuary perched on a forested hill about five kilometres east of Braga, approached via a monumental staircase that zigzags up through gardens, chapels, and fountains representing the Stations of the Cross. The staircase, built between the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries, is a masterpiece of baroque theatre: each landing introduces a new fountain or small chapel, the whole composition building toward the church at the summit.

You can climb on foot (about twenty minutes and worth doing at least once for the views back over the Minho countryside) or take the water-powered funicular from the base. The summit offers panoramic views, a shaded park, and a cluster of hotels dating from the days when this was a fashionable summer retreat for the Braga bourgeoisie. The church itself is eighteenth-century neoclassical — fine but secondary to the spectacle of the climb.

The site was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage property in 2019, as part of a broader nomination of Iberian baroque sanctuaries.
