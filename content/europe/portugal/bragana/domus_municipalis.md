The Domus Municipalis is the most remarkable single building in Bragança, sitting within the citadel walls next to the castle. It is a twelve-sided council chamber in the Romanesque style, dating from the twelfth century, and is widely considered the only surviving example of secular Romanesque civic architecture in the Iberian Peninsula. The exterior is dignified and simple — a pentagonal plan with Romanesque arches and a corbelled frieze running around the top.

The building was used as the town council chamber in medieval times and later as a water cistern. The interior can be visited on a guided tour and shows the original Roman-arched arcade inside. It sits immediately beside the Church of Santa Maria do Castelo. Its very rarity — nothing else like it exists in Portugal — makes it worth a careful look.
