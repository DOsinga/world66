  The Iberian wolf, once hunted to near-extinction across the peninsula, still maintains a breeding population in Montesinho. The park is one of only a handful of places in western Europe where the wolf remains a native wild animal rather than a reintroduced one. Numbers are small and sightings rare, but the knowledge that they are there gives the landscape a different quality.

Montesinho Natural Park begins just north of Bragança city and extends to the Spanish border, covering over 75,000 hectares of rolling hills, oak forests, granite plateaus, and stone villages where traditional farming and pastoralism continue largely as they have for centuries. The park is one of the wildest landscapes in western Iberia.

Wildlife is diverse: wolves, wild boar, red deer, golden eagles, and black storks all inhabit the park, along with a rich variety of reptiles and raptors. Hiking trails connect the stone villages, most of which have populations of only a few dozen people. The park lacks the tourist infrastructure of more visited areas but rewards those with their own transport. The village of Rio de Onor, on the Spanish border, is particularly atmospheric — the village straddles the border and has maintained a communal way of life for centuries.
