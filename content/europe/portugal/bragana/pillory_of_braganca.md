The Pillory of Bragança is a fifteenth-century stone column that stands in the square within the citadel walls. Pillories were once the central symbol of a town's right to self-governance in medieval Portugal — only a town that had received a royal charter could erect one — and Bragança's is one of the finest surviving examples in the country.

What makes this pillory unusual is its base: the column rises from the back of a carved stone pig, a wild boar figure rooted in the pre-Roman Celtic traditions of the region. Known locally as the "Porca de Bragança" (sow of Bragança), the animal is a symbol of the city and of the broader Trás-os-Montes region. These stone pigs are found across northern Portugal and northwestern Spain and are thought to have had ritual significance in the pre-Roman Iron Age.
