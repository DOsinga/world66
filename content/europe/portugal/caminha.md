Caminha is the northernmost town on the Portuguese coast, the place where the country runs out and the [Minho river](/europe/portugal/caminha/foz_do_minho) becomes the border with Spanish Galicia. From [Porto](/europe/portugal/porto) the road and the railway both follow the coast and then the river up to this point and stop. There is something appealing about an end-of-the-line town: the square is small and lively, the granite is grey, and the river is broad and slow.

The old centre is built around a single handsome square, the [Praça Conselheiro Silva Torres](/europe/portugal/caminha/praca_conselheiro_silva_torres), framed by a fortified medieval [clock tower](/europe/portugal/caminha/torre_do_relogio) and a sixteenth-century Renaissance fountain. A few steps east stands the [Igreja Matriz](/europe/portugal/caminha/igreja_matriz), a late-Gothic parish church with an extraordinary Mudéjar wooden ceiling that is the single best reason to step inside any building in town.

The other reason to come is the river. A small [ferry crosses to A Guarda](/europe/portugal/caminha/ferry_to_a_guarda) in Spain — a ten-minute crossing that ends at one of the better seafood ports in Galicia. The boat sets out from the quay just below the old town and is at its most appealing in late afternoon, with the lights of both shores just coming on. The same stretch of riverbank makes an excellent setting for a long meal if you are waiting for the last train back to Porto.

Caminha is a half-day town for most travellers, or an overnight stop on a slow coastal route between Porto and Galicia. Either way, it rewards a slower pace than its size might suggest.
