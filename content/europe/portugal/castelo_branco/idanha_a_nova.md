Idanha-a-Nova is a quiet municipality in eastern Portugal, close to the Spanish border and the empty spaces of Beira Baixa. The modern town is modest, but the municipality matters to travellers because it includes Monsanto, the granite village that climbs a steep hill northeast of Castelo Branco.

Monsanto is the place to aim for. Houses are wedged between boulders, lanes squeeze under stone overhangs, and the climb to the ruined castle gives wide views across one of Portugal's least crowded corners. The village was once promoted as "the most Portuguese village in Portugal", which sounds like a slogan until you start walking through its rock-built streets.

The wider area rewards slow travel by car. Idanha-a-Velha, Penha Garcia, and the dry borderland villages nearby are close enough to combine with Monsanto, but public transport is thin. Come for the landscape, the stone villages, and the feeling of being far from Portugal's coastal tourist routes.
