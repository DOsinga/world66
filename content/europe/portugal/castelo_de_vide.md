Castelo de Vide is a small Alentejo hill town with more character than its size suggests. The castle sits above a slope of white houses, narrow lanes and fountains, with the Serra de Sao Mamede rising just to the west. It has long been called the "Sintra of the Alentejo", which is a little generous, but the mix of stone, gardens and mountain air is real.

The town's strongest thread is its Jewish heritage. Below the castle, the old Judiaria keeps the tight street pattern of the medieval quarter, and the synagogue now works as a small museum of the community that lived here before and after Portugal's expulsion order.

Castelo de Vide pairs naturally with nearby Marvao and Portalegre, but it is not just a stop on the way. Give it a few hours for the castle, the synagogue, the fountains and the walk up to Nossa Senhora da Penha if the weather is clear.
