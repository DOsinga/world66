The reserve covers more than 2,000 hectares of tidal mudflat, salt pan, and dry scrub along the Guadiana estuary — the first nature reserve created in Portugal, designated in 1975. It is on a major migration route, and the bird list runs to over 200 species: greater flamingo, spoonbill, white stork, kentish plover, avocet, and a healthy population of breeding little terns.

The visitor centre at Cerro do Bufo, about 2 km out of town, has hides and a short interpretive trail through the salt pans. The best months for birds are March to May and September to November; flamingos are present year-round but most numerous in autumn. The working salt pans here produce flor de sal sold across Portugal — guided tours of the harvest run in July and August.
