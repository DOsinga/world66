Mercado de Escravos occupies a small building in central Lagos that is traditionally associated with one of Europe's earliest slave markets. Today it functions as a museum, using the site to confront the city's role in the 15th-century Atlantic slave trade rather than letting Lagos be only beaches, cliffs, and marina life.

The museum is compact, but the subject is large. It is worth visiting precisely because it complicates the town's sunny surface, linking the Age of Discoveries to forced labour, colonial violence, and the human cost behind Portugal's maritime expansion.
