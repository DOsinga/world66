Alcântara is the old industrial waterfront west of the centre, where Lisbon's textile mills, warehouses, and the docks once drove the city's economy. The great red span of the Ponte 25 de Abril comes down here, and the district has spent the last two decades reinventing its disused factories into one of the most creative corners of the city.

The anchor is the LX Factory, a complex of 19th-century industrial buildings now packed with design studios, restaurants, bars, and the cavernous Ler Devagar bookshop. Down by the river, the Doca de Santo Amaro lines a former yacht dock with waterfront bars directly beneath the bridge, and the Pilar 7 experience takes you up inside one of its pillars. Between them the streets are still half working, half reinvented, which is much of the appeal.

Alcântara sits between the riverside and the hill suburb of Campo de Ourique, a tram or short train ride from the centre. It comes alive in the evening, when the LX Factory restaurants and the dock bars fill up and the nightclubs along the waterfront open their doors.
