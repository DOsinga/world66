One of the great private art collections in the world, assembled over a lifetime by the Armenian oil magnate Calouste Gulbenkian and given to Portugal on his death in 1955. The collection spans 5,000 years and six continents, and unlike many large museum collections it was selected by a single eye — every piece was chosen because Gulbenkian personally loved it.

The highlights include Egyptian antiquities, Islamic art, Armenian manuscripts, Rembrandt, Rubens, Turner, Degas, Renoir, and Lalique jewellery. The Lalique room alone — a suite of Art Nouveau jewels of extraordinary fineness — is worth the journey. The museum is in Parque de Palhavã in the Avenidas Novas district, about 25 minutes walk north of the Baixa or two metro stops from Marquês de Pombal. Allow two to three hours for the permanent collection.
