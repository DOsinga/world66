title: "Books"

---

**"The Stationmaster of Lordsburg"** by Ferreira de Castro. The Portuguese novelist visited Madeira in the 1930s and wrote some of his most evocative travel sketches about the island — short, careful prose about levada workers and emigrant villages. Not always easy to find in English, but worth the search if you can read Portuguese or French.

**"Madeira: The Mid-Atlantic Island"** by Ovidio Bensaude. A 1930s account by a Madeiran writer that captures the island in transition — wine trade, emigration, and the early tourist boom. Old-fashioned but warm and detailed.

**"Letters from Madeira"** by Isabella de França. A young Englishwoman's journal of a long stay in the 1850s, full of vivid observation of village life, the wine trade, and the European convalescents who flocked here in the Victorian era. Reissued in modern editions.

**"Madeira Walks: 60 of the Best"** by Paddy Dillon (Cicerone). Not literature, but the definitive walking guide and an essential companion for anyone planning to do levadas properly. Detailed maps, route descriptions, and honest difficulty assessments.

**"The Fortunate Isles"** by Robert White. A classic 19th-century travel account covering Madeira and the Canaries, with the leisurely pace of an era when a single trip could last six months. Useful background for understanding the island's connection with the wider Atlantic world.
