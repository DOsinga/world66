  At 580 metres straight down to the ocean, Cabo Girão is often described as the highest sea cliff in Europe. The claim is contested — there are higher cliffs in Ireland and Norway — but the glass skywalk on top, opened in 2012, is unambiguous enough; you stand on a transparent floor with nothing between you and the surf except thin air.

Cabo Girão is a vast vertical sea cliff west of Funchal, rising 580 metres straight up from the Atlantic. At the top, a small skywalk extends out over the edge with a glass floor, offering a queasy direct view down to the ocean and to the small terraced fields the locals farm on a narrow shelf at the foot of the cliff.

The whole thing takes ten minutes to look around, but the view along the coast in both directions is worth the trip up. The cliff sits above the village of Câmara de Lobos and can easily be combined with a stop there for lunch.
