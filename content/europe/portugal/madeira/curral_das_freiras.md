  The "Nuns' Valley" got its name from the Convent of Santa Clara in Funchal, whose sisters were sent to hide in this remote bowl in 1566 when French corsairs attacked the city. Curral das Freiras is a small village in an extraordinary natural amphitheatre — a deep volcanic crater ringed by 1,000-metre cliffs, accessible by a single dramatic mountain road from the south coast. The standard tourist stop is the Eira do Serrado viewpoint, which sits on the rim and offers an aerial view straight down into the village and across to the central peaks.

The road then drops down into the valley itself through a series of tunnels. The village is famous for its chestnuts — chestnut bread, chestnut soup, chestnut liqueur — and the late-October chestnut festival draws hundreds of visitors. Even out of season, a stop at one of the small cafés for a chestnut cake and a poncha is the obvious thing to do.
