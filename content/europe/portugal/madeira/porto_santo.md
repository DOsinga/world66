Porto Santo is [Madeira](/europe/portugal/madeira)'s smaller sister island, about 40 km to the northeast and reached by ferry or short flight from Funchal. Where Madeira is steep, green, and dramatic, Porto Santo is dry, low, and sandy. Its great argument is simple: [Praia do Porto Santo](/europe/portugal/madeira/porto_santo/praia_do_porto_santo), a long golden beach running along the south coast, backed by a small town and pale volcanic hills.

The island is more than a beach escape, though it remains a quiet one. Vila Baleira has the [Casa Colombo](/europe/portugal/madeira/porto_santo/casa_colombo), a small museum tied to Christopher Columbus's stay on the island, while the interior has short walks to viewpoints such as [Pico Ana Ferreira](/europe/portugal/madeira/porto_santo/pico_ana_ferreira) and [Pico do Castelo](/europe/portugal/madeira/porto_santo/pico_do_castelo). The whole island and surrounding sea are part of a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve, a reminder that Porto Santo's dry landscapes, islets, dunes, and marine life are not just Madeira's beach annex.

Porto Santo works as a long day trip from Funchal if you only want sand and a quick look at Vila Baleira. It is better with one night, when the ferry crowds thin out and the island's slower rhythm starts to make sense. Do not come expecting Madeira's levada drama; come for warm water, open sky, small volcanic peaks, and a gentler Atlantic mood.
