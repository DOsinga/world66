The Grutas de São Vicente are a network of lava tubes on Madeira's north coast, formed about 400,000 years ago when molten rock from the eruption that built Pico Ruivo drained through the older lava flows beneath. A 700-metre section is open to the public via a 30-minute guided walk, with low ceilings, smooth walls, and small underground pools.

A small adjoining volcanism centre uses films and interactive displays to explain how Madeira was built up from the seabed over millions of years — surprisingly well done for a small museum, and especially worth a stop with children. The whole visit takes about 90 minutes.
