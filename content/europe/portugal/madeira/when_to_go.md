---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

Madeira's reputation as "the island of eternal spring" is not far wrong. Temperatures on the south coast stay between 16°C and 26°C all year, with most rain falling between October and March. Even in the wet months it is rarely cold, and the rain tends to come in short bursts rather than long grey days.

The best months are probably April to October — warm, mostly dry, good for hiking and sea swimming. July and August get busy, particularly with European package tourists, and the higher-elevation walks can be hot. May and June are arguably the sweet spot: the flowers are in full bloom, the days are long, and the crowds are still light. The Festa da Flor (Flower Festival) in early May fills Funchal with parades, costumes, and elaborate floral displays.

Winter brings cooler temperatures, more rain in the mountains, and occasional snow on Pico Ruivo — but Madeira's New Year fireworks display over the bay of Funchal is famous, regularly cited as one of the largest in Europe and worth timing a visit around.
