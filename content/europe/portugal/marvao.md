Marvão is one of Portugal's great high places: a white village squeezed inside walls on a quartzite ridge, with the Alentejo falling away on one side and Spain visible beyond the hills. It is small, but the setting gives it the drama of a much larger destination.

The [castle](/europe/portugal/marvao/marvao_castle) is the obvious reason to come. Its battlements run along the ridge and give long views over the Serra de São Mamede, Castelo de Vide, and the old frontier country. The village below is part of the same experience, a tight run of lanes, gates, and whitewashed houses where the walls are never far away.

Marvão also has a deeper layer at [Ammaia](/europe/portugal/marvao/ammaia), the remains of a Roman town in the valley below. Taken together, the hilltop fortress and Roman ruins make Marvão a strong day from Portalegre or Castelo de Vide, especially if you like border towns where the landscape explains the history.
