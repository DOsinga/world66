Miranda do Douro sits at Portugal's far northeastern edge, high above the Douro where the river cuts a hard border with Spain. It is small, remote, and more interesting than its size suggests: a fortified old town, a serious 16th-century cathedral, gorge viewpoints, and one of the few places where you will hear **Mirandese**, a local Astur-Leonese language recognised officially in Portugal.

The town belongs as much to the **Terra de Miranda** plateau as to the Douro. The stone streets around the **[co-cathedral](/europe/portugal/miranda_do_douro/concatedral_de_miranda_do_douro)** feel austere and inland, while the cliffs just beyond town drop into the Parque Natural do Douro Internacional. The contrast gives Miranda a good spare-day case: half cultural border town, half gorge landscape.

Come for the old centre, the **[Museu da Terra de Miranda](/europe/portugal/miranda_do_douro/museu_da_terra_de_miranda)**, and a walk or drive to a viewpoint such as **[Miradouro do Castrilhouco](/europe/portugal/miranda_do_douro/miradouro_do_castrilhouco)**. If you have time, boat trips below town show the Douro cliffs from the water, with birds of prey often circling above the rock walls.
