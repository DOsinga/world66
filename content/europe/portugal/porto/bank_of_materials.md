The Banco de Materiais is one of the most practically useful institutions in Porto, though most visitors never hear of it. The city council maintains a warehouse of salvaged architectural materials — historic azulejo tiles, carved stone, decorative ironwork, original door hardware — that building owners undertaking restoration of listed properties can borrow free of charge. The idea is to keep the visual consistency of the historic centre intact even as individual buildings are restored, by ensuring that replacement materials match the originals in period and style.

The warehouse is open to the public for viewing even if you are not a building owner in need of tiles. Rows of salvaged azulejo panels, organized by period and pattern, represent a cross-section of Porto's tile history that is more varied and historically representative than most formal museum collections. The Atlas Obscura community considers this one of Porto's genuine curiosities, and it is: a working archive of building materials that functions incidentally as a museum of the city's architectural heritage.
