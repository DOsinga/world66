Bonfim is where Porto's creative scene has largely relocated as it has been priced out of the Ribeira and Cedofeita. The neighbourhood lies east of the historic centre, an easy downhill walk from the cathedral, and it has the right combination of crumbling architecture, good bones, and enough authentic working-class life to be genuinely interesting rather than merely fashionable.

The main streets — Rua de Antero de Quental, Rua de Álvares Cabral, and the areas around the Jardim de Bonfim — are lined with independent restaurants, natural wine bars, design studios, and the occasional ceramic workshop that has been here for forty years. It is the right place to eat well without paying Ribeira prices, and the Sunday flea market at the Mercado de Bonfim draws a good mix of dealers and buyers. The neighbourhood also holds one of Porto's better miradouros — the elevated gardens at Passeio das Virtudes have a terrace view over the Douro that few tourists find.
