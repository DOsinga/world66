The adjacent Carmelitas and Carmo churches are an architectural curiosity worth examining closely. The churches share almost the same footprint, separated only by a narrow strip of building — reportedly built to enforce the canonical separation of the two religious communities that occupied them. The story is probably apocryphal in its details, but the buildings are genuinely adjacent in a way that invites examination.

The Carmo church is the showpiece: its exterior side wall is covered in a vast azulejo tile panel completed in 1912 by Silvestre Silvestri, depicting scenes from the legend of the founding of the Carmelite Order on Mount Carmel in the 12th century. The composition is ambitious and the execution excellent — it is one of the finest exterior tile panels in Porto. The church interior, in contrast, is relatively subdued. The pairing of the two churches, the tiny house between them, and the magnificent tile wall outside make this a five-minute stop that rewards attention.
