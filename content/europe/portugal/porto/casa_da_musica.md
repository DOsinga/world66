The Casa da Música is one of the great concert halls of Europe, and the building that houses it is one of the more radical pieces of 21st-century architecture in Portugal. Rem Koolhaas's design — a faceted concrete form anchored to the Rotunda da Boavista — looks from the outside like a piece of geological strangeness dropped into the city. Inside, the main auditorium's transparent end walls let natural light into the hall and give performers an unobstructed view of the city they are performing in.

The Orquestra Sinfónica do Porto Casa da Música is the resident ensemble, and the programming is adventurous — contemporary music, fado, world music, and jazz appear alongside the standard orchestral repertoire. The building also houses smaller performance spaces, a bar with panoramic views, and a restaurant. Guided tours of the building run daily if you want to understand the architecture; book tickets for the evening performances well in advance, especially for the better-known ensembles.
