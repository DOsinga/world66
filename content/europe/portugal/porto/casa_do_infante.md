The Casa do Infante is one of Porto's oldest surviving buildings, a 14th-century structure on the Ribeira waterfront that has been claimed as the birthplace of Henry the Navigator, the Portuguese prince who organized the systematic exploration of the African coast in the early 15th century. Whether or not Henry was actually born here, the building's location at the heart of Porto's medieval merchant district makes it a fitting monument to the commercial and maritime ambitions that drove Portuguese exploration.

The interior has been converted into a small museum tracing Porto's history from its Roman origins through the medieval and early modern periods. The archaeological layers exposed in the basement — including Roman mosaics and medieval foundations — are the highlight. The collection of documents and objects related to the city's commercial history is modest but gives good context for the rest of the historic centre. The building's position between the Cais da Ribeira and the Alfândega do Porto customs house makes it a natural stop on any circuit of the waterfront.
