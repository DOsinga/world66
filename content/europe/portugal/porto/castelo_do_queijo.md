The Castelo do Queijo sits on the coastal rocks at the mouth of the Douro where the river meets the Atlantic at Foz. The small 17th-century fort is named for the cheese-like appearance of the granite boulders it is built on, and while it is modest by Portuguese castle standards — no keep, no great hall — the position is superb: the open Atlantic to the west, the Douro estuary to the south, and the rocky coast of Matosinhos stretching north.

The fort is maintained by the Câmara Municipal de Porto and can be visited. The interior is unremarkable but the walls offer a panoramic view of the coast and the river mouth. More important is the setting: the rocks and coast immediately around the fort are excellent for watching the waves in winter (the Atlantic swell here is formidable) and for swimming in calmer summer conditions. The esplanade and garden outside the fort is one of the better places in Foz to sit in the late afternoon with a coffee and watch the sun decline toward the Atlantic horizon.
