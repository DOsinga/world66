story: Cedofeita takes its name from the Romanesque church of São Martinho de Cedofeita, which some historians have claimed as the oldest surviving church in Iberia — dating its origins to the Suevic kingdom of the 6th century, though what survives above ground is mostly 12th century. The neighbourhood around it has been Porto's most bourgeois quarter for 200 years, which explains both the quality of the commercial streets and the density of good cafés.

Cedofeita is the neighbourhood where Porto shops and eats away from the tourist circuit. The Rua Miguel Bombarda is the centre of the contemporary art and design scene — a strip of galleries, concept stores, and ateliers that becomes a proper street market on the first Saturday of each month. Rua de Cedofeita itself is lined with independent clothing shops, bookshops, record stores, and the kind of small restaurants that fill at lunch with office workers rather than tourists.

The Igreja de Cedofeita, the 12th-century Romanesque church at the neighbourhood's heart, is easy to miss because it sits slightly back from the main street behind a garden wall. It is worth finding: the carved portal and the simple nave interior are among the earliest surviving Romanesque work in northern Portugal. The neighbourhood as a whole is the right place to spend an afternoon when you've done the headline sights and want Porto at a more human scale.
