story: The cachorrinho — literally "little dog," a miniature version of the cachorro hot dog — was invented in Porto in the 1950s as a budget street food using the bread and pork-derived sausages that were already staples of the Porto diet. The Rua de Belomonte area in the Ribeira became the cachorrinho district, with competing restaurants each claiming the authentic version. Porto has its own version of the hot dog, and it is better than most of its global competition. The cachorrinho — a torpedo roll split lengthways, filled with pork sausage, and soaked in a spiced beer-and-tomato sauce similar to the francesinha sauce — is served in the small sandwich restaurants concentrated along Rua de Belomonte in the Ribeira. The roll is griddled, the sausage is pork-rich, and the sauce is the point of difference between establishments.

The competition between the Belomonte cachorrinho restaurants — Gazela and its rivals a few doors down — has been running for decades and is taken seriously by Porto residents who have strong opinions about which sauce is superior. The format is casual: sit at a counter, order one or two cachorrinhos with a beer, eat fast. This is not a romantic dinner option, but it is a genuine Porto working-class food experience at extremely modest cost. Lunch hours see the longest queues; the restaurants stay open into the evening.
