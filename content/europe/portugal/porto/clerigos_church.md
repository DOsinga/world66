story: The Clérigos Tower was the tallest building in Portugal when it was completed in 1763, and it remained so for over a century. Nicolau Nasoni, the Italian architect responsible for both the tower and the church below, also designed the façade of the Episcopal Palace and the Casa de Mateus — he essentially gave Porto its Baroque visual identity. The tower's 76 metres are climbed via 225 steps and reward with the best 360-degree view of the historic city.

The Clérigos Tower is Porto's most recognizable silhouette, visible from much of the city and identifiable from photographs before you've even arrived. Up close, the oval Baroque church that Nasoni designed to accompany it is equally impressive — his Italian sensibility translated remarkably well into the local granite, and the exterior decorative carving is among the finest in northern Portugal.

Climb the tower. The 225 steps are tight and spiral, and at the top the terrace is barely large enough for a few people at a time, but the view is worth every step. You see the Douro, the bridges, the terracotta roofscape of the old town, Vila Nova de Gaia across the water, and on a clear day the Atlantic glinting to the west. The church interior is worth a look too — the gilded retable and the overall proportions of Nasoni's oval plan repay close attention.
