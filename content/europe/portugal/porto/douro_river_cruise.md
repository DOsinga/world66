story: The six-bridge cruise — passing under all six bridges that cross the Douro within Porto — takes about an hour and is the best way to understand the city's relationship with the river, which has been its defining characteristic since the Phoenicians or earlier. The bridges span different eras: the wrought-iron Dom Luís I from 1886, the Arrábida arch bridge from 1963 (at its completion, the longest concrete arch bridge in the world), and the Infante Dom Henrique bridge from 2003 by Adão da Fonseca.

The Douro river cruises that depart from the Cais da Ribeira are simultaneously touristy and genuinely worthwhile. The one-hour "six bridges" circuit takes you upriver past all the bridges that span the Douro within the greater Porto area, giving you the view of the city and Gaia from the water — a perspective unavailable from any land-based viewpoint. The boats are comfortable, the English commentary is adequate, and the experience of floating on the Douro beneath the iron arch of the Dom Luís I is one worth having.

Longer cruises run upriver toward the Douro Valley — one-day and multi-day cruises that penetrate the gorge and reach the wine country. These are more expensive but give access to the Douro landscape that makes the train journey worthwhile but surpasses it in scenery. The shorter version — the six bridges — is the right choice if you have limited time; the longer Douro Valley option is worth considering if you want to see the wine-growing terraces from the river rather than from above.
