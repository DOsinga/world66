story: The Foz neighbourhood occupies the mouth of the Douro where the river meets the Atlantic, and its character is shaped by that conjunction — the Douro has been an Atlantic trade route since the Romans, and the wealthy merchant families who controlled that trade built their villas here from the 18th century onward. The result is Porto's most elegant residential district, which also happens to have the nearest ocean beaches to the city centre.
---

Foz do Douro sits at the river's mouth, where the Douro slides into the Atlantic between two forts — the São João da Foz fortress on the Porto side and the São Francisco Xavier fort (known as the Cheese Castle) across the water in Matosinhos. The neighbourhood has the well-maintained quality of money: wide streets, villa gardens, esplanades, and good restaurants along the waterfront.

The seafront promenade from Foz toward Matosinhos is one of Porto's best walks — about 5 kilometres of Atlantic coast, with surf beaches, rock pools, and the fort at Castelo do Queijo offering views back toward the city. The tram line from the city centre to Foz is a heritage route using historic rolling stock, which makes for a pleasant and practical journey if you don't want to take the bus. On summer evenings half of Porto seems to be here watching the sun go down over the Atlantic.
