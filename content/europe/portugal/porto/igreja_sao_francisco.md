The effect is overwhelming, and the contrast with the austere Gothic bones beneath it is part of what makes the church so unsettling and magnificent.

The Igreja de São Francisco contains what is arguably the most extravagantly gilded interior in Portugal, which is saying something. The Gothic nave was built in the 14th century; in the late 17th century craftsmen covered almost every surface with carved and gilded wood in the full exuberance of the Portuguese Baroque. The effect is staggering — you step in from a plain stone exterior into an interior where the walls appear to ripple with gold, and the eye doesn't know where to land.

Beneath the church are the catacombs — ossaries where members of the Franciscan Third Order were buried from the 17th century onward. The catacombs are included in the entrance ticket and are more atmospheric than macabre: vaulted chambers with the bones of generations of Porto's merchant class, a suitably sombre counterpoint to the gilt excess above. The adjacent Palace of the Bolsa, a 19th-century stock exchange, is worth a separate visit for its astonishing Arab Room.
