story: Jardim do Morro sits directly above the Ponte de Dom Luís I on the Gaia side, at the same elevation as the bridge's upper deck. The Serra do Pilar Monastery behind it — a circular church with a circular cloister, unique in Portugal — was used as a fortified position by Wellington during the crossing of the Douro in 1809, an operation that helped turn the Peninsular War. The monastery is a UNESCO site as part of the Historic Centre of Porto designation.

The Jardim do Morro is in Vila Nova de Gaia rather than Porto proper, but the view it provides of Porto is the definitive one: the entire historic centre spread across the hillside above the Douro, the Ponte de Dom Luís I in the foreground, and the river sweeping away in both directions. This is the view on the postcards, the photographs, and the travel posters — and it is best in the late afternoon when the sun lights the azulejo facades of the Ribeira from the west.

The upper metro station (Dom Luís I on the D line) deposits you here from the Porto side, having crossed the upper deck of the bridge. The Serra do Pilar Monastery is a short walk up from the garden — the circular church is open for visits and the cloister terrace gives the same panorama from a slightly higher elevation. The port wine lodge strip of the Gaia embankment is a five-minute walk down the hill, making this viewpoint the natural pivot point between a morning in Porto's historic centre and an afternoon tasting port wine across the river.
