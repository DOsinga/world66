Miragaia is the neighbourhood between the Ribeira and the Alfândega do Porto, running along the Douro waterfront in the stretch where the historic city begins to give way to the 19th-century extension toward Foz. The area was Porto's Moorish quarter in the medieval period and later its Jewish neighbourhood, and the dense lanes above the waterfront still follow a pre-modern street pattern of considerable antiquity.

The neighbourhood is in a permanent state of partial renovation — some buildings restored, others leaning at angles that suggest imminent collapse, and a core of actual residents going about their lives in between. It is the most atmospheric part of the waterfront to walk through on your way between the Ribeira and the tram terminus at the Alfândega, and the Museu do Vinho do Porto at its western edge is a good reason to come specifically. The small boats moored along the Cais de Miragaia are sometimes available for private hire on the river.
