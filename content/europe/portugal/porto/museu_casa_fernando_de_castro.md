The Casa Museu Fernando de Castro in Cedofeita is one of Porto's most eccentric and rewarding small museums. Fernando de Castro spent forty years filling his house with salvaged azulejo panels, religious statuary, paintings, furniture, and decorative objects — not as a systematic collector but as someone who could not pass a beautiful thing without bringing it home. The result is an interior of remarkable density, where the walls are entirely covered in tiles of different periods and styles, every alcove holds a figure of a saint or a devotional object, and the overall effect is of a private world arranged exactly as its owner intended.

De Castro's bequest specified that nothing be moved or removed, and the museum has kept to that condition. This makes the house feel inhabited rather than institutionalized — you are entering someone's vision of how to live surrounded by beautiful things, which is a different experience from a conventional museum display. The garden behind the house has its own azulejo installation. Entry is inexpensive and the museum is usually quiet.
