The Museu do Azulejo in the Solar dos Condes de Resende, just across the Douro from Porto in Vila Nova de Gaia, is the best single place to understand Portuguese tile art in depth. The collection is arranged chronologically and covers the full range of the tradition — Mudéjar geometric tiles from the 15th century, Dutch-influenced blue-and-white panels from the 17th, the great narrative scenes of the 18th century (including railway stations and palace walls), and the more experimental 20th-century work.

The building that houses the collection is itself a showpiece: an aristocratic manor house whose staircase and reception rooms are tiled with 18th-century panels of exceptional quality. The museum is small enough to cover properly in ninety minutes and large enough to be genuinely comprehensive. It receives far fewer visitors than it deserves, largely because it is on the Gaia side of the river — combine it with a visit to the port wine lodges and the Jardim do Morro viewpoint on the same trip.
