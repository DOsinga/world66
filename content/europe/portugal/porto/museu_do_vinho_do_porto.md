The Museu do Vinho do Porto occupies a converted wine warehouse on the Douro waterfront in Massarelos, between the Ribeira and Foz. The permanent exhibition traces the history of port wine from its origins in the upper Douro through the shipping and trade networks that made it a global commodity — the role of British merchants, the regulatory history, the methods of production and aging.

The museum is not flashy, but it is thorough and well-organized, and the Art Nouveau poster collection associated with the Ramos Pinto brand is genuinely impressive — bold, sensuous images that stand up well against European commercial art of the same period. The riverside location is a bonus: the building has an esplanade on the Douro, and the area immediately around it is quieter and more local in character than the Ribeira. Entry is inexpensive and the museum is rarely crowded.
