Portugal's oldest national museum occupies the Carrancas Palace, an 18th-century palace that also served briefly as the residence of Napoleon's brother Joseph Bonaparte during the Peninsular War. The collection spans Portuguese art from the 12th century to the early 20th — gilded altarpieces, Flemish-influenced devotional paintings, 18th-century porcelain, and a strong 19th-century sculpture section anchored by Soares dos Reis.

The Soares dos Reis gallery is the reason to come. His sculptures — particularly O Desterrado and the later Flor Agreste — have a psychological directness that sets them apart from most academic 19th-century work. The museum also holds good examples of Portuguese silver, faience from Vista Alegre, and a collection of decorative arts that gives a solid sense of the material culture of the Porto merchant class. It is consistently undervisited relative to its quality.
