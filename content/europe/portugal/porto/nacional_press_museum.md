The Museu Nacional da Imprensa occupies a converted factory building on the Douro waterfront in Massarelos, and it houses the most comprehensive collection of printing technology in Portugal. The machines themselves are the main attraction: wooden hand presses, Linotype compositing machines, early offset lithography equipment, and newspaper printing presses — all in working order and demonstrated by guides who know how to use them.

The museum's collection of Portuguese printed matter is equally interesting: first editions, censored publications with the Estado Novo's stamps visible, illegal underground press runs from the Salazar period, and the first editions of newspapers that still publish today. The river view from the museum's esplanade is excellent — this stretch of the Douro waterfront, between the Alfândega and the tram terminus at Massarelos, is one of the quieter and more atmospheric parts of the waterfront. Entry is inexpensive.
