The Palácio da Bolsa — Porto's 19th-century stock exchange, built on the ruins of the Monastery of São Francisco after it burned — is required viewing, but only if you take the guided tour. On your own, you see grandly proportioned rooms. With a guide, the rooms become a story about Porto's mercantile ambitions: the commercial exchange where deals were struck, the portrait gallery of the city's benefactors, and then, at the end, the Arab Room.

The Salão Árabe is one of the most elaborate interiors in Portugal — walls covered in gilded stucco imitating Moorish interlace patterns, Quranic inscriptions in Arabic, coloured glass, and a ceiling that took a decade to complete. The room was used for receptions and state banquets; today it hosts official ceremonies and occasional concerts. Tours run throughout the day and last about 30 minutes; the English-language tours are frequent and good.
