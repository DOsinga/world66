Porto's six Douro bridges span two centuries of bridge engineering, and the Infante Dom Henrique — the newest — is a fitting modern addition to the sequence. Completed in 2003 to the design of Adão da Fonseca and Ove Arup, the bridge carries the IP1 road between Porto and Gaia on a single-level deck supported by a flattened concrete arch with no intermediate piers. The span is elegant and the profile low — the bridge inserts itself into the gorge without competing with the more dramatic Dom Luís I upstream.

Walking across the bridge is permitted on dedicated pedestrian lanes, and the view from mid-span gives a good sense of the gorge's depth and of the other five bridges in their upstream arrangement. The Infante is best seen from a distance — from the Ribeira waterfront or from the Gaia embankment — where the clean arch against the hillside shows the engineering to best advantage. It is worth crossing at least once if you are walking between Porto and Gaia on the eastern route.
