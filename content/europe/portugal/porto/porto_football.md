FC Porto is one of the three great clubs of Portuguese football and has twice won the UEFA Champions League — in 1987 and 2004. The 2004 win under José Mourinho, coming from a Portuguese club with a fraction of the budget of the traditional European powerhouses, was one of the more unexpected results in the tournament's history. Porto fans are passionate in the way that football-as-civic-identity communities always are, and attending a league match at the Estádio do Dragão is a good way to experience that.

The Dragão was built for Euro 2004 to the design of Manuel Salgado and holds 50,000 spectators. League matches against Braga, Vitória de Guimarães, and the Lisbon clubs draw the best atmospheres; the Porto-Benfica fixture is the most important domestic match in Portuguese football. The stadium is in the eastern suburbs, accessible by metro (Estádio do Dragão stop on Line B, C, E). Tickets are available through the club website; the best prices are for the upper tiers on the ends. The club museum adjacent to the stadium is worth a visit even outside match days — the Champions League trophies and the history of Portuguese football are well presented.
