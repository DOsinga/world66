Praça da Batalha is one of Porto's principal squares, linking the lower city with the streets running toward the university and the Boavista. The square anchors the southern end of Rua de Santa Catarina and is defined by three significant buildings: the neoclassical façade of the Teatro Nacional de São João, the restored art deco Cinema Batalha, and the 18th-century church of São Ildefonso with its enormous azulejo exterior.

The Igreja de Santo Ildefonso is covered in 11,000 blue-and-white tiles by Jorge Colaço depicting biblical scenes and allegories — the same painter responsible for São Bento station. The exterior tile programme was completed in 1932 and is one of the largest tile compositions in Porto. The square itself functions as a bus terminus and a gathering point; it is not a place of great beauty, but it connects many of the city's most important streets and is worth knowing as an orientation point.
