Porto has a wine bar scene that quietly matches anything in Lisbon, and unlike the capital it has not yet been entirely colonized by tourists. The concentration of good wine bars is in Cedofeita and Bonfim — the same neighbourhoods where the creative economy has made its home.

The reference point is Vinho Verde, the young white wine from the Minho region to Porto's north: typically low in alcohol, high in acidity, slightly sparkling, and ideally drunk cold with grilled fish. But the more interesting bars push further: natural wines from small Douro producers, orange wines, skin-contact whites, and the occasional genuinely obscure regional variety. O Afonso, Franginho, and Flor de Café are consistent recommendations; the Rua dos Caldeireiros strip in Cedofeita tends to collect the better wine-focused bars within walking distance of each other. Prices remain modest by western European standards, which adds considerably to the pleasure.
