The Ribeira is where Porto began, and the weight of that history is palpable in the tightly packed streets that climb from the waterfront. The Cais da Ribeira — the riverside promenade — is the obvious starting point: café tables overlooking the Douro, the Ponte de Dom Luís overhead, and the old merchant houses stacked up behind in varying degrees of renovation and decay. But the real character of the Ribeira is in the lanes above: Rua de Cima do Muro, Rua de São João, and the staircases connecting them to higher streets.

Walk uphill from the Cais and you move through successive layers of the city's history: Romanesque foundations, medieval stone houses, Baroque church fronts, 18th-century tile facades. The Ribeira is most atmospheric early morning or on weekday afternoons when the tour groups thin out. The neighbourhood becomes genuinely local again after dark — the restaurants along the quay stay busy, but the alleys above them belong to Porto's residents.
