story: Porto's cathedral was begun in the 12th century and has been modified so many times that its current form represents nearly every major period of Portuguese ecclesiastical architecture. The Romanesque nave survived; the Gothic cloister was added in the 14th century and later covered with azulejo panels by Nicolau Nasoni; the Baroque silver altarpiece replaced a Gothic one destroyed in the 18th century. The building is a palimpsest of the city's religious history.

The Sé sits on the highest promontory of the old city, where a fortified settlement existed before Porto was Porto. The fortress-like Romanesque exterior — twin towers, thick walls, minimal ornament — reflects the cathedral's origins as both church and defensive stronghold, and it still looks more like a castle than a house of God from certain angles.

Inside, the 14th-century Gothic cloister is the highlight. The lower arcade is tiled with 18th-century azulejos designed by Nicolau Nasoni depicting scenes from the Song of Songs and the life of the Virgin — a startling combination of Old Testament eroticism and devotional imagery in blue and white tile. A small museum occupies the upper floor of the cloister with ecclesiastical silver and textiles. The terrace outside the cathedral offers one of the better views over the Ribeira and the Douro, and it is free.
