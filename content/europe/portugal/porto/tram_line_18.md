story: Porto's electric tram system began operating in 1895 — one of the earliest in Iberian Europe — and at its peak extended across the city and into neighboring municipalities. The system was progressively dismantled from the 1960s onward as car culture prevailed, leaving only the three heritage routes that remain today. The surviving trams are some of the oldest rolling stock in regular operation anywhere in Europe.
---

Porto's historic tram routes are among the few surviving examples of functioning urban heritage tramways in Europe — not a tourist novelty operating on a fixed circuit, but actual old trams on actual old tracks running between useful points in the city. Three routes survive: Line 1 along the Douro waterfront from the Infante stop to Passeio Alegre in Foz, Line 18 from Carmo to Massarelos, and Line 22 through the upper city.

The trams themselves are beautiful — yellow wooden cars dating from the early 20th century, with polished brass fittings and wooden slatted seats. They are slow, they are crowded in summer, and they represent the best way to travel the Douro waterfront between the Ribeira and Foz in either direction. Line 1 is the most scenic, running directly along the river for most of its length. Tickets are bought from the driver; the same Andante card used for the metro is not valid on the historic trams, which require a separate heritage ticket.
