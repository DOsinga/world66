The small 18th-century fortress at the south end of the harbour is Póvoa's most distinctive monument. Begun in 1701 and finished around 1740 during the reign of João V, it is a textbook example of the small star-shaped coastal forts that defended Portugal's Atlantic harbours from privateers.

The exterior walls are open to public viewing year-round; the interior houses a small museum and is open with limited hours. The site is most striking at sunset, when the granite walls catch the light over the boats in the harbour.
