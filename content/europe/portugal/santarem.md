Santarém sits on a bluff above the Tagus river in central [Portugal](/europe/portugal), about an hour's drive northeast of [Lisbon](/europe/portugal/lisbon). It was once one of the most important cities in the country — a Moorish stronghold taken by Afonso Henriques in 1147, and later a favoured residence of medieval Portuguese kings. The city still has a remarkable concentration of Gothic churches for its size.

The viewpoint at the [Portas do Sol](/europe/portugal/santarem/portas_do_sol) garden, on the site of the old Moorish citadel, offers sweeping views over the Tagus plain — one of the best panoramas in central Portugal. Below, the Ribatejo flatlands are cattle country, and Santarém is the unofficial capital of Portuguese bullfighting. The [Feira Nacional de Agricultura](/europe/portugal/santarem/feira_nacional_agricultura) in June is one of the largest agricultural fairs in Europe.

The old town is dense with Gothic architecture. The [Igreja da Graça](/europe/portugal/santarem/igreja_da_graca) has an extraordinary stone rose window and the tomb of Pedro Álvares Cabral, discoverer of Brazil. The [Igreja de Santa Maria de Marvila](/europe/portugal/santarem/marvila_church) is covered floor-to-ceiling with exceptional 17th-century azulejo tiles. The [Igreja de São João de Alporão](/europe/portugal/santarem/sao_joao_de_alporao) is now an archaeology museum with one of the most unusual tombs in Portugal. Santarém is also known for its pastry shops — try the queijadas and the celestes.
