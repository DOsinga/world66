Santiago do Cacém is a small Alentejo hill town that looks modest at first, then starts to justify a day once you climb toward the castle and realise how much history is packed around it. The old centre is a tangle of white streets below medieval walls, with wide views west toward the plains and the Atlantic coast.

The real reason to stop is [Miróbriga](/europe/portugal/santiago_do_cacem/mirobriga), one of the better Roman sites in Portugal, just outside town. Add the [castle](/europe/portugal/santiago_do_cacem/castle_of_santiago_do_cacem), the parish church, and the Santo André lagoon country, and Santiago becomes more than a lunch break between Lisbon and the Algarve. It is still quiet, sometimes sleepy, but it has enough substance for travellers who like ruins, old streets, and the dry light of the Alentejo.

This is not a place to oversell. Come with a car, keep expectations grounded, and Santiago do Cacém makes a good one-day inland counterpoint to the beaches and resort towns of the Alentejo coast.
