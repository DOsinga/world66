Sesimbra is a fishing town on the south side of the Serra da Arrábida, about an hour south of [Lisbon](/europe/portugal/lisbon). For most of its history it lived off the Atlantic — the harbour still launches the daily fleet at five in the morning and lands swordfish and amberjack at lunchtime — but since the 1960s it has also become the closest proper beach town for Lisboetas, who pour in on summer weekends. Out of season it relaxes back into being a working port with a long curve of beach attached.

The town sits at the end of a half-moon bay, hemmed in to the east and west by limestone cliffs that drop straight into the sea. Above the centre, the moorish [Castle of Sesimbra](/europe/portugal/sesimbra/castle_of_sesimbra) — a long curtain of stone walls on the ridge — gives the classic view down to the harbour, the beach, and the white-and-blue houses tumbling toward the water. On the seafront, the seventeenth-century [Fortress of Saint James](/europe/portugal/sesimbra/fortress_of_saint_james) anchors the eastern end of the promenade; the little [Chapel of Espírito Santo dos Mareantes](/europe/portugal/sesimbra/chapel_of_espirito_santo_dos_mareantes), tucked into a backstreet, has the faded outlines of medieval votive ships scratched into its walls by sailors hoping for safe return.

The beach itself, [Praia do Ouro](/europe/portugal/sesimbra/praia_do_ouro), runs the length of the town — sheltered, south-facing, and the warmest swimming on the Lisbon coast. From the western end, a coast road climbs out to Cabo Espichel, a wind-scoured headland with a baroque pilgrimage sanctuary and a lighthouse perched on cliffs that drop 130 metres straight to the Atlantic. The dinosaur footprints at the foot of the cliff are visible at low tide.

The eating is what you would hope — grilled fish in the simplest possible preparation, served in tiled tascas along the back streets. The local speciality is choco frito, deep-fried strips of cuttlefish. Two nights covers the town and Cabo Espichel comfortably; combined with [Setúbal](/europe/portugal/setubal) across the Arrábida, it is a satisfying weekend.
