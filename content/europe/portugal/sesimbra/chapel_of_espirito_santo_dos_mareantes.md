The Chapel of the Holy Spirit of Mariners — Capela do Espírito Santo dos Mareantes — is a small, plain, easy-to-miss chapel a couple of streets back from the harbour. It is part of what was once a medieval hospital and confraternity for the town's sailors, founded in the fourteenth century. The hospital is long gone; the chapel survives.

The exterior is unremarkable. The interior is whitewashed and simple — a single nave, a Mannerist altarpiece, a few painted azulejos. The real interest is the walls: scratched into the plaster behind the altar, on the columns, and around the doorways are dozens of faint outlines of ships from the fifteenth, sixteenth, and seventeenth centuries — caravels, naus, galleons — carved by sailors hoping that the Holy Spirit would bring them back from the next voyage. Some did not. The chapel is normally open only on Sunday mornings; the parish office across the square will open it on request.
