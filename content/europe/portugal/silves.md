Silves is the historic capital of the Algarve, sitting on a ridge above the River Arade in the hills inland from the coast. Before the Portuguese reconquest in the 13th century, it was Xelb — an important Moorish city of some 30,000 people, larger than Lisbon at the time. That history is visible in the red sandstone castle that dominates the town and in the narrow streets below it.

The castle and the Gothic Sé (cathedral) beside it are the main draws. The Museu Arqueológico on Rua das Portas de Loulé displays the full span of the town's history, with the highlight being an Arab water cistern left in situ at the centre of the museum. Below the old town, the River Arade is still navigable from Portimão — a few visitors arrive by boat.

Silves is quieter and more atmospheric than the coastal resorts, and rewards a half-day stopover from the Algarve beaches. It makes a good contrast to Faro and Lagos. The Café Inglês, at the foot of the castle walls, is the social centre of town on Sunday afternoons when local bands set up in the courtyard.
