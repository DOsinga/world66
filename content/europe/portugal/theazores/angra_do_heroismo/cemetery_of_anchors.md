The Bay of Angra looks calm and well-sheltered, and that was exactly the problem. Angra was the main restocking and repair point for the Atlantic fleets — Spanish, Portuguese, Dutch, English — and ships would anchor in the bay during the westerlies that could blow for weeks. When storms intensified, ships dragged their anchors and were lost on the harbor rocks. Over five centuries, the accumulation became extraordinary: hundreds of anchors, cannon, trade goods, and ship timbers now lie on the relatively shallow seafloor of the bay.

The underwater site is protected as a UNESCO World Heritage component, and diving on it requires going with a licensed local operator who knows the regulations. Several dive companies in Angra offer guided dives to the main anchor fields, which lie in 10-20 meters of water and are accessible to sport divers with standard open-water certification. The experience of swimming over a field of 16th and 17th-century anchors, each one representing a ship in distress, is genuinely affecting. The visibility in the bay is generally good and the water is warmer than the open Atlantic — Angra is one of the better dive destinations in the Azores even apart from the archaeological interest.
