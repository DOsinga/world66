The Forte de São João Baptista is the most substantial military construction in the Azores — a full star-fort occupying the base of Monte Brasil, the volcanic peninsula that shelters Angra's harbor. The scale is impressive: this was built by a government that understood, after several painful lessons, that Atlantic control required permanent, expensive stone. The fortifications took most of the 17th century to complete in their final form and were regularly updated as military technology changed.

The fort remained in military use until 1998, which is both a testament to its continued utility and the reason its interior is somewhat restricted — the Portuguese military only recently vacated, and public access to various sections is still being worked out. The exterior walls, bastions, and the approach along the Monte Brasil promontory are fully accessible and give a clear picture of the defensive logic: the fort commands the harbor entrance from a position that would have been extraordinarily difficult to attack by sea. Bring water and comfortable shoes if you're walking the outer perimeter — it's a longer circuit than it appears on the map.
