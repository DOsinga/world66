The Hermitage of Nossa Senhora da Conceição is a small Renaissance chapel on the slope of the convent hill, built in the mid-sixteenth century and designated a national monument in 1910. It sits within the walls of the Convent of Christ complex but slightly apart from the main buildings, tucked into the hillside vegetation with a forecourt and terrace that look out over the river valley. Architecturally it is a refined contrast to the exuberance of the main convent — pure Renaissance geometry, with Doric columns and a restrained classical interior.

The chapel was built under the reign of João III, the same king who commissioned the great Renaissance cloister of the main convent, and it shares that cloister's preference for disciplined proportion over decorative excess. The barrel-vaulted interior has a simple altar and is decorated with carved reliefs. It is usually open to visitors entering the main convent complex, included in the general admission.

Worth a deliberate stop rather than a passing glance — it is easy to walk past it on the way to the more dramatic sections of the convent, but the building itself is architecturally significant and its terrace is one of the quieter spots in the complex.
