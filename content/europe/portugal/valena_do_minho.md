Valença do Minho sits on the northern border of [Portugal](/europe/portugal), perched above the Minho River with [Spain](/europe/spain) visible on the opposite bank. The town is dominated by its massive [double-walled fortress](/europe/portugal/valena_do_minho/fortress), built in the 17th century to defend against Spanish incursions. The fortifications are remarkably intact — you can walk the full circuit of the walls, with views over the river, the Eiffel-designed railway bridge, and the Spanish town of [Tui](/europe/portugal/valena_do_minho/tui_day_trip) across the water.

Inside the fortress walls, the old town has been colonised by shops selling linen, towels, and household goods, which draw busloads of Spanish day-trippers looking for bargains. The commercial bustle can feel at odds with the medieval setting, but if you venture past the shops you will find quiet corners, a Romanesque church, and some excellent views from the ramparts.

Beyond the fortress, there is not a great deal to detain you in Valença itself. The town makes a natural stop if you are travelling the Minho valley — from here you can continue upriver to Monção or Melgaço, both of which have more character as destinations. The railway bridge connects directly to Tui, making a cross-border day trip easy on foot.
