Viana do Castelo sits where the Rio Lima meets the Atlantic, a handsome northern Portuguese city with medieval bones and a dramatic hilltop church watching over it all. Founded in 1258, it grew wealthy on cod fishing and colonial trade, and the old centre still shows it — ornate Manueline facades, wrought-iron balconies, and a compact web of streets that reward aimless wandering.

The city's crown jewel is the [Basilica of Santa Luzia](/europe/portugal/vianadocastelo/basilica_santa_luzia), perched on the hill above town. You can take the funicular up or walk the steep path through the pines. The views from the top — across the city, the river estuary, and the coast — are among the finest in Portugal. At the summit you will also find the ruins of the [Citânia de Santa Luzia](/europe/portugal/vianadocastelo/citania_santa_luzia), a pre-Roman Iron Age hillfort whose circular stone foundations are still clearly legible.

Down in the centre, the [Praça da República](/europe/portugal/vianadocastelo/praca_republica) anchors everything. The [15th-century cathedral](/europe/portugal/vianadocastelo/cathedral), the Renaissance [Igreja da Misericórdia](/europe/portugal/vianadocastelo/misericordia_church), and the old town hall cluster around this square and the streets radiating from it. On the waterfront, the hospital ship [Gil Eannes](/europe/portugal/vianadocastelo/gil_eannes) — once the floating clinic for Portugal's Grand Banks cod fishermen — is now a museum and hostel. A short walk south, the [Ponte Eiffel](/europe/portugal/vianadocastelo/ponte_eiffel) crosses the Lima on iron lattice girders designed by Gustave Eiffel's company in 1878. Viana is small enough to cover on foot in a day, but the food and the setting tend to make people stay longer than they planned.
