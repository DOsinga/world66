The Basilica of Santa Luzia dominates Viana do Castelo from its hilltop position above the city, visible from the coast for miles. The neo-Byzantine structure — all domes, white marble, and mosaic work — was built in the early 20th century on the site of a medieval chapel, and the effect from below is theatrical: a white crown floating above the forested hill.

You can reach it by the vintage funicular railway from the edge of the old town, a gentle ride through pine trees that has been running since the 1930s. From the forecourt the view is already exceptional, but the real reward is climbing the narrow spiral staircase inside to the dome's exterior walkway: the panorama from there — across the river estuary, the bridges, the old centre below, and the Atlantic — is among the finest urban viewpoints in northern Portugal. The interior is richly decorated with azulejo panels depicting the life of Christ.

Just behind the basilica lies the Citânia de Santa Luzia, the ruins of a pre-Roman Iron Age settlement where you can walk among the circular stone foundations of houses dating back more than 2,000 years.
