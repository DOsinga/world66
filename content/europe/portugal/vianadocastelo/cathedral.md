The Cathedral of St. Mary the Great — Sé de Viana do Castelo — anchors the Praça da República and has been the religious centre of the city since the 15th century. The facade blends Gothic solidity with early Manueline decoration, and the rose window above the entrance is a fine example of the distinctly Portuguese late-Gothic style that emerged at the height of the Age of Discoveries.

Inside, the nave is austere by Iberian standards, with the main decoration concentrated in the side chapels. The sacristy holds some good 18th-century azulejo panels depicting biblical scenes. The cathedral stands just steps from the Misericórdia church and the old town hall, making this corner of the Praça da República the densest concentration of historic architecture in northern Portugal outside Braga.
