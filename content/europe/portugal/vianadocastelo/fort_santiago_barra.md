Fort Santiago da Barra occupies the tip of the headland where the Lima River meets the Atlantic, guarding the approach to the port of Viana do Castelo. The fortress was built in the late 16th century and expanded over subsequent centuries, and its star-shaped bastions, thick stone walls, and sea-facing cannon emplacements make it one of the better-preserved examples of early-modern coastal fortification in northern Portugal.

The fort is still used by the Portuguese Navy and public access is limited, but the exterior — the walls, the main gate, and the view of the river mouth — is visible from the riverside promenade just south of the centre. At low tide you can walk along the beach below the walls and get a sense of the position's natural strength. The fort is best seen as part of a walk along the Lima waterfront south from the city centre.
