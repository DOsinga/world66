The Igreja da Misericórdia sits on the corner of the Praça da República and is considered one of the most accomplished Renaissance buildings in Portugal. The lower facade consists of a three-arched loggia carried on carved stone figures — a treatment rare in Portuguese architecture and striking enough to draw architectural pilgrims from across the country.

The interior is lined almost entirely with 18th-century azulejo panels, blue-and-white tiles depicting scenes of charity and mercy that seem to glow against the white walls. The combination of the austere Renaissance exterior and the exuberant tile-covered interior is a characteristic Portuguese contrast. Entry is usually possible during church hours; a small donation is customary.
