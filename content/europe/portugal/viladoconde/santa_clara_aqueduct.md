The Aqueduct of Santa Clara is the second largest aqueduct system in Portugal, after Lisbon's famous Águas Livres. Built in the 18th century to supply the Monastery of Santa Clara, its 999 arches run across the landscape on the northern edge of Vila do Conde in a long, elegant procession that is one of the most striking pieces of civil engineering in northern Portugal.

The best views are from the road and path below, where the arches frame the monastery beyond. The aqueduct is freely accessible and makes a good anchor for a walk through the upper town. The sight of it cutting across the landscape on a clear day — the clean lines of the arches, the moss on the stone, the monastery at the far end — is one of those views that stays with you.
