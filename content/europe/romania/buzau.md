Buzău is the principal city of Buzău County, sitting at the foot of the Carpathian foothills about 130 km north-east of Bucharest, where the plains meet the mountains. It is an old city — documents from the 15th century mention it — but most of what you see today is modern, the old centre having been largely rebuilt in the Soviet era. The city itself is unremarkable; the reason to come is what lies outside it.

About 25 km north, near the village of Berca, the [Berca Mud Volcanoes](/europe/romania/buzau/berca_mud_volcanoes) are one of the most genuinely strange landscapes in Romania. Natural gas forces cold brine and grey clay up through hundreds of small vents, creating a field of bubbling cones on a barren, salt-encrusted plain. Nothing grows around the craters, and the effect is genuinely lunar. The reserve became part of the Buzău Land UNESCO Global Geopark in 2022.

In the city, [Crâng Park](/europe/romania/buzau/crang_park) is the main green space — a large forest park on the western edge with a boating lake and the [Buzău County Museum](/europe/romania/buzau/buzau_county_museum) at its entrance, housed in a fine 1930s neo-Romanian building. The [Vergu-Mănăilă House](/europe/romania/buzau/vergu_manaila_house) on Războieni Street is the oldest surviving building in town, an 18th-century boyar's mansion that now holds the ethnography collection. The [Cathedral of the Ascension](/europe/romania/buzau/ascension_cathedral), consecrated in 2009, is the dominant Orthodox church.

East of the city, the Buzău portion of the Dealu Mare wine region produces good whites — Tămâioasă Românească and Fetească Regală — from the Pietroasele estates, worth visiting if you have a car and an afternoon to spare.
