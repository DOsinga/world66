Constanța is Romania's main port and the gateway to the Black Sea coast. Founded in the 6th century BC by Greek merchants as **Tomis**, it was later developed by the Romans and renamed after Emperor Constantine. The poet **Ovid** was exiled here by Augustus in AD 8 and spent his last years writing elegies about the cold and the barbarians — [his statue](/europe/romania/constanta/ovid_square) still stands in the main square.

The city has a faded grandeur that rewards exploration. The Art Nouveau [Casino](/europe/romania/constanta/casino) on the waterfront, overlooking the sea, is Constanța's most iconic building, recently restored after decades of dereliction. The old town has Roman mosaics, an [archaeological museum](/europe/romania/constanta/history_museum), the [Grand Mosque](/europe/romania/constanta/grand_mosque) with its panoramic minaret, and the Orthodox [Cathedral of Saints Peter and Paul](/europe/romania/constanta/cathedral). The [Mamaia](/europe/romania/constanta/mamaia) resort strip stretches north of the city along a narrow sand bar between the sea and a lagoon — it is Romania's most popular seaside destination, busy in summer with Romanian and international tourists.
