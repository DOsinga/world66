title: "Books"
type: section
---

**Ovid, *Tristia* and *Epistulae ex Ponto* (AD 9–17).** The most famous literature ever written about Constanța. Ovid was exiled to Tomis by Augustus and produced nine years of beautifully self-pitying elegies about the cold, the language, the barbarians and the absence of Rome. Any modern English translation (the Peter Green volume is excellent) gives a vivid if jaundiced sense of the city in the first century AD.

**David Malouf, *An Imaginary Life* (1978).** Australian novelist Malouf wrote a short, lyrical novel based on Ovid's exile at Tomis. The poet befriends a feral child and slowly loses his Roman self in the steppe. A different, deeper way into the same period.

**Olivia Manning, *The Balkan Trilogy* (1960–65).** Not set in Constanța specifically, but Manning's novels of inter-war Bucharest give an extraordinary picture of Romania in the months before the German invasion, and several scenes drift to the coast. A masterpiece.

**Patrick Leigh Fermor, *Between the Woods and the Water* (1986).** The second volume of Leigh Fermor's account of his 1933 walk from the Hook of Holland to Constantinople includes a slow passage through the Romanian plains, with one of the best descriptions in English of Dobruja's lost multi-ethnic culture.

**Mihail Sadoveanu, *The Hatchet (Baltagul)* (1930).** Not coastal — this short Moldavian novel is set in the Carpathian foothills — but it is the closest you get to the texture of inter-war Romanian rural life and is essential background reading.
