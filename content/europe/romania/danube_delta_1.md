The Danube Delta is where Europe's second-longest river finally unravels into the Black Sea, splitting into three main arms — Chilia, Sulina, and Sfântu Gheorghe — and a tangle of smaller channels, lakes, and reed beds in between. At 580,000 hectares it is the continent's largest and best-preserved wetland, and most of it is protected as a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve. Over 5,500 species of plants and animals live here, including the largest population of white pelicans in Europe and a long roll-call of herons, cormorants, kingfishers, ibis, and migrating raptors. For many travellers the delta is the single reason to come to this corner of Romania.

[Tulcea](/europe/romania/tulcea) is the practical gateway: boats to the channels and villages leave from its river station, and almost everyone heading into the delta passes through the city first. From there you can do anything from a half-day pootle into the nearest side-channels to a multi-day liveaboard cruise that reaches the wilder edges of the reserve. Shared scheduled boats are cheap but slow; private speedboats are faster and let you push further out to the Letea forest and the Caraorman dunes. The classic full-day itinerary winds off the main Sulina arm through reed forests, water-lily lakes, and willow swamps, and a competent guide will pull up alongside a feeding raft of Dalmatian pelicans within an hour of leaving the dock.

The delta is also a place where people live, not just a nature reserve. Scattered fishing villages — Mila 23, Crișan, Sulina, Sfântu Gheorghe — are reachable only by water, and many travellers base themselves in a guesthouse there for a few days to cycle, kayak, fish, and catch the dawn chorus when the birdlife is at its most active. Sulina, at the very mouth of the river, has the faded grandeur of a once-cosmopolitan free port, complete with a multi-faith cemetery and a lighthouse that now stands oddly inland.

Late spring through early autumn is the best window, when the channels are alive with birds and the weather is reliable. Bring binoculars, sunblock, a hat, and insect repellent — the mosquitoes can be ferocious in the reeds. Basically the only way to get around the delta is by boat, so build your visit around the boat schedules and the village you choose as a base.
