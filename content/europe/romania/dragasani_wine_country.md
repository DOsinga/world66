Drăgășani, about 50km south of Râmnicu Vâlcea along the Olt Valley, is one of Romania's oldest and most distinctive wine regions — its vineyards documented since the 14th century and its wines praised by Ottoman chroniclers in the 17th. The region produces whites from native varieties that are largely unknown outside Romania: Crâmpoșie Selecționată, with its clean minerality; Tămâioasă Românească, an aromatic, sometimes lightly sweet wine from the Muscat family; and Fetească Regală, a delicate white with a long finish. These are not international varieties marketed for export — they are wines that taste of this particular stretch of river valley and reward an afternoon's exploration.

The vineyards cover the rolling hills on both banks of the Olt, and driving through them in late summer, when the vines are heavy and the light is golden, is one of the pleasures of southern Romania. A handful of estates — Avincis, Stirbey, and Prince Stirbey among them — have opened their cellars to visitors for tastings; call ahead or check their websites, as opening hours are informal. The town of Drăgășani itself has a small wine museum in the centre and a pedestrian street that shows off the modest prosperity the trade has brought to the area.

A half-day from Vâlcea is enough to drive down, visit a winery, and eat lunch at one of the roadside restaurants. Combine the return trip with the late-afternoon light on the vine-covered hills for the best of the scenery. The road south from Râmnicu Vâlcea is fast and straightforward.
