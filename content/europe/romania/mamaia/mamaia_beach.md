  Mamaia was developed as a resort during the Communist era, when the Romanian state built hotels along the coast to cater to workers' holidays. Many of the larger concrete blocks along the beachfront date from the 1960s and 1970s. Since 1990, private investment has transformed the promenade, but the resort layout — dense buildings on a sliver of sand — still reflects its planned origins.
---

The beach at Mamaia stretches for seven kilometres along the Black Sea coast, separated from Lake Siutghiol on the west side by a strip of land rarely more than a few hundred metres wide. The sand is fine and golden, the sea is generally shallow for a considerable distance from the shore, and the water temperature in summer reaches 25–27°C — warm enough for children to play in all day. The beach is divided into sectors, some private (with sunbeds and umbrellas for hire), some free.

The resort is busiest in July and August when Romanians take their main summer holidays, and the beach fills up considerably. May, June, and September offer the best combination of good weather and manageable crowds. The northern end of the beach, away from the main hotel strip, is quieter and less developed.
