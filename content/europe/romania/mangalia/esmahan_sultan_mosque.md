The Esmahan Sultan Mosque, built in 1590, is the oldest working mosque in Romania and one of the few surviving pieces of Ottoman architecture on the Romanian Black Sea coast. The building is a simple single-domed structure with a wooden portico, set in a small garden with an old cemetery. It is modest but genuine — it has been in continuous use, served the local Muslim (mainly Turkish and Tatar) community throughout the communist period, and continues as an active place of worship.

The mosque can be visited outside prayer times; the caretaker is generally on site in the morning. The cemetery beside it holds Ottoman-era tombstones with carved decorations. The building is a five-minute walk from the beach and the town centre, and most visitors spend 20–30 minutes here.
