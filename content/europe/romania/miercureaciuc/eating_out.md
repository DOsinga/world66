---
title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

Food in Miercurea Ciuc is firmly Székely, which means closer to Hungarian than to Romanian cooking: heavy on meat, paprika, sour cream, and stuffed cabbage. The cooking is hearty rather than refined; portions are generous.

**San Gennaro** near the centre is the long-running favourite for Italian — wood-fired pizza, decent pasta, a quiet courtyard in summer.

**Borsika Étterem** does proper Székely cuisine: paprika-rich pork stews (pörkölt), stuffed cabbage rolls (töltött káposzta), and the obligatory chimney cake (kürtős kalács) for dessert. Atmosphere is folksy in a sincere way.

**Csíki Söröző**, a brewpub on Piața Libertății, pours Csíki Sör on tap with grilled meats, pretzels, and salads. The terrace in summer is one of the better evening spots in town.

**Két Szarvas** does upscale modern takes on Hungarian and central-European cooking — game dishes, duck, lamb with sour cherries — in a more polished dining room. Reservations advised in season.

For something light, the bakeries around the central square sell warm kürtős kalács and pogácsa savoury cheese scones for almost nothing — the best snack between visits.
