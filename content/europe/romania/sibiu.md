Sibiu (Hermannstadt in German) is one of the best-preserved medieval cities in Central Europe, sitting in the foothills of the Southern Carpathians in Transylvania. It was founded by Saxon colonists in the 12th century, grew into a powerful trading centre, and defended itself successfully against repeated Ottoman attacks in the 15th century with [walls, bastions, and towers](/europe/romania/sibiu/city_walls) that still ring the old town.

The old centre has three interlocking squares — [Piata Mare (the Grand Square)](/europe/romania/sibiu/piata_mare), [Piata Mica (the Small Square)](/europe/romania/sibiu/piata_mica), and Huet Square — linked by covered passages and surrounded by colourful townhouses, many dating from the 16th and 17th centuries. The characteristic "eye" dormers on the rooftops give the houses a watchful, slightly melancholy look that is unique to Sibiu. The [Brukenthal National Museum](/europe/romania/sibiu/brukenthal_national_museum) on the Grand Square holds one of Romania's most important art collections, housed in a fine Baroque palace. The 15th-century [Evangelical Cathedral](/europe/romania/sibiu/evangelical_cathedral) towers over Huet Square; climb the tower for views over the tile rooftops.

The medieval [Stairs Passage](/europe/romania/sibiu/stairs_passage) and the [Council Tower](/europe/romania/sibiu/tower_of_the_council) connect the two main squares. The [ASTRA Open-Air Museum](/europe/romania/sibiu/astra_open_air_museum) in the Dumbrava forest outside town is one of the finest ethnographic museums in Europe. The [Pharmacy Museum](/europe/romania/sibiu/pharmacy_museum) in its original 1600 premises is a curiosity worth seeking out.

Sibiu was named a European Capital of Culture in 2007, which helped revive its restaurant and cultural scene — there are now good cafes and restaurants around the old city. The weekly food market and the traditional Christmas market in December are both worth timing a visit around.
