The Gothic Evangelical Cathedral on Huet Square is the dominant building in Sibiu's skyline, its squat stone tower rising above the red tile rooftops. Built in the 15th and 16th centuries on the site of an earlier Romanesque church, it is the largest Lutheran church in Romania and the spiritual centre of the Transylvanian Saxon community for centuries.

The interior is impressive in the unadorned way of Lutheran churches — whitewashed walls, stone floors, and a vast 16th-century organ that is still in use for concerts. The tomb of Mihnea the Bad, a Wallachian prince assassinated at the cathedral door in 1510, is on display. Climb the tower for the best views in the city, looking down over the "eyes" of the rooftop dormers and across the surrounding hills.
