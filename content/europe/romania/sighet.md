Sighetu Marmației — usually just Sighet — sits in the far north of Romania, pressed against the Ukrainian border in the heart of Maramureș. This is one of the most culturally distinct regions in Europe: wooden churches with impossibly tall steeples, carved gates, haystacks built by hand, and a rural way of life that has barely changed in centuries.

The town itself is modest, but it holds one of Romania's most important museums. The [Memorial of the Victims of Communism](/europe/romania/sighet/memorial_to_victims_of_communism), housed in the former political prison where dozens of Romanian intellectuals and political leaders were tortured and killed, is a harrowing and essential visit. Sighet is also the birthplace of Nobel laureate Elie Wiesel, and his [childhood home](/europe/romania/sighet/elie_wiesel_house) is now a museum. Beyond the town, the Maramureș countryside — the wooden churches of [Bârsana](/europe/romania/sighet/barsana_monastery) and Budești, the [Merry Cemetery of Săpânța](/europe/romania/sapinta), the [Iza Valley](/europe/romania/sighet/iza_river) villages — is among the most rewarding in Eastern Europe.
