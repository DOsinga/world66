  Suceava's Armenian community was one of the largest in Romania from the 15th century onward, drawn by the Moldavian princes' tolerance and the city's role as a trading hub on routes between East and West. The monastery's defensive walls date from the 1600s, when it was fortified against Ottoman raids. The community largely dispersed in the 19th century, but the monastery still stands as evidence of how cosmopolitan medieval Moldavia was.
---

A monastery founded by Suceava's Armenian community in the 15th century, on a hill on the western edge of the city. The stone church and surrounding fortified walls survive largely intact. The Armenians were a significant presence in Moldavia for several centuries — merchants who settled along the trade routes — and this monastery is one of the most tangible reminders of that history. The setting is quiet and slightly overgrown, with views over the city.
