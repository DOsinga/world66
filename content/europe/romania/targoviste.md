Târgoviște was the capital of Wallachia from the thirteenth to the seventeenth century, when the princes of this Romanian principality held court here before moving to Bucharest. Today it is a mid-sized city about 80 kilometres northwest of Bucharest, and its history gives it more layers of interest than most Romanian towns of similar size.

The main sight is the [Curtea Domnească](/europe/romania/targoviste/princely_court) (Princely Court), the ruined palace complex that was home to Wallachian rulers including Vlad the Impaler — the historical figure who inspired Bram Stoker's Dracula. The [Chindia Tower](/europe/romania/targoviste/chindia_tower) (Turnul Chindiei), a fifteenth-century watchtower built by Vlad himself, still stands at the heart of the complex and can be climbed for views over the city. Outside the walls, the [Dealu Monastery](/europe/romania/targoviste/dealu_monastery) a few kilometres away preserves the severed head of Mihai Viteazul (Michael the Brave), the prince who briefly unified the three Romanian principalities in 1600.

Târgoviște also has a grim footnote in twentieth-century history: it was here, in a military barracks on the outskirts of the city, that Nicolae Ceaușescu and his wife Elena were tried and executed on Christmas Day 1989. The [barracks where this happened](/europe/romania/targoviste/ceausescu_execution_site) is now a small museum.

The city is an easy day trip from Bucharest or a stopping point on the way to the Prahova Valley and the Carpathian mountains.
