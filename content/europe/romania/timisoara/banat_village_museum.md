The Banat Village Museum is the largest open-air ethnographic museum in western Romania, occupying around 17 hectares of woodland on the northern edge of the city near the Green Forest. It assembles around 30 vernacular buildings — houses, churches, mills, blacksmiths' shops, granaries — relocated and reconstructed from villages across the multi-ethnic Banat region.

The exhibits cover the Romanian, German (Banat Swabian), Hungarian, Serbian, Bulgarian and Slovak rural cultures that historically shared the Banat plain. Buildings include several entire timber Orthodox churches, a 1796 mill, and a Swabian farmstead with original tools and furnishings. The site is laid out as a notional village; the walk between buildings takes you through quiet woodland. Allow two to three hours. Tram lines 5 and 9 run nearby.
