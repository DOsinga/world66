The Lloyd Palace is the most photographed of the Baroque palaces on Victory Square — a four-storey Eclectic building with an ornate stucco facade in faded pink-and-cream, completed in 1912 for the Lloyd Bank. The ground floor is a long colonnaded arcade with cafe seating that extends most of the length of the square's western side.

Cafe Lloyd at the southern end of the arcade has been a meeting place for the city's intellectuals since the building opened. Upstairs the original mosaic-tiled stair lobby and several decorated reception rooms are preserved; access depends on what is currently operating in the building. The palace anchors the long Eclectic ensemble along Victory Square together with the Dauerbach Palace and the Palace of the Chamber of Commerce.
