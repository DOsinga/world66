The Loffler Palace is a small but conspicuous Secession-Eclectic palace on Victory Square, completed in 1912 and named after its original owner, the engineer Max Loffler. It is one of a series of four palaces along the western side of the square that together form one of the most coherent stretches of Habsburg-era commercial architecture in eastern Europe.

The facade decoration is unusually delicate — coloured ceramic medallions, wrought-iron balcony rails, and a small cupola at the corner. The ground floor housed a long-running pharmacy through the 20th century. The building's significance lies less in any single feature and more in the way it completes the rhythm of Victory Square's western side together with the Lloyd, Weiss and Hilt-Vogel palaces.
