title: "Things to Do"
type: section
---

Târgu Jiu's reason for existing, touristically speaking, is the sculptural ensemble that Constantin Brâncuși created here in 1937–38. The three works are arranged along a single east–west axis running through the city, from the Jiu River to the public park at the far end — a walk of about 1.3 kilometres that is one of the most significant art experiences in Romania.

Start at the western end, at the river, with the [Table of Silence](/europe/romania/tirgujiu/table_of_silence). Move east along the Alley of Chairs — 30 limestone seats arranged in pairs along the avenue — to the [Gate of the Kiss](/europe/romania/tirgujiu/gate_of_the_kiss) in the park. The [Endless Column](/europe/romania/tirgujiu/endless_column) stands at the eastern extremity, 29 metres of cast-iron rhomboid modules rising against the sky.

The [Gorj County Museum](/europe/romania/tirgujiu/gorj_county_museum) in the city centre covers the natural and human history of the region, with good archaeological collections from the Dacian period. The [Church of the Holy Archangels](/europe/romania/tirgujiu/church_of_the_holy_archangels) on Piața Victoriei, a short walk from the ensemble, is one of the finest examples of Romanian Orthodox church architecture in the area.

For Brâncuși enthusiasts, the village of Hobița — 24 kilometres south of Târgu Jiu — is the sculptor's actual birthplace. His memorial house is open for visits and gives context to the Târgu Jiu ensemble.
