Tirgu Mures sits in the heart of Transylvania, in north-central Romania, where the Mures River cuts through a broad valley ringed by gentle hills. With a population of around 150,000, it is one of the larger cities of Transylvania and one of the few in Romania with a substantial Hungarian-speaking community — roughly half the city identifies as ethnic Hungarian, and both Romanian and Hungarian are official languages here. That bilingual character shapes everything from street signs to theater programs to the way people greet strangers.

The old city center is compact and walkable. The 15th-century **Fortress and Protestant Church** — built within the same medieval walls — is the oldest structure in the city and the most striking example of the mixed Romanian-Hungarian architectural tradition. The adjacent **Teleki-Bolyai Library** holds a remarkable collection of rare books and manuscripts and doubles as a cultural center. The **National Theater** stages performances in both languages, and the city has its own Philharmonic Orchestra.

Tirgu Mures is well positioned for exploring Transylvania. [Sighisoara](/europe/romania/sighisoara), one of the best-preserved medieval citadels in Europe, is less than 60 km to the southeast. The Dracula's Castle at Bran is a few hours by road. Locally, the Peninsula Festival in summer brings rock, pop, and electronic acts to an outdoor site near the city and is one of the largest music events in the region. The climate is continental — cold winters, warm summers — and the autumn, when the hills around the city turn gold, is a particularly fine time to visit.
