The building on Corneliu Coposu Street in Sighetu Marmației looks unremarkable from outside — a grey stone block, compact, severe. Inside it is one of the most unsettling places in Romania. This was the prison where the communist regime sent the people it most wanted to erase: former cabinet ministers, bishops, academics, generals, journalists, anybody who had held a position of influence before 1947 and might therefore form the nucleus of resistance. They were not tried publicly. They were simply made to disappear.

The cells are small and the walls are thick. The prisoners were kept in near-total isolation, denied communication, denied adequate food and heat, denied medical care. Dozens died here. The regime chose Sighetu deliberately — it sits hard against the Ukrainian border, far from Bucharest, far from anywhere that might generate embarrassing attention. When the prisoners died, their bodies were buried in unmarked graves outside town.

The memorial opened in 1993 and has been expanded several times since. It is run by the Civic Academy Foundation and does its work carefully: the cells have been restored to show conditions as they were, and each room is accompanied by detailed documentation about who was held there and what happened to them. The courtyard outside, where prisoners took their brief daily exercise, is now called the Courtyard of Lost Steps. Allow two hours minimum. It is not a comfortable visit, but it is an important one.

story: >
  Among those imprisoned here was Iuliu Maniu, the leader of the National Peasants' Party and twice prime minister of Romania. Arrested in 1947, he died in this prison in 1953 aged 80. His jailers refused to tell his family he had died, and his grave was kept secret for decades. He is now considered one of Romania's great democratic martyrs.
