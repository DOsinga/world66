Vâlcea is a county in southern Romania where the Olt river cuts through the Carpathian foothills, creating a valley that has been a trade route and a place of retreat for centuries. The main draw is the painted monasteries of the Olt Valley — a string of Orthodox foundations that rival Bucovina's painted churches, though they are far less visited.

The star is [Horezu Monastery](/europe/romania/valcea/horezu_monastery), a UNESCO World Heritage Site founded in 1690, famous for its Brâncoveanu-style architecture and frescoes of extraordinary delicacy. The pottery tradition of Horezu — bold, geometric designs in white, brown, and terracotta — is alive and well in workshops around the town. Further south, [Cozia Monastery](/europe/romania/valcea/cozia_monastery) sits dramatically in the Olt gorge, its 14th-century church one of the oldest surviving in Wallachia.

The county capital, [Râmnicu Vâlcea](/europe/romania/valcea/ramnicu_valcea), is a practical base without much character of its own, but the surrounding countryside of forested hills, thermal springs at [Călimănești-Căciulata](/europe/romania/valcea/calimanesti_caciulata), and hilltop monasteries rewards slow exploration. The quiet [Bistrița Monastery](/europe/romania/valcea/bistrita_monastery) in the beech forests north of the capital is worth seeking out for those who want to see fewer other tourists.
