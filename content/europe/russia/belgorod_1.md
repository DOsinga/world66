Belgorod is a mid-sized city in southwestern Russia, about 40 km from the Ukrainian border. It sits on the chalk hills above the Seversky Donets River, and the city's name — "white city" — comes from the white chalk outcrops that define the [surrounding landscape](/europe/russia/belgorod_1/chalk_hills). Founded as a frontier fortress in the 16th century, Belgorod was largely destroyed during World War II and rebuilt in Soviet style, so the architecture is mostly functional rather than beautiful.

The city centre has a few points of interest worth the detour. The [Diorama Museum](/europe/russia/belgorod_1/diorama_museum) is one of the most impressive military museums in Russia — its 67-metre panoramic painting of the Battle of Prokhorovka is genuinely spectacular. The [Holy Trinity Cathedral](/europe/russia/belgorod_1/holy_trinity_cathedral), one of the few pre-war buildings to survive, dates from the 19th century and is still in active use. [Sobornaya Square](/europe/russia/belgorod_1/sobornaya_square) is the social heart of the city, and the cafes and restaurants around it benefit from the energy of [Belgorod State University](/europe/russia/belgorod_1/belgorod_state_university)'s 30,000 students.

Belgorod is not a typical tourist destination, but it serves as a base for visiting the Prokhorovka battlefield memorial — about 60 km north — which marks one of the largest tank engagements in history. The surrounding countryside of black earth farmland and chalk valleys has a quiet, open beauty. **Visitors should note** that as of 2024 the city's proximity to the Ukrainian border has made it the site of security incidents; check current travel advisories before planning a trip.
