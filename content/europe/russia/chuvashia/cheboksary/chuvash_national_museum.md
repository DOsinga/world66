  The Chuvash people are one of the larger Turkic ethnic groups in Russia, with a distinct language, script, and decorative tradition. Traditional Chuvash embroidery is perhaps the most recognisable element of that tradition — intricate geometric patterns that encode information about the wearer's status, region, and family. Each design was historically unique, making every garment a kind of identity document in thread.

The Chuvash National Museum is the city's flagship cultural institution and the best place to understand what makes Chuvashia distinct from its Russian surroundings. The collections cover archaeology, ethnography, and the history of the Chuvash people from prehistoric times through the Soviet period and into the present.

The ethnographic rooms are the highlight, with extensive displays of traditional Chuvash costume, jewellery, and embroidery. The geometric patterns of Chuvash needlework are highly distinctive — they bear a resemblance to the decorative traditions of Central Asia and the Middle East, a reminder that the Chuvash are a Turkic people with ancient connections to the Volga Bulgar civilisation. The museum also covers the Soviet transformation of the region, with photographs and documents from the collectivisation period that are thoughtfully rather than hagiographically presented.
