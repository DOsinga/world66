Yekaterinburg is Russia's fourth-largest city and the capital of the Urals, straddling the geographic [boundary between Europe and Asia](/europe/russia/ekaterinoburg/europe_asia_border). A major stop on the Trans-Siberian Railway, it is the place where the last tsar and his family were executed in 1918 — the [Church on Blood](/europe/russia/ekaterinoburg/church_on_blood), built on the site, is the city's most visited landmark. The nearby monastery at [Ganina Yama](/europe/russia/ekaterinoburg/ganina_yama) marks the spot where the bodies were taken afterwards.

The city has a lively cultural scene, with a strong tradition of theatre, music, and contemporary art. The wide central avenues are lined with [constructivist architecture](/europe/russia/ekaterinoburg/dom_pechati) from the 1920s and 1930s, some of the best examples in the country. The [Yeltsin Center](/europe/russia/ekaterinoburg/yeltsin_center), a modern museum dedicated to Russia's first president (a local), is surprisingly engaging regardless of your politics. The [Iset embankment](/europe/russia/ekaterinoburg/iset_river_embankment) is the heart of the centre, with the great green-and-white [Sevastyanov House](/europe/russia/ekaterinoburg/sevastyanov_house) presiding over it.

Yekaterinburg makes a natural base for exploring the Ural Mountains — hiking, skiing, and visits to old mining towns are all within reach.
