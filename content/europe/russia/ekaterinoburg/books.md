**The File on the Tsar** by Anthony Summers and Tom Mangold (1976) — the first serious Western investigation into the death of the Romanovs, arguing controversially that the family might have survived the night of 16/17 July. Outdated by the 1990s DNA evidence, but a vivid evocation of the city and the events.

**The Race to Save the Romanovs** by Helen Rappaport — a brisk and readable history of the final months of the Romanov family, the failed rescue attempts, and the political pressures that led to the killings in the Ipatiev House.

**Midnight in Siberia** by David Greene — a recent traveller's account of the Trans-Siberian, with a chapter on Yekaterinburg that captures the curious mixture of imperial pilgrimage and Soviet hangover that gives the city its character.

**The Last Tsar** by Edvard Radzinsky — a Russian historian's full-length biography of Nicholas II, drawing on Soviet archives opened after 1991. The Yekaterinburg chapters are particularly strong.
