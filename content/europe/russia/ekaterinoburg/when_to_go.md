---
title: When to Go
type: section
---

Yekaterinburg has a brutal continental climate — summers can reach 35°C, winters routinely drop below –30°C — so timing matters.

**June to early September** is the best window: warm days, long evenings (the city is on the edge of the Russian "white nights" zone), and parks at their greenest. **May** can still be cold but the trees are bursting into leaf.

**Mid-December through January** is the time for the Ural Ice Town festival on Ploshchad 1905 Goda and the floodlit Romanov pilgrimage trail around 17 July — though the 17 July anniversary actually falls in midsummer. Winter days are very short but the snow on the Old Believer churches is photogenic, and Russian café and theatre culture is at its strongest.

**March and November** are the worst months — grey, wet, and either side of the deep freeze.
