---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

Irkutsk has a sharp continental climate — Siberian winters are genuine. January temperatures regularly drop to -25°C (-13°F) or colder, and the cold can be striking in its completeness: the Angara, fed by Lake Baikal, doesn't freeze because Baikal's thermal mass keeps the water warm, but the air is biting. The cold is dry, which makes it more bearable than it sounds, and the city carries on regardless.

The best months to visit are June through August, when temperatures are warm (20–25°C), days are long, and Baikal is at its most accessible. Spring and autumn are short and variable. If you want to see the city in the cold — and it is a striking place under snow — February and March are better than January: the worst cold has usually passed, daylight is returning, and the Angara embankment looks extraordinary under ice.
