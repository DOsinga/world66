Izborsk is a small village west of [Pskov](/europe/russia/pskov), close to the Estonian border, but it carries the weight of a much larger place. It was one of the old fortified settlements of northwestern Rus, and the surviving [Izborsk Fortress](/europe/russia/izborsk/izborsk_fortress) still has the blunt, defensive look of a frontier that expected trouble.

The appeal here is not urban life. Izborsk is a half-day of stone walls, sloping meadows, lakeside paths, and stories that mix archaeology with legend. The fortress stands on Zheravya Hill above Gorodishchenskoye Lake; a short walk away, the [Slovene Springs](/europe/russia/izborsk/slovene_springs) pour out of the limestone bank in cold streams that locals have treated as holy for centuries.

Older still is [Truvorovo Gorodishche](/europe/russia/izborsk/truvorovo_gorodishche), the early settlement north of the fortress, where a large stone cross is still attached in popular memory to Truvor, the legendary brother of Rurik. Treat the legend with caution, but the place itself is atmospheric: grass-covered earthworks, a village church, views across the lake, and a strong sense of the western borderlands.

Izborsk works best as a day trip from Pskov, ideally combined with Pechory and the Pskov-Caves Monastery. Give the fortress a couple of hours, then walk down to the springs and out to the old hillfort before heading back.
