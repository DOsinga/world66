The ring of 19th-century forts built to protect Königsberg is one of the most remarkable military engineering relics in Europe — over a dozen heavy brick-and-earthwork fortifications still stand in the forests and suburban areas surrounding the city. Fort No. 5 is the most visited, having been converted into a military history museum dedicated to the April 1945 battle for the city.

The fort is massive: multiple interconnected brick buildings and tunnels set into earthwork embankments, surrounded by a water-filled moat. You can walk through the casemates and corridors, inspect the artillery positions, and understand the engineering logic of this type of late-19th-century fortification. The museum exhibits focus on the 1945 battle, with weapons, uniforms, maps, and personal effects. The fort is about five kilometres north of the city centre and best reached by taxi or car.
