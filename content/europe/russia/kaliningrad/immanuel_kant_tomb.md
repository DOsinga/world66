Immanuel Kant's tomb is attached to the north wall of Königsberg Cathedral, housed in a neoclassical open portico that was added to the medieval building in 1924. The tomb itself is a long stone sarcophagus with a simple inscription in German and Latin. Fresh flowers are regularly placed here by visiting philosophers, students, and admirers — the tomb has a kind of secular pilgrimage quality.

Kant is buried in his birthplace, which happened to be bombed out of existence and renamed by a state whose ideology he would have found philosophically problematic. The contrast between the universal reach of his ideas and the specific, strange fate of the city he never left is one of the more interesting ironies in European intellectual history. The cathedral with its tomb is a natural starting point for any visit to Kaliningrad.
