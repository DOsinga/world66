  The monastery was founded in the 14th century, though its exact origins remain disputed between Russian and Finnish historians. During the Winter War of 1939–40, the Finnish monks evacuated the entire community — icons, bells, and centuries of accumulated liturgical objects — across the frozen lake to Finland, where they established New Valamo in Heinävesi. The Russian monastery was left deserted until Soviet-era restoration began in the 1980s.

The Transfiguration Monastery of Valaam is one of the most important Orthodox monasteries in Russia, a walled complex of churches, workshops, and residences set on a rocky bluff above Lake Ladoga. The main cathedral, rebuilt in the 19th century in the Russian Revival style, dominates the central square with its sky-blue domes and golden crosses. The interior is richly decorated with frescoes and gilded iconostases.

The monastery functions as a working religious community — not a museum — so visitors are expected to dress modestly and keep quiet during services. Morning liturgy, which can last several hours, fills the cathedral with candlelight and choral singing of an intensity that leaves most visitors shaken, regardless of their faith.
