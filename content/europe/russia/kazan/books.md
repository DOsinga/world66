title: "Books"
type: section
---

For Kazan's role in shaping Tatar identity, **Daniel R. Brower's *Russia and Asia: Encountering the Russian Empire*** has the best concise English treatment of the Tatar–Russian encounter and the Kazan Khanate. Travellers interested in the wider Tatar world will find **Azade-Ayse Rorlich's *The Volga Tatars: A Profile in National Resilience*** the standard modern history of how the community survived from the 1552 conquest to the present.

For atmosphere, **Anna Vasilyeva's *Kazan: A Walking Guide*** is a small bilingual book sold at the city tourist office that puts the streets and buildings of the old centre into context better than any general Russia guide. In fiction, **Gabdulla Tukay**, the Tatar national poet, has a slim *Selected Poems* in English translation from the Kazan Academy of Sciences press — at least one of his poems is sold in every Kazan bookshop and the lives of every Tatar schoolchild starts with him.

For a foreign-traveller's view, **Colin Thubron's *Among the Russians*** has a memorable Kazan chapter from his 1980s drive across the Soviet Union — still readable, still recognisable.
