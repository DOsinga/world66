  Tatar legend holds that the treasures of the Kazan Khans were thrown into Lake Kaban as Russian troops besieged the city in 1552, and rest there still. Several Soviet-era expeditions tried to find them; the lake bed was repeatedly dredged in the 1960s. Nothing was ever recovered.

Lake Kaban is the long urban lake that defines the western edge of the Old Tatar Quarter, with a paved promenade running for several kilometres along its banks. The northern end is the most pleasant — cafes, paddleboats in summer, ice skating in winter, and views back toward the Kremlin.

The lake is divided by causeways into three connected basins: Lower, Middle, and Upper Kaban. The Lower Kaban, closest to the centre, is the easiest to walk around in an hour. A drained and replanted promenade runs the full length of the western shore.
