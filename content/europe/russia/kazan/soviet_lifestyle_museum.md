  Founded in 2011 by a local collector who had spent his adult life acquiring stoves, vinyl records, posters, transistor radios and tea sets, the museum is the largest privately assembled collection of late-Soviet domestic life in Russia and the closest thing the country has to a small-scale Smithsonian of the USSR.

The Soviet Lifestyle Museum, in a basement under a courtyard off Bauman Street, is a dense, charmingly chaotic private collection of 1970s and 1980s domestic Soviet objects — radios, televisions, tinned-food packaging, school exercise books, Pioneer scarves, posters, plastic toys, vodka bottles, the whole texture of late-Soviet life.

You can pick up many of the objects, sit at a Soviet desk, and pose with a 1980s record player. The curator usually walks visitors through the highlights in patchy English. Modest entry fee. An hour is plenty; an unexpected highlight for anyone old enough to remember the 1980s.
