Krasnodar is the informal capital of southern Russia, a sprawling city of over a million people on the Kuban River. Founded as a Cossack fort in 1793, it serves as the gateway to the Black Sea coast and the Caucasus mountains.

The city centre is greener than you might expect, with long tree-lined boulevards and [Gorky Park](/europe/russia/krasnodar/gorky_park) stretching along the river. [Krasnaya Street](/europe/russia/krasnodar/krasnaya_street), the pedestrianised main drag, is where locals promenade on warm evenings past 19th-century merchant houses and Soviet-era cinemas. The [Regional History Museum](/europe/russia/krasnodar/regional_history_museum) and the [Kovalenko Art Museum](/europe/russia/krasnodar/krasnodar_art_museum) are both worth a stop. The privately funded [Krasnodar Stadium](/europe/russia/krasnodar/kuban_stadium) and its adjacent botanical garden are an unexpected highlight — one of the finest club grounds in Russia, built by a local billionaire as a civic gift.

Krasnodar's real draw for travellers is its position: the beach resorts of Sochi, Anapa, and Gelendzhik are all within a few hours, and the western Caucasus — with skiing at Krasnaya Polyana and hiking in the Caucasus Biosphere Reserve — is easily reached from here.
