story: Krasnaya Street was laid out in the 1790s when the Cossack fort of Yekaterinodar
  was established on the Kuban River. The Cossack Host of the Black Sea, relocated
  here from the Zaporozhian Sich by Catherine the Great's order, named their main
  street Krasnaya (meaning both "red" and "beautiful" in Russian) in keeping with
  the naming traditions of their Ukrainian homeland. The street has been the social
  centre of the city for over 200 years.

Krasnaya Street is the pedestrianised main boulevard of Krasnodar, running through the heart of the historic centre and lined with 19th-century merchant buildings, Soviet-era cinemas, churches, and modern shops. On warm evenings, the street fills with people promenading between the fountains and cafe terraces — a southern Russian social ritual that has been going on since the 19th century.

The street holds Krasnodar's best concentration of pre-revolutionary architecture: merchant mansions, the city administration building, and the Kuban Regional Art Museum. The monument to Catherine the Great (destroyed in the Soviet period, rebuilt in 2006) stands at the northern end. Walking the full length and exploring the side streets to either side gives a good overview of the city's layered history.
