The National Museum of the Republic of Adygea houses the most important collection of Maykop culture artefacts in existence. The Maykop culture was a Bronze Age civilisation that flourished in the northern Caucasus from roughly 3700 to 3000 BC, and the burial mounds (kurgans) scattered around the region contain some of the most sophisticated metalwork and goldsmithing found anywhere in the ancient world.

The museum's collection includes the original finds from the Royal Kurgan of Maykop discovered in 1897: gold and silver vessels decorated with animal figures, precious stone jewellery, copper axes and remarkable figurines. The craftsmanship is startling — these are not primitive objects but refined, ambitious works of art from people who had mastered complex metallurgical techniques 5,000 years ago. The display puts them in their archaeological context and explains what is known about the Maykop culture's trade networks and social organisation.

The museum also covers the later history of Adygea and the Circassian people, with exhibits on traditional crafts, costumes and the 19th-century Circassian resistance to Russian conquest. Signage is primarily in Russian; some English materials are available.
