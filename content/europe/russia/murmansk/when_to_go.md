---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

Murmansk's two great experiences are the polar night and the polar day, and they push visits to the two extremes of the year. The **polar night** runs from about 2 December to 11 January, with the sun never rising above the horizon — though there is usually a few hours of twilight around noon. This is the best time for the **aurora borealis**, which is visible on roughly half of clear nights between late September and late March. December and January also bring the lowest temperatures, regularly below -20°C and occasionally below -30°C.

The **polar day** runs from about 22 May to 22 July. The sun never sets, the snow has largely gone, and the surrounding tundra blooms briefly with wildflowers. Summer temperatures average around 12-15°C and rarely exceed 20°C. This is the best season for hiking the Khibiny Mountains and travelling along the Kola coast. The shoulder seasons (March-April and September-October) are cold but offer normal day/night cycles, fewer tourists, and good aurora chances.
