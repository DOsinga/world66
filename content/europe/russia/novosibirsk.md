Novosibirsk is the heart of Siberia and its largest city, home to around 1.6 million people. By any standard it is young — founded in 1893 as Novonikolaevsk, a settlement at the point where the Trans-Siberian Railway crossed the River Ob. It grew explosively through the 20th century and is now the business, transport, and cultural hub of the entire region.

The [Novosibirsk Opera and Ballet Theatre](/europe/russia/novosibirsk/opera_theatre) is the city's architectural centrepiece, one of the largest opera houses in Russia, and its ballet and opera companies are known internationally. The city also has six theatres, a philharmonic orchestra with a strong reputation, and a conservatory. The centre around Lenin Square preserves a fascinating cluster of early Soviet Constructivist buildings by the architect Andrey Kryachkov — one of the better urban-scale records of that brief architectural experiment.

The most unusual attraction is [Akademgorodok](/europe/russia/novosibirsk/akademgorodok) (Academic City), a purpose-built scientific township about 30 km south of the centre in a birch forest on the shores of the [Ob Sea](/europe/russia/novosibirsk/ob_sea) reservoir. Founded in 1957 as the Siberian branch of the Russian Academy of Sciences, it has a distinctive atmosphere — part university campus, part forest village, with a concentration of research institutes and a lively cafe and bar scene fuelled by students and scientists. Near one of the genetics institutes stands a small bronze monument to the [laboratory mouse](/europe/russia/novosibirsk/laboratory_mouse_monument) — wearing glasses and knitting a double helix of DNA — that has become one of Siberia's most photographed objects.
