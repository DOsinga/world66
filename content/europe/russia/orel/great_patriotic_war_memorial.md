The Second World War memorial complex in Oryol marks one of the city's most significant historical moments: its liberation in August 1943 after two years of German occupation. The Battle of Kursk, fought in July and August of that year, was the largest tank battle in history, and Oryol was at its northern edge. The liberation was celebrated in Moscow with a special artillery salute — the first of the war — marking the victory.

The memorial includes an eternal flame, bronze figures of Soviet soldiers, and plaques listing units that fought in the area. The site is carefully maintained and remains a place of genuine civic importance, particularly on Victory Day (9 May) when local ceremonies are held here. The setting on a rise above the Oka River gives it a quiet dignity.
