The Ivan Turgenev Literary Museum in central Oryol is dedicated to Russia's great novelist of the countryside. Housed in a handsome 19th-century building, it contains manuscripts, first editions, personal effects, and portraits — including Turgenev's writing desk and the travel cases he carried between Russia and France. The collection is detailed and well-presented, and the staff are knowledgeable about Turgenev's life and circle.

Turgenev grew up at Spasskoye-Lutovinovo, about 70km north of Oryol, but the city itself was the regional centre of his childhood world. The museum explores his literary output from *Sketches from a Hunter's Album* — which described the peasant landscape around Oryol with such vividness that Tolstoy credited it with helping to build the case for the emancipation of serfs — through to *Fathers and Sons* and the late novels. Allow a couple of hours to do it properly.
