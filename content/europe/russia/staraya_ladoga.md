Staraya Ladoga — Old Ladoga — has a claim to being the very first capital of Russia. Founded in the 9th century on the banks of the Volkhov River, it predates Moscow, Novgorod and Kiev as a seat of power. Today it is a quiet village about 250 km east of [Saint Petersburg](/europe/russia/stpetersburg) along Route M8, but the sense of deep history here is real and immediate.

The centerpiece is the well-preserved 15th–16th century [fortress](/europe/russia/staraya_ladoga/fortress) perched above the river, with the 12th-century [Church of St. George](/europe/russia/staraya_ladoga/st_george_church) still standing inside its walls. The views from the ramparts over the Volkhov are genuinely lovely. Two ancient monasteries flank the village, each with its own medieval cathedral — the [Uspensky (Dormition) Monastery](/europe/russia/staraya_ladoga/uspensky_monastery) to the north and the [Nikolsky (St. Nicholas) Monastery](/europe/russia/staraya_ladoga/nikolsky_monastery) to the south. Beyond the monasteries, a line of 10th-century Varangian [burial mounds](/europe/russia/staraya_ladoga/burial_mounds) lies along the river. The scale is small, the crowds are thin, and the atmosphere is nothing like the big-ticket Russian heritage sites.

Guided tours from Saint Petersburg are available, or you can arrive independently and take your time walking between the fortress and the monasteries on your own.
