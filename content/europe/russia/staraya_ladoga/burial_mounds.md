A line of seven large prehistoric burial mounds (*sopki*) along the high bank of the Volkhov River, north of the village. The largest are 6–7 metres high and 30 metres across, and date from the 9th and 10th centuries — the period of the Varangian Rus, the Scandinavians who founded the first eastern Slavic state. Excavations have revealed cremated remains, Scandinavian weapons, and Byzantine coins.

The mounds are a 20-minute walk north from the Uspensky Monastery along the river, on open grassland with views over the water. There is no museum on site — interpretation is minimal — but climbing the largest mound is freely allowed and the view from the top covers the village, the river, and a wide stretch of the Volkhov plain. Combine with the monastery on the same walk.
