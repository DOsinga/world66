The fortress (*krepost*) of Staraya Ladoga occupies a low promontory above the Volkhov River where it meets the Ladozhka stream. The visible stone walls and towers — five circular towers connected by curtain walls — were built in the late 15th and early 16th centuries, after the unification of Russia by Ivan III, but they sit on the foundations of repeated earlier fortifications going back to the 8th century. The site was a key stronghold against Swedish incursion until Peter the Great moved the front line west with the founding of Saint Petersburg in 1703.

The fortress today is a museum complex run by the Russian Federation. Inside the walls are the small **Church of St. George**, the museum exhibitions, and a section of the original stone construction left exposed for visitors. The view from the ramparts over the Volkhov is one of the finest in the Russian north-west. Entrance is modest; guided tours are available in Russian only.
