The Cathedral of the Nativity of the Theotokos stands at the centre of the Kremlin and is the defining image of Suzdal — its blue domes spangled with golden stars visible from across the surrounding meadows. A cathedral has stood on this site since the 11th century; the current structure, much rebuilt over centuries, retains its 13th-century lower walls and the extraordinary Golden Gates on its south and west portals.

Inside, frescoes cover nearly every surface, layered across different centuries of painting and restoration. The earliest fragments date from the 13th century. The interior is tall and dim, the light falling through small windows set high in the walls — a different sensibility from later Orthodox churches, closer to the weight of the medieval period that produced it. The cathedral is part of the UNESCO World Heritage listing for the White Monuments of Vladimir and Suzdal.
