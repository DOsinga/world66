The Suzdal Kremlin is the oldest part of the town — its earthen ramparts were first raised in the 10th century — and it remains the natural starting point for any visit. Unlike the Kremlins of Moscow and Novgorod, Suzdal's was never rebuilt in stone: the defensive walls are still earthen banks, giving the compound an unusually open and pastoral feel.

Inside, the Cathedral of the Nativity of the Theotokos is the visual anchor — a blue-domed structure originally dating from the 13th century, with remarkable 13th-century bronze doors (the Golden Gates) that survived the Mongol destruction. The Archbishop's Chambers alongside house the main museum collection: applied art, embroidery, icons, and exhibits on the town's medieval history. The refectory and the bell tower complete the ensemble. Allow a full morning to do it justice.
