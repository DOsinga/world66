Tomsk is one of Siberia's oldest and most attractive cities, founded in 1604 as a fortress on the Tom River. Unlike most Siberian cities, which were rebuilt in Soviet concrete, Tomsk preserved a remarkable collection of wooden houses — ornately carved, painted in faded pastels, and leaning slightly with age. The [wooden architecture](/europe/russia/tomsk/wooden_architecture) alone makes it worth a detour.

The city is a major university centre. [Tomsk State University](/europe/russia/tomsk/tomsk_state_university), founded in 1878, was the first university in Siberia, and the student population gives the city a livelier, younger feel than you might expect this deep into Russia. The campus grounds are pleasant for a walk, and the university's botanical garden is one of the oldest in Siberia.

The [Resurrection Hill](/europe/russia/tomsk/resurrection_hill) area offers good views over the river and the old town, with the [Tom River embankment](/europe/russia/tomsk/tom_river_embankment) below providing a pleasant walk. The [Regional Museum](/europe/russia/tomsk/tomsk_regional_museum) gives an excellent grounding in the region's history before you explore the streets. An unexpected highlight is the [Church of the Intercession](/europe/russia/tomsk/intercession_church) — a full Catholic church built by Polish exiles in 1900, one of the oldest Catholic churches in Western Siberia. In winter, Tomsk is genuinely cold — minus 30°C is not unusual — but the snow-covered wooden houses are beautiful. Summer is brief and warm, and the best time to visit.
