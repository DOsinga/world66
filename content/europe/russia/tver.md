Tver sits on the upper Volga between [Moscow](/europe/russia/moscow) and [St. Petersburg](/europe/russia/stpetersburg), close enough to both to be overlooked and old enough to resent that fact. In the medieval period it was a serious rival to Moscow; later it became a planned imperial stop on the road between the two capitals. The result is a city with broad classical streets, river views, and a quieter provincial confidence.

The best part of Tver is the Volga-facing centre. The [Imperial Travel Palace](/europe/russia/tver/imperial_travel_palace) anchors the old ceremonial quarter, the [City Garden](/europe/russia/tver/city_garden) opens behind it, and the [Afanasy Nikitin Monument](/europe/russia/tver/afanasy_nikitin_monument) looks across the river to the bridges and embankments. It is a good city for walking, especially in late spring and early autumn.

Tver's museums are modest but useful. The palace now houses the regional art gallery, the [Museum of Tver Life](/europe/russia/tver/museum_of_tver_life) gives a more intimate view of merchant households, and the [Botanical Garden of Tver State University](/europe/russia/tver/botanical_garden) is a pleasant detour when the weather is kind.

Come for a day if you are breaking the Moscow-St Petersburg journey by train; stay overnight if you want to see a Russian regional capital without the Golden Ring crowds. Tver does not have Suzdal's fairy-tale polish, but it has river light, space, and enough rough edges to feel real.
