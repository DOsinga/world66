Tyumen holds the distinction of being the first Russian city in Siberia, founded in 1586 on the ruins of a Tatar settlement along the [Tura River](/europe/russia/tyumen/tura_embankment). Today it is the administrative capital of the oil-rich Tyumen Oblast, and the wealth shows — the centre is better maintained than many Russian cities of similar size, with restored merchant houses, a lively embankment, and a handful of good restaurants.

The city is not a typical tourist destination, but it has genuine character. The [Lovers' Bridge](/europe/russia/tyumen/lovers_bridge) across the Tura is a local landmark, and the cluster of [wooden houses](/europe/russia/tyumen/wooden_architecture) in the old town — carved window frames, painted facades — gives a sense of what Siberian towns looked like before Soviet-era concrete. The [Church of the Saviour](/europe/russia/tyumen/church_of_the_saviour) dominates the historic skyline, and the [Tyumen Museum of Fine Arts](/europe/russia/tyumen/tyumen_museum) contains an unexpectedly good collection of icons and Siberian folk art. The [hot springs at Verkhniy Bor](/europe/russia/tyumen/verkhniy_bor_hot_springs), about thirty minutes outside the city, are popular year-round and worth a half-day trip — particularly in winter, when soaking in steaming outdoor pools surrounded by snow-covered pines is a genuinely Siberian experience.
