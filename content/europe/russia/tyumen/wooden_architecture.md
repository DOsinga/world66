Tyumen's old quarter contains some of the finest surviving examples of traditional Siberian wooden domestic architecture in Russia. The carved window frames and decorated facades of the merchant houses along Respubliki Street and the surrounding lanes represent a building tradition that flourished in the 19th century, when local merchants had money and skilled craftsmen had time.

The houses are distinguished by the complexity of their carvings: stylised floral patterns, geometric designs, and figurative elements worked into shutters, window surrounds, and roofline decorations. Many are painted in faded pastels — blue, green, ochre — against the natural dark wood of the walls. Walking these streets in the late afternoon light, when the low sun picks out the carved shadows, is one of Tyumen's genuine pleasures. Several houses are protected landmarks; others are in private use and slowly deteriorating, which adds a certain urgency to the walk.
