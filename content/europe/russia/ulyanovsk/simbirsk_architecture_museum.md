  The wooden merchant houses preserved in the Simbirsk Architecture Museum are not reconstructions — they were moved here from various parts of the city as the Soviet urban renewal programme threatened to demolish them. The museum is essentially a small open-air rescue operation: some of the most elaborate carved wooden facades in the Volga region ended up here because no one could agree on what else to do with them.
---

The Simbirsk Architecture and Urban Planning Museum occupies a cluster of preserved 19th-century wooden buildings near the city centre, documenting the built environment of the former Simbirsk through architectural fragments, models, photographs, and period interiors. It is one of the few places in Russia where you can see the elaborate carved wooden decoration that characterised Volga merchant-class architecture — gingerbread trim, painted shutters, decorative pediments — in its proper context.

The museum's permanent collection covers the development of Simbirsk from its foundation as a Muscovite frontier fortress in 1648 through to the early 20th century. An adjacent outdoor section preserves several relocated wooden structures. For anyone interested in pre-revolutionary Russian provincial life, this is one of the most rewarding small museums in the Volga region.
