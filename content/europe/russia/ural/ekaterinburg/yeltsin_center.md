The Yeltsin Presidential Center is one of the most remarkable museums in Russia — an honest, thoughtful, and sometimes uncomfortable account of the Soviet collapse and the turbulent decade of Boris Yeltsin's presidency. The main exhibition covers seven "days" in Russian history, from the 1917 revolution through the Soviet era to Yeltsin's death in 2007, using film, documents, photographs, and immersive installations to tell a story that official Russian history now prefers to gloss over.

The museum does not shy away from the chaos and suffering of the 1990s, including the economic collapse, the Chechen wars, and the oligarch privatisations that defined Yeltsin's years. At the same time it genuinely believes in what Yeltsin believed in — democracy, freedom of speech, individual rights — and the contrast with contemporary Russia gives the museum a quiet political charge that visitors feel even without being told to feel it. The complex also includes a cinema, library, restaurant, and event spaces. It is architecturally striking, sitting on the Iset River with views across the water.
