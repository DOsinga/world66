The Vladivostok Synagogue is a striking Moorish Revival building on Praporshchika Komarova Street, one of the better-preserved historic religious buildings in the city. The exterior features arched windows, ornamental brickwork, and Star of David details that were hidden or removed during the Soviet period and carefully restored since the 1990s.

The synagogue serves the small but active Jewish community of Vladivostok today. Visitors are generally welcome outside service times — check ahead for access. The building stands as a reminder of the genuinely cosmopolitan character of pre-revolutionary Vladivostok, when the city's merchant class included Germans, Chinese, Japanese, Koreans, and Jews as well as Russians.
