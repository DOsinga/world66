The Vologda Region (Vologodskaya Oblast) occupies a large swath of Russia's European north, a landscape of birch and spruce forests, river systems, and the chain of lakes that includes Beloye Ozero and Kubenskoye — some of the largest in European Russia. It is not a place most international travellers know, but it holds some extraordinary things: medieval monasteries, a city of extraordinary wooden architecture, and a kind of deep Russian north that feels genuinely remote even though [Vologda](/europe/russia/vologda_region/vologda) city is only eight hours by train from Moscow.

[Vologda](/europe/russia/vologda_region/vologda), the regional capital, was founded in the 12th century and served as a northern trading centre for centuries. Its historic centre preserves an unusual number of carved wooden merchant houses alongside stone churches and the walls of the 16th-century kremlin that Ivan the Terrible ordered built when he briefly considered making Vologda his alternative capital. Vologda lace and Vologda butter — both with genuine traditions behind them — are still produced and sold here.

The region's most remarkable monument is the [Kirillo-Belozersky Monastery](/europe/russia/vologda_region/sights), founded in 1397 on the shore of Siverskoye Lake near the town of Kirillov. At its height this was the largest and wealthiest monastery in Russia — a fortified city of stone towers and churches covering several hectares. The walls, towers, and churches are preserved in extraordinary condition. Nearby, the smaller Goritsky Nunnery has a poignant history as a place of imprisonment for disgraced Tsarist noblewomen.

The region is best visited in summer, when the long days and river-and-lake landscape come into their own. Roads outside the main towns are variable.
