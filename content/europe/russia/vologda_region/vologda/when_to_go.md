---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

Vologda is a northern Russian city and experiences the corresponding climate extremes. **Summer** (June–August) is the best season: long days (the city is close enough to the Arctic Circle to experience white nights in June), temperatures of 18–25°C, and the Russian countryside at its most hospitable. July and August are the main travel months.

**Winter** (November–March) is severe: temperatures routinely fall below -20°C, and January averages around -13°C. The city is fully functional in winter and has a particular beauty under heavy snow — the wooden houses, the monastery, and the onion domes look their best in winter light — but the cold requires serious preparation. The short daylight hours (fewer than 8 hours in December) limit what can be seen comfortably.

Spring and autumn are transitional and often grey. May is a good compromise month: the worst of the cold is over, the summer crowds have not arrived, and the countryside is coming to life. The **Vologda Butter Festival** in summer celebrates the regional dairy industry with tastings, demonstrations, and folk music — check local listings for the exact date, which varies by year.
