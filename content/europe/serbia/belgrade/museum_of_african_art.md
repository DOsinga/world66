Belgrade’s Museum of African Art is unusual in the best sense: a Yugoslav-era institution devoted to African art, born from diplomatic and cultural ties with the non-aligned world. Masks, sculpture, textiles, and everyday objects make it a very different museum from the national-history stops elsewhere in the city.

It is worth seeking out because it explains something specific about Belgrade’s 20th-century outlook: the city once imagined itself as a meeting point beyond the Cold War blocs. The museum is compact, thoughtful, and strongest when read as both an art collection and a piece of Yugoslav cultural history.
