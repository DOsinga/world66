The Church of Christ the Saviour is one of the most contested structures in Kosovo — a large, unfinished Serbian Orthodox church on the University of Pristina campus that has been at the centre of disputes about identity, sovereignty, and memory since the 1999 war.

Construction began in 1992 during a period of rising Serbian nationalism. The planned scale was enormous: the finished church would have 1,389 golden crosses, explicitly referencing the year of the Battle of Kosovo. Work stopped in 1999 when Serbian forces withdrew and the Serbian population largely left Pristina. The building has sat in various states of disuse and partial completion since.

For visitors, the church is interesting precisely because of its unfinished, contentious state. The shell of a vast domed structure sits in a small park on the university campus, with scaffolding and fences that have become semi-permanent. It is a stark reminder of the unresolved tensions that independence has not yet fully settled. The university students who walk past it every day have grown up knowing nothing but a Kosovo without Serbian rule, and for many of them the church is an anomaly in their city.
