The Great Hammam of Pristina is one of the oldest surviving Ottoman structures in Kosovo, built in the 15th century during the reign of Sultan Mehmed II. The building — with its characteristic lead-domed roof perforated with small glass oculi to let in light — sits within the old bazaar district, not far from the Jashar Pasha Mosque.

The hammam functioned as a public bathhouse for centuries, playing a central role in the social life of the Ottoman city. The interior is divided into the standard Ottoman arrangement of cold room (soğukluk), warm room (ılıklık), and hot room (sıcaklık). The building's exterior, with its domes and stone masonry, is the more photogenic element today.

Although no longer operating as a bath, the hammam has been conserved as a heritage site and is visible from the surrounding lanes. The cobblestoned area around it, with its traditional workshops and coffee houses, gives the best sense of pre-modern Pristina. Allow twenty minutes to walk through the whole bazaar district, of which this hammam is the centrepiece.
