Liburnia is a traditional Albanian restaurant near Mother Teresa Boulevard that has been feeding Pristina for years without changing much — which is either a recommendation or a warning depending on what you are looking for. The kitchen focuses on the basics done well: grilled meats, fresh bread baked in-house, seasonal salads, and the kind of soup that feels like it has been simmering since morning.

The room is simple, the service is direct, and the prices are about as low as you will find in the centre. Lunch here is a practical and satisfying option between sightseeing. Qebapa — small minced-meat sausages served with flatbread and raw onion — are the reliable order; the house-made yoghurt alongside is worth asking for.

This is the kind of place that feels genuinely local, and at midday it fills with people who work in the nearby government ministries and offices.
