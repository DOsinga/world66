---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

Pristina has a continental climate: cold, snowy winters and hot summers. Spring (April–May) and early autumn (September–October) are the most comfortable times to visit, with mild temperatures and manageable rainfall.

Summer (June–August) can be hot — temperatures in the low to mid-thirties are common in July — but the city is lively and the outdoor cafe culture is at its best. Heat is rarely oppressive for more than a few days at a time.

Winter is cold and grey, with regular snowfall. The city is not particularly set up for winter tourism, though the mountains around Kosovo do offer some skiing (Brezovica is the main resort). The Kosovo capital shuts down a little between December and February.

Independence Day (17 February) is the biggest celebration of the year and worth timing a visit around if you want to see Pristina at its most exuberant — the Newborn monument is repainted, there are concerts and fireworks, and the city fills with members of the diaspora returning from Switzerland, Germany, and Austria.
