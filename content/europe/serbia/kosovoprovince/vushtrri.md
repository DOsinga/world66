Vushtrri (also written Vučitrn in Serbian) is a small city in central Kosovo, about 25 kilometres north of Pristina on the road toward Mitrovica. It is an old market town with an Ottoman-era centre — a stone bridge, remnants of a bazaar — and a history stretching back through Byzantine and Roman settlement to Illyrian times.

The town sits on the Sitnica River and is surrounded by agricultural land. Most visitors come as a day trip from Pristina or as a stop between Pristina and the divided city of Mitrovica to the north. The centre is compact and walkable: the Ottoman bridge, the old mosque, and the House of Culture (which houses the Historical-Ethnographic Museum on its upper floor) are all close together.

The [Muzeu Historik-Etnografik](/europe/serbia/kosovoprovince/vushtrri/muzeu_historiketn) on the third floor of the House of Culture covers the history of the Vushtrri region from ancient times through the Ottoman period, with archaeological finds, traditional costumes, and photographs of local life. Small but worthwhile if you are in town. The museum is open on weekdays; check hours locally.
