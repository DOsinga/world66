Novi Sad is Serbia's second-largest city and the capital of the Vojvodina province, set on the Danube about 80 km north of [Belgrade](/europe/serbia/belgrade). It is a cultured, relaxed city with a handsome Austro-Hungarian centre and a lively cafe scene.

The [Petrovaradin Fortress](/europe/serbia/novisad/petrovaradin_fortress) dominates the right bank of the Danube — a massive 18th-century citadel with underground tunnels, artist studios, and panoramic views over the city. Every July, the fortress grounds host [EXIT](/europe/serbia/novisad/exit_festival), one of Europe's biggest music festivals, drawing hundreds of thousands of visitors.

The old centre is compact and walkable. [Dunavska Street](/europe/serbia/novisad/dunavska_street) and Zmaj Jovina Street are the main strolling and cafe strips, leading to [Liberty Square](/europe/serbia/novisad/liberty_square) with its [neo-Gothic cathedral](/europe/serbia/novisad/name_of_mary_cathedral). The [Štrand beach](/europe/serbia/novisad/strand_beach) along the Danube is where locals swim and sunbathe in summer. The big art museums — the [Museum of Vojvodina](/europe/serbia/novisad/museum_of_vojvodina) and the [Gallery of Matica Srpska](/europe/serbia/novisad/gallery_matica_srpska) — are an easy walk apart, and the Hungarian Secessionist [synagogue](/europe/serbia/novisad/novi_sad_synagogue) is one of the most beautiful buildings in the city. Across the river, Fruška Gora and its medieval monasteries make for an easy day trip.
