Vršac sits near Serbia's Romanian border, where the flat Vojvodina plain suddenly rises into vineyards and the Vršac Mountains. That contrast gives the town more character than many of the lowland settlements around it: wine cellars below, a ruined castle above, and monastic villages just beyond the edge of town.

The old centre is compact and easy to walk, with cafes, churches, and a calm provincial rhythm. The skyline belongs to [Vršac Castle](/europe/serbia/vrsac/vrsac_castle), a medieval tower on the hill east of town. From there the plain opens in every direction, with [Gudurica Peak](/europe/serbia/vrsac/gudurica_peak) marking the highest point in Vojvodina.

Wine is the other reason to come. Vršac has been a vineyard town for centuries, and the September Grape Ball keeps that identity public rather than tucked away in cellars. Add [Mesić Monastery](/europe/serbia/vrsac/mesic_monastery) and the hill walks, and Vršac clears the spare-day test for travellers already moving between Belgrade, Novi Sad, and western Romania.
