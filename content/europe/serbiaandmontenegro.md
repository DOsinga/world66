Serbia and Montenegro existed as a joint state from 2003 until 2006, when Montenegro voted for independence in a referendum. The two republics are now separate sovereign countries — see [Serbia](/europe/serbia) and [Montenegro](/europe/montenegro) for current pages — but much of the original World66 content was written when they were still united, and is preserved here.

Geographically, the territory ranges from the flat Pannonian plains of northern Serbia, through the rolling hills and rivers of central Serbia, down to the dramatic Montenegrin coast on the Adriatic and the high karst mountains of the interior. Belgrade, the capital of Serbia, is a city with a turbulent history written into its architecture: Roman, Ottoman, Austro-Hungarian, communist, and contemporary layers overlap in the urban fabric.

[Novi Sad](/europe/serbiaandmontenegro/novisad), on the Danube in northern Serbia, is the second city and the intellectual capital, home to a substantial Hungarian minority and the Petrovaradin Fortress. [Kragujevac](/europe/serbiaandmontenegro/kragujevac) in central Serbia carries the heavy memory of a 1941 Nazi massacre. The [Bay of Kotor](/europe/serbiaandmontenegro/bayofkotor) in Montenegro is one of the most beautiful natural harbours in Europe. [Sokobanja](/europe/serbiaandmontenegro/sokobanja) is a long-established Serbian spa town in the east. The [Kosovo Province](/europe/serbiaandmontenegro/kosovoprovince) was then under UN administration; it has since declared independence, though recognition remains contested.
