Kragujevac is Serbia's fourth-largest city, with a population of around 175,000, set in the rolling hills of the Šumadija region in central Serbia. It holds an outsized place in the country's history: from 1818 to 1841 it served as the first capital of modern Serbia, and the country's first printing press, the [Princely Serbian Theatre](/europe/serbiaandmontenegro/kragujevac/princely_serbian_theatre), and the [First Kragujevac Gymnasium](/europe/serbiaandmontenegro/kragujevac/first_kragujevac_gymnasium) were all established here. Today the city is primarily industrial, home to the [Zastava](/europe/serbiaandmontenegro/kragujevac/zastava_arms) car and arms factories and a large university.

What gives Kragujevac genuine weight as a destination is the [Šumarice Memorial Park](/europe/serbiaandmontenegro/kragujevac/sumarice_memorial_park) on its outskirts. On 21 October 1941, German occupiers shot nearly 3,000 civilians here — teachers, schoolchildren, workers, anyone they could round up — as a reprisal for partisan attacks. The memorial park, with its modernist sculptures and the preserved execution grounds, is one of the most moving and sobering sites in the Balkans. It deserves to be far better known outside the region. In the centre, the surviving 19th-century buildings cluster around the old princely court at [Stari Grad](/europe/serbiaandmontenegro/kragujevac/stari_grad) — the [Amidža Konak](/europe/serbiaandmontenegro/kragujevac/amidza_konak) and the [National Museum of Šumadija](/europe/serbiaandmontenegro/kragujevac/national_museum_sumadija) are the natural starting points.
