Bratislava is one of Europe's smallest and most underrated capitals. Sitting on the Danube where [Slovakia](/europe/slovakia), [Austria](/europe/austria), and [Hungary](/europe/hungary) meet, it spent centuries in the shadow of nearby Vienna — and for much of that time, it was the coronation city of the Hungarian kings.

The old town is compact and walkable. [Bratislava Castle](/europe/slovakia/bratislava/bratislava_castle), perched on a hill above the Danube, dominates the skyline and houses several museums including the [Museum of Jewish Culture](/europe/slovakia/bratislava/museum_of_jewish_culture). Below, the medieval streets lead to the [Main Square](/europe/slovakia/bratislava/main_square), where the Renaissance Maximilian Fountain and Gothic Town Hall anchor a cluster of pastel-coloured houses. The [Primate's Palace](/europe/slovakia/bratislava/primates_palace), a few steps away, is the finest Classicist building in Slovakia.

[St Martin's Cathedral](/europe/slovakia/bratislava/st_martins_cathedral), a 13th-century Gothic church where 19 Hungarian monarchs were crowned, anchors the western edge of the old town. The [Slavín war memorial](/europe/slovakia/bratislava/slavin), on the city's highest hill, offers panoramic views and a sombre reminder of the 6,845 Soviet soldiers buried here. For a lighter afternoon, pack a picnic for [Janko Kráľ park](/europe/slovakia/bratislava/janka_krala_park) on the south bank of the Danube, or head to Horský Park for a walk in the woods.

The Christmas Market on the [Main Square](/europe/slovakia/bratislava/main_square) is one of the best in Central Europe — worth timing a visit around if you are in the region in December.
