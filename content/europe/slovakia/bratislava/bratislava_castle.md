Bratislava Castle sits on a rocky hill above the Danube, at the western edge of the old city. The current reconstruction dates mostly from the 1950s–60s communist-era restoration, but the site has been fortified since at least the Great Moravian era in the 9th century. The position is commanding — from the terrace, you can see across the Danube into Austria and Hungary, and on a clear day the plains stretch to the horizon.

Inside, the castle houses two branches of the Slovak National Museum: the Historical Museum, which covers Slovak history from prehistory to the 20th century, and a Treasury with a collection of decorative arts, furniture, and royal regalia. The Gothic throne hall on the top floor is the most impressive interior space. The castle gardens offer a peaceful break from the old town below, with views over the city and the SNP bridge.

The Korunná Bašta (Crown Bastion) at the northeast corner of the complex houses a permanent exhibition on the history of Bratislava Castle. Allow 2–3 hours for a thorough visit.
