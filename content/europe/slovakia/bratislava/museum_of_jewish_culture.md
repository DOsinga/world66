The Museum of Jewish Culture is one of the most thoughtful and well-curated museums in Slovakia. Housed in Bratislava Castle, it documents the history of Jewish life in Slovakia from the early medieval period through to the Holocaust and its aftermath. Slovakia's Jewish community was one of the most vibrant in Central Europe before the Second World War; the museum tells that story honestly.

The collection includes religious artefacts, personal documents, photographs, and detailed accounts of daily life in Jewish communities across Slovakia. A significant section is devoted to the deportations and genocide of 1942–1944, when the collaborationist Slovak State facilitated the transportation of over 70,000 Slovak Jews to Nazi death camps — the majority of the Jewish population. Fewer than 15,000 survived.

The museum is located within the castle grounds and is run by the Slovak Jewish Heritage Centre. Entry is included in the general castle ticket or available separately. Give it at least 90 minutes.
