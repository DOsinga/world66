The Neolog Synagogue Memorial marks the site of the Great Synagogue of Bratislava, a massive Moorish-revival building completed in 1893 that served the city's large Neolog (Reform) Jewish community. The building survived the Second World War intact but was demolished in 1969 when the communist government built the approach road to the SNP bridge — destroying most of the historic Jewish quarter in the process.

The memorial, designed by architect Marek Šilpoch and unveiled in 1996, consists of a metal menorah and a series of inscribed plaques describing the history of the community. It stands on Rybné námestie, directly below the SNP bridge and within sight of St Martin's Cathedral. The proximity of the memorial to the highway that replaced the synagogue makes the politics of the demolition tangible.

The adjacent Museum of Jewish Culture in the castle provides the fuller context. The memorial is small and quiet, but the site deserves a few minutes of attention.
