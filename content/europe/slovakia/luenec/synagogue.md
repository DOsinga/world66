The Lučenec Synagogue is the most striking building in the town and one of the most architecturally significant in southern Slovakia. Built in 1925 in a bold Moorish-revival style, it served the Neolog Jewish congregation of Lučenec — then called Losonc under Hungarian rule — at a time when the town had one of the largest Jewish populations in the region.

The fate of that community was almost total destruction. Following the German occupation of Slovakia in 1944, the Jewish residents of Lučenec were deported to Auschwitz. Of a community that had numbered over 2,000 before the war, fewer than 200 survived. The synagogue itself survived the war but fell into disuse. It has since been restored and now operates as a cultural and events venue. The building's ornate exterior — with its Moorish arches, twin towers, and elaborate brickwork — is a reminder of the community that built it. An interior visit, when possible, reveals the scale of the original congregation hall.
