Modra is a small wine town about 35 km northeast of [Bratislava](/europe/slovakia/bratislava), tucked against the eastern slopes of the [Small Carpathians](/europe/slovakia/small_carpathians). It is best known for two things, both of which it has been doing for centuries: making wine and making pottery. The combination makes it one of the most satisfying half-day trips from the capital — you can taste a local Frankovka, watch a potter at the wheel, and be back in Bratislava for dinner.

The town's pottery is its signature. Since the 18th century, Modra has produced a distinctive cobalt-glazed earthenware — blue floral designs hand-painted on cream-coloured clay, a tradition that merged Hungarian and Moravian techniques along the old trade routes through the hills. The town's coat of arms even carries the characteristic blue motif. The main workshop, Majolika Modra, sits near the central square and runs a shop alongside occasional demonstrations of the production process. The pieces are practical things — plates, jugs, bowls, coffee cups — and they survive the journey home better than most souvenirs.

Wine is the town's other livelihood. Modra sits squarely in the Small Carpathians wine region, and its cellars and wine bars open for tasting on weekends, with more activity during the September–October harvest. The local Welschriesling and Frankovka Modrá were once poured at the court of the Hungarian kings, and the producers here are part of the quiet revival of Slovak viniculture that has been gathering pace since the 1990s.

Beyond the pottery and the wine, Modra rewards a slow wander. The historic centre has a modest baroque square, a surviving stretch of town wall and gate, and the relaxed pace of a country town that has not yet been overrun by visitors. Pair it with neighbouring Pezinok, the region's main wine town, for a fuller day along the wine route.
