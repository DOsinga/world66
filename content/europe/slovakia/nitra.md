Nitra is one of the oldest cities in Slovakia, sitting at the foot of a wooded hill crowned by a castle that has watched over the Nitra River valley for more than a thousand years. This is where Prince Pribina built the first documented Christian church in the Great Moravian lands in the 9th century, a founding moment that Slovaks regard as the birth of their nation's Christian identity.

The city divides neatly into two faces: the compact Old Town clustered around [Nitra Castle](/europe/slovakia/nitra/nitra_castle) and the cathedral hill, full of Baroque facades and a slow pace that contrasts sharply with the sprawling socialist-era university campus and shopping districts that surround it. With two universities — including the Slovak University of Agriculture — the town is young and lively in term-time, though it never quite becomes a proper party city.

What draws travelers here is the weight of history in a small package. You can walk from a cathedral whose foundations date to the 9th century to the old Jewish quarter and its striking [Nitra Synagogue](/europe/slovakia/nitra/nitra_synagogue) in under ten minutes. Just north of town, perched on a lone hill above the plains, stands the [Dražovce church](/europe/slovakia/nitra/drazovce_church) — a Romanesque gem from the 11th century that looks exactly as a medieval church should: small, solitary, and a little austere. Nitra is an easy day trip from [Bratislava](/europe/slovakia/bratislava) but rewards a night's stay to let its quieter moods sink in.
