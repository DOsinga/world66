The Church of St. Michael the Archangel at Dražovce stands on a solitary hill about four kilometres north of central Nitra, visible for miles across the flat agricultural plain. It is one of the oldest and best-preserved Romanesque churches in Slovakia, built in the 11th and 12th centuries and remarkably intact, its squat stone tower and rounded apse largely unchanged since medieval times.

Getting there requires a walk up a dirt track — about fifteen minutes from the nearest parking — which helps keep it quiet. The interior is small and austere, with fragments of original fresco still visible. The walk itself, through vineyards and along field edges, is pleasant. The church is technically in the village of Dražovce, now absorbed into greater Nitra, and is open only at limited hours; check locally before making the trip specifically to enter.
