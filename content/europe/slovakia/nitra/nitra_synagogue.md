The Nitra Synagogue, built in 1911 in a Moorish Revival style, stands on a side street near the Old Town as one of the few surviving remnants of Nitra's once-substantial Jewish community. The building's distinctive horseshoe arches and ornate facade make it unmissable even to someone who didn't know what it was.

The synagogue no longer functions as a place of worship and now serves as an exhibition space dedicated to the history of the Jewish community in Nitra and the broader Nitra region. The Holocaust exhibition inside is sobering and carefully presented. The building itself is worth seeing as an example of the eclectic historicist architecture popular among Central European Jewish communities in the late 19th and early 20th centuries.
