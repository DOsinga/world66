The Small Carpathians (Malé Karpaty) are a low, wooded mountain range that begins right at the northern edge of [Bratislava](/europe/slovakia/bratislava) and runs northeast toward Trnava. They are gentle hills rather than serious mountains — vineyards on the lower slopes, oak and beech forest above — but their real fame comes from what grows on them. This is one of the only places in Europe where a proper wine country starts within 20 km of a capital city's centre.

The Small Carpathians Wine Route threads through a string of small towns along the foot of the hills: Svätý Jur, Pezinok, [Modra](/europe/slovakia/modra) and Vinosady, each with its own producers, cellars open for tasting, and the unhurried charm of a Slovak town rediscovering its viniculture. Pezinok, about 25 km from Bratislava, is the main wine town, with a castle winery and several independent producers. Modra, 10 km further on, is known for blue folk pottery as much as for its wine. The region's Welschriesling and Frankovka Modrá were drunk at the Hungarian royal court back when Bratislava was the kingdom's capital.

Getting here is easy. The wine towns are about 30 minutes by car or 45 minutes by regional bus from central Bratislava, and the route is popular with cyclists — the hills are forgiving and the vineyards are at their best in early autumn. Tastings can often be arranged with little notice during the September–October harvest; outside the season it is worth calling ahead.

The hills themselves offer more than wine. Marked trails climb through the forest to ruined castles and viewpoints, and the range shelters several nature reserves. A day here can combine a morning walk in the woods with an afternoon of cellar-hopping — a very Slovak way to spend a day out from the city.
