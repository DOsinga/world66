Žilina is the main city of northwestern Slovakia, set in a valley where the Váh and Kysuca rivers meet, with the Beskydy mountains visible on the eastern horizon. With around 80,000 people, it's Slovakia's third-largest city — a regional industrial and university centre that sees relatively few foreign visitors.

The historic centre is anchored by [Mariánske námestie](/europe/slovakia/zilina/marianske_namestie) (Marian Square), a well-preserved arcaded Renaissance square that dates mainly from the 16th century. The [Holy Trinity Cathedral](/europe/slovakia/zilina/holy_trinity_cathedral) anchors one end of the square with its distinctive freestanding Baroque tower. Just off the main square, the [New Synagogue](/europe/slovakia/zilina/new_synagogue), designed in 1929 by Peter Behrens — the Modernist master who mentored Gropius, Mies van der Rohe, and Le Corbusier — is one of the architecturally significant buildings in the region, and one of the most sobering, given the near-total destruction of the community it served. The nearby [Budatín Castle](/europe/slovakia/zilina/budatin_castle), at the confluence of the rivers, is the city's most distinctive landmark and houses a regional museum.

Žilina is a practical base for the surrounding region: [Malá Fatra national park](/europe/slovakia/zilina/mala_fatra) is 15 kilometres east, with excellent hiking and skiing. The castles of Strečno and Starhrad, perched dramatically above the Váh gorge, are worth a half-day trip. The [Church of Saint Stephen the King](/europe/slovakia/zilina/church_of_saint_stephen) in the Závodie district is the oldest building in the city, a 12th-century Romanesque church that predates the town's medieval growth.
