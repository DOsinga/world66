  particles ground out of the surrounding Julian Alps during the last
  Ice Age. The lake is fed largely by warm thermal springs from the
  Bohinj fault — the reason a high alpine lake at 475 metres can warm
  to 26°C in late summer, when most Slovenes drive up to swim before
  the autumn rains turn it cold again.

The lake itself is the main event in Bled. It is a small, near-perfect oval — about 2.1 km long, less than half a kilometre across — set in a bowl of forested hills with the Julian Alps and the snow-capped Triglav massif beyond. A flat 6 km walking path circles the shore, broken occasionally by lakeside bathing platforms and the odd swan.

In summer the water warms enough that swimming is genuinely pleasant; designated bathing areas with changing rooms are scattered along the south shore. Rowing boats, electric punts and traditional pletna are all available for hire. In winter the lake occasionally freezes over solidly enough to walk on, though the Alpine climate has made this rarer than it used to be.
