Izola is a small fishing town on Slovenia's short Adriatic coast, wedged between [Koper](/europe/slovenia/koper) and [Piran](/europe/slovenia/piran). It lacks Piran's postcard-perfect Venetian architecture, but that is part of its charm — Izola is quieter, less polished, and more genuinely lived-in. The old town sits on what was once an island (the connecting causeway was filled in centuries ago), and the narrow streets and pastel-coloured houses still have an Italian feel. Italian and Slovene are both official languages here; street signs are in both, and most older locals will switch between them mid-sentence.

The harbour is the heart of the town — fishing boats unload their catch here, and several restaurants along the waterfront serve fresh seafood at reasonable prices. The skyline above the boats is dominated by the slender campanile of the Church of St Maurus, the eighteenth-century parish church. Just behind the church, Manzioli Square is the most charming piece of old town: a triangular cobbled space with the Renaissance Manzioli Palace on one side and a handful of small wine bars spilling tables onto the stones in the evening.

The swimming is mostly off rocks and small concrete platforms, though Simonov Zaliv to the east has a stretch of beach and the ruins of the Roman port of Haliaetum just behind it. The Parenzana cycle trail, following the route of a former narrow-gauge railway between Trieste and Poreč, passes through Izola and runs along the coast toward Piran — a flat, scenic ride through olive groves, vineyards and tunnels cut through the limestone hills. To the west, the Strunjan Nature Reserve protects a dramatic stretch of cliffs and a sheltered lagoon and is one of the few protected pieces of natural coastline left on the upper Adriatic.
