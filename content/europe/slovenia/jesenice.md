Jesenice is not the pretty face of the Slovenian Alps, and it is more interesting for admitting that. This is a working Upper Sava town shaped by iron, railways, and the steep wall of the Karawanks, sitting between [Bled](/europe/slovenia/bled), [Kranjska Gora](/europe/slovenia/kranjska_gora), and the Austrian border.

The reason to stop is the industrial heritage around [Stara Sava](/europe/slovenia/jesenice/stara_sava), where old ironworks buildings, workers' housing, and manor houses have been gathered into a compact historic quarter. The [Upper Sava Museum](/europe/slovenia/jesenice/upper_sava_museum) gives context to a town that most travellers otherwise see only from the train window.

Jesenice also has a practical role. Its railway station is an important junction for the Bohinj Railway and cross-border routes, and buses run toward Bled and the ski valleys. If you are chasing alpine views, continue west; if you want to understand the labour and industry behind this part of Slovenia, give Jesenice a few hours.
