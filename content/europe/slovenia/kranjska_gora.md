Kranjska Gora is Slovenia's best-known mountain resort, tucked into the far northwest where the Julian Alps press against Austria and Italy. It is busier and more developed than the quieter valleys around [Lake Bohinj](/europe/slovenia/lakebohinj), but it earns that traffic with skiing, walking, cycling, and some of the country's most dramatic road scenery.

In winter the [Kranjska Gora Ski Resort](/europe/slovenia/kranjska_gora/kranjska_gora_ski_resort) drops almost into town, making it one of Slovenia's easiest ski bases. In summer the focus shifts to [Lake Jasna](/europe/slovenia/kranjska_gora/lake_jasna), the emerald pools at [Zelenci Springs](/europe/slovenia/kranjska_gora/zelenci_springs), and the hairpin road over [Vršič Pass](/europe/slovenia/kranjska_gora/vrsic_pass) toward the Soča Valley.

The wider valley has a sporting mythology of its own. [Planica Nordic Centre](/europe/slovenia/kranjska_gora/planica_nordic_centre) is one of the great names in ski jumping and ski flying, while the [Russian Chapel](/europe/slovenia/kranjska_gora/russian_chapel) on the Vršič road gives the mountains a sombre First World War memory.

Kranjska Gora is not a lonely alpine village, and in peak seasons it can feel thoroughly organised. That is also its strength: trails, buses, ski lifts, bike routes, and day trips are easy to arrange, making it a practical base for travellers who want the Julian Alps without too much logistical fuss.
