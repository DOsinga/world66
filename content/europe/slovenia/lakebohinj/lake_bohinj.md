The largest permanent lake in Slovenia, four kilometres long and a kilometre wide, held in a glacial basin at the southern foot of the Julian Alps. The water is cold even in midsummer — a steady 18 to 22 degrees in July and August — and so clear that you can see the bottom in many places near the shore. A flat, easy footpath circles the entire lake; the full loop is about twelve kilometres and takes three to four hours at a relaxed pace.

There are public swimming spots all the way around, the busiest at the eastern end near [Ribcev Laz](/europe/slovenia/lakebohinj/ribcev_laz) and the quietest along the northern shore, where you can usually find a stretch of pebbly beach to yourself. Rowing boats, stand-up paddleboards and small electric boats are for hire at both ends. Motorised craft are banned. The only village on the lake itself is Ukanc at the western end; the rest of the foreshore is forest and meadow inside the [Triglav National Park](/europe/slovenia/lakebohinj/triglav_national_park).
