Maribor is Slovenia's second city — overlooked by most visitors who rush between Ljubljana and the Slovenian Alps, which is their loss. A university town of around 100,000 on the Drava river in the northeastern corner of the country, it has a relaxed, lived-in quality that the more self-consciously picturesque capital sometimes lacks.

The old town stretches along the left bank of the Drava, with [Lent](/europe/slovenia/maribor/lent_promenade) — the riverside promenade — as its social heart. This stretch comes alive every summer for Festival Lent, a sprawling music and cultural festival that draws performers and visitors from across Central Europe. The [main square, Glavni trg](/europe/slovenia/maribor/glavni_trg), is anchored by an 18th-century plague column, and the [Maribor Castle](/europe/slovenia/maribor/maribor_castle) above it houses the regional museum.

Maribor sits in the middle of Styrian wine country, and the surrounding hills are thick with vineyards. The [Maribor Vineyards](/europe/slovenia/maribor/maribor_vineyards) along the south-facing slopes above the old town include the [oldest productive grapevine in the world](/europe/slovenia/maribor/old_vine) — over 400 years old, according to the Guinness World Records — which produces a small quantity of wine each year. Wine tourism is a serious part of the regional economy, and the [Mariborske Pohorje](/europe/slovenia/maribor/pohorje) hills immediately south of the city offer skiing in winter and hiking in summer. The [Maribor Synagogue](/europe/slovenia/maribor/maribor_synagogue), one of the oldest in Central Europe, is an unexpected highlight in the old Jewish quarter.
