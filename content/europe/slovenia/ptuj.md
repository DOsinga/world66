Ptuj is the oldest town in Slovenia, with town rights dating back to 977, and it wears that age well. The compact historic centre clusters beneath a hilltop castle and is filled with the kind of detail that rewards slow walking: carved stone doorways, wrought-iron window grills, and patrician houses lining quiet streets.

[Ptuj Castle](/europe/slovenia/ptuj/ptuj_castle) houses a museum with an excellent collection of musical instruments and period rooms, and a striking display of Kurent carnival costumes. Below it, the [Dominican Monastery](/europe/slovenia/ptuj/dominican_monastery) and the Provost Church anchor the old town, and the [vaulted wine cellars](/europe/slovenia/ptuj/ptuj_wine_cellars) beneath the city store vintages from a tradition that stretches back more than seven centuries. In the main square, the [Orpheus Monument](/europe/slovenia/ptuj/orpheus_monument) — a Roman marble stele that has stood here since the 2nd century — is one of the best-preserved pieces of Roman public art in Slovenia.

Ptuj is also home to [Kurentovanje](/europe/slovenia/ptuj/kurentovanje), the pre-Lenten carnival held in February. Locals dress in sheepskin costumes with elaborate masks and cowbells to chase away winter — it is one of the most distinctive folk traditions in Europe and draws crowds from across Slovenia.
