The Soča Valley runs southwest through the Julian Alps from the Vršič Pass to the Italian border, carrying one of the most beautiful rivers in Europe. The Soča (Isonzo in Italian) is famous for its colour — an astonishing turquoise-green, as if someone had poured glacial melt into a tropical sea. It has been photographed, painted, and described in superlatives for two centuries, and the reality still surprises first-time visitors.

The valley is divided into two distinct zones. The upper valley, centred on [Bovec](/europe/slovenia/bovec), is the adventure-sports heartland: white-water kayaking and rafting on the Soča, canyoning in its side gorges, and paragliding from the slopes above. The town of [Kobarid](/europe/slovenia/socavalley/kobarid) — known to the wider world as Caporetto, site of one of the decisive battles of the First World War — anchors the lower valley, where the river broadens and the gorge walls relax into vine terraces and apple orchards.

The military history is inescapable. From 1915 to 1917, the Soča Front was among the most brutal theatres of the First World War, with Italian and Austro-Hungarian forces fighting over these same valleys and ridges. Ernest Hemingway served as an ambulance driver here — his experience on the Isonzo Front became *A Farewell to Arms*. The [Kobarid Museum](/europe/slovenia/socavalley/kobarid_museum) is one of the finest First World War museums in Europe, and the Italian Charnel House above the town holds the remains of thousands of soldiers.

The natural setting rewards those who slow down. The [Soča Trail](/europe/slovenia/socavalley/soca_trail) follows the river from its source to the Italian border on a marked path. The [Boka Waterfall](/europe/slovenia/socavalley/boka_waterfall) — the highest in Slovenia — crashes 144 metres into a gorge near Bovec. The [Kluže Fortress](/europe/slovenia/socavalley/kluzhe_fortress), built across a narrow defile to control the approach from the north, is a perfectly preserved piece of Habsburg military engineering.
