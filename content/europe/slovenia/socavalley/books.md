**A Farewell to Arms** by Ernest Hemingway (1929). Hemingway drove ambulances on the Isonzo Front in 1918 and drew directly on that experience for this novel. Lieutenant Henry's retreat from Caporetto — the Battle of Kobarid — is one of the most vivid pieces of writing about the chaos of military collapse. Reading it before visiting makes the landscape and the Kobarid Museum resonate very differently.

**The White War: Life and Death on the Italian Front 1915–1919** by Mark Thompson (2008). A detailed and unsparing history of the Italian campaign on the Soča/Isonzo, covering the eleven Battles of the Isonzo in sequence. Thompson's research in Italian and Austro-Hungarian sources makes this the most thorough account in English of what happened in this valley.

**Alamut** by Vladimir Bartol (1938). Not directly about the Soča Valley, but this Slovenian novel — a fictional account of the Assassins of medieval Persia — is one of the most significant works of Slovenian literature and worth reading to understand something of the Slovenian intellectual tradition.
