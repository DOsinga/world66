Almería is the dry, bright eastern edge of Andalucía, a port city where the old Moorish frontier, a working harbour, and the desert landscapes of Cabo de Gata all meet. It is less polished than [Granada](/europe/spain/andalucia/granada) or [Seville](/europe/spain/andalucia/seville), and that is part of the appeal: the city still feels local, sun-baked, and faintly maritime rather than dressed for visitors.

The [Alcazaba](/europe/spain/andalucia/almeria/alcazaba) is the great reason to come. Its walls climb the hill above the old quarter, giving wide views over the port, the cathedral roofs, and the bare sierras beyond. Below it, the [Cathedral of Almería](/europe/spain/andalucia/almeria/cathedral) looks almost like a fortress because it was built in an age when corsair raids were a real threat. That defensive edge gives the centre a character you do not find in softer Andalusian towns.

Almería also has a modern, industrial grace. The restored [Cable Inglés](/europe/spain/andalucia/almeria/cable_ingles), once used to load iron ore directly onto ships, now stands as a skeletal landmark beside the water. Inland from the promenade, the [Civil War Shelters](/europe/spain/andalucia/almeria/civil_war_shelters) are a sobering counterpoint to all the sunshine: tunnels dug under the city during the Spanish Civil War.

Use Almería as a base if you want beaches without resort gloss, tapas without fuss, and day trips into the lunar hills of the province. [Almerimar](/europe/spain/andalucia/almerimar) lies west along the coast, while the wilder beaches and volcanic headlands of Cabo de Gata pull most strongly to the east.
