Almerimar is a purpose-built beach resort on the Costa de Almería, developed in the 1970s around a large marina about 30 kilometres west of [Almería](/europe/spain/andalucia/almeria) city. It is not the Spain of whitewashed villages and Moorish heritage — it is a deliberately constructed leisure development, with wide grid streets, golf courses, and a marina full of leisure craft. What it offers is equally deliberate: reliable sunshine, a good Blue Flag beach, and a calm that package resort towns elsewhere have long since traded away.

The [marina](/europe/spain/andalucia/almerimar/marina) is the resort's actual heart. Several hundred boats are moored here year-round, and the restaurants and bars around the harbour cater to a mix of European yacht crews and land-based visitors who prefer the marina atmosphere to the beach. Windsurfing and kitesurfing are the sports that bring serious practitioners — the consistent levante wind that blows down the coast makes Almerimar one of the better wind sport locations in southern Spain.

A few kilometres west along the coast, the [Castillo de Guardias Viejas](/europe/spain/andalucia/almerimar/castillo_guardias_viejas) stands on a headland above the sea: a 16th-century watchtower and fortress built to watch for Barbary Coast pirates. The interior is mostly gone but the structure remains, and the walk out to the headland along the coast gives the best views in the area.
