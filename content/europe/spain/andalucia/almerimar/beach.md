story: The Costa de Almería receives less annual rainfall than any other part of mainland Spain and more sunshine hours than virtually anywhere else in Europe — the semi-arid climate created by the Sierra de los Filabres to the north blocking Atlantic weather systems from the inland basin. The Almerimar coast gets around 320 sunny days a year, which accounts for both the thriving horticultural industry in the plastic greenhouses to the north and the beach resort that exploits the same conditions.

Almerimar's Blue Flag beach stretches along the coast east of the marina: a straight, sandy beach backed by the resort's low-rise apartment and hotel development, sheltered enough to be swimmable for most of the long season that runs from May through October. The water is clean, the sea floor is sand rather than rock, and the Mediterranean here is warmer than on Spain's Atlantic coast. In July and August the beach is busy by the standards of this relatively low-key resort; in June and September it is noticeably quieter.

The levante wind that makes Almerimar attractive to windsurfers and kitesurfers can also make the beach uncomfortable on days when it blows strongly. The west end of the beach, closer to the marina, is better sheltered from the east wind; the east end tends to get the full force of the levante but has cleaner water away from the marina entrance. The beach is free, has Blue Flag facilities (showers, lifeguards in season, accessible ramps), and is the right length — about 1.5 kilometres — for a proper morning walk.
