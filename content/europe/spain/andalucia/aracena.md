Aracena is a handsome hill town in the Sierra de Aracena, the western end of the Sierra Morena in northern [Andalucia](/europe/spain/andalucia). It sits at the highest point of the range, crowned by the remains of a Moorish castle and the [Iglesia Prioral](/europe/spain/andalucia/aracena/iglesia_prioral), a 13th-century church built by the Knights of Santiago with views across the forested hills.

The main attraction is the [Gruta de las Maravillas](/europe/spain/andalucia/aracena/gruta_de_las_maravillas), a spectacular cave system beneath the castle hill and one of the largest in Spain. Guided tours run throughout the day and take you through chambers of stalactites, underground lakes, and dramatic rock formations. It is genuinely impressive.

The [Sierra de Aracena walking trails](/europe/spain/andalucia/aracena/sierra_walks) are among the best in Andalucia — shaded cork and chestnut forest, old mule tracks between whitewashed villages, and a cooler climate than the lowlands below. The *jamón ibérico de bellota* produced here — from acorn-fed black pigs — is considered the finest ham in Spain, and the village of Jabugo nearby is its spiritual home. Wild mushrooms in autumn and white asparagus in spring round out a cuisine that is simple, seasonal, and excellent. The surrounding villages of Almonaster la Real, with its hilltop mosque, and Cortegana are well worth a day trip.
