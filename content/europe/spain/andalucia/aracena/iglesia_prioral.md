The castle and Prioral Church crown the highest point in Aracena, visible from the surrounding countryside for kilometres. The church was built by the Knights of Santiago beginning in the 13th century, making this hilltop a continuous site of religious significance for Moorish, Christian, and earlier populations.

The climb to the top rewards with views across the cork oak and chestnut forests of the Sierra de Aracena in every direction. The castle ruins alongside the church are fragmentary but evocative — stone arches and wall sections draped with wildflowers in spring. The combination of hilltop fortress, medieval church, and cave entrance at its feet makes the centre of Aracena more layered with history than most small Spanish towns.
