Conil de la Frontera is one of the most enjoyable beach towns on the Atlantic coast of Andalucia — less self-conscious than Zahara, less overrun than Nerja, and far more Spanish in character than anything on the Costa del Sol. It sits on the [Costa de la Luz](/europe/spain/andalucia) between [Cádiz](/europe/spain/andalucia/cadiz) and [Tarifa](/europe/spain/andalucia/tarifa), where the Atlantic coast is wide and exposed and the beaches are long, pale, and regularly cleaned by the southwest wind.

The [old town (Barrio de la Villa)](/europe/spain/andalucia/conil/barrio_de_la_villa) is worth taking seriously. It was built inside a medieval wall, entered through the [Arco de la Villa](/europe/spain/andalucia/conil/arco_de_la_villa), with the [Torre de Guzmán](/europe/spain/andalucia/conil/torre_de_guzman) at its heart — a 14th-century watchtower built to watch for Barbary pirates. The lanes inside the walls are whitewashed, narrow, and largely pedestrian-free in the evening, when the tapas bars fill up and the town shows its better side.

The town's most important culinary event is the almadraba tuna season, which runs from late April through June. The [almadraba](/europe/spain/andalucia/conil/almadraba_tuna_fishing) is a traditional trap-fishing technique, Phoenician in origin, that captures Atlantic bluefin tuna during their spring migration through the Strait of Gibraltar. The fish sold in Conil's [market](/europe/spain/andalucia/conil/mercado_de_abastos) and served in its restaurants during these weeks is genuinely exceptional — one of the better arguments for timing a visit carefully.

[Playa de los Bateles](/europe/spain/andalucia/conil/playa_de_los_bateles), the main town beach, is wide enough to absorb summer crowds without feeling claustrophobic. The northern coves — particularly [Cala del Aceite](/europe/spain/andalucia/conil/cala_del_aceite) — are smaller and more intimate. South along the coast, [El Palmar](/europe/spain/andalucia/conil/el_palmar_beach) is the nearest surf beach, and [Cabo de Trafalgar](/europe/spain/andalucia/conil/cabo_de_trafalgar) is where Nelson died in 1805 — a windswept headland with a lighthouse and a long, excellent beach to the north of the cape. None of this requires a car, but a car makes the beaches and the [day trips](/europe/spain/andalucia/conil/day_trips) considerably easier.
