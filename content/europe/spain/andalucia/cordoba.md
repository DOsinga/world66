At its peak in the 10th century, Córdoba was the largest city in western Europe — the capital of the Umayyad caliphate, dubbed the "Athens of the West" by contemporaries who marvelled at its libraries, running water, and religious coexistence. The city's modern population is a fraction of its medieval size, but the historic centre is still one of the finest in Andalusia.

The [Mezquita-Catedral](/europe/spain/andalucia/cordoba/mezquita_catedral) is the centrepiece — an extraordinary 8th-century mosque where a Gothic cathedral was rammed into the heart of the prayer hall in the 16th century, provoking Charles V to famously condemn his own bishops. Around it, the [Judería](/europe/spain/andalucia/cordoba/juderia) (Jewish Quarter) spills through whitewashed lanes and flower-hung patios, with the tiny medieval [Synagogue](/europe/spain/andalucia/cordoba/synagogue) and the birthplace of the philosopher Maimonides. The [Alcázar de los Reyes Cristianos](/europe/spain/andalucia/cordoba/alcazar), with its long reflecting pools and tiered gardens, is where Columbus pitched his westward voyage — and was turned down.

Eight kilometres west of the city, the ruins of [Medina Azahara](/europe/spain/andalucia/cordoba/medina_azahara) are among the most atmospheric in Spain — a UNESCO-listed palace city that rose and fell within a single century. Cross the [Roman Bridge](/europe/spain/andalucia/cordoba/roman_bridge) at sunset, eat [salmorejo](eating_out) in a tiled bodega, and let the pace slow down. Córdoba rewards those who stay more than a day.
