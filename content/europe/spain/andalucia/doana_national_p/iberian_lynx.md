Doñana is one of the two original strongholds of the Iberian lynx, a spotted, tufted-eared wild cat found nowhere else in the world. At the species' lowest point in the early 2000s, fewer than 100 individuals survived, split between Doñana and the Sierra Morena in Jaén. The park's El Acebuche breeding centre became the heart of a captive breeding and reintroduction programme that is now widely cited as a model for large carnivore conservation. Cubs born in captivity were released across southern Spain and Portugal, establishing new populations in areas where the lynx had been locally extinct for decades.

Seeing a wild lynx is genuinely difficult — they are crepuscular, secretive, and present in low densities even where the population is recovering. Your best chance is at dawn or dusk near the scrubland and cork oak forest at the park's northern edge, particularly in winter when vegetation is thinner. Guides on the official 4x4 tours have the best eyes and the most up-to-date knowledge of where individuals have been spotted. Even without a sighting, the story of the lynx's recovery is woven into any visit to Doñana — the park's visitor centres cover it in detail, and the signage makes clear that the restrictions on access exist, in part, to protect exactly this kind of fragile recovery.
