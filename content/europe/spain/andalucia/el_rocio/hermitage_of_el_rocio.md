The Hermitage of El Rocío is the reason the village exists. It houses the Virgen del Rocío — Our Lady of the Dew — a small medieval figure of the Virgin that has been venerated here since the thirteenth century, when legend says a shepherd discovered the image in a hollow tree in the marshes. The current whitewashed baroque church was rebuilt in the late twentieth century after the previous one fell into disrepair, but the setting, right at the edge of the Doñana marshes with flamingos visible in the lagoon beyond, remains otherworldly.

At almost any hour of the day you will find people inside praying, some with a remarkable intensity. The image is kept behind glass in a silver camarin and is only removed for the romería itself. The hermitage is free to enter and worth visiting even outside the pilgrimage period — the devout atmosphere and the natural backdrop give the place a genuine mystical weight that is rare in modern Andalusia.
