**Tales of the Alhambra** by Washington Irving (1832) is the book that made Granada famous to the English-speaking world. Irving spent several months living inside the Alhambra — then partly inhabited, partly ruined — and wrote a collection of stories and sketches about Moorish Spain. Romantically embroidered but historically grounded, it is still the best introduction to the palace complex and the city.

**The Poet in New York** (*Poeta en Nueva York*) by Federico García Lorca. Lorca was Granada-born and his poetry is inseparable from the city's landscape, light, and flamenco culture. This collection, written during his time in New York in 1929–30, is his most experimental work — a useful counterpoint to the romanticised Granada that Irving presents.

**Blood Wedding** (*Bodas de Sangre*) by Federico García Lorca. His most performed play, drawn from a real murder that happened in the Almería countryside in 1928. The Andalucían landscape, the code of honour, the heat — all of it is recognisably Granadan.

**The Hand of Fatima** (*La mano de Fátima*) by Ildefonso Falcones. A long, detailed historical novel set during the Moorish uprising of 1568–71 in the Alpujarras mountains south of Granada. Dense but rewarding for understanding the deep roots of the city's Moorish-Christian conflict.
