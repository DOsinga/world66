El Bañuelo is one of the best-preserved medieval hammams in Spain, and seeing it makes the later Arabic Bath Al Andalus feel modern by comparison. Built in the 11th or 12th century, the bathhouse has survived largely intact on the Carrera del Darro, the atmospheric riverside street at the base of the Albaicín. The star-shaped skylights in the vaulted ceilings are the defining visual — shafts of light cut through the dimness in a way that makes the space feel designed for contemplation as much as bathing.

The rooms run through the typical hot-warm-cold sequence of an Islamic hammam. The columns supporting the arches are a miscellany of Visigothic, Roman, and Nasrid capitals recycled from earlier buildings — a reminder of how layered Granada's history really is.
