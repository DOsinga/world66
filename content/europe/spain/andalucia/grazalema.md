Grazalema is one of the most picturesque white towns of Andalucía, perched at 825m in the [Sierra de Grazalema Natural Park](/europe/spain/andalucia/grazalema/sierra_de_grazalema_natural_park) about 120km east of Cádiz. The village is a maze of narrow whitewashed streets, baroque churches, and flower-filled balconies, focused on the [Plaza de España](/europe/spain/andalucia/grazalema/plaza_de_espana) and the [Iglesia de Nuestra Señora de la Aurora](/europe/spain/andalucia/grazalema/iglesia_de_la_aurora). It also receives more rain than anywhere else in Spain, which explains why the surrounding mountains are so dramatically lush.

The Natural Park is the main reason to linger. The [Pinsapar](/europe/spain/andalucia/grazalema/pinsapar) — a protected forest of Spanish fir on the slopes above the village — is one of only two remaining stands of this ancient species in Spain; access requires a permit from the park office. Other walking routes fan out across the sierra: the [Garganta Verde](/europe/spain/andalucia/grazalema/garganta_verde) gorge, the climb to [El Torreón](/europe/spain/andalucia/grazalema/torreon), the dramatic rock outcrops of the [Salto de Cabrero](/europe/spain/andalucia/grazalema/salto_de_cabrero). Vultures nest in spring and some walks are restricted during the nesting season.

The road from Grazalema to Zahara climbs to the [Puerto de las Palomas](/europe/spain/andalucia/grazalema/puerto_de_las_palomas) at 1,350m — the second highest pass in Andalucía — with extraordinary views in both directions. Don’t miss the local version of gazpacho serrano, a thick mountain soup served at the restaurants on the main square.
