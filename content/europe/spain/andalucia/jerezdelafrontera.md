Jerez de la Frontera is the sherry capital of the world. The town in southern Andalucía gave its name to the fortified wine — sherry is simply the English corruption of Jerez — and the great bodegas that line the streets around the old town still produce the fino, manzanilla, amontillado, and oloroso that made the city wealthy. Tours and tastings at bodegas like González Byass (home of Tío Pepe) and Sandeman are among the best free-flowing afternoons in Spain.

The city is also the home of Spanish flamenco. Jerez claims, with some justice, to have a deeper and more authentic flamenco tradition than Seville or anywhere else, and the tablaos and peñas here draw serious enthusiasts rather than tourists. The Barrio de Santiago, north of the old town, is the historic gypsy quarter where the art form took root. The Museo de Arte Flamenco in the Palacio de Pemartín holds a collection of instruments, costumes, and documents. Horse culture is the third pillar of Jerez identity: the Real Escuela Andaluza del Arte Ecuestre (Royal Andalusian School of Equestrian Art) puts on spectacular displays of classical dressage in their arena.

The old town itself has fine monuments. The [Cathedral of San Salvador](/europe/spain/andalucia/jerezdelafrontera/cathedral_san_salvador) was built over a mosque and retains a Gothic body with a later baroque tower. The Alcázar, a Moorish fortress, is well preserved and contains a small mosque and an eighteenth-century palace. Jerez is easily reached by train from [Seville](/europe/spain/andalucia/seville) (about an hour) or [Cádiz](/europe/spain/andalucia/cadiz) (30 minutes). Jerez airport has budget flights from several northern European cities.
