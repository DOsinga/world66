story: >
  The walls of Marbella were built in the 10th century by the Moorish taifa of Málaga to protect a small but strategically placed settlement on the coast. The walls survived the Christian Reconquista in 1485 — when the city surrendered peacefully to Fernando and Isabella, the Catholic Monarchs — and remained largely intact until the 19th century, when urban expansion led to the demolition of most of the circuit. The remaining sections are among the best-preserved Moorish urban fortifications on the Costa del Sol.
---

Sections of the 10th-century Moorish defensive walls that once encircled the old town of Marbella, preserved in the streets around the historic centre. The most complete stretch runs along the northern edge of the casco antiguo, rising above a small garden. The walls are squat and thick, built of local rubble masonry, and give a clear sense of the original compact shape of the medieval town.

Worth a few minutes' detour from the Plaza de los Naranjos. Look for the Torre del Moro, a surviving watch tower, and the gate opening that once marked the main entrance from the north. Free to view from the street at all times.
