Ronda is split in half by a sheer river gorge that drops 120 metres — one of the most dramatic townscapes in Spain. The [Puente Nuevo](/europe/spain/andalucia/ronda/puente_nuevo), a spectacular arched bridge spanning the chasm, connects the old Moorish town to the newer districts and offers dizzying views of the valley below. The gorge walls are near-vertical, with the white houses of the old city perched right at the edge.

The white-washed old town (La Ciudad) has a relaxed atmosphere, a welcome contrast to the tourist-heavy coast. Ronda's [Plaza de Toros](/europe/spain/andalucia/ronda/plaza_de_toros) is one of the oldest bullrings in Spain and the birthplace of modern bullfighting as a pedestrian art. The [Palacio de Mondragón](/europe/spain/andalucia/ronda/palacio_mondragon) — a former Moorish palace — now houses the city museum and has the best gorge-edge garden terrace in the city. The well-preserved [Arab Baths](/europe/spain/andalucia/ronda/arab_baths) below the old town are among the finest Moorish bathhouses in Andalusia.

The gorge itself is excellent for walking — paths descend from the [Down the Gorge](/europe/spain/andalucia/ronda/downthegorge) trail below the Mondragón, with views up to the bridge and across the surrounding sierra. The [Museo Lara](/europe/spain/andalucia/ronda/museo_lara), a private collection of clocks, weapons, and witchcraft artefacts, is an oddity well worth an hour.

Ronda is easy to reach by train from Málaga or from Madrid, making it a good day trip from the Costa del Sol. But it truly rewards an overnight stay — the Parador, perched right on the gorge edge, has one of the most extraordinary positions of any hotel in Spain.
