The Arab Baths (Baños Árabes) at the foot of the gorge are among the best-preserved Moorish bathhouses in Spain. Built in the thirteenth century, the complex follows the standard hammam layout of three rooms at different temperatures — cold, warm, and hot — with star-shaped skylights cut through the vaulted ceilings to allow light and steam to circulate. The structure survived nearly intact because it was buried under later construction rather than demolished.

The baths were rediscovered and excavated in the twentieth century and are now open to visitors. The site is tucked below the main town, near the old city gate, and most visitors pass it without noticing. The horseshoe arches and the brick vaulting are in excellent condition for their age.
