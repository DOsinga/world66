Setenil de las Bodegas is one of the most unusual of Andalucia's white villages — a town built into and under the overhanging rock of a gorge, with streets running beneath natural rock ledges that serve as roofs for the houses below. The effect is striking: two-storey buildings with rock faces for walls and stone canopies jutting out overhead, casting the streets into permanent shadow.

The gorge was carved by the Trejo river, and the caves within it have been inhabited since prehistoric times. The medieval castle and tower above the town were held by the Moors until 1484, when Ferdinand and Isabella finally took it after seven separate sieges — the [Castillo de Setenil](/europe/spain/andalucia/setenil/castillo_de_setenil) held out long enough that its fall effectively marked the end of Moorish resistance in western Andalucia. The [Aljibe Árabe](/europe/spain/andalucia/setenil/aljibe_arabe), the Arab cistern near the castle, supplied the garrison through all those sieges.

The two cave streets are what most visitors come for. [Calle Cuevas del Sol](/europe/spain/andalucia/setenil/calle_cuevas_del_sol) runs along the south bank in relative light, lined with tapas bars whose tables spill out under the overhang. [Calle Cuevas de la Sombra](/europe/spain/andalucia/setenil/calle_cuevas_de_la_sombra) on the north bank is darker and more enclosed, the rock pressing closer overhead. The [Mirador del Carmen](/europe/spain/andalucia/setenil/mirador_del_carmen) above gives the best view of both streets and the gorge between them.

Simply walking the streets is the main activity here, and the friendly atmosphere makes it worth an overnight stay if you want to experience the village after the day-trippers have left. [Ronda](/europe/spain/andalucia/ronda) is 20 km to the southwest — a sensible base if you are exploring this part of the Serranía.
