The Museo Arqueologico de Sevilla gives depth to a city better known for Moorish palaces, churches, and flamenco. Its collections reach back through prehistoric, Tartessian, Roman, and Islamic layers, with important material from nearby Italica anchoring Seville in a much longer regional story.

It is a quieter, more scholarly stop than the Alcazar or cathedral, and that is part of its appeal. Pair it with Plaza de Espana and Maria Luisa Park, then use the museum to understand why Seville was important long before it became the showpiece of imperial Spain.
