---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

The Costa del Sol earns its name — Torremolinos gets over 320 sunny days a year, and even January is mild enough for outdoor café sitting in a light jacket. The real question is not whether the weather will cooperate, but how crowded you want the beaches.

**Spring (March–May)** is the best time for exploring. Temperatures climb from the mid-teens to low twenties Celsius, the sea is still cold for swimming, and the resort crowds have not arrived. Hotel prices are lower and the town still feels like a place that real people live in.

**Summer (June–August)** is hot, busy, and expensive. July and August see temperatures regularly above 30°C, and the beaches pack out. If you do visit in summer, arrive at the beach before 10am or after 5pm, stick to La Carihuela for eating, and accept that the pace will be frantic.

**Autumn (September–October)** is nearly as good as spring — the sea is warm from three months of sun, the crowds thin after mid-September, and the evenings are pleasant. October can bring short sharp storms, but they clear quickly.

**Winter (November–February)** is genuinely mild compared to northern Europe. Rain is rare, daytime temperatures typically reach 17–20°C, and the town quietens down to something more like its year-round self.

The **Verbena de la Virgen del Carmen**, held in July in La Carihuela, is the most important local fiesta and worth timing a visit around if you like street celebrations with free food and music.
