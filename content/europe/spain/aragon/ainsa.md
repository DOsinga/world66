Aínsa is one of the most beautiful medieval villages in the Spanish Pyrenees, perched on a hilltop above the confluence of the Cinca and Ara rivers in the Sobrarbe comarca of [Aragón](/europe/spain/aragon). The lower, modern part of town is unremarkable, but climb the hill and you arrive in one of the finest old quarters in northern Spain.

The [Plaza Mayor](/europe/spain/aragon/ainsa/plaza_mayor) is enormous — a vast, arcaded square of honey-coloured stone surrounded by cloister-like porticoes, with the [Romanesque church of Santa María](/europe/spain/aragon/ainsa/iglesia_santa_maria) at one end and the [castle](/europe/spain/aragon/ainsa/castillo) at the other. Two steep parallel lanes lead down from the square through the old town, and the views from the castle ramparts extend across the river valleys to the snow-capped peaks beyond. Aínsa makes a natural base for visiting the [Ordesa y Monte Perdido National Park](/europe/spain/aragon/ordesa_national_park), one of the great mountain parks of Europe, which lies a short drive to the north. The [Cañón del Añisclo](/europe/spain/aragon/ainsa/canon_anisclo) and [Valle de Pineta](/europe/spain/aragon/ainsa/valle_de_pineta) — two of the park's four main valleys — are both more easily reached from Aínsa than from the northern approach via Torla.
