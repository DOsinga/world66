Huesca is a modest provincial capital sitting in the foothills of the Pyrenees, an easy stopover between Zaragoza and the mountain passes to the north. It's not a place most people put on their itinerary, but if you find yourself here you'll discover a well-preserved old town with real depth.

The [Cathedral](/europe/spain/aragon/huesca/huesca_cathedral), begun in the 13th century and embellished for several hundred years afterward, anchors the historic centre. The [Diocesan Museum](/europe/spain/aragon/huesca/diocesan_museum) next door holds altarpieces and murals rescued from churches that no longer exist. Nearby, the [Plaza de la Universidad](/europe/spain/aragon/huesca/plaza_de_la_universidad) is fronted by handsome 16th-century university buildings that now house the city's main museum.

The [Church of San Pedro el Viejo](/europe/spain/aragon/huesca/san_pedro_el_viejo) is arguably the most interesting stop: built on the site of an ancient temple, it contains the tombs of several Aragonese kings and has a Romanesque cloister that rewards a slow walk around. The [Casino de Huesca](/europe/spain/aragon/huesca/casino_huesca), a fine 1905 Modernista building on the main square, rounds out the architectural sights.

Huesca works well as a base for day trips into the Pyrenees — the town is calm and reasonably priced, the old quarter takes no more than an afternoon to explore, and the surrounding countryside is worth the detour.
