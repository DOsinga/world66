Sos del Rey Católico is a beautifully preserved medieval town in the Aragonese Pyrenean foothills, and one of the most handsome small towns in Spain. Its name commemorates Ferdinand II of Aragon — the Catholic Monarch — who was born here in 1452. Ferdinand later married Isabella of Castile, effectively uniting Spain, and together they sponsored Columbus's voyage to the Americas.

The town itself is a maze of narrow stone lanes, Romanesque churches, and arcaded squares that feel almost entirely unchanged from the Middle Ages. The [Iglesia de San Esteban](/europe/spain/aragon/sosdelreycatl/iglesia_de_san_esteban) — where Ferdinand was born — stands at the town's heart, while the [Plaza de la Villa](/europe/spain/aragon/sosdelreycatl/plaza_de_la_villa) below it provides a shaded square for a glass of wine after the walking. The [castle and walls](/europe/spain/aragon/sosdelreycatl/castle_and_walls) are mostly intact and walkable. It's a quiet place with few tourists and excellent local food — this is serious ham country, and the restaurants take their ingredients seriously. The [Parador de Sos](/europe/spain/aragon/sosdelreycatl/parador_de_sos) is the obvious place to stay if you're spending a night, as you should be if you're traveling between Zaragoza and Pamplona.
