Teruel Cathedral is a fascinating accumulation of architectural styles built up over centuries. The original Mudéjar tower, with its geometric brick patterning and ceramic tile inlays, dates to the 13th century and is the oldest of the city's four UNESCO-listed towers. The rest of the building was extended and altered through the Gothic period and beyond, but it is the interior that shocks.

The cathedral's painted Mudéjar ceiling is extraordinary — a vast wooden ceiling decorated in the 13th century with secular and religious scenes in vivid detail. It was whitewashed at some point and forgotten, rediscovered only in 1929. Entry to the cathedral includes access to a museum of religious art and the tower. Allow 45 minutes minimum.
