Zaragoza is the capital of Aragon and the fifth-largest city in Spain, sitting in the broad Ebro valley halfway between Madrid and Barcelona. It is emphatically Spanish — not much visited by foreign tourists, which is part of its appeal. The city has a compact historic centre with an exceptional density of monuments: Roman ruins, Moorish palaces, Mudéjar churches, and Baroque basilicas within walking distance of each other.

The heart of the city is the enormous [Plaza del Pilar](/europe/spain/aragon/zaragoza/plaza_del_pilar), flanked by the twin cathedrals — the vast Baroque [Basílica del Pilar](/europe/spain/aragon/zaragoza/basilica_del_pilar) and the architecturally richer [Catedral de la Seo](/europe/spain/aragon/zaragoza/catedraldelaseo). The [Aljafería Palace](/europe/spain/aragon/zaragoza/aljaferia_palace), a 10-minute walk west, is the finest Moorish palace outside Andalucía and is frequently overlooked. Underground, the [Roman Theatre](/europe/spain/aragon/zaragoza/roman_theatre) and the other excavated sites of Roman Caesaraugusta offer a well-presented window into the city's ancient past.

Zaragoza is also Goya country. The painter was born in a village 44km south, and the [Museo Camón Aznar](/europe/spain/aragon/zaragoza/camnaznar) holds his full print series. The [Museo de Zaragoza](/europe/spain/aragon/zaragoza/zaragoza_museum) has additional works, and the basilica itself contains two of his ceiling frescoes.

During the [Fiesta del Pilar](/europe/spain/aragon/zaragoza/when_to_go) (around 12 October) the city explodes into nine days of parades, folk costumes, and concerts. The rest of the year it is a lively but manageable city with excellent Aragonese food and a genuinely local atmosphere.
