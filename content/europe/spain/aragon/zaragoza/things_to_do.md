Zaragoza is an easy city to walk. The heart of it all is the [Plaza del Pilar](/europe/spain/aragon/zaragoza/plaza_del_pilar) — one of Europe's largest public squares — with the enormous [Basílica del Pilar](/europe/spain/aragon/zaragoza/basilica_del_pilar) at one end, the older [Catedral de la Seo](/europe/spain/aragon/zaragoza/catedraldelaseo) at the other, and [La Lonja](/europe/spain/aragon/zaragoza/lalonja) in between. The old town west of the basilica is a dense grid of medieval lanes and tapas bars.

Beyond the obvious, Zaragoza has Roman ruins in excavated sites including the [Roman Theatre](/europe/spain/aragon/zaragoza/roman_theatre), the Moorish [Aljafería Palace](/europe/spain/aragon/zaragoza/aljaferia_palace) on the western edge of the centre, and a string of Goya-related museums. Goya was born just outside the city: the [Museo Camón Aznar](/europe/spain/aragon/zaragoza/camnaznar) holds his full print series, and the [Museo de Zaragoza](/europe/spain/aragon/zaragoza/zaragoza_museum) has additional works. The sculptor Pablo Gargallo is celebrated at his own museum, the [Museo Pablo Gargallo](/europe/spain/aragon/zaragoza/museo_pablo_gargallo). The Mudéjar tower of [San Pablo church](/europe/spain/aragon/zaragoza/san_pablo_church) is a UNESCO World Heritage Site worth the short walk from the centre.
