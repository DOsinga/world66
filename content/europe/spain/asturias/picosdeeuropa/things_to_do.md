The Picos are a hiking and climbing destination above all else. The [Cares Gorge](/europe/spain/asturias/picosdeeuropa/cares_gorge) trail is the most famous walk — a spectacular path cut into the walls of a kilometre-deep gorge, 12 km end to end. For the best views without serious hiking, the [Fuente Dé cable car](/europe/spain/asturias/picosdeeuropa/fuente_de) rises 753 metres to the high plateau in minutes.

The [Covadonga sanctuary](/europe/spain/asturias/picosdeeuropa/covadonga) and the [glacial lakes above it](/europe/spain/asturias/picosdeeuropa/lagos_de_covadonga) form the most visited part of the western massif — accessible to all, beautiful, and genuinely impressive. For the full mountain experience, base yourself at [Sotres](/europe/spain/asturias/picosdeeuropa/sotres) or take the funicular to [Bulnes](/europe/spain/asturias/picosdeeuropa/bulnes) for access to the Central Massif interior and the approach to [Naranjo de Bulnes](/europe/spain/asturias/picosdeeuropa/naranjo_de_bulnes), Spain's most celebrated rock climbing peak. Year-round, the park's [wildlife](/europe/spain/asturias/picosdeeuropa/wildlife) rewards patient observers — chamois, eagles, vultures, and occasional encounters with bears or wolves in the remoter areas.
