S'Arenal (also known as El Arenal) is a beach resort on the southern coast of [Mallorca](/europe/spain/balearicislands/mallorca), straddling the boundary between the municipalities of Palma and Llucmajor. It sits at the eastern end of the [Platja de Palma](/europe/spain/balearicislands/mallorca/playa_de_palma), a wide sandy beach stretching some seven kilometres to Ca'n Pastilla, backed by a flat promenade ideal for walking and cycling.

The town is lively and unpretentious, with shops and restaurants at every price level. A large German tourist community has defined much of the character here, particularly along the [Ballermann strip](/europe/spain/balearicislands/mallorca/arenal/ballermann_strip) — a stretch of beach bars where oompah bands and litres of draft beer have been the soundtrack since the 1980s. The name itself comes from [Balneario 6](/europe/spain/balearicislands/mallorca/arenal/balneario_6), the original beach concession that became shorthand for this entire style of German package tourism. [Aqualand S'Arenal](/europe/spain/balearicislands/mallorca/arenal/aqualand_sarenal), on the outskirts of town, is one of the island's largest water parks and a reliable draw for families.

The [Club Nàutic S'Arenal](/europe/spain/balearicislands/mallorca/arenal/club_nautic_sarenal) at the marina end of the beach is the resort's quieter, more characterful corner — a sailing club with waterfront dining and views across the [Bay of Palma](/europe/spain/balearicislands/mallorca/arenal/bay_of_palma) toward the cathedral. [Palma Aquarium](/europe/spain/balearicislands/mallorca/arenal/palma_aquarium), a short bus ride away along the coast, is the best indoor attraction in this part of Mallorca.

Frequent buses run to [Palma](/europe/spain/balearicislands/mallorca/palma1) every ten minutes (about twenty-five minutes, around one euro), making S'Arenal a convenient and affordable base for day trips to the capital. There is also a limited service east to Manacor, Porto Cristo, and the Coves del Drac.
