The Castle of Capdepera sits on a rocky hill above the town of Capdepera, visible from much of the northeastern tip of Mallorca. Built largely in the fourteenth century, it is one of the best-preserved medieval fortifications on the island — a complete circuit of crenellated walls enclosing a chapel, watchtower, and the ruins of the governor's house.

The castle is about 4 km from Cala Ratjada — a pleasant drive or an easy cycle inland. From the ramparts there are expansive views across the cape to the sea in all directions, and on clear days you can make out the outline of Menorca to the north. The small village of Capdepera at the foot of the hill is quiet and well-preserved, with a few cafes and the typical whitewashed houses of inland Mallorca. Entry to the castle is cheap and it's rarely crowded.
