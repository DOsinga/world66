Magaluf sits on the southwest coast of [Mallorca](/europe/spain/balearicislands/mallorca), about 15 kilometres from [Palma](/europe/spain/balearicislands/mallorca/palmademallorca). Its reputation as a package-holiday party strip is well earned — the [Punta Ballena strip](/europe/spain/balearicislands/mallorca/magaluf/punta_ballena_strip) of bars and clubs is loud, cheap, and aimed squarely at young British and German holidaymakers. If that is not your scene, you have been warned.

That said, [the beach](/europe/spain/balearicislands/mallorca/magaluf/magaluf_beach) itself is genuinely good — a long, wide arc of fine sand with calm, shallow water. The recent renovation of the seafront promenade has improved things considerably, and away from the strip, Magaluf is slowly reinventing itself with better hotels and the [Western Water Park](/europe/spain/balearicislands/mallorca/magaluf/western_water_park) that keeps families entertained.

Beyond the resort, the coastline to the south is worth exploring. The headland walk to the old watchtower at [Cap de Cala Figuera](/europe/spain/balearicislands/mallorca/magaluf/cap_de_cala_figuera) takes about 20 minutes and gives views over the Bay of Palma. The small rocky island of [Illa de sa Porrassa](/europe/spain/balearicislands/mallorca/magaluf/illa_de_sa_porrassa) sits just off the southern bay and is reachable by kayak or paddleboard. The neighbouring resort of [Palma Nova](/europe/spain/balearicislands/mallorca/magaluf/palma_nova) is a calmer alternative a short walk up the coast.
