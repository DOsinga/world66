Pollença is a small inland town in the northeastern corner of Mallorca, set at the foot of the Tramuntana mountains. It has the feel of a genuinely lived-in Mallorcan town rather than a resort — cobbled streets, a [Roman bridge](/europe/spain/balearicislands/mallorca/pollensa/roman_bridge), a large [plane-tree-shaded main square](/europe/spain/balearicislands/mallorca/pollensa/placa_major), and a weekly Sunday market that attracts both locals and visitors.

The town's most striking feature is the [Calvari staircase](/europe/spain/balearicislands/mallorca/pollensa/calvari_staircase): 365 steps climbing to a hilltop chapel with views over the valley and the surrounding mountains. The climb is steep but worth it, especially in the early morning. The old monastery of [Puig de Maria](/europe/spain/balearicislands/mallorca/pollensa/puig_de_maria) sits on another hilltop above the town, accessible on foot or by car, with a small hostel at the top.

The beach resort of [Port de Pollença](/europe/spain/balearicislands/mallorca/pollensa/port_de_pollensa) is 6 km away and has a long arc of sandy beach along a protected bay. The [Cap de Formentor](/europe/spain/balearicislands/mallorca/pollensa/cap_de_formentor), the dramatic headland at the tip of the peninsula, is a 20 km drive from town and offers some of the most spectacular coastal scenery in the Balearics.
