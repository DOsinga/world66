---
title: Beaches
type: section
---

Porto Cristo's own beach sits at the innermost point of the harbour inlet, sheltered by the rocky headlands on either side. It's a compact stretch of sand — not a place for solitude in high summer — but the calm, shallow water makes it ideal for families, and the setting, ringed by old buildings climbing the hillside, has real charm. The promenade runs right alongside, so stepping off the beach for a coffee or an ice cream takes no effort at all.

The real coastal riches lie a few kilometres south. Cala Romantica (also called Cala Anguila on some maps) is a pair of beautiful coves separated by a low headland, fringed with pine trees that shade the path down to the water. The sea here is a vivid turquoise, the sand coarse and golden, and the atmosphere a notch quieter than Porto Cristo itself. Cala Anguila and Cala Magrana, tucked further into the same stretch of coast, are smaller still and can feel almost private if you arrive early or out of season. None of these coves has much in the way of facilities, so bring water and something to eat. Access is by car or bicycle along the coastal road, and most have a small car park a short walk from the water.
