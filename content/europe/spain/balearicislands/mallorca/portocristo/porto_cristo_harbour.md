Porto Cristo owes its existence to its harbour — a deep, narrow inlet formed where a river once cut through the coastal limestone, creating one of the most naturally sheltered anchorages on Mallorca's east coast. The Romans knew it; there are remains of a Roman port at the site of the current quay. Today the inlet is lined with pleasure boats and working fishing vessels, the smell of salt and diesel mixing on the breeze in a way that still feels genuinely maritime rather than merely decorative.

The Passeig Moll, the esplanade that curves along the western shore, is the social centre of the town. In the evening especially it fills with locals and visitors alike — families with children feeding the ducks, couples occupying café tables, fishermen tinkering with gear. The old part of Porto Cristo climbs the hillside above the harbour in tight streets of whitewashed houses, and from the higher points there are excellent views back down over the inlet and out to sea. It is a good place to spend an hour doing nothing in particular.
