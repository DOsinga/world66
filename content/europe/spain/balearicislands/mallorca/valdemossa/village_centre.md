Valldemossa's village centre is small enough to walk in twenty minutes, but deserves longer. The steep stone lanes connect flower-draped courtyards, a Baroque parish church, and views south and east across terraced olive groves to the sea. Many houses still follow the traditional Mallorcan pattern: thick walls, deep-set windows, and an inner patio where a lemon tree takes up all the light.

The village is genuinely lived-in rather than just a tourist set-piece. The local pa amb oli bars are the best evidence of this — a round of flatbread rubbed with tomato and olive oil, topped with jamón or cheese, remains the staple lunch of anyone who lives here. The Sunday market draws produce from nearby farms. Early morning, before the tour coaches arrive from Palma, is when Valldemossa is at its most itself.
