The Museu Diocesà de Menorca occupies a former convent adjacent to the cathedral and holds one of the most varied collections on the island. Its range is genuinely eclectic — religious art from the diocese, prehistoric artefacts including Talayotic bronzes and ceramics, natural history specimens, and a collection of historical documents and maps. The building itself, with its cloister, is one of the more pleasant museum spaces in Menorca.

The prehistoric section is particularly worth seeing: the bronze figurines from Talayotic culture sites around the island are remarkable, small human and animal figures of considerable refinement for their period. The religious art spans several centuries and gives a fair picture of the quality of craftsmanship that the island's churches once housed. Not all of it is ecclesiastical high art, but the accumulated effect of the collection is to give Menorca a deeper historical texture than the beaches-and-boats version of the island suggests.
