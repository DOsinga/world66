Bilbao is the largest city in the Basque Country and the industrial heart of northern Spain — a gritty port on the Nervión river that spent most of the 20th century producing steel and shipping it out to the world. Then, in 1997, Frank Gehry's [Guggenheim Museum Bilbao](/europe/spain/basque_country_euskadi/bilbao/guggenheim_museum) opened on the waterfront, and the city changed overnight. The building — a swirling, titanium-clad explosion that looks different from every angle and shimmers like a fish in the rain — is genuinely one of the most important pieces of architecture built in the last fifty years, and the art inside (large-scale contemporary installations, with a permanent Richard Serra room that justifies the trip on its own) matches the exterior's ambition.

But Bilbao is not just the Guggenheim. The city has used the museum as a catalyst for a broader transformation that has turned the whole riverfront from derelict docks into a handsome promenade of parks, bridges (including Santiago Calatrava's [Zubizuri footbridge](/europe/spain/basque_country_euskadi/bilbao/zubizuri)), and new buildings. The [Casco Viejo](/europe/spain/basque_country_euskadi/bilbao/casco_viejo) (old town), on the opposite bank, is a dense grid of narrow streets centred on the seven original calles (streets) of medieval Bilbao, full of [pintxo bars](/europe/spain/basque_country_euskadi/bilbao/pintxo_bars), small shops, and the 14th-century Gothic [Cathedral of Santiago](/europe/spain/basque_country_euskadi/bilbao/church_of_santiago). The covered [Mercado de la Ribera](/europe/spain/basque_country_euskadi/bilbao/mercado_de_la_ribera), the largest indoor market in Europe, sits on the riverbank at the edge of the old town and is worth a morning for the fresh produce, the pintxo counters, and the building itself.

The pintxos are the other essential Bilbao experience. The bars of the Casco Viejo — particularly along Calle Ledesma, Calle del Perro, and the Plaza Nueva — lay out elaborate small plates on the counter every evening, and the tradition is the same as in [San Sebastián](/europe/spain/basque_country_euskadi/sansebastian): walk from bar to bar, eat one or two at each, drink txakoli or a small beer (zurito), and keep moving. The food quality is exceptional. The [Museo de Bellas Artes](/europe/spain/basque_country_euskadi/bilbao/museo_de_bellas_artes) next to the [Doña Casilda park](/europe/spain/basque_country_euskadi/bilbao/dona_casilda_park) is often overlooked by Guggenheim visitors but holds a serious collection of Spanish Old Masters and Basque painters.

The [Bilbao Metro](/europe/spain/basque_country_euskadi/bilbao/bilbao_metro) is worth riding just for the architecture — the glass entrance canopies, nicknamed *fosteritos*, have become city icons in their own right. And the drive or bus ride from Bilbao to [San Sebastián](/europe/spain/basque_country_euskadi/sansebastian) along the Basque coast, past the fishing villages of Lekeitio, Getaria, and Zarautz, is one of the finest coastal roads in Spain.
