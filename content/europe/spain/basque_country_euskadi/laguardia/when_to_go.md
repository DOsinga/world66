---
title: When to Go
type: section
---

Laguardia sits in a natural hollow in the Sierra de Cantabria that traps warmth and shields the valley from the Atlantic weather that dominates further north. Summers are hot and dry — temperatures regularly reach 35°C in July and August — while winters are cold and clear, with frost common and occasional snow. Spring and autumn are the best seasons for visiting.

**Spring (April–June)** brings the vines back to life with fresh green growth across the slopes. Temperatures are pleasant (18–25°C), the light is good, and the village is busy but not overwhelmed. The wildflowers around the Lagunas de Laguardia are at their best in April and May.

**Autumn (September–October)** is the most dramatic time to visit. Harvest (vendimia) runs through September, when the bodegas are in full operation and the valley smells of fermenting grapes. The vines turn gold and red in October, making the landscape around the town particularly beautiful. Most wineries hold harvest events; check with Rioja Alavesa wine tourism bodies for dates.

**Summer (July–August)** is busy, hot, and crowded on weekends. The Fiestas de San Juan run in late June around the 24th, with medieval-themed events, txistulari (Basque flute) processions, and bonfires. Book accommodation well in advance for summer weekends.

**Winter (November–March)** is quiet and cold. Many restaurants reduce their hours and some close for weeks at a time. The village has a starkly beautiful quality in winter light, and the wineries are less crowded. Weekend visits can still work well if you check opening hours in advance.
