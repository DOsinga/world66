Vitoria-Gasteiz is the capital of the Basque Country and the seat of its regional government, but it remains remarkably under the tourist radar compared to Bilbao or San Sebastián. It's a pleasant, walkable city with a well-preserved [medieval old town](/europe/spain/basque_country_euskadi/vitoria/medieval_quarter), a ring of parks and green corridors — the [Anillo Verde](/europe/spain/basque_country_euskadi/vitoria/anillo_verde) — that earned it the title of European Green Capital in 2012, and a lively pintxos scene that doesn't cater to tourists so much as to the locals who actually live here.

The old city sits on a low hill, with its medieval streets radiating outward from the [Cathedral of Santa María](/europe/spain/basque_country_euskadi/vitoria/santa_maria_cathedral) — currently the subject of one of the most engaging restoration projects in Europe. The 19th-century expansion below is tidy and bourgeois, full of covered arcades and good bars, focused around the [Plaza de la Virgen Blanca](/europe/spain/basque_country_euskadi/vitoria/plaza_virgen_blanca) and the colonnaded [Plaza de España](/europe/spain/basque_country_euskadi/vitoria/plaza_espana). Spanish and Basque are both spoken; English less so than in Bilbao, which makes the city feel more genuinely Basque.

Time a visit for the [Jazz Festival](/europe/spain/basque_country_euskadi/vitoria/jazz_festival) in July or the patron-saint Fiestas de la Virgen Blanca in early August if you can — they are the loudest, best weeks of the year. Otherwise, plan on a couple of days of slow walks, gallery visits at [Artium](/europe/spain/basque_country_euskadi/vitoria/artium_museum), pintxos along [Calle Cuchillería](/europe/spain/basque_country_euskadi/vitoria/calle_cuchilleria), and a side trip into the [Salburua wetlands](/europe/spain/basque_country_euskadi/vitoria/salburua) on the edge of town.
