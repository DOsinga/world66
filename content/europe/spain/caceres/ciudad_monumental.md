The Ciudad Monumental is what makes Cáceres one of Spain's most remarkable cities. This walled upper town, declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1986, covers roughly a square kilometre and contains an extraordinary concentration of medieval towers, Gothic churches, and Renaissance palaces in a state of preservation that has few equals in Europe.

The main entrance is through the Arco de la Estrella, a 18th-century baroque arch cut through the medieval wall, which opens onto the Plaza de Santa María. From here, the streets radiate into a maze of flagstoned lanes lined with golden stone facades. The towers — originally built by rival noble families before the Catholic Monarchs ordered their tops demolished — are the visual signature of the city. The Torre de las Cigüeñas (Tower of the Storks) is the exception: it kept its battlements because the owner was on the right side of the political dispute.
