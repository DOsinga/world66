---
title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

Extremaduran food is among the most underrated in Spain. The region produces some of the country's best ibérico pork — acorn-fed black pigs from the dehesa — and the local cheesemakers produce torta del Casar, a rich, runny sheep's milk cheese that you eat by cutting off the top and scooping out the interior with bread. Migas (fried breadcrumbs with chorizo and peppers) is the quintessential local peasant dish, and it's delicious.

Tapas culture is alive in Cáceres: ordering a drink often brings a free tapa. The best area for tapas bars is around the Plaza Mayor and the streets leading into the old town. The old town itself has restaurants in atmospheric medieval settings, but the cooking is often mediocre — go for the atmosphere, eat for the food outside the walls.
