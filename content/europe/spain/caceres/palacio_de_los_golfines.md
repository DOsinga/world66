  The Golfines de Abajo palace served as the headquarters of Francisco Franco during several visits to Cáceres, and it was here in 1936 that he was proclaimed Generalísimo and Head of State of Nationalist Spain. A plaque commemorating this was removed in 2009 under Spain's historical memory laws. The building predates Franco by four centuries — it was one of the premier noble residences of 15th-century Extremadura, its facade a masterwork of late Gothic and Mudéjar carving.

The Palacio de los Golfines de Abajo is one of the most impressive civil buildings in the Ciudad Monumental. Built in the late 15th century by the Golfín family — one of Cáceres' major noble clans — the facade is extraordinary: carved stone armorial bearings, Mudéjar decorative elements, and late-Gothic tracery combined in a way that is distinctly Extremaduran rather than anything else in Spain.

The palace remains in private ownership but can be visited at certain hours. The interior is less accessible than the facade, but the entry courtyard alone is worth seeing. The building faces the Plaza de Santa María, making it easy to combine with a visit to the co-cathedral.
