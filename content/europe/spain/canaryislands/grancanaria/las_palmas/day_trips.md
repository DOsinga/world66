Gran Canaria is small enough that most of the island is accessible as a day trip from Las Palmas. The interior is spectacular and rarely visited by the beach-holiday crowd, which means you will often have it to yourself.

**The Gran Canaria interior.** The GC-21 road climbs south from Las Palmas through pine forests and into a landscape of eroded volcanic ridges. Roque Nublo, a 65-metre basalt monolith rising from a plateau at 1,813 metres, is the island's iconic landmark and a 30-minute walk from the nearest parking area. The village of Tejeda below it is the most photogenic place on the island — white houses, almond trees, and views in every direction. Allow a full day.

**[Maspalomas](/europe/spain/canaryislands/grancanaria/maspalomas).** The great sand dune field at the southern tip of Gran Canaria is 50 kilometres from Las Palmas via the GC-1 motorway, about 45 minutes' drive. The dunes themselves are a nature reserve and striking in the early morning before the resort crowds arrive. The lighthouse at Faro de Maspalomas marks the island's southernmost point.

**[Tenerife](/europe/spain/canaryislands/tenerife).** Fred Olsen fast ferries run from Las Palmas's Puerto de la Luz to Santa Cruz de Tenerife in about 1.5 hours, making a day trip to the neighbouring island feasible if you start early. Tenerife is bigger and has its own distinct character — the lunar landscapes around Mount Teide, the colonial architecture of La Laguna, and the steep north coast are all different from anything on Gran Canaria.
