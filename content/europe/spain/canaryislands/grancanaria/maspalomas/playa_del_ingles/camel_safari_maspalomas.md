The camel station at Maspalomas has operated near the lighthouse for decades, running short guided tours across the edge of the dune reserve. The camels are dromedaries — one hump, not two — and the rides last about 20 to 30 minutes, following a fixed route across the sand and back. It is unambiguously a tourist attraction, and it functions well as one: the animals are accustomed to the work, the handlers manage the group efficiently, and the elevated view from camel-height across the dunes is genuinely different from what you get on foot.

Children tend to enjoy it more than adults, but the dune landscape makes the experience worthwhile regardless. The station is easy to find: walk west along the Maspalomas beach toward the lighthouse and look for the group of dromedaries tethered near the car park. Sessions run throughout the day and pricing is per person, with a small discount for children. There is no need to book ahead — just turn up and join the next available group.
