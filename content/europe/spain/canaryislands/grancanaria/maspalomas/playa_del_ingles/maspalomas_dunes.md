The Maspalomas dunes cover around 400 hectares of shifting sand between Playa del Inglés and the lighthouse at the southernmost tip of the island. They look nothing like you expect: the scale is larger, the colours shift from pale gold to orange depending on the time of day, and in the interior you can walk for twenty minutes without seeing another person. The Charca de Maspalomas, a small freshwater lagoon at the dune's western edge, draws over 200 bird species during migration season.

Entry is free, and the dunes are accessible from the beach promenade at the western end of Playa del Inglés or directly from the Maspalomas beach side. Go early in the morning or in the late afternoon — the sand gets hot enough to burn through sandals by midday, and the midday light flattens everything out. The reserve has marked paths, but most visitors ignore them; stay aware of the vegetation markers, which indicate areas being left to recover.
