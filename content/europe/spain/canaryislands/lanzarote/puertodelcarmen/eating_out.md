---
title: 'Eating Out'
type: section
---

The Avenida de las Playas has restaurants of every nationality — Italian, Indian, Chinese, English, German, Irish — most of them indistinguishable in quality and aimed squarely at the package-tour trade. The honest local food is in two places: the row of seafood restaurants around the **old harbour at Varadero**, and a handful of small family-run Canarian taverns in the back streets of the resort.

For genuinely local cooking, look for *papas arrugadas con mojo* (Canarian wrinkled potatoes with red and green pepper sauce), *pulpo a la gallega* (octopus on potato), *vieja* (parrotfish — the local catch of the day), and *cabra* (Lanzarote goat stew). The Canarian wines from the volcanic vineyards of La Geria — particularly the dry whites of the Malvasía grape — are well worth ordering and often cheaper than the imported Spanish options.

A few names that consistently get it right: **La Cañada** in the old town for traditional Canarian, **La Bodega de Santiago** in Yaiza (worth the short drive) for the same, and **Casa Roja** at the old harbour for seafood. Avoid the avenue places with English-language menu pictures and street touts; they are reliably worse than they look.
