The Convento de San Francisco was founded in 1588, one of the first religious houses on Lanzarote, and despite repeated pirate raids and several rebuildings it still preserves its sixteenth-century church and cloister. The complex sits a short walk south of the main square, on a quiet street of low whitewashed houses.

The church is the centrepiece — a long single nave with a Mudéjar-style coffered wooden ceiling, an altarpiece in gilded baroque, and a series of side chapels with paintings and polychrome statues collected from across the island. The adjoining cloister is given over to the **Museo de Arte Sacro**, with reliquaries, embroidered vestments, and a small collection of paintings from the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries. The whole place is calm and rarely busy.
