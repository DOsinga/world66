Teguise is small and most travellers stay only a few hours, but if you base yourself in the village it is well placed for exploring the rest of Lanzarote.

The **Mirador del Río** at the northern tip of the island is a César Manrique-designed viewpoint cut into the cliffs, with a tea-room that opens onto a thousand-foot drop and views across to La Graciosa. **Cueva de los Verdes** and the **Jameos del Agua** — both spectacular collapsed lava tubes — are within easy driving distance to the northeast. **Timanfaya National Park** is the other essential trip on Lanzarote: the volcanic landscape that erupted in the 1730s, still warm enough at the surface to boil water and roast meat on a grate at the park restaurant, El Diablo. The famous market town of **Haría** sits in a valley of a thousand palms a short drive north.

For something more relaxed, the village of **Famara** has Lanzarote's best Atlantic beach — long, wild, popular with surfers — and a row of seafood cafés on the dune behind. **Arrecife**, the island's capital, is twenty minutes away and worth half a day for the Charco de San Ginés, the lagoon ringed by white houses at the centre of the old town.
