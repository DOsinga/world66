Icod de los Vinos is one of the better reasons to leave Tenerife's beach resorts for the greener north-west. It is a wine town first, a tourist stop second, with steep streets, old Canarian houses, banana plots, and the famous dragon tree that has become one of the island's symbols.

Most visitors come for the **Drago Milenario**, and that is fair enough. The tree is enormous, old, strange, and theatrical, with a crown that looks almost designed rather than grown. But the town works best if you give it a few hours rather than treating it as a photo stop: wander the streets around the church of San Marcos, taste a glass of local malvasia, and look down toward the black-sand cove of San Marcos.

The strongest reason to stay longer is **Cueva del Viento**, a lava-tube system in the hills above town. Visits are by guided tour and need advance booking, but the cave gives Icod a geological anchor that most Tenerife towns do not have. It is easy to pair Icod with [Puerto de la Cruz](/europe/spain/canaryislands/tenerife/puertodelacruz), Garachico, or the Teno road if you have a car.
