The Jardín Botánico, formally known as the Jardín de Aclimatación de La Orotava, is one of the oldest botanical gardens in Spain, founded in 1788 under Carlos III. The garden's original purpose was to serve as an acclimatisation station for plants being brought from Spain's tropical colonies — a staging post between the tropics and the mainland.

The garden is compact — about three hectares — but remarkably dense with specimens: palms, cycads, ferns, dragon trees, and the extraordinary Moreton Bay fig that now dominates the centre of the garden with its enormous buttress roots. The collection includes plants from the Americas, Asia, Africa, and Australia, many of them very old. Admission is cheap and the garden is usually peaceful. It is about a kilometre south of the town centre.
