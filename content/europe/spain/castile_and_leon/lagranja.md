La Granja de San Ildefonso is a small town about 15 km southeast of [Segovia](/europe/spain/castile_and_leon/segovia), built around the [Royal Palace](/europe/spain/castile_and_leon/lagranja/royal_palace_la_granja) that Felipe V commissioned in 1720 as a retreat from Madrid. The palace follows the classical French model — Felipe was a Bourbon, grandson of Louis XIV — and the gardens are the real attraction: elaborate fountains, clipped hedges, and long avenues of trees that make it Spain's answer to Versailles.

The fountains run on gravity-fed water from the mountains above and are only turned on for special occasions, so check the schedule before visiting. Inside, the palace has a surprising collection of Flemish tapestries. The town itself is quiet and pleasant, with a couple of good restaurants and a [glass factory](/europe/spain/castile_and_leon/lagranja/la_granja_glass_factory) that has been operating since the 18th century. The [Sierra de Guadarrama](/europe/spain/castile_and_leon/lagranja/parque_nacional_sierra_de_guadarrama) rises directly above town, offering excellent hiking in summer and skiing in winter.
