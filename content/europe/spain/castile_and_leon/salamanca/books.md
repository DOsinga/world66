**San Manuel Bueno, Mártir** by Miguel de Unamuno (1931). Unamuno was rector of the University of Salamanca and the most influential Spanish intellectual of the early 20th century — his statue stands on the Plaza de Anaya in front of the university. This short novel, his masterpiece, deals with faith and doubt through the story of a village priest; Unamuno's Spain is present on every page. His confrontation with Franco's general Millán-Astray in the university's assembly hall in October 1936 — "Venceréis, pero no convenceréis" (You will win, but you will not convince) — is one of the most dramatic episodes in modern Spanish history.

**As I Walked Out One Midsummer Morning** by Laurie Lee (1969). Lee's classic account of walking through Spain in 1935, with a violin and almost no money, passes through Castile and gives a vivid picture of the country on the eve of the Civil War. Essential reading before any journey through this part of Spain.

**The Rough Guide to Spain** or **DK Eyewitness Spain**. Both give thorough coverage of Salamanca and Castile y León, with practical detail on opening hours, tickets, and the lesser-known sights. The DK volume has good architectural diagrams of the cathedrals and the university façade.

**The Art of God: Plateresque Architecture in Spain** (various authors). The plateresque style — named for its resemblance to silversmith's work — reached its highest expression in Salamanca. Background reading on the style transforms a visit to the university façade and the Convento de San Esteban from admiration of general ornament into something you can read and understand.
