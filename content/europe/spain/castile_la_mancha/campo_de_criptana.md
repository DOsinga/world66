Campo de Criptana is a town of around 13,000 in the province of Ciudad Real, sitting on a low ridge in the heart of La Mancha about 30 km southwest of Mota del Cuervo and 130 km south of Madrid. Ten windmills crown the ridge above the town — the largest surviving group in La Mancha — their white towers visible from the surrounding plain long before you arrive. Three of them date from the sixteenth century and are among the oldest working mills in Spain; the rest are eighteenth-century reconstructions that look nearly identical from the outside. On a breezy day their sails actually turn.

The windmills are the draw, but the town below repays a walk. The old quarter climbs the hillside beneath the mills through narrow streets and whitewashed houses — the classic La Mancha vernacular at its most intact. The flamenco dancer Sara Montiel, one of Spain's great film and cabaret stars of the 1950s and 60s, was born here, and the town has a small museum dedicated to her memory in the Casa de la Tercia. The combination of windmills, old town, and genuine local character makes Campo de Criptana a more satisfying stop than some better-known mill towns, which can feel more stage-set than lived-in.

The Don Quixote connection is stronger here than almost anywhere else in La Mancha. Three of the original sixteenth-century mills survive, and local scholars make a compelling case that these are the actual giants Cervantes described in Part I, Chapter VIII — the route Quixote and Sancho Panza would have travelled between Toledo and the southern meseta passed directly through this area. Whether or not the argument is definitive, standing on the ridge among the mills with the flat plain stretching out below you in every direction, the novel suddenly feels like journalism.

Campo de Criptana is an easy day trip from Mota del Cuervo (30 km) or Alcázar de San Juan (15 km), or a natural stopping point on a longer circuit through windmill country. The town has a handful of restaurants and a small hotel, enough to linger rather than merely pass through.
