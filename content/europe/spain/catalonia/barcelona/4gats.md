The café at Carrer de Montsió 3 opened in 1897, explicitly modelled on Le Chat Noir in Paris, and almost immediately became the nerve centre of Catalan Modernisme. Gaudí, Rusiñol, Casas, and Utrillo were regulars. The irony worth noting: the building itself, Casa Martí (1896), was designed by Josep Puig i Cadafalch — who sat at the same tables as his clients. In 1900, a teenage Picasso pinned 150 charcoal drawings of bar regulars to the walls for what became his first public solo exhibition.

The name means "four cats" — Catalan slang for "just a handful of people," a self-deprecating joke that turned out to be wrong. The bar ran for only six years, closing in 1903 when the modernisme circle dissolved, then passed through a beer-hall phase before eventually reopening as a restaurant. The long closure means the surviving interior is partly a reconstruction, but the building's tile work, arched doorways, and wrought-iron detail are original Puig i Cadafalch.

Today it operates as a restaurant and café. It is a tourist destination, and it knows it — but the Gothic Quarter location, the building itself, and the density of the history here are genuine. Order a coffee and a look at the reproduction Casas paintings on the walls, which replaced the originals when they left for museums.
