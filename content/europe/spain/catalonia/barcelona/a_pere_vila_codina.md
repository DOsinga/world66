Pere Vila i Codina (1860–1916) left the tiny Catalan village of Les Oluges for Barcelona in 1875 at the age of 15, barely able to read. He emigrated to Argentina, settled in Rosario de Santa Fe, prospered in the grain trade, and educated himself as an adult in the country he had moved to. When he knew he was dying, he drafted a will leaving substantial bequests to schools in multiple Catalan cities, specifying that the funds were for "children of poor parents lacking means for education."

Barcelona used his bequest to build the Grup Escolar Pere Vila in 1920, designed by architect Josep Goday i Casals in the Noucentist style — one of the most formally accomplished school buildings of the period, treating the education of working-class children as a project worthy of serious architectural ambition. The bronze bust on a limestone column, by sculptor Josep Dunyach i Sala, was unveiled on 12 November 1932 by Mayor Jaume Aiguader, more than fifteen years after Vila Codina's death. He never saw Barcelona's response to his will.

The Enciclopèdia Catalana classifies him under "Economia" rather than philanthropy — his contemporaries saw him first as a merchant who built a fortune, then as a man who chose how to spend it. The monument stands at the school entrance in the Ciutadella area, a few metres from a building that would not exist without a self-taught grain trader's decision, made thousands of kilometres away, to invest his last act in the education of other people's children.
