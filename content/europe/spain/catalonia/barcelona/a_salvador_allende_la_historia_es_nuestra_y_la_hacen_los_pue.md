The title of this memorial is a direct quotation from Salvador Allende's last radio address, transmitted from the besieged La Moneda palace in Santiago on 11 September 1973, minutes before his death: "La historia es nuestra y la hacen los pueblos" — "History is ours and it is made by the people." The phrase captures both his philosophy and his circumstances: a man who believed in collective democratic will, speaking at the moment that will was being crushed by bombers, tanks, and a military backed by the CIA.

Barcelona had particular reasons to commemorate Allende. The city had its own recent experience of fascist takeover — Franco's forces had entered in January 1939 — and Chile's coup landed in European left-wing consciousness with the force of a second trauma. Thousands of Chilean exiles passed through Catalonia in the 1970s and 1980s, and the solidarity between Barcelona's working-class and progressive movements and the Chilean diaspora was deep and lasting.

The memorial's location in the Nou Barris district — historically one of the city's most working-class and politically radical neighbourhoods, built largely by internal migrants from Andalusia and Murcia — is not accidental. The words inscribed here are addressed to exactly the community that surrounds them.
