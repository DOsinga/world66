The Anella Olímpica — Olympic Ring — is the name for the cluster of venues on Montjuïc's summit plateau built for the 1992 Summer Games. The centrepiece is the Estadi Olímpic Lluís Companys, which began life as the Estadi de Montjuïc for the 1929 International Exposition. In 1936 it was chosen to host the Popular Olympics, an anti-fascist alternative to Hitler's Berlin Games; those were cancelled by the outbreak of the Civil War. When Barcelona won the 1992 bid, the stadium's 1929 façade was preserved — the ornate gate towers and Ionic colonnades — while the interior was completely reconstructed, including lowering the playing field by 2.5 metres to meet modern specifications.

Around the stadium, architects Ricardo Bofill, Arata Isozaki, and Vittorio Gregotti all left buildings. The Palau Sant Jordi indoor arena — Isozaki's contribution — is widely considered the most architecturally successful of the group, its roof structure assembled on the ground and then hydraulically raised into position over eighteen days. The telecommunications tower by Santiago Calatrava, its needle visible from most of the city below, was built as a symbolic gesture and a sundial: the shadow tracks time across the esplanade.

What the site captures, post-Olympics, is the specific melancholy of athletic infrastructure in the off-season. On most days the esplanade is quiet; the views across the port and the Llobregat delta are unobstructed and extraordinary.
