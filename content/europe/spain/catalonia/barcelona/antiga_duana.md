The Antiga Duana stands at the foot of La Rambla where it meets the port, its long neoclassical façade facing the water. It was completed in 1790 to a design by Joan Soler i Faneca, built by order of the Bourbon crown to consolidate customs inspection in a single monumental building. Before it existed, trade goods were assessed in scattered warehouses along the quayside; the new building brought all functions — weighing, registration, duty collection, bonded storage — under one roof and one administration.

The building is structured around a central courtyard with a double arcade of Doric columns supporting Ionic above. This courtyard, closed to the general public but visible through the main gate, is one of the most coherent surviving examples of Bourbon-era public architecture in Barcelona — a city that tended to demolish its eighteenth-century fabric during the expansions of the nineteenth. The proportions follow French models of the period rather than Catalan ones.

Since the early twentieth century the building has served as the seat of the Spanish Government's delegation in Catalonia. The political resonance has been considerable: during periods of tension between Madrid and the Generalitat, photographers regularly set up outside the wrought-iron gates. The stone entrance inscription still reads, in Latin, of goods and commerce, with no hint of what followed.
