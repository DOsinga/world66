August Kuentzmann Damm, an Alsatian brewer who had been forced to leave his home region after the Franco-Prussian War transferred Alsace to Germany in 1871, arrived in Barcelona in 1872. He brought with him the lager-brewing techniques of central Europe, which were at that time almost unknown in Spain, where wine was the universal drink of the population and beer was a novelty consumed mainly by northern European visitors.

Damm established his brewery on the Carrer de Rosselló in the Eixample in 1876, in what was then the recently opened expansion zone north of the old city. The choice of location was practical: the Eixample was being rapidly built and the population was expanding, creating exactly the market for a new product. The Estrella (Star) brand appeared on the label from the beginning.

The brewery remained on the same site as the city grew around it. By the mid-20th century, it was an industrial facility in the middle of a dense urban neighbourhood — an anomaly that should, in theory, have prompted relocation. Instead, the building was updated in place. The brewery still produces beer at the Carrer de Rosselló site, and it runs public tours. The combination of an active lager brewery and the surrounding apartment buildings — the hops smell drifts through the neighbourhood on brewing days — is a reminder that the Eixample was designed to be mixed-use, not purely residential.
