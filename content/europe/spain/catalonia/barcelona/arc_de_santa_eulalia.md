The Arc de Santa Eulàlia bridges the narrow Carrer de Santa Llúcia beside the Cathedral of Barcelona, connecting the cloister wall to the building opposite. The arch is a Gothic structure of the 14th century, but it marks a route with a much older significance.

Santa Eulàlia was a teenage girl from Roman Barcelona who according to hagiography was martyred in 304 CE during the Diocletianic persecution of Christians. Her supposed remains were kept in an early Christian basilica outside the Roman walls, then moved to the church of Santa Maria del Pi, and finally transferred in 878 to the site of the Cathedral — which was then an early medieval church of much smaller scale than the current Gothic building. The procession that brought her relics to their current resting place passed through this arch.

The Cathedral's crypt, constructed in the 14th century when the Gothic building was begun, now houses a marble sarcophagus said to contain the relics. Santa Eulàlia remained Barcelona's co-patron saint — sharing the role with the Virgin of La Mercè — until the 20th century. The city's name for the girls born around her feast day (February 12) was Laia, a diminutive form still widely used. The arch, which most visitors use simply as a shortcut between the Cathedral cloister and the surrounding streets, marks a passage that the city's founding Christian narrative ran through 1,100 years before the arch itself was built.
