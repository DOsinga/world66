The Arc de Triomf on the Passeig de Lluís Companys is unusual among triumphal arches: it does not commemorate a battle. The triumphal arch form — borrowed from Roman military tradition — was applied to an entirely peaceful occasion. The arch was built in 1888 as the main ceremonial entrance to the Universal Exposition of Barcelona, which occupied the Parc de la Ciutadella and its surroundings for six months.

The architect Josep Vilaseca i Casanovas designed the arch in the Neo-Mudéjar style: red brick with Moorish-influenced ornamental detail. This was a deliberate reference to Islamic architecture, which in the 1880s was being celebrated as a uniquely Spanish aesthetic tradition — a way of asserting Spanish identity in the international context of the Exposition while deploying an architectural vocabulary distinct from the neoclassicism of Paris or the Gothic revival of London.

The bas-relief on the main face of the arch shows Barcelona receiving the nations of the world — a scene that positions the city as the welcoming host rather than the military conqueror that triumphal arches conventionally celebrated. The choice was architecturally and politically significant. The 1888 Exposition was Barcelona's first serious projection of itself onto the world stage; the arch through which visitors entered was designed to make a specific claim about what kind of city they were entering. It remains the only significant public monument in Barcelona that does not commemorate either a Catalan or a Spanish historical event.
