The Arc de Sant Agustí is the main surviving element of the Convent de Sant Agustí, the Augustinian house that occupied a large block in the Raval from its foundation in the 14th century until the 1835 anti-clerical fires. The arc — a single Gothic pointed arch spanning the Carrer del Carme — was one of several arcades that connected the convent buildings to structures on the other side of the street.

The Augustinian convent was one of the major religious establishments in the Raval zone outside the medieval city walls. Religious houses of this type occupied large amounts of land — cloisters, gardens, agricultural plots — that was released for development after the 1835 destruction and the subsequent government confiscation. The blocks around the arc are all 19th-century constructions on the liberated convent land.

The arc survived because it was a structural element connecting buildings on both sides of the street, and because it was simply too solidly built to demolish cheaply. It now frames a view of the Carrer del Carme, serving as a passageway for pedestrians and cyclists who pass through it without necessarily registering that they are passing through the surviving remnant of a medieval religious complex. The arc is one of those architectural objects that is most revealing when you stop to look at it rather than passing through it.
