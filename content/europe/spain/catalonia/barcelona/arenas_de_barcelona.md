August Font i Carreras completed the Arenas bullring in 1900, working in the Neo-Mudéjar style that Spanish architects had adopted for bullrings in the 19th century — horseshoe arches, decorative tilework, ornate brickwork — and producing what turned out to be one of the last significant examples of the form. No comparable bullring was built afterward; by 1900 the architectural fashion had already started to move on. The building thus became a period piece while still in use.

Catalonia voted to ban bullfighting in 2010, the first region in mainland Spain to do so. The Constitutional Court overturned the ban in 2016 on the grounds that bullfighting is a cultural heritage matter regulated by the national government, and the Catalan parliament later reinstated it through different legislation. The legal argument continues. Arenas had already hosted its last bullfight and been converted to a shopping centre by 2011. Richard Rogers added a glass dome to the roofline and a rooftop terrace with views of Montjuïc; the ornate exterior was kept because to remove it would have been impossible to justify.

Inside, nothing of the original ring survives. The controversy over the conversion tends to run along predictable lines — those who consider it an act of architectural salvage, and those who consider stripping the seating bowl a form of irreversibility no less complete than demolition. The Rogers dome, meanwhile, has the distinction of being both the most prominent new addition and the element that most people, having seen the rooftop view, forget to argue about.
