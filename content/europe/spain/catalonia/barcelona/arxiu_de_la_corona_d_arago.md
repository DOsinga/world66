The archive's collection predates the kingdom it is named after. The oldest documents — Carolingian-era charters and land grants — were drawn up before the Crown of Aragon existed as a political entity, making this one of the very few archives whose records reach back into Europe's early medieval period. By the 13th century it was functioning as a true state archive, systematically cataloguing royal correspondence, treaty instruments, commercial contracts, and the fiscal records of a federation that controlled much of the western Mediterranean.

The archive is housed in the Palau del Lloctinent, a Renaissance palace commissioned in 1549 by Charles V as the residence of the king's lieutenant in Catalonia. The building's courtyard — with its carved wooden ceiling and exterior staircase — is one of the finest pieces of 16th-century civic architecture in Barcelona. The archive itself occupies rooms built over the medieval royal palace; beneath the floors, sections of Roman-era walls and the throne room of the old Palau Reial Major survive.

During the Spanish Civil War, the Palau Reial next door was burned, but the Arxiu's physical separation and the quick action of its archivists meant that the collection came through intact. Today it remains a working research archive, holding some 35,000 registers and 12 million documents — the administrative skeleton of a state that ceased to exist in 1716.
