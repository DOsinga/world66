Bar Marsella at Carrer de Sant Pau 65 opened in 1820, founded by a man who came from Marseille — hence the name. It is one of the oldest bars still operating in Barcelona, and it looks it without apology. The flyspecked mirrors, the amber light filtered through dusty glass, and the upper shelves lined with bottles that have not been moved in living memory are not a designed aesthetic: they are simply what a room looks like when it has not been renovated for two centuries. Gaudí drank here. So, later, did Hemingway and Picasso.

The bar's specialty has always been absinthe — legitimately so, at a time when absinthe was banned or restricted across most of Europe. It continued serving it when most establishments had stopped. The bottles on the highest shelves, coated in sediment, are said to date from the 19th century and to have never been sold or moved. Whether that is literally true is unverifiable. What is certain is that nobody has cleaned up there in a very long time.

Bar Marsella does not advertise and does not need to. It opens evenings, closes when it closes, and runs on a logic entirely its own. Do not go for efficient service or a wide drinks menu. Go because there is almost nowhere else in Europe where you can sit in a room that has not changed since before your great-great-grandparents were born.
