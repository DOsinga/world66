Joan Prim (1814–1870) was one of the most consequential Spaniards of his century and the most prominent Catalan in Spanish national politics since the eighteenth century. His military career spanned colonial campaigns in Africa and Mexico, but his political achievement was the Gloriosa revolution of September 1868, which toppled the unpopular Isabella II and opened the Sexenio Democràtico — six years in which Spain briefly experimented with a constitutional monarchy under Amadeo I and then a federal republic.

Prim was the indispensable figure of that transition: he organised the revolution, found and installed the new king, and served as prime minister while the new constitutional framework was being built. His assassination in December 1870 — shot in his carriage in Madrid, almost certainly with the complicity of members of the Bourbon restoration movement — robbed the experiment of its only figure capable of holding its contradictions together. The republic that followed lasted barely a year.

The 'Barcelona a Prim' monument commemorates a man who was simultaneously a Spanish nationalist and a Catalan hero — a combination that would become increasingly difficult to maintain in the century that followed. His statue in Barceloneta stands near the port he helped modernise, in the city that gave him his first military and political education.
