The Order of Our Lady of Mercy was founded in Barcelona in 1218 with a specific and extraordinary mission: to ransom Christian captives held by Muslim rulers in North Africa and Iberia. Members of the order took a fourth vow, in addition to the usual three, to offer themselves as hostages in exchange for captives if the ransom money ran out. This was not metaphor. Mercedarian friars spent months in Moroccan and Algerian prisons, negotiating with captors, organising prisoner exchanges, and occasionally remaining behind while others left.

The cult of the Verge de la Mercè grew from this history of practical compassion, and Barcelona's attachment to it has been unusually durable. She was proclaimed co-patron of the city (alongside Sant Jordi) in 1868, and the La Mercè festival, held each September 24th, has become the city's largest popular celebration — a week of free concerts, human towers, fire runs, and street parties that draws over a million people. The devotion is entirely genuine, rooted in a community that found its expression.

The current basilica, built in the eighteenth century on the site of an earlier church, is Baroque in architecture but older in spirit. The neighbourhood around it — the Barri Gòtic running down to the port — was the heartland of medieval Barcelona, and the church's relationship to the surrounding streets has the intimacy of a structure that grew with its community rather than being imposed on it.
