Between 1329 and 1383, the merchants, sailors, dockers, and artisans of the Ribera neighbourhood built what many architectural historians consider the finest Gothic church in Catalonia, and one of the finest in Europe. The key word is 'built': unlike the cathedral, which was a royal and episcopal project with funds from multiple sources over centuries, Santa Maria del Mar was funded and physically constructed by the local community. The bastaixos — the dockers who carried stone from the royal quarry at Montjuïc on their backs — are commemorated in stone reliefs on the exterior doors.

The church's architectural qualities — the extraordinary unity of its interior, the perfect proportions of its three naves, the forest of octagonal columns rising to ribbed vaults — reflect the fact that it was designed by a single architect, Berenguer de Montagut, and largely completed to that design. Most medieval cathedrals are accumulations of different periods and styles; Santa Maria del Mar is a single sustained vision executed in fifty-four years. The result is a interior of almost musical coherence.

Ildefons Cerdà used the church's floor plan as one of the models for his Eixample block size — the octagonal block dimensions that define modern Barcelona were partly calculated to allow Santa Maria del Mar to fit within a single block. You can stand outside and see the Eixample grid accommodating the medieval church, two different urban logics finding a way to coexist.
