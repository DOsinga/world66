The triangular site is the building's real subject. Plaça de Lesseps is one of those urban nodes where several street grids meet at angles that don't quite resolve, leaving a wedge of land that most developers would treat as an awkward constraint. Llinàs treated it as the point. Each of the library's three street fronts has a different character — different heights, different window rhythms, different relationships to the pavement — so that the building reads as a collection of specific responses rather than a single object dropped onto a difficult plot. There is no obvious main facade and no obvious back.

Llinàs is not a name that travels well outside Catalonia, but within the discipline he is considered one of the most careful architects of his generation. His work is typically described as contextual, which undersells it: he is interested in what a building owes to its surroundings, but also in what it can add that the surroundings cannot provide for themselves. The Jaume Fuster library adds a civic scale and a formal clarity that Gràcia — a neighbourhood of narrow streets and low-rise residential blocks — doesn't have much of. It marks the top of the neighbourhood the way a town hall marks a plaza.

The interior is straightforward and well-used: generous reading rooms, natural light from multiple orientations, the kind of public calm that good library architecture reliably produces. The building won the City of Barcelona Prize in 2005. The neighbourhood uses it as a library, which is the better award.
