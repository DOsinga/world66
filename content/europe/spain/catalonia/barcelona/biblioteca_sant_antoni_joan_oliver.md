RCR Arquitectes — Rafael Aranda, Carme Pigem, and Ramon Vilalta — built their reputation in rural Girona, designing restaurants, wineries, and cultural buildings that seem to grow from the Garrotxa landscape. Their Pritzker, awarded in 2017, surprised the architecture world partly because the jury chose a practice with almost no major urban work. This library in Sant Antoni is the exception that proves that rule, and it is worth examining carefully for what RCR chose to translate and what they left behind.

The street elevation is restrained: a long facade in Corten steel panels, the material oxidising to a warm red-brown that reads as both industrial and deliberate — a reference, perhaps, to Sant Antoni's own history as a working district. The interior relies on RCR's habitual device of controlled natural light: skylights and high windows that wash surfaces rather than illuminate desks directly, so the building has a quality of light distinct from the typical municipal library. The transition from street to reading room passes through a sequence of thresholds that slow the entry and signal that something different is happening inside.

The library was part of a broader renovation of the Sant Antoni Mercat neighbourhood, a district that has changed rapidly in the past decade. RCR's building predates much of that change and was designed for a neighbourhood that was quieter and less internationally noticed than it subsequently became. It now sits in a context of weekend market crowds and restaurant queues, absorbing all of that without appearing to have expected it.
