*Caçador de Lleons* — Lion Hunter — is a nineteenth-century bronze sculpture that was installed in the Parc de la Ciutadella and became one of the park's familiar landmarks without ever acquiring much scholarly attention. The work depicts a dramatic moment: a hunter grappling with a lion, the outcome still uncertain, in the kind of vigorous Romantic animal combat scene that was popular in French and Catalan sculpture of the period.

The attribution of the work was disputed for many years, caught between competing claims about which sculptor's workshop produced it and whether it was an original or a cast from an existing model. This kind of attribution problem is common with public bronzes from the later nineteenth century, when workshops produced multiple casts from successful models and records of provenance were inconsistently kept.

The sculpture stands in a park that was itself created out of political conflict — the demolished Bourbon citadel — and it shares the space with Fontserè's cascade, the artificial lake, and a greenhouse by the same architect. The Ciutadella in the late nineteenth century became Barcelona's premier leisure landscape, and the lion hunter occupies a position in it that is confident rather than prominent, discovered by those who walk through rather than sought out.
