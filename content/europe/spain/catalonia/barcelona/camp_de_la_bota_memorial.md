The Camp de la Bota (Boot Field) was a stretch of sandy ground between the old city and the Besòs river, used from 1714 onward as a military training and execution ground. After the Nationalist victory in 1939, the Franco regime used it as its principal execution site in Barcelona. Prisoners condemned by military tribunals were brought here in the early morning hours, shot in small groups, and buried in mass graves on the beach.

Estimates of the number executed at the Camp de la Bota between 1939 and 1952 — when the executions moved to the Presó Model — range from 1,700 to over 4,000. The victims included Republican politicians, trade union leaders, members of the Generalitat government, soldiers, and civilians caught by the Francoist repression. Among those shot here was Lluís Companys, the President of the Generalitat of Catalonia, who was extradited from France by the Nazis and executed in October 1940.

The Camp de la Bota was covered by the construction of the Diagonal Mar development in the 2000s: a shopping centre, a park, and apartment buildings now occupy the execution ground. The Fraternitat memorial was installed in 2004, before the development was complete, as a condition of the building permits. It marks the approximate location of the executions on a coast that, within living memory, was one of Europe's most actively used mass killing sites. The memorial is adjacent to a shopping centre. The beach where the bodies were buried is now a tourist beach.
