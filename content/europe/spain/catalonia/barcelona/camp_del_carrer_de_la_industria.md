FC Barcelona used four grounds between its founding in 1899 and its move to Les Corts in 1922. The first, on Carrer de la Muntaner, is unmarked; the second, on the Velòdrom de la Bonanova, is also gone without trace; the third, on Carrer de la Indústria in Gràcia, was used from 1909 to 1922 and carries this plaque. The plaque was installed retrospectively; the field was built over with housing within years of the club's departure, and nothing about the block suggests its history.

The Indústria ground was where Barça played its first competitive European matches and where the club's early trophies were contested. The Copa del Rey — then called by various names through constitutional changes — was played at grounds like this one, open fields with minimal infrastructure in residential neighbourhoods. The progression from the Indústria ground to Les Corts in 1922, and then to Camp Nou in 1957, tracks the club's growth from a middling Spanish football club to one of the largest sports organisations in the world.

The plaque uses the full Catalan form of the club's name and records the dates of use. It is a small ceramic rectangle on an otherwise unremarkable building facade, easy to miss and not signposted from the street. The absence of any other marker — no change to the street plan, no footprint of the old pitch in the building layout — makes the plaque's existence more significant: it is a conscious act of memory in a place where no physical trace of the past survived.
