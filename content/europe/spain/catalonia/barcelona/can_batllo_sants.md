The Can Batlló complex in the Sants-Montjuïc district is one of the largest surviving factory complexes in Barcelona. The textile mill was established in the 19th century and expanded to its current scale in the early 20th century, employing thousands of workers from the surrounding working-class neighbourhood. The factory closed in 1968 as Barcelona's textile industry declined.

The 2.7-hectare site then sat largely empty for more than four decades while the city government debated its future use. A neighbourhood association — La Plataforma Can Batlló — campaigned for decades to have the space converted into community facilities. In 2011, after years of negotiations and a deadline ultimatum, the city government agreed to transfer management of part of the complex to the neighbourhood association.

The result is one of the largest examples of bottom-up urban management in Barcelona. The community-run spaces now include a library, workshops, a beer brewery, event spaces, and a climbing wall — all managed by the neighbourhood rather than the city. The industrial buildings themselves, brick-built factory halls from the late 19th and early 20th centuries, have been minimally converted: the machines are gone, but the scale and character of the manufacturing spaces remain. Can Batlló represents a model of what can happen when urban industrial heritage is retained rather than demolished — and when communities are given the resources to manage it themselves.
