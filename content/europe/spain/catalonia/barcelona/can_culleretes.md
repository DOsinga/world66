The restaurant at Carrer dels Quintana 5 in the Gothic Quarter has been in operation since 1786, making it the oldest restaurant in Catalonia by a significant margin. It began as a *granja* — a dairy shop selling milk, cheeses, and provisions to the neighbourhood — before expanding into a full kitchen in the 19th century. The Guinness Book of Records has listed it among the world's oldest continuously operating restaurants. The fact that it started as a dairy and is now known for its braised meats and game dishes is its own small history of Catalan food.

The walls are layered with photographs of famous clients going back decades: opera singers, politicians, bullfighters, actors. The menu has not chased trends. Escudella i carn d'olla (the great Catalan stew), cargols (snails), crema catalana, and seasonal game appear as they always have. Portions are large; prices are moderate; the room is busy.

Can Culleretes is in the tourist core of the Gothic Quarter but draws a significant local clientele precisely because it has not adapted to serve tourists. The cooking is the reason to come — not the history, though the history is real.
