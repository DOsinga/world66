The Framis factory was built in the late sixteenth century as a wool-washing and processing facility, part of the textile industry that made Poblenou a productive zone long before it became Barcelona's 'Catalan Manchester' in the nineteenth century. The building's survival is almost accidental: it was solid enough to be used continuously for various industrial purposes over four centuries, too useful to demolish and too ordinary to attract the attention of heritage campaigners until almost too late.

The conversion to a museum of Catalan painting by the Vila Casas Foundation in 2009, designed by the architects Jordi Badia and Joan Sanahuja, is a model of sensitive adaptive reuse. The medieval walls are fully exposed, their rubble masonry contrasting with the clean contemporary interventions — glass corridors, a new glazed volume — that provide light and circulation without disguising the building's age. The collection it houses, focused on figurative painting from the eighteenth century to the present, is displayed in a setting that gives the work an unusual weight: you feel the centuries in the walls.

Surrounding Can Framis, the 22@ district — Barcelona's attempt to convert Poblenou's industrial land into a knowledge economy campus — has built glass towers, co-working spaces, and tech company headquarters on top of the old factory landscape. The museum sits in the middle of this transformation as a reminder that the innovation district has a pre-innovation history, and that the buildings which processed wool here for four hundred years were, in their time, as advanced as anything the knowledge economy has produced.
