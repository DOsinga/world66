The garden around Can Framis in Poblenou preserves traces of the agricultural complex that surrounded the farmhouse when it was built in the late 16th century. Stone foundations — walls reduced to a single course, threshold stones, cistern edges — are set into the lawn, marking the positions of outbuildings that were demolished as the industrial development of Poblenou progressed in the 19th century.

The farmhouse itself survived because it was large enough and solid enough to be converted to industrial use. The outbuildings — smaller, less substantially built — were taken down when their ground was needed for the factory buildings that surrounded Can Framis. The Fundació Vila Casas, which restored the farmhouse in the 2000s, chose to mark the footprints of the lost outbuildings in the garden rather than build over them, creating an outdoor archaeology of the agricultural complex.

The garden is accessible as part of the museum visit and provides a useful spatial context for understanding what the farmhouse was: not an isolated building but the centre of a working agricultural enterprise, with barns, stables, storage, and processing facilities surrounding the main house. The industrial city that grew around and eventually destroyed the farmhouse's agricultural function is visible in every direction from the garden — the glass office towers of 22@ now surround the low stone walls of a 16th-century Catalan farmstead.
