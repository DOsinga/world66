Barceloneta was constructed between 1753 and 1763 as a planned district for the fishing and working-class population displaced when Philip V demolished the Ribera quarter to build the Ciutadella fortress. Can Ramonet, at Carrer de la Maquinista 17, opened in 1763 — the year the neighbourhood was finished — making it coeval with the district itself. It began as a *granja*, a provisions and dairy shop serving the new fishing community, and converted to a tavern as the neighbourhood's character solidified around the port.

The wine barrels built into the walls are original and functional. The kitchen has always centred on whatever came off the fishing boats nearby: shellfish, salt cod, anchovies, and fresh catch prepared simply. This is not a creative-cuisine destination; it is the direct descendant of a provisioner's shop that sold food and drink to dock workers for 260 years.

The neighbourhood around it has changed substantially — Barceloneta is now a beach district with significant tourism pressure — but Can Ramonet has absorbed that without reorganising itself around visitors. The seafood is the reason to go. Sit inside for the barrels; sit outside for the narrow street.
