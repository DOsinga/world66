Barcelona's Canòdrom opened in 1963 as a greyhound racing venue, one of the few legal gambling facilities in Franco's Spain — a peculiar privilege that made it a place where working-class Barcelonans could gather under the regime's watchful eye. Racing continued until 2011, when animal-welfare concerns and shifting tastes shuttered the sport for good. The track sat empty for nearly a decade before the city's *Barcelona Digital City* initiative chose it as the home for something radically different.

Since 2021 the Canòdrom has operated as an *ateneu* — the Catalan word for an athenaeum, the civic cultural centres that were once the backbone of working-class intellectual life in the region. This one focuses on digital rights, algorithmic accountability, and participatory democracy. The same building that once amplified the roar of the crowd over mechanical rabbit races now hosts workshops on open-source software, data sovereignty, and AI ethics.

The juxtaposition is pointed: gambling odds were the original opaque algorithm deciding people's fortunes. The Canòdrom now invites residents to examine and challenge the opaque systems that still shape their lives. Its track record — literal and metaphorical — makes it one of the more historically self-aware cultural conversions in Europe.
