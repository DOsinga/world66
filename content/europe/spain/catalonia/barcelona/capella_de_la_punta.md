The Capella de la Punta (Chapel of the Point) stands at the southern tip of the Barceloneta peninsula, at the point where the neighbourhood's triangular form terminates at the sea. The chapel was built in the 18th century, approximately simultaneously with the neighbourhood itself, as a place of maritime devotion for the fishermen who lived in the new planned settlement.

The chapel is dedicated to Saint Michael, the archangel who served as the patron of sailors in the Western Mediterranean tradition. Before setting out to sea, fishermen would stop here to pray; returning boats that had survived dangerous conditions might make a votive offering. The relationship between the chapel and the sea was practical and immediate: the building stands almost at the water's edge, and the boats it served were dragged up on the beach directly below it.

The Barceloneta waterfront was transformed by the 1992 Olympic construction. The beach was extended, the fishing boats removed, and the area behind the chapel was developed with hotels and the Olympic Port. The Capella de la Punta survived because it was small enough and old enough to be listed as heritage, and because the neighbourhood association fought for its retention. It now sits in a landscape entirely changed from the one it was built to serve: the fishermen are gone, the boats are gone, and the chapel that once commanded a working beach now stands beside a swimming beach that receives several million visitors annually.
