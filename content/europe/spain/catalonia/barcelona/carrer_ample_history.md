The Carrer Ample (Broad Street) received its name in the medieval period for a straightforward reason: it was wider than the surrounding streets. In a city where most streets were barely wide enough for two people to walk abreast, a street where two ox-carts could pass each other was genuinely remarkable. The width — approximately 8 to 10 metres — was a luxury that reflected the street's commercial importance.

The street runs parallel to the port, connecting the Rambla to the end of the Barceloneta grid. In the 16th and 17th centuries it was one of the principal addresses of the merchant class: a location prestigious enough for important business but close enough to the port for practical commercial work. The consulates of foreign trading nations occupied palaces on this street; notaries and lawyers clustered here because their clients were nearby.

The 18th and 19th centuries saw the commercial function shift eastward as the port expanded and the Barceloneta neighbourhood was built. The Carrer Ample became a street of middle-class apartment buildings and, eventually, of businesses that served the neighbourhood rather than the international trade. The baroque church of Nostra Senyora de la Mercè stands at its eastern end, and the cafes that now occupy the ground floors of the buildings that once housed consulates are a reminder of how completely the street's function has changed while its physical form has not.
