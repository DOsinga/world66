The name Banys Nous means New Baths. There were Old Baths too — the Banys Vells, which gave their name to another street in the La Ribera neighbourhood. The distinction was relative: these baths were "new" when they were built in the 12th century, beside the Jewish Call, as part of the medieval urban infrastructure that provided communal bathing facilities to a city that had no domestic plumbing.

Medieval Barcelona had several such bathhouses, and they operated on a commercial basis. You paid to enter, received access to heated rooms in sequence — from warm to hot to very hot — and emerged clean in the Arab hammam tradition. The baths served the Jewish community of the Call especially closely, since Jewish law required ritual immersion. The Banys Nous on this street served that community until the destruction of the Call in 1391.

Nothing of the bathhouse survives above ground; the building was demolished long ago and absorbed into the medieval street pattern. What remains is the name, pressed into the ceramic tile street sign as if it were simply descriptive, which it was for the centuries when bathers actually queued here. The street is now lined with antique shops and small galleries — the antique trade has occupied this part of the Gothic Quarter since at least the 19th century, drawn here by the density of old buildings and the proximity to wealthy clients from the Eixample who arrived with storage problems. Look at the ground-floor windows and doorways: many retain their medieval stone frames, carved at a time when the New Baths were still operating down the street.
