The Carrer d'Avinyó runs through the southern part of the Gothic Quarter, a fairly ordinary street connecting the Carrer de Ferran to the Carrer de la Boqueria. In the early 20th century it was known for its brothels — a concentration of commercial sex work in an area that had historically been associated with the entertainment industry adjacent to the port.

In 1906 and 1907, the young Pablo Picasso was a regular visitor. He was living in Paris but returned to Barcelona and used the Carrer d'Avinyó as a site of observation and, according to various accounts, participation. He made numerous preparatory drawings of the women he encountered here, studies that fed directly into the painting he was developing in his Paris studio.

That painting, completed in 1907, is known as Les Demoiselles d'Avignon — the Women of Avignon Street. It shows five women in poses that simultaneously invoke classical nudes, ancient Iberian sculpture, and African masks. It is generally considered the first painting of Cubism, or at least its immediate precursor. The "Avignon" of the title is this Carrer d'Avinyó (Avinyó being the Catalan name for the French city of Avignon), translated into French by Picasso's friends who preferred the more respectable-sounding geographical reference.

The street is now entirely residential and commercial. The brothels closed long ago. A small plaque marks the approximate location where Picasso studied the subjects for his revolutionary painting.
