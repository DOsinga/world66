José de Espronceda was the leading poet of Spanish Romanticism, a bohemian figure who lived a turbulent life of exile, revolutionary politics, and prolific writing before dying of diphtheria at 34 in 1842. His poems — particularly "The Pirate's Song" and "The Student of Salamanca" — celebrate freedom, defiance, and the doomed hero with an extravagance that made him enormously popular in the period when Barcelona's industrialisation was beginning.

The street named for him in Poblenou runs through the zone that was, in Espronceda's lifetime, beginning its transformation from farmland to factory district. By the time the street was named in the late 19th century, Poblenou had become the industrial heartland of Catalonia — packed with cotton mills, metal foundries, and chemical works. The choice of a Romantic poet's name for a street in this environment reflects the cultural politics of the Restoration period, when naming streets after literary and intellectual figures was a way of asserting a cultural identity against the industrial and commercial character of the neighbourhood.

Espronceda has no particular connection to Barcelona. He lived primarily in Madrid and Paris. The street is one of many in Spanish cities that bear his name — he was popular enough as a poet to merit widespread commemoration — but his image of the free pirate and the doomed rebel sits oddly in a street surrounded by the factories that produced the wealth of the Catalan bourgeoisie.
