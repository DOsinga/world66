Most of Barcelona's medieval craft streets name a single trade: sword-makers, carpenters, mirror-makers. Carrer d'Obradors is different. Obrador (workshop) is generic — a working space rather than a specific craft — which suggests either that this street housed a deliberately mixed group of trades, or that it served as an overflow zone where smaller workshops set up without forming a recognised guild quarter.

The word comes from Latin operatorium, a place where work is done. Medieval Catalan used obrador for any artisan's working room, and the term appears frequently in notarial records alongside specific trades: tailor's workshop, silversmith's shop. That this street kept the generic name rather than acquiring a specific trade name implies the mix of crafts here was too varied or too changeable to define by any single one.

The street sits in the Gothic Quarter, close to Carrer de la Portaferrissa and the medieval city's main commercial spine. A working street of mixed trades here served the same function as a modern industrial estate: flexible space for small producers supplying the retail market that operated a few streets away toward the waterfront.
