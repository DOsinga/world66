The Gran de Gràcia (Great Street of Gràcia) runs from the Passeig de Gràcia at the bottom of the Eixample to the Plaça de Lesseps at the top of the neighbourhood. It was the main road connecting Barcelona to the village of Gràcia before annexation in 1897 — the route along which goods moved, people travelled, and, eventually, the city itself expanded northward.

The street's width and relative straightness (compared to the surrounding Gràcia streets) reflect its origin as a road between two settlements rather than a street within a village. As the Eixample was built around the Passeig de Gràcia in the 1860s and 1870s, the old road to Gràcia became the Gran de Gràcia — widened and formalised as the Eixample's northward extension.

The street retains a mixed character: Modernista apartment buildings at the lower end (where the street meets the Eixample), smaller-scale Gràcia village buildings at the upper end, and the whole range of commercial evolution between. Several notable Modernista buildings are on or just off the street, including the Casa Fuster at the Passeig de Gràcia junction — designed by Domènech i Montaner, it is the last major Modernista apartment building to be built before the movement gave way to Noucentisme. The Casa Fuster was completed in 1911; by then, the street it terminated was already both Barcelona avenue and Gràcia village main street simultaneously.
