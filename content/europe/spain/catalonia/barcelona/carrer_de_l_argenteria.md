The street terminates at the western portal of Santa Maria del Mar, which means the silversmith quarter effectively had the cathedral as its parish church — the merchants who financed that building campaign and the craftsmen who worked this street were the same community.

The guild of argenters — silversmiths, and by extension goldsmiths — was among Barcelona's most powerful in the 13th and 14th centuries, the city's mercantile peak. Precious metals arrived by ship from Castile and later the Americas; the proximity to the waterfront was deliberate. The argenters processed coin, crafted liturgical objects, and supplied the jewelry trade for a city that was then one of the wealthiest ports on the western Mediterranean.

The guild street system concentrated craftsmen by trade so that buyers knew where to go and guilds could police quality collectively. Carrer de l'Argenteria was the silversmiths' stretch of what is now the axis running from the old city center toward the sea, a commercial corridor that has been in continuous use since the medieval period.
