The Carrer de l'Hospital takes its name from the Hospital de la Santa Creu, which has occupied the north side of the street since 1401. Before the hospital's founding, Barcelona had several smaller and less systematic charitable medical institutions scattered across the city. The 1401 consolidation — bringing together six smaller hospitals under a single administration on this site — was one of the first large-scale charitable medical rationalisation projects in European history.

The Hospital de la Santa Creu operated as Barcelona's main charitable hospital for 530 years, until medical functions transferred to the new Hospital de Sant Pau in 1930. During that period, it cared for the sick poor, the injured, the mentally ill (in a separate ward that was one of the first European institutions to treat mental illness as a medical rather than a moral problem), and — in the Casa de la Convalescència — those recovering from illness. The complex also housed a pharmacy and, from the 15th century, an anatomical theatre where the city's surgeons conducted dissections.

The street that runs past the hospital's main entrance has consequently been associated with illness and care for six centuries. The institutions along it — not just the hospital but the associated facilities that clustered around it — made the Carrer de l'Hospital one of the most institutionally dense streets in the pre-industrial city. The medieval buildings of the hospital complex are now largely occupied by the Biblioteca de Catalunya and the Escola Massana art school, their medical function replaced but their architectural presence unchanged.
