Carrer de la Cadena runs east from the Rambla del Raval into the southern part of El Raval, and its name — Chain Street — preserves a memory of medieval port administration. Chains were used across medieval Mediterranean harbours to close the port entrance after dark and during festival periods: a physical seal against nocturnal maritime movement that also symbolised the city's authority over its waters. The Barcelona chain crossed the port entrance between the Drassanes and the breakwater; a secondary administrative boundary ran further inland along this approximate line.

The street sits in the zone that was, in the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, the most densely populated and most policed part of El Raval. The blocks here held tenement buildings of extraordinary density — single rooms occupied by entire families, shared privies at the end of each corridor — and the political press that served the anarchist labour movement. Many of these buildings were demolished in the 1980s urban renewal programs; the Rambla del Raval was cut through a section of the densest fabric.

The current Carrer de la Cadena retains its original width and alignment but is now flanked by a mix of post-demolition housing from the 1990s and earlier surviving structures. The contrast in scale between the newer buildings and the few remaining nineteenth-century ones is visible in the varied rooflines and the change in stone colour partway down the block.
