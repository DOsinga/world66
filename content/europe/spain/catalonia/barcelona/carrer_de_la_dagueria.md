The Carrer de la Dagueria (Dagger Street) records the medieval knife and dagger makers who occupied workshops along this short street in the southern Gothic Quarter. The daguer s who worked here produced the short blades — daggers, hunting knives, and the functional knives used by butchers, cooks, and craftspeople — that were among the most essential everyday items of the medieval city.

Bladed weapons were ubiquitous in the medieval urban environment. Every adult male carried a knife as a basic tool; daggers served both as weapons and as eating utensils; specific blade types were required for different crafts. The daguers occupied a position between the blacksmiths who produced raw metal and the cutlers who made the finished product — they specialised in the forging and shaping of blade blanks that others might then hilt and grind to a fine edge.

The street is very short — barely 50 metres — and connects the Carrer de Ferran, one of the Gothic Quarter's main east-west arteries, to the Carrer de Jaume I. The trades it records — dagger-making, with its requirement for fire, hammering, and quenching — must have been both audible and visible from the street: the forge sound, the smell of hot metal, the spark-shower of the grindstone. The current carrer has none of this sensory presence; it is a quiet tourist-traffic lane. But the name insists on its past.
