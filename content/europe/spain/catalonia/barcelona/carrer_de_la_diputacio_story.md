The Carrer de la Diputació runs east-west through the Eixample, named for the Diputació Provincial de Barcelona — the provincial government institution that managed local affairs under the Restoration-period centralised Spanish state from 1875 onward. The Diputació presided over Barcelona's fastest period of industrial growth, managing infrastructure and public works in a city that was doubling in population with each generation.

The street was one of the key cross-axes in Cerdà's Eixample plan, running roughly at the mid-point of the residential zone. Like most Eixample streets it has changed character over time: what began as a residential street of bourgeois apartment buildings became, in the 20th century, a more commercially mixed street. The current Carrer de la Diputació has a concentration of small hotels, which moved here as property prices made the street relatively affordable compared to the Passeig de Gràcia.

The Diputació building itself — the Palau de la Generalitat's pre-cursor in provincial government — was located on the Carrer de la Diputació before being superseded by the Generalitat's role in the 1979 Statute of Autonomy. The administrative history encoded in the street name reflects the complicated relationship between provincial and national institutions in Catalonia, a relationship that has been periodically renegotiated but never fully resolved.
