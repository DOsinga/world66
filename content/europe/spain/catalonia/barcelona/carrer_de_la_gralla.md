The Carrer de la Gralla is one of the narrower lanes in the Gothic Quarter, barely wide enough for two people to pass comfortably. The name refers to the gralla — a Catalan reed instrument similar to an oboe, traditionally used in casteller (human tower) competitions and popular festivals. How or why an instrument name attached itself to this particular lane is not recorded.

The street's physical significance is in its walls. On one side, the wall of a Roman building — possibly a structure associated with the provincial forum that occupied the area around the current Plaça de Sant Jaume — is incorporated into the base of the medieval masonry. The Roman structure's straight-cut stones are visible as a horizontal course in the lower section of the wall, distinct from the rougher medieval stonework above them.

This kind of incorporated Roman fabric is common in the Gothic Quarter but rarely explicitly labelled. Walking through the medieval streets, you are frequently walking past Roman walls that were simply too useful to demolish. The medieval builders worked over and around the Roman city, adapting its fabric to new uses, in the same way that they adapted the buildings of the Visigoths who preceded them. The gralla — the reed instrument — attached its name to a street over a Roman wall, producing a combination that is entirely characteristic of the Gothic Quarter's layered history.
