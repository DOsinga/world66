Carrer de la Lluna — Moon Street — runs through the middle of El Raval from the Rambla del Raval northward, taking its name from a medieval inn or tavern that displayed a moon on its sign, a common naming practice when street addresses were identified by landmarks rather than numbers. The current street has been widened twice since the nineteenth century, losing buildings on one side each time, but the name has persisted.

The social history that the street embodies is the history of El Raval itself: a working-class and craft district through the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries; an industrial tenement neighbourhood in the nineteenth; a zone of extreme poverty, prostitution, and political radicalism in the early twentieth century (the "Barrio Chino"); a site of immigration, first from rural Catalonia and later from Morocco, Pakistan, and the Philippines, in the second half of the twentieth century. Carrer de la Lluna, as a central artery, has registered all of these changes in its commercial mix, its residents, and its physical fabric.

The blocks along the street include some of the oldest standing structures in the Raval — eighteenth-century rental buildings with their characteristic wide doorways and interior stairwells — as well as postwar infill that replaced demolished blocks. The Rambla del Raval opened in 2000 just to the south, creating a new public space by demolishing a block of tenements; Carrer de la Lluna terminates at the edge of this open space and serves as one of its main approaches.
