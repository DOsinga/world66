La Maquinista Terrestre i Marítima was established in 1855 as a factory for building ships and railway locomotives. Its location in Poblenou, on the former industrial waterfront east of the city, gave it access to the sea for ship launches and to the railway for locomotive deliveries. At its peak in the early 20th century, it employed over 2,000 workers and was the largest engineering enterprise in Catalonia.

The factory built everything from steam engines and hydraulic presses to complete railway trains. Spanish railway expansion in the second half of the 19th century created enormous demand for locomotives; La Maquinista supplied many of them. The factory also built ships' engines, bridge components, and industrial machinery for the Catalan textile industry. It was, in effect, the manufacturer of Catalonia's industrial infrastructure.

La Maquinista closed in 1966, when it could no longer compete with larger European engineering firms. The Poblenou factory site was subsequently redeveloped: the space is now occupied by a shopping centre, La Maquinista, which borrowed the factory's name in an irony that the original factory workers' descendants find difficult to appreciate. A small museum display in the shopping centre acknowledges the industrial history; the Carrer de la Maquinista, which runs along the site, preserves the name more directly.
