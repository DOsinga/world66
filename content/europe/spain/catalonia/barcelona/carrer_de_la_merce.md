The Carrer de la Mercè runs from the Plaça de la Mercè, where the 18th-century church of Nostra Senyora de la Mercè stands, toward the port. The Mercedarians — the Order of Our Lady of Mercy, founded in Barcelona in 1218 specifically to ransom Christian captives from North African corsairs — had their principal Barcelona church on this street, and the neighbourhood around it became associated with the maritime administration of the city.

In the 18th century, after the Bourbon victory in 1714, the naval command was housed in buildings along this street and the adjacent Carrer del Consolat de Mar. The officers who administered Barcelona's port operations lived in the mansions whose ground floors now house bars. The Baroque church, built between 1765 and 1775, replaced an earlier Gothic structure and is the civic and religious symbol of the neighbourhood of la Mercè — the patron saint of Barcelona, whose feast day on September 24 is the city's principal festival.

The street's cava and wine bars are among the oldest-established drinking establishments in the city. Some of the basement bodegas have been serving wine from barrels since the 19th century. The juxtaposition of the Mercedarians' anti-piracy mission, the naval administration's record-keeping offices, and the working-class drinking culture that grew up around them has produced a street whose layers are too compressed to read from the outside.
