The Carrer de la Pietat runs along the north side of the Cathedral complex, connecting the Plaça de la Seu to the smaller square in front of the Casa de l'Ardiaca. The street's name references the devotional image set into the wall at its mid-point: a carved stone relief of the Pietà — the Virgin Mary holding the body of the dead Christ — that dates from the 15th century.

Gothic relief carvings set into the exterior walls of churches were a common form of public religious art in medieval Barcelona: they provided passers-by with images for private devotion without requiring entry into the church. The relief on the Carrer de la Pietat served the worshippers and passers-by who moved between the Cathedral and the Bishop's Palace. Many similar carvings have been lost to weathering, demolition, or deliberate removal; the survival of this one is partly attributable to its location in a recessed niche that protected it from direct rain.

The relief is now behind a protective glass panel, which both preserves it and slightly distances it from the street experience. The carved image — Mary's grief and the weight of the dead body she supports — was placed in this specific location deliberately: the Carrer de la Pietat was one of the principal processional routes for the Holy Week observances that were among the most important liturgical events of the medieval city's calendar. Walking the street on Good Friday, with the Pietà in view, was itself a devotional act.
