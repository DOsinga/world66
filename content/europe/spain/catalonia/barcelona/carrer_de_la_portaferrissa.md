The Carrer de la Portaferrissa (Iron Gate Street) takes its name from one of the principal gates in the first medieval extension of Barcelona's city wall, built in the 11th and 12th centuries. The Porta Ferrissa — the Iron Gate — was named for the iron fittings on its doors, which distinguished it from the stone gates of the Roman wall. It stood approximately at the point where the Carrer de la Portaferrissa meets the Rambla, until the expansion of the Raval district in the 13th and 14th centuries made it redundant and it was demolished.

The street that preserved the name runs eastward from the Rambla into the Gothic Quarter, crossing streets whose names also record the medieval city: Carrer del Pi, Carrer de Petritxol. This zone was the commercial centre of the intramural city in the medieval period — the Portaferrissa gate was one of the main points through which traders and travellers entered.

The fountain at the beginning of the street — the Font de la Portaferrissa — is a 19th-century replacement of a medieval fountain that stood at the gate. The current fountain has a painted tile panel showing the original gate. It is the kind of interpretive element that tries to make visible what has been erased: a coloured image on a fountain, telling the story of a gate that stood on this spot 800 years ago. Most of the people who pass it are heading to the shops on either side.
