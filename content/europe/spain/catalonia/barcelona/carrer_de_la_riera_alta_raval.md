The Carrer de la Riera Alta (Upper Stream Street) takes its name from the seasonal stream that ran through what is now the Raval neighbourhood before urbanisation. The stream — technically a riera, meaning a stream that carries water only during and after rain rather than continuously — drained the slopes of Montjuïc and the lower Collserola hills, running through the extramural zone and into the sea at the port.

The street's slightly irregular course through the Raval grid reflects the original watercourse: where other streets follow the Roman grid or the medieval street plan, the Riera Alta takes a diagonal line that corresponds to the path of the stream. The stream itself was paved over and directed into the sewer system as the neighbourhood densified in the 18th and 19th centuries; the street now sits on top of it without any indication of the drainage infrastructure beneath.

The Raval neighbourhood was built on marshy and flood-prone ground. The rieres that ran through it — the Riera Alta and the Riera Baixa — were regular flood hazards, turning the streets that followed their courses into water channels during heavy rain. The paving over of these streams was an 18th and 19th century public works project that made the neighbourhood more liveable but also removed the last visible connection between the urban fabric and the natural drainage system it was built on.
