The Carrer de la Riera Baixa (Lower Stream Street) takes its name from a seasonal stream — a riera — that drained from the Collserola hills through what is now the Raval, running roughly along the line of this street before reaching the sea. The stream was paved over in the 19th century as the neighbourhood densified, but the slight depression in the street surface still reflects the original watercourse.

The street's contemporary significance is as the centre of Barcelona's vintage clothing trade. From the early 1990s, when the Raval's low rents attracted creative enterprises that could not afford the Eixample, second-hand clothing shops began opening on this block. The initial clients were the young and the economically marginal; by the early 2000s, vintage clothing had become fashionable enough to attract buyers from across the city.

The Riera Baixa vintage strip survived the Raval's subsequent gentrification better than similar commercial clusters in other cities, partly because the street's physical character — narrow, irregular, without the long sightlines that attract chain retail — discouraged commercial property development. The shops that remain are a mixture of genuine vintage dealers, selling pre-war and mid-century garments, and the more commercial end of the secondhand market. On Saturday mornings, a small market sets up on the pavement. The stream that gave the street its name has been invisible for 150 years; the secondhand economy it hosts is its current and more visible identity.
