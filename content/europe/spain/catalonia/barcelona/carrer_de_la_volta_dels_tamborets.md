The Carrer de la Volta dels Tamborets (Street of the Vault of the Little Drums) is a Gothic Quarter street whose name is among the most specific and evocative in the old city. The volta (vault) refers to a covered arcade or passage; the tamborets were small tambourines or frame drums that were sold in stalls under this vault. The combination produces a name that preserves, with unusual precision, the commercial identity of a specific medieval covered arcade.

The tambourine trade occupied a niche at the intersection of music, popular entertainment, and religious procession. Tambourines were used in the processional and theatrical performances of the medieval city — the mystery plays, the Corpus Christi processions, the popular entertainments of market days. The sellers of tamborets were providing instruments for this acoustic landscape.

The name is occasionally cited as an example of how medieval Barcelona's streets were named with specificity about uses that have since entirely disappeared. The vault itself was demolished long ago; the street that replaced it retains the name in a slightly compressed form. Several other Barcelona streets preserve equally specific medieval commercial identities — the Carrer dels Flassaders (blanket weavers), the Carrer de la Dagueria (dagger makers) — but none quite matches the Tamborets for the specificity of combining a spatial feature (the vault) with a commercial activity (drum-selling) in a single name.
