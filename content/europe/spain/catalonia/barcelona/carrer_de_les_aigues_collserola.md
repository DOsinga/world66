The Carrer de les Aigües (Water Street) is a pedestrian and cycling path running along the lower slopes of the Collserola hills at roughly 130 metres elevation, from the Vallvidrera area above Sarrià to the neighbourhoods above Gràcia. The path is approximately 14 kilometres long and maintains a constant elevation, following the alignment of the aqueduct that the Companyia d'Aigües de Barcelona built in the 1870s to carry spring water from the Collserola sources to the city's distribution system.

The aqueduct was a covered channel — a rectangular stone-and-brick duct, partially underground — that ran along the hillside at an elevation that allowed gravity to carry water to the reservoirs and fountains of the city below. The constant elevation was required by the hydraulics: water flows by gravity, so the aqueduct had to maintain a very slight downward slope along its entire length. The path that now follows its alignment maintains this characteristic horizontal line along a hillside that otherwise rises and falls with the terrain.

The path became a popular recreational route in the 20th century as the Collserola was preserved as a park and access from the city improved. It is now one of the most used recreational paths in Barcelona, particularly on weekend mornings when runners and cyclists from across the city come to the hillside. The view from the path, looking down across Barcelona to the sea, changes character across its length: from the industrial zones of the Zona Franca in the southwest to the high towers of Poblenou in the northeast, the entire horizontal span of the city is visible below.
