The Carrer de les Carolines in Gràcia takes its name not from a person named Carolina but from the Caroline Islands, a Pacific archipelago that Spain claimed as a colony in 1885. The naming of the street in the 1880s reflected the political moment: Spain's claim to the Carolines was disputed by Germany, and the resulting crisis — resolved eventually by papal mediation — briefly made the islands famous in Barcelona, where the colonial question was closely followed by the liberal press.

The street is significant for a different reason: it is where Casa Vicens stands. Antoni Gaudí's first major commission, built between 1883 and 1888 for the tile manufacturer Manuel Vicens Montaner, the house is the earliest expression of what would become his mature style. The building is covered in green and white ceramic tiles (manufactured by the client's own factory), with Moorish and Orientalist details derived from Gaudí's study of Islamic architecture in Spain and North Africa.

Casa Vicens was opened as a museum in 2017 after decades as a private residence and then a private garden. The decision to open it transformed the Carrer de les Carolines from an ordinary Gràcia residential street into a cultural destination. The Carolines connection — Pacific colonialism filtered through Barcelona naming politics — is now entirely forgotten; the house that Gaudí built in the street is the only thing most visitors know about it.
