The Carrer de les Egipcíaques takes its name from the Casa de les Egipcíaques, a religious house established in the 17th century for the rehabilitation of former sex workers. The term egipcíaques (Egyptian women) was a euphemism: it referred to Mary of Egypt, a 4th-century saint who according to hagiography had been a prostitute in Alexandria before her conversion and whose story was widely used in the Counter-Reformation as a model of repentance. Women referred to the house were those who, in the language of the period, sought to "leave an evil life."

The institution combined a correctional function with a religious one. Women entered voluntarily or under pressure from magistrates, followed a regime of prayer, work (usually spinning or weaving), and religious instruction, and were eventually released back into the general population or placed in domestic service. Similar institutions existed across Catholic Europe in the Counter-Reformation period; Barcelona's version was one of the more long-lived.

The street is now entirely residential and commercial, with no visible trace of the institution it was named for. But the name itself is unusual in the Barcelona street map for being a direct, historically specific reference to a social welfare institution rather than to a trade guild, a saint, or a topographic feature. It is one of the few street names in the Raval that encodes the history of a city trying to manage poverty and moral deviance through religious institutions, in the period before secular social services existed.
