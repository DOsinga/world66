The Carrer de les Flors in the Raval neighbourhood takes its name from the flower vendors who occupied stalls at the corner with the Ronda de Sant Pau from the early 19th century onward. Fresh flowers were sold here on the way to the Hospital de la Santa Creu, which lies nearby — an obvious market for people visiting the sick. The flower-sellers established the name long before the street acquired its more dramatic historical associations.

From 1836, the street became home to the Presó Model, a penitentiary that occupied what had previously been a Carmelite convent. The prison held political prisoners throughout the troubled 19th and early 20th centuries. During the Civil War, it was used by both sides at different moments: Republican militias improvised a holding facility here in 1936; after the Nationalist victory, it became one of the principal detention centres for Catalans and leftists.

The prison was closed in 1904 when the Presó Model was built in the Eixample, and the building eventually demolished. The street today is quiet, lined with apartment buildings and small workshops. It connects the Raval's institutional core — the hospital complex, the Sant Pau del Camp monastery — to the Parallel, the entertainment avenue that bounded the neighbourhood to the south. The flower-sellers are long gone, but the name has outlasted the prison.
