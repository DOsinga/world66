Carrer de les Magdalenes runs between Via Laietana and the old city core, taking its name from the Convent de les Magdalenes established here in 1401. The convent was a specifically medieval institution: a house for women who worked in prostitution and wished to leave it, funded by municipal authorities and private donors, and run by the Dominicans under the patronage of Mary Magdalen — the reformed sinner of the Gospels. Barcelona established one of the earliest such houses in the western Mediterranean, preceded only by Rome and Valencia.

The convent operated for nearly four hundred years, through the suppressions and reversals of religious houses in the eighteenth and early nineteenth centuries, until it was finally dissolved in the 1820s and the building converted to secular use. The Via Laietana construction of 1908 demolished most of the remaining fabric. The street name is the sole surviving trace of the institution's location.

The history the name carries is contested ground in contemporary scholarship: some historians emphasise the care and genuine exit the convent offered to women in desperate situations; others argue the institution functioned primarily as a mechanism of patriarchal control, with women who entered having little effective choice to leave once admitted. The street runs through a neighbourhood that in the nineteenth century retained much of the mixed character the medieval city had — craft workshops, taverns, housing — before the Via Laietana imposed a commercial axis through it.
