The Carrer de les Mosques (Street of the Flies) is a short lane in the Born neighbourhood that preserves a medieval name referring to a basic fact of urban life in a pre-modern city: flies congregated wherever food was processed, animals were slaughtered, or organic waste accumulated. The specific use that created this particular fly concentration is not recorded — the most likely candidates are a meat stall, a tanning operation, or a middens site — but the name was specific enough to stick.

Medieval Barcelona, like all pre-modern cities, had a complex olfactory and acoustic landscape that the modern city has entirely erased. Animals were slaughtered in neighbourhood butcher shops rather than abattoirs; fish were gutted at market stalls; tanning and dyeing operations used processes that produced powerful smells. Streets were named for these sensory realities: the Carrer de les Mosques records the flies; other streets record the smell of fish (Carrer del Peix), the sound of the mills (Carrer dels Molers), the presence of blood (Carrer de la Sang, in several cities).

The Born neighbourhood's current identity — galleries, bars, restaurants, tourist shops — is built on a spatial framework whose names record an entirely different economy. Walking the Carrer de les Mosques, past its current businesses selling wine and artisanal food products, requires some imaginative effort to recover what the medieval street actually smelled and sounded like.
