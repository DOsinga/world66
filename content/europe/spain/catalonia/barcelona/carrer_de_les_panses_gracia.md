The Carrer de les Panses (Raisin Street) in upper Gràcia records the agricultural character of the hillside before the neighbourhood was built. The area around Gràcia was covered with vineyards in the early modern period — the slopes below the Collserola were well-suited to viticulture, with adequate sun exposure and drainage. The grapes produced were used for wine, for fresh eating, and for drying into raisins (panses in Catalan), which were traded in the Barcelona market.

The raisin trade was significant in the pre-industrial economy. Dried fruits were one of the few ways to preserve the sugar and energy of fruit through the winter, and raisins from the Catalan vineyards supplied the confectionery, the baking, and the direct consumption of a city that had no refrigeration. The panses of Gràcia were made by laying harvested grapes on flat rocks or frames in the sun, a process that required specific conditions of heat and air circulation that the south-facing hillside provided.

The vineyards were grubbed out as the population expanded northward from Barcelona in the 19th century and the agricultural land was converted to housing plots. The street name is one of the few surviving references to the agricultural landscape that preceded the dense residential neighbourhood. The vine terraces that were once visible on the Gràcia hillside are entirely built over; the Carrer de les Panses preserves the memory of what grew there before the houses.
