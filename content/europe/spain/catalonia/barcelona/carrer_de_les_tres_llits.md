The Carrer de les Tres Llits (Three Beds Street) is by some measures the shortest named street in Barcelona's old city — a passage barely 10 metres long connecting two adjacent streets in the Gothic Quarter. Its name records a very specific past use: a boarding house with three beds to rent, offering the cheapest accommodation available in the medieval city.

Three-bed boarding houses occupied the lowest rung of the medieval hospitality economy. A single bed in a shared room was the cheapest option; a private room was luxury. Three beds — presumably in a single room, shared by strangers — represented accommodation at the price point of the itinerant poor, the arriving migrant worker, the pilgrim with no money for a hostel. The fact that a house in this position was well-known enough to name a street suggests it was a fixed institution in the neighbourhood for some time.

The street is often cited in Barcelona's municipal records about the medieval urban fabric precisely because of its extreme brevity and its specific name. Several other Barcelona streets are almost as short — the Carrer del Pi Sec, the Carrer de les Mosques — but few have names as specific as Tres Llits in recording a past economic function. The three-bed boarding house closed centuries ago; the 10-metre passage it left behind is now primarily a shortcut used by residents of the surrounding apartments.
