The Carrer de Pujades is named for Jeroni Pujades, a 17th-century Catalan historian and lawyer who wrote the Crónica Universal del Principat de Catalunya, one of the foundational texts of Catalan historiography. Pujades worked in the period of the Catalan revolt of 1640 — the Reapers' War that briefly separated Catalonia from Spain — and his historical writing was implicated in the political project of asserting Catalan identity against Castilian centralisation.

The street itself runs through Poblenou from the Rambla del Poblenou eastward, crossing the industrial zone that was the heart of Barcelona's manufacturing economy. At its peak in the early 20th century, the street was lined with factories, workshops, and workers' housing. The smell of dye vats, the sound of looms, and the rhythmic output of industrial production defined the street's character for over a century.

The 22@ transformation has been working along the Carrer de Pujades since the early 2000s. Factory buildings have been converted to offices; new buildings have appeared on former industrial sites. The street is now a mix of technology companies and surviving residential buildings, with the occasional surviving manufacturing enterprise that has not yet been displaced. The historian whose name it bears would probably not have anticipated this specific trajectory — from 17th-century Catalan historiography to 21st-century innovation district — but the persistence of a politically coded name through a century of industrial and post-industrial change is itself a small piece of Catalan cultural history.
