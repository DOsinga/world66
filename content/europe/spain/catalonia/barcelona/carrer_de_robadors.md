The Carrer de Robadors (Robbers' Street) appears in Barcelona records from at least the 14th century under various names that all point to the same function: a zone outside the effective control of municipal authority where activities of questionable legality could take place. The name robadors appears in the 16th century and has stuck through every subsequent attempt to improve the street's image.

The street's reputation was tied throughout its history to the concentration of brothels, taverns, and gambling dens that characterised the zone below the Hospital de la Santa Creu. The hospital, founded in the 15th century, attracted the sick and the poor, and around them clustered the auxiliary economies of urban poverty: moneylenders, pawnshops, cheap eating houses, and the sex industry. The Raval remained outside Barcelona's city wall until the 19th century, which meant it was outside the reach of municipal regulation for most of its history.

In the early 21st century, the Carrer de Robadors became associated with the Filmoteca de Catalunya, the national film archive, which opened a public cinema on the street in 2012 as part of an urban regeneration initiative. The juxtaposition of Barcelona's official film culture with the street's historical identity produced a neighbourhood tension that has not been entirely resolved. The street still has some of its traditional character in the late evening hours; the Filmoteca's audience walks through it to see classic films. Both things are true.
