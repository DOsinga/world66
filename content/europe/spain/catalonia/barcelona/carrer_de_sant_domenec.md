The Carrer de Sant Domènec del Call takes its name from the church of Sant Domènec, a Dominican foundation established in the Jewish quarter in the years following the pogrom of 1391. In August of that year, mobs attacked the Jewish quarter of Barcelona — the Call — killing a significant portion of the community, destroying property, and forcing survivors to convert to Christianity or flee. The Great Synagogue, which had stood near the current Plaça de Sant Jaume, was demolished and a church built in its place.

The Dominicans were associated with the conversion and persecution of Jews in the Iberian peninsula. Their Inquisition role — the Dominican order ran much of the institutional machinery of the Inquisition — made them the natural ecclesiastical institution to occupy the site of the destroyed synagogue. The naming of the church and the street for their founder, Dominic de Guzmán, encoded the replacement of the Jewish community with a Christian institution in the topography of the neighbourhood.

The 1391 pogrom effectively ended the Jewish community of Barcelona. The surviving converts — conversos or New Christians — continued to live in the former Call but were subject to suspicion and periodic persecution by the Inquisition. The Alhambra Decree of 1492 expelled all unconverted Jews from Spain. The Barcelona Call, which had been one of the most significant Jewish communities in the western Mediterranean, ceased to exist entirely. The Dominican church eventually fell into disuse; the street retains the name of the institution that replaced what was destroyed.
