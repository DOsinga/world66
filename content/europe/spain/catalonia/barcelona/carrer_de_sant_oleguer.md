The Carrer de Sant Oleguer commemorates Oleguer Bonestruga, Archbishop of Barcelona from 1116 to 1137 and simultaneously Archbishop of Tarragona. Oleguer was a canonical reformer who reorganised the Catalan church in the 12th century, but his contemporary fame rested on his role in the Reconquista — the Christian campaigns to reconquer Iberian territory held by Muslim rulers.

Oleguer organised and led the Catalan military expedition that captured Tarragona in 1118, beginning the process by which the city would be re-Christianised and repopulated. He was a direct participant in the military campaign, not merely a spiritual advisor. The concept of a bishop commanding armies in battle is alien to modern church practice but was entirely normal in the 12th century, when senior clergy controlled territory, resources, and armed men.

The street named for him in the Raval is in the zone of the city that in Oleguer's time was outside the walls — the extramural area that would be enclosed in the 14th-century Raval wall. It is named for a medieval bishop in a neighbourhood that became, in the 19th and 20th centuries, one of the most anti-clerical in Spain. The Raval was a centre of anarchist activity in the early 20th century; the churches and convents that occupied its streets were targets in the 1909 Semana Trágica and the 1936 anti-clerical violence. The archbishop's name on the street sign has survived through all of it.
