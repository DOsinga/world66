Carrer de Sant Pere Més Alt runs parallel to Més Baix one block north, slightly higher on the slope that descends toward the port. The two streets frame the core of what was, from the fourteenth to the eighteenth century, Barcelona's primary silk and velvet weaving district. The upper street served the more prestigious end of the trade: the guild masters' premises, the fabric samples rooms, and the offices of the international cloth merchants who bought in bulk for re-export.

At the eastern end, where the street approaches the Via Laietana, stands the former seat of the Consolat del Mar for the textile trades — a guild building erected in 1763 with a carved coat of arms above the door. The consolat functioned as a commercial court for disputes between merchants and weavers and as the certification authority for apprentices completing their training. The building has had various institutional uses since the guild system collapsed in the 1830s but retains its façade largely intact.

The street's most famous building is actually at the western end: the Palau de la Música Catalana stands at the corner where Carrer de Sant Pere Més Alt meets the small square of Sant Francesc de Paula. The proximity of Domènech i Montaner's concert hall to the textile district was not accidental — the Orfeó Català, which commissioned it, was deeply embedded in the craft-guild culture of the neighbourhood.
