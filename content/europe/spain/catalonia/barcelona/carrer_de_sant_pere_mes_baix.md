Carrer de Sant Pere Més Baix — Lower Saint Peter Street — is the main commercial artery of the Sant Pere neighbourhood, running east-west between the arc of the old city wall and the Via Laietana cut of 1908. The street is older than the wall itself: it follows an early medieval track that predates the expansion of the city into this quarter. The "Més Baix" (lower) distinguishes it from its parallel to the north, Carrer de Sant Pere Més Alt, by a difference of about fifteen metres in elevation — significant in a district that climbs toward the old textile hospital.

In the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries the street was lined with establishments of the textile trades: fabric merchants, ribbon-makers, and particularly velvet-weavers, whose looms required the specific humidity conditions that characterised the Sant Pere area near the Rec Comtal. The prosperity generated by the velvet trade explains the unusual density of Modernista shopfronts on this street — decorated tiles, wrought ironwork, carved stone — commissioned by merchants who wanted their premises to reflect their success. Several survive intact.

The Via Laietana's construction bisected the neighbourhood in 1908, demolishing blocks of medieval fabric and redirecting traffic patterns in ways the community never entirely recovered from. Carrer de Sant Pere Més Baix now terminates at the Via rather than continuing through to the waterfront as it once did.
