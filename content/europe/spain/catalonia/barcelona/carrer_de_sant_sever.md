Carrer de Sant Sever is one of the shortest streets in the Barri Gòtic, a lane that barely widens beyond two metres as it runs between Carrer del Bisbe and the small space in front of the church of Sant Sever. The church itself is seventeenth-century Baroque — unusually elaborate for a neighbourhood parish, with a carved stone portal featuring saints in niches and a relief tympanum depicting the bishop-martyr Sever of Barcelona, who was beheaded in the fourth century AD for refusing to sacrifice to Roman gods.

The Baroque elaboration of Sant Sever's church reflects the Jesuit influence on popular religious culture in the seventeenth century; the order operated a college immediately adjacent, and the church served as their public chapel. Jesuits were expelled from Spain in 1767 but returned in the nineteenth century, and the church passed through several administrations before settling into its current role as a tertiary Jesuit chapel, open for weekday masses but otherwise quiet.

The lane's narrowness makes it almost permanently shaded. On the wall at the end, before the church entrance, someone has mounted a small ceramic tile with the street name in Gothic script — a twentieth-century affectation but one that matches the atmosphere. The lane is a useful shortcut between the Cathedral cloister zone and the old Call, used primarily by residents of the blocks above.
