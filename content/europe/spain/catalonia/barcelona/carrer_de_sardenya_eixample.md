The Eixample's cross streets were named, when the grid was laid out in the 1860s and 1870s, by a systematic pattern: streets running north-south (the avenues and passages) received the names of Catalan towns and regions; streets running east-west received the names of historical figures, concepts (Consell de Cent, Diputació), or territories of the medieval Crown of Aragon.

The Carrer de Sardenya belongs to this last category: it is named for Sardinia, the large Mediterranean island that was conquered by the Crown of Aragon in the 14th century after a long military campaign. The Catalan conquest of Sardinia was one of the most significant episodes in the expansion of the Crown of Aragon's Mediterranean empire, and Sardinia remained under Aragonese (later Spanish) rule until 1718, when it was transferred to the Duke of Savoy in exchange for Sicily.

The street names that commemorate the medieval Aragonese empire — Sardenya, Sicília, Còrsega (Corsica), Nàpols (Naples) — form a pattern in the Eixample grid that maps the territorial aspirations of a state that no longer exists. Walking these streets is like reading a historical atlas: each island or territory name represents a chapter in the history of Catalan maritime expansion, embedded in the grid of a 19th-century industrial city built on the ruins of that empire's commercial wealth.
