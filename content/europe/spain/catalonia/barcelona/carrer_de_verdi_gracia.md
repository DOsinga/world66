The Carrer de Verdi is Gràcia's most visited street by visitors from outside the neighbourhood — it is lined with independent cinemas, bars, and restaurants, and connects the Plaça de Rius i Taulet to the Parc Güell. The street's name has a peculiar etymology.

In the mid-19th century, a wine cellar on the street called itself "La Bodega Italiana" — a name suggesting Italian wine. The shop's owner, a certain Verdaguer (a common Catalan surname), had the word "Verdaguer" gradually simplified in colloquial use to "Verdi" — the Italian form of the Catalan "verd" (green). The street acquired the informal name "Carrer den Verdi" (Verdi's street) from this commercial association. When streets were formally named, the name Verdi stuck, creating an accidental commemoration of the Italian opera composer whose music was popular in the same period.

The street's current identity as a cultural and nightlife hub grew in the 1980s and 1990s, when the Verdi cinema opened and made the street a destination for Barcelona's film culture. The Verdi Park cinema (a six-screen multiplex in a converted industrial space behind the street) followed. The combination of independent cinemas and the Parc Güell nearby creates a visitor flow that has made Carrer de Verdi one of the most consistently busy streets in Gràcia without quite becoming tourist infrastructure.
