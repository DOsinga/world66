The Duke of Wellington appears as a street name in several Spanish cities because his campaigns in the Peninsular War (1808-1814) made him a symbol of the struggle against Napoleonic occupation — and thus, in the political coding of the early 19th century, an ally of Spanish liberalism against both French imperialism and the absolutist Spanish monarchy. Streets named for Wellington were a political statement as well as a historical commemoration.

The Wellington street in Poblenou dates from the period when the neighbourhood was being developed and formally named in the mid-19th century. The naming of streets after foreign military figures associated with liberalism was a way of encoding political positions in the street map — one of the few forms of political expression available in a Spain that alternated between liberal and absolutist governments throughout the century.

The Carrer de Wellington runs through what was, in the 19th century, the edge of the Poblenou industrial development. Today it is a residential and commercial street in the 22@ zone, where the street's international-history name sits alongside the names of other Poblenou streets that encode the neighbourhood's working-class and industrial identity. The juxtaposition of Wellington — British aristocrat and field marshal — with the streets named for local workers and trade unionists is one of Poblenou's minor historical paradoxes.
