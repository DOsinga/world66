The Carrer del Balluard (Bulwark Street) in Barceloneta takes its name from the defensive earthwork that ran along the neighbourhood's western edge when it was first built in the 1750s. The balluard was a low military fortification — not a full wall but a raised earthen bank with strategic firing positions — designed to protect the new neighbourhood from attack from the sea or from the direction of the port.

Barceloneta's military context in the 18th century was significant. The neighbourhood was built immediately after the Bourbon conquest of 1714, and the entire settlement was designed with military considerations: the buildings were kept low to prevent them from serving as cover for attackers, the streets were laid out to allow artillery fire down their length, and the defensive perimeter was maintained as long as the neighbourhood was considered to have strategic value.

The balluard earthwork was gradually absorbed into the built fabric of the neighbourhood as the military threat receded and the defensive logic that had shaped the settlement was forgotten. The street that follows its line is now entirely residential — a typical Barceloneta narrow street with apartment buildings on both sides. The name balluard is the only remaining indicator that this street once ran alongside a military earthwork, in a neighbourhood that was conceived from the beginning as a strategic military settlement rather than a fishing village.
