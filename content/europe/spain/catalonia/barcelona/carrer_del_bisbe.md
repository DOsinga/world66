The Carrer del Bisbe (Bishop's Street) runs between the Plaça de Sant Jaume and the Plaça de la Catedral, cutting through the heart of the Gothic Quarter. The street is famous for the neo-Gothic bridge that crosses it overhead, connecting the Palau de la Generalitat to the Casa dels Canonges. It is one of the most photographed views in Barcelona and appears in every guidebook as an example of medieval Gothic architecture.

The bridge was built in 1928. The architect Joan Rubió i Bellver, a former associate of Gaudí, designed it in a Gothic revival style as part of a comprehensive renovation of the Palau de la Generalitat. His intention was to evoke the medieval bridges of Venice and Florence that connect civic buildings across narrow streets. He succeeded so completely that most visitors assume they are looking at a 14th-century structure.

The 1928 date matters for several reasons beyond mere accuracy. The bridge was built during the period of Primo de Rivera's dictatorship, under which Catalan cultural institutions were under political pressure. Building a Gothic bridge over the street of the Bishop — asserting Catalan Gothic identity in public stone — was itself a political act. The neo-Gothic choice was not nostalgic decoration: it was a statement about continuity with the medieval Catalan state, made in the immediate political context of suppression. The fact that it is now accepted as simply "Gothic" is evidence of how well the statement worked.
