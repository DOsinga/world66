Carrer del Bonsuccés runs off the upper Rambla into the Raval, taking its name from a hermitage of Nostra Senyora del Bon Succés that occupied this site from the medieval period. When the city began densely developing the Raval district in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries, the hermitage was incorporated into a larger convent complex; the convent was subsequently suppressed, demolished, and replaced with residential blocks. The chapel itself survived and was rededicated; its eighteenth-century portal is still visible from the street, now functioning as a neighbourhood parish entrance.

The street's character today is typical of the commercial Raval: a mix of wholesale textile traders, phone repair shops, and the occasional surviving pharmacy or hardware dealer. What distinguishes it from the lanes further into the barrio is the relative width — enough for two vehicles to pass — and the quality of the surviving corner buildings, some of which retain late-nineteenth-century ceramic tilework at the ground-floor level.

The name Bon Succés — Good Outcome — was a Marian title particularly popular in Catalan cities from the fourteenth century, invoked for safe childbirth and the resolution of difficult situations. The hermitage bearing this title predates the Raval's urbanisation by centuries, making it one of the few elements that survived the wholesale redevelopment of what had been market gardens and then slums.
