The Benedictine monastery of Sant Pere de les Puel·les, one of Barcelona's oldest religious foundations, controlled much of the surrounding land and, by extension, the commerce that happened on it. Monasteries in medieval cities were landlords as much as spiritual institutions, and the trade they permitted or organized on their property shaped entire neighborhoods. In this case, a livestock market and the butchers who operated around it clustered near the monastery's walls, with the ox — bou in Catalan — serving as both practical commodity and symbolic identifier for the whole trade.

The ox symbol appeared on guild signs, market stamps, and eventually in street names as a kind of shorthand address in an era before numbered buildings. The name Carrer del Bou de Sant Pere distinguished this livestock-associated street from other bou streets elsewhere in the city.

The street name now preserves what the neighborhood looked like before the early 18th century. After the War of the Spanish Succession, Philip V ordered the construction of the Ciutadella fortress over much of this quarter in 1715, demolishing roughly a thousand buildings and dispersing the guilds that had operated here for five centuries. The street survived; the world it named did not.
