The Carrer Nou de la Rambla (New of the Rambla Street) runs south from the Rambla into the Raval. In the 1880s, when Eusebi Güell commissioned Antoni Gaudí to design a family palace on this street, the surrounding neighbourhood was among the roughest in Barcelona: cheap boarding houses, taverns, and the peripheral zone associated with the entertainment district of the Paral·lel nearby.

Güell's decision to build here was not accidental. He owned property in the area and was developing the street as a residential address — a project that ultimately succeeded only partially. The Palau Güell was his own family residence from 1890 to 1910, when the family moved to the Park Güell. After their departure, the building was used as a police headquarters and prison during the Civil War; prisoners were held in the basement and some were executed there.

The building — now a museum — is remarkable for its early position in Gaudí's development. It predates the mature organic style of Park Güell or the Sagrada Família; instead it shows Gaudí working within Moorish and Venetian architectural traditions, producing a building that is extraordinary for its roofscape of ceramic-covered chimneys and its interior planning rather than for external organic form. The street it sits on has not been much gentrified. The boarding houses and cheap hotels that surrounded it in 1890 have been replaced by slightly different establishments in the same price range. The palace remains as incongruous as it was when it was built.
