The Carrer del Pallars runs through the heart of the Poblenou neighbourhood, serving as the main street of the 22@ innovation district — the zone designated for the conversion of former industrial land into offices, research centres, and technology companies. The 22@ plan, launched in 2000, covered 200 hectares of the most intensely industrialised part of the city.

Walking the Carrer del Pallars reveals the plan in progress: converted factory buildings now housing design studios and startup offices stand alongside surviving manufacturing enterprises and empty lots waiting for development. The typical 22@ building is a brick factory shell from the late 19th or early 20th century whose interior has been gutted and fitted with suspended ceilings, open-plan floors, and the infrastructure of the digital economy. The exteriors are preserved — listed or protected — while the interiors are entirely new.

The plan has been contested since its launch. Critics argue that it displaced working-class residents and surviving industrial enterprises in favour of technology companies that provide fewer local jobs per square metre than the factories they replaced. Defenders argue that without the transformation, Poblenou would have continued to decline into dereliction. The Carrer del Pallars, where several large-scale factory conversions sit across the street from small metalworking shops that have been there for fifty years, makes both arguments simultaneously visible.
