The street called Carrer del Paradís runs from behind the Cathedral toward the Plaça de Sant Jaume, appearing on every map of the Gothic Quarter but still managing to feel like a discovery when you find it. It is significant for a reason visible from the outside: at number 10, attached to the façade of a medieval building, a medieval carved stone column marks the site of the mons taber, the highest natural point of the Roman city of Barcino. The ground here stands about 5 metres above the surrounding streets.

Inside the building at number 10 — the Centre Excursionista de Catalunya, a hiking and mountaineering club founded in 1876 — stand three and a half Corinthian columns of the Temple of Augustus, originally built in the 1st century BCE. The temple occupied the northwest corner of the Forum of Barcino, which lay beneath what is now the Plaça de Sant Jaume. The columns are roughly 9 metres tall and among the best-preserved Roman remains in Catalonia.

The building that now contains them was a medieval palace that incorporated the columns into its structure rather than demolishing them. They were rediscovered and made accessible to the public in 1931. The Centre Excursionista still operates from the same building; members use the entrance that passes through the Roman atrium. The combination — classical columns, medieval vaulting, 19th-century mountaineering prints — in a narrow lane named for Paradise is one of Barcelona's characteristic layerings of history.
