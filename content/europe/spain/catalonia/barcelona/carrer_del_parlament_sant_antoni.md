The Carrer del Parlament in the Sant Antoni neighbourhood takes its name from the parliamentary building that was established in the area during the brief First Spanish Republic of 1873-1874. Spain's First Republic lasted only eleven months before a military coup restored the Bourbon monarchy. In that short period, a federal constitution was drafted that would have granted Catalonia extensive autonomy — the most generous framework for Catalan self-governance since the Bourbon conquest of 1714.

The parliament building that gave the street its name was a repurposed religious or public building used temporarily for parliamentary sessions while a permanent home was being debated. The republic's collapse meant the permanent building was never constructed. The street retained the name after the republic fell, an unusual instance of topographic memory preserving a political aspiration that was quickly defeated.

The Sant Antoni neighbourhood that surrounds the street has been one of Barcelona's more politically active zones since the 19th century. The proximity of the Mercat de Sant Antoni, the Raval's working-class population, and the Eixample's artisan workshops created a neighbourhood with strong trade union and left-wing political traditions. The Carrer del Parlament is now a gentrifying street with wine bars and design shops; the political history it records has been supplanted by a different kind of social activity.
