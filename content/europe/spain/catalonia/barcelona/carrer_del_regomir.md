The name Regomir is among the most debated in Barcelona's toponymy. It appears in documents from the 12th century, which already makes it old by any measure, but its origin is genuinely obscure. One theory derives it from the Latin *regomarius*, a term related to a type of Roman magistrate or boundary marker. Another suggests it is a corruption of a phrase meaning something like "recovered the marvel" — *recuperavit mirabile* — though this smells more like folk etymology than philology. What is not debated is that the street follows a route that predates the medieval city: it runs along the line of what was once a gate in the old Roman wall of *Barcino*, and excavations have confirmed Roman-era remains beneath it.

The street sits in the Barri Gòtic, the Gothic Quarter, where the grid of the original Roman colony is still legible in the urban fabric if you know how to read it. Carrer del Regomir is short and slightly irregular — the signature of a route that was already there when the medieval city built around it. The nearby Portal del Regomir, a section of the ancient wall, is one of the better-preserved Roman structures in the city.

Streets with names this old are historical documents in their own right. The fact that Regomir's meaning was already unclear to medieval scribes suggests the word is pre-Romance — possibly a survival from an earlier language stratum. Whatever its origin, the street has carried the name continuously for at least nine centuries, which is a kind of immortality that most civic infrastructure does not achieve.
