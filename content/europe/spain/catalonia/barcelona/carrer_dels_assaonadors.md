The assaonadors were leather finishers: craftspeople who took raw tanned hides and worked them into soft, coloured, polished leather suitable for gloves, shoes, and the decorative leatherwork that was a prestige commodity in medieval Barcelona. The guild occupied this street because of its proximity to the Rec Comtal, the canal that carried water through the neighbourhood. Water was essential to their trade: the softening, dyeing, and finishing of leather required constant washing and rinsing.

The leather trades formed a cluster in the Born neighbourhood: the blanquers (tanners, who processed raw hides) operated further south, closer to the sea, where the smell was less offensive to neighbours; the assaonadors worked in a middle stage; and the cordovans and glove-makers who produced the final product occupied streets further inland. The spatial arrangement of these streets records the stages of a medieval production process.

The Carrer dels Assaonadors is now part of the Born's gallery and restaurant district, its workshop functions replaced by entirely different commerce. But the street's slight curve — following the bank of the Rec Comtal, which once ran along what is now the Carrer del Rec — records the urban geography of a medieval industrial quarter more accurately than any museum exhibit. Walk along it in either direction and you are tracing the edge of a canal that hasn't carried water for more than a century.
