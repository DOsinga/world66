The Carrer dels Banys Vells (Old Baths Street) in the Born neighbourhood takes its name from a medieval bathhouse that operated on this site from at least the 13th century. The banys — public baths — were essential institutions in medieval Barcelona, serving the large majority of residents who had no bathing facilities in their homes. The La Ribera neighbourhood's baths were among the most significant in the city.

Public baths in medieval Barcelona had a complex social history. They were associated, in the popular imagination and in municipal regulation, with the Jewish and Moorish communities who had brought bath culture from their traditions into the Christian city. The banys were permitted institutions that nonetheless carried a stigma in certain periods: the 15th-century Inquisition periodically investigated conversos who were suspected of maintaining Jewish practices, and regular bathing was one of the suspicious behaviours that might attract attention.

The Alhambra Decree of 1492 expelled Spain's Jewish community and with it the population that had most consistently maintained the bathhouse. The Banys Vells de la Ribera declined in use after 1492 and eventually closed. The building was repurposed; the street kept the name. The modern association of public bathing with relaxation and wellness would be unrecognisable to the 15th-century users of the Carrer dels Banys Vells, for whom the bathhouse was both a practical necessity and a potential theological liability.
