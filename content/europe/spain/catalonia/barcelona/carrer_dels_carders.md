Carrer dels Carders runs parallel to Carrer de la Princesa through the Born district, one block north, and takes its name from the carders — workers who combed raw wool to align the fibres before spinning. The carders' guild was one of the city's oldest, operating under municipal regulations that confined the noisier parts of the process to streets outside the original city wall: the raking and beating of wool created dust and sound that residents closer to the centre complained about. Carders was accordingly built up in the zone between the old Roman wall and the thirteenth-century wall, a position it still occupies.

The medieval craft geography of this neighbourhood is preserved almost entirely in street names. Carders sat between Assaonadors (tanners, to the south) and the dyers' quarter (to the west, near the Rec Comtal canal that provided the water for dyeing). At its peak in the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries, this grid of two or three blocks was the productive heart of one of the largest wool-textile industries in the western Mediterranean, supplying cloth across the trade routes of the Crown of Aragon.

Today the street is residential, with a scattering of workshops converted to studios, and the occasional old shop front that has survived from the early twentieth century. The scale — narrow, two or three storeys — is consistent with the medieval builds, updated and repaired but not fundamentally altered in footprint.
