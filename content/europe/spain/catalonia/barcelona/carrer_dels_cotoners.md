The Carrer dels Cotoners (Cotton Merchants Street) in the Sant Pere neighbourhood records a technological and economic revolution. For most of the medieval period, wool was the dominant textile fibre in Barcelona's manufacturing economy. Cotton existed — it came from Egypt and the Levant — but it was a specialty product, more expensive than wool and used for higher-grade textiles.

In the 18th century, this relationship reversed. New spinning and weaving technologies developed in England made cotton cloth producible at industrial scale. Catalan entrepreneurs adopted these technologies rapidly, initially using hand-powered machines and then, from the 1830s, steam. The cotton industry transformed Barcelona from a declining commercial city into the industrial capital of Spain. The Plaça de Catalunya and the Eixample were built on textile wealth.

The Carrer dels Cotoners preserves the memory of the transitional period, when cotton was still imported in raw form and distributed by specialist merchants to the weavers' workshops. By the time the factories of Poblenou were running steam-powered looms in the 1840s, the small-scale merchant organisation the street records had already been superseded by industrial capitalism. The merchant street and the factory district exist within three kilometres of each other, separated by about a century of economic transformation.
