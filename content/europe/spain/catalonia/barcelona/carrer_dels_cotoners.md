Cotton was a premium commodity in medieval Europe, imported via Levantine trade routes before the Atlantic economy made it ordinary. The cotoners on this street were not cloth-sellers in the modern retail sense but wholesale merchants dealing in raw and semi-processed cotton — sourcing it, storing it, and selling it on to weavers and finishers who worked in the same neighborhood. The Born district was the commercial engine of a textile industry that in the 13th and 14th centuries rivaled that of the Italian city-states.

Barcelona's textile wealth was built on wool as much as cotton, but the cotoners occupied a specific and lucrative niche: cotton blended with other fibers produced fustian, one of the most traded cloths in the Mediterranean world, used for everything from bedding to military padding worn under armor. The merchants on this street were participants in a genuinely international trade network, with supply chains running through Venetian and Genoese intermediaries back to Egypt and the Levant.

The proximity of Carrer dels Cotoners to Santa Maria del Mar is no coincidence. The merchant guilds of the Born district collectively funded that church, and the cotoners were among the contributors — their wealth is literally built into the fabric of the building a few streets away.
