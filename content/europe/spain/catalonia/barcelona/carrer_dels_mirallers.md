Carrer dels Mirallers is a short, slightly curved lane in the Born district that ends, abruptly and dramatically, at the curved apse of Santa Maria del Mar. The name records the mirror-makers' guild that operated here in the medieval period. Glass mirrors — as distinct from polished metal — became commercially available in Barcelona from the fifteenth century, initially imported from Venice, then made locally, and the guild settled in this block near the waterfront where the raw materials arrived.

The dead-end quality of the street, stopping at the church's stone flank, gives it an atmosphere that guidebooks about the Born consistently photograph. There is nothing else here: two rows of modest buildings, some with medieval stonework at the base, and then the massive limestone blocks of the fourteenth-century basilica's east end. The contrast in scale — three-storey domestic buildings meeting a wall of church stone — is jarring in the best way.

The mirror-makers eventually shifted production further from the port as the craft required workshop conditions less compatible with the narrow street. By the eighteenth century the guild had dispersed and the name became primarily historical. What survives of the trade's legacy in the Born is the name itself and, arguably, the glass-front boutiques that now line Carrer de Montcada nearby — a different kind of reflecting surface.
