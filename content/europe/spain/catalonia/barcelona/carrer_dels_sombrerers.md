The hat-making guild of Barcelona was among the more prosperous of the medieval craft associations. Hats were status objects: the quality of a person's hat communicated their social position as clearly as their clothes. A wealthy merchant wore a different hat from an artisan; a guild member wore a different hat from a journeyman. The sombrerers who worked in this street made hats for the full social spectrum of Barcelona's population, from the rough felt caps of the working poor to the fine velvet hats of the merchant class.

The street runs between the Carrer de Montcada — the address of the great merchant palaces — and the Plaça de Santa Maria del Mar, putting the hat-makers in the centre of the Born's commercial zone. Their proximity to Montcada was not accidental: the merchants who lived in those palaces were precisely the clients who wanted fine headwear.

The street is now associated primarily with the bar El Xampanyet, which has operated at number 22 since the 1920s and is perhaps the most famous single bar in the Born neighbourhood. The contrast between the medieval guild function and the current identity of the street — a narrow lane leading to what tourists call "the most authentic bar in the neighbourhood" — is one of the Born's characteristic ironies. The hats are gone; the cava is not.
