The Carrer dels Tallers (Workshop Street) was the main artery of the artisan quarter in the Raval north of the old city. The tallers in question were the workshops — not of a specific trade but of the general manufacturing economy that occupied this zone from the 14th century onward. Shoemakers, tailors, carpenters, and blacksmiths worked in the ground-floor premises of the buildings along this street, with their families living above.

The street runs from the Rambla to the Plaça de Castella, cutting through the Raval at an angle that reflects a pre-grid street pattern. Unlike the surrounding grid of the Eixample, the Raval's streets were laid out informally as the neighbourhood grew outside the walls, producing the irregular angles and odd corners that characterise the neighbourhood.

By the 1970s and 1980s, the Carrer dels Tallers had become the centre of Barcelona's record shop culture. Discos Castelló, established in 1934, remained on the street when most other record retailers moved to shopping centres; independent shops selling punk, jazz, electronic music, and secondhand vinyl followed. The street is now one of the few remaining examples in Barcelona of a street defined by a single type of specialist retail. The tallers — workshops — have been replaced by record shops, but the principle of a street with a defined commercial identity persists.
