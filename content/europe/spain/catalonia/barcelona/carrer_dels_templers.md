The Carrer dels Templers (Street of the Templars) is one of the shorter streets in the southern Gothic Quarter, a lane connecting the Carrer de Ferran to the Carrer de la Dagueria. The name records the presence of a Templar house — a commandery — in the neighbourhood in the medieval period. The Knights Templar maintained a significant presence in Barcelona from the 12th century onward, when Aragonese participation in the Crusades brought the order into close alliance with the Catalan nobility.

The Templars in Barcelona were primarily financiers and transport operators rather than warriors. They ran money-changing and early banking operations across their network of commanderies; pilgrims could deposit money in one Templar house and withdraw equivalent funds at another, reducing the risk of carrying cash on long journeys. They also shipped goods and people around the Mediterranean, using the same logistics network that supported their military operations.

The order was dissolved by Pope Clement V in 1312, under pressure from the French king Philip IV, who owed the Templars enormous sums and chose to destroy them rather than repay. In Aragon, as in other kingdoms, the dissolution was contested: the Aragonese king argued that the Templars had committed no crimes and their property should not be confiscated. The matter was eventually resolved by transferring most Templar property to the newly established Order of Montesa. The street in the Gothic Quarter is all that remains visible of the Templar presence in medieval Barcelona.
