Enric Granados was one of the major figures of Catalan Romanticism in music — a composer and pianist whose suite of piano pieces Goyescas, inspired by Francisco Goya's paintings, became internationally celebrated. In 1916, at the peak of his fame, he was returning to Europe after the American premiere of the opera he had drawn from the Goyescas when the ship was torpedoed by a German submarine in the English Channel. Granados swam free of the wreck but drowned trying to rescue his wife. He was 48. The street in the Eixample was named for him shortly after.

For most of the 20th century, the Carrer d'Enric Granados was a standard Eixample street occupied by parked cars and traffic. In 2012, the city pedestrianised the central section and planted trees and café terraces along it, in explicit imitation of the Rambla. The project recovered the middle lane that had existed in the original Cerdà plan — the Eixample's block structure always included provision for interior gardens and shaded walkways — but that had been lost to the car in the mid-20th century.

The street has since become one of the more pleasant walking routes in the Eixample: a linear sequence of café terraces, small food shops, and a weekly antique market on Sunday mornings. The composer whose name it bears is now remembered principally by a bronze plaque near the southern end, marking a brief life cut short by a war that he had nothing to do with.
