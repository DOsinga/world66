In medieval Barcelona, the area between the Cathedral district and the waterfront was where the craft guilds worked and lived, and the streets still carry their names. Carrer de la Fusteria is Carpenters Street. Nearby, Carrer dels Flassaders is Blanket-makers Street. Carrer de l'Argenteria, a few blocks north, is Silversmiths Street. The medieval system sorted trades by location — partly for fire safety, partly for quality control by guild inspection, partly so that buyers knew where to find a particular craft.

The carpenters who worked on Carrer de la Fusteria in the 13th and 14th centuries made furniture, structural timbers, ships' fittings, and barrels. The proximity to the waterfront was not coincidental: the port was where the timber arrived, unloaded from ships from the forests of the Pyrenees and from the maritime trade with the rest of the Mediterranean. The carpenters worked close to their materials.

The guild system that gave this street its name was a powerful social structure in medieval Catalonia. Guilds controlled apprenticeships, fixed prices, maintained quality standards, and provided a form of social insurance for members and their families. They also had political representation in the city's governance through the Consell de Cent (Council of One Hundred), Barcelona's medieval municipal government. The craft that once defined this street is long gone, but the street's name is a kind of corporate memory — the city still knows who lived here.
