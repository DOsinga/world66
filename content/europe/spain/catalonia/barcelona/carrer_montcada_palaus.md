The Carrer de Montcada is unusual in Barcelona in having preserved an almost intact streetscape of 15th and 16th-century buildings. The palaces that line it — now housing the Picasso Museum, the Museu Tèxtil, the Palau Dalmases, and several other cultural institutions — were built by the merchant families who had enriched themselves through Barcelona's commercial empire in the Mediterranean.

The street was laid out in the 12th century by the Montcada family, one of the great noble houses of medieval Catalonia, who built their palace here and gave the street its name. As Barcelona's commercial wealth grew through the 13th and 14th centuries, other wealthy families built adjacent palaces, creating a street of impressive civic architecture whose scale was unusual in the medieval city.

The palaces follow a consistent type: a large ground-floor entrance leading to an interior courtyard, with residential rooms on upper floors accessible via an external staircase in the courtyard. The arrangement prioritised the courtyard as the social and representational space of the palace: business was conducted there, guests were received there, and the staircase was the first element that visitors saw after entering. Several of the courtyards are accessible as part of the museum visits; they vary in their degree of preservation but all give a sense of the merchant aristocracy's domestic scale in the 15th century.
