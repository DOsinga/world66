The Carrer Nou de Sant Francesc (New Saint Francis Street) near the port takes its name from the Franciscan friary of Sant Francesc de Paula, which occupied a large block in this area from the 17th century until its destruction in the 1835 anti-clerical fires. The friary's Church of Sant Francesc de Paula was one of the significant Baroque ecclesiastical buildings of Barcelona's port zone; the friary complex included cloisters, a garden, and extensive storage facilities.

The 1835 fires — and the subsequent government confiscation of Church property under the Desamortització programme — released the friary's land into the commercial property market. The lots were sold to private developers who built the apartment and commercial buildings that now line the surrounding streets. The process was typical of the transformation of Barcelona's urban fabric in the 19th century: religious institutions removed, their land values captured by private developers, the streets renamed to record what had been there rather than what replaced it.

The zone between the port and the Rambla — now a relatively upmarket area of restaurants and boutique hotels — was transformed in this period from a neighbourhood of monasteries, convents, and their associated dependencies into a commercial district. The carrer's combination of "Nou" (new) and a saint's name records both the street's 19th-century construction date and the religious institution it replaced. The combination is common enough in Barcelona to go unnoticed.
