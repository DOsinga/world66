The Carrer de Roc Boronat in Poblenou was named for Roc Boronat Gironès, a textile worker and trade union activist from Poblenou who was arrested in 1941 during the Franco dictatorship's repression of labour movement leaders. Boronat had been active in the anarcho-syndicalist trade union CNT before the Civil War; after the Nationalist victory, the CNT was outlawed and its leaders were systematically arrested, tried in military courts, and executed or imprisoned.

Boronat's specific fate is not always clearly documented in accessible sources — the names and stories of Franco's thousands of victims were suppressed for decades and are still being recovered — but his commemoration in the Poblenou street map reflects a pattern of post-Franco naming that sought to restore the memory of those who were persecuted. The transition to democracy in the 1970s and the subsequent Memory Law (Ley de Memoria Histórica, 2007) created a framework for this kind of recognition, though implementation across different municipalities has been uneven.

The Carrer de Roc Boronat runs through the 22@ district, past the offices of technology companies and design studios that occupy the converted factories where Boronat's workmates once stood at looms. The street name is a piece of public memory that requires knowing who Boronat was to have any significance — which is exactly the condition that concerned the people who fought to have streets named after victims of the dictatorship.
