Casa Calvet, completed in 1900 for the textile merchant Pere Màrtir Calvet, represents Gaudí at his most disciplined — which is to say, still unmistakably himself but working within the conventions of Baroque and Rococo ornament rather than shattering them. The client was a religious man, and Gaudí incorporated cypress trees (symbols of hospitality in the old Catalan tradition), the letter *J* for the patron saint Genis, and a cross-shaped door knocker that would force visitors to knock in the gesture of crucifixion. Every ornamental decision was a theological argument.

The building won the Ajuntament's award for the best building constructed in Barcelona that year — Gaudí's only such prize during his lifetime, and all the more notable because the jury was choosing among some extraordinary competition. Barcelona in 1900 was in the full flood of *Modernisme*, with Domènech i Montaner and Puig i Cadafalch also working at peak intensity.

The ground floor is occupied by a restaurant, which gives visitors a rare opportunity to eat inside a Gaudí interior without the hustle of the Casa Batlló or Casa Milà visitor experience. The dining rooms still contain original furniture Gaudí designed for the building — making a meal here an oddly intimate encounter with one of the great designers of the modern era.
