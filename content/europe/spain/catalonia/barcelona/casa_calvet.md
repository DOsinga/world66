Casa Calvet (1900) is the anomaly in Gaudí's Barcelona work: a relatively restrained Baroque-influenced building on Carrer de Casp that could almost pass for conventional. Commissioned by the textile merchant Pere Màrtir Calvet, it was Gaudí's most bourgeois project, designed to satisfy a client who needed something that looked appropriate for a commercial building in a respectable Eixample street.

It was also the only building Gaudí completed that won an official prize — the Barcelona City Council's annual award for the best building completed that year. Gaudí never won it again; his subsequent work became too strange for award committees. The prize is commemorated by a small plaque. The building is now a restaurant at ground level, which means the extraordinary lobby and staircase are accessible to non-residents.

The details reward attention: the doorbell pushers are shaped like crosses with bugs on them (the bugs are a pest-control reference — the client was a textile merchant and bugs were an occupational hazard), the ground-floor lobby has Gaudí's characteristic ceramic and ironwork, and the upper floors have subtle Baroque curves that hint at what would follow. It is the last moment before Gaudí abandoned any pretence of fitting in.
