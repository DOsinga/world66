Casa Comalat was designed by Salvador Valeri i Pupurull and completed in 1911, commissioned by the businessman Rupert Comalat. Valeri was clearly influenced by Gaudí — the building's curved ceramic surfaces and organic ironwork place it firmly within the *Modernisme* orbit — but he pushed the structural daring in his own direction, particularly on the building's rear.

The facade on Carrer de Còrsega, less photographed than the front on Carrer de Còrsega, is where Valeri's ambition is most visible: the wall undulates in deep three-dimensional waves, with balconies that sweep in and out like the breath of the building itself. It is one of the most radical surfaces in the Eixample, a grid neighbourhood not known for allowing its buildings to spill beyond their plot boundaries.

The building is still residential, which means the interiors are unavailable, but the architectural conversation between its two facades rewards a deliberate walk around the block. Most visitors see one side; the other reveals a designer who was willing to save his most extreme experiment for the street that fewer tourists would ever walk down.
