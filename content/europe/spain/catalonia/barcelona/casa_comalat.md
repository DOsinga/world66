Salvador Valeri i Pupurull was a student of Antoni Gaudí, and the Casa Comalat on the Carrer de Còrsega is the most extravagant expression of what he learned. Built between 1909 and 1911, the building has no straight lines on its main façade: the balconies undulate, the columns curve, the windows bow outward like eyes, and the ceramic ornamentation spills across the surface without any conventional compositional framework. Valeri pushed Gaudí's organic vocabulary to its logical extreme.

The building has two façades, which is itself unusual: it occupies a corner lot in the Eixample grid but is designed to be read from the Carrer de Còrsega as its primary face, with the secondary façade on the Carrer de Còrsega exposed to the lane behind. The rear façade is notably more restrained than the front, suggesting that Valeri understood the flamboyance of his main composition and chose to confine it.

Casa Comalat is less visited than the famous Manzana de la Discòrdia buildings on the Passeig de Gràcia — Casa Batlló, Casa Amatller, and Casa Lleó Morera — but it is arguably more radical than any of them. Gaudí's buildings are theatrical, but they maintain a certain compositional logic. Valeri's building seems to have simply abandoned the idea that architecture needs a frame. It sits among the standard Eixample blocks like something that escaped from a dream.
