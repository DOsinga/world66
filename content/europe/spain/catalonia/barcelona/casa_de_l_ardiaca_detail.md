The Casa de l'Ardiaca (Archdeacon's House) on the Carrer de Santa Llúcia was the residence of the archdeacon of Barcelona Cathedral from the 12th century onward. The building has been extensively rebuilt, but its Gothic courtyard — a cloister-like space with orange trees and a fountain — is well preserved. The city's municipal archives have been housed here since 1919.

The building's most discussed element is a small marble letterbox set into the exterior wall, facing the Plaça de la Seu. The letterbox was installed in the 1890s when the building was used by the Barcelona Bar Association as a place for lawyers to deposit legal documents. The letterbox's decorative surround, carved in white marble, shows swallows in flight above and a tortoise walking slowly below.

The interpretation of the carving has been debated since its installation. The most widely accepted reading — proposed initially in the early 20th century and attributed to the architect Lluís Domènech i Montaner, who renovated the building — is that the swallows represent the flight of justice and the tortoise represents the slow pace of legal proceedings, and that the combination constitutes a quiet joke at the lawyers' expense. This interpretation may or may not be correct; the original designer's intentions were never recorded. But the image of a tortoise ambling toward a marble mail slot, on the building where lawyers deposited their documents, has been too good a story to abandon.
