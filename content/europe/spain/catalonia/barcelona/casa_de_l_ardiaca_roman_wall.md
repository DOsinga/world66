The Plaça de la Vila de Madrid contains an archaeological garden set below street level, where a section of the Roman city wall of Barcino is preserved in situ, along with a stretch of the Roman road that ran outside the wall and a number of funerary monuments. The sunken garden makes the relationship between the Roman ground level and the current street level immediately visible: the site is approximately 2 metres below the current pavement.

The Roman road outside the wall was lined with funerary monuments, following the tradition by which Romans placed their tombs beside roads leading into and out of cities rather than inside the city itself. Several of these monuments survive at the Plaça de la Vila de Madrid site, allowing visitors to see the relationship between the road, the tombs, and the city wall in their original spatial arrangement.

The site was excavated in the 1950s and has been maintained as an open-air archaeological park. It is accessible from the street by a ramp, and the excavated area can be walked through at the Roman level. The wall section visible here is of the late 3rd-century fortification — built hurriedly in response to Germanic incursions across the Pyrenees — and is constructed of large irregular blocks, some of which are repurposed from earlier buildings. The speed of the 3rd-century construction is visible in the wall's fabric: quality material used without the finish of the earlier Roman construction.
