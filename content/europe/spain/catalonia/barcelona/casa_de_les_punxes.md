Casa Terradas — universally known as Casa de les Punxes, or House of Spikes — was designed by Josep Puig i Cadafalch between 1903 and 1905 for three sisters from the Terradas family. Puig's design drew on Flemish and northern European Gothic rather than the Catalan Gothic tradition, covering the building in brick, slate, and terracotta ornament of a kind more common in Bruges or Ghent than in the Mediterranean. The six pointed towers that give the building its nickname rise above the Eixample grid with an almost aggressive foreignness.

The building is unusual in the Eixample because it occupies an entire triangular block — the triangular plots created by Cerdà's diagonal cuts at intersections were considered awkward, but Puig turned the irregularity into an asset, designing a building that reads differently from every direction and has no single "correct" front.

The decision to use a northern European Gothic idiom was politically pointed. Puig i Cadafalch was a prominent Catalan nationalist, and the building displays a shield with the Catalan legend *Sant Jordi, Patró de Catalunya* (Saint George, Patron of Catalonia) on its facade. Choosing Flemish Gothic — the style of another stateless nation with a distinct cultural identity within a larger political structure — was a deliberate architectural argument about Catalan distinctiveness.
