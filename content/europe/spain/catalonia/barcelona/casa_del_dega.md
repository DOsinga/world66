The Casa del Degà — the Dean's House — stands on Carrer de Santa Llúcia, immediately beside the cloister entrance of the Cathedral. It was the official residence of the cathedral's dean, a senior chapter official responsible for discipline and the administration of the clerical community. The building's claim to being Barcelona's oldest continuously inhabited structure rests on its lower walls: they incorporate sections of the Roman defence tower that anchored this corner of the first-century city, making the foundations some two thousand years old.

The visible stonework is layered across periods: Roman rusticated blocks at ground level, medieval ashlar above, and later additions at the upper floors. The house is narrow and tall, pressed against the Roman wall section that runs along the cathedral precinct. The Romanesque portal is probably twelfth century, contemporary with the cathedral chapter's first formal organisation of its residential buildings.

The building has been used continuously by the Church: as a dean's residence, then as a priests' welfare house, and now as an annexe of the cathedral administration. It is not open to the public, but the façade on Carrer de Santa Llúcia is fully visible, and the juxtaposition of Roman, medieval, and later masonry is legible from the street. The Cathedral's own foundation incorporated the Roman wall in a similar way; the Dean's House is the domestic-scale version of the same phenomenon.
