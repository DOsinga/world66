Casa dels Lleons — the House of Lions — sits in the neighbourhood that developed around the edges of the Ciutadella park after the military fortress was demolished in 1869 and the land handed over to the city. The streets here filled quickly with residential buildings for the middle class, and the apartment building with its lion-guarded entrance was part of that first wave of construction.

The lions themselves are carved in a style that hovers between heraldic and naturalistic — authoritative enough to signal bourgeois respectability, approachable enough that children have always climbed on them. The worn stone of their backs and heads tells the story of a century and a half of use more eloquently than any plaque.

The neighbourhood around the Ciutadella park has changed character several times since the 1870s: artisan workshop zone, then residential middle-class district, then partially declined, now gentrifying again in the wake of the park's popularity and the proximity of the Born cultural quarter. The lions have watched all of it, requiring only the occasional child to keep them company.
