Casa Fuster (1908–1911) stands at the top of Passeig de Gràcia where it meets Carrer de Provença, a white marble Modernisme building that announces the end of the boulevard with considerable authority. Lluís Domènech i Montaner designed it for Mariano Fuster Fuster, a wealthy Mallorcan entrepreneur whose wife wanted a home that would surpass anything else on the most prestigious street in Barcelona. The budget was essentially unlimited; the result was the most expensive private house built in the city up to that date.

Domènech had already designed the Palau de la Música Catalana and was completing the Hospital de Sant Pau at the same time. Casa Fuster is more classical in approach than either — white marble rather than coloured ceramic, Venetian Gothic arches rather than Modernisme exuberance — which gives it a slightly different character from his other major works. It was as if he was showing he could do restraint when required.

The building is now a luxury hotel, which has the advantage of making the ground-floor café and lobby accessible without a ticket. The original basement café, the Vienès, hosted Barcelona's intellectual and artistic life in the early 20th century — Picasso, Casas, Nonell, and others came here. Sit in the street-level café and you are more or less where they sat.
