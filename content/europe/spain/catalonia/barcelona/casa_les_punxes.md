The building that stands on the corner of Avinguda Diagonal and Carrer del Rosselló looks so much like a medieval Flemish guild hall that it seems to have been transported from Bruges. Six round towers with pointed stone spires rise from the roofline; the stone façade is carved with heraldic shields, Gothic tracery, and the kind of decorative programme associated with Northern European civic architecture of the 15th century. It was built in 1905 by Josep Puig i Cadafalch. Barcelona's nickname for it is Casa de les Punxes: House of the Spikes.

Puig i Cadafalch designed it for the Terrades family — specifically for three sisters, Joana, Rosa, and Angela Terrades, who had inherited the building lot from their father and commissioned a single building divided internally into three separate dwellings, one for each sister. The building thus solved a specific domestic problem: how to house three independent households while creating a unified architectural statement at one of the Eixample's most prominent intersections.

The Flemish Gothic style Puig chose was deliberate. He argued that Catalan Gothic architecture was related to its Flemish counterpart — that the same family of building traditions ran across the medieval trading networks of the Crown of Aragon, connecting Barcelona to the Low Countries. Casa de les Punxes was therefore a kind of argument about Catalan cultural genealogy. Whether the argument is historically defensible is less important than its visual effect: the spires of this building change the Barcelona skyline at the top of the Eixample in a way that nothing else does.
