Casa Lleó Morera was completed in 1906 by Lluís Domènech i Montaner for the widow Francesca Morera i Ortiz, and it represented the architect's most spectacular residential commission. The building's corner location on Passeig de Gràcia allowed Domènech to create a sculptural programme of extraordinary ambition — the ground floor alone was covered in allegorical figures representing modern technology: the telephone, the gramophone, the camera, the electric light.

In 1943, a leather goods firm called Loewe took over the ground floor and, in an act of commercial vandalism that still provokes outrage among architectural historians, demolished most of the sculptural decorations on the lower level to install their shopfront. Several of the stone figures were reportedly smashed; some were sold to private collectors and have since been partially traced. The ground floor was not restored to anything approaching its original character until partial reconstruction in the early 2000s.

The building sits between Casa Amatller (by Puig i Cadafalch) and Casa Batlló (by Gaudí), and together they form the *Manzana de la Discordia* — Block of Discord — a name that is partly a pun on the word *manzana*, which means both "block" and "apple," evoking the golden apple of Eris. Each building represents a different strain of Modernisme, and the tension between their approaches is still visible and still productive.
