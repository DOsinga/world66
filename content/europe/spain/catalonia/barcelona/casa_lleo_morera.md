The block of Passeig de Gràcia between Carrer d'Aragó and Carrer del Consell de Cent is called the Manzana de la Discordia — the Block of Discord — because three of Catalonia's greatest Modernista architects each put their most ambitious buildings here in the same decade. Casa Batlló (Gaudí) and Casa Amatller (Puig i Cadafalch) draw the biggest crowds. The third building, Casa Lleó Morera at number 35, is often overlooked.

It was designed by Lluís Domènech i Montaner and built between 1902 and 1906 for Francesca Morera, widow of the leather industrialist Albert Lleó. The building's original ground floor was a masterwork of integrated Modernista design: stone sculptures, ceramic tiles, mosaics, and stained glass worked into a unified composition. The figures in the stone represented allegories of the new technologies — a woman speaking into a telephone, another with a photograph, another with an early electric lamp.

In 1943, the ground floor was gutted. The Loewe leather goods company took over the commercial space and demolished the original sculpted portal and stonework to create a modern storefront. Most of the original sculptures were lost or destroyed; a few fragments were salvaged and are now in the Museu Nacional d'Art de Catalunya. The upper floors of the building survive intact and are extraordinary — the turrets, the ceramic surface, the carved stone — but the ground floor amputation is visible to anyone who looks: the building starts talking at the first floor and goes silent where the street-level sculpture used to be.
