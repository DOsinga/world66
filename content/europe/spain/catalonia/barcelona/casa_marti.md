Casa Martí was built in 1896 by Josep Puig i Cadafalch in a style deliberately evoking Flemish Gothic — a pointed reference to the cultural connections between Catalonia and northern Europe. But the building's reputation rests entirely on what happened in its basement: Els Quatre Gats (The Four Cats) opened in 1897 as a self-conscious imitation of the Chat Noir in Paris, designed by the artist and bohemian organiser Pere Romeu as a meeting place for the Barcelona artistic avant-garde.

The young Pablo Picasso — then Pablo Ruiz Blasco, recently arrived from Málaga and hungry for recognition — made Els Quatre Gats his headquarters. In February 1900 he mounted his first solo exhibition there, showing portraits of his fellow regulars. The guest list for the café in those years reads like a directory of Catalan cultural history: Ramon Casas, Santiago Rusiñol, Isidre Nonell, Miquel Utrillo, Joaquim Mir.

The café closed in 1903 as the bohemian moment passed, became a series of other businesses, and was eventually re-opened as a restaurant in 1978 with a careful recreation of the original atmosphere — right down to reproductions of the Ramon Casas poster showing two men riding a tandem bicycle, one of the most iconic images of Barcelona's *Modernisme*. Whether this constitutes preservation or pastiche is a question the city has decided not to answer.
