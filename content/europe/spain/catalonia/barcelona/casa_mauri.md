Casa Mauri sits in the Born neighbourhood, close to the church of Santa Maria del Mar, in a district that was the commercial and artisan heart of medieval Barcelona. The building itself has the characteristic layered quality of old-city properties — medieval foundations, subsequent modifications, a facade that has been updated more than once without ever entirely erasing what came before.

The chocolate shop at street level has deep roots in the neighbourhood's trading history. Barcelona was one of the first cities in Europe to embrace chocolate after Spanish colonial contacts with Mesoamerica, and the city's chocolatiers and confectioners established themselves in the Born quarter in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries. The craft tradition in the shop represents a direct connection to that history, even as the tourist trade has now become the primary audience.

The juxtaposition of medieval architecture and artisan food production is not accidental in the Born. The neighbourhood's rehabilitation after years of decay has been careful to preserve precisely this kind of layered commercial continuity, insisting that the buildings should still smell of something rather than becoming purely decorative shells for boutique hotels and concept stores.
