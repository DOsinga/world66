Enric Sagnier i Villavecchia was one of the most prolific architects of the Barcelona *Modernisme* era, responsible for hundreds of buildings across the city and its suburbs, yet his name rarely appears in the standard accounts of the movement. Casa Mulleras, commissioned by Ramon Mulleras in 1904, is typical of his best work: technically accomplished, ornamentally precise, and thoroughly overshadowed by the famous buildings nearby.

The building sits on Passeig de Gràcia just around the corner from the Block of Discord, which means it competes for attention with Gaudí, Domènech i Montaner, and Puig i Cadafalch simultaneously. Sagnier's approach was more classical in its proportions and more restrained in its ornament than any of those three architects, but the ironwork balconies and stone carving show a craftsman's attention to detail that rewards a slow look.

Sagnier's career is a useful corrective to the heroic narrative of *Modernisme* that focuses exclusively on three or four geniuses. The movement succeeded architecturally because there were dozens of competent practitioners capable of executing the vocabulary at a high level. Casa Mulleras represents the serious, unheroic middle of that achievement.
