Josep Maria Jujol designed the ironwork, mosaics, and much of the decorative detail on Gaudí's greatest buildings — the benches at Park Güell, the ceiling of the Batlló, the ceramic collage of La Pedrera's rooftop. He was Gaudí's most talented collaborator and in some respects his artistic equal, but he never achieved the same fame and his independent buildings are almost unknown outside Catalonia.

Casa Planells (1924) at the corner of Avinguda Diagonal and Carrer de Sicília is the main exception visible in Barcelona. The building curves around the Eixample chamfered corner in a way that Jujol made completely his own: the balconies undulate, the window openings are irregular, and the overall effect is organic rather than Modernisme-exuberant. It was completed six years before Gaudí's death and shows Jujol moving in a direction entirely different from his mentor — quieter, more introspective, less interested in symbolic programme.

The building is residential and not open to visitors. It is best seen from across the Diagonal, where the full curve of the corner becomes visible. Most people walk past it without registering that it is the work of the man who made Park Güell's famous serpentine bench.
