Manuel Sayrach i Carreras designed Casa Sayrach in 1918, toward the very end of the *Modernisme* period — by that point the movement had largely given way to Noucentisme, a classicising reaction against what critics saw as *Modernisme*'s excess. Sayrach was one of the last architects to work in a fully Gaudí-influenced idiom, and the building shows both the strengths and the slightly overwrought quality of a style being pushed past its natural moment.

What makes the building unusual is that Sayrach built it for himself, which meant there was no client to moderate his choices. The result is one of the more personal buildings in the Eixample — every curved surface, every piece of ironwork, every ornamental detail reflects the architect's own priorities rather than a negotiation with a patron. The building is simultaneously a home and a manifesto.

The Eixample grid was designed by Ildefons Cerdà in the 1860s with the explicit goal of creating equal, rational, airy city blocks. What happened in practice was a competition among the wealthy to see who could most dramatically violate the grid's democratic intentions while remaining technically within the building regulations. Casa Sayrach is a late and particularly exuberant entry in that competition.
