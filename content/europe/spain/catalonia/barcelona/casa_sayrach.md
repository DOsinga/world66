Casa Sayrach stands on Carrer d'Enric Granados, a pedestrianized street in the Eixample that runs parallel to and a few blocks west of the Passeig de Gràcia. The building was completed in 1918 by Manuel Sayrach i Fatjó, an architect whose work falls outside the standard Modernisme canon precisely because it came at the end of the period, when the movement was already fading. By 1918 the great commissions had been built — Gaudí's Casa Milà was finished in 1912, Casa Batlló in 1906 — and the architectural fashion was already shifting toward Noucentisme's more restrained classical references.

Sayrach built anyway, and Casa Sayrach is the result: a building with an undulating stone facade that recalls the biological curves of Gaudí's work while being entirely its own thing. The surfaces ripple and flow; balconies are shaped like leaves or waves; the stone is worked in ways that conventional Eixample buildings would never attempt. Sayrach was a mystic as well as an architect — he wrote religious and philosophical texts — and he reportedly embedded theological proportions into the building's design, making the dimensions correspond to symbolic numbers from Marian numerology and Pythagorean philosophy. Whether these encodings survive in the built structure is a question for specialists.

The building houses apartments and receives very few visitors compared to the celebrated Passeig de Gràcia buildings four blocks east. The Carrer d'Enric Granados street itself was converted from a two-lane road to a pedestrian boulevard with trees and bicycle lanes around 2012, which has made the building easier to look at — you can stand back far enough to see the whole facade without traffic passing between you and it.
