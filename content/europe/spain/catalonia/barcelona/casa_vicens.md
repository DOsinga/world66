Casa Vicens (1883–1888) is where Gaudí started. Built for the tile manufacturer Manuel Vicens i Montaner in what was then the independent town of Gràcia, it is his first major completed work and the one that shows most clearly where he began: Orientalism, Moorish architecture, and the geometric tile patterns that his client's factory produced. The house is covered in green and white tiles with marigold motifs — Vicens grew marigolds in his garden.

The building was a private residence until 2014 and is now a museum, which means it was missed by generations of Gaudí tourists who didn't know it existed. It sits on a corner in a residential Gràcia street with no particular fanfare, the façade a dense pattern of colour and geometry that looks more like a Persian palace than a Barcelona townhouse. This was the point: Gaudí was 31 years old, recently qualified, and trying to show that Catalan architecture could draw on the full heritage of the Iberian Peninsula, including its Islamic past.

The ironwork gate has palm fronds. The dining room ceiling inside is painted with swallows. The formal language is nothing like what Gaudí would develop later — no parabolic arches, no organic forms — which makes Casa Vicens essential for understanding the arc of his thinking. He was interested in historical style before he found his own.
