Casa Vicens was Gaudí's first significant independent commission, built for the tile manufacturer Manuel Vicens i Montaner between 1883 and 1885 in what was then the separate municipality of Gràcia. Vicens presumably hired the young architect in part because the tile industry connection was obvious — Gaudí covered the building in his client's product with a freedom that suggests either great trust or great nerve on the designer's part.

What is remarkable about the building, looking back, is how completely it prefigures everything that came after. The Moorish-influenced geometric ornament that wraps the walls anticipates the intricate geometric patterns of the Sagrada Família crypt. The use of natural forms in the ironwork — palm leaves, in particular — points toward the organic naturalism of Casa Batlló and Casa Milà. The structural use of the wall as a surface to be animated rather than simply enclosed already breaks with the conventions Gaudí had been trained to follow.

Casa Vicens became a museum in 2017 after more than a century of private occupation, which meant the interior had been modified significantly but the exterior had been preserved with unusual fidelity. The neighbourhood around it in Gràcia remains one of the more architecturally coherent parts of the city — a dense, human-scaled urban fabric that makes the building's extravagance land differently than it would on a grand boulevard.
