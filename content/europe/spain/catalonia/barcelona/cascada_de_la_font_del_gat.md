La Font del Gat — the Cat's Spring — is one of the natural water sources on Montjuïc that gave the hill its character as a place of recreation for city dwellers long before any formal gardens were laid out. The name *gat* (cat) may refer to the shape of the original spring's rock formation, or to local legend; the etymology is disputed, as it often is with place names that predate written records.

The restaurant that grew up around the spring served as a destination for Sunday excursions from the working-class neighbourhoods below Montjuïc, where families would carry their own food and rent a table for the afternoon shade. The 1918 cascade was added as an ornamental improvement to the existing water source, in the years when the city was beginning to think seriously about landscaping Montjuïc ahead of the 1929 International Exhibition.

The gardens here were later designed by the botanist Joan Prat, and they retain a character different from the formal gardens higher up the hill: shadier, wilder, and more genuinely oriented toward pleasure than display. The cascade is the garden's focal point, but the whole area rewards the kind of unstructured afternoon wandering that city parks seldom seem to encourage anymore.
