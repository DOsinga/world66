The Cascada del Parc de la Ciutadella was built between 1875 and 1882 under the direction of Josep Fontserè i Mestre for the 1888 Universal Exhibition. Fontserè was a self-taught architect and hydraulic engineer, and the cascade — a theatrical composition of arches, grottos, winged horses, and a chariot of Aurora at the summit — was his most ambitious project.

The young Antoni Gaudí worked as an assistant to Fontserè during this period, still a student at the architecture school, and his biographers have long debated which elements of the design bear his fingerprints. The standard claim is that Gaudí contributed to the hydraulic calculations and possibly some of the ornamental detail, but the attribution remains uncertain because student assistants were not credited in the documentation of the time.

The cascade is deliberately theatrical in a way that the rest of the Ciutadella park is not. Fontserè designed a park landscape of lawns, promenades, and the artificial lake that still functions today — but the cascade was the centrepiece, designed to be impressive at a distance and discovered up close through a series of grottos and pools. The military citadel it replaced had been built to overawe the city; the cascade was built to delight it, which is a more interesting form of ambition.
