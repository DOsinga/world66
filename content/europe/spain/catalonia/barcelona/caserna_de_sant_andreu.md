The Caserna (Barracks) de Sant Andreu was a military installation built in the mid-19th century in what was then the independent municipality of Sant Andreu de Palomar. The barracks occupied a large urban block, with high walls enclosing the parade ground and buildings within. Military installations of this type were placed in the expanding suburban municipalities of Barcelona partly for tactical reasons — they were positioned on key roads — and partly because land was cheaper outside the city walls.

The barracks remained in use until the late 20th century, when the Spanish army reduced its urban presence as part of the post-Franco military reform. When the military vacated, the city acquired the land and converted the buildings to social housing — replacing an institution that had excluded the public from a large urban block for 150 years with housing for residents who needed it.

The conversion preserved the barracks' outer walls and the spatial structure of the internal courtyard, creating a housing development with an unusual perimeter: high 19th-century military walls surrounding a newly created residential interior. The neighbourhood around the former barracks is shaped by its presence — the surrounding streets were built to follow the barracks perimeter, creating the slightly awkward angles and oversized blocks that characterise areas where large institutional buildings once imposed their geometry on the surrounding city.
