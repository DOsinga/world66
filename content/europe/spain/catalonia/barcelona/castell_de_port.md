Castell de Port was a watchtower and small fortification built in the medieval period to observe the coastline and the approach to Barcelona's port from the southwest. The site had strategic value when the landscape around it was open — you could see ships arriving, and you could signal to the city in time to prepare. The hill it occupies, the Turó de la Rovira of the Zona Franca, once offered clear sightlines.

The surrounding landscape has since been completely transformed by the industrial development of the Zona Franca, the vast logistics and port zone that occupies the southwestern corner of the city. Container terminals, warehouses, highways, and rail yards now surround the castle ruins, which sit on their small hill like a marooned medieval island in a sea of twentieth-century infrastructure. The strategic view that justified the castle's location is now entirely blocked.

This kind of stranding — ancient monument islanded inside industrial or residential development — is common enough in Europe but rarely as visually dramatic as at Castell de Port. The castle's isolation from any tourist infrastructure means it sees almost no visitors, which gives the site an authenticity that more accessible ruins often lack. Getting there requires navigating the Zona Franca's logistics streets on foot, which is its own minor adventure.
