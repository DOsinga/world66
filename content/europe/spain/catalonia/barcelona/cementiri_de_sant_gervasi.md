The village of Sant Gervasi was an independent municipality in the hills north of Barcelona until it was annexed by the city in 1897, along with several other surrounding towns. The village had its own church, its own mayor, and its own cemetery — the small burial ground now called the Cementiri de Sant Gervasi, set on the hillside above the neighbourhood that still bears the village's name.

The cemetery is significantly smaller than the Cementiri de Poblenou and less visited than the Cementiri de Montjuïc. Its character reflects the social composition of the Sant Gervasi neighbourhood: the tombs are those of professional families, architects, doctors, and small industrialists who built villas in the hills north of Barcelona in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The area was fashionable as a residential choice for those who wanted distance from the city's density without losing access to it.

Several notable Barcelona cultural figures are buried here. The cemetery is also unusual in having Catalan-language inscriptions on many tombs from before the Civil War — evidence that the village's educated class maintained Catalan in private and funerary contexts even during the periods when it was suppressed in public. The hillside setting, with views across the Collserola, gives the cemetery a character significantly different from the more monumental urban cemeteries of Barcelona proper.
