The Pati Llimona civic centre takes its name from the lemon-tree courtyard (*pati* means courtyard; *llimona* means lemon) that was once part of the complex of buildings on this site. But the real story is underground: the centre was built above the preserved remains of a Roman watchtower and section of the city wall of Barcino, as well as traces of a medieval episcopal palace and the old Gothic city fabric above.

The site lies within what was the administrative heart of the Roman colony — the area around the forum, where the praetorium (governor's headquarters) stood. Barcelona's extraordinary density of Roman remains in the Gothic Quarter exists because the medieval city was built directly on top of the Roman one, using the old walls as foundations, meaning the Roman archaeology was preserved rather than cleared away.

The basement of the Pati Llimona is accessible during opening hours and offers one of the more affecting experiences of layered time in the city: Roman masonry below, medieval stonework above it, and a functioning contemporary civic space on top. The civic centre itself hosts neighbourhood activities, exhibitions, and cultural events — the lemon-tree courtyard, though the tree is long gone, still functions as a gathering place.
