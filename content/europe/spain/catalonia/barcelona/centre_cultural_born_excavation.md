The excavation beneath the Born market hall revealed not a single archaeological layer but a complex stratigraphy of urban life. The upper strata showed 17th-century modifications; deeper, the streets and foundations of the neighbourhood as it existed before 1714 emerged. Archaeologists could follow specific house plans: a room with a hearth, a cellar with storage vessels intact, a street corner where two houses met, a well still holding groundwater.

The destruction of the La Ribera neighbourhood in 1715 was executed with systematic thoroughness. The Bourbon forces demolished approximately 1,000 houses, relocating the inhabitants — against their will and without adequate compensation — to other parts of the city and to the new Barceloneta settlement built on the sandbank to the south. The rubble was spread across the former neighbourhood and used as fill for the Ciutadella fortress's parade ground and surroundings.

The fill preserved the underlying structures perfectly. Sealed beneath 2-3 metres of demolition rubble, the street surfaces, thresholds, and lower wall courses of the 1714 neighbourhood remained largely intact for 290 years until the discovery in 2001. The Born Cultural Centre's exhibition allows visitors to walk on paths above the excavation, looking down at the preserved level. The most affecting moments are when an individual object is visible: a ceramic bowl where it was left in 1715, or a doorstep worn smooth by centuries of feet that stopped in 1715.
