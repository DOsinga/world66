The Centre Martorell d'Exposicions was designed by the architect Antoni Rovira i Trias and built in 1882-84 as part of the infrastructure being assembled for Barcelona's 1888 Universal Exhibition. It takes its name from Joan Martorell i Montells, the architect and restorer who was closely associated with the Ciutadella park's development and who was also, incidentally, the teacher who recommended Gaudí for several of his early commissions.

The building housed the natural history collections of the city — geological specimens, botanical samples, fauna — in a Neoclassical pavilion that was deliberately restrained in comparison to the more theatrical structures elsewhere in the park. Natural history in the 1880s was still primarily a matter of classification and collection, and the architecture suited that purpose: clear, orderly, didactic.

The collections eventually moved to what is now the Museu de Ciències Naturals in the Forum area, and the Martorell building was repurposed as a general exhibition space. The transition from permanent scientific collection to temporary cultural programming reflects a broader pattern in Barcelona's cultural infrastructure: buildings outlast their original purposes and find new ones, which is itself a kind of institutional natural selection.
