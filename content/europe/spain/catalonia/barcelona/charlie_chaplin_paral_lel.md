The bronze figure of Charlie Chaplin's Tramp character stands on the Avinguda del Paral·lel, the entertainment avenue that runs along the southern edge of the Raval neighbourhood from the Plaça d'Espanya to the Columbus Monument. The choice of location is historically specific: the Paral·lel in the early 20th century was Barcelona's principal popular entertainment district, hosting dozens of theatres, music halls, cabarets, and cinema houses.

Chaplin's films were shown on the Paral·lel almost simultaneously with their American release. The Tramp character — immigrant, poor, resourceful, sentimental — resonated with a neighbourhood audience that included many internal migrants and the working-class population of the Raval. The Paral·lel's theatres showed everything from serious drama to circus acts; the cheap cinemas that eventually displaced the music halls brought Chaplin to an audience that had few other cultural options.

The Paral·lel's entertainment identity has been partially recovered since the 1980s, after decades in which it served primarily as a traffic artery. The Teatro Apolo, the Teatre Condal, and other venues have reopened or continued operating along the avenue. The Chaplin bronze was installed as part of this recovery programme — a nostalgic gesture toward the early 20th-century character of a street that remains recognisably an entertainment district even if the audience and the entertainment have changed entirely.
