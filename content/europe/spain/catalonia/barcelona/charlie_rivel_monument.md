Josep Andreu Lasserre, who performed as Charlie Rivel, was born in Cubelles near Barcelona in 1896 and died in the same town in 1983. He spent the intervening 87 years largely on stage — he was performing into his eighties — and became one of the most celebrated clowns in the history of European circus, known for an act that combined acrobatic skill, musical comedy, and a trademark cry of "Acrobat!" delivered in a high-pitched nasal voice.

Rivel's career took him to every major circus in Europe and to numerous appearances in film and television. He performed under Franco without political incident, toured the Soviet Union in the 1970s, and was received by royalty across Europe. The clown's profession, like the sardana, occupied a cultural space that political authorities found difficult to regulate — it was too popular, too entertaining, and too apparently innocent to suppress.

The monument on the Paral·lel, near the circus venues where Rivel performed in the early decades of his career, is part of the avenue's commemoration of its entertainment history. The Paral·lel was where Rivel made his Barcelona reputation before taking his act to international venues. The bronze captures the sad-clown persona that was his performance identity — the melancholy beneath the comic, which is the tradition of the great European clowns from Grock to Chaplin that Rivel inhabited.
