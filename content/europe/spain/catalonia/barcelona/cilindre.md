Andreu Alfaro was Valencia's great minimalist, and *Cilindre* — installed in 1992 as part of Barcelona's Olympic public art programme — is one of his most emphatic statements. Where other sculptors gave the Olympic Village heroic figures and allegorical groups, Alfaro offered a cylinder: nothing more, nothing less. The form is so simple it feels like a provocation.

Alfaro spent his career arguing that geometry itself carries emotional weight. The circle, repeated in three dimensions as a cylinder, stood for him as a universal symbol — complete, self-contained, democratic. He had no interest in depicting power or triumph; he wanted a shape that any person, regardless of language or education, could encounter on equal terms.

The cylinder's placement at the edge of the new district, where the Olympic Village opened onto the sea, gives the work a quietly dramatic context. The geometry holds its ground against the horizon, and the piece functions less as decoration than as a kind of orientation mark — something fixed and clear in the planned landscape around it.
