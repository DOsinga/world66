The Cinema Maldà occupies an unusual position: it is inside the Pati del Maldà, a covered shopping arcade in the Gothic Quarter, entered through a passage from the Carrer del Pi. The cinema itself is not visible from the street. You walk through a narrow passage, past small shops, and the cinema appears at the back — a single auditorium that has been operating more or less continuously since 1931.

The building was originally the palace of the Marquis of Maldà, a Catalan noble family. The conversion to commercial use came in the 19th century, when the palace was transformed into an arcade of shops — a common fate for aristocratic palaces as the merchant class displaced the hereditary nobility. The cinema was added in the 1920s as the silent film era reached Barcelona.

The Maldà survived the transition from silent to sound film, from Spanish-language dubbing to original-language programming (which it introduced in the 1980s), and from single-screen to multiplex competition. It survived primarily by programming art-house and independent cinema — a specialisation that gave it an audience not served by the commercial cinemas. By the 2020s, it was one of fewer than ten single-screen cinemas still operating in Barcelona, most of which had converted to multiplexes or closed entirely. The palace arcade that contains it, entered from the Carrer del Pi, is itself one of the more atmospheric surviving 19th-century commercial interiors in the Gothic Quarter.
