The Romans built a circus — a chariot racing track — in Barcino in the 1st century CE. The circus lay northeast of the forum, running along the line of what is now the Carrer dels Comtes toward the current Plaça del Rei. The structure was approximately 350 metres long and 75 metres wide, typical dimensions for a Roman provincial circus, capable of holding several thousand spectators.

The circus fell out of use as Roman imperial power declined in the 4th and 5th centuries. Its massive vaulted substructure — the arcaded basement that supported the seating banks — survived because it was too solid to demolish and too useful to ignore. Medieval buildings were constructed on top of and within the circus substructure; the arcade vaults became the ground level of the new buildings.

The Saló del Tinell — the great reception hall of the medieval count-kings, built in the 14th century — sits on the eastern end of the former circus. The Count's palace and the current Plaça del Rei occupy the footprint of the chariot track. The MUHBA underground museum makes portions of the Roman circus visible, including sections of the vaulted substructure that supported the seating banks. This specific geological layering — a Roman sports venue beneath a medieval palace beneath a tourist attraction — compresses urban history into vertical space rather than horizontal distance.
