The Columna Olímpica stands at the heart of the Nova Icària neighbourhood, which the Olympic Village replaced. That neighbourhood had been part of Poblenou — the so-called "Manchester of Catalonia" — an area of factories, workers' housing, and small industries that was demolished to make way for the Games. The column, erected in 1992, marks the moment of transformation, though its classical form says nothing directly about what was lost.

The design draws on the language of Roman triumphal columns — a tradition stretching from Trajan's Column to Napoleon's Place Vendôme pillar — and transplants it into the context of a democratic sporting event. The tension between that imperial visual language and the stated values of the Olympics is one the column doesn't resolve; it simply holds both in the same vertical form.

For walkers coming from the beach end of the Olympic Village, the column operates as a kind of landmark, marking the transition between the coastal promenade and the residential blocks of the new neighbourhood. Its height is calibrated to be visible from some distance, giving the otherwise flat grid a vertical anchor.
