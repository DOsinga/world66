In the Fossar de la Pedrera — the old stone quarry-turned-cemetery on the Montjuïc hillside — there is a memorial to Lluís Companys i Jover, President of the Generalitat of Catalonia, who was executed by firing squad here on 15 October 1940. He was arrested in France, where he had fled after the fall of the Republic, by the Gestapo acting at Franco's request, delivered to Spanish authorities, subjected to a military trial that lasted twenty minutes, and shot at dawn in the fortress above.

Companys refused to wear shoes for the execution. He wanted to die with his feet on the Catalan soil where he had been born. He is said to have cried "Per Catalunya!" — For Catalonia! — before the order was given. He was 58.

The detail that makes his case historically singular is its European context. Companys is the only democratically elected head of government who was arrested by the Gestapo, extradited by the Third Reich to a third country, and executed there. The Spanish government formally rehabilitated him in 2006, sixty-six years after his death. The memorial in the Fossar de la Pedrera — a semicircular wall of red brick behind a garden — is an active place of commemoration. On 15 October each year, representatives of Catalan political parties lay wreaths here. His case is also memorialized in the naming of the Passeig de Lluís Companys in the city centre, the promenade that leads from the Arc de Triomf to the Parc de la Ciutadella.
