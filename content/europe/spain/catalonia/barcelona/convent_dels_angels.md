The Convent dels Àngels was built in the 1560s for the Dominican tertiary sisters on what was then the far edge of El Raval, beyond the old city walls. The order ran the convent until the 1820s, when the suppression of religious orders under Liberal government forced them out. For the rest of the nineteenth century the building served as a barracks, a depot, a school — uses that stripped out the interior but left the shell standing.

What saved it was architecture. When the Foment de les Arts Decoratives (FAD) federation needed a permanent home in the 1990s, the convent offered both prestige and a Gothic cloister that could serve as an exhibition courtyard. The lobby and the surrounding rooms now host design and craft exhibitions throughout the year, with a reading room in what was once the chapter house. The cloister arcade in particular — low, sturdy Gothic arches around a central well — is exceptionally well preserved for a building that spent 150 years as a military store.

The square in front, Plaça dels Àngels, is now best known as the skate spot at the base of the MACBA ramp. The two functions — medieval convent and skate culture — coexist at close range, separated only by a metre of cobblestone.
