Carmelo Cappello was a Sicilian sculptor who spent much of his career in Milan, and *Crescendo appare* belongs to a body of work preoccupied with rhythm, movement, and the translation of musical ideas into three dimensions. The title borrows from musical terminology — a crescendo is a gradual increase in volume — and Cappello applied it to a form that seems to expand and rise as you move around it, changing silhouette with each angle.

The work was installed on Montjuïc, the hill that has served as Barcelona's exhibition ground since the 1929 International Exposition, and later as the site of the 1992 Olympic stadium and sports complex. Cappello's sculpture sits within a tradition of putting ambitious public art on the hill as a way of signalling cultural ambition — the Fundació Joan Miró is a hundred metres away.

The Italian title is itself a kind of puzzle for Catalan and Spanish passersby — *crescendo* is recognisable from musical vocabulary, but *appare* (it appears) requires Italian. The slight foreignness of the phrase enacts what the sculpture itself attempts: something that reveals itself gradually, that requires patient attention rather than instant recognition.
