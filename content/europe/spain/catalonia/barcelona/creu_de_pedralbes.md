The Creu de Pedralbes (Cross of Pedralbes) stands near the entrance to the Monastery of Pedralbes, which Queen Elisenda de Moncada founded in 1326. The cross is a Catalan creu de terme — a wayside cross marking the boundaries of an estate or the jurisdiction of a religious institution. Crosses of this type were placed at the roads leading into a monastery's property to indicate that travellers were entering consecrated or protected territory.

The Pedralbes cross is a Gothic structure of the 14th century, with a decorated shaft and a carved cross head showing the Crucifixion on one face and the Virgin on the other. It is among the most complete surviving examples of this type in Catalonia — most creus de terme have been damaged or lost over the centuries. The cross was originally freestanding in open countryside; the monastery's rural estate extended for considerable distance around it.

The Pedralbes area was agricultural land in the 14th century, well outside the city. The monastery was built here precisely because it was in the countryside, away from the noise and distraction of the urban environment. The 14th-century decision to put a monastery in open farmland has now resulted in one of Barcelona's finest medieval buildings being surrounded by the expensive residential neighbourhood that grew up in the 20th century. The creu de terme that marked the edge of the monastic estate now stands on a busy street, surrounded by parked cars.
