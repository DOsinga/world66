The Dama del Paraigua (Lady with Umbrella) is a cast-iron fountain sculpture in the Parc de la Ciutadella, created in 1884 by the sculptor Joan Roig i Solà. The figure shows a woman in 19th-century dress holding an open umbrella, from which water flows. The combination of an elegant bourgeois lady and a functional water-spouting mechanism is characteristic of the decorative public fountain type popular in the late 19th century.

The sculpture became one of the most affectionately regarded public objects in Barcelona — less monumental and more domestic than the city's larger statuary, more immediately approachable than the bronze generals and politicians that fill its squares. It was reproduced as a postcard image throughout the 20th century and became something of an unofficial symbol of the park.

The phrase "la dama del paraigua" entered Catalan popular usage as a reference to a well-dressed woman with pretensions — the image of bourgeois propriety made slightly absurd by the function of dispensing water. The fountain has been restored several times; the current installation is a faithful reproduction of the original. Its location in the Ciutadella park, near the children's boating pond, gives it a setting of deliberate family accessibility that fits its character as a beloved rather than intimidating artwork.
