Nou Barris — 'Nine Neighbourhoods' — was for most of the 20th century one of Barcelona's most underserved districts: a ring of working-class settlements built quickly and cheaply to house migrants from southern Spain during the Franco years. When the city began its major programme of public art investment in the 1980s and 1990s, Nou Barris was deliberately included. The sculptures placed there were not afterthoughts but assertions that the periphery deserved the same cultural investment as the Eixample or the Gothic Quarter.

*Dell'Arte* — Italian for 'of art' — is a large bronze piece celebrating performance and creativity. The Italian title, in a Catalan city, gestures toward the Mediterranean shared culture that transcends national borders, and toward the tradition of commedia dell'arte that gave European popular theatre much of its vocabulary. The performing arts have deep roots in Nou Barris: the district has produced street performers, musicians, and festival traditions that predate any official cultural investment.

The work's scale is deliberately ambitious for its location — the kind of serious, large-format public sculpture that was previously reserved for central Barcelona. Its presence in Nou Barris is an argument that working-class neighbourhoods are not waiting to receive culture from above but already have it, and that public art should acknowledge rather than import that fact.
