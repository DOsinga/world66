Josep Llimona created the original *Desconsol* — Desolation, or Inconsolable Grief — in 1907, and the white marble figure of a woman collapsed in silent despair immediately became one of the most admired works in Barcelona's public collection. The figure's posture conveys complete surrender to grief: she does not perform her pain for an audience but withdraws entirely into it. There is no theatrical gesture, no upturned face appealing to heaven — just a body folded over its own sorrow.

Picasso was living in Barcelona during the period when the sculpture was new, and he returned to the image of Llimona's figure in his own work, absorbing its formal qualities — the compressed volume, the turned-in posture — into his developing vocabulary. The *Desconsol* sits at the intersection of modernisme's religiosity and its psychological depth; Llimona was a deeply Catholic artist, and the figure can be read as Mary in lamentation, though nothing requires that reading.

The version in the Ciutadella park is a later cast; the original marble is in the Museu Nacional d'Art de Catalunya on Montjuïc. But the park setting, with water nearby and the surrounding trees creating a kind of enclosure, gives the work exactly the atmosphere Llimona seems to have imagined: private grief in a public place, left undisturbed.
