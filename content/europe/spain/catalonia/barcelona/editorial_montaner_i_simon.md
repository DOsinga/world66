Lluís Domènech i Montaner designed the Editorial Montaner i Simón building in 1880, a full decade before the term *modernisme* had settled into common usage. He was working at the intersection of several influences: the Gothic Revival's enthusiasm for honest materials, the Arts and Crafts movement's rejection of industrial ornament, and a specifically Catalan tradition of brick construction that went back to medieval guild halls. The result was a building that used exposed red brick not as a sign of poverty or incompletion but as deliberate aesthetic choice.

The publishing house that occupied the building produced, among many things, the *Gran Enciclopèdia Catalana* — the comprehensive Catalan-language encyclopaedia that became one of the most significant cultural projects of the 20th century's push to preserve and revitalise Catalan identity. When the Fundació Antoni Tàpies acquired the building in 1990, it completed a circle: a space built for the dissemination of Catalan knowledge became home to the archive and legacy of the artist who was perhaps most responsible for putting Catalan art on the international map.

The crown of the building — the wire sculpture by Tàpies himself, *Núvol i Cadira* (Cloud and Chair) — sits on the roofline of Domènech's brick facade, and the collision between the 1880 structure and the 1990 addition is one of the most productive visual arguments in the Eixample's streetscape.
