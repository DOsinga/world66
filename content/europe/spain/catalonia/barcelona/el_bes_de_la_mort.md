The Bes de la Mort (Kiss of Death) is a white marble sculpture group in the Cementiri de Poblenou, installed in 1930 on the tomb of the Llorenç family. The sculpture shows a winged skeletal figure — Death personified — bending over a young man and placing a kiss on his forehead. The young man appears to be asleep rather than dead; his face is peaceful, his body relaxed. The skeletal figure is not threatening but tender.

The sculptor Jaume Barba created the piece at the height of Art Nouveau and Symbolist funerary art in Catalonia, when wealthy families commissioned elaborate sculptural programmes for their tomb monuments. The tradition had been developing since the late 19th century and produced some of the finest sculptural work in Barcelona — work that happened to be in a cemetery and was therefore less visible to the general public than the monuments in parks and plazas.

The Bes de la Mort became the most celebrated of these cemetery sculptures almost immediately after its installation. Photographs of it circulated widely; it was reproduced on postcards and in magazines. The combination of beautiful sculptural technique, erotic charge (the embrace is intimate, not merely devotional), and the subject of death-as-lover gave it a resonance that more conventional funerary sculpture lacked. It has been the most visited single object in the Cementiri de Poblenou for nearly a century, and the cemetery has become something of a pilgrimage destination specifically because of this one sculpture.
