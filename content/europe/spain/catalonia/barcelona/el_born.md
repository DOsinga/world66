El Born — officially part of the Sant Pere, Santa Caterina i la Ribera district — was the commercial heart of medieval Barcelona, where merchants and craftsmen clustered in guild streets that still carry their names: Carrer dels Sombrerers (hatters), Carrer de l'Argenteria (silversmiths). Grand stone palaces line Carrer de Montcada, several now converted into museums, and the whole quarter has reinvented itself as the city's most stylish browsing ground.

The basilica of Santa Maria del Mar, a masterpiece of Catalan Gothic built in just 54 years by the parishioners themselves, anchors the southern end. To the north, the iron-and-glass Mercat del Born — once the city's wholesale market — now shelters excavated 18th-century streets beneath a glass floor. Between them runs the Passeig del Born, a wide promenade that was the site of medieval jousts and is now lined with cocktail bars.

This is the place for the Picasso Museum, for cava and anchovies at El Xampanyet, and for the independent boutiques and ceramics shops along the narrow lanes. It is compact, walkable and consistently one of the most charming corners of the old city.
