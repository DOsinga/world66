The street sign at the top of Passeig del Born reads simply EL BORN — the name of the neighbourhood and, before it was a neighbourhood name, the name of the open ground that occupied this space outside the medieval city walls. Born comes from the Catalan version of the French word buhurt, a type of tournament in which mounted knights charged each other in groups rather than in the one-on-one jousting of popular imagination. The buhurt was noisier, more dangerous, and more popular: a mass cavalry charge, controlled only loosely, which sent both riders and spectators scrambling.

Barcelona's Born tournament ground was in use from at least the 13th century, when the La Ribera neighbourhood was developing rapidly and needed an open space for public events. Tournaments were the sports of the medieval nobility, but the crowds who watched them were drawn from all social levels. The ground also served as the site of public executions and religious processions, which in medieval civic life were part of the same category of public spectacle as tournaments.

The area was gradually built up from the 15th century onwards as the neighbourhood expanded into the former tournament ground. The Passeig del Born — the promenade that replaced it — follows exactly the dimensions of the old field. Stand at one end and look toward the other: the width and length of the boulevard is the width and length of the medieval tournament ground. The neighbourhood name persists everywhere: El Born for the area, Born for the market, Born for the cultural centre, Born for the bar on the corner. The knights are long gone but the word for their melee has never left.
