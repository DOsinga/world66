El Call — the word comes from the Hebrew qahal, meaning community or congregation — was the Jewish quarter of medieval Barcelona, occupying a small dense cluster of streets in the southwest corner of the Gòtic quarter, bounded roughly by Carrer del Call, Carrer de Sant Sever, and Carrer de la Fruita. At its peak in the 13th and 14th centuries, the community numbered around 4,000, making it one of the largest and most intellectually important Jewish communities in Catalonia and the Crown of Aragon.

The community produced scholars of significance: Nachmanides (Rabbi Moses ben Nachman), born in Girona, maintained close connections with the Barcelona community and was summoned to the famous Barcelona Disputation of 1263, in which he was forced to debate Christian theology before King Jaume I. Jewish physicians, astronomers, and cartographers worked in royal service across Aragon and Castile; the Cresques family, who produced the Catalan Atlas of 1375 — the most accurate world map of the medieval period — were from Mallorca but part of the broader Catalan Jewish intellectual network.

In August 1391, the community was destroyed. Anti-Jewish violence that had spread across Castile reached Barcelona; the Call was invaded by a mob and the community was killed or forcibly converted over several days. The community never recovered: the survivors who had converted nominally, the conversos, were subjected to ongoing suspicion, and the quarter was not reestablished. The physical traces were largely erased or absorbed into the surrounding city. In 1996, workers renovating a building on Carrer de Marlet uncovered what archaeologists identified as a medieval synagogue — one of the oldest surviving synagogue structures in Europe. It is now open as a small museum.
