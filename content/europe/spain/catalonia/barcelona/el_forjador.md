The Gothic Quarter's street names are a directory of medieval crafts: the Carrer dels Cotoners (cotton workers), Carrer dels Flassaders (blanket makers), Carrer dels Sombrerers (hat makers). The neighbourhood was organised by guild, and the guild system structured every aspect of artisan life — training, quality control, prices, the right to work. To be a *forjador*, a blacksmith or metalworker, was to belong to one of the most essential trades, one that supplied tools to every other craft.

*El Forjador* — The Blacksmith — stands as a tribute to that world. The figure is caught mid-strike, hammer raised, and the pose carries the full weight of physical labour without romanticising it. This is not a decorative craftsman but a working one, and the scale of the sculpture — larger than life — treats manual work with the seriousness usually reserved for military or ecclesiastical subjects.

The placement in the Gothic Quarter gives the work its strongest resonance. The streets that surround it still carry the names of the people who once worked in them, and the cobblestones underfoot were laid by other forgotten labourers. The blacksmith stands in the middle of a neighbourhood that has largely converted to tourism but was shaped by centuries of hard physical work, and his presence insists on that memory.
