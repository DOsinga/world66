La Pedrera in Montjuïc means 'the quarry', and the quarry in question supplied stone for much of Barcelona's 19th-century construction. When the quarry was exhausted, its hollows were incorporated into the cemetery on the hill — and during and after the Spanish Civil War, those hollows became the burial sites of thousands executed by Francoist forces. The numbers are approximate and contested, as was the intention: Republican prisoners, Catalan nationalists, trade union leaders, ordinary people caught on the wrong side of the firing squad were buried without individual markers.

Lluís Companys, the elected president of the Generalitat de Catalunya, was executed here on 15 October 1940 after being handed over by the Nazis from occupied France. He requested to be shot barefoot, wanting to die touching Catalan soil. His grave is one of the most visited in the cemetery.

The space was reclaimed and redesigned as a memorial in 1985, with a geometric structure by Beth Galí that creates a contemplative garden above the mass graves. Every year on September 11, Catalonia's national day, thousands come here. The *Fossar de la Pedrera* is not a conventional war memorial; it is a site of ongoing grief and ongoing political meaning, where the dead are invoked in present-tense arguments about Catalan autonomy and justice.
