The building known as El Gasòmetre stands near the Barceloneta neighbourhood as a remnant of the mid-19th-century infrastructure that made Barcelona the first city in Spain with gas street lighting. The Société Barcelonaise de l'Eclairage au Gaz was established in 1842, and the gas plant was built in 1853 to supply the growing demand. The circular main building — the gasometer itself, where gas was stored under pressure — was a technological marvel of its era.

Gas lighting transformed urban life in ways that are difficult to overstate: it extended the effective day, changed the economics of night-time commerce, and made certain spaces — particularly theatres, cafés, and the Rambla — social centres in a new way. Barcelona's Boqueria market, the Liceu opera house, and the major hotels all had gas lighting before the 1860s. The Gasòmetre supplied them all.

The building was subsequently converted for other industrial uses as electricity replaced gas for lighting in the early 20th century. What remains is the shell of the circular structure, now part of the industrial zone between Barceloneta and the Olympic Port. It serves as a reminder that the Barceloneta neighbourhood was, for most of its history, as much an industrial district as a fishing community — the fishing quarter imagery that dominates today's tourist identity is largely a post-war and post-Olympic construct.
