Philip V built the Ciutadella fortress in 1715 to control Barcelona after its surrender on 11 September 1714 — a date Catalans still commemorate as La Diada (National Day). The fortress was designed with its cannons capable of pointing into the city it supposedly defended. When Catalans finally won the right to demolish it in 1869, the choice of what to build on the footprint was politically loaded: a public park, free and open, was the most direct possible inversion of the site's function. Park designer Josep Fontserè i Mestre began work in 1872, and the oval lake was part of the original design.

The waterfall — Cascada — and its sculptural programme were completed in time for the 1888 Barcelona Universal Exposition held in the park. The commonly repeated claim that this is "Gaudí's fountain" requires qualification: Fontserè was lead designer; Gaudí contributed as a young student assistant, working on the rocky grotto elements and the hydraulic engineering of the water system. The attribution to Gaudí has grown with his reputation; the fountain is more accurately Fontserè's.

Rowboats have operated on the oval lake with interruptions since the park opened, and the lake accommodates up to five people per boat year-round. The lake hosts over 100 bird species and is surrounded by small islands and aquatic plants. Its elliptical form allows boats to complete circuits without reversing — a practical choice that also gives the lake an elegance of geometry suited to its role as the park's calm centre, the still point around which the rest of the 1888 spectacle revolved.
