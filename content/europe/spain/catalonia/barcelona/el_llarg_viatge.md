The Diagonal Mar neighbourhood, built in the late 1990s and early 2000s on former industrial land at the mouth of the Besòs river, is one of Barcelona's most self-consciously designed districts — glassy towers, wide pavements, a large shopping mall, and a park with an artificial lake. The area was developed as part of the Forum 2004 project, which attempted to leverage another major international event (the Universal Forum of Cultures) in the same way the Olympics had transformed Poblenou a decade earlier.

*El llarg viatge* — The Long Journey — sits in this designed landscape and takes the port city as its conceptual ground. Barcelona has been a place of arrival and departure for two millennia: Phoenician traders, Roman colonists, medieval merchants, 20th-century labour migrants from Andalusia and Murcia, and more recently arrivals from South Asia, North Africa, and Latin America. The journey is always long; the city at the end of it is always changing.

The sculpture's placement at the new waterfront extension gives it an appropriate context. Diagonal Mar was itself built on a long journey — from industrial wasteland to residential neighbourhood to aspirational waterfront district — and the piece invites reflection on whether each transformation carries the memory of what came before it, or whether new construction is always also a form of forgetting.
