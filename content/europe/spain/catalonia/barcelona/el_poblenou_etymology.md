The name Poblenou (New Village) is startling in its simplicity. Unlike most Barcelona neighbourhood names — which refer to saints, noble families, topographic features, or historical events — Poblenou simply acknowledges that it is a newer settlement than the surrounding ones. The name dates from the 19th century, when the fishing community that had occupied the waterfront east of Barcelona distinguished itself from the older fishing village of Barceloneta by referring to itself as the "new" settlement.

The neighbourhood's origins are in the 17th and 18th centuries, when fishermen established a community on the sandy coast east of the Ciutadella fortress. The community remained small until the industrial expansion of the 19th century transformed the waterfront. The fish market was important to Barcelona, but it was displaced by the factories and shipyards that occupied the best waterfront positions. The fishing community moved slightly inland as the industrial economy took over the shore.

The name Poblenou persisted through the industrial period, when the neighbourhood became one of the most densely industrialised zones in Spain — more appropriately called a factory district than a village. It persists into the post-industrial present, when the factories are being converted into offices. "New Village" is now a century-old name for a neighbourhood that has been a factory zone and is becoming a technology district. The instability of all three identities is somewhat obscured by the name's cheerful stability.
