*Resolís* — sometimes spelled *ratafia* in other Catalan traditions — is a sweet liqueur made from anise, cinnamon, and brandy, historically produced in Catalonia and served as a digestif or restorative. The bar at its named location in the old city was founded when such drinks were still everyday commerce; it sold and served the local liqueur at a time when every neighbourhood had its own production and its own version. The drink has since retreated from common Barcelona bar culture, which makes the name a small piece of preserved nomenclature.

The bar has centenary character in a part of the old city — streets adjacent to the Gothic Quarter — that has been transformed almost entirely by tourism. The surrounding streets now run dense with restaurants catering to visitors; the bar has persisted on a different logic, serving the diminishing population of old-city residents who have not been displaced by rising rents. That persistence is increasingly rare in this geography.

El Resolís is worth a stop not for spectacle but for orientation: it represents what the old city's own social infrastructure looked like before the tourist economy reorganised the neighbourhood around visitors' needs. Order whatever they have on draught and consider the street outside.
