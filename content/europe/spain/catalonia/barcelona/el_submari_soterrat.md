Àngel Jové's *El Submarí Soterrat* — The Buried Submarine — belongs to a tradition of conceptual public art that treats absence as its primary material. The 'work' is a submarine that may or may not be buried underground at this site; what is visible at street level is only a marker, a sign indicating that something significant lies beneath. Whether the submarine is really there is, in one sense, beside the point — the piece operates on the question rather than the answer.

The work was installed in the Gràcia district, a neighbourhood that has historically cultivated a spirit of independence and eccentricity distinct from the surrounding city. Gràcia's artistic community has tended toward the experimental, and a piece that refuses to show itself fits the local tradition of testing the boundaries of what public art is allowed to do and to be.

The buried submarine is also a metaphor with obvious political resonance in a Catalan context. Things hidden underground — history, identity, language, bodies — have a particular weight in a culture that spent forty years of dictatorship concealing aspects of itself from official view. Jové's piece does not labour this point, but it creates a space where the question of what lies beneath the surface of the everyday can be asked.
