Water has been the axis of Barcelona's civic life since the Roman settlement of Barcino tapped the Besòs river. Every major expansion of the city — the 19th-century Eixample, the Olympic waterfront transformation, the Diagonal Mar development — required first solving the question of water: where it came from, who controlled it, how it was distributed. *Elogi de l'aigua* places itself in that long tradition by choosing water as its explicit subject.

Antoni Llena, who is better known for conceptually charged works like *David i Goliat* in the Olympic Village, here produced something more celebratory. The fountain is large and the flowing water central to the piece's effect — the sculpture is not complete without the sound and movement of water working with its forms. Llena treats the fountain tradition seriously rather than ironically, producing a work in which praise is the genuine intention.

The Vall d'Hebron district, where the piece is installed, was developed as part of the Olympic infrastructure — the velodrome and archery range were here — and subsequently integrated into the surrounding residential fabric. The fountain serves partly as a landmark for the new neighbourhood and partly as a reminder that the Olympic transformation was always also a question of infrastructure, of water and roads and public space, not just sports facilities.
