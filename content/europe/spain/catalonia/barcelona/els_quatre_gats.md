Els Quatre Gats (The Four Cats) opened in 1897 in the Casa Martí, a neo-Gothic building by Josep Puig i Cadafalch on Carrer de Montsió. The founders modelled it consciously on Le Chat Noir in Paris — the name is a Catalan expression meaning "a handful of people" (just four cats), but also a direct reference to the black cat logo of the Montmartre cabaret. The idea was to transplant bohemian Paris into Barcelona.

It worked, for a few years. Picasso, then 18, designed the menu cover and held his first public exhibition here in February 1900. Ramon Casas, Santiago Rusiñol, and Miguel Utrillo were regulars. The café staged puppet shows, cycling demonstrations, shadow plays, and artistic tertúlies (discussion gatherings) that made it the centre of Catalan Modernisme's social life. It closed in 1903 — four years was apparently enough.

The building itself is worth examining even if you don't go inside. Puig i Cadafalch designed the Casa Martí in 1896 in his signature neo-medieval Catalan Gothic style, with pointed arches and stone tracery that make it look older than it is. A reproduction of the Casas painting of himself and Pere Romeu on a tandem bicycle (the original is in the MNAC) hangs inside. The café reopened in 1978 and still functions as a restaurant; the prices reflect the address.
