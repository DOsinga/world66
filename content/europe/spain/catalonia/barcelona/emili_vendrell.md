Emili Vendrell (1893–1962) was one of the great Catalan tenors of the early 20th century, celebrated for a voice of exceptional warmth and his deep immersion in the traditional music of his culture. He was born in Barcelona's Barceloneta neighbourhood and trained in the city before becoming a fixture of the Liceu opera house and the concert halls of Catalonia. But his international reputation rested less on operatic roles than on his interpretations of Catalan folk songs.

The song most associated with Vendrell is *El Cant dels Ocells* — The Song of the Birds — a traditional Catalan lullaby and folk melody. His 1938 recording of it, made as the Civil War was ending in Franco's favour, became extraordinarily charged. The melody was adopted by the Catalan exile community as an unofficial anthem; Pablo Casals, the cellist who also went into exile and refused to perform in Franco's Spain, played it at every concert for the rest of his long life as a statement of Catalan identity and resistance.

The plaque in the neighbourhood where Vendrell lived connects his story to a specific Barcelona geography — the streets and sounds of the Barceloneta, the bars where Catalan songs were sung quietly when singing them loudly was dangerous. His voice, preserved on recordings, carried something of the city with it into exile and, eventually, back.
