Jean-Claude Nicolas Forestier arrived in Barcelona in 1915 at the invitation of city planners preparing Montjuïc for the 1929 International Exposition. The French architect was already celebrated for his work on the Bois de Boulogne in Paris and the Seville parks, and he brought to the hill a rigorous Mediterranean sensibility — terracing, water, shade, and axial drama all working together. The stairway that bears his name was conceived as a ceremonial approach linking the lower gardens to the exposition grounds above.

What makes the Forestier Steps genuinely unusual is the layered geometry: each landing opens onto a different garden room, so descending is less like using a staircase and more like moving through a sequence of outdoor salons. Forestier believed parks should choreograph experience rather than simply provide greenery, and the cascade exemplifies that philosophy — every level offers a framed view of the city below, timed to emerge as you pause.

Forestier died in 1930, the year after the Exposition closed, and his name faded from popular memory while Montjuïc's more theatrical landmarks took the credit. The steps remain one of the most carefully wrought pieces of landscape architecture in Barcelona, beloved by local joggers and picnickers who rarely know the man who designed them.
