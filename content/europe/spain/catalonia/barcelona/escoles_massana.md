The Hospital de la Santa Creu complex on the Carrer de l'Hospital housed the Escola Massana — a school of art and design — in some of its medieval ward buildings. The school was established in 1929 and has occupied the hospital's medieval halls continuously since the hospital's medical functions moved to the modern Hospital de Sant Pau in 1930. The combination of 15th-century Gothic vaulting and contemporary art and design education creates an architectural context that would have seemed surreal to either the medieval founders or the 20th-century pedagogues.

The Escola Massana's specific connection to the hospital is through Antoni Massana, a Barcelona art teacher who left a bequest in 1929 to found the school. The choice of the hospital as the venue was pragmatic — the city needed to find uses for the medieval building after the hospital relocated — but it has produced an institution with an unusually specific character. Design students working on typography or jewellery in a 15th-century Gothic ward are aware, usually, of where they are.

The hospital complex has a further particular significance: the ward where Antoni Gaudí was brought after being struck by a tram on the Gran Via in June 1926 is identified by a plaque. Gaudí died in the hospital three days after the accident. He was not immediately identified at the scene — he was dressed poorly and was not carrying identification — and was brought to the charity ward of the medieval hospital as an unknown pauper. The most celebrated architect in Barcelona died in the institution his city had built for its poor in the 15th century.
