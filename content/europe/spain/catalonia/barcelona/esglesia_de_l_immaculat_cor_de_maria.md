The Claretian Missionaries — officially the Congregation of the Missionary Sons of the Immaculate Heart of Mary — established their Barcelona house in the Eixample at the turn of the twentieth century. The order had been founded in 1849 by Antoni Maria Claret, a Catalan priest who became Archbishop of Santiago de Cuba and later confessor to Queen Isabella II. The Barcelona church was intended as a missionary base, not a parish, which partly explains its different character from the neighbourhood churches around it.

The facade is restrained by the standards of modernista Barcelona, with ornament concentrated at the entrance and the cornice rather than spreading across the entire surface. Whether this reflects a deliberate theological statement about missionary simplicity or simply the preferences of the architect is unclear from the historical record, but the contrast with the exuberant facades of nearby Eixample churches gives the building a composed, almost severe quality.

Claret himself was canonised in 1950, and the church became a minor pilgrimage point for the Claretian network. The order runs educational and media operations globally — Claret was a prolific publisher and founded what became one of Spain's largest Catholic publishing houses — and the Barcelona church sits within that tradition of combining religious practice with organised communication.
