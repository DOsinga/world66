The Estació de França (France Station) was built for the 1929 International Exposition as the principal railway terminus for long-distance trains arriving from France via the Pyrenean border crossings. The existing station on the site was demolished and replaced with a new building in an eclectic neoclassical style: a dignified street façade of stone columns and pilasters, concealing a magnificent iron and glass train shed behind it.

The train shed — the operational part of the station — is 244 metres long and covers eight tracks under a barrel-vaulted roof of steel and glass. Its scale makes it among the most impressive surviving railway interiors in Spain, comparable to the Atocha station in Madrid or the Príncipe Pío. The combination of neoclassical formality in the façade and industrial engineering in the shed is typical of late 19th-century railway architecture, applied here in 1929 in a deliberate act of stylistic conservatism.

The station declined steadily after the opening of the Sants central station in 1979, which took over as Barcelona's main long-distance terminus. The Estació de França now serves regional and some long-distance routes, with far fewer trains than its capacity was designed for. The result is a magnificent building operating at perhaps a tenth of its intended volume: enormous waiting halls, grand concourses, and the great vaulted shed serving a fraction of the passengers it once handled. It is possible to walk through almost all of it without encountering another person.
