Cobi was designed by Javier Mariscal in 1987 and immediately divided opinion. The Catalan graphic artist had created a figure that looked nothing like the friendly, legible mascots that sporting events typically deployed: Cobi was geometrically fractured, expressionist, clearly descended from Cubism and the Catalan avant-garde tradition rather than from cartoon conventions. Sports officials were alarmed; the design press was fascinated; the public eventually adopted the strange dog as their own.

The mascot's name derived from COOB — the Catalan Olympic Organising Committee's initialism — which Mariscal converted into a fictional proper name. Cobi appeared everywhere during the Games, selling merchandise and representing Barcelona's self-conscious effort to present not just an athletic event but a cultural identity. The design became a case study in brand identity and the use of avant-garde aesthetics for mass communication.

The pool on Montjuïc is a gentle memorial to that cultural moment. It sits within the Olympic Ring, the ensemble of venues built and renovated for 1992, which transformed Montjuïc and — more broadly — transformed Barcelona's international image. The city that emerged from the Olympics was confident, design-conscious, and globally recognised in a way it had not been before, and Cobi was one of the faces of that transformation.
