The four rusting iron cubes stand at the edge of the Barceloneta beach at the end of Carrer del Mar, leaning at angles to each other like a stack of books about to fall. They were installed in 1992, when Barcelona hosted the Olympic Games, and they are the work of the German sculptor Rebecca Horn. The title means "Wounded Star" in Catalan.

The cubes are not just abstract. They are the same dimensions as the chiringuitos — the beach shacks and bars — that lined this stretch of Barceloneta beach for most of the 20th century. The chiringuitos were demolished by the city in advance of the Olympics, swept away as part of an urban improvement programme that cleaned the beach and the seafront but also erased a decades-old informal culture of cheap food, music, and local gathering. Horn's response was to memorialize what had been removed in the same material — iron — at the same scale, in the same location, deliberately oxidized so that the rust reads as wounds or tears.

The sculpture is deliberately uncomfortable in its posture. The cubes lean at angles that suggest instability, as if they might fall at any moment, which creates a persistent low-level unease that seems appropriate given what it marks. Horn has described it as a way of "returning the memory of the place to the place itself." Stand at the waterline and look back at the four cubes against the Barceloneta apartment blocks: the architecture of the city behind, the sculpture in front, the sea coming in.
