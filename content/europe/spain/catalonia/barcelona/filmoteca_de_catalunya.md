The Filmoteca's location is as significant as its collection. The Raval — Barcelona's most densely populated neighbourhood through much of the 19th and early 20th centuries — was historically the zone outside the city walls where activities too loud, dirty, or morally suspect for the old town were tolerated: tanneries, foundries, hospitals, convents, and eventually the Barri Xino, the red-light district that attracted writers from Jean Genet to George Orwell. The neighbourhood's 20th-century rehabilitation has been deliberate and ongoing, and the Filmoteca, along with the MACBA and the CCCB, is part of the sustained institutional effort to convert the Raval's cheap space and architectural density into cultural infrastructure.

Josep Lluís Mateo's 2012 building takes its street presence seriously. The main façade is set back to create a small public plaza — the Plaça de Salvador Seguí, named after an anarchist labour leader assassinated nearby in 1923 — that gives the archive a forecourt the surrounding streets don't otherwise allow. Inside, two cinemas with a combined capacity of around 500 seats show daily screenings of archive material alongside new films, making the Filmoteca an active cinema rather than a preservation vault.

The collection runs to approximately 40,000 titles, with particular strength in Catalan and Spanish cinema from the Franco period — decades during which Catalan-language production was suppressed, making the archive's holdings a form of cultural recovery as much as conservation. The Filmoteca also holds the papers of several major Catalan directors, and its library and media library are open to researchers.
