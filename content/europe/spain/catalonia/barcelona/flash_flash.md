Flash Flash opened in 1970 on Carrer de la Granada del Penedès in the Gràcia-adjacent stretch of the Eixample. The designers were Leopoldo Pomés, a photographer and graphic designer, and Cirici Pellicer. The concept was total: white silhouetted figures of models on black walls, floor to ceiling, photographed by Pomés himself and integrated into the room as architectural elements rather than decoration. The result is one of the most coherent pop-art interiors in Spain, and it has survived essentially unchanged.

The menu has always specialised in tortillas — Spanish omelettes — in a deliberate refusal of the nouvelle cuisine that was redefining Barcelona restaurants in the same period. The tortilla menu runs to dozens of variations: the kitchen does one thing and does it comprehensively. This was a creative choice in 1970 and remains a creative choice now, when the restaurant's contemporaries have mostly been renovated out of recognition or closed. Dalí and Miró ate here; so did the Barcelona creative class of the 1970s, for whom it functioned as a canteen.

Flash Flash is in the Guinardó-adjacent Eixample, away from the tourist core, and draws a clientele of Barcelona residents who appreciate both the food and the room. The tortilla de patatas is the anchor of the menu. The interior, seen in full on a busy evening, is as vivid as it was 55 years ago.
