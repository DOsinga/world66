The fountain stands at the head of La Rambla — an ornate four-armed cast-iron post with a globe lamp and four small taps, installed in 1892 to carry water from the springs of Collserola into the city. There is nothing obviously remarkable about it. Hundreds of people walk past it without pausing. But locals know that if you drink from it, you will come back to Barcelona, and after 1936, the fountain acquired a second identity: it became the place where FC Barcelona fans gather to celebrate championships.

The name comes from the springs that once fed it: canaletes, little channels, referring to the wooden pipes that first carried mountain water down to this spot in the 16th century. The Victorian-era cast-iron structure replaced an older marble fountain in the 1880s, but the location was already known as the watering point where travellers from the north entered the city — and where, according to popular tradition, any water you drank bound you to the place.

The football connection began organically. When Barça won the league title in 1936, the celebrations spilled spontaneously onto La Rambla and concentrated around the fountain, partly because it was already the gathering point of the northern end of the boulevard and partly because supporters drank the fountain water to seal the celebration. The tradition has continued ever since: when Barça wins the Champions League, the Copa del Rey, or the Liga, this is where the crowds assemble, sometimes tens of thousands strong, spilling off the pavement and into the road while television helicopters hover overhead. On ordinary days, the fountain taps still work, and the water is still cold.
