The legend of the Font de Canaletes says that anyone who drinks from this four-spigot cast-iron fountain will return to Barcelona. The legend is old, though its exact origins are disputed — versions of it appear in 19th-century popular literature about the city. The word canaletes (little channels) refers to the system of water channels that brought spring water from the Collserola hills to the city; a water distribution point at this location has existed since at least the 16th century.

The current cast-iron fountain was installed in 1892, replacing an earlier stone structure. It is not, therefore, the "original" fountain of the legend, though it occupies approximately the same position. This distinction is rarely mentioned in descriptions of the site.

The fountain became the gathering point for FC Barcelona supporters following matches in the 20th century, a function that has given it its most contemporary associations. After significant Barcelona victories — particularly in the Champions League finals — the fountain is surrounded by thousands of people. The tradition was established in the 1930s, when the cafe directly opposite broadcast match reports by radio to crowds who could not afford to attend or who were following teams playing away from home. The crowd gathered at the fountain to hear the results; a winning result meant celebration in place.

The fountain is thus simultaneously a piece of 19th-century urban infrastructure, a mythological element in the city's self-image, and a sports monument — three overlapping identities that coexist without contradiction.
