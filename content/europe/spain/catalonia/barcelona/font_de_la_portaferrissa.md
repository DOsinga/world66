The name *Portaferrissa* means 'iron gate', and this street was the site of one of the gates in Barcelona's second medieval wall, built in the thirteenth century as the city expanded beyond the Roman perimeter. The gate had an iron grille and a small fountain built into the wall beside it, where travellers entering the city could drink. The current fountain, rebuilt with a ceramic tile panel showing the old gate and its surroundings, is a direct memorial to that medieval original.

The tile panel is the key document. Painted in the Catalan ceramic tradition, it shows the gate as it appeared in engravings from the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries: an arched opening in a thick wall, with the fountain at its foot and the city stretching behind. It is one of the few places in the Gothic Quarter where you can stand on the exact spot of a medieval landmark and see a faithful image of what was once there.

The Portaferrissa gate was demolished in the nineteenth century, as were most of Barcelona's medieval walls, to make way for the expanding city. The street that replaced it became a commercial artery — it still is today, lined with shops — but the fountain with its ceramic panel holds the ghost of the gate in place. It is the kind of small, precise memorial that rewards slow walkers.
