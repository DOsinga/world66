The name alone stops you: the Cod Fountain. It stands on Carrer de les Colomines, a narrow lane in the old Born district near the Santa Caterina market, and it does exactly what it says — it is a fountain that was named for the salt cod once sold in the stalls here. Bacallà, dried and salted cod, was a staple of Catalan working-class cooking for centuries; the smell of it would have been part of the street's daily air.

The fountain is a collaboration between Enric Miralles and Benedetta Tagliabue — the same architectural partnership responsible for the Santa Caterina market's undulating ceramic roof just around the corner. It is built from marble, functioning as both a mural and a water source. The design is restrained compared to the exuberance of the market roof, which is perhaps the point: a small street piece, a neighbourhood amenity, not a landmark.

Miralles died in 2000 before the market was completed; the firm he founded with Tagliabue finished it in 2005. This fountain is a quieter piece of their work in the neighbourhood — findable mainly if you know to look. The area around it has changed enormously since the days when the smell of bacallà defined the street: it is now all restaurants and short-stay apartments. The name survives.
