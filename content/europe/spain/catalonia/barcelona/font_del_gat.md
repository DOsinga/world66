Font del Gat — the Fountain of the Cat — sits in the pine woods on Montjuïc's lower north slope, about halfway up the hill from Paral·lel. The current building is a modest Modernista pavilion from 1906, designed by Lluís Domènech i Montaner's studio, set into the hillside around a natural spring whose water was channelled through a carved stone cat's mouth. For decades it operated as a café-restaurant, and it still does on summer weekends, though the kitchen is simpler than in its heyday.

The linguist Pompeu Fabra — author of the normative grammar that standardised modern Catalan — made Font del Gat his daily destination for much of the 1910s and 1920s. He is reported to have walked up from his flat in the Eixample most afternoons, ordered coffee, and worked on his dictionary at the same table. The café's association with writers and intellectuals during the Noucentisme period gave it a reputation far beyond its modest appearance.

The spring is still running. The sound of water through the stone cat below the terrace gives the place a quality entirely absent from the tourist-oriented Montjuïc above: it feels like what a Barcelonan neighbourhood park feels like, not a municipal attraction. The walk up from Carrer de la Lleialtat takes about twelve minutes through gardens.
