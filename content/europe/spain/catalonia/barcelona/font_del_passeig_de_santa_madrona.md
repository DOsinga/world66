The Passeig de Santa Madrona climbs from the port district into the lower slopes of Montjuïc, and before the mountain became a park it was a working landscape: quarries, fortifications, smallholdings, and the chapel of Santa Madrona that gave the path its name. The saint was venerated by dockers and port workers — the *estibadors* — who would walk up to her chapel to seek protection before heading out to the dangerous work of loading and unloading ships.

Santa Madrona herself is a figure from Catalan hagiography whose historical basis is uncertain; she is said to have been a Christian martyr whose remains were discovered in Barcelona and became a focus of popular devotion among the working people of the port district. Her chapel on Montjuïc was a destination for pilgrimage and oath-taking by men whose work at the waterfront was physically dangerous and economically precarious.

The fountain on the passeig is the kind of functional marker that tells you about the human geography of a place before it was beautified. Workers climbed this hill; they needed water on the way. The saint watched over the port below. The fountain and the path together are a map of a devotional and working landscape that has since been overlaid by parks, museums, and the Olympic ring.
