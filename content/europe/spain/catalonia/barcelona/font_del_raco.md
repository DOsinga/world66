The Font del Racó (Corner Fountain) is one of several natural springs in the lower Collserola hills that were used by Barcelona residents for centuries before the city's water infrastructure was modernised in the 19th century. The spring emerges from the hillside at a point where a small depression — the racó, or corner — catches the slope's drainage.

Before the Companyia d'Aigües de Barcelona began constructing modern water infrastructure in the 1860s, the city relied on a combination of the Rec Comtal canal, wells, and natural springs for its water supply. The springs in the Collserola foothills were considered superior in quality to the well water and canal water of the city, and it was common practice for residents of Sant Gervasi, Gràcia, and the northern Eixample to make Sunday walks to the springs to collect water for drinking.

Several of these springs have been preserved as public fixtures, with carved stone spouts and stone basins. The Font del Racó is one of the better-preserved examples, reached by footpaths through the lower Collserola park. The tradition of walking to the spring for clean water, which in the 19th century was a necessity, became a leisure activity in the 20th century as piped water made the walk optional. People still come, often with bottles, as much for the walk and the setting as for the water itself.
