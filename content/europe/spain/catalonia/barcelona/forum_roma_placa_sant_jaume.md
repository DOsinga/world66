The Plaça de Sant Jaume, where the Generalitat and the City Hall face each other across the cobblestones, is not merely Barcelona's current civic centre. It is the direct successor to the Forum Romanum of Barcino — the central public space of the Roman colonial city established in the 1st century BCE. The Roman forum occupied approximately the same area as the current square, slightly shifted but overlapping substantially.

This continuity — civic space in the same location for 2,000 years — is not unique to Barcelona among Roman cities, but it is unusually complete here. The square was the commercial, judicial, and religious centre of Roman Barcino; it became the administrative centre of the medieval Catalan state; it is now the political centre of autonomous Catalonia. The institutions that face each other across it — the Generalitat (autonomous government) and the Ajuntament (city hall) — are the 21st-century equivalents of the Roman praetorium and the curia that occupied the same space in the 2nd century.

The forum's physical remains lie beneath the square and the surrounding buildings. The Museu d'Història de Barcelona, accessed from the Plaça del Rei, allows visitors to walk through the excavated Roman streets and structures that extend beneath both squares and the Cathedral. The Temple of Augustus — whose columns survive on the Carrer del Paradís — stood at the forum's edge. The continuity of this specific urban location from Roman colony to medieval county capital to modern autonomous government is one of the most complete examples of civic persistence in Western Europe.
