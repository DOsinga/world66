The Casa Montaner i Simó (1880) on Carrer d'Aragó is generally considered the first Modernisme building in Barcelona — completed eight years before the 1888 Universal Exhibition that made the movement famous. Lluís Domènech i Montaner designed it for his family's publishing firm, and the building's exposed brick, iron columns, and large windows were a deliberate rejection of the historicist styles fashionable at the time. He published a manifesto the same year calling for a new Catalan national architecture.

The building became the Fundació Antoni Tàpies in 1990. Tàpies, Catalonia's greatest post-war artist, worked in textures and materials that often looked more like geological formations or building sites than conventional painting — tar, varnish, marble dust, rags pressed into canvas. The foundation holds the largest collection of his work anywhere.

The most visible element from the street is not the building but what sits on top of it: Tàpies's 1990 wire sculpture *Núvol i Cadira* (Cloud and Chair), a tangle of steel tubing and barbed wire with a recognisable chair form emerging from it. It sits on the roofline like something between a transmitter and an accident, which is more or less the point. Tàpies thought art should be uncomfortable.
