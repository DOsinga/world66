The large metallic prawn — *gamba* in Catalan — that crouches at the entrance to the covered section of La Boqueria market is the work of Javier Mariscal, the Valencia-born designer who created Cobi, the 1992 Barcelona Olympics mascot. Mariscal was the defining graphic voice of the *Movida barcelonina* — the explosion of creative energy that followed Franco's death — and the Gamba captures his aesthetic perfectly: playful, slightly cartoon-like, and impossible to miss.

The prawn is an entirely apt subject for a marker at a food market famous for its seafood. La Boqueria's stalls near the Rambla entrance are piled with ice and gleaming crustaceans; the gamba is both a sign and a sculpture. Mariscal gave it the specific posture of a prawn about to be eaten — tail curled, antennae alert, legs still — which makes it simultaneously appetising and slightly unsettling.

What makes the Gamba worth knowing is the frequency with which it gets misattributed. Guidebooks have credited it to Miró, to Lichtenstein, and to anonymous craftsmen. It is in fact a late work of one of the sharpest and most inventive designers of the late twentieth century, installed at the height of Barcelona's transformation into one of the world's great design cities. The prawn is funnier and more knowing than it appears.
