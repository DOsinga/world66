The bar's significance is less about its drinks than its timing. Glaciar opened in 1967 and became the fixed point of Barcelona's countercultural map during the Transición — the years from Franco's death in 1975 to the 1978 constitution, when the city was remaking itself and its public spaces were claimed by new voices. The colonnaded terrace of Plaça Reial gave whoever sat there visibility; to be seen at Glaciar was to announce your political and cultural affiliations.

The square itself was another story. By the 1980s, Plaça Reial had become notoriously dangerous after dark — drug dealing, petty crime — and the tourist-friendly version of it now barely resembles the place that surrounded Glaciar in its formative decades. The bar kept serving through all of it.

Most of Plaça Reial's other establishments arrived after the 1992 Olympics cleaned up the neighbourhood. Glaciar predates that transformation by twenty-five years, which is why it still feels different from the tourist operations around it — though the distinction is narrowing.
