Sònia was a trans woman who was murdered in Barcelona in the 1990s. The decision by the Barcelona city council to name a public space in her memory — a small glorieta, or circular garden feature, in the Eixample Esquerra — was at the time almost without precedent in Europe. No other city on the continent had formally commemorated a transgender person killed for being transgender in this way.

The Eixample Esquerra had been a centre of LGBTQ+ life in Barcelona since the transition to democracy in the late 1970s, when the neighbourhood's bars and associations provided the organisational infrastructure for a community emerging from decades of repression under Franco. The choice of location was deliberate: the memorial sits in a neighbourhood that already understood what it was marking.

The naming provoked controversy when it was proposed, and the controversy itself became part of the record. That record is now part of what the Glorieta de la Transsexual Sònia documents: not just a death, but the political struggle over whether that death deserved public acknowledgement at all.
