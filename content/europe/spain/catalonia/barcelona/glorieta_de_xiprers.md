Cypress trees have a specific resonance in Catalan landscape. Mediterranean cemeteries have used them for centuries as windbreaks and as markers of the sacred enclosure; Romanesque churches across the Pyrenean foothills are often accompanied by a single cypress or a paired row. The tree's vertical form — almost a column, almost an exclamation mark — is associated with both death and the aspiration upward that death can prompt.

The Glorieta de Xiprers brings that tradition into urban space. Arranged as an artwork rather than incidentally planted, the cypress grove creates a small enclosed space with its own atmosphere: darker, cooler, more vertical than the surrounding city. The sound of traffic changes inside it; the sky is framed differently.

Installations that use living plants as their material age in unpredictable ways. Cypress trees grow slowly but they do grow; the composition changes year by year as the canopy closes further. Visiting now, you are seeing a different work than existed at its inauguration and a different one again from what future visitors will find.
