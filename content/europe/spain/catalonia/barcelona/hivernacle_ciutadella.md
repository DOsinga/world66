The Hivernacle (Winter Garden) in the Parc de la Ciutadella was built between 1884 and 1887 by the architect Josep Amargós, in preparation for the 1888 Universal Exposition. The structure is an iron and glass vaulted greenhouse of considerable elegance: a central nave approximately 70 metres long and 20 metres wide, flanked by side aisles under lower iron arcades, with elaborate cast-iron column capitals and decorative ironwork throughout.

The hivernacle type — a large heated glass house for protecting tropical and tender plants through winter — was a major building form in the Victorian period. The Crystal Palace in London (1851), the Jardins d'Hiver in Paris, and similar structures across Europe demonstrated both the technical possibilities of iron-and-glass construction and the cultural desire to bring the natural world indoors under glass. Barcelona's Hivernacle is one of the southern European examples of this tradition.

The building was used for botanical purposes after the Exposition, then fell into various states of disrepair and renewed use through the 20th century. It now serves as a bar, concert venue, and event space — a use that both preserves it financially and fills it with people in the evenings. The choice to use a Victorian greenhouse as a contemporary cultural venue is typical of Barcelona's approach to heritage structures: rather than museifying them, the city tends to find uses that keep them active. The result is an iron and glass Victorian structure where, on weekend evenings, people drink under the tropical plants.
