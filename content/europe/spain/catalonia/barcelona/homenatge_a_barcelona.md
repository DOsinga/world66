Beverly Pepper was an American sculptor who first came to Barcelona in the 1950s and returned so frequently that she eventually maintained a studio in the city. Her relationship with Catalonia was long and close enough that when the city commissioned a major sculpture for the waterfront, her name was an obvious choice. The work, installed in 1992 as part of the Olympic transformation of the seafront, sits in the Parc de la Ciutadella extension near the sea.

The piece uses Pepper's characteristic vocabulary of angular steel forms arranged to create interior spaces and shadow patterns that shift through the day. Like much of her work it combines industrial materials with an almost classical interest in human scale — the sculpture is large enough to be monumental but proportioned so that walking around and through it feels intimate rather than dwarfed.

The title is direct: a tribute to the city. Coming from an artist who was not born here but chose it, the dedication has a particular quality — less automatic than native pride, more considered. It suggests that Barcelona repays close attention, which the sculpture itself, through its interplay of light and solid form, also requires.
