The Mútua Escolar Blanquerna was founded in 1924 by a group of Catalan families who wanted a school that taught in Catalan, applied progressive pedagogical methods, and was governed cooperatively by parents. It was named after the 13th-century novel by Ramon Llull — the first great work of Catalan literature — and the name choice was deliberate: the school positioned itself as part of a continuous Catalan cultural tradition.

Under the Second Republic, Blanquerna flourished. Under Franco, it was shut down. The suppression of Catalan-language education was one of the most systematically damaging acts of the dictatorship: an entire generation grew up unable to read or write in the language they spoke at home. Blanquerna was eventually permitted to reopen in modified form, but the break was irreparable.

The memorial stands as recognition that schools are not just buildings but cultural projects, and that their destruction is a cultural loss even when the buildings survive. The families who founded Blanquerna understood that language education was political; the regime that closed it understood the same thing.
