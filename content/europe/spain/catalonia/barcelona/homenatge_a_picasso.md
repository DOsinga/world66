Antoni Tàpies was not interested in making a flattering portrait. When Barcelona commissioned him to create a tribute to Picasso in 1983, in the street named after the artist near the Picasso Museum, Tàpies produced something that looks, at first glance, like a removal van's contents sealed inside glass: chairs, sofas, fabric falling, objects arranged without obvious logic. It is a homage that declines to be reverent.

Tàpies and Picasso occupied very different positions in 20th-century art — Picasso the global superstar who left Spain and became the most famous artist in the world, Tàpies the committed Catalan who stayed and developed a dense private iconography from humble materials. The choice of furniture and domestic objects as the tribute medium is very Tàpies: the everyday as the site of meaning, the interior world made briefly visible.

Water cascades over the exterior of the glass cube in some configurations, making the whole thing shimmer. The piece divides opinion strongly, which may be the most accurate tribute available. Picasso himself, had he lived to see it, would probably have appreciated the difficulty.
