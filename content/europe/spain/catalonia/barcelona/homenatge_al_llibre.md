The Homenatge al Llibre (Homage to the Book) is a marble sculpture group near the top of the Rambla, installed as part of the cultural infrastructure around the celebrations of Sant Jordi's Day — April 23, the feast day of Catalonia's patron saint and the date on which the tradition of exchanging books and roses has been celebrated since 1923.

The Sant Jordi tradition in its current form was established by the bookseller sector in the 1920s, drawing on an older custom of flower-giving and connecting it to the book trade. The date was chosen partly because it is the anniversary of the deaths of both Miguel de Cervantes and William Shakespeare in 1616 — an anniversary that UNESCO would later use to establish World Book Day in 1995.

Barcelona's Sant Jordi celebration is one of the most distinctive cultural events in Europe: on April 23, the city's streets fill with outdoor book and flower stalls, and the tradition is for men to give women roses and women to give men books (though this gender division has become optional). The Rambla, already a promenade, becomes on this day a kilometre-long book fair. The Homenatge al Llibre near its top was placed here as a permanent marker of the street's identity as Barcelona's principal cultural promenade.
