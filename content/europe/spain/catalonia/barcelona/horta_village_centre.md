Horta was one of the oldest agricultural settlements in the Barcelona area. Records of a Roman villa on the site date to the 1st century CE; the medieval village developed around a church dedicated to Sant Joan Baptista and functioned as an agricultural community supplying food to the city throughout the medieval and early modern periods. The name Horta simply means "orchard" or "garden" in Catalan.

The village was incorporated into Barcelona in 1904, one of the last in the wave of annexations that began in 1897. The surrounding territory was subsequently developed for housing, but the core of the village — the church, the Plaça d'Eivissa, the narrow streets around them — survived the development pressure and retains a character visibly different from the planned housing blocks that surround it.

The church of Sant Joan Baptista d'Horta, with origins in the 12th century and substantially rebuilt in later periods, stands at the centre of what was the village. The square in front of it, the Plaça d'Eivissa, was the village's market square. The streets immediately around the church — Carrer de l'Horta, Carrer de la Peira — preserve the medieval street alignment of the village, a curved, organic pattern that contrasts with the grid of the surrounding development. Walking from the housing blocks of the Horta district into these few surviving medieval streets produces an abrupt shift in urban texture.
