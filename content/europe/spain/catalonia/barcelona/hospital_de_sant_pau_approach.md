The Avinguda de Gaudí is a short, wide boulevard connecting the Sagrada Família to the Hospital de Sant Pau on a direct axis. The alignment is not accidental: Domènech i Montaner, designing the hospital at the same time Gaudí was beginning his church, positioned the main entrance of the hospital so that it would face the as-yet-unbuilt church. The two architects were rivals — Domènech had objected to Gaudí receiving the church commission and their professional relationship was cold — but their buildings were designed in conscious relation to each other.

The Hospital de Sant Pau was completed in stages between 1902 and 1930 and is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site alongside the Palau de la Música. It operated as a working hospital until 2009, when the medical functions were moved to a new facility and the Recinte Modernista was opened for cultural visits.

The approach along the Avinguda de Gaudí reveals the hospital's composition gradually: the orange-tiled domes and towers become visible first over the surrounding Eixample buildings, then the full entrance pavilion comes into view. The axis from the Sagrada Família to the hospital is approximately 400 metres long, passing through the standard Eixample grid. Walking it makes visible something that is invisible from within the buildings themselves: that these two extraordinary buildings, by professional antagonists, were positioned to create an urban relationship across the grid that neither one could have managed alone.
