The Hospital de la Santa Creu i Sant Pau (1902–1930) is arguably Domènech i Montaner's greatest work — a UNESCO World Heritage site that most visitors to Barcelona miss entirely because it sits several blocks north of the Sagrada Família axis. Domènech designed it as the opposite of the typical hospital: instead of a single institutional block with corridors, he created 48 separate pavilions in a garden campus, each one a Modernisme set piece of coloured ceramics, stained glass, and sculptural programme.

The underlying idea was medical: Domènech and the doctors he worked with believed that natural light, fresh air, gardens, and beautiful surroundings were themselves therapeutic. Each pavilion was oriented to maximise sun; underground tunnels connected them so patients wouldn't have to cross the garden in bad weather. The whole campus was built on a grid rotated 45 degrees from the Eixample, which is why it sits at a diagonal to the streets around it and why the approach from Avinguda de Gaudí gives you the full symmetrical vista.

Domènech died in 1923, with the complex unfinished. His son Pere completed it to his plans. The hospital moved to a modern building in 2009; the old campus is now a cultural centre and museum. The main pavilion's hypogeum — the underground space — contains the original operating theatres, now perfectly preserved.
