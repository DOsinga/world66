The Hotel Espanya was built in 1859, during the first wave of tourist infrastructure along the Rambla, but it is the renovation carried out between 1902 and 1903 that gives it its current significance. The commission went to Lluís Domènech i Montaner, who was simultaneously working on what would become the Palau de la Música Catalana and the Hospital de Sant Pau. The hotel is on a different scale from those projects, but the interior work he produced here is some of the finest surviving Modernista decoration in the city.

The dining room — the Sala dels Mosaics — has seafood imagery in tiles and plasterwork by the ceramicist Mario Maragliano: mermaids, fish, sea plants, and ships arranged around the walls in a programme that celebrated Barcelona's maritime identity. The adjoining Sala de Festes has a painted frieze by Ramon Casas, the leading Barcelona painter of the period and a central figure in the Modernista movement. A third room has a large fireplace sculpted by Eusebi Arnau, whose work also appears on the Palau de la Música and the Hospital de Sant Pau.

The hotel was renovated again in 2010 under the architect Richard Rogers partnership, which restored Domènech i Montaner's interiors while modernising the building behind them. The Sala dels Mosaics now operates as a restaurant; it is accessible to non-guests who want a meal. Walking in from the Carrer de Sant Pau, the shock of finding Modernista perfection inside an anonymous hotel façade is one of the reliable surprises of the Raval.
