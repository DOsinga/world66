The Jardins Artigas were created by Antoni Gaudí in 1906 for the manufacturer Joan Artigas, at a site in the La Pobla de Lillet valley in the Collserola hills above Barcelona. The garden is accessed from a road above the Vallvidrera reservoir and represents one of Gaudí's least-known projects — a private commission for a client who wanted a garden incorporating the rocky hillside and natural watercourse that ran through his property.

Gaudí designed a sequence of spaces using the stream as the organising element: stone bridges, grottos, a pergola, and paths that follow the water down the hillside. The vocabulary is similar to Park Güell — rough stonework, organic forms that echo geological shapes — but in a smaller and more intimate scale. The garden feels more experimental than the park: Gaudí was testing ideas about incorporating natural topography that he would develop more systematically in his larger commissions.

The garden was restored in the 1990s and is open to visitors, though it receives a fraction of the attention paid to Park Güell. The combination of a working stream, Gaudí's stonework, and the surrounding forested hillside creates an environment that is more pastoral than the monumental park and arguably more revealing of Gaudí's thinking about the relationship between architecture and landscape. The visit from Barcelona requires a drive or a long walk through the Collserola.
