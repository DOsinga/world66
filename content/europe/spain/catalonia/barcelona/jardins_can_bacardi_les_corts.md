The Jardins de Can Bacardí occupy the garden of a 19th-century villa that belonged to the Bacardí family — the same family whose Facund Bacardí Massó founded the rum company in Cuba in 1862. The Barcelona branch of the family maintained properties in the city, and the Les Corts villa with its garden represented the local estate of a family whose wealth was generated in the Caribbean.

The garden is small and relatively simple — a municipal park of the neighbourhood type, with benches, paths, and ornamental planting. Its historical significance is in the connection it preserves between the Les Corts neighbourhood and one of the most successful Catalan emigrant business stories. The Bacardí company was founded by a Catalan in Cuba, used Cuban labour (including enslaved people), became the world's best-selling rum brand, was expropriated by the Cuban revolution in 1959, and relocated to Puerto Rico. The family's Barcelona estate passed to the city.

The gardens are used primarily by residents of the surrounding neighbourhood as a place to sit in the shade. The Bacardí connection is recorded in a small plaque but is not the reason most people come here. The rum that filled the passatge in the Gothic Quarter, built on profits from the same business, has a more architectural presence in the city than this garden, which simply happens to be what a powerful emigrant family left behind when they left.
