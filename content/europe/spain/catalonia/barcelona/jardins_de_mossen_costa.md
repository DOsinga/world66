The Jardins de Mossèn Costa i Llobera occupy the south-facing cliff face of Montjuïc, above the old ferry port. The garden was created in 1970, using the dry, rocky terrain and the southern exposure — which makes this the warmest and driest microclimate in Barcelona — to cultivate cacti and succulents from across the Americas, Africa, and the Canary Islands. Over 800 species are represented in a collection that is among the largest of its type in Europe.

The garden is named for Miquel Costa i Llobera, a 19th-century Majorcan poet associated with the Renaixença movement who wrote a celebrated poem, El Pi de Formentor (The Pine of Formentor), about a wind-bent pine at the tip of a Mediterranean promontory. The association between a poet known for writing about plants clinging to exposed clifftops and a garden of sun-adapted plants on an exposed Montjuïc cliff is intentional.

The garden is unusual in Barcelona for requiring no irrigation beyond rainfall in summer, when other parts of Montjuïc need constant watering. The cacti that dominate the collection are adapted to survive months without rain; the steep, rocky terrain of the cliff face provides drainage conditions that prevent root rot. The result is a garden that looks after itself in a way that Barcelona's parks generally do not. The cliffside location, with views across the port to the city and the sea, creates a setting that would be remarkable even without the cacti.
