Joan Brossa was a Catalan poet, visual artist, and playwright who worked in Barcelona from the late 1940s until his death in 1998. He was associated with the Dau al Set group — a surrealist-influenced collective that operated in the immediate post-war period, when overt political opposition to Franco was impossible and cultural resistance took oblique and hermetic forms. Brossa wrote poetry in Catalan at a time when Catalan publication was banned, distributing work through samizdat-like networks.

The gardens on Montjuïc that bear his name were created on the site of the Parc d'Atraccions del Tibidabo's former Montjuïc branch — an amusement park that operated from the 1960s to the 1990s. After it closed, the site was converted into gardens with an emphasis on botanical variety and Brossa's visual poetry: letters and typographic elements appear as sculpture throughout the garden, referencing his work in visual language.

Brossa's poetry is difficult to translate because much of it plays with the visual appearance of Catalan words and letters. His "visual poems" — compositions using letterforms, symbols, and images as poetic elements — were among the most original works produced in Barcelona in the second half of the 20th century. The gardens' use of letters as landscape elements is a deliberate translation of his methods into three dimensions. Brossa himself died in 1998, three years before the gardens opened.
