The Laribal gardens occupy a terraced slope on the upper flank of Montjuïc, between the Fundació Joan Miró and the castle summit. They were designed between 1917 and 1922 by the landscape architect Nicolau Maria Rubió i Tudurí, working with the French architect Jean-Claude Nicolas Forestier, who was simultaneously redesigning several of Paris's parks. The commission came from a Catalan industrialist named Màxim Laribal — hence the name — who wanted a private villa garden inspired by the Nasrid gardens of the Alhambra.

The result is a series of six descending terraces linked by water channels, pergolas covered in wisteria, and fountains where water trickles through carved fish mouths into pools. The style is Hispano-Moorish: formal geometry softened by planting, with water as the central organising element. Rubió i Tudurí would go on to design several other major Barcelona parks, including the Ciutadella gardens, but the Laribal represents his most concentrated achievement.

The gardens passed to the city in the 1920s and were used as the setting for the Greek Theatre performances during the same period. They are now open to the public but remain significantly less visited than other Montjuïc attractions, even on summer weekends. The upper terrace, reached through a pergola of climbing roses, overlooks the city with no intervening structures — a view that the villa's original occupant would have considered his private property.
