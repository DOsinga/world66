The Turó del Putxet is a small hill rising above the Sant Gervasi neighbourhood, now occupied by a public park with paths, gardens, and a viewpoint overlooking the Eixample grid and the sea. The hill was outside the built-up area of Barcelona until the early 20th century, when the extension of the city northward incorporated it into the urban fabric. Before that, it was a place of Sunday walks for the residents of the upper-class villas that were being built in the Sant Gervasi area from the 1870s onward.

A small hermitage — the Ermita del Putxet — stands near the summit. This type of small isolated chapel, associated with a specific hilltop and its local celebrations, is common in the Catalan landscape outside the cities but rare within Barcelona's built-up area. The hermitage was built in the 18th century as part of the devotional landscape of the Sant Gervasi village and its associated farms. It is one of the few surviving examples of this rural religious type within the continuous urban fabric of Barcelona.

The park is significantly quieter than most Barcelona parks of similar size. Its somewhat steep paths and distance from the main tourist zones keep the visitor numbers low. The view from the summit, across the entire Eixample grid to the sea, is one of the better free panoramas available within the city. Unlike Tibidabo or the Bunkers del Carmel, the Putxet summit is accessible on foot in about ten minutes from the nearest metro station.
