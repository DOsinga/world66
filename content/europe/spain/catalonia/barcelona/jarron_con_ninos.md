The Jarrón con niños belongs to the tradition of fountain sculpture that the city deployed throughout its parks and gardens in the late 19th and early 20th centuries: allegorical, slightly antiquarian in flavour, intended to give public space the atmosphere of a private garden expanded for democratic use. The children arranged around a central vase or urn were a standard formula, derived from classical models and popularised through 18th-century French and Italian garden design.

Barcelona adopted this vocabulary wholesale when it began building its modern public parks after the demolition of the city walls in 1854. The parks of the Ciutadella, Güell, Laribal and others were filled with fountains, sculptures and ornamental ironwork that signalled cultural seriousness as much as civic generosity.

The fountain retains its original function: water moves through it and around the base, and the children's figures preside over the flow with the slightly detached serenity that classical sculpture gives to human figures. Over time the stone has acquired a patina that makes it look older than it is, which was always part of the intended effect.
