Barcelona's relationship with its anarchist and labour history is complicated by the fact that the Franco regime spent nearly forty years systematically erasing it. Streets were renamed, monuments removed, archives destroyed, survivors exiled or imprisoned. The recovery of that history since 1975 has been a decades-long project of research, commemoration, and often contentious negotiation about which figures deserve public recognition.

Joan Agustí i Carreras was a figure from that suppressed tradition — a labour organiser whose contribution to the workers' movement in Catalonia earned him a memorial once democracy made it possible to place one. The details of his life and work are documented in the labour history archives that began to be reassembled after the transition.

Memorials like this one, placed in the streets of a city that once could not acknowledge them, carry a double weight: they mark both the person commemorated and the historical silence that preceded the commemoration. Walking past, you are walking through layers of Barcelonan time.
