Joan Miró (1893-1983) was born in Barcelona, spent decades in Paris and Mallorca, and died in Palma at the age of 90. He was buried in the Cementiri de Montjuïc, in the hill below which his foundation and museum — the Fundació Joan Miró, designed by his friend Josep Lluís Sert — had been built in 1975. The proximity of the grave to the museum is striking: Miró's body is interred approximately 500 metres below the institution that holds his work.

Miró spent the Franco years primarily in Mallorca, returning to Catalonia only after the regime's cultural controls began to loosen in the 1960s. His decision to have his foundation built in Barcelona — specifically on Montjuïc, the hill that had served as an execution ground for Republicans during and after the Civil War — was a political statement as well as an aesthetic one. The hill's association with Franco's repression was being contested by the creation of cultural institutions in the same space.

The foundation opened in 1975, in the final months of Franco's life. Miró was 82. He would live another eight years, continuing to work until shortly before his death. The grave in the Montjuïc cemetery is simple — a stone slab with his name — but its location, below the museum that houses his life's work, creates one of the more unusual relationships between an artist's biography and their artistic legacy available in any city.
