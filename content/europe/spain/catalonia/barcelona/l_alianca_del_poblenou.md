Before compulsory public education reached Barcelona's working class, L'Aliança del Poblenou ran its own school. Founded in 1868 in Poblenou — then known as the "Catalan Manchester" for its density of textile and metalwork factories — the association operated a library, a theatre company, and classes for workers' children as part of the same institution. This was a 19th-century workers' mutual aid society doing what the state had not yet agreed to do.

The restaurant opened in the early 20th century as the association's social arm, serving a set menu to members and neighbours. It has survived the industrial era, the urban decline of the late 20th century, and the design-and-technology-sector conversion of Poblenou that accelerated after 2000 — the area is now branded as @22, Barcelona's designated innovation district. The clientele remains predominantly local.

L'Aliança is one of the oldest continuously operating workers' associations in Spain. The fact that it still functions — still serves lunch, still has members — in a neighbourhood that has otherwise been almost entirely reinvented makes it a significant piece of Barcelona's social history that most visitors walk past without registering.
