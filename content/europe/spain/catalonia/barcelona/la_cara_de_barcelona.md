Joan Miró spent much of his life working from Mallorca, but Barcelona was always his emotional home, and in the final years before his death in 1983 he created several large-scale interventions across the city. *La Cara de Barcelona* — the Face of Barcelona — is among the most intimate of them. Embedded into the retaining wall of the Parc de la Ciutadella, the oversized ceramic face peers out with the simplified, almost childlike forms that made Miró one of the twentieth century's most recognisable visual voices.

The park itself has a charged history. Built on the grounds of a citadel that the Bourbon kings used to suppress Catalan uprisings after 1714, it was converted into public green space in the 1870s as a democratic gesture — and later hosted the 1888 Universal Exposition. For Miró to plant his signature playful face here, in a place that had once been a symbol of repression, carries a quiet political charge the artist would have been conscious of.

Look for it on the wall that runs along the Passeig de Pujades side of the park. It is easy to walk past if you are not expecting it, blending into the stone with the relaxed confidence of something that has always belonged there.
