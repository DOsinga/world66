The building at Carrer de Sant Pau 128 ran as a *confiteria* — a shop selling sweets, confections, and pharmaceutical preparations — from 1912 to 1984. When the family closed the business, new owners took the space and made the decision that defines the bar: they changed almost nothing. The wooden shopfront, the glass display cabinets, the ornate plasterwork ceiling, and the pharmacy shelves along the walls were kept intact. The labels on the shelves still identify their original categories of preparations. The conversion from sweet shop to vermouth bar involved removing the stock and putting in a bar counter; everything else stayed.

The result is one of the finest surviving early 20th-century commercial interiors in the Raval — a neighbourhood that lost most of its pre-war architectural detail to decades of disinvestment. The space looks like a film set, but is not one. The ceiling is original. The wood is original. The labels are original. What looks like meticulous restoration is simply what happens when someone declines to renovate.

La Confiteria is a functioning bar serving vermouth, beer, and wine. It is well-known enough to be a destination rather than a local spot, but the interior absorbs the crowd without feeling staged. Come before 2 pm on a weekend for vermouth — the traditional Catalan vermut hour — and the room is at its best.
