Sant Andreu was an independent municipality — a working-class town north of the old city — until Barcelona absorbed it in 1897. The neighbourhood has retained its own strong identity ever since, with a civic pride that expresses itself in local markets, street festivals, and public art that tends toward the human rather than the heroic. *La Família* fits squarely in that tradition: three figures holding one another, the sculpture stripped of ideology and focused on the ordinary miracle of belonging to people who belong to you.

Joaquim Ros i Sabaté was a prolific sculptor whose work appears across the city, mostly in neighbourhood contexts like this one rather than on grand civic axes. His figures tend to be solid and warm, more interested in weight and closeness than in elegance.

The placement here, in a square that serves as a neighbourhood node rather than a tourist destination, means this piece is primarily seen by people going about their actual lives — shopping, meeting friends, waiting. That ordinariness is exactly the point.
