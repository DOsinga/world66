The 1929 International Exposition transformed Montjuïc from a bare hill into a showcase of allegorical sculpture, and *La Fertilitat* was among the figures commissioned to populate its avenues and pavilions. Josep Viladomat i Massanas was one of the most sought-after sculptors of the period, responsible for several of the monumental pieces that still define the hill's atmosphere. Fertility as a theme — abundance, growth, the body's creative power — was thoroughly fashionable in the interwar exposition aesthetic, and Viladomat's version struck a balance between classical formality and the softer, more naturalistic tendencies of Catalan Noucentisme.

After the exposition closed, many of its sculptures were redistributed across the city rather than melted down or stored. *La Fertilitat* eventually settled near what is now the Parc de Joan Miró, a part of the city that was thoroughly industrial in 1929 and has been remade over decades into a quieter residential quarter.

The figure is a trace of the moment when Barcelona last presented itself to the world before the Republic, before the war, before the long Franco years. Walking past her now, you are looking at a city's aspirations frozen in stone.
