The building that became La Font del Gat was not designed as a restaurant. In 1897, at the height of Modernisme, a modest refreshment pavilion went up on Montjuïc for visitors to the hill; the architect was from Lluís Domènech i Montaner's studio, working in the same decade he was building the Hospital de Sant Pau and the Palau de la Música. It was a kiosk with Modernista pretensions, serving water and light refreshments to people walking the gardens.

The name predates the building. A small spring — font — on the hillside was marked by a carved stone cat, and the spot had been a named landmark before the pavilion was built around it. As Montjuïc developed — the 1929 International Exposition transformed the hill — the pavilion expanded into a full restaurant, occupying the same terrace in the Jardins de Laribal, which were designed by Nicolau Maria Rubió i Tudurí in the 1920s.

What remains is unusual: a lunch restaurant on Montjuïc where the setting is genuinely the point. The formal gardens run above and below the terrace; the view extends over the port. For a city as densely visited as Barcelona, a place like this is not easy to find.
