The Presó Model — the Model Prison — was built between 1887 and 1904 on a design by Josep Domènech i Estapà, based on the panopticon principles developed by Jeremy Bentham: a central circular watchtower surrounded by six radial wings, each with cells visible from the central point. The idea was that prisoners would never know if they were being observed, and the knowledge of possible surveillance would regulate their behaviour.

The prison held anarchist agitators, trade union organisers, and political activists from its opening onward. During the Civil War, it was used by both sides: Republican militias held captured fascists here in 1936; after the Nationalist victory, it became one of the main prisons for captured Republicans, Socialists, and Catalans. Political prisoners continued to be held here under Franco. After the transition to democracy, it remained a conventional prison until it closed in 2017.

The city has been unable to agree on what to do with the building. Various proposals — cultural centre, university campus, memorial museum — have been advanced and abandoned. As of the early 2020s it remained largely empty, maintained by the city while the political argument about its future continued. A building that held political prisoners across five different political regimes — the Restoration monarchy, the First Republic, the Second Republic, the Civil War, the Franco dictatorship, and democracy — raises questions about commemoration that are not easily resolved by converting it into student accommodation.
