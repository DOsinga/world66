Josep Viladomat i Massanas was among the most prolific sculptors working in Barcelona in the first half of the twentieth century, and his allegorical female figures appear throughout the city — in parks, on avenues, in the plazas that accumulated public art as democracy and civic pride allowed. *La Serenitat* — Serenity — belongs to the Noucentista tradition that prized classical composure, Mediterranean rootedness, and a certain idealized dignity of the human form.

The concept of serenitat carries particular resonance in Catalan culture, where it has sometimes functioned as an aesthetic and ethical ideal — a counterweight to both the turbulence of Modernisme and the anguish of political history. To be serene was to be grounded, proportionate, self-possessed. In a city that has lived through so many upheavals, the aspiration is understandable.

The figure sits in the Montjuïc area near the Parc del Migdia, a quieter side of the hill away from the grand exposition pavilions. The serenity on offer here is the real kind — not performed for an audience, but simply present in the afternoon light.
