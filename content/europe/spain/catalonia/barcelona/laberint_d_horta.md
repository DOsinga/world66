The Laberint d'Horta (Horta Labyrinth) was laid out between 1791 and 1808 by the Marquis of Alfarràs, Joan Antoni Desvalls, on his estate in the hills north of the city. The garden was designed by the Italian architect Domenico Bagutti in the Neoclassical style fashionable in late 18th-century Europe, with formal terraces, fountains, classical statuary, and at its centre a hedge labyrinth in cypress that gives the garden its name.

The labyrinth itself — planted in cypress with paths about 750 metres long — is the oldest surviving garden labyrinth in Spain and among the oldest in Europe. The cypress hedges are now centuries old and stand several metres tall; the paths between them are dark and cool even in summer heat. Finding the centre of the labyrinth, where a pool and a statue of Eros mark the end point, takes the average visitor about fifteen minutes of trial and error.

The garden was acquired by the city of Barcelona in 1967 and opened to the public in 1971. Its distance from the main tourist zones and the cost of the entry ticket keep visitor numbers manageable. The Laberint is simultaneously an 18th-century aristocratic garden — with all the class politics that implies — and a genuine recreational space that generations of Barcelona families have used for Sunday afternoons. The combination of centuries-old cypress, statues of classical mythology, and children trying to find their way out of the maze is one of the more charming scenes in a city not short of charm.
