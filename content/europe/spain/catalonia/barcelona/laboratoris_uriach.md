Joan Uriach opened his pharmacy in the Raval district in 1838, during the early decades of Barcelona's industrial transformation. What distinguished Uriach from other apothecaries was the move to industrial-scale manufacturing: rather than compounding medicines to individual prescriptions, Uriach & Cia began producing standardised pharmaceutical products in volume, making it one of the first companies in Spain to industrialise drug manufacture. The shift tracked Barcelona's broader transformation from artisan to factory economy, applying the same logic to pharmaceuticals that the textile industry had applied to cloth.

The Poblenou factory, built in the late 19th century as the company's production headquarters, represents this industrial ambition in brick and iron. Poblenou — then called the Catalan Manchester — was where Barcelona's manufacturers located their operations, close to the rail lines and away from the crowded old city. The factory complex operated through the entire arc of Catalan industrial history: the belle époque, the Republic, the Civil War, the autarky of the Franco years, and the economic opening of the 1960s, each period presenting different regulatory and commercial pressures.

The company remains family-owned and headquartered in Barcelona, which places it in a very small category: 19th-century Catalan industrial firms that survived two dictatorships, the structural disruptions of the 20th century, and the consolidation of the pharmaceutical industry by multinationals without being absorbed or liquidated. The Poblenou buildings are now surrounded by the 22@ innovation district, a juxtaposition of old industrial fabric and new-economy offices that defines the neighbourhood's current character.
