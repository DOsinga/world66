The Presó de Dones de les Corts was a women's prison that operated in the Les Corts neighbourhood from 1939, immediately after Franco's forces occupied Barcelona, until its closure in 1955. The building had previously been a children's correctional home; the Franco regime converted it to hold the women who had been detained for political reasons — members of the Republican government, party members, women who had worked for Republican organisations, relatives of men who had fled into exile.

The prison held several thousand women during its sixteen years of operation, in conditions that were deliberately harsh: overcrowding, malnutrition, forced religious observation, and the separation of mothers from their children. Some of the children born to prisoners were removed from their mothers and placed with families considered ideologically acceptable — a practice of politically motivated adoption that Spain has only begun formally to investigate in recent decades.

The memorial, installed in 2006 on the site of the former prison (now occupied by an FC Barcelona sports facility), consists of a series of plaques naming the women who were imprisoned here. The choice of location — across the street from Camp Nou, in one of Barcelona's wealthiest neighbourhoods — is itself a historical irony: the Francoist sports culture that helped build the Camp Nou crowd coexisted for two decades with a political prison operating within sight of the stadium.
