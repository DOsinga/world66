Hemp — *cànem* in Catalan — was once one of the most strategically important crops in the Mediterranean economy. Ships needed rope, and rope needed hemp, and the growing, retting, spinning, and weaving of hemp fibre employed thousands of people across Catalonia. In the areas north of Barcelona, where Sant Andreu stands, women and girls did much of this labour, processing the raw plant through its chemical stages and producing the cordage on which Mediterranean navigation literally depended.

This memorial recognises that the women who did this work — repetitive, physically demanding, economically essential — have been largely absent from official history. They left no written records, held no office, owned no companies. What they left was rope, and rope perishes. The memorial is therefore an act of historical imagination as much as commemoration: naming a category of person who would otherwise have vanished entirely from the record.

The placing of the memorial in Sant Andreu is geographically appropriate — this was exactly the zone where hemp cultivation and processing took place, close to the pre-industrial city but beyond its walls. The neighbourhood's history runs through the hands of these unnamed women.
