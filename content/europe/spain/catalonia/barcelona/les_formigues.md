A calligram is a poem whose letters are arranged on the page to form the image of their subject. Joan Salvat-Papasseit (1894–1924), the Catalan avant-garde poet who pioneered futurism and ultraism in the language, published such a poem in his 1921 book L'irradiador del port i les gavines: three short lines that read "camí de sol / per les rutes amigues / unes formigues" — "sun path / through friendly routes / some ants" — arranged vertically on the page so that, from a distance, they look exactly like a line of ants marching. The ceramic mural at Passeig del Born 25 translates that typographic image into a 7-by-7-metre wall piece.

Salvat-Papasseit was a dockworker's son who educated himself through Barcelona's anarchist cultural networks and died of tuberculosis at 30, leaving a body of work that crammed an enormous amount of formal experimentation into a short career. He celebrated the port, the Barceloneta waterfront, and the working-class life of La Ribera neighbourhood — the streets around where this mural stands. Placing a tribute to him here is an act of local memory as much as literary commemoration.

The mural was inaugurated on 1 June 2004 by the deputy mayor as the first installation of the Mapapoetic project, which placed poetry at 26 city locations for the Forum of Cultures 2004. Unlike the other 25 works in the programme, which were temporary, this one was made permanent. The artist, Josep Manuel Pinillo, was a former collaborator of La Fura dels Baus — the theatrical group famous for spectacular, physically demanding performances — which gives the ceramic ant calligram an unexpected pedigree in Barcelona's performance art history.
