The three chimneys (xemeneies in Catalan) standing beside the Avinguda del Paral·lel are the surviving visible remnants of the Central de la Canadenca — the Barcelona Traction, Light and Power Company electricity plant that began operations in 1911. The company, nicknamed "La Canadenca" for its Canadian ownership, was the dominant electricity supplier for Barcelona through the early 20th century.

The plant is historically significant for a labour action that predates anything comparable in European industrial history. In February 1919, workers at La Canadenca went on strike. Because the plant supplied essentially all of Barcelona's electricity, the strike blacked out the city within two days. Trams stopped, factories lost power, lights went out. The general strike that followed was the first successful general strike in Spanish history. Within days, the workers won the eight-hour working day — something that Spanish law had not yet recognised and that most of Europe would not achieve for years.

The three chimneys are all that is publicly visible of the plant, which was extensively rebuilt in the 1950s and later converted for other uses. Plans to demolish them have been debated since the 1990s; neighbourhood campaigns have preserved them as industrial heritage. They stand in the middle of the Paral·lel, surrounded by the entertainment district that has always characterised this avenue, as a monument to the labour movement whose 1919 victory they mark.
