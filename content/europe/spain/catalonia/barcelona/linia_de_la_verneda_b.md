The Verneda is one of the most resolutely working-class corners of Barcelona, built rapidly in the 1950s and 1960s to house the workers who arrived from Andalusia, Murcia, and Extremadura to staff the factories and construction sites of the Francoist economic miracle. The neighbourhood was built with minimal public space and almost no cultural infrastructure, a pattern that was common across Barcelona's periphery and that democratic city governments from the 1980s onward tried systematically to correct.

The *Línia de la Verneda* project — a line of artworks running through the neighbourhood — was part of that correction. By commissioning works specifically for this context, the city acknowledged that public art is not only for the Eixample, not only for tourists and the middle class, but belongs wherever people live. The (B) designation indicates this is the second element in a related sequence, suggesting an artistic conversation spread across the neighbourhood's streets.

The piece rewards attention from people passing through on foot or bicycle — it is scaled to the street rather than to the monument, calibrated for ordinary movement through an ordinary neighbourhood.
