At number 6 Carrer de Rauric, a narrow street in the Gothic Quarter between Plaça Reial and La Rambla, two large red A's are affixed to the exterior wall of a shop. They are not doing what you expect letters to do. One is upright but leaning; the other tips at an angle. Together they form what Joan Brossa titled Lletres Gimnastes — Gymnastic Letters — a visual poem installed in 1997 and dedicated to the shop's founder, one Mr. Cardona.

Brossa spent his career pulling letters out of their expected contexts. He made poetry by treating the alphabet as material — things that could be repositioned, deformed, placed in unexpected relationships. The A is the first letter, the beginning of all reading. Two of them, neither in standard upright position, suggest the act of warm-up: the letters are stretching, loosening, preparing for the labour of meaning. There's a companion piece inside the shop's entrance, two A's arranged vertically, one atop the other.

The outside elements disappeared in July 2023 under circumstances that remain unclear — whether stolen, removed for restoration, or simply lost. When you arrive you may find them there or not. The bracket holes in the wall remain. Either way, knowing what was here makes the wall worth looking at: the ghost of a poem that stood for twenty-six years, doing its silent exercises.
