Joan Brossa was one of the twentieth century's great visual poets — a Catalan artist who worked at the intersection of surrealism, concrete poetry, and theatre, and who became increasingly interested in letters as objects rather than merely as carriers of meaning. For Brossa, a letter was a thing with weight, angle, personality; it could stand up, fall over, balance on another letter, perform. His *Bàrcino* on the Passeig de Gràcia is perhaps his most famous Barcelona intervention, but *Lletres Gimnastes* — Gymnast Letters — is more exuberant.

The Rambla del Poblenou was reinvented as a neighbourhood boulevard in the 1980s, a pedestrian axis for a district that had been entirely industrial and was beginning its long transformation into a mixed residential and creative quarter. Public art was embedded from the start, and Brossa's letters became one of the defining pieces of the new Poblenou's character.

Watching children interact with the letters — climbing on them, hiding behind them, trying to read what they say — is its own argument for the value of public art that invites physical engagement rather than merely requesting respectful contemplation.
