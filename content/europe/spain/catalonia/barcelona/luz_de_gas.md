The building at Carrer de Muntaner 246 was not built for music. In 1879 Barcelona's gas utility erected an ornate facility here — the elaborate facade and gilded interior were how a 19th-century utility company signalled prosperity and municipal importance, at a moment when gas lighting was new and profitable. The ballroom-scaled interior was a function space, not a concert hall.

It was converted to a music hall in the 1980s with minimal structural change, which is why the interior looks the way it does: the proportions, the chandeliers, the mirrored surfaces, and the painted ceiling belong to an entirely different era and purpose. Luz de Gas has hosted jazz, cabaret, Latin music, and pop from that gilded box without the space ever having been built for any of it.

The neighbourhood is upper Eixample — conservative, expensive, and a long walk from the areas of Barcelona that tourists associate with nightlife. That Luz de Gas thrived there for decades, drawing a mixed audience that wouldn't have crossed the city for the same acts in the Raval, is its own commentary on how Barcelona's musical geography works.
