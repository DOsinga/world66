The story of how a mammoth ended up in one of Barcelona's most fashionable medieval quarters is a story about participatory democracy at the neighbourhood scale. When the city offered the Born area a public sculpture and consulted residents about what they wanted, the woolly mammoth — improbable, pre-historic, enormous — won. It is difficult to imagine a more eloquent demonstration of the gap between what official culture assumes people want and what people actually choose when given the opportunity.

The Born neighbourhood had been declining for decades by the time the mammoth arrived, but was simultaneously being discovered by artists and galleries, and would soon become one of the most desirable addresses in the old city. The mammoth occupies a small square with a certain knowing irony: here is an extinct creature, in a neighbourhood whose character was also being slowly extinguished by the same gentrification that was making it attractive.

Children adore it unconditionally, which is perhaps the best measure of a public sculpture's success. Adults tend to grin, then photograph it, then grin again. The mammoth receives all of this with the unflappable calm of an animal that has seen ice ages come and go.
