The Poblenou was Barcelona's industrial engine for more than a century, a zone of factories, warehouses, and workers' housing that powered the city's economic growth from the mid-nineteenth century onward. The 22@ innovation district project, launched in 2000, began transforming the area into a hub for technology and creative industries, a process that has continued for a quarter century and produced a neighbourhood that is simultaneously hyper-contemporary and haunted by its industrial past.

Public sculpture has been part of the 22@ transformation — a way of marking that this is now a place for people, not just production. *Marc*, with its title suggesting a frame or a threshold, fits the sensibility of a district that is constantly redefining its own boundaries: between old and new, between working class and creative class, between Catalan and global.

The word *marc* in Catalan also carries the sense of a context or setting — the frame that determines how we see what's inside it. A sculpture with that name, placed in a neighbourhood in the act of reframing itself, is quietly self-aware.
