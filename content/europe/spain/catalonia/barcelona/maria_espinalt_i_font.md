Maria Espinalt i Font taught in the Sant Andreu neighbourhood for most of her working life, eventually directing a school that became a model of the progressive pedagogy that flourished during the Second Spanish Republic. Republican education in Catalonia was a genuine experiment: coeducational, secular, rooted in active learning and Catalan language instruction at a time when most schools were controlled by the Church and conducted in Castilian. Teachers like Espinalt were at the forefront of that project.

When Franco's forces took Barcelona in January 1939, this entire educational world was dismantled. Schools were purged of teachers who had worked under the Republic; Catalan was banned from classrooms; the books, the methods, and in many cases the teachers themselves were eliminated. Espinalt survived but her school was transformed beyond recognition. She died in 1958, seven years before the Catalan language was cautiously readmitted to some educational contexts.

The memorial sits in the district where she worked, among the streets her students walked. Naming a space after her is a form of the restoration that the teachers of the Republic never fully received in their own lifetimes.
