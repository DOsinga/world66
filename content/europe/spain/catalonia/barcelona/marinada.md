The *marinada* is what Barcelonans call the sea breeze that arrives reliably in the late afternoon, cooling the city after the heat of the day. It is one of the great pleasures of the Barcelona summer — you can feel the exact moment it arrives, the shift in temperature and humidity that moves inland from the port and climbs the hills. The breeze is so characteristic and so beloved that it has acquired the status of a personality, a reliable visitor whose absence is noted with complaint.

Montjuïc, where the sculpture stands, receives the *marinada* with particular force since the hill rises directly from the sea and intercepts the wind before it disperses into the city below. The placement of a work with this title on this hill is therefore apt in a way that is almost too neat — and yet the aptness is real, not constructed, because this is genuinely where the sea breeze first arrives.

The choice to name a sculpture after a weather phenomenon rather than a person, an event, or an abstract virtue is itself interesting: it says that this city's relationship with the sea and the air is worth commemorating, that the invisible forces that make urban life livable are as worthy of attention as the visible monuments of history.
