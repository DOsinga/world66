In March 1938, Italian Aviazione Legionaria bombers flying from Mallorca carried out 13 raids on Barcelona over 40 hours. The raids targeted the port, the Estació de França, and central Barcelona, but caused massive civilian casualties because of the imprecision of the bombing and the density of the urban population. Over 1,000 civilians were killed; the raids were the most intensive aerial bombardment of a European city up to that date, exceeding the scale of the Guernica bombing by a factor of ten.

The raids were ordered by Mussolini with the agreement of Franco, and were intended both to destroy Barcelona's war production capacity and to demoralise the Republican population. The residential buildings hit in the Eixample — where many of the bombs fell — provided no military advantage; they were simply part of the urban fabric in the path of imprecisely dropped bombs.

The memorial, installed in 2003, is a modest installation near the Carrer del Carme — not far from where several of the most devastating hits occurred. Barcelona's public memory of the Civil War bombings was suppressed under Franco (who could hardly commemorate what his Italian allies had done) and has been reconstructed only since the 1980s. The 1938 raids are less famous internationally than Guernica, though they were larger in scale, because they targeted a large city rather than a small town and because the large city's war has been written about in terms of its many other tragedies.
