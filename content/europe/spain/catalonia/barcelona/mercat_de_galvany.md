The Mercat de Galvany was built in 1927 to serve the Sant Gervasi and Sarrià-Sant Gervasi residential areas — the upper-city neighbourhoods that became, in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, the preferred address of Barcelona's professional and upper-middle class. The market reflects the social character of the neighbourhood it serves: the stalls tend toward higher-quality produce and more specialised vendors than the markets in working-class districts.

The building is a standard iron-frame covered market of the early 20th century type — similar in structure to the Abaceria in Gràcia or Sant Antoni in the Eixample, with a central nave and side aisles under a corrugated roof. Its unassuming exterior gives no particular indication of its social context; only the price of the produce and the dress of the shoppers distinguishes it from other neighbourhood markets.

The Galvany is significant precisely because it has maintained its neighbourhood function without converting to tourism or gentrifying its vendor mix in the way that the Boqueria and, to some degree, Santa Caterina have. Householders from the surrounding apartments shop here for daily provisions; the stalls are stocked for Barcelona residents rather than for visitors who want an Instagrammable food experience. The market represents the more ordinary face of Barcelona's covered market network — the part that was always about feeding the city rather than displaying it.
