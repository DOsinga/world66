Rovira i Trias won the 1859 competition to plan Barcelona's expansion beyond the medieval walls. His radial plan was chosen by the city; the central government in Madrid rejected it and substituted the Cerdà grid without a competition or appeal. A statue in Gràcia's Plaça del Diamant commemorates Rovira i Trias and his unbuilt plan. The Barceloneta market, inaugurated in 1884, is one of the few major works he actually got to build.

The cast-iron structure with its distinctive octagonal lantern served the community of fishermen and dock workers who inhabited the 18th-century grid neighbourhood behind the beach. Barceloneta was built on reclaimed land in the 1750s to rehouse residents displaced by the construction of the Ciutadella citadel; its tight grid of narrow streets and low buildings gave the fishing community a compact district close to the water. The market was the commercial anchor of that community.

More than a century later, the Barceloneta market's fish stalls remain the most reliable indicator of what the Mediterranean fleet actually catches — which changes by season and by luck far more than restaurant menus suggest. The seafood restaurants that line the waterfront are supplied from here; visiting the market in the morning shows what those restaurants will be serving that evening and which species are currently in season.
