The Mercat de la Llibertat (Freedom Market) in Gràcia was built in 1888, the same year as the Universal Exposition, by the architect Miquel Pascual i Tintorer. Its name was politically significant at the moment of its construction: "Llibertat" (Freedom or Liberty) was a word with explicit liberal and revolutionary associations in 19th-century Spain, having been the rallying term for the anti-absolutist movements of the 1820s, 1830s, and 1868 revolutions. Naming a public building "Freedom" was a statement of political identity.

The building's iron and glass structure is typical of the covered market type developed in the second half of the 19th century: a central nave with side aisles under a vaulted roof supported by cast-iron columns. The Llibertat is smaller and less architecturally ambitious than the Born market hall or the Boqueria, but it is the right scale for the Gràcia neighbourhood it serves — more intimate, more accessible, more neighbourhood-focused.

The market was renovated in 2009 and retained its original iron structure while updating the interior. It now serves primarily as a neighbourhood food market, though like most Barcelona markets it has developed a small tourism component. The name Llibertat has outlasted every political context in which it could have been controversial: under the Restoration monarchy, under Primo de Rivera's dictatorship, under Franco (who renamed many streets but apparently left this market alone), and into the present democracy. The market's freedom is now simply the freedom to buy good bread.
