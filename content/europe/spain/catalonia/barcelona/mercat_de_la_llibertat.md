The name came first, the building came twelve years later. Gràcia declared itself a republic during Spain's First Republic in 1873; the municipality was absorbed back into Barcelona in 1897, but the market it had named *Llibertat* in 1876 kept its title through every subsequent regime. That no authority ever bothered to rename it says something about the priorities of markets versus monuments.

The iron structure itself, completed in 1888, is among the earliest examples of industrial market architecture in Catalonia — a covered hall using prefabricated ironwork at a moment when most markets were still open-air or housed in converted monasteries. The 2009 renovation preserved the original ironwork while modernising the infrastructure beneath it; visitors who arrive expecting a renovation-era interior will find the bones of the Victorian original still visible in the roof.

This is where Gràcia residents actually shop. The tourist economy that has transformed the Mercat de la Boqueria into something closer to a food hall has barely touched the Llibertat; the stalls sell to the neighbourhood, the prices reflect local incomes, and the conversation at the fish counter is conducted in Catalan.
