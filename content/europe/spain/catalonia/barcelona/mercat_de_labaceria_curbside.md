The Mercat de l'Abaceria Central in Gràcia takes its name from the abacers: medieval traders in certain foodstuffs — rice, legumes, dried fruits, oil — who occupied a distinct niche in the medieval trading system. The abacers were the wholesale distributors of staple goods, positioned between the producers and the retail stalls. The term was archaic even in the 19th century, but it was used for the market because the site had been associated with this type of trading for several centuries.

The market building was constructed between 1888 and 1892, in the same cast-iron style as several other Barcelona markets of the period. It is less architecturally distinguished than the Born's Mercat de Santa Caterina or the Boqueria, but it has remained a functioning neighbourhood market in a way that both of those have not: the Abaceria serves the Gràcia neighbourhood rather than the tourist trade, and the stalls sell to local residents rather than selling cured meats to visitors.

A Sunday antique and flea market in the street outside the Abaceria has been operating since the 1970s. It spills across the Travessera de Gràcia and along the Carrer de la Providència, selling furniture, books, clothing, and the accumulated possessions of the neighbourhood. The market and the street flea market together create a commercial ecosystem that has survived gentrification pressure largely intact — partly because the neighbourhood is large enough to maintain a diverse population.
