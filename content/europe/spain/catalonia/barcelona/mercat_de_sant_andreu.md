The Mercat de Sant Andreu was built in 1909, twelve years after the former independent municipality was annexed by Barcelona. The construction of a covered iron market was itself a statement: the city's infrastructure reaching into what had been a separate town, imposing the standard model of the covered municipal market that was being built across Barcelona's expansion zones.

The market's structure is typical of the iron market type: a rectangular building with a central nave and side aisles under a corrugated iron and glass roof, supported by cast-iron columns. It is smaller than the Boqueria or the Born market hall but follows the same engineering logic. The interior has been renovated several times without losing its basic spatial character.

Sant Andreu's market has maintained its neighbourhood function more successfully than some of the more tourist-affected Barcelona markets. The district's distance from the main tourist zones — it is well north of the Eixample and requires a deliberate journey to reach — means the stalls serve a local population rather than a tourist one. The mercat de barri (neighbourhood market) functions that were designed into the 19th-century market system but that have largely been displaced by supermarkets and tourism in the city centre survive more intact in Sant Andreu than in almost any other part of Barcelona.
