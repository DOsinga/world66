The Mercat de Santa Caterina in the Sant Pere neighbourhood was the first covered food market built in Barcelona — opened in 1848, on the site of the Dominican convent of Santa Caterina that had been demolished after the anti-clerical fires of 1835. The original market building was a standard 19th-century iron-frame structure with a gabled roof.

The renovation carried out between 1997 and 2005 was designed by the Catalan architect Enric Miralles, who replaced the original roof with an undulating wave structure covered in a mosaic of approximately 325,000 ceramic pieces in multiple colours. The roof was designed to be visible from the apartment windows overlooking the market, creating a view from above that is distinct from the experience at street level.

Miralles died in 2000, at the age of 45, before the renovation was complete. His office, under the direction of his widow and collaborator Benedetta Tagliabue, completed the project. The mosaic roof is thus simultaneously Miralles's most prominent Barcelona work and a memorial to an architect who was considered one of the most important of his generation. The excavation work that preceded the renovation also revealed the remains of the Dominican convent beneath the market floor; these remains are partially visible through glass panels set into the market pavement, adding another layer to a building that sits on a demolished convent, beneath an extraordinary roof, in a neighbourhood whose medieval street plan encodes a wool-trading economy.
