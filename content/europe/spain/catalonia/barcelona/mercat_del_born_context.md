The El Born market hall — the large iron and glass structure in the centre of the Born neighbourhood — was built between 1874 and 1876 to designs by Josep Fontserè, the architect who also designed the Parc de la Ciutadella. It is an outstanding example of 19th-century iron architecture, with a central nave and four side aisles under a vaulted iron roof. It functioned as a wholesale food market until 1971, when the construction of the Mercabarna wholesale market in the Zona Franca made it redundant.

Plans to convert the building into a provincial library were abandoned in 2001 when construction workers began excavating the floor for the library's infrastructure. What they found transformed the project entirely: beneath the market floor lay the preserved street plan, house foundations, and artefacts of the La Ribera neighbourhood that had been demolished in 1714 after Barcelona's defeat in the War of the Spanish Succession. The Bourbon forces demolished the neighbourhood to build the Ciutadella fortress; they simply bulldozed the houses and used the rubble as fill. The iron market had been built on top of the fill, sealing the ruins.

The discovery was not merely archaeological — it was politically explosive. The 1714 defeat is the founding myth of Catalan nationalism, and finding the physical remains of the neighbourhood destroyed by Castilian forces was intensely resonant. The library project was abandoned; the building was converted into the El Born Cultural Centre, which displays the excavated ruins as its principal exhibit. The iron market hall became, in effect, a reliquary for the memory of what was destroyed.
