The ninot turned up during excavation of the foundations, a painted wooden figure of no identified origin or purpose, and the workers named the new market after it. Barcelona's other markets carry the names of saints, neighbourhoods, or abstract nouns; the Ninot carries the name of an object that was simply in the way. The original puppet is displayed inside the market.

The 1933 building was demolished in 2015 and replaced on the same footprint — a reconstruction that preserved the street-facing facade while rebuilding the interior to modern food market standards. This was the same programme applied to several Barcelona markets in the 2000s and 2010s; some emerged as tourist-oriented food halls, but the Ninot serves the Esquerra de l'Eixample and has retained the character of a neighbourhood provisioner.

The Esquerra de l'Eixample — the left side of Cerdà's grid, west of Passeig de Gràcia — has been the centre of Barcelona's LGBTQ+ community since the 1980s, and the Ninot reflects the neighbourhood it feeds. The market's character comes as much from its regulars as from its stalls, which run to meat, fish, and produce without the cured-ham theatrics of more tourist-facing markets.
