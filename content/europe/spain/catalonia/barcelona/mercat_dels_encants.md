The canopy does not just shelter the stalls — it duplicates them. Walk beneath the Mercat dels Encants and you see the jumble of furniture, clothes, and bric-a-brac repeated overhead in distorted reflection, so the market appears larger and stranger than it is. The faceted aluminium panels were engineered to avoid a single dominant reflection point; from any position you see a different fragment of sky, city, and crowd.

The market itself predates the building by centuries. Written records of the Encants — the word means "enchantments" but in Catalan commercial usage came to mean cheap bargains — go back to the fourteenth century. For most of its modern history it operated as a chaotic, unlicensed open-air sprawl near the old Glòries roundabout, and it was genuinely grimy in a way that Barcelona's tourist infrastructure is not. The relocation was contested: dealers worried a formal structure would sanitise the market and price them out. The canopy arrived as a compromise — open sides, no glass walls, the same dust and improvisation, but legible from across the Avinguda Meridiana.

Fermín Vázquez of b720 Arquitectes designed the canopy as a single gesture rather than a roof-plus-supports system: the twelve steel columns are external, leaving the 25-metre interior clear. The result sits directly opposite Jean Nouvel's Torre Glòries, which glows red at night. The two buildings have become an accidental architectural landmark pair for a district — the old Poblenou industrial zone turned 22@ tech district — that otherwise lacks a coherent identity.
