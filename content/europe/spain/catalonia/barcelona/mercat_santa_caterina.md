The roof is visible from the upper floors of buildings two blocks away: a billowing, undulating surface of mosaic hexagons in deep reds, yellows, greens and blues, like a pixelated version of the Catalan landscape seen from the air. The market it covers, Mercat de Santa Caterina, stands on the site of the 13th-century convent of Santa Caterina de Siena, which was demolished in 1836 during the liberal-era suppression of religious houses. A market was built on the ruins in 1848 and fell gradually into disuse. The current structure is its reinvention.

The architect Enric Miralles won the competition to redesign the market in 1997. He died of a brain tumour in 2000, at age 45, while the project was still in construction. His wife and architectural partner Benedetta Tagliabue completed the work, and the market opened in 2005. The roof, which Miralles had conceived as a landscape of colour rather than a conventional covering, became his memorial as much as his design. The 325,000 hexagonal tiles were fabricated in Seville and installed one by one.

Stand at the north end and look at the full sweep of the roof. The archaeologists who excavated the site before construction found extensive remains of the medieval convent, Roman-era structures beneath those, and human burials from multiple periods. The brief was to incorporate these findings without burying them again; part of the building's lower floor is excavated and the remains are visible through glass, so the market operates over its own archaeology.
