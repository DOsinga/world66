Núñez Targa was arrested by the Gestapo in southern France in 1944, having fled Spain in 1939 after the Republican defeat. She was deported to Ravensbrück, the SS camp north of Berlin built specifically for women, where she survived until liberation. Her memoir took decades to reach print: Spanish-language testimony from the concentration camps was sparse, and women's experiences within it sparser still.

*El carretó dels gossos* (The Dog Cart) appeared in Catalan in 1980 and was among the first accounts in any Iberian language to describe the specific conditions of a Nazi women's camp — the forced labour, the prisoner hierarchies, the daily mechanics of survival. A Spanish edition followed in 1984. Núñez Targa died in 1986, having seen it published on both sides of the linguistic divide.

Barcelona's Stolpersteine programme has placed stones for several women deported to Ravensbrück, an explicit effort to mark a group largely absent from official memorial records. The stone here gives her name, her birth year, and the name of the camp — the minimum information Demnig's project requires, enough to make a disappearance legible in a residential pavement.
