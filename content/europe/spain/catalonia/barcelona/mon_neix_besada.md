The title, which translates as "The world is born in every kiss," is printed in small letters beneath the mural, easily missed. The image itself is harder to miss: two figures in close embrace, their kiss rendered at monumental scale across a wall on the edge of Plaça d'Isidre Nonell, a tiny square just steps from the Rambla but largely overlooked by the people streaming past.

The work was created in 2014 by Joan Fontcuberta — one of Catalonia's most celebrated photographers, a man known for constructing elaborate fictions that look like documentary truth — together with ceramicist Toni Cumella. The technique is photomosaics: the central image is composed entirely of smaller images, thousands of them, each a photograph of couples kissing from different countries and cultures. Up close it is a grid of intimacy from around the world; step back and the whole picture resolves.

The piece was commissioned by the University of Barcelona and the city council. Fontcuberta described it as a monument to ordinary love — not the heroic, not the historical, but the daily private act of two people closing the distance between themselves. At 8 metres wide by 3.8 metres high, it is built from fired ceramic tiles designed to last centuries. It seems likely that future residents of this neighbourhood, centuries from now, will still walk past it without reading the small text beneath.
