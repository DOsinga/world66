The Monastery of Pedralbes was founded in 1326 by Elisenda de Moncada, the fourth and last wife of King James II of Aragon. The king died in 1327, less than a year after the monastery's consecration. Elisenda was 35 years old. She entered the Poor Clares community she had founded, adopted the habit, and lived in the monastery for the remaining 34 years of her life. She died in 1364, at roughly 70, having spent most of her adult life in the institution she had built.

The cloister she commissioned is three storeys tall — unusual for a Gothic cloister, which were typically single-storey or two-storey — and is the finest surviving example of Catalan Gothic cloister architecture. The ground-floor arcade with its slender columns and pointed arches, the first-floor gallery above, and the cells that open off it at the upper level all survive in exceptional condition. Elisenda's own tomb is in the church; she is depicted twice — in royal dress on the side facing the public, in the Poor Clare habit on the side facing the cloister, indicating that she considered herself both queen and nun.

The monastery remained an active Poor Clares community until 1983, when the community agreed to move to new buildings while the historic monastery was opened as a museum. A small Poor Clares community still occupies part of the complex; they follow a strict enclosure rule and are not seen by visitors. The medieval monastery is now administered by the city of Barcelona and is one of the less-visited of the city's major monuments, located in the upper residential district of Pedralbes, far from the main tourist routes.
