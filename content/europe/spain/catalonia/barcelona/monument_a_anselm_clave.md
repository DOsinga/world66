title: Monument a Anselm Clavé

Few monuments in Barcelona honour a man who genuinely changed daily life for the city's factory workers. Josep Anselm Clavé believed that music was not a luxury of the bourgeoisie but a right of the labouring poor. In 1845 he founded the first of his workers' choral societies, convincing men who had spent twelve hours at a loom to gather in the evenings and sing together in parts. Within two decades the movement had hundreds of thousands of members across Catalonia.

Clavé wrote the music his choirs sang — sardanas, havaneres, and popular songs that blended folk melody with romantic harmonics. His compositions were vehicles for a politics of dignity: the lyrics urged solidarity, temperance, and pride in Catalan identity at a time when that identity was systematically suppressed. The choral movement he founded became one of the infrastructures of what historians call the Renaixença, the Catalan cultural revival of the nineteenth century.

The monument, inaugurated in 1884 in the Parc de la Ciutadella, was among the first public sculptures in Barcelona to honour a man of the people rather than a general or a king. It was placed deliberately near the workers' districts, in the park the city had just opened to the public after decades as a military citadel that had oppressed those same neighbourhoods.
