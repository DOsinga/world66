title: Monument a Frederic Soler (Serafí Pitarra)

Frederic Soler adopted the pen name Serafí Pitarra when he began writing zarzuelas — short musical comedies — in Catalan in the 1860s, at a time when the language had been systematically excluded from the stage for over a century. He used the pseudonym partly for plausible deniability and partly because it fit the irreverent, popular spirit of his work: Pitarra (the name suggests something slightly scruffy and street-level) wrote farces about Barcelona working-class life that audiences recognised as their own experience.

His plays were enormously successful, and their success helped create the conditions for the Teatre Català movement — the argument that Catalan could sustain a serious, professional theatre. He went on to found the Teatre Romea in 1863, which became the principal venue for Catalan-language drama for the next century and a half. The Romea still operates today, barely fifty metres from this monument on the Rambla, making the sculpture something like a theatre sign in marble.

Soler eventually dropped the pseudonym and wrote under his own name, but the pen name stuck as his public identity. The monument, inaugurated in 1906, shows him in a relaxed pose — not heroic but familiar, the way a popular entertainer rather than a statesman might be remembered. It was placed on the Rambla because that was where Barcelona's theatrical and bohemian life concentrated, and where his audiences had lived.
