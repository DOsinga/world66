Poblenou was Barcelona's industrial engine in the 19th century — its factories processed cotton, manufactured chemicals, and powered the city's rapid expansion, earning it the nickname "the Manchester of Catalonia." Among its industries was a significant concentration of printing and publishing houses, which made the area a natural setting for a monument referencing one of the great novels about the literary world: Balzac's *Illusions perdues*, whose young hero goes to Paris to make his fortune as a writer and discovers that publishing is a business driven by money and cynicism rather than by art.

The monument was installed as part of the urban regeneration of the 22@ district — the "innovation district" created from the old industrial Poblenou from the early 2000s onward, when former factories were converted into offices for technology and creative companies. The open bronze book is a tribute both to Balzac and to the specific industrial history of this neighbourhood: the workers who set type and ran printing presses here for over a century.

The choice of title — lost illusions — carries a particular resonance in a district undergoing rapid gentrification. The creative industries that the 22@ district was designed to attract have displaced many of the working-class residents and artisan businesses that gave Poblenou its character. Whether the monument's irony is intentional is a question visitors can contemplate as they look at the gleaming new office towers that now surround it.
