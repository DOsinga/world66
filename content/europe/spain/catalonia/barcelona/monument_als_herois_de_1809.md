title: Monument als Herois de 1809

Napoleon's occupation of Spain from 1808 onward is remembered very differently in different parts of the country. In Catalonia the French were not liberators bringing Enlightenment reforms but conquerors who had already invaded twice in living memory — in the 1640s and again in the 1690s. The occupation of Barcelona by French troops in 1808 was experienced as yet another in a long series of impositions by a power that had always treated Catalonia as a buffer zone or a prize.

The conspirators honoured by this monument were civilians and minor military figures who organised resistance networks in occupied Barcelona. When their plot was discovered in 1809, the French military authorities executed several of them as an example. Their deaths had less immediate strategic impact than similar resistance actions elsewhere, but their commemoration in the 19th century served the broader project of Catalan historical memory: a continuous narrative of resistance to external power, whether Castilian, French, or later Bourbon.

The monument is in the Parc de la Ciutadella, the old Bourbon fortress whose construction in 1714 had itself required the demolition of an entire working-class neighbourhood. The park is therefore layered with political memory: built on the site of oppression, it was also the place where the Universal Exposition of 1888 announced Barcelona's modernity, and where several monuments to Catalan resistance — including this one — now stand. The choice of the Ciutadella as the setting is not accidental.
