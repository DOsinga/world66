The memorial to Salvador Allende on the slope above Gràcia was installed in 1985, twelve years after Allende's death in the Chilean coup of September 1973. The inscription quotes his last radio address: "La historia es nuestra y la hacen los pueblos" — History is ours, and it is made by the peoples. Allende died on 11 September 1973, a date that resonates in Barcelona where the same date marks the fall of the city to Bourbon forces in 1714.

Barcelona had received a significant Chilean exile community after the 1973 coup. Allende's Socialist government had been a reference point for the Catalan left, operating in the Franco period under severe repression but maintaining connections with exiled Latin American politicians who had in turn maintained connections with exiled Catalans in Latin America. The exile communities created solidarity networks that made Allende's death feel personally immediate to many in Barcelona.

The memorial is part of a larger pattern. Barcelona's working-class and politically active neighbourhoods have, since the return of democracy, installed memorials to a range of international left-wing figures — Mandela, Lumumba, Che Guevara, various Latin American leftists — who represent solidarity with anti-colonial and anti-fascist struggles that Barcelona's own 20th-century experience made legible. These memorials are concentrated in neighbourhoods far from the tourist circuits, addressed to a local audience rather than to visitors.
