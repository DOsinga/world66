Lluís Companys i Jover (1882-1940) served as President of the Generalitat de Catalunya from 1934. He had previously been a trade union lawyer and republican politician. When the Nationalist forces took Barcelona in January 1939, Companys fled to France with the rest of the Generalitat government. In 1940, the Gestapo arrested him in occupied France and handed him to the Spanish authorities in an extradition that violated international law.

Companys was returned to Barcelona, tried by a military tribunal in a secret session lasting approximately an hour, convicted of "military rebellion" — the charge the Franco regime applied to those who had defended the legitimate Republican government — and shot at Montjuïc castle on October 15, 1940. His last words, according to witnesses, were "Per Catalunya!" (For Catalonia).

The monument in Sant Andreu is one of several across Barcelona and Catalonia commemorating Companys. The Sant Andreu location reflects the neighbourhood's connection to the man who grew up in its working-class streets. Companys was born in a rural Catalan town but spent his formative years in Barcelona's labour movement, and Sant Andreu — with its factory workers and trade union culture — was the social environment that shaped his politics. The monument was installed after the transition to democracy, when commemorating victims of Franco became legally possible.
