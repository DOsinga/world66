At the corner of Plaça de Catalunya facing La Rambla, a white geometric sculpture rises from a low base: three-dimensional forms in the Constructivist manner, angular and spare, representing the Catalan flag and a stylised human figure. This is the Monument a Francesc Macià, designed by the sculptor Josep Maria Subirachs (later famous for completing the façade of the Sagrada Família) and installed in 1991.

Francesc Macià was the founder of Esquerra Republicana de Catalunya and the first person to hold the title of President of the Generalitat in the 20th century. On 14 April 1931 — the same day that Alfonso XIII abdicated and the Spanish Republic was proclaimed — Macià stepped onto a balcony of the Generalitat building on Plaça de Sant Jaume and proclaimed the Catalan Republic. The proclamation lasted three days before Macià was persuaded by representatives of the Spanish Republican government to reframe it as autonomy within a federal Spain.

The monument is placed at Plaça de Catalunya because the square — the symbolic centre of Barcelona, the point where the old city meets the Eixample — was where the crowds gathered in April 1931 to hear the news. Macià died in office in December 1933 without seeing a statute of autonomy enacted. The monument's location in the most visible public space in the city is deliberate: Macià is most visible precisely where his republic was most briefly proclaimed.
