The Parc de la Ciutadella has its famous sculpture collection, but Montjuïc has its own open-air sculpture park, less visited and more atmospheric, scattered among the gardens and pine woods on the hillside. The park grew around the facilities left over from the 1929 International Exposition and the 1992 Olympics, both of which shaped Montjuïc's landscape considerably. Between these waves of construction and renovation, the hillside accumulated artworks from various programmes of public art commissions.

Beat Zoderer is a Swiss conceptual artist whose work frequently deals with perception — the way that simple geometric forms in specific materials produce different experiences of colour, light, and mass depending on the angle and time of day. Granite in morning light behaves differently from granite in afternoon shadow, and a work titled *Morgen* makes a quiet argument that the title is part of the piece: you are meant to come back at a different hour and notice the difference.

Montjuïc has always been Barcelona's hillside of multiple identities — a cemetery, an amusement park, an Olympic venue, a castle that served as a prison, a series of gardens, and a place where Barcelona's various immigrant communities have found space. The outdoor sculpture park sits within this layered landscape, and works like Zoderer's contribute to the hill's character as a place that rewards slow, repeated attention rather than a single tourist visit.
