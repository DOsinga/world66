Sant Martí de Provençals was an independent municipality on the Pla de Barcelona — the flat agricultural plain east of the old city — until its annexation by Barcelona in 1897. Like Sarrià, it had its priest-archivist who understood that the rapid changes of the industrial era were destroying the physical and documentary record of a way of life that had persisted for centuries. Relats worked in the tradition of Catalan ecclesiastical historiography, collecting parish records, local documents, and oral histories that formed the basis of later scholarly work on the area.

The timing of this work was urgent because Sant Martí transformed faster than almost any other part of the Barcelona area in the 19th century. The Poblenou district — the industrial zone within the former municipality — went from market gardens and fishermen's cottages to factories, workers' tenements, and railway yards within two or three generations. The physical landscape that Relats described in his historical work had been largely obliterated by the time of his death, which makes his documentation all the more valuable.

The neighbourhood of Sant Martí that he served is now part of the 22@ innovation district, where former industrial buildings have been converted into offices for technology companies. The layers of history that Relats helped to preserve — medieval farming, early industrialisation, working-class political organisation, post-industrial reinvention — lie beneath the polished surfaces of a district that is constantly remaking itself, and his work is one of the anchors that prevents that history from disappearing entirely.
