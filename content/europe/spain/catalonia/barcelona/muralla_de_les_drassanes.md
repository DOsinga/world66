The city wall built between the 13th and 14th centuries to enclose the Raval neighbourhood survives in one of its longest accessible sections along the back of the Drassanes Reials — the Royal Shipyard complex that now houses the Maritime Museum. The wall here is approximately 10 metres tall and runs for over 100 metres, visible from the Avinguda del Paral·lel.

This section of wall was preserved precisely because of the Drassanes. The shipyard was established in the 14th century at the southwestern corner of the city, where it had direct access to the sea (now much further away, due to land reclamation). The wall of the Drassanes was integrated with the city wall: the shipyard's perimeter was also the city's perimeter at this point. The two structures — defensive wall and industrial complex — reinforced each other structurally and functionally.

The Drassanes are among the largest surviving Gothic industrial buildings in Europe. The building's long, parallel naves — originally covered building slips where galleys were constructed — cover approximately 15,000 square metres. The city wall section along their back boundary gives a sense of the scale of the medieval fortification programme: this was not a decorative or ceremonial structure but a serious military and civic infrastructure designed to make the city defensible against naval and land attack. The view of the wall from the Paral·lel, between the theatre buildings and the traffic, is one of the stranger visual juxtapositions in the city.
