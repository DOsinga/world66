Samaranch became president of the IOC in 1980 and held the position until 2001, presiding over the organisation through the Los Angeles and Seoul games and the end of the amateur requirement that had kept professional athletes out of the Olympics. His twenty-year tenure was not without controversy — he was accused of tolerating corruption within the IOC, and he had been a collaborator of the Franco regime in his earlier career in Catalan and Spanish sports politics. But his role in bringing the 1992 Games to Barcelona is undisputed, and the transformation those Games catalysed is visible in every direction from this hillside.

The 1992 Olympics gave Barcelona a waterfront. Before 1992 the port and the industrial installations along the coast had cut the city off from the sea; the Games required the construction of the Olympic Village on the Poblenou waterfront, which involved clearing the old factories, building a new urban district, and opening four kilometres of beach that had previously been inaccessible. The new coastline, the new ring roads, the renovated Montjuïc facilities, the revamped metro network — all of it was compressed into a six-year construction programme of staggering intensity.

The museum itself documents the history of the Olympic movement and of Spanish sport, with particular emphasis on the 1992 Games. Its location on Montjuïc, directly below the Olympic stadium, means that visitors can move from the exhibition to the actual venues without any gap — the history and the physical evidence are in immediate proximity, which is something museums rarely achieve.
