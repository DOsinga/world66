The five Gothic palaces on Carrer de Montcada — the Palau Aguilar, Palau del Baró de Castellet, Palau Meca, Casa Mauri, and Palau Finestres — were built between the 13th and 15th centuries by Barcelona's merchant aristocracy, when this street was the city's most prestigious address. By the 20th century the palaces were in various states of disrepair. The museum opened in 1963 in the Palau Aguilar under the direction of Picasso's friend Jaume Sabartés; subsequent acquisitions connected the adjoining palaces into a single institution spanning the full length of the block.

The collection is weighted toward the Barcelona years: Picasso arrived at age fourteen when his father took a teaching post at the art school, and between 1895 and 1904 he developed the technical range that preceded his Paris work. The museum holds the juvenilia that shows the trajectory — academic exercises, portraits, early experiments — that major retrospectives tend to skip in favour of Cubism. The complete *Las Meninas* series, painted in 1957, occupies its own rooms: 58 canvases in which Picasso systematically dismantled and reassembled Velázquez's 1656 painting, panel by panel.

Picasso donated key works directly and stipulated Barcelona as the location; after his death in 1973, further donations from his widow Jacqueline and from Sabartés's estate expanded the collection. The museum's character — early work, Barcelona focus, Gothic setting — reflects Picasso's own preference for how his formation should be remembered, rather than the Paris period that made him famous.
