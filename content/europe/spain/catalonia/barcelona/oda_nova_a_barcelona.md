Joan Maragall's *Oda a Barcelona* of 1883 was one of the defining texts of the Catalan Renaixença: an address to the city in the second person, calling it beautiful and terrible, praising its vitality and condemning its injustices. The poem set a template for a particular kind of urban lyric in Catalan — the city addressed directly as a *tu*, a you, simultaneously loved and criticised. It was inevitably the model for later poets who wanted to speak to and about Barcelona.

Ernest Maragall — grandson of Joan, and mayor of Barcelona from 2006 to 2011 — wrote his *Oda nova a Barcelona* as an explicit dialogue with the ancestor's poem, updating the address to a city transformed by democracy, migration, tourism, and the 1992 Olympic reinvention. The work commissioned here takes its title from the poem and uses it as a framework for a public art piece that sits on the slopes of Montjuïc, looking out over the city the poems address.

The installation near the MNAC creates a suggestive triangulation: the great Catalan museum on the hill, the city spreading below, and a work of art that explicitly connects to a literary tradition of speaking to that city. It asks visitors to think about Barcelona not just as a place of visual spectacle but as a city with a literary history — a city that has been addressed in verse for 150 years and is still, arguably, listening.
