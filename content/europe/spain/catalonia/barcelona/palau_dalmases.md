The mansion at number 20 Carrer de Montcada was built between 1698 and 1717 for Pau Ignasi de Dalmases i Ros, a member of Barcelona's merchant aristocracy and patron of the arts who founded the Acadèmia dels Desconfiats (Academy of the Mistrustful), an early literary society. The building's exterior is restrained by the standards of its era, but the interior courtyard is exceptional: a staircase of carved stone with Neptune and his horses emerging from the balustrade, sea-nymphs on the newel posts, and grape clusters twisting up the columns — Baroque excess in a city that generally ran to Gothic austerity.

The courtyard sculpture references the sea not by accident. Dalmases made his fortune in the Atlantic trade that had transformed Catalan merchant families in the decades following the union of the Aragonese and Castilian crowns. The imagery of Neptune — god of the commerce-enabling sea — was a standard statement of mercantile wealth in the period.

The palace later housed the Omnium Cultural, the Catalan cultural association that has been one of the main organisations promoting the Catalan language during and after the Franco period. It now operates as a Baroque music venue and cocktail bar, which means the courtyard — the finest in Barcelona's old city — can be entered by anyone who wants a drink. The pairing of a 17th-century carved staircase with jazz and cocktails is either an outrage or entirely appropriate, depending on your disposition.
