The main façade of the Palau de la Generalitat on the Plaça de Sant Jaume is a Renaissance addition from 1602. The building's actual historical core is accessible through the Gothic portal on the Carrer del Bisbe, created between 1416 and 1425 by Marc Safont under the commission of the Diputació del General — the representative body of the Crown of Aragon in Catalonia that would evolve into the Generalitat.

The portal is one of the finest examples of Catalan Gothic civic architecture. A carved tympanum shows Sant Jordi (Saint George, patron of Catalonia) slaying the dragon, flanked by angels. The proportions are characteristic of Catalan Gothic: relatively flat, horizontally emphatic, with ornament concentrated at the portal frame rather than distributed across the surface. The relief carving is of the quality more usually seen in religious buildings — Safont brought ecclesiastical skill to secular commission.

The Generalitat is the institution of Catalan self-government that has existed, in various forms, since the 14th century. It survived the Bourbon conquest of 1714, though with its powers radically reduced. Under Franco it was abolished entirely. It was restored in 1977 as part of Spain's transition to democracy. The medieval portal thus represents an institution that has outlasted monarchies, empires, and dictatorships. The Gothic Saint George on the tympanum is the oldest surviving depiction of the saint in his role as Catalonia's patron in a civic rather than religious context.
