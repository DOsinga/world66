The Palau del Baró de Quadras was built between 1902 and 1906 for Manuel de Quadras, a wealthy industrialist who wanted a residence on the Diagonal that would surpass anything his neighbours had managed. He commissioned Josep Puig i Cadafalch, who was at that moment the most technically ambitious of the Modernista architects, and Puig responded with a façade of astonishing intricacy: carved stone gargoyles, heraldic reliefs, Gothic tracery windows, and Plateresque decoration all assembled in a way that is simultaneously medieval in spirit and entirely original in execution.

What makes the commission historically interesting is that Quadras was simultaneously building a second house — the Casa de les Punxes, a few hundred metres away on the same boulevard — and the two buildings represent almost opposite sides of Puig's imagination. The Casa de les Punxes is massive and castle-like; the Palau del Baró de Quadras is jewel-like, surface-obsessed, more concerned with ornamental richness than structural drama.

The building is now the home of the Institut Ramon Llull, named after the thirteenth-century Mallorcan philosopher and polymath who wrote in Catalan at a time when vernacular languages were not considered fit for serious intellectual work. The Institut promotes Catalan language and culture internationally, which gives the building a pleasing continuity: it was built to project the status of a Catalan family, and it now projects the status of Catalan culture itself.
