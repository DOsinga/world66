The Palau del Baró de Vilagaià sits among the dense medieval streets of the Born district, its façade offering little hint of the courtyard beyond. Like most of the great merchant palaces of this neighbourhood, it was built in the fifteenth century for a family whose wealth came from maritime trade and whose status required a residence that communicated both prosperity and connection to Catalan Gothic tradition.

The courtyard follows the standard schema of the pati gòtic: an arcaded ground floor opening onto an internal space, with an exterior staircase rising to the upper floor under pointed arches. What distinguishes this courtyard from comparable examples is the quality of the stone carving on the capitals and the relative completeness of the surviving elements — the well, the staircase, and the arcading are all present rather than partially reconstructed.

The building has passed through many uses over the centuries, including a period as a storage facility during the nineteenth century when many of the Born palaces fell into industrial use. Its current condition is the result of gradual private restoration. The courtyard is occasionally accessible during cultural events and Open House Barcelona, and architectural historians cite it as one of the quieter arguments for taking a long, slow walk through the Born rather than heading directly to the Basílica de Santa Maria del Mar.
