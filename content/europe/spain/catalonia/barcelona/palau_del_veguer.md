The Palau del Veguer stands on Carrer dels Comtes, between the Cathedral and the Palau del Lloctinent, and was for several centuries the residence of the veguer — the royal official who governed Barcelona on behalf of the count-kings of Aragon. The position of veguer was one of the most powerful in the city's medieval administration, combining judicial, fiscal, and military functions. The palace served both as an official residence and as a court of first instance for most civil disputes in the county of Barcelona.

The building's great hall — a vaulted room with stone walls and a wooden ceiling of the fifteenth century — was the primary formal space. It was here that the veguer received petitions, presided over hearings, and published royal decrees. After the position of veguer was abolished in 1714 following the Nueva Planta decrees that centralised Bourbon administration, the palace passed through several institutional uses, including a brief period as a prison, before becoming the seat of the Conservatori Municipal de Música in the twentieth century.

The Conservatori's use of the building has been both preserving and intrusive: the great hall functions today as a concert room, which has protected the vaulted ceiling from the partitioning that affected other areas. The exterior is visible from Carrer dels Comtes; the entrance to the music school is through a later portal that does not do justice to what is behind it.
