The Palau Episcopal (Bishop's Palace) occupies the block to the southwest of the Cathedral, with its main entrance on the Carrer del Bisbe. The palace has been the residence and administrative centre of the Bishop of Barcelona for most of the city's Christian history, though the current building is largely a 12th-century Romanesque construction that has been substantially modified in later centuries.

The main gate of the palace spans what is preserved as one of the clearest accessible sections of the Roman wall of Barcino. Looking down from the arched entrance, you can see the Roman wall footing below street level — Barcelona has risen approximately 3 metres in average ground level since the Roman period, burying the lower sections of ancient walls below the current surface. The Palau's entrance gate uses a section of Roman wall as its foundation in a way that makes the relationship between the 3rd-century fortification and the medieval building visible.

The Carrer del Bisbe between the palace and the Generalitat was widened in the early 20th century as part of the Gothic Quarter renovation that also produced the neo-Gothic bridge across the street. The widening revealed sections of Roman wall that were then preserved in place. The palace is not routinely open to the public, but the street elevation makes the Roman layer visible from outside. The main courtyard, glimpsed through the gate, contains a Romanesque arcade from the 12th century that is among the oldest surviving architectural elements in the Gothic Quarter.
