The Plaça del Rei complex is often photographed for its Gothic façades — the great staircase, the Capella de Santa Àgata above, the tower of Martí. But the building on the north side of the square, the Palau del Lloctinent (Palace of the Lieutenant), is Renaissance, built between 1549 and 1557 for Ferran d'Àragó, the Viceroy of Catalonia, who served as the representative of the Spanish crown in what was still a semi-autonomous principality.

The name Lloctinent means literally "place-holder" — the viceroy held the king's place in Catalonia when the king was elsewhere, which for the Habsburgs of the 16th century was most of the time. The building was therefore a seat of delegated imperial authority, and it was built to project appropriate dignity. Its main courtyard, visible through the entrance arch, has arched galleries on two floors and a notable wooden ceiling in the staircase hall — a coffered ceiling of geometric intricacy, installed in 1557, with hundreds of carved panels that expand as they rise toward the lantern above.

The building now houses the Crown of Aragon Archive, one of the oldest continuous archive systems in the world, documenting the affairs of the Aragonese dynasty from the 12th century onwards. The courtyard is accessible during archive hours. The ceiling of the staircase hall is visible from the entrance. Look up: the carpenter who made it worked at a scale that assumed the viewer would be standing at a distance, and the effect is better seen slowly.
