Palau Moja occupies a long, formal neoclassical façade at the top of La Rambla, facing the Portaferrissa fountain. Built between 1774 and 1784 for the Marquès de Moja, it is one of the few substantial eighteenth-century aristocratic palaces in Barcelona to have survived the nineteenth-century urban transformations more or less intact. The main floor retains ceiling frescoes by Francesc Pla ("El Vigatà"), depicting allegorical scenes of Barcelona's trade and commerce — among the most complete examples of Catalan Baroque ceiling painting.

The building's literary significance centres on its connection to Jacint Verdaguer. In the 1870s and 1880s, when the Moja family had taken in the young priest-poet as a chaplain, Verdaguer worked at the palace on his long poem L'Atlàntida, an epic in Catalan about the lost continent seen through the lens of Columbus's voyage. The poem's publication in 1877 was a landmark of the Renaixença — the Catalan cultural revival — and Verdaguer became the movement's most celebrated figure. The palace's Rambla address placed him at the centre of Barcelona's intellectual life during the years he spent here.

The Generalitat de Catalunya now owns the building. The ground floor operates as a bookshop specialising in Catalan literature and culture, open to the public. The principal hall above is used for official receptions and is occasionally open for guided visits.
