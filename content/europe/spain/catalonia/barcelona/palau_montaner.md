The coincidence of surnames is not a coincidence. The Montaner family, whose Editorial Montaner i Simón was one of Barcelona's major 19th-century publishing houses, commissioned a mansion on their own street — Carrer de Mallorca — from their relative Josep Domènech i Estapà in 1889. When the project stalled, they turned to another relative to finish it: Lluís Domènech i Montaner, the architect who also built the Palau de la Música Catalana and the Hospital de Sant Pau. The hyphenated surname tells you exactly how the commission was arranged.

Domènech i Montaner completed the mansion in 1896, and the result is among the finest examples of Catalan Modernisme applied to a private residence. The facade is deliberately restrained compared to his public buildings — the Eixample street imposes a certain uniformity — but the interior opens into a sequence of rooms decorated with stained glass, elaborate tile friezes, carved stone, and ironwork that demonstrate every technique the movement deployed. The central staircase hall, lit from above through coloured glass, is the building's set piece.

The Montaner i Simón publishing house operated out of a separate building by Domènech i Montaner on Carrer d'Aragó, now the Antoni Tàpies Foundation. The Palau Montaner itself was converted to government offices in the 20th century and is now the seat of the Delegació del Govern de l'Estat a Catalunya — the Spanish central government's representative office in Catalonia. It opens to the public on specific heritage days.
