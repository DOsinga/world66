The Palau Montaner at Carrer de Mallorca 278 was begun by Josep Domènech i Estapà in 1889 but taken over and substantially redesigned by Lluís Domènech i Montaner — his relative — when construction stalled. The building was commissioned by the Montaner family, who owned the publishing house Montaner i Simon, and it served as both residence and a statement of cultural aspiration in the years when the Eixample was being built as Barcelona's new bourgeois city.

Domènech i Montaner used the façade to develop some of the ideas he would later scale up for the Palau de la Música: mosaic tile work, ceramic friezes, decorative ironwork, and windows that treat the structural frame as a secondary element to be wrapped in ornament. The interior staircase is an extraordinary piece of Modernista craftsmanship — wrought iron, stained glass, sculpted stone, and painted plasterwork combining in a space designed to make anyone ascending it feel as though they were rising through an enchanted forest.

The building is now the headquarters of the Delegació del Govern de l'Estat a Catalunya — the central government's representative office in Catalonia. It can be visited on a limited number of days per year as part of the European Heritage Days programme. The Montaner family's publishing house, whose commissions for illustrated books drove the demand for Barcelona's illustrators and graphic artists in the 1880s and 1890s, was one of the cultural institutions that created the Modernista moment; the palace is its most visible legacy.
