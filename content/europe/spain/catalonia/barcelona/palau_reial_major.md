The Palau Reial Major — the Great Royal Palace — was the principal residence of the counts of Barcelona and, after the union of Catalonia with Aragon, of the Crown of Aragon. Its construction spanned the thirteenth and fourteenth centuries, and the result was one of the finest secular Gothic complexes in Europe. The centrepiece is the Saló del Tinell, a barrel-vaulted hall of such scale and ambition that it continued to be used for state functions long after the court moved elsewhere.

The Saló del Tinell is where, in April 1493, Christopher Columbus appeared before Ferdinand and Isabella to present the first inhabitants of the Caribbean he had brought across the Atlantic, along with gold, parrots, and other proofs of his voyage. The monarchs received him from a throne dais at the far end. The scene is often painted, but the actual room where it happened is less often remarked upon: its six great arches span twenty-three metres without any intermediate support, a structural feat that made it the largest unsupported vault in medieval Catalonia.

The palace complex also includes the chapel of Santa Àgata, built over a corner of the old Roman wall. Embedded in the chapel floor you can see the Roman stonework through glass panels, and the altarpiece inside — the Epiphany by Jaume Huguet, painted around 1465 — is one of the masterworks of Catalan Gothic painting. The entire complex now forms part of the Museu d'Història de Barcelona.
