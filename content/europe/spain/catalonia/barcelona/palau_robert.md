The Palau Robert was built at the turn of the twentieth century for the Marquis of Robert, a figure of the Barcelona haute bourgeoisie who wanted a residence on the grandest boulevard of the expanding Eixample. The architect Enric Sagnier — responsible for dozens of Modernista buildings across the city — designed it in a style that blended French academic classicism with local decorative flourishes, producing a mansion that read as cosmopolitan without quite abandoning Catalan roots.

The Generalitat de Catalunya acquired the building in the 1980s and converted it into the Centre d'Informació per a Catalunya, a space for promoting Catalan culture, language, and tourism. The ground floor and garden are free to enter, and the exhibitions rotate through subjects that range from Catalan geography and architecture to contemporary design and photography. It is one of the few buildings on Passeig de Gràcia where you can walk in without a ticket.

The rear garden is the detail most worth knowing. Hidden behind the mansion, it extends into a formal green space with mature trees that provide genuine shade — a rarity in the stone-and-asphalt grid of the Eixample. The garden connects through to Carrer de Còrsega and is often used by office workers eating lunch, entirely unaware that they are sitting in the grounds of a Modernista palace.
