The dome was not built in the air. In the summer of 1990, the entire steel lattice roof of the Palau Sant Jordi — weighing around 2,400 tonnes and spanning 128 by 106 metres — was assembled on the arena floor at ground level. Hydraulic jacks then lifted it in a single coordinated operation to its final height of 45 metres above the playing surface. The lift took nine hours. It was the first time the technique had been used at this scale in Spain, and it significantly shortened the construction programme by allowing interior finishing work to proceed under cover while the roof was still being raised.

The arena was designed by Japanese architect Arata Isozaki, who won the commission in an international competition organised for the 1992 Barcelona Olympics. Isozaki worked with engineer Mamoru Kawaguchi, the inventor of the pantadome lifting system used for the roof. The building sits at the head of the Olympic ring on Montjuïc, flanked by the restored 1936 Olympic stadium and the telecommunications tower by Santiago Calatrava. Its profile — a low dome clad in ceramic tile that echoes Gaudí's trencadís technique — was a deliberate gesture toward Barcelona's architectural traditions from an architect working several thousand kilometres from them.

At 17,000 capacity the Palau Sant Jordi is Barcelona's largest indoor venue. It hosted gymnastics and volleyball in 1992, and has since served as a concert hall, basketball arena (FC Barcelona played here), and the site of major political gatherings. The 2017 Catalan independence referendum's counting process was broadcast from inside this building.
