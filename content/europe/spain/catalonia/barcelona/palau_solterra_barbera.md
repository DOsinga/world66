Most of Barcelona's medieval palaces were demolished or gutted during the nineteenth-century boom, when the city tore down its own past as quickly as it could afford to. Palau Solterra-Barberà survived because it passed through enough private hands without ever attracting the attention of a developer with sufficient capital to replace it.

The palace dates from the late fifteenth century, when the Barri Gòtic was the commercial and administrative heart of a Mediterranean empire. The families who owned it — Solterra and later Barberà — were part of the merchant patriciate who financed the Aragonese Crown's Mediterranean ambitions and expected to be housed accordingly.

The courtyard, with its Gothic arches and external staircase, follows the same typology as the grander Palau del Lloctinent nearby — the external stair leading to the piano nobile was the standard way of announcing wealth and status in late-medieval Catalan domestic architecture. Most such courtyards are gone. This one remains.
