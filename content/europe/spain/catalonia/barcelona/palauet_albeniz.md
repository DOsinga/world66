Puig i Cadafalch designed the Palauet Albéniz for the 1929 International Exposition as a residence for the royal family during their attendance at the opening. It was named after the composer Isaac Albéniz, who had no connection to Montjuïc but whose *Iberia* suite was considered an appropriate piece of Catalanist cultural prestige.

The building sits in the gardens of the Anella Olímpica, and the gardens themselves are part of Forestier's 1929 landscape design. It survived the transition from Exposition to parkland, and from monarchy to Republic, because it was simply too useful and too well-built to abandon.

Every Spanish monarch since Alfonso XIII has used it as their official residence during visits to Barcelona — making it, despite its modest size, one of the most politically loaded buildings in the city. The Catalan government and the Spanish Crown have not always agreed on the terms of those visits.
