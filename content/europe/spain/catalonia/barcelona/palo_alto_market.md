The Palo Alto market occupies the courtyard and buildings of a former factory complex at the Carrer dels Pellaires in Poblenou. The factory, which made hardware and metalwork products in the early 20th century, was converted in the 1990s into what the Spanish call a "vivero" — a business incubator for creative enterprises. Design studios, architecture practices, and photography agencies occupied the renovated factory buildings.

The first-weekend-of-the-month design market began as a pop-up event and became permanent. It now brings together several hundred vendors selling handmade clothing, jewellery, furniture, and food, in the same factory courtyards where metalwork had been produced. The name Palo Alto is a deliberate provocation: it references the California town associated with Silicon Valley, implying that Poblenou's creative cluster aspires to the same cachet. Whether this aspiration is sincere or ironic is left ambiguous.

The market is significant as an example of what the 22@ plan did to the informal economy of the area. The factory buildings that were not large enough or well-located enough for large-scale conversion became hosts for smaller creative enterprises — a pattern familiar from similar transformations in London's East End, Berlin's Mitte, and Brooklyn's waterfront. The Palo Alto complex predates 22@ and retains a character somewhat different from the plan's larger, more homogeneous conversions.
