Santiago Rusiñol was a painter, playwright, and cultural entrepreneur who became one of the central figures of Catalan Modernisme in the 1890s. He is closely associated with Sitges, the coastal town south of Barcelona where he established the museum El Cau Ferrat (Cave of Iron) and organised the Festes Modernistes — festivals that became the defining cultural events of the movement.

The concept Rusiñol pioneered at El Cau Ferrat — the idea of an artist's house as a complete aesthetic statement, where art, furniture, architecture, and everyday objects were chosen and arranged as a unified whole — was enormously influential. He collected everything: paintings (his own and others'), medieval iron objects (hence the name), ceramics, glass, furniture, and what would now be called decorative arts. The house in Sitges was the physical embodiment of the idea that art was a way of life rather than a career.

This concept fed directly into the way Barcelona's bourgeoisie thought about the Modernisme buildings they were commissioning. Casa Batlló, Casa Amatller, and the rest were not merely architecturally interesting buildings: they were total aesthetic environments, in which furniture, ironwork, stained glass, ceramics, and mosaic were designed as part of a unified whole. Rusiñol's El Cau Ferrat was the proof of concept. The museum in Sitges is worth visiting as the document of an idea that created the buildings Barcelona is famous for — even though it is 40 kilometres from the city.
