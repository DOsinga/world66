The Espanya Industrial textile factory opened in 1847 and became one of the anchor employers of the Sants working-class neighbourhood. It employed hundreds of workers at a time when Sants was a separate municipality from Barcelona, and the factory's rhythms structured neighbourhood life for over a century. The factory closed in 1977, two years after Franco's death, as the Spanish textile industry collapsed under international competition.

The city council acquired the site and ran a design competition for a public park. The winning design, by Basque architect Luis Peña Ganchegui, was completed in 1985 and remains deeply controversial. Ten concrete lighthouse towers line the northern edge of the park, each 8 metres tall, evoking a panopticon or a Roman amphitheatre's towers — the architects described them as watchtowers for a maritime park. A central lake holds a bronze Neptune by Andreu Alfaro. Classical sculpture, surveillance architecture, and post-industrial landscape are layered together in a way that seems either thoughtful or absurd.

The park was a harbinger of the urban regeneration that would transform Barcelona's industrial zones in the lead-up to the 1992 Olympics. But unlike many of those projects, which attempted to erase the industrial past, this park decided to inhabit the contradiction rather than resolve it. The towers are real and can be climbed; the views from the top encompass the Sants neighbourhood, Montjuïc, and, on clear days, the Collserola hills.
