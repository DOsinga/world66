The Parc del Poblenou, opened in 1992 as part of Barcelona's pre-Olympic urban improvements, occupies a site that had been a factory complex. Rather than demolishing all traces of the industrial past, the park design retained a large brick chimney from the former factory as a sculptural element at the park's centre. The chimney, approximately 25 metres tall, now stands surrounded by palm trees and garden paths, a relic of the manufacturing landscape that the entire Poblenou neighbourhood is in the process of dismantling and revaluing.

The chimney's preservation was a deliberate choice in a context where most of Poblenou's industrial heritage was being either demolished or converted into offices and apartments. The park was one of the first Barcelona projects to treat industrial remains as worth keeping — a position that would become more common in subsequent decades as the 22@ development spread through the neighbourhood.

The park itself is a neighbourhood amenity rather than a tourist destination: local families, dog-walkers, and children use it. Its function is unremarkable. The chimney's presence gives the park a specificity that most urban parks lack: a physical connection to the industrial landscape that produced the neighbourhood's working-class identity, preserved not in a museum but in the middle of a park where people bring their children on Saturday afternoons.
