The Parlament de Catalunya sits at the heart of the Parc de la Ciutadella in a long, low neoclassical building that was originally the arsenal of the Ciutadella fortress. The fortress itself was the instrument of Philip V's control over Barcelona after the defeat of 1714: a star-shaped military installation built directly on top of the demolished La Ribera neighbourhood, designed so that its cannons could threaten the city as effectively as they could threaten any attacker from outside. The arsenal stored the weapons that enforced Bourbon sovereignty over the conquered city.

The fortress was never popular. The people of Barcelona petitioned for its demolition for most of the 18th century and finally succeeded in 1869, when the liberal revolution allowed the city to reclaim the land. The park that replaced it — the Parc de la Ciutadella — was designed for the 1888 Universal Exhibition; the republican city created a democratic public garden precisely where the authoritarian fortress had stood. Most of the buildings were demolished; the arsenal was converted to serve as the exhibition's main building.

After 1888, the arsenal became the palace of the General Captaincy and then, from 1932, the seat of the revived Generalitat's legislative chamber. The Catalan Parliament meets there now in the very building that stored the weapons of the regime that abolished Catalonia's institutions in 1714. The building has been thoughtfully refurbished; the ironwork, the internal courtyard, and the formal façade survive. The institution that meets inside is a direct descendant of the parliamentary tradition the original fortress was built to suppress.
