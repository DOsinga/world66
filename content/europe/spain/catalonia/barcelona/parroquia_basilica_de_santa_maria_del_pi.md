Santa Maria del Pi — Saint Mary of the Pine, named for a pine tree that stood on the site — was built between 1322 and 1453 by the guilds and merchants of the Raval neighbourhood, who wanted their own parish church rather than having to trek to the cathedral in the Barri Gòtic. The result is one of the purest examples of Catalan Gothic: a single, wide nave with no transepts, side chapels tucked behind the main walls, and a vast uninterrupted interior space.

The rose window in the west facade has a diameter of 10.6 metres, making it the largest Gothic rose window in the world. It was destroyed during the Civil War in 1936 — the church was burned and ransacked in the anticlerical violence of the first weeks — and rebuilt from fragments and historical photographs after the war.

The single-nave plan was a deliberate Catalan preference over the French Gothic model with its forest of columns and side aisles. Catalan Gothic architects were interested in dematerialising the interior walls and creating a single unified space, partly for acoustics (the guilds held concerts and civic ceremonies here) and partly as a theological statement about the undivided Christian community.
