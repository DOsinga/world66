The apparitions at Lourdes in 1858, in which a fourteen-year-old girl named Bernadette Soubirous reported seeing the Virgin Mary eighteen times, transformed popular Catholicism across Europe and produced a wave of new churches, shrines, and pilgrimage routes dedicated to the Virgin of Lourdes.

In Barcelona, the Eixample expansion was just beginning in the 1860s and 1870s, and the new working-class and middle-class parishes that the expansion required were often named after newly popular Marian devotions. The Parròquia de la Mare de Déu de Lurdes was one of several Lourdes-dedicated churches built across Catalonia in the second half of the nineteenth century.

The church sits in a neighbourhood that was, when it was built, at the edge of the expanding city. The Eixample blocks around it were still being filled in, and the parish served a population of new arrivals who had moved from the old city into the new grid. Lourdes as a devotion was particularly popular with women migrants, for whom the image of an apparition to a poor, uneducated girl resonated.
