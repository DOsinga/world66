The rosary as a devotional practice was systematically promoted by the Dominican order from the fifteenth century, particularly in Catalonia, where Dominican convents organised Confraternities of the Rosary that spread the practice through the popular classes. The image of the Mare de Déu del Roser — Our Lady of the Rosary — became one of the most common Marian dedications in Catalan parishes.

This church in the Gràcia district carries that tradition into the modern city. Gràcia was an independent town until it was absorbed into Barcelona in 1897, with its own parish network; many of those parishes continued under their original dedications after incorporation.

The Dominicans' promotion of the rosary was partly about creating a portable, memorisable religious practice that didn't require literacy — the beads served as a mnemonic for the sequence of prayers. In a Catalonia where the majority of the population was illiterate until well into the nineteenth century, this made the rosary one of the principal forms of religious education for working people.
