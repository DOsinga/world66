Anthony of Padua (1195–1231) was a Portuguese-born Franciscan friar who became famous for his preaching and his reported ability to find lost things. His cult spread through the Franciscan network across the Mediterranean and took particularly deep root in Catalonia, where the tradition of asking Sant Antoni for help finding lost objects persists as an active popular practice.

The parish church in Horta-Guinardó serves one of the older outlying districts that were absorbed into Barcelona during the twentieth century. Horta was an independent municipality until 1904, and the parish infrastructure reflects that history — churches built to serve village communities that gradually became urban neighbourhoods.

The Franciscan connection to Catalan popular religion runs deep. The Franciscans, along with the Dominicans and Augustinians, provided much of the pastoral care for the urban poor from the medieval period onward, and their saints — Francis, Anthony, Clare — became anchors of neighbourhood identity in ways that have outlasted the convents themselves.
