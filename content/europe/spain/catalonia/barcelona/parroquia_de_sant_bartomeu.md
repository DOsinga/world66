Bartholomew the Apostle is patron saint of tanners because of the manner of his martyrdom — he was reportedly flayed alive, which linked him permanently to the leather trade. In a district like Sant Andreu, which was historically an industrial and working-class area with significant tanning and textile industries, the dedication carries a specific occupational resonance.

Sant Andreu de Palomar was an independent town until 1897, when it was annexed by Barcelona. Its parish network predates the annexation by centuries; the church of Sant Andreu, which still stands at the centre of the old town, was the main parish, with smaller chapels serving the surrounding agricultural and artisan communities.

The Parròquia de Sant Bartomeu serves one of those peripheral communities, now fully urban but still with traces of the village structure that preceded the industrial city: the scale of the streets, the persistence of local commerce, the square in front of the church that was once a market and is still a gathering place.
