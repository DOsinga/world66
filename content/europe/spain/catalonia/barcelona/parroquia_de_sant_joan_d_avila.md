Juan de Ávila (1499–1569) was a Spanish priest and mystic who spent most of his life in Andalucía, preaching to the poor and mentoring a generation of spiritual reformers that included Teresa of Ávila and John of God. He was beatified in 1894 but not canonised until 1970 — a long gap explained partly by the complexity of his theological legacy and the Inquisition's repeated scrutiny of his writings.

Parishes dedicated to recently canonised or beatified figures reflect the moment of their dedication: a church built or named in the 1970s would naturally look to figures canonised in that era. The parish serves a residential neighbourhood in one of Barcelona's expanding districts of the post-war period.

Juan de Ávila's declaration as a Doctor of the Church in 2012 — one of only 37 people ever given that title — has given the parish a saint of unexpected theological stature. Most church-goers in the neighbourhood know him primarily as a patron of the Spanish clergy rather than as a figure of international doctrinal importance.
