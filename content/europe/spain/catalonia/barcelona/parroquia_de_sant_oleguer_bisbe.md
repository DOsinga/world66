Oleguer Bonestruga was born in Barcelona in 1060 into a family of minor nobility, entered the Augustinian order, and rose to become Bishop of Barcelona in 1116 and Archbishop of Tarragona the following year. The double appointment was political as much as religious: the Archbishop of Tarragona held primacy over all the ecclesiastical territories of the Crown of Aragon, and Oleguer was chosen for his capacity to organise the Church's participation in the ongoing military expansion into Muslim-held territory.

His most significant achievement was the recovery of Tarragona itself, which had been abandoned since the Arab conquest of the eighth century. He recruited Norman and Genoese settlers, organised the military defence of the city, and reestablished the archbishopric in a city that was little more than ruins. He died in 1137 and was canonised in 1675.

The parish bearing his name is in a modern Barcelona neighbourhood, but the dedication is a reminder that the city's Catholic identity is rooted in a period when the Church and the military expansion of the Catalan-Aragonese state were inseparable projects.
