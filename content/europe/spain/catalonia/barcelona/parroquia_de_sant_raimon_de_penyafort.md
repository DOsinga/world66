Ramon de Penyafort (c.1175–1275) was a Catalan Dominican friar and one of the most influential canon lawyers in the history of the Catholic Church. At the request of Pope Gregory IX, he compiled and organised the *Decretales* — the definitive collection of papal rulings that governed Church law for centuries — a task that required synthesising five previous collections and imposing a systematic order on an enormous body of inconsistent material.

The convent of Montsió, where this parish now stands, was a Dominican house in the old city. The order's presence in Barcelona was central to the medieval Church's institutional life: the Dominicans ran the Inquisition, staffed the university, and provided much of the theological backbone of the Crown of Aragon's religious administration.

The original convent was suppressed in the 1835 disentailment, when the liberal government dissolved the religious orders and confiscated their properties. The buildings were demolished; the site was eventually absorbed into the Eixample. The parish carries the name as a reminder of a Dominican presence that shaped the city for six centuries.
