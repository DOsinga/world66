Thomas Aquinas (1225–1274) produced the *Summa Theologica*, one of the most ambitious intellectual projects in the history of Western thought: a systematic reconciliation of classical philosophy (primarily Aristotle, recently retranslated from Arabic into Latin) with Christian doctrine. The Church declared his work officially normative in 1879 under Leo XIII, making Thomism the required philosophical framework for all Catholic education.

Parishes dedicated to Aquinas are typically associated with university or educational communities — his patronage of scholars, universities, and students is his primary liturgical function. The neighbourhood this parish serves in Barcelona suggests an academic or middle-class residential area where the intellectual associations of the dedication would have carried weight with the founding community.

Aquinas is also, less famously, the patron saint of pencil-makers — a medieval attribution based on a misreading of a Latin text. The Dominican order, to which he belonged, was the order most associated with intellectual life in the medieval university city, and their presence in Barcelona dates from the thirteenth century.
