Dorothy of Caesarea was a Christian martyr executed in Cappadocia around 311 CE during the Diocletianic persecution. The most famous element of her legend is her exchange with a pagan lawyer named Theophilus who mocked her, asking her to send him fruits from the paradise she claimed to believe in. According to the account, an angel delivered a basket of apples and roses to Theophilus moments after Dorothy's execution, converting him on the spot.

The story made Dorothy the patron saint of brides, newlyweds, gardeners, and florists — the flowers and fruit of the paradise basket became her iconographic attribute. Small neighbourhood parishes dedicated to minor saints like Dorothy often reflect the choice of the founding community, the local clergy, or the benefactor who funded the building.

Les Corts was an independent municipality absorbed into Barcelona in 1897. Its parish infrastructure was built up over the following decades as the district urbanised. The dedication to Santa Dorotea gives this particular church its own distinct identity within a neighbourhood that is otherwise dominated by the presence of Camp Nou and the urban fabric of the mid-twentieth-century city.
