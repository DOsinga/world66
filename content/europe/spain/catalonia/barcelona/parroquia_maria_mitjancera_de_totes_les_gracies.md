The title *Maria Mitjancera de totes les Gràcies* — Mary Mediatrix of All Graces — expresses the theological claim that Mary mediates all divine grace between God and humanity. This idea has roots in medieval devotional theology but became a matter of formal doctrinal dispute in the twentieth century, when a significant movement pushed to have it defined as a dogma by the Catholic Church.

The Second Vatican Council (1962–1965) considered the proposal and declined to define it formally, folding the concept into a broader discussion of Mary's role without making a binding doctrinal statement. The question remains technically open, with periodic petitions to the Vatican asking for a formal definition.

Parishes bearing this title were typically founded in the interwar or immediate post-war period, when Marian devotion was at a high point in Catalan Catholicism and the movement for a formal dogma was active. The dedication reflects a particular moment in the internal debates of twentieth-century Catholicism, preserved in the name of a neighbourhood church long after those debates have moved on.
