Dominic of Guzmán founded his order in 1216 specifically to address the challenge posed by the Cathar heresy in southern France: an educated, organised alternative to Catholicism that conventional parish priests were ill-equipped to counter. The Dominicans — *Friars Preachers* — were trained theologians who could debate, preach, and persuade rather than simply administering the sacraments.

In Barcelona, the Dominicans established themselves early. Their first house was on the site of what is now the Barri Gòtic, and they remained a major institutional presence until the 1835 dissolution. The Inquisition was staffed primarily by Dominicans, giving the order a dual reputation: as the Church's most intellectually sophisticated defenders and as the agents of its most notorious coercive institution.

Parishes dedicated to Saint Dominic are typically in neighbourhoods with strong Dominican historical connections, or were founded in periods when the reestablishment of the order after the 1835 dissolution led to a revival of Dominican devotional culture in Catalan parishes.
