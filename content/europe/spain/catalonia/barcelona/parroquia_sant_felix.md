The name Felix appears so frequently in the early Christian martyrology — there are at least sixty saints named Felix in the Roman catalogue — that parish dedications to *Sant Feliu* or *Sant Felix* can refer to several different figures depending on the local tradition and the feast day observed. The most common in Catalonia is Felix of Girona, a fourth-century martyr executed during the Diocletianic persecution who became the patron saint of Girona and one of the primary saints of the Catalan church.

The proliferation of Felix martyrs reflects both the popularity of the name in the Roman world (Felix means happy, fortunate) and the broader pattern of early Christian communities attaching themselves to local martyrs as their distinctive patrons. Each village or neighbourhood wanted its own holy protector, and the early church was generous in recognising martyrs at the local level.

This parish serves a residential neighbourhood in one of Barcelona's twentieth-century expansion zones. The dedication to a common saint gives it a generic quality that the local community has filled with its own particular identity over decades of parish life.
