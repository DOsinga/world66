The Passatge Bacardí is a short colonnaded passage from the late 19th century, running between the Carrer de Ferran and the Carrer de la Unió in the Gothic Quarter. It was built by the Bacardí family — specifically by Facund Bacardí Massó, who was born in Sitges south of Barcelona in 1814 and emigrated to Cuba in 1830.

Facund Bacardí transformed the rum industry in Cuba. Before his innovations, rum was a rough spirit associated with sailors and the poor. Working in Santiago de Cuba, he developed a new distillation process using charcoal filtering that produced a lighter, cleaner spirit. He founded his company in 1862, using fruit bats as his symbol (the Bacardí warehouse in Santiago was full of them). The Bacardí brand became the world's best-selling spirit.

The passage in Barcelona was built with profits from the Cuban enterprise — a return on success, as was the custom of the indianos, the Catalan emigrants who made fortunes in the Americas and came home to build. The passage's construction was also a political statement of a kind: Facund Bacardí used his money to create a public thoroughfare in his home city, something more permanent than a private house. The Bacardí company was expelled from Cuba after the 1959 revolution; it moved its headquarters to Puerto Rico. The Barcelona passage remains, named for a man whose significance is remembered primarily through a brand that now has no connection to the city at all.
