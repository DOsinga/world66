The Passatge de la Concepció is one of several enclosed pedestrian passages tucked behind the street façades of the Eixample. It runs between Carrer d'Aragó and Carrer de Provença, squeezed into the interior of an Eixample block — the kind of private interior passage that developed in the 1860s and 1870s when the new expansion district was being built and private developers created residential lanes that were quieter and more intimate than the public streets.

In 1876, during the early construction of the Eixample, a Roman sarcophagus was discovered in the ground here. The discovery suggested that this area — well outside the Roman walls of Barcino — had been used as a burial ground in late antiquity, as Roman cemeteries typically lay along roads outside city limits. The sarcophagus was removed and is now in the Museu Arqueològic de Catalunya on Montjuïc.

Josep Puig i Cadafalch, the Modernista architect who designed the Palau del Baró de Quadras and the Casa de les Punxes, maintained his first professional architectural office in one of the buildings on this passage. The passage is now primarily residential, its ground-floor units taken over by small professional offices and studios. It is accessible from the street but quiet enough that it functions like a private enclave: cooler than the street in summer, shielded from the wind in winter, with the slightly suspended quality of space that urban interior courts often develop.
