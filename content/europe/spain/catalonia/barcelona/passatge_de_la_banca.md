Passatge de la Banca cuts through the block between Carrer de Casp and Carrer d'Ausias Marc, just off Passeig de Gràcia, in a stretch of the Eixample that was colonised by banking institutions from the 1880s onward. It is barely forty metres long and connects two parallel streets with no particular drama — a light-filled slot between office buildings of various eras — but the name has survived each successive renovation of the surrounding block.

Cerdà's Eixample plan included passages through interior blocks as a mechanism for pedestrian permeability. Most were never built; the commercial pressure to use every metre of block interior as lettable space meant that passages were the first amenity sacrificed when developers bought out the original plots. The ones that survived, like this one, did so usually because they were built into the original structure of a large single building rather than depending on cooperative construction across multiple owners.

The banking associations of the immediate area — the Borsa, several major Catalan bank headquarters, and the offices of the insurance companies that grew alongside them — are still visible in the ornate stone façades of the buildings on Carrer de Casp. The passage itself is an afterthought, but the name is an accurate archival record of what this corner of the city was in 1900.
