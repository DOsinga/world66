The Passatge de la Concepció is a short private passage running off the Carrer d'Aragó between the Passeig de Gràcia and the Carrer de Pau Claris. It was built in the 1860s as one of the first residential passages in the new Eixample — a semi-public street within a private development, lined with attached houses of a scale smaller than the apartment blocks fronting the main avenues.

The passage type was common in the early Eixample as a way of maximising the residential capacity of a block. Developers would build grand apartment buildings on the main street frontage and then fill the interior of the block with smaller houses accessible through a gate and passage. The smaller houses were cheaper than the main-street apartments and attracted a different social stratum — artisans, small shopkeepers, and lower-middle-class families rather than the bourgeoisie who inhabited the main avenue buildings.

The Passatge de la Concepció retains this character. Its houses are modest by comparison with the Modernista apartment buildings on the surrounding streets. The gate at the street entrance, which can be closed at night, preserves the semi-private character of the passage as a residential address rather than a public street. Walking into it from the Carrer d'Aragó produces an immediate shift in scale and character — from the Eixample's wide pavements and grand buildings to a narrow passage of small houses that belongs to a different economic register of the same neighbourhood.
