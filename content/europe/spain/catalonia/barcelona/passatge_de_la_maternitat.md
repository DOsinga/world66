Passatge de la Maternitat runs through the interior of an Eixample block in the Sant Antoni neighbourhood, between Carrer del Consell de Cent and Carrer de la Diputació. The name preserves a memory: a lying-in hospital, the Casa de Maternitat i Expòsits, operated on this block from 1853 until the institution was moved to a larger purpose-built complex in Les Corts in 1884. During those decades the building's interior courtyard functioned as the main entrance to the hospital, and the passage was the route by which staff and supplies moved between streets.

Ildefons Cerdà's original vision for the Eixample explicitly included social infrastructure — schools, hospitals, markets, gardens — within the interior blocks rather than on the street frontages. The Maternitat building was one of the few early cases where this vision was actually implemented, before commercial development pressure transformed most block interiors into enclosed private courtyards or, eventually, additional building floors. The passage survives because the property lines of the original hospital were never entirely consolidated by a single developer.

The current passage is mostly used by residents of the surrounding apartments. The paving retains an older pattern than the street outside, and the walls of the buildings on either side show the different construction dates layered over time: late nineteenth century brick, early twentieth century tile cladding, 1970s render.
