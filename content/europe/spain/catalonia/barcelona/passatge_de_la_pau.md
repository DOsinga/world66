The Passatge de la Pau (Passage of Peace) connects the Carrer de la Pau to the area near the Plaça del Duc de Medinaceli, cutting through the block at a point where, in the medieval city, a gate once stood in the first ring of city walls. The passage is unusual in this part of the Gothic Quarter: most of the area between the Rambla and the port was rebuilt extensively in the 18th and 19th centuries as the naval and commercial administration of the city expanded. This passatge survived because it served as a service corridor for the buildings on either side.

The walls of the passage retain some medieval stonework, visible where later plaster has been removed. The buildings on either side were part of the administrative complex associated with the Consolat de Mar — the maritime court that adjudicated disputes between merchants and ship owners from the 13th century onward. The Consolat de Mar's code of maritime law, drawn up in the 14th century, became the basis for Mediterranean commercial law across the region.

The passage leads to what was formerly a garden and is now a small piazza, with the neoclassical façade of the Capitania General (military headquarters) on one side. The military presence here dates to 1715, when the Bourbon forces established their administrative centre in Barcelona after the city's defeat. The buildings they built replaced several earlier structures; the Passatge de la Pau is what slipped through the cracks of that reconstruction.
