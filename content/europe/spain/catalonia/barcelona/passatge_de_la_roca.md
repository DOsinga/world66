The Passatge de la Roca in Gràcia is a small private passage — a cul-de-sac lined with two-storey houses — that dates from the mid-19th century, when Gràcia was still an independent municipality rapidly expanding its residential stock for the artisan and worker population. The houses are typically Catalan artisan: ground floor for a workshop or animal storage, upper floor for the family, with a small façade garden or terrace accessible from the first floor.

The passatge type was common in 19th-century Catalan cities: developers would buy a plot behind a main street, run a passage through the centre of it, and line both sides with small houses that sold or rented for less than the main-street frontage properties. The residents got cheaper housing; the developer got more units per plot. The passages created a two-speed urban fabric — a visible public front on the main streets and a more intimate private interior accessible only through a gate or passage.

Many Barcelona passatges have been demolished as urban renewal removed the smaller-scale fabric of the 19th-century city. The Passatge de la Roca survives because it is in Gràcia, where the smaller scale of the neighbourhood and the listing of several buildings have slowed demolition. Walking into it from the surrounding streets produces an immediate shift in scale: from the 5-6 storey apartment buildings of the main streets to the 2-storey artisan houses of the passage, with the narrowness and domesticity of a completely different urban scale.
