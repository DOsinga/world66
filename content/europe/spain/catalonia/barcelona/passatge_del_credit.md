The Passatge del Crèdit is a short covered passage running between the Carrer de Ferran and the Carrer de la Boqueria, a few steps from the Plaça de Sant Jaume. The name refers to the credit institutions — banks, lending houses, and financial agencies — that occupied it in the 19th century, as Barcelona's financial sector began to formalise along modern lines.

The passage is significant in Catalan cultural history as the birthplace of Josep Pla, who was born in 1897 at number 4. Pla became the most significant prose writer in Catalan in the 20th century: his works — travel writing, journalism, memoirs, and novels — total nearly 40,000 pages and constitute the largest single body of work in the Catalan language. His style is deceptively simple, precise, and deeply observational; he wrote about Catalonia, the Mediterranean, and European culture with a clarity that made him both popular and critically revered.

Pla was born in Palafrugell on the Costa Brava, and his family's connection to the Passatge del Crèdit was through business rather than permanent residence. But the plaque marking his birth in this passage has made it a literary landmark. The passage itself, with its 19th-century iron columns and modest shopfronts, is the kind of commercial interior that Pla himself described repeatedly in his writing about Barcelona — precise, functional, and entirely indifferent to its own significance.
