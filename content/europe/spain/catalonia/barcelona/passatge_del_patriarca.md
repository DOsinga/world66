The Passatge del Patriarca (Patriarch's Passage) in the Gothic Quarter takes its name from the title "Patriarca" applied to the 17th-century priest and healer Josep Oriol Boginyà (1650-1702), who became one of the most celebrated popular religious figures in Barcelona. Oriol was born in the city, studied theology at the University of Barcelona, and spent much of his adult life as the curate of the church of Santa Maria del Pi in the Gothic Quarter.

His popular fame derived from his reported miraculous healings. In the decade before his death, Oriol was visited by thousands of sick people from across Catalonia seeking cures; his touch was believed to restore sight, end fevers, and cure diseases that conventional medicine could not treat. The miracles were documented by witnesses and submitted to the church authorities for examination. He was beatified by Pope Pius VI in 1806 and canonised by Pius X in 1909.

The "Patriarca" title — an archaic honorific applied to particularly venerable local saints — attached itself to Oriol in the 18th century as his fame spread. The passage near the Rambla that bears his name was named in the 19th century, when the popular veneration of the Barcelona-born healer was at its height. The church of Santa Maria del Pi, where he served, contains his tomb; it remains a site of active veneration, with ex-voto offerings of crutches and other instruments of disability marking the wall near his remains.
