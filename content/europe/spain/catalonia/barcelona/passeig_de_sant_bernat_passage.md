The Passatge de Sant Bernat is a covered passage of perhaps 20 metres, connecting two streets in the Gothic Quarter. It runs between the Carrer dels Banys Nous (Old Baths Street) and the Carrer del Call (the street of the Jewish quarter), through a building that was constructed on or near the boundary of Barcelona's medieval Jewish community, the Call.

The Call was established in the 12th century and occupied a dense cluster of streets in the southwest quadrant of the old city. By the 14th century it was one of the most significant Jewish communities in the western Mediterranean, with a major synagogue, a cemetery outside the walls, and a community of merchants, physicians, and scholars who played significant roles in the economic and intellectual life of the Crown of Aragon.

The Passatge de Sant Bernat passes through the zone where the Call boundary was drawn. The passage itself is of uncertain date — the building around it may incorporate medieval fabric — but its function as a shortcut through a densely built block has been constant for centuries. Walking through it connects two streets whose names record the medieval Jewish presence (Banys Nous was where the Jewish community's baths were) with no indication, beyond the walls on either side, of the specific history of the ground beneath it.
