The Passeig de Sant Joan is one of the Eixample's three main north-south boulevards, less famous than the Passeig de Gràcia but arguably richer in surviving Modernista architecture. The boulevard runs from the Arc de Triomf to the Diagonal, and along its upper half contains several Modernista buildings by Josep Puig i Cadafalch — including the Casa Macaya (1901) and the earlier Casa Martí (1896, now better known as Els Quatre Gats café) — alongside works by other architects of the period.

Puig i Cadafalch chose the Passeig de Sant Joan as the address for several of his most ambitious commissions, possibly because the boulevard's prestige was slightly lower than the Passeig de Gràcia, making lots more affordable for clients of the upper professional class rather than the very rich. The result is a more diverse and in some ways more interesting Modernista streetscape: less concentrated and famous than the Manzana de la Discòrdia but more representative of the full range of what Catalan Modernisme produced.

The iron kiosk near the Arc de Triomf end of the boulevard is the oldest surviving example of the cast-iron news kiosk type in Barcelona, dating from the late 19th century. These kiosks — selling newspapers, magazines, and later lottery tickets — were standardised pieces of urban furniture that Barcelona installed throughout the Eixample as part of the boulevard planting programme. Most have been replaced; this one survives as an example of the late 19th-century street furniture that originally punctuated the boulevard.
