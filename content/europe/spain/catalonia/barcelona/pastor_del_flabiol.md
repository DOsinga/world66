The *flabiol* is a small end-blown flute traditionally played with one hand, leaving the other hand free to beat a small drum called a *tamborí*. The combination — flabiol and tamborí played by one person — is the acoustic signature of Catalan popular festivity: it accompanies the sardana, the *gegants*, the castellers, and the *diables* of the *correfoc*. Without it, those celebrations lose their characteristic sound.

The shepherd figure playing the flabiol is an archetype in Catalan pastoral and religious imagery, rooted in the Christmas traditions where shepherds figure prominently in the *pessebre* — the Catalan nativity scene that has evolved into one of the country's most elaborate popular art forms.

*Pastor del Flabiol* stands in one of the parks or squares of the Olympic waterfront district, placed there in 1992 as part of the programme to give the new public spaces some cultural anchoring. The shepherd figure in an urban setting is deliberately anachronistic — a reminder of the agrarian origins of a musical tradition that has survived total urbanisation.
