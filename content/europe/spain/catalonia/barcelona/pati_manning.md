The Hospital Militar de Barcelona was built between 1888 and 1897 on land that was then at the edge of the new Eixample grid. The Spanish army needed a hospital large enough to handle the casualties of the colonial wars in Cuba and the Philippines, which were at their most intense in the 1890s and ended with the Spanish-American War of 1898.

The complex followed the pavilion plan — separate buildings set in gardens, connected by walkways, with each pavilion dedicated to a different medical function. This model, which Domènech i Montaner was simultaneously developing at the Hospital de Sant Pau, was considered the most advanced approach to hospital design at the time, allowing ventilation, sunlight, and the separation of infectious patients.

The military hospital closed in the 1990s as the Spanish army's medical services were reorganised. The buildings were converted into a cultural centre for the Eixample district, retaining the original courtyard — the *pati* that gives the centre its name — as the main public space. The conversion preserved the pavilion structure while finding new uses for spaces that had served, in their time, as operating theatres, wards, and convalescent gardens.
