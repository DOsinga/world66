Joan Miró designed the mosaic for the Pla de l'Os — the section of La Rambla at the intersection with Carrer de la Boqueria — as a gift to the city where he was born. The design, a burst of primary colours in his characteristic biomorphic language, was meant to be walked over rather than looked at, part of the pavement rather than a monument raised above it.

Miró chose the location deliberately. La Rambla is the spine of Barcelona's public life; to place a work there, underfoot, was to make it part of the city's daily experience rather than its museum culture. Miró was consistently interested in public art that could not be avoided — not displayed in a gallery or placed on a plinth, but encountered as part of ordinary urban movement.

He died in Palma de Mallorca in December 1983. The mosaic was installed in 1986. It has been damaged and restored several times since — partly by the volume of foot traffic, partly by the improvisational street performances and market stalls that periodically cover it. FC Barcelona fans traditionally gather around it to celebrate victories, adding a layer of contemporary ritual to a work that was conceived as a contribution to the city's permanent fabric.
