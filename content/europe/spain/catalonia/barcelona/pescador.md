Roger de Llúria, the medieval admiral whose statue stands nearby, sailed these waters for glory — but the anonymous fisherman immortalised in *Pescador* spent his life hauling nets from them. Sculptor Francesc Carulla Rebull created this figure in the 1970s as an elegy to the fishing communities that had worked the beaches of Poblenou long before the factories arrived. The neighbourhood itself takes its name from the whitewashing pots (*poblet nou*, or 'new village') of those early settlers.

The statue occupies a small plaza near what was once the shoreline, now pushed back by the reclamation works that preceded the 1992 Olympics. Before those Games remade the coast, Poblenou's fishermen launched their boats from rough sand where parking lots and promenades now stand. The figure's downward gaze — fixed on a catch that no longer exists — captures the melancholy of a livelihood erased within a single generation.

Fishing rights in this stretch of coast were historically controlled by the *Confraria de Pescadors*, one of Barcelona's oldest guilds. The guild kept meticulous records of catches and weather going back centuries, and those archives, now held at the Maritime Museum, offer an astonishing portrait of the sea as it once was — rich with tuna, swordfish, and bluefin that today's fishermen can only name in stories.
