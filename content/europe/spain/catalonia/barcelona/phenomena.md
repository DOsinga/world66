The absence of a concession stand at Phenomena is not an oversight — it was a founding decision, part of the argument that genre film deserves the same conditions of attention as any other cinema. Dubbing was similarly excluded from the start. In a country where virtually all foreign films reach audiences in dubbed form, showing horror or science fiction in the original language with subtitles signals that the films are worth watching carefully, not just consuming.

The Phenomena Experience brand extends the cinema's scope beyond regular screenings: retrospectives of directors (Carpenter, Cronenberg, and Verhoeven have all received extended programmes), 4K restorations of films that rarely get theatrical revivals, and special events built around specific films or cycles. The venue at Carrer de Sant Francesc de Sales 36 in the Sarrià-Sant Gervasi district is small enough that these events sell out quickly; the mailing list is the main way audiences track what is coming.

Before Phenomena opened, genre cinema in Barcelona existed only through sporadic festival slots and occasional revivals. A permanent venue with consistent programming changed the terms: the films are now repertory items rather than curiosities, available on a schedule rather than by accident. Whether the cinema survives commercially on that argument is a question it has been answering, year by year, since 2013.
