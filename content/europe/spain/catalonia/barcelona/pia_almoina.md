The building facing the Cathedral's main apse — the Gothic structure on Avinguda de la Catedral — is the Casa de la Pia Almoina, the Pious Almshouse. Founded in the 11th century by the Bishop of Barcelona as a charitable institution to feed the poor, it operated as a soup kitchen, hospital, and refuge for the homeless for nearly seven hundred years. At its most active in the 14th century, the Pia Almoina distributed food to a hundred people every day, paid for by Cathedral revenues and private donations.

The current building dates mostly from the 15th century, with Gothic arches and a characteristic Catalan stone façade. It was rebuilt several times as the institution grew. In 1923 it was converted to other uses, and since 2002 it has housed the Museu Diocesà, the Diocesan Museum. The museum displays the cathedral treasury, Romanesque art, and changing exhibitions. Entry is charged.

The lower floors are where the building becomes remarkable. Excavations carried out during the museum conversion revealed Roman structures dating to the 1st century BCE directly beneath the medieval foundations: a section of the Roman forum, the colony's main public space. The museum has preserved these remains in situ, visible through glass floors and in basement galleries. You walk over the Roman forum to reach the Catalan altarpieces. The building layers cleanly: Roman colony, medieval charity, Baroque renovation, contemporary museum — each phase preserved rather than erased, stacked one on top of the other in the soil of the highest point of Barcino.
