Poblenou's *Rambla del Poblenou* was laid out in the 1980s and 1990s as the neighbourhood reinvented itself after decades as Barcelona's factory belt. The street's public art programme was deliberately eclectic — a mix of zodiac signs, abstract forms, and figurative references to local history — and *Piscis* falls neatly at the intersection of all three. The fish carries the weight of the zodiac tradition, but in a neighbourhood that once launched fishing boats from its beaches, the image lands with extra resonance.

The mosaic technique places the work in a long Barcelona tradition reaching back to the ceramic decorations of Modernisme and the tile work of the Palau de la Música. But where those precedents were usually placed high on buildings, out of easy reach, *Piscis* sits at street level, visible from the ground, inviting the casual glance of a passing shopper. That democratic positioning is deliberate: the Rambla del Poblenou was conceived as a neighbourhood street, not a tourist corridor, and its public art was meant for the people who live there.

Poblenou's transformation from industrial wasteland to creative district followed the 1992 Olympics, which reclaimed the adjacent waterfront. The neighbourhood is now home to design studios, tech companies, and restored factory buildings — and the mosaic fish presides over this changed landscape, reminding anyone who pauses that the sea was here first, long before the machines, and that the workers who fished it left their mark on the street.
