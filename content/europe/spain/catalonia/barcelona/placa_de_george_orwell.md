The square was renamed after George Orwell in 1996, a specific choice with a specific argument behind it. Orwell had arrived in Barcelona in December 1936, joined the POUM (Workers' Party of Marxist Unification) militia, and witnessed the May 1937 street fighting in which Soviet-backed Communists moved to suppress the POUM and the anarchist CNT. His account in *Homage to Catalonia* (1938) documented that suppression from the inside — and was, for decades, an inconvenient book for the mainstream left, which preferred a simpler narrative of the war. Naming a Raval square after Orwell was a way of putting that inconvenient history back on the map.

Orwell's Barcelona — the revolutionary city of early 1937 where, as he wrote, the working class was in the saddle — was concentrated in exactly this part of the city. The Rambla a few hundred metres east, the workers' militia headquarters, the cafés where POUM members debated strategy: these were the streets he walked before going to the Aragon front, where he was shot through the throat by a sniper in May 1937 and narrowly survived.

The square is a plain, slightly sunken open space with bars and outdoor tables around the edges. A large CCTV camera is mounted on a pole at its centre — a detail that locals find impossible to ignore given Orwell's best-known novel. Whether the camera was placed ironically or in ignorance is not recorded. The square is still routinely called Plaça del Trippy, a nickname that predates the renaming and has never fully given way to the official version.
