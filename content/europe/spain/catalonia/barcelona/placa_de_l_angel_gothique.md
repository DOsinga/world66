The Plaça de l'Àngel (Angel Square) marks the position of the principal gate in Barcelona's Roman and medieval city wall on the eastern side: the Porta Praetoria of the Roman city, and later the gate through which the road from the north and east — the Via Laietana, which then ran roughly where the modern Carrer de Jaume I now runs — entered the city.

The angel that gives the square its name was placed in a niche above the gate in the medieval period. According to one tradition, the city placed an image of the Archangel Michael above the gate to protect the entrance to the city; according to another, the specific angel commemorates a miraculous apparition reported at the gate in the early medieval period. The gate itself was demolished when the city wall was removed in the 19th century; the square occupies the approximate footprint of the gate and its flanking towers.

The modern Via Laietana — cut through the dense medieval fabric of the old city between 1907 and 1913 in a process that demolished thousands of buildings — runs from this square northward. The cut was a late expression of the Haussmann-inspired urban surgery that had been reshaping European cities for half a century. The Via Laietana demolished the most densely built medieval quarter of Barcelona to create a straight banking and commercial boulevard — an act of destruction that was controversial then and is considered a catastrophic loss now. The Plaça de l'Àngel marks the point where the medieval city's main eastern gate stood and where the 20th-century city-planning bull ran through the heart of the old town.
