The Plaça de la Boqueria is the slight widening in the Rambla at the point opposite the Boqueria market's main entrance — a traditional gathering point where the Rambla's width increases to accommodate the traffic generated by the market. The widening is not a planned square but an organic consequence of the market's magnetic pull on the surrounding pedestrian flow.

The pavement mosaic here is by Joan Miró — a circular design of approximately 4 metres in diameter, installed in 1976 as part of the democratic renewal of Barcelona's public space. The Miró mosaic is set into the pavement rather than displayed as sculpture, meaning that most people walk across it rather than looking at it. Miró chose this form deliberately: he wanted an artwork that was part of the city's floor rather than something elevated above it.

The spot where the mosaic is set was historically one of the Rambla's most active commercial points. The covered market entrance opposite, the pavement flower stalls, and the bars and cafes that surrounded the widening created a concentration of commercial activity that made this the most crowded point on the promenade for most of the 20th century. The mosaic was placed in the middle of this activity as an aesthetic claim on a fundamentally commercial space — art inserted into the pavement of a market street, to be walked on rather than displayed.
