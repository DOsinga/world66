The Plaça de la Font (Fountain Square) in Gràcia is one of several small neighbourhood squares in the village-within-the-city that Gràcia remains. The square takes its name from the fountain at its centre — an 19th-century stone and iron structure that served as the water collection point for the surrounding streets before domestic piping reached Gràcia in the late 19th century.

Communal fountains in neighbourhoods like Gràcia were not merely practical: they were social institutions. Women came daily to collect water, and the queue at the fountain was one of the primary sites of neighbourhood information exchange — births, deaths, marriages, illness, conflict. The social networks that were built and maintained at the fountain were the social networks of the neighbourhood.

When domestic water arrived in the apartments, the fountain's practical function ended but its social function persisted for some time. People continued to use the square as a gathering point, and the local bar that occupied the corner of the square (as local bars occupied the corners of Gràcia squares generally) inherited the social function of the fountain. The transition from fountain to bar as the social centre of the neighbourhood is a piece of urban social history that can be traced in almost every Gràcia square. The font remains, still with water flowing, as a heritage object rather than a utility.
