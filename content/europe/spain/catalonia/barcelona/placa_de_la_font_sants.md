The Plaça de la Creu Coberta (Covered Cross Square) in Sants takes its name from the covered stone cross that marked the boundary of the independent municipality of Sants and the crossing of two major roads. The covered cross — a cross placed under a stone canopy, a type common in Catalan towns — was a threshold marker: travellers crossing it from Barcelona were entering Sants jurisdiction, and those crossing it in the other direction were leaving.

Threshold crosses of this type served multiple functions: they marked municipal boundaries, provided a point for devotional acts before or after travel, and identified the approach to a settlement for travellers who might not otherwise know where they were. The creu coberta of Sants was at the junction of the road from Barcelona (now the Carrer de Sants) and the road from Lleida (now the Avinguda de Madrid) — two major routes that made this corner one of the most significant intersections in the approaches to Barcelona.

The original covered cross has been replaced or restored multiple times over the centuries. The current structure is a 19th-century version on the same site. The square around it was transformed in the 20th century by traffic infrastructure, and the covering that gave it its name has been periodically damaged and repaired. The intersection remains active — it is one of the busier traffic points in the Sants neighbourhood — but the cross itself, in its canopy, continues to mark the point that the medieval municipality of Sants defined as its entrance.
