The Plaça de la Independència (Independence Square) in Gràcia was named in the 19th century at a moment when "independence" referred to political liberalism in the Spanish context — specifically to the constitutional independence of the municipalities from central government control. The name was not specifically Catalan separatist; it reflected the liberal federalist tradition that had briefly flourished in the First Spanish Republic of 1873.

The square's name acquired different resonances in different periods. Under Franco, "independence" was a dangerous word in any political context; the square's name was changed. After the transition to democracy, the original name was restored. In the 21st century, as the Catalan independence movement grew in strength and visibility, a square called Plaça de la Independència in one of Barcelona's most politicised neighbourhoods acquired associations that the 19th-century liberals who named it could not have imagined.

The square itself is a modest Gràcia neighbourhood space — the kind of enclosed square with a bar on one side and apartment buildings on the others that characterises the neighbourhood. On September 11, Diada Nacional, it fills with flags. On ordinary days it is used by the residents of the surrounding streets for the same purposes all Gràcia squares serve. The name carries the weight of 150 years of different political meanings; the square itself is relatively indifferent to all of them.
