The Plaça de la Llana (Wool Square) was the wholesale market for raw wool in medieval Barcelona. Wool arrived from the sheep-farming areas of the Pyrenees and the Ebro valley, driven south by drovers along the seasonal migration routes. At the Plaça de la Llana, the bales were inspected by guild officials, weighed on the municipal scales, taxed, and distributed to the various wool-working trades.

The wool economy of medieval Barcelona was one of the most complex industrial systems of the pre-modern city. Raw wool went first to the carders, who cleaned and separated the fibres; then to the spinners, who made it into thread; then to the weavers, who produced cloth; then to the fullers, who thickened and strengthened it; then to the dyers, who coloured it; then to the shearers, who finished the surface. Each of these trades had its own guild, its own street, and its own regulatory structure. The Plaça de la Llana was the input node of this system.

The square now serves as a small neighbourhood piazza in the Sant Pere district, with a fountain and benches. The Sant Pere neighbourhood retains more of its medieval street pattern than the more heavily renovated Gothic Quarter, and the Plaça de la Llana sits at the intersection of several streets whose names — Basses de Sant Pere, Carrer dels Cotoners (cotton merchants), Carrer dels Telers (weavers) — record the textile economy of which the wool market was the beginning.
