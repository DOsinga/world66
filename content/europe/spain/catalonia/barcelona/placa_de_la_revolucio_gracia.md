The Plaça de la Revolució in Gràcia takes its name from the Revolució Gloriosa of 1868 — the military uprising and popular revolution that overthrew Queen Isabella II and initiated Spain's brief experiment with a democratic monarchy. The revolution brought a constitutional government, freedom of religion, universal male suffrage, and the abolition of slavery in Puerto Rico; it was the most radical political opening Spain had experienced since the Napoleonic period.

The revolution was ultimately unsuccessful: the constitutional monarchy period ended in 1874 with the Bourbon restoration. But the six years of the democratic experiment (1868-1874) produced a political culture — liberal, federalist, anti-clerical — that persisted in Spanish cities long after the political restoration. Naming squares and streets for the "Revolució" was one way this culture maintained itself in public space.

In Gràcia, which had a strong liberal and subsequently anarchist political tradition, the Plaça de la Revolució kept its name through the Restoration, through the dictatorship of Primo de Rivera (1923-1930), and even through parts of the Franco period, when the regime renamed streets and squares throughout Spain to eliminate the political landscape of the Republic. The name's survival suggests either that the Franco authorities overlooked it or that a 19th-century revolution was considered sufficiently distant and insufficiently threatening to require suppression. The square now hosts one of Gràcia's more pleasant café terraces.
