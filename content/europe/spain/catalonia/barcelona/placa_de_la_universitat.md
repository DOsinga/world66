The Plaça de la Universitat is named for the Universitat de Barcelona, whose main building faces it. The university was relocated to this site at the edge of the Eixample in the 1860s, occupying a building designed by Elies Rogent in a Romanesque revival style that was then fashionable for institutional buildings. The building is now one of the more distinguished 19th-century civic structures in Barcelona, though its historical significance for the university is as the location where Catalan academic culture developed through the Renaixença period.

The square in front of the university was the central hub of Barcelona's tram network from the late 19th century until the trams were abolished in 1971. Dozens of tram lines converged here; the noise, the metal rails, and the overhead cables defined the square's character for seventy years. When the trams were removed — a decision driven by the car industry and by the traffic engineers of the 1960s — the square was converted to a traffic roundabout. It has since been partially pedestrianised.

Student demonstrations have historically gathered at the Plaça de la Universitat before marching toward the city centre. The location makes it a natural assembly point: the university's prestige provides symbolic legitimacy, the square provides space, and the routes toward the Rambla and the Plaça de Catalunya are direct. During the Franco period, student demonstrations at the university were a significant form of political pressure; the police responses to them were often violent and widely covered in the foreign press.
