The Plaça de la Virreina in Gràcia takes its name from an unusual honorific: Viceregent's wife. The Virreina in question was Maria Francesca de Fivaller i de Bru, widow of Manuel d'Amat i de Junyent, who served as Viceroy of Peru from 1761 to 1776. Amat returned to Barcelona extraordinarily rich and began building the Palau de la Virreina on the Rambla — the neoclassical palace that now houses the city's photography and design exhibitions. He died in 1782 before it was complete. His widow inherited the fortune and finished the palace, hence becoming known as "La Virreina."

The Virreina also funded the construction of the church of Sant Joan de Gràcia, which stands at the edge of this square. The church served the growing village of Gràcia in the early 19th century and was rebuilt several times as the population expanded. What the Virreina herself had to do with the Gràcia neighbourhood — as opposed to the Rambla palace where she lived — is not entirely clear; the naming may simply reflect the practice of honouring the city's great benefactors wherever their money was visible.

The square today is one of Gràcia's most convivial: a terrace bar occupies its centre, and the afternoon light makes it one of the pleasantest places in the neighbourhood to sit. The church façade and the village-scale buildings around it create the impression of a small-town square that has, largely, survived the century of tourism that has transformed its surroundings.
