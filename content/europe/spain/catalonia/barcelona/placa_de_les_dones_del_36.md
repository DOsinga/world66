In the summer and autumn of 1936, Catalonia mobilised for the Republic on a scale unmatched elsewhere in Spain, and women were part of that mobilisation in a way that had no precedent. They joined the anarchist and POUM militias in significant numbers, some in frontline roles. They ran the committees that kept Barcelona supplied, staffed the hospitals, and managed the industries collectivised by the CNT. The square in Sant Antoni — a working-class barrio that sent many of those volunteers — takes their name without apology.

The erasure came from two directions. Francoism suppressed the entire Republican memory. But within the Republican and exile tradition, the women combatants also disappeared: too radical, too independent, too disruptive of the narrative of the war as a straightforward antifascist struggle led by men. It was only under democracy, and especially from the 1990s onward, that researchers and activists began recovering their names and stories. The square's dedication followed.

The name *les Dones del 36* — the Women of 36 — is deliberately collective rather than heroic. There is no single statue, no individual face. The square acknowledges a category of people who were present and then made absent, and it does so in a neighbourhood that still has the density and the memory to understand what that means.
