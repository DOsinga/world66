The summer of 1835 saw systematic arson attacks on religious buildings across Spain, triggered by a complex mix of anti-clerical sentiment, liberal politics, and economic resentment at the wealth of religious institutions. In Barcelona on the night of 25 July 1835 — the feast of Saint James — convents and monasteries across the city were attacked and burned. The Augustinian convent of Sant Agustí Vell, which had stood on this site since the 14th century, was destroyed.

The destruction of the convents had a transformative effect on Barcelona's urban geography. Religious institutions had occupied large areas of land within the city walls for centuries, using them as gardens, cloisters, and agricultural space while the population density around them increased dramatically. The fires of 1835 and the subsequent government confiscation of Church property in the Desamortització of 1836–1837 released enormous quantities of urban land into the property market.

The land where the Augustinian convent had stood became a small public square — one of the few open spaces in the densely built Sant Pere neighbourhood. The square retains the saint's name and a small fountain at its centre. It is used today primarily as a children's playground and dog-walking space: entirely ordinary neighbourhood functions that it has served for nearly two centuries. The Augustinians built their convent here in the 14th century; the residents who play and walk here have inherited the space that the church's destruction opened up.
