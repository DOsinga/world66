Plaça de Sant Cugat is one of the Barri Gòtic's several pocket-sized squares, so small that its name appears on few tourist maps. It sits between the cathedral quarter and the former Jewish Call, accessible via Carrer de Sant Sever on one side and Carrer dels Arcs on the other. The square takes its name from an oratory of Sant Cugat that stood here in the medieval period, serving the domestic staff of the cathedral chapter; the oratory was demolished in the eighteenth century and the space became an open square.

Below the current paving there is a medieval cistern, one of several that served the cathedral close. These underground water stores were critical infrastructure in a city whose surface wells were chronically contaminated; the chapter maintained its own supply separate from the public fountains. The cistern was sealed in the nineteenth century when the municipal water system reached the old town, and its location is now marked only by a subtle depression in the stone.

The square's main visible feature is the Roman wall fragment that emerges from the cathedral's south flank on one side — a section of the first-century defence that was incorporated into the medieval cathedral precinct and is now integrated into the basements of the buildings. Carrer dels Arcs (Street of the Arches) takes its name from the Roman aqueduct sections that ran along this alignment before the wall was built over them.
