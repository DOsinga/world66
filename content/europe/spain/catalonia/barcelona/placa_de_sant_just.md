Tucked behind the Ajuntament on a slope that was already built up when the Romans were here, Plaça de Sant Just i Pastor has been a local hub since at least the ninth century. The Church of Sants Just i Pastor is one of the oldest in the city, its origins traced to Visigothic times, though the current Gothic shell dates from the fourteenth century. The square itself is intimate and slightly sunken, edged by medieval mansions and often entirely empty except for pigeons.

The fountain in the corner is singular. Dating from 1367, it carries a carved coat of arms and a spout fashioned as a gargoyle-like face. By the fourteenth-century ordinances, residents of the raval behind the cathedral had the right to draw water here — a right that was fiercely guarded and frequently litigated. For centuries, local notaries held their outdoor offices in this square, drafting wills and property transfers under the shade of the church porch.

What makes the spot easy to miss is precisely its scale: no grand vista, no tourist marker at the entrance. The alley of Carrer del Lledoners leads in from one side and the narrow Carrer dels Gegants from another, names that recall the giants' tradition and the elder trees that once lined the approach. On Friday evenings the neighbourhood puts chairs out; the square becomes a sitting room.
