Plaça de Sant Pere opens in front of the church of Sant Pere de les Puelles (Saint Peter of the Little Girls), one of the oldest monastic foundations in Catalonia. The nunnery was established in 945 on the edge of the early medieval city, dedicated to noble daughters who took religious vows, and its church was among the most prominent in Barcelona before the construction of the cathedral. In 985 it was comprehensively sacked by Al-Mansur's armies, which also destroyed the early city — the rebuilding of the church and community immediately after is documented in the cartulary of the count's court.

The current church dates from the eleventh to the thirteenth centuries, with subsequent modifications, and is one of the few surviving Romanesque structures in central Barcelona. Its Greek-cross plan and octagonal crossing dome are visible from the square, though the building is often locked outside Mass times. Two ancient Greek marble columns, reused as doorposts, are set into the portal — spolia of unknown origin that have been there since at least the twelfth century.

The square itself is the functional centre of the Sant Pere neighbourhood: market stalls on weekday mornings, the neighbourhood association's banner on the wall, and the traffic from the textile wholesale warehouses that still operate in the surrounding blocks. The church sits slightly askew relative to the street grid, a reminder that the nunnery predates the medieval urban plan by several centuries.
