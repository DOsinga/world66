The Plaça de Santa Anna is reached through a gateway from the Carrer de Santa Anna, a short street connecting the top of the Rambla to the Gothic Quarter interior. Passing through the gate reveals a medieval cloister garden and the church of Santa Anna, one of the few surviving Romanesque churches in Barcelona's old city.

The church was built in the 12th and 13th centuries for a community of the Order of the Holy Sepulchre, a crusading military order established after the First Crusade. The Romanesque nave, with its round arches and plain stone walls, is the oldest intact element; the Gothic cloister was added in the 14th and 15th centuries. The combination — a Romanesque church with a Gothic cloister, hidden behind a street just 50 metres from the tourist crowds of the Rambla — is one of the Gothic Quarter's most effective contrasts of scale and atmosphere.

The cloister garden is planted with orange trees and a central palm, and is significantly quieter than the surrounding streets. The church remains an active parish, which means it is closed during services but accessible at other times. The Order of the Holy Sepulchre connection means the church has custody of relics brought from the Holy Land by the crusaders — a connection to the medieval Christian understanding of the Mediterranean as a contested territory that is not otherwise visible in the secular post-industrial city above ground.
