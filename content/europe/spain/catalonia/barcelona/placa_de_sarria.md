The church of Sant Vicenç de Sarrià stands at the centre of Sarrià's village square, on a foundation that dates to the 10th century. The current building is a Baroque structure with later modifications, but the site has been a Christian place of worship since well before Barcelona's medieval expansion reached this far into the foothills of the Collserola.

The square around the church served as the municipal square of independent Sarrià: market days, official announcements, and festival events took place here. The pavement in front of the church was the measuring point from which distances to the village were calculated in the era before standardised road numbering. The surviving village morphology — a square with a church, a main street running from it, side streets following the slope of the hill — is more complete in Sarrià than in any other annexed Barcelona municipality.

The Barcelona poet and novelist J.V. Foix, one of the major figures of 20th-century Catalan literature, lived on the Plaça de Sarrià for most of his life. His family ran a pastry shop on the square. Foix stayed in Sarrià through the Franco period, when Catalan publication was suppressed, and wrote much of his work in the neighbourhood he was born in. A commemorative plaque marks his family's shop. The square remains the social centre of what is still, despite a century of formal incorporation into Barcelona, a distinct village.
