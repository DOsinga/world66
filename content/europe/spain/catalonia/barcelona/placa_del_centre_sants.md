The Plaça del Centre (Centre Square) in Sants was the municipal square of the independent town of Sants before its annexation by Barcelona in 1897. Like Gràcia and Sant Andreu, Sants had its own town hall, its own mayor, and its own civic identity as a working-class municipality defined by its factories rather than by the bourgeois culture of the expanding city to its east.

Sants was the centre of Barcelona's textile industry in the 19th century. The factories that surrounded the Plaça del Centre produced cloth, thread, and finished garments that were exported across Spain and to the colonies. The workers who lived in the densely built residential streets around the factories formed one of the largest concentrations of industrial labour in Spain, and the political culture that developed among them — anarchist, syndicalist, strongly anti-clerical — shaped Barcelona's labour movement for a generation.

The annexation of 1897 ended Sants's formal independence but not its distinct identity. The neighbourhood continues to identify itself as Sants rather than as simply a part of Barcelona; the Festa Major de Sants, held in late August, is one of the most elaborate neighbourhood festivals in the city, with streets decorated by neighbourhood associations in competition. The Plaça del Centre is still its focal point — still the square where the town hall stood, still the gathering point for the festival that the independent municipality established long before Barcelona arrived.
