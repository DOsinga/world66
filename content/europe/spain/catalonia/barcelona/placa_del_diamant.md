There is nothing visually remarkable about this small square in the heart of the Gràcia neighbourhood: a few trees, a fountain, apartment buildings on all sides. Its fame is entirely literary. In 1962, Mercè Rodoreda published La plaça del Diamant, a novel narrated by Natàlia — renamed Colometa (Little Dove) by her first husband — who meets that husband at a dance here, lives through the Civil War and its aftermath in this neighbourhood, and ends the book screaming in this square as an act of release from everything she has endured.

The novel was written in exile in Geneva, where Rodoreda had fled after the Republican defeat. It was published in Mexico by exiled Catalan publishers; inside Spain, where Catalan publishing was suppressed, it circulated clandestinely. When the Franco regime began to relax literary censorship in the late 1960s, it became the first major novel in Catalan to reach a mass readership in a generation. It has since been translated into more than thirty languages.

A bronze statue of Colometa stands in the square today, installed in 1982 to mark the twentieth anniversary of the novel's publication. The sculptor is Josep Maria Subirachs, who would later become controversial for his work on the Sagrada Família's Passion façade. The Rodoreda connection has turned what would otherwise be a neighbourhood square into a place of literary pilgrimage — particularly for Catalan readers for whom the novel represents the recovery of a suppressed culture.
