The Plaça del Doctor Andreu sits at the point where the blue Tramvia Blau (Blue Tram) ends and the Tibidabo funicular begins. The square is named for Andreu Martí Palau, a pharmacist and municipal health official who developed the Tibidabo area at the turn of the 20th century and is credited with popularising the idea of the mountain as a recreational destination for Barcelona's residents.

The bar at the square — the Mirablau — opened in 1920 and has served as a viewpoint and refreshment stop for everyone ascending or descending the Tibidabo mountain ever since. Its terrace looks directly over Barcelona from a height of roughly 270 metres above sea level, providing a panoramic view across the entire city to the sea. On clear winter days the Mallorca island chain is visible on the horizon.

The Tibidabo area was developed as a leisure resort in the early 20th century deliberately to take advantage of the cool air and open views that were absent in the dense, increasingly polluted city below. The funicular railway that connects this square to the Tibidabo summit opened in 1901; the amusement park at the top opened the same year. The Tramvia Blau — the historic blue tram that runs along the Avinguda del Tibidabo from the Plaça del Kennedy — is one of the few surviving examples of original tram infrastructure in the city, having been preserved as a tourist and heritage service after the rest of Barcelona's tram network was dismantled in 1971.
