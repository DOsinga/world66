The Plaça del Nord (North Square) in Gràcia is one of the neighbourhood's smaller squares — not as famous as the Plaça del Sol or the Plaça de la Virreina, but typical of the neighbourhood's character. It serves as the daily gathering point for the immediate surrounding residents: the bars around it open early for coffee, the benches fill on weekend mornings, and the square's fountain marks a gathering point that pre-dates the domestic water supply.

Gràcia's squares are often described as the key to understanding the neighbourhood, and the Plaça del Nord illustrates why. The square is not a tourist destination or a design statement; it is a functional public space at the scale of the surrounding housing. The buildings around it are four and five storeys — typical Gràcia residential buildings of the late 19th and early 20th centuries — and the square's dimensions are calibrated to the block structure rather than to any civic ambition.

The neighbourhood's traditional summer festival — the Festa Major de Gràcia, held in August — decorates all of the neighbourhood's squares elaborately, with each square's residents competing for the best decorative scheme. The Plaça del Nord has participated in this festival since the mid-20th century. The festival's square-by-square organisation reflects Gràcia's identity as a collection of smaller communities within a neighbourhood, each with its own focal point — a functional model of urban public life that is rare in cities where squares are designed for tourism rather than residents.
