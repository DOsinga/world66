The Plaça del Pedró takes its name from a pedró — a stone column or cross erected as a landmark, waymarker, or jurisdictional boundary. The pedró that gave the square its name marked the edge of Barcelona's medieval city: the point where the streets of the intramural city met the extramural zone that would become the Raval. Travellers entering from the north and west encountered the pedró as the first fixed point of the city; those leaving the city passed it as the last.

The square has been the site of the Santa Eulàlia Cross since the 18th century, when the medieval pedró was replaced with a more elaborate monument dedicated to Barcelona's first patron saint. The cross stands at the centre of the square as a relic of the devotional landscape of the pre-modern city, in which sacred markers were placed at threshold points — gates, intersections, bridges — to sanctify movement and to define the Christian territory of the city.

The Plaça del Pedró was substantially rebuilt in the 1980s as part of the urban renovation of the Raval. The square's current form is a 20th-century piazza rather than a medieval thoroughfare; the cross was preserved as a heritage element within the new design. The surrounding neighbourhood — cheap restaurants, internet cafes serving migrant communities, a market selling household goods at discount prices — is one of the more economically mixed parts of the Raval, maintaining a character closer to the neighbourhood's pre-gentrification identity than many of the adjacent streets.
