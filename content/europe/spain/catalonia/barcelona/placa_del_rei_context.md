The Plaça del Rei (King's Square) is enclosed on three sides by medieval buildings of exceptional quality: the Saló del Tinell (the reception hall of the Counts of Barcelona), the Capella de Santa Àgata, and the Palau del Lloctinent (Viceroy's Palace). The fourth side opens onto the Carrer dels Comtes. The ensemble represents the royal complex of the medieval city of Barcelona in its most coherent surviving form — a set of buildings that functioned together as the seat of the Crown of Aragon.

The square's most famous historical moment is disputed: it is claimed, though not definitively established, that Ferdinand and Isabella received Columbus here on his return from the Americas in April 1493, and that the six Indigenous Taíno people he had brought back with him as evidence of his discovery were presented to the court in this space. The Saló del Tinell, with its seven great arches spanning 33 metres, would have been the setting. If the scene happened, it was one of the defining moments in the creation of the modern world: the first encounter between European monarchy and the peoples of the Americas, staged in a 14th-century throne room.

The square sits above a Roman layer of the city: the Museu d'Història de Barcelona beneath it allows visitors to walk through 4,000 square metres of excavated Roman streets and structures. Barcelona's layers are everywhere compressed, but nowhere more so than here.
