Plaça del Sol is the social centre of the Vila de Gràcia neighbourhood, a wide, slightly irregular square that faces southeast — toward the sun, as the name indicates. The orientation was deliberate: when the square was laid out in the 19th century, when Gràcia was still an independent town outside Barcelona, the design prioritized winter sunlight in a square intended as a daily gathering place. The bars and restaurants that now line its edges are the contemporary version of that programme.

In 1987 and 1988, Plaça del Sol became the site of one of the most significant neighbourhood mobilizations in post-Franco Barcelona. The city council had approved a plan to build an underground car park beneath the square, arguing that the Gràcia district needed more parking. The local residents organized in opposition, arguing that the construction would destroy the square's character and benefit car-owning outsiders at the expense of the pedestrians who actually used the space. The campaign became a model for neighbourhood participation in urban planning: demonstrations, occupations, alternative design proposals. The car park was not built.

The square now has no underground infrastructure of any kind, which is part of why it remains pleasantly undisturbed. On warm evenings, every table is full from around 7pm and the square functions as a single enormous terrace. The 1987-88 fight over it is sometimes cited by urban planners as an early European example of successful citizen resistance to car-centric redevelopment. The residents of Gràcia won, and the sun still falls on the square most of the year.
