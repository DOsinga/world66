The Plaça dels Àngels (Angels Square) in the Raval was created when the Museum of Contemporary Art of Barcelona (MACBA) was built, opening in 1995 to designs by Richard Meier. The museum's white rationalist exterior faces a large paved plaza — the kind of open urban space that was rare in the dense Raval neighbourhood.

Within a year of the museum's opening, the plaza had been colonised by skateboarders, attracted by its smooth marble paving, its steps, and its long uninterrupted surfaces. By 2000, the MACBA steps had become one of the most celebrated skateboarding spots in Europe, appearing in countless skate videos and establishing Barcelona as a destination for skateboarding tourists.

The museum's administration, the city, and the skateboarders have been in intermittent conflict ever since. The marble steps were modified with metal inserts (called "skate stoppers") to prevent grinding; the skateboarders moved to other surfaces. The stoppers were eventually removed after significant public debate; the skateboarders returned. The cycle has repeated several times. The plaza is now often cited as a case study in the unintended uses of designed public space, and in the difficulty of controlling what happens in an open urban plaza when what happens is cultural rather than commercial or illegal. Richard Meier's design produced a space that serves purposes he did not intend, in a neighbourhood his museum was supposed to revitalise.
