Sant Andreu de Palomar was an independent municipality of considerable age — the church of Sant Andreu is documented from the 10th century — until it was annexed by Barcelona in 1897 along with several other surrounding towns. The village had been growing rapidly as Barcelona's industrial expansion pushed workers northward, but the original medieval core of the settlement survived the demographic pressure.

The Plaça d'Orfila, with the church of Sant Andreu at one end, preserves the character of the old village square. The church — rebuilt in the 19th century in a neoclassical style — stands on a foundation that has been a place of Christian worship since the medieval period. The square around it, with its market stalls and neighbourhood cafes, functions as a genuine local centre rather than a heritage display.

Sant Andreu was a working-class neighbourhood defined by its factories and railway workshops. The Maquinista Terrestre i Marítima, the large engineering works, had a significant plant in Sant Andreu. The neighbourhood's political traditions were shaped by the labour movement — the CNT trade union was strong here, and the 1917 general strike had significant support in the factories of Sant Andreu. The Plaça d'Orfila sits at the intersection of these layers: medieval church, village square, and working-class neighbourhood identity that persisted through a century of industrial Barcelona.
