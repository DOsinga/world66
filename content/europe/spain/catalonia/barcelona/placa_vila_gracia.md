The square with the tall clock tower is Gràcia's historic civic centre — the Ajuntament (town hall) that faces it was the seat of municipal government when Gràcia was an independent town. Gràcia was not incorporated into Barcelona until 1897, and throughout the 19th century it had its own political identity that was markedly different from the more conservative city it adjoined. The town was a stronghold of radical republicanism, federalism, and early labour organizing; its population of textile workers and artisans made it one of the most politically active communities in Catalonia.

The clock tower that dominates the square was built in 1862, six years after a major uprising that began here. In the summer of 1856, the conscription lottery — the quintas, which drafted poor men for military service while allowing the wealthy to buy exemptions — provoked a revolt that spread from Gràcia throughout Barcelona. The square was where the initial protests concentrated. The uprising was suppressed, but it established Gràcia's reputation as a place where working-class political organizing could find concentrated expression.

The neighbourhood has changed socially since the 1890s, but the Festa Major de Gràcia — held every August — retains the civic pride that the independent municipality expressed. The streets around the square are decorated with elaborate hand-made installations for the festival, transforming each block into a competition entry for the most original themed decoration. The clock tower still strikes the hours as it has since 1862.
