The Bourbon military took control of Montjuïc after the siege of Barcelona in 1714, which ended the War of the Spanish Succession and the city's resistance to Philip V. The fortress they built and expanded over the following decades was presented as coastal defence, but its orientation told a different story: the main artillery emplacements faced down into the city, not out toward the sea. Montjuïc was as much an instrument of occupation as of protection, and the gunpowder stored in this 18th-century magazine supplied the guns that periodically bombarded Barcelona when the city revolted against Madrid — in 1842, in 1843, and at other moments of 19th-century political crisis.

The polvorí (powder store) is a solid, functional military structure, built for security rather than display: thick walls, minimal openings, sited where a stray spark would cause the least catastrophic collateral damage. It is a working building from a military complex that has a longer and darker history than most visitors to the Parc de Montjuïc appreciate. The fortress above was also the prison where Catalan president Lluís Companys was held before his execution in 1940, and where anarchist leader Francisco Ferrer i Guàrdia was shot in 1909.

The powder store now sits within the Jardins de Laribal, a terraced garden of fountains and pergolas built in the early 20th century on the upper slopes of the hill. The transition from military magazine to ornamental garden is complete enough that most visitors walk past without registering what the structure was for. The walls that stored the propellant for Barcelona's bombardment now provide a backdrop for roses.
