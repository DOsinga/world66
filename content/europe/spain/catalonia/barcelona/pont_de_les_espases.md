The railway that arrived in Barcelona in 1848 — the first line in Spain, connecting Barcelona to Mataró — had to cross the city's northern edge. As the network expanded, the main railway corridor ran along what is now the Carrer d'Aragó, cutting through the territory that would become the Eixample and creating a physical barrier between the old city and the new expansion zone.

The Pont de les Espases (Bridge of the Swords) was one of the bridges built to cross this railway cut. The name's origin is disputed: one explanation is that the bridge is near the Carrer dels Espasers, the sword-makers' street; another is that the bridge's ironwork had a sword-like decorative element. The bridge's ironwork design, typical of late 19th-century engineering aesthetics, treated structural elements as opportunities for ornament.

The railway cut was eventually covered and the Avinguda del Paral·lel extended in the mid-20th century, eliminating the need for many of the crossing bridges. The Pont de les Espases survived by becoming a pedestrian connection in an area where the street grid on either side did not align perfectly — a common consequence of the boundary between the medieval street pattern and the Eixample grid. Such bridges are the joints where two incompatible urban geometries meet.
