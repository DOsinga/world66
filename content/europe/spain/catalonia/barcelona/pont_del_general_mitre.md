The Viaducte del Vallcarca crosses the Torrent de les Monges ravine in the upper part of the city, at the point where the Gràcia neighbourhood meets the Vallcarca district. The viaduct was built in 1925 to carry the tram line connecting the Plaça de Catalunya to Tibidabo — the mountain-top amusement park that had opened in 1901 and required improved transport links to grow its visitor numbers.

The tram line that ran over the viaduct was part of a network built specifically for leisure rather than commuting. Tibidabo was conceived as a recreational destination for the middle classes of Barcelona, and the transport infrastructure — the Tramvia Blau, the funicular, and this viaduct — was built as part of the resort's development. The viaduct's concrete structure was designed for the tram load but included pedestrian walkways on either side.

When Barcelona's tram network was dismantled in 1971, the line across the viaduct closed. The structure became a pedestrian bridge, connecting the upper Gràcia streets to the Vallcarca neighbourhood and providing an elevated view over a ravine that is one of the less-known topographic features of the upper city. The ravine itself — now largely built over — was the natural boundary between Gràcia and the hillside settlements above it.
