The Pont del Treball Digne (Bridge of Dignified Work) crosses the Gran Via de les Corts Catalanes at the Rambla del Poblenou, providing a pedestrian connection between the Poblenou neighbourhood and the Glòries area. The bridge was designed by Robert Brufau and opened in 2011. Its name — Dignified Work — references the labour history of the neighbourhood it serves.

Poblenou was the industrial heart of Barcelona from the 1840s to the 1960s. The factories that lined its streets employed tens of thousands of workers who organised into trade unions, supported anarchist and socialist movements, and fought some of the defining battles of 20th-century Spanish labour history. The 1919 La Canadenca strike — which won the eight-hour day — began in a Poblenou electricity plant. The 1936 revolutionary uprising that briefly made Barcelona the capital of anarchist experimentation was centred in Poblenou.

The bridge's name acknowledges this history in the present tense. "Dignified work" is not nostalgia for factory labour but a claim about what current workers deserve — a reference to the precarious employment conditions that characterise the knowledge economy that has replaced manufacturing in the neighbourhood. Naming a pedestrian bridge "Dignified Work" in the middle of the 22@ tech district is a political statement, placed where the commuters who work in the converted factories will walk over it daily.
