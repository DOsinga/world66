The Porta de la Pau (Gate of Peace) is now a traffic roundabout at the bottom of the Rambla, next to the Columbus Monument. In the medieval and early modern city, it was the principal gateway between the port and the urban fabric — the point where sailors coming off ships entered the city and where merchants going down to the docks left it.

The gate was part of the medieval sea wall that ran along the edge of the port. Barcelona's waterfront in the pre-modern period was not a continuous open shore: it was a walled edge, with gates at specific points controlling access between the commercial port and the city. The Porta de la Pau was the principal gate, named for the peace (pau) that was supposed to characterise commercial exchange — arrivals at the port were coming in peace, as merchants rather than as warriors.

The gate was demolished in the 19th century as the port expanded and the old sea wall became irrelevant to the new urban geography. The Columbus Monument was built on the approximate site of the gate for the 1888 Exposition. The roundabout now occupies the space. What was the most significant point of arrival in medieval Barcelona — the threshold through which the entire Atlantic and Mediterranean trade passed — is now a place where pedestrians wait for the lights to change.
