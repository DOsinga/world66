The gate now standing at the entrance to the Universitat de Barcelona's Faculty of Pharmacy on the Avinguda de Joan XXIII was originally the main entrance gate to the Güell industrial colony at Santa Coloma de Cervelló — the workers' settlement near Barcelona that Eusebi Güell established for his textile factory employees in the 1890s. Gaudí designed the gate, completed around 1905, with the same organic vocabulary he was developing at Park Güell.

The gate's most striking element is the dragon: a curved iron construction of remarkable precision, showing a serpentine dragon with spread wings. The dragon appears to have been interrupted mid-stride by being fixed to the gate structure. It is the direct forerunner of the dragon sculpture that guards the staircase at Park Güell — the same form, the same technique, executed slightly earlier and originally intended for an industrial rather than a leisure context.

The gate's current location is a consequence of property development. When the Güell colony property was redeveloped in the mid-20th century, the gate was dismantled and re-erected at the Faculty of Pharmacy, where it now serves as an entrance ornament entirely disconnected from its original function. The dragon stands guard over a pharmaceutical school in Les Corts — a slightly absurd deployment of one of Gaudí's most remarkable metalwork pieces. The Park Güell dragon (the more famous one) is the familiar version; this one is the less-visited precursor.
