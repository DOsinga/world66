The Portal Miralles at Passeig de Manuel Girona 55-57 is the entrance gate and boundary wall that Antoni Gaudí designed in 1902 for the property of Hermenegild Miralles Anglès. The main house the gate served was demolished long ago; the gate and wall are what survive, now attached to a later apartment block that replaced the original building.

Gaudí's approach to gates and boundary walls was characteristically ecological: rather than imposing a formal geometric structure, the wall follows the terrain and incorporates organic curves that echo the natural landscape. The gate pillars are faced in irregular stone, the wall coping rises and falls like a wave, and the ironwork of the gate itself incorporates botanical forms. The combination is similar to the boundary walls of Park Güell, designed in the same decade.

The survival of the gate alone — without the house it served — creates an architectural object that Gaudí did not intend to be freestanding. Gates are transitions: they mark the boundary between public and private, street and garden, outside and inside. Without the property it opened onto, the Portal Miralles is a threshold to nothing. But the object itself is beautiful and unusual, and its survival without context is itself revealing: the gate outlasted the house, the boundary outlasted the garden, the ornament outlasted the function it ornamented.
