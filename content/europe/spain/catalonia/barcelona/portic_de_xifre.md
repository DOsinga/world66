The Casa de Xifré, or Pòrtic de Xifré, occupies the block between the Carrer dels Canvis Nous and the Passeig d'Isabel II, directly opposite the port. It was built between 1836 and 1840 by Josep Xifré Casas, a Catalan emigrant who had spent thirty years in Cuba and the United States accumulating a fortune in commerce. When he returned to Barcelona, he commissioned the largest private construction project the city had seen for a generation.

The building is in the neoclassical style fashionable in the 1830s: a long arcade at street level with upper floors above, giving the block a continuous covered pedestrian walkway along its full length. This arcade function was the principal innovation: it was modelled on the Rue de Rivoli arcades in Paris and made the block a promenading destination as well as a residential address. The sea-themed ornamentation on the first-floor arches — tridents, anchors, sea-gods — refers both to Xifré's maritime fortune and to the location overlooking the port.

The building was immediately prestigious; it became the address of the city's most fashionable restaurant, the Set Portes (Seven Doors), which opened in 1836 and still operates on the same site — making it among the oldest continuously operating restaurants in Spain. Xifré had created not just a building but a social institution. The fact that his fortune derived from Cuban colonial commerce — including, very probably, involvement in the slave trade that sustained the Cuban economy — is not mentioned in any of the building's plaques.
