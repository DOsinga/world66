Ramon Berenguer III (1082-1131) was one of the most consequential Counts of Barcelona: he united the County of Provence with Catalonia through marriage, established Catalan maritime forces in the western Mediterranean, and negotiated the alliance with Pisa that would eventually give Catalonia its Mediterranean empire. The equestrian statue in the square that bears his name — cast in bronze in 1951, designed by the sculptor Josep Llimona — shows him in armour on horseback in the medieval style.

The statue's setting is exceptional: it stands in the small square created by the surviving sections of the Roman and medieval walls that run along the back of the Cathedral quarter. The walls behind the statue are 3rd-century Roman on the lower courses and medieval on the upper; the count on horseback in front of them is a medieval figure in a medieval landscape, with the Roman underlayer visible just behind him.

The juxtaposition is not accidental. The count who built Catalan naval power was working with and against the Roman infrastructural inheritance that 12th-century Barcelona still inhabited — the walls, the road network, the urban form. The decision to place his monument at this specific point, where the Roman and medieval are most clearly layered, was made with the landscape in mind. The 1951 date — under Franco, when Catalan history was suppressed — makes the monument's existence more complex; it was permitted because medieval Aragonese counts were not considered politically threatening.
