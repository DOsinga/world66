The building on Carrer dels Almogàvers was a manufacturing warehouse for railway components — part of the industrial infrastructure that made Poblenou the productive core of Barcelona's 19th-century economy. When the industrial economy vacated the neighbourhood, it left behind a stock of large, high-ceilinged, structurally sound buildings that had no obvious peacetime use. The 22@ urban plan, launched in 2000, designated Poblenou for conversion into a technology and creative industries district, which brought investment, startups, and cultural venues into the old factory shells.

Razzmatazz opened that same year in this warehouse, running five separate rooms — The Razz Club, Lolita, The Loft, Pop Bar, and Rex Room — each with its own door from the street and its own programming. On a weekend night the building simultaneously hosts electronic music, indie, mainstream pop, and experimental sounds, with separate crowds who may never encounter each other. The model is unusual: most large venues consolidate audiences into one room to maximise capacity; Razzmatazz fragments them deliberately, which means the programming can be genuinely diverse rather than averaged toward a single demographic.

The venue has hosted a significant roster of acts at various stages of their careers — the Arctic Monkeys, Franz Ferdinand, Daft Punk, and many others played here before or during their commercial peaks. Its location in Poblenou, surrounded by architecture firms, tech companies, and converted lofts rather than by the old entertainment districts of the Raval or the Paral·lel, is part of its identity: a club that belongs to a neighbourhood defined by transformation rather than by tradition.
