Josep Clarà was the great sculptor of Catalan Noucentisme, the cultural movement that rejected the exuberant organic forms of Modernisme in favour of classical Mediterranean restraint. *Repòs* (Rest) is his most iconic work — a woman lying at ease, her body simplified into pure curves, the whole composition breathing a Mediterranean stillness that the Noucentistes believed was intrinsic to Catalan culture and identity.

The work has had an unusual afterlife for a cemetery sculpture. Reproduced in postcards, posters, and art history textbooks, the recumbent figure became shorthand for a certain idea of Catalonia: classical, civilised, European, distinct from Castilian Spain. During the Franco era, when Catalan language and culture were suppressed, works like *Repòs* carried an extra political charge simply by existing — evidence that Catalonia had its own aesthetic tradition, its own relationship with antiquity, its own claim to cultural dignity.

The Montjuïc cemetery where *Repòs* is installed is itself worth the visit: a hillside necropolis with extraordinary views over the harbour, layered with monuments to Barcelona's bourgeoisie, its artists, its politicians, and its forgotten poor. Clarà himself is buried there. The original *Repòs* has company in the form of dozens of other important sculptures, making the cemetery one of the largest open-air collections of Catalan art in existence — almost entirely unvisited by tourists.
