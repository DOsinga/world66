The Sala Apolo opened in 1943 as a dance hall on the Avinguda del Paral·lel, the entertainment avenue that ran along the southern edge of the Raval. The Paral·lel — named because it runs along the 41°22'N parallel of latitude, a fact that was seen as cosmically significant by someone in the 19th century — had been Barcelona's entertainment district since the 1890s, housing theatres, music halls, cabarets, and circus venues in a dense strip of popular culture.

The Apolo served big band dancing from the 1940s through the 1950s, then followed the market through various popular music formats: flamenco shows in the 1960s, rock concerts in the 1970s. After the return of democracy, it became a venue for the Catalan rock and pop scene. In the 1990s, it made the transition to electronic music — the genre that would define its subsequent reputation. The Apolo's late-night techno and house sessions became significant on the European club circuit in the early 2000s.

The survival of a 1940s dance hall as a functioning music venue reflects both the durability of its infrastructure — the sprung dance floor, the balconied interior, the stage — and the commercial resilience of the Paral·lel neighbourhood, which has maintained a entertainment function continuously since the 1890s despite all the social and political changes of the intervening century. The building itself is listed as a heritage site; the music playing inside it would not have been recognisable to the 1943 dancers.
