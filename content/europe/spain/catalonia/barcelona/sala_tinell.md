The great hall at the back of Plaça del Rei was built between 1359 and 1380 for King Pere III of Aragon, intended as the throne room and principal reception hall of the Royal Palace of Barcelona. It is one of the most remarkable rooms in Catalonia: seventeen metres wide, spanning the full width with six rounded stone arches rising from the floor without any intermediate columns. The arches are the whole structure — there are no pillars inside the space. The effect, walking in, is of extraordinary roominess: the stone curves above you and the floor is clear.

This is where tradition places the audience that Columbus gave to Ferdinand and Isabella on his return from the Caribbean in April 1493. The historical record confirms that Columbus reported to the court in Barcelona that spring; whether the specific meeting took place in the Sala del Tinell or in one of the other royal apartments is uncertain. But the hall was the ceremonial heart of the palace and the appropriate setting for a state reception of this magnitude. If the tradition is correct, Columbus walked into this room carrying gold, parrots, and six Taíno men he had brought from the islands, and stood before the monarchs under these same arches.

The hall now forms part of the Museu d'Història de Barcelona complex and is used for temporary exhibitions. During the Franco era it was used as a court for political trials — the Francoist military used the resonance of the royal hall to lend authority to proceedings that had no other legitimacy. This history sits alongside the Columbus story in the same walls, under the same arches. The Tinell has held several centuries of ceremony and the room has absorbed all of it.
