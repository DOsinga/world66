The convent of Sant Josep i Santa Mònica was founded by the Augustinian Recollect friars in the seventeenth century, at the point where the Ramblas meets the harbour — then as now, one of the busiest intersections in the city. The Augustinians chose the site deliberately: proximity to trade, travel, and cosmopolitan exchange suited a mendicant order whose mission was preaching to the urban poor.

The 1835 wave of anticlericalism that followed the First Carlist War resulted in the burning of monasteries across Barcelona and the expulsion of their occupants. The convent of Sant Josep i Santa Mònica was seized by the state under the disentailment laws and converted to military use, becoming first a barracks and then a military cultural centre. The Augustinians returned briefly but could not regain their property. When the Ajuntament eventually acquired the building and converted it to the *Arts Santa Mònica* contemporary arts centre in 1988, it joined a long list of Barcelona's religious buildings repurposed for secular culture.

The building's location at the Ramblas end has given it an unusual visibility — it is one of the first or last significant buildings visitors encounter when walking Barcelona's most famous street. The programming of *Arts Santa Mònica* has always leaned toward digital arts, interactive installation, and socially engaged practice, making it a peculiar but entirely appropriate successor to the friars who once preached at this corner about the things of this world.
