Sant Martí de Provençals takes its toponym from Martin of Tours — the Roman soldier who cut his cloak to share with a beggar and became one of the most popular saints in the Frankish world — and from the *Provençals*, a term that in medieval Catalan referred broadly to settlers from the Frankish territories north of the Pyrenees. The Franks drove the Moors out of Catalonia in the late eighth century and established the Hispanic March as a buffer zone; their settlers, the *Provençals*, gave their name to the village that grew up around this church.

The original settlement was a farming village on the alluvial plain east of Barcelona, surrounded by market gardens that supplied the city with vegetables. The *Horta* (garden) tradition of this area was one of the economic foundations of medieval Barcelona, and the farmers of Sant Martí de Provençals were part of a dense network of agricultural communities that the nineteenth-century industrial expansion would transform beyond recognition. By 1900, what had been market gardens was covered with textile factories, chemical plants, and the dense worker housing that gave Poblenou its character.

The church was absorbed into Barcelona along with the rest of the municipality in 1897, but the parish retained its identity and its ancient dedication. Martin of Tours is the patron saint of soldiers, innkeepers, and wine-makers — a combination that perfectly captures the mixed economy of a village that served both the military roads and the wine trade of Roman and Frankish Catalonia.
