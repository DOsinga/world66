Sant Martí de Provençals was an independent municipality to the east of Barcelona until its annexation in 1897. The village had existed as an agricultural community since the early medieval period; the church of Sant Martí, documented from the 10th century, was its religious and civic centre. The name "Provençals" refers not to Provence in France but to the word "pro vinciales" — relating to the lands of the province — indicating the settlement's character as a rural agricultural community dependent on Barcelona.

The 19th century transformed Sant Martí from an agricultural village into an industrial suburb. The Poblenou zone attracted cotton mills, chemical works, and engineering factories as Barcelona's industrial expansion pushed eastward along the coast. The village's agricultural identity was rapidly supplanted; by the time of annexation, the municipality was predominantly industrial.

The original church of Sant Martí de Provençals was damaged in the Civil War and rebuilt in the 1940s. The surrounding neighbourhood — now called simply "Sant Martí" — is one of Barcelona's most internally diverse districts, containing both the gentrifying Poblenou innovation district and some of the city's largest post-war social housing estates. The medieval church at the centre of the old village stands in a neighbourhood that has changed almost beyond recognition from the rural settlement the church was built to serve.
