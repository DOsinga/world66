The Barceloneta neighbourhood was planned and built by military engineers between 1715 and 1755. After the Bourbon forces demolished the La Ribera district to build the Ciutadella fortress, the displaced population needed somewhere to live. The engineer Juan Martín Cermeño designed a grid of narrow streets on the triangular sandbank between the city wall and the sea, creating the neighbourhood that still bears its grid plan today.

The church of Sant Miquel del Port was part of the original plan: a parish church at the geometric centre of the new neighbourhood, consecrated to the patron saint of sailors. The building, completed in 1755, is late Baroque in style, with a single nave and a simple façade that was designed to be visible from the sea as a navigational landmark. The statue of Sant Miquel — a warrior angel with sword and scales — stands in the niche above the entrance.

The church served a neighbourhood that was, from its founding, almost entirely composed of fishermen, sailors, and maritime workers. The economic and social character of Barceloneta remained remarkably stable from its founding through the early 20th century: the narrow streets, the small apartments stacked four and five storeys tall on plots only 8 metres wide, the fishing boats pulled up on the beach. The tourist invasion of the post-Olympic decades has transformed what people come here to do, but Sant Miquel del Port, still at the centre of the grid, is an unchanged axis of the original urban plan.
