Sant Pau del Camp — Saint Paul in the Fields — is Barcelona's oldest surviving church, founded in the ninth century on what was then countryside outside the city walls. The church building visible today is largely twelfth-century Romanesque, with a distinctive triple-apsed plan and blind arcading on the exterior. It sits in the middle of El Raval and is frequently overlooked by visitors focused on the area's contemporary reputation.

The cloister attached to the south side of the church is the reason to enter. It is small — only four bays per side — and dates from the twelfth to the thirteenth centuries. The carved capitals on the cloister arcade are among the finest Romanesque sculpture in Catalonia: each capital is different, depicting biblical scenes, geometric patterns, and human figures. One capital in particular has attracted scholarly attention: it shows a figure with an inscription that art historians interpret as naming the sculptor himself — making it a candidate for the earliest attributed portrait in medieval Catalan art, though the identification remains contested.

The monastery was suppressed in 1835 and the community dispersed, but the church building was taken over by a Benedictine house in the late nineteenth century and has been maintained continuously since. Masses are still held in the Romanesque church; entry to the cloister costs a small fee and is usually possible throughout the day.
