The church of Sant Pere de les Puelles — Saint Peter of the Girls — occupies one of the oldest Christian monastic sites in Barcelona, a location where religious women have been documented since 945 AD, when a Benedictine community of noble women ("puelles" being the medieval Latin for girls or maidens) was established under the protection of the Count of Barcelona. The convent accepted daughters of the Catalan aristocracy and served as both a religious house and a means of providing for noblewomen outside marriage, a common function for such establishments across medieval Europe.

The building's history is one of repeated destruction and reconstruction. Al-Mansur, the Moorish regent of Córdoba who raided Barcelona in 985, reportedly sacked the convent along with much of the city. It was rebuilt and continued through the medieval period, accumulating privileges and property. The church that existed by the high medieval period was substantially Romanesque; fragments of that structure, including the crypt and some column capitals, survived later rebuildings and remain visible. The nave and apse of the current church date to 1911, when a restoration rebuilt much of the structure in a Romanesque Revival style intended to evoke what had been there — a project of imagination as much as archaeology.

The small square in front of the church, Plaça de Sant Pere, is one of the neighbourhood's gathering points. The neighbourhood itself — Sant Pere, Santa Caterina i la Ribera — takes Saint Pere de les Puelles as one of its anchor buildings, a presence in this corner of the old city that predates the medieval streets surrounding it.
