In 1996, a real estate developer preparing to renovate a building on Carrer de Marlet discovered chambers beneath the ground floor that subsequent archaeological study identified as a synagogue. The Sinagoga Medieval de Barcelona is now considered the oldest surviving synagogue structure in Europe, with elements of the hall — particularly the orientation of the Torah niche and a fragment of original mosaic pavement — dating possibly to the third or fourth century AD, before the Christian empire and certainly before the Muslim period. This would make it older than any synagogue currently preserved above ground anywhere in Europe.

The Barcelona Jewish community, the Call, was established in the Roman period and flourished through the medieval centuries, reaching its peak in the thirteenth and fourteenth centuries, before the pogroms of 1391 destroyed it. The Call's geography was largely obliterated by subsequent construction, making archaeological finds of this significance exceptionally rare. The synagogue appears to have continued in use — with modifications — until 1391, when the community was scattered.

The space is small — two interconnected rooms — and visits are guided. What is displayed includes the original mosaic section, the niche orientation toward Jerusalem, and materials explaining the detective work of the discovery. The building above is largely medieval, with a Roman column embedded in one wall from an even earlier structure beneath. Carrer de Marlet is in the heart of what was the old Call quarter.
