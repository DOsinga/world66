The Taller de Músics (Musicians' Workshop) was founded in 1979, four years after Franco's death, as an open music school focusing on jazz, improvised music, and non-classical traditions. Its location in the Raval was deliberate: the neighbourhood was cheap, culturally mixed, and outside the institutional music world centred on the Liceu opera house and the conservatory.

Barcelona's jazz scene had survived the Franco years in a constrained form: jazz was tolerated as American entertainment more than suppressed as political expression, but public performance required permission and the best musicians worked informally. The transition to democracy released an enormous amount of pent-up creative energy. The Taller de Músics arrived at exactly the moment when musicians could finally organise, teach, and perform without fear of censorship or political risk.

The school grew rapidly. It now operates from larger premises and accredits formal musical qualifications, but it retains the character of an institution built around musical community rather than academic hierarchy. Several of the musicians who studied or taught at the Taller in the 1980s went on to significant international careers. The Raval neighbourhood's subsequent gentrification has pushed the original economic logic of the location — cheap rent for cultural experimentation — upward in price, but the institution remains in the district it helped to define.
