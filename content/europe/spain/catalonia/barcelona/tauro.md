Antoni Roselló designed three obelisks of weathering steel and aluminium for the Nova Icària seafront as part of the 1992 Olympic coastal redevelopment, spacing them roughly 400 metres apart to mirror the Cerdà grid's block dimensions. The three are named Acuario, Tauró, and Piscis — zodiac signs, but not chosen abstractly: Roselló named each after the astrological sign of his wife and two daughters, embedding a private family tribute inside what reads as straightforward civic infrastructure. That the Olympic-era street furniture quietly commemorates one man's family is the kind of detail that never appears on the information panel.

Despite the Catalan spelling tauró (which means "shark"), the name refers to the zodiac sign Taurus — the sculpture uses the Spanish form without an accent. The confusion is productive: these tall steel forms, angular and elemental against the Mediterranean sky, do carry something of both readings — the stolid bull of Taurus and the predatory efficiency of a shark's fin.

The obelisks also function as giant street lamps: at dusk the aluminium elements illuminate the seafront promenade. Seeing them lit against the evening sky, spaced evenly along the waterfront, gives a sense of what the Olympic planners were reaching for — a seafront that was simultaneously monumental and domestic, sculpture at the scale of a city block.
