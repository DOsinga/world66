The Coliseum opened as the Cinema Coliseum, built to Bonaventura Bassegoda's design with a facade that mixed classical and Baroque elements in the manner of Spain's golden-age picture palaces. It was a prestige building from the start: large, ornate, positioned on the Gran Via de les Corts Catalanes where it would be seen by everyone passing through the city.

During the silent era the cinema hosted the premieres of major Spanish productions; when sound arrived it converted in 1930 and continued as a first-run cinema. The transition from cinema to theatre happened gradually over the latter half of the 20th century, as the city's cinema economics changed and the building's scale suited live performance better than it suited the screen.

As a musical theatre venue the Coliseum now hosts major Spanish-language productions of Broadway and West End shows — the kind of programming its scale was always suited for. The facade, rarely replicated because nobody builds this way anymore, remains one of the cleaner examples of 1920s Spanish cinema architecture still attached to its original building.
