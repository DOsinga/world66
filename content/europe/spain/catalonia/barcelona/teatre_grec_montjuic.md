The Teatre Grec (Greek Theatre) on Montjuïc was created in 1929 for the International Exposition of Barcelona, designed by the architect Ramon Reventós in the style of an ancient Greek open-air theatre. The name is accurate: the form — a semicircular seating bank cut into the hillside facing a stage — is directly modelled on the theatres of classical antiquity, particularly those of mainland Greece and Sicily.

The site had been used as a stone quarry before the Exposition. The quarrying had conveniently created a concave hillside that needed minimal excavation to become a theatre bowl; Reventós simply regularised the cut faces, added seating terraces, and constructed a stage building. The surrounding terraced gardens were designed simultaneously, creating an approach through cypress trees and laurel hedges that deliberately evoked the approach to a Greek sanctuary.

The theatre became the main venue of the Festival Grec — the summer arts festival that has run annually since 1976. The festival was established in the early years of democratic Spain as one of the first major public cultural events of the new era: theatre, dance, and concerts in an outdoor venue, in July and August, free or cheaply priced. The Teatre Grec's 1,900 seats fill on warm summer evenings with audiences who have been coming here for three or four decades. The location on Montjuïc — with the castle visible on the ridge above — is part of the experience. The Greek pastiche has been inhabited long enough to feel like the real thing.
