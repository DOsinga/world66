Eduardo Chillida (1924–2002) did not simply accept a location when the Barcelona municipality purchased Topos V at a retrospective exhibition in 1986 — he chose where it should go. The Basque sculptor argued that Plaça del Rei, with its severe medieval architecture and ochre stonework, would create the exact dialogue his forms required: the brown iron of weathering steel against the honey-coloured stone of the Palau Reial Major, the geometric angles of his sculpture echoing the arched windows of the Saló del Tinell. The municipality agreed, and the piece was inaugurated on 6 March 1986.

The form is a corten steel dihedral angle closed on two sides by semicircular caps, measuring roughly 2.10 by 2.37 by 1.70 metres. Those curved caps were deliberately designed to echo the arched windows of the medieval buildings behind it — and, according to Chillida's own account, to trace the letter "B" for Barcelona when seen from the right angle. The word "topos" in ancient Greek means "place": Chillida's consistent argument was that the specific place defines the work, not the other way around.

Barcelonans have called the sculpture "l'urinari" — the urinal — since shortly after its installation, a nickname affectionate enough to have stuck in guidebooks. Abstract public sculpture across Spain has a long tradition of popular renaming; being given an irreverent nickname by the people who walk past it daily is, by one measure, a sign of genuine adoption.
