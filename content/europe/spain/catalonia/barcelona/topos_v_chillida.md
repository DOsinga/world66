Most people walking into Plaça del Rei are looking up — at the Gothic palace façade, the staircase, the tower. Topos V sits low at the plaza's edge and demands you look down. The Basque sculptor Eduardo Chillida placed this rusted iron form here in 1986, and it has aged into the square like something that was always there: dark, dense, its surfaces oxidized to the colour of old blood.

Chillida was deliberate about the placement. He argued that the geometric forms — a dihedral angle, closed on two sides, open on two — would "dialogue with the sober medieval architecture surrounding the square." He was right. The sculpture echoes the arches in the palace arcade, the rectangular window openings, the thickness of walls built to last. What looks like an abstract form starts to read as a fragment of something ancient.

Topos is Greek for "place." Chillida made several versions in this series, each one a meditation on how a sculpture can belong to — and generate — a specific location. This is version five, and of all the places his Topos series landed, this plaza of Roman foundation and medieval kings may be the best argument for the whole idea.
