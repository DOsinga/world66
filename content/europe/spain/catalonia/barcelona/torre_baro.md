The Torre Baró (Baron's Tower) stands on a ridge in the Collserola foothills at the northern extreme of Barcelona's built-up area. The tower is a medieval defensive structure, probably 14th or 15th century in origin, associated with the control of the road leading north from Barcelona through the Collserola hills. At a height of over 400 metres, it commanded views across the entire coastal plain.

Below the tower, on the slopes descending toward the city, one of Barcelona's most extreme informal settlements was built in the 1950s and 1960s. Internal migrants from southern Spain arrived faster than the city could build formal housing; they established shanty towns — barraques — on the hillsides that the formal city had not yet reached. The Torre Baró neighbourhood was one of these: a settlement of self-built houses on steep terrain, without services, official recognition, or legal status.

The city eventually recognised the neighbourhood and connected it to services in the 1970s. The original barraques were replaced with public housing blocks. The tower survived on its ridge, repaired and listed, now a monument in a neighbourhood that barely existed when the tower was the tallest thing on the horizon. The combination of a medieval watchtower and a 1960s informal settlement, both at the edge of what counts as Barcelona, is one of the more extreme examples of the city's layered history.
