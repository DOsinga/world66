The Torre de les Aigües (Water Tower) in the Eixample is a cylindrical stone tower built in 1867 as part of the Companyia d'Aigües de Barcelona's new water supply system. The tower served as a pressure reservoir: water pumped up into it from the intake facilities could then flow by gravity to buildings throughout the Eixample, providing the pressure needed for domestic plumbing.

The tower is notable not just as engineering infrastructure but as evidence of the ambitions of the Eixample's developers. Building a planned expansion district with modern water supply — rather than waiting for the neighbourhood to develop and then adding infrastructure retroactively — was an unusually systematic approach that reflected Ildefons Cerdà's rational planning principles. The Eixample was designed as modern from its foundations: regular streets, interior block gardens (largely never built), and centralised water supply.

The tower now stands in the garden of a block interior, accessed through a passage in the Carrer de Roger de Llúria. In summer, the block interior is converted into an outdoor swimming pool for neighbourhood residents — an informal tradition that has been operating for several decades, using the walled garden of the Eixample block for what was originally intended as communal green space. The water tower that once supplied the neighbourhood now overlooks families swimming in an inflatable pool. The functional relationship between the tower and water has persisted, although the specific water it oversees has changed.
