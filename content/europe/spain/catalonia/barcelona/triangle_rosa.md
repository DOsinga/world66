The inverted pink triangle was the badge sewn onto the uniforms of gay men in Nazi concentration camps; the LGBTQ+ rights movement reclaimed it as a defiant symbol in the 1970s and 1980s. Spain's relationship with this history is specific: Franco's regime, though not Nazi Germany, systematically persecuted gay men under the Ley de Vagos y Maleantes (1954) and its successor the Ley de Peligrosidad y Rehabilitación Social (1970), which explicitly criminalised homosexuality and sent thousands to work camps and psychiatric institutions.

The memorial — a horizontal inverted pink triangle of Montjuïc stone set into the ground in the Jardins de Fontserè i Mestre — was inaugurated in March 2011. Its location carries weight that extends beyond the Franco-era persecutions: it stands near where Sònia Rescalvo Zafra, a transwoman, was beaten to death by neo-Nazi skinheads on the morning of 6 October 1991. That murder, and the prosecution that followed, ended the effective impunity for anti-LGBTQ+ hate crimes in Spain. In 2013, the nearby bandstand was renamed the Glorieta de la Transsexual Sònia — making Barcelona the first city in Europe to name a public space after a trans person.

The Catalan inscription reads: "En memòria dels gais, les lesbianes i les persones transsexuals que han patit persecució i repressió al llarg de la història. Barcelona 2011." At the inauguration, all parties in Barcelona city council were represented except the Partido Popular.
