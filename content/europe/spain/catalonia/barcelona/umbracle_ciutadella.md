The Umbracle stands beside the Hivernacle in the Ciutadella park and is often confused with it, but they serve opposite purposes. Where the Hivernacle is an enclosed iron-and-glass greenhouse that traps heat and light for cold-sensitive tropical plants, the Umbracle is an open structure — cast-iron arches supporting a latticed wooden roof — designed to provide shade for plants that would be damaged by direct Barcelona sun. The name comes from the Catalan for shadow or shade, and the structure is essentially a large, permanent pergola, unusual enough in formal botanical architecture that there are few comparable examples in Spain.

It was built in 1884 by the architect Josep Fontserè, who was responsible for much of the park's infrastructure, and was intended as part of the park's function as a botanical garden and public recreation space. The wooden slats of the roof filter the light without blocking airflow, creating the dappled, humid conditions that certain ferns, palms, and shade-loving exotics require. In the late nineteenth century, when the park was new and the Eixample was still being built around it, the Umbracle and its companion glasshouse were serious horticultural facilities as well as architectural attractions.

Today the Umbracle is most visited at night, when it opens as an outdoor bar and club space during the summer months. The cast-iron columns and arched ceiling, lit from below, make an unexpectedly theatrical setting, which perhaps explains why the structure has survived the twentieth century in reasonable condition. During the day, with the tourists scattered through the park and the plants growing unmolested in their filtered light, it is worth pausing to appreciate the structural logic: a building designed not to keep weather out, but to shape the specific quality of light that passes through.
