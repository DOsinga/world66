Una Habitació on sempre Plou — A Room Where It Always Rains — was inaugurated on 21 July 1992, just days before the Olympics opened, as part of "Configuracions urbanes," a programme of eight works by international artists curated by Gloria Moure for Barcelona's Olympic cultural agenda. The structure is a latticed iron cage-pavilion, inspired by the Umbracle greenhouse in Parc de la Ciutadella, containing five bronze human figures with covered eyes and spherical bases instead of legs — immobile, unseeing, rooted to the ground by forms that deny locomotion.

The title names a plan that failed. Juan Muñoz (1953–2001) intended a permanent internal irrigation system that would create continuous rain inside the structure regardless of the weather outside. Technical difficulties prevented installation. Muñoz died in 2001 before approving any retrofit, and the city has kept the work as-is, retaining the original title as homage to his intention. The cage was dismantled in 2004 to build an underground car park and reinstalled on a new marble floor about half a metre above its original ground level. The rain it promises still does not come.

The gap between the work's title and its actual condition is not exactly a flaw — it becomes, over time, another layer of meaning. A room where it always rains that never rains is a description of an aspiration kept permanently open, an unfulfilled condition named and maintained. Muñoz, whose work consistently concerned figures in states of suspension or constraint, might have appreciated the irony.
