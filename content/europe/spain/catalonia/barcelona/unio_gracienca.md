The Sant Medir festival is peculiarly Gràcian: each year on 3 March, mounted riders and floats travel from the neighbourhood to the hermitage of Sant Medir in Sant Cugat del Vallès and return throwing sweets — tens of thousands of bags of candy — to crowds lining the streets. The tradition combines a religious pilgrimage with what amounts to a neighbourhood-scale carnival, and the colles (associations) that organise it are the institutional backbone of Gràcian civic life.

The Unió Gracienca is the third-oldest Sant Medir association in all of Barcelona and the first founded within Gràcia itself, established in 1901 at a now-vanished bar called Can Garcia, on what is now Torrent de l'Olla. The marble plaque was installed in 2001 for the centenary, placed at the spot where a handful of neighbours agreed to found an association that has continued — with a pause during the Civil War — ever since. In 2000, the City of Barcelona awarded the association its Medal of Honor.

The plaque is catalogued as public art, but it commemorates something that is most visible one day a year, when the colla rides out of Gràcia toward Sant Cugat in the early morning and returns in the afternoon with empty saddlebags and crowds of children with sticky hands. A small marble square on a wall is a slender monument to what is, in practice, a living institution.
