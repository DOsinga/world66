Josep Pérez Pérez (Barcelona 1887 – Alcoy 1978), known professionally as Peresejo, specialised in mythological subjects and female nudes across a career that ran from the Exposition of 1929 to the final decades of Francoism — a span of artistic and political regimes that most Spanish sculptors of the period did not survive intact. Venus moderna was commissioned for the 1929 Barcelona International Exposition in a classicising style consistent with the decorative programme of that event: naturalistic, mythological, warmly Mediterranean.

After the Exposition closed, the piece went into municipal storage, where it remained until the Parc de l'Espanya Industrial was designed in the 1980s and city officials retrieved works from the 1929 collection to populate it. The setting is deliberately incongruous: Peresejo's 1929 marble sits beside the artificial lake of an aggressively modernist park designed by Basque architect Luis Peña Ganchegui, which features a row of lighthouse-like watchtower slides looming over the water and a large dragon sculpture. The 1929 survivor and the 1985 provocation face each other across the same space.

The title — Venus moderna, Modern Venus — carries the weight of a claim rather than a description. In 1929, "modern" was an aspiration; by the time the park was built, modernity had been redefined around her several times. The word now reads differently than it was intended, which is part of what makes the pairing with its 1980s surroundings so charged.
