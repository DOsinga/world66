Barcelona's tram network was dismantled between 1951 and 1971 as part of a city policy that prioritised private car traffic; the Vallcarca line was abandoned in 1968, leaving the 1894 iron viaduct without purpose. Other cities demolished their redundant tram infrastructure as a matter of course; Barcelona mostly followed suit, which makes the Vallcarca viaduct unusual — a piece of 19th-century railway engineering that survived by being in a location awkward enough that demolition was never prioritised.

The viaduct carried the tramway over the Vallcarca ravine in what was then the edge of the city, connecting the upper Gràcia neighbourhood to the hillside developments above. The ravine itself — the Torrent de les Monges — is now largely built over, but the viaduct's height above the surrounding streets still communicates the scale of the original topographic obstacle. The conversion to pedestrian use in 2012 gave the neighbourhood a bridge with views across the hillside toward the Serra de Collserola.

The structure is iron lattice construction of a type common to late 19th-century railway and tramway engineering but rare in surviving urban form. Most comparable structures in Barcelona were demolished when the lines they served were abandoned; the Vallcarca viaduct survived long enough to be recognised as a specimen worth preserving. The tramway tracks are gone, but the span and its abutments are original.
