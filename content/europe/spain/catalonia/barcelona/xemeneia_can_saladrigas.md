The factory chimney of Can Saladrigas stands in the Poblenou neighbourhood as one of the clearest visual markers of the district's industrial past. The Can Saladrigas factory was a textile mill established in the 19th century, part of the cotton manufacturing complex that made Poblenou the most intensely industrialised zone in Catalonia.

Factory chimneys were among the most visible elements of the 19th and early 20th-century industrial landscape. The tall brick stacks — some reaching 50-70 metres — were required to create the draught that kept the furnaces burning at temperatures sufficient to drive the steam engines. They were visible from across the city, and their smoke was the sign of a working factory — of employment, of production, of economic activity.

As Poblenou's factories closed through the 1960s and 1970s, the chimneys were demolished along with the buildings they served. The 22@ redevelopment plan, which began converting the former industrial land to offices and apartments in 2000, accelerated the demolitions. But some chimneys were retained as heritage markers — listed structures that developers had to incorporate into their redevelopment plans. The Can Saladrigas chimney is one of these: a brick tower standing in the middle of new development, a vertical marker of what the neighbourhood was. The factories are gone; the chimney remains, serving no function except memory.
