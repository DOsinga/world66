The Faculty of Geography and History of the Universitat de Barcelona opened in the Raval neighbourhood in 2006. Archaeological remains were found during preliminary construction, and in 2012 the university and city council formalised an excavation programme — ArqueUB — that uses the courtyard garden as a live teaching site. Students dig real stratigraphy, make real decisions about what to record and how, and work with finds that will not survive the process of being studied. It is the kind of fieldwork experience that a training trench in a simulated context cannot replicate.

The site has revealed continuous occupation across roughly 700 years from the fourteenth century onward. The most significant find: a nineteenth-century pottery workshop belonging to Antoni Tarrés, described as the first factory in the city to create ornamental elements for architectural applications — the workshop that produced many of the decorative ceramic tiles still visible on Ciutat Vella building facades today. Standing in the courtyard of a 2006 faculty building and looking down at the excavated remains of the factory that made the tiles on the buildings across the street is a compressed version of what urban archaeology does: it makes the present legible by recovering what preceded it.

The programme has periodically opened to secondary school students and the public, with undergraduates serving as guides. The site is about 600 metres from one of Barcino's Roman city gates, placing it just outside the ancient city's perimeter — extramural territory where workshops and trades clustered in Roman and medieval times alike.
