Cadaqués is one of the most beautiful villages on the Catalan Costa Brava — a whitewashed fishing port tucked into a rocky bay on the Cap de Creus peninsula. Its relative inaccessibility (a winding mountain road with no train connection) kept it from the worst of coastal overdevelopment and gave it a character that larger resorts have lost.

Salvador Dalí lived here for much of his life, and the connection is genuine: the landscape of [Cap de Creus](cadaqus/cap_de_creus), with its eroded limestone formations and strange seascape, runs throughout his surrealist paintings. The [Casa Museu Dalí in nearby Portlligat](cadaqus/casa_museu_dali_portlligat) gives you a look inside the eccentric compound where he worked for decades.

The [Cap de Creus Natural Park](cadaqus/cap_de_creus) surrounding the village is the easternmost point of the Iberian Peninsula and excellent walking country. The ruined monastery of [Sant Pere de Rodes](cadaqus/sant_pere_de_rodes) surveys the whole peninsula from the ridge above. South of Roses, the remote cove of Cala Montjoi is where Ferran Adrià's legendary restaurant [El Bulli](cadaqus/el_bulli) once operated. The evening light bouncing off the [whitewashed waterfront](cadaqus/cadaques_waterfront) explains why artists kept coming here.
