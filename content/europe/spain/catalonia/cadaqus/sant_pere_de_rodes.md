Sant Pere de Rodes is a ruined Benedictine monastery perched high on the ridge above the Cap de Creus peninsula, a spectacular and somewhat brutal walk or drive above the coastal towns. Founded in the 9th century, it grew into one of the most powerful monasteries in Catalonia before being sacked by French forces in 1798 and subsequently abandoned. What remains is roofless but largely intact: a great Romanesque church with carved columns, a tower, cloisters, and extensive outbuildings.

The view from the monastery is one of the great panoramas of the Costa Brava: the entire Cap de Creus peninsula spreads below, the bay of Roses to the south, the Pyrenean foothills to the north, and on clear days the coast stretching into France. The walk up from the village of El Port de la Selva takes about two hours and is the most rewarding approach. A road also reaches the site from the south, if the walk is not an option.
