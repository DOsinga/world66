Sant Llorenç is a Romanesque church dating from the late twelfth century, with Gothic additions grafted on in the fifteenth. It is one of the oldest standing buildings in Lleida's lower city, tucked into a pedestrian quarter a few minutes' walk from the main shopping streets. The exterior is restrained and worn, the stonework darkened by centuries of urban air — a reminder that this city had a significant past long before it became a modern Catalan market town.

The interior is worth entering if it is open: the original Romanesque nave is solid and sober, and the Gothic apsidal chapels added later give the building an interesting layered quality. Sant Llorenç is often overlooked in favour of La Seu Vella on the hill, which is understandable — but for visitors interested in medieval architecture it makes a worthwhile stop on the way up to the old cathedral.
