Mataró is a coastal city about 30 km northeast of [Barcelona](/europe/spain/catalonia/barcelona), the capital of the Maresme comarca. It was the terminus of Spain's first railway line, opened in 1848, and today the Cercanías train still makes it an easy [day trip from Barcelona](/europe/spain/catalonia/mataro/getting_there).

The old town has a pleasant pedestrianised centre with the [Basílica de Santa Maria](/europe/spain/catalonia/mataro/basilica_de_santa_maria) and the [Roman Villa of Can Llauder](/europe/spain/catalonia/mataro/roman_villa_can_llauder), whose well-preserved mosaic floors are among the finest in Catalonia. The [beaches](/europe/spain/catalonia/mataro/beaches) are wide and sandy, less crowded than Barcelona's, and the seafront promenade is good for a walk. Mataró is also known for its modernist architecture — the [Nau Gaudí](/europe/spain/catalonia/mataro/nau_gaudi), an early industrial building by a young Antoni Gaudí, is the only surviving industrial work by the architect anywhere in the world.
