Mataró's headline attraction is the [Nau Gaudí](/europe/spain/catalonia/mataro/nau_gaudi), an early industrial building by Antoni Gaudí dating from 1878 — one of his first commissions, and the only surviving industrial work by the architect. It stands alongside the [Can Marfà Knitwear Museum](/europe/spain/catalonia/mataro/can_marfa_knitwear_museum), which tells the story of the cotton and knitwear industry that shaped the city through the nineteenth and twentieth centuries.

In the old town, the [Basílica de Santa Maria](/europe/spain/catalonia/mataro/basilica_de_santa_maria) anchors the historic centre — a substantial baroque church with an ornate interior that took over a century to complete. A short walk away, the [Roman Villa of Can Llauder](/europe/spain/catalonia/mataro/roman_villa_can_llauder) preserves the remains of a prosperous first-century Roman estate, including well-preserved mosaic floors that are among the finest in Catalonia.

The [Mataró Museum](/europe/spain/catalonia/mataro/mataro_museum) in the Renaissance building of Can Serra holds the city's art and history collections, including materials documenting the 1848 railway — Spain's first — and Mataró's role in Catalan modernisme.
