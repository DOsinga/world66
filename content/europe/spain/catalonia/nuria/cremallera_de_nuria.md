The Vall de Núria Rack Railway — always called the Cremallera (Catalan for zipper) — is not just transport but the central experience of any visit to Núria. Opened in 1931, it climbs 1,000 metres in about 45 minutes from Ribes de Freser through the spectacular Gorges del Freser, a narrow rocky gorge where the track clings to cliffs above the cascading Freser river.

The railway uses the Abt rack system: a toothed centre rail that engages a cog on the locomotive, allowing ascent on gradients where ordinary adhesion would fail. The steepest section reaches 65 per mille. Modern rolling stock replaced the original cars in the 1990s, but the track itself follows the same route blasted through the gorge in the late 1920s.

The journey breaks at Queralbs, a small mountain village where passengers can board or alight, and from here the scenery opens as the train crosses a bridge over the gorge and continues up to the treeline. The final approach to the sanctuary, with the valley floor spreading out and the peaks closing in on three sides, is genuinely impressive.

Buy your ticket before boarding at Ribes de Freser station. FGC (Ferrocarrils de la Generalitat de Catalunya) operates the service; timetables and combined packages are available on their website. The lower terminus coordinates given here are for Ribes de Freser station.
