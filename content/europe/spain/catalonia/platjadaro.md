Platja d'Aro is a Costa Brava resort town on the Catalan coast between Barcelona and the French border, about 110 km north-east of Barcelona. The name translates roughly as "beach of Aro," and the long sandy beach — backed by a promenade and lined with shops and restaurants — is the town's primary attraction. It draws a largely domestic Spanish and French clientele rather than the international package-holiday crowd that dominates other stretches of the Costa Brava.

The historic part of the municipality is [Castell d'Aro](/europe/spain/catalonia/platjadaro/castell_daro), a medieval village 3 km inland from the beach resort, with the ruined 11th-century Benedormiens Castle at its edge. The two settlements function as one municipality but feel quite different: the beach strip is busy and commercial from June to September, while the old village retains its year-round Catalan character. The [Camí de Ronda](/europe/spain/catalonia/platjadaro/cami_de_ronda) coastal path connects the resort to smaller coves including the beautiful [Sa Conca](/europe/spain/catalonia/platjadaro/sa_conca).
