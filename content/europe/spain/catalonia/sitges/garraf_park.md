The Garraf Natural Park covers more than 12,000 hectares of stark white limestone hills west of Sitges, dropping into the Mediterranean along dramatic cliff roads. The landscape is unusual for Catalonia: arid, lunar, scattered with palm and prickly-pear, and riddled with caves and underground rivers carved into the porous stone.

The coastal road from Sitges to Castelldefels skirts the eastern edge of the park along clifftops with constant sea views — one of the most spectacular short drives in the country. Marked walking trails reach the summit of Morella (594 m) and the medieval ruined castle of Olesa. The Celler Güell, a Gaudí-designed wine cellar near Garraf village, is open by appointment for a striking small detour.
