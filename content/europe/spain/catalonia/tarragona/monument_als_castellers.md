story: >
  The *castell* (human tower) is the most distinctive surviving folk tradition in Catalonia, and Tarragona has been one of its centres since the late 18th century. The biennial **Concurs de Castells** competition in Tarragona's bullring draws teams from across Catalonia and is considered the world championship of castelling. Towers of nine, ten, or even eleven storeys are routinely built; falls happen, and serious injury is rare but not unheard of.
---

A vivid bronze sculpture on the Rambla Nova depicting a complete Catalan **castell**, or human tower — the trunk of strong adults at the base, the smaller climbers above, and at the top a child of seven or eight raising a hand in the traditional gesture that completes the tower. Designed by Francesc Anglès i García and installed in 1999, the monument is roughly twelve metres tall and life-size from base to summit.

A five-minute stop, but worth it for the explanation of the tradition. The biennial **Concurs de Castells** in early October (held in Tarragona's Tarraco Arena) is the world championship of the sport — book accommodation months ahead if you want to attend.
