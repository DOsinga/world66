The Roman temple of Vic is one of the best-preserved Roman temples in the Iberian Peninsula. Built in the second century AD as part of the Roman town of Auso (modern Vic), it consists of a raised podium with six Corinthian columns on the facade and supporting columns along the sides. It is a genuinely impressive survivor.

The temple stands in the upper part of the old town, in a small square near the castle hill. Its setting among the medieval streets makes it all the more striking — the Roman stonework rises from among the Catalan houses as if the centuries had simply folded together. The temple can be viewed from outside at any time; the interior is accessible during opening hours.
