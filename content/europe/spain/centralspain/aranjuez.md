Aranjuez sits about 50 kilometres south of [Madrid](/europe/spain/centralspain/madrid) on the banks of the Tagus, and has been a royal retreat since the time of Philip II. The town is officially designated a Royal Site and a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and it shows — the scale and grandeur of the place is entirely out of proportion to its modest size.

The [Royal Palace](/europe/spain/centralspain/aranjuez/royal_palace) is the centrepiece, but the real draw is the gardens: the formal [Parterre](/europe/spain/centralspain/aranjuez/parterre_garden) alongside the palace, the shaded island garden ([Jardín de la Isla](/europe/spain/centralspain/aranjuez/isla_garden)), and the vast [Jardín del Príncipe](/europe/spain/centralspain/aranjuez/jardin_del_principe) stretching along the Tagus for two kilometres. The [Casa del Labrador](/europe/spain/centralspain/aranjuez/casa_del_labrador), a small neoclassical palace decorated in a wild mix of Rococo, Imperial, and Pompeian styles, is at the far end of the Jardín del Príncipe and worth the detour. The [Museum of Royal Barges](/europe/spain/centralspain/aranjuez/royal_barges_museum) nearby contains the extraordinary gilded pleasure boats the royal family used on the river.

Beyond the monuments, Aranjuez is a pleasant place to stroll. The streets and squares have a relaxed, unhurried feel, and the town is famous for its strawberries and asparagus — both grown in the alluvial soil along the Tagus and at their best in spring. In May and June you can ride the historic [Strawberry Train](/europe/spain/centralspain/aranjuez/strawberry_train) from Madrid, a vintage steam service with strawberry tastings on board. The [18th-century bullring and bullfighting museum](/europe/spain/centralspain/aranjuez/plaza_de_toros) are also worth a visit.
