The Jardín del Príncipe is the largest of Aranjuez's royal gardens, stretching for about 150 hectares along the right bank of the Tagus. It was created in the 18th century as a romantic landscape garden in the English style, contrasting with the formal geometry of the Parterre garden alongside the palace. Shaded avenues of plane and elm trees, ponds, fountains, and the river give it a lush, pastoral character that can feel worlds away from dry Castilian summer.

The garden contains several points of interest beyond the general landscape: the Chinese Pond (Estanque de los Chinescos) with its 18th-century pagoda, a large boating lake, and the Casa del Labrador at the eastern end. The garden stretches far enough that it rewards a leisurely walk rather than a quick circuit. In spring the tree canopies are spectacular.
