---
title: "Day Trips"
type: section
---

**Ávila** (90km east, 1.5 hours by bus or car). One of the best-preserved walled medieval cities in Europe: the complete circuit of 11th-century walls, with 88 towers, still stands intact and can be walked on top. Ávila is also the birthplace of St Teresa, the 16th-century mystic and reformer; her convents and the cathedral where she was baptised are all within the walls. Regular buses from Salamanca bus station.

**Ciudad Rodrigo** (90km west, 1 hour by bus). A walled medieval city on the Agueda river close to the Portuguese border, largely off the tourist trail. The cathedral, the castle (now a parador), and the intact circuit of Roman and medieval walls repay a half-day; the weekly market is good for local produce including excellent jamón. Regular buses from Salamanca.

**La Alberca** (75km south, 1 hour by car). The best-preserved medieval village in the Sierra de Francia: a tight cluster of half-timbered stone houses, cobbled lanes, and an arcaded Plaza Mayor that has barely changed in 500 years. La Alberca is a National Monument and one of the most atmospheric villages in Castile. No direct public transport from Salamanca — a car or taxi is needed.

**Valladolid** (110km north, 1.5 hours by train). Once Philip II's capital and the most important city in Spain, Valladolid now has an underrated old town and the Museo Nacional de Escultura — the finest collection of polychrome religious sculpture in the world, housed in a stunning plateresque building. Regular Alvia trains from Salamanca.
