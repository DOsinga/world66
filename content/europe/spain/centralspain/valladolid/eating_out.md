---
title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

Valladolid takes its food seriously and prices are remarkably low for a city of this status. The local style is robust Castilian: roast suckling lamb (lechazo) and suckling pig, hearty stews, jamón ibérico, and the cured sausages of the surrounding province (cecina, morcilla). All of it pairs naturally with the red wines from nearby Ribera del Duero, Toro, and Cigales.

Pinchos — Castilian-style tapas, often more elaborate than their Basque cousins — are a way of life. The streets just east of the Plaza Mayor (Calle Correos, Calle Pasión, Calle del Val) are crammed with bars; you stand at the counter, point at what looks good, eat it in two bites, drink a glass of Verdejo, and move on. The annual Concurso Nacional de Pinchos y Tapas held in November is a serious culinary event.

**Los Zagales de Abadía** — Calle Pasión. Famous for award-winning creative pinchos; arrive early or expect to wait.

**La Mejillonera Carabela** — Calle Lonja. A Valladolid institution serving cheap, excellent mussels (mejillones) by the kilo, with simple Spanish wines.

**Restaurante La Parrilla de San Lorenzo** — Calle Pedro Niño. Roast lamb and a wood-fired grill in a vaulted 16th-century basement, theatrical and very Castilian.

**Jero** — Calle Correos. Tiny, traditional bar that has won the national pinchos competition multiple times. Sit at the bar and order anything.
