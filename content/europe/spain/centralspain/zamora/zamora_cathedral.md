  The Byzantine-style dome of Zamora Cathedral is one of the most unusual pieces of architecture in Spain — 16 fish-scale slate tiles cover each of its 16 turrets, and the whole lantern sits on a drum of round-arched windows in a way that looks more Ottoman than Castilian. Architectural historians believe the design was influenced by the dome of the Old Cathedral of Salamanca (built around the same time, by possibly the same master builder), but Zamora's version is bolder and stranger.

The Cathedral of Zamora occupies the highest point of the old town ridge and is the city's most important monument. Built primarily in the 12th century and completed around 1174, it is one of the finest examples of Romanesque architecture in Spain. The exterior is impressive from any angle, but the distinctive dome — a scaly, Byzantine-inspired structure unlike anything else in Castile — is the element that stays with you.

The interior is large and austere, with a nave of impressive proportions. The cathedral museum houses an outstanding collection of 15th and 16th-century Flemish tapestries — among the best in Spain and largely unknown beyond the specialist. Allow time for the museum; the tapestries alone justify the entry fee. The cathedral is open daily, with slightly reduced hours during services.
