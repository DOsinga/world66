Ceuta's position makes it an excellent base for short excursions in two directions.

**Tetouan (Morocco)** — Forty kilometres south of the border, Tetouan is a UNESCO World Heritage medina and one of the finest examples of an Andalusian-influenced Moroccan city. The historic centre was built largely by Muslim refugees expelled from Spain after 1492, and its architecture — whitewashed houses, intricate tiling, shaded souks — reflects that Andalusian heritage. Take a shared taxi or grand taxi from Fnideq (the Moroccan town immediately south of the Ceuta border) to Tetouan; the journey takes about half an hour. The medina is easily walked in an afternoon.

**Tangier (Morocco)** — Ninety kilometres to the southwest, Tangier is accessible in a couple of hours by shared taxi or bus from Fnideq. The city was an international zone from 1923 to 1956 and retains a raffish, cosmopolitan character. The Kasbah, the medina markets, and the views across to Spain from Cap Spartel are the main draws. An achievable day trip for those with an early start.

**Gibraltar** — Visible from the Ceuta waterfront on clear days, the Rock of Gibraltar is about two and a half hours by ferry to Algeciras and then by road. It shares something of Ceuta's character — a small European enclave on a strategic strait, caught between two worlds — but with a distinctly British flavour. Worth visiting for the comparison, and for the Barbary macaques.
