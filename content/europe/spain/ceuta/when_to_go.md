---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

Ceuta has a Mediterranean climate moderated by its position at the strait: mild winters, hot but rarely extreme summers, and a westerly wind that keeps things bearable even in August. Rain falls mostly between November and March; the rest of the year is reliably dry.

**Spring (March–May)** is the most pleasant time to visit. Temperatures are warm without being hot (18–24°C), the light is clear, and the walls and gardens are at their best. The streets are quiet by Spanish standards, which makes it easier to explore the fortifications.

**Summer (June–September)** is hot and busy, particularly in July and August when the Parque Marítimo fills with families and the ferry from Algeciras runs at full capacity. The heat rarely exceeds 32°C thanks to the sea breeze, but accommodation books up and the city feels crowded.

**Autumn (October–November)** is the other good window — still warm enough to sit outside (20–26°C in October), with the summer crowds gone and the sea still swimmable. Temperatures drop noticeably in November and the first autumn rains arrive.

**Winter (December–February)** is mild by northern European standards (12–16°C), but overcast and occasionally rainy. The fortifications and museum are pleasant in the quiet; the maritime park closes for the season.
