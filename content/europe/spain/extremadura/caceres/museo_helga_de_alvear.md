story: >
  Helga de Alvear grew up in Germany and moved to Madrid in the 1960s, where she became one of Spain's most significant contemporary art dealers. She began collecting in the 1980s with a focus on conceptual and minimalist work, and her collection eventually outgrew what any commercial gallery could show. The decision to build a permanent museum in Cáceres — her adopted home — rather than in Madrid was a deliberate choice to bring world-class contemporary art to a city that rarely features on the international art circuit.
---

The Museo Helga de Alvear opened in 2010 and houses one of the most important private collections of contemporary art in Europe — over 2,700 works spanning the 1950s to the present day, with particular strength in conceptual, minimalist, and video art. Artists represented include Louise Bourgeois, Gerhard Richter, Ai Weiwei, and Christian Boltanski, alongside significant holdings of Spanish contemporary work.

The building is a striking piece of contemporary architecture, designed to work alongside (rather than against) the historic character of the lower city. Inside, the galleries are well-proportioned and the hanging is intelligent — the collection rewards close attention rather than a quick walk-through.

For a city that trades primarily on its medieval heritage, the Helga de Alvear is an unexpected and impressive presence. It is the kind of museum you would make a special trip to see in Madrid; finding it in Cáceres is a genuine surprise. Combined with the medieval old town twenty minutes' walk away, it makes Cáceres one of the more rewarding places in western Spain.
