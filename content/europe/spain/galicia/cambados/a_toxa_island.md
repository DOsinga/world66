A Toxa (Isla de la Toja in Castilian) is a small pine-covered island connected to the O Grove peninsula by a short bridge. It became fashionable in the early 20th century when a thermal spring was discovered here, and the Grand Hotel La Toja — a vast Belle Époque pile that looks as though it was transported from a French seaside resort — was built to exploit it. The island has a slightly surreal quality: manicured gardens, a casino, and a hotel that seems far too large for its surroundings, all wrapped in pine forest and surrounded by the ría.

The most visited sight is the tiny **Chapel of San Caralampio**, whose exterior walls are entirely covered in scallop shells — thousands of them, cemented in overlapping rows, giving the building a texture somewhere between medieval armour and folk art. The shells are a Galician tradition and a reference to the Camino de Santiago, but the density of coverage here is extreme enough to make the chapel genuinely strange. The island is easily reached from O Grove and makes a natural combined visit.
