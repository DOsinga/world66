The Praza de Fefiñáns is the heart of Cambados and one of the most handsome squares in all of Galicia. Three sides are enclosed by the 17th-century Pazo de Fefiñáns itself — a granite manor house with corner towers, arcaded ground floor, and a monumental entrance arch that frames the view perfectly. The fourth side opens directly onto the Ría de Arousa, so on a clear day the square feels as though it is floating above the water. The effect is both grand and effortlessly natural, which is the genius of Galician baroque at its best.

The pazo was built in stages through the 17th century by the Bazán and Valladares families and has been in the same hands since the 18th century. Today it also houses the Bodegas del Pazo de Fefiñáns, one of the oldest Albariño producers in the denomination. The square fills up completely during the August wine festival, when the arcades are lined with producers and the towers are lit from below. Outside festival season it is calm and largely untouched, the perfect place to sit with a glass of wine and watch the light change over the estuary.
