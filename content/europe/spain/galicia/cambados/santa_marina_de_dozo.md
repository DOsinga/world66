story: >
  Santa Mariña was a 3rd-century Christian martyr from the Galician town of Ourense, said to have been tortured and beheaded for refusing to renounce her faith or marry a Roman official. Her legend spread across Galicia and Portugal, where she is also venerated as Santa Marina. The church built in her name at Dozo dates from the 15th century — the tower and the skeleton of Gothic arches are still standing, though the roof collapsed long ago. The site was never abandoned: the cemetery around it has been in continuous use, and the juxtaposition of medieval ruins and living memorials is entirely Galician in character.
---

Few ruins in Galicia are as quietly affecting as Santa Mariña de Dozo. The 15th-century church lost its roof centuries ago, but its Gothic arches and bell tower are largely intact, rising above a cemetery that is still actively used. Headstones and flowers fill the nave where the congregation once sat; moss and lichens cover the stone. The combination of medieval architecture and living burial ground gives the place a quality that photographs struggle to capture — it is neither morbid nor picturesque but something more unsettling and honest than either.

The site is freely accessible and a short walk from the Praza de Fefiñáns, making it an easy addition to any morning in Cambados. Go early or late in the day when the light is low and the stone turns gold. Even on summer weekends it sees far fewer visitors than it deserves, which is partly why it remains so good.
