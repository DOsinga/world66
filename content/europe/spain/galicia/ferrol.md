Ferrol is a naval city of about 65,000 people at the head of a long, deep ría on the north Galician coast, roughly 60 kilometres up the coast from [A Coruña](/europe/spain/galicia/lacoruna). Spain founded its principal Atlantic shipyards here in the mid-eighteenth century, and the city was built — quite deliberately and almost from scratch — to serve them. The result is a place that looks unlike anywhere else in Galicia: a planned Enlightenment grid of straight streets and arcaded squares laid out around the [Arsenal](/europe/spain/galicia/ferrol/arsenal_de_ferrol), with the older fishing town of Ferrol Vello tucked along the waterfront beside it.

The walkable part of town is the Barrio de la Magdalena, the eighteenth-century quarter of nine parallel streets and grand cross-axes — Real, Magdalena, María — that was conceived as a model of rational urban design and is now protected as a national heritage zone. It is small, easily covered on foot in an afternoon, with the [Co-Cathedral of San Julián](/europe/spain/galicia/ferrol/ferrol_co_cathedral) as the architectural set piece. The shipyards still dominate the western end of the city, fenced off and unvisitable in detail, but their cranes and dry-docks are visible from any high point and from the ría.

The military history continues outside the centre. The narrow mouth of the ría is guarded by the [Castle of San Felipe](/europe/spain/galicia/ferrol/castle_of_san_felipe) on one bank and matching batteries on the other, and the headland north of the city carries the abandoned coast-defence emplacements of [Cabo Prior](/europe/spain/galicia/ferrol/cabo_prior_lighthouse). Across the water, the [Monastery of Santa Catalina](/europe/spain/galicia/ferrol/monastery_of_santa_catalina_de_montefaro) sits high on the Montefaro peninsula. Ferrol is also the starting point of the Camino Inglés, the shortest of the official routes to [Santiago de Compostela](/europe/spain/galicia/santiagodecompostela) — pilgrims set off from the Co-Cathedral.

Ferrol is not a beach city, but the best Atlantic beaches in the area — Praia de Doniños, San Jorge, Ponzos — are all within 15 minutes by car on the open coast west of town. Most visitors come for a day, often as a side trip from A Coruña. It rewards an overnight if you like working ports and serious naval architecture.
