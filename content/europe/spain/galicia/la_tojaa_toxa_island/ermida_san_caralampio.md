The Ermida de San Caralampio is a small Romanesque chapel tucked into the pine trees of A Toxa Island, a short walk from the bridge connecting the island to O Grove. Its date is given as 12th century, though the building you see today is heavily restored — what makes it extraordinary is not the architecture but the covering: every centimetre of exterior wall is encrusted with scallop shells, cemented in overlapping rows, giving the building a texture that is simultaneously medieval and strange.

The tradition began as a votive offering by Galician fishermen, the scallop shell being the symbol of the Camino de Santiago and a marker of Galician identity. Nailing a shell to the chapel wall was a thanksgiving for a good catch or a safe return. The practice became so established that the chapel is now maintained in its fully-shelled state. Close up, the texture is more interesting than it appears in photographs: each shell sits at a slightly different angle, the whole surface rippling in light. The interior is tiny and simple, with a plain altar; almost all the meaning is on the outside.
