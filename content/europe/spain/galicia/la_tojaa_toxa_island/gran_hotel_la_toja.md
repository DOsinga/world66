The Gran Hotel La Toja is a vast Belle Époque hotel that occupies the southern end of the island, its ornate facade completely at odds with the surrounding pine forest and fishing ría. Built in 1907 to exploit the island's thermal springs, it became one of the fashionable resorts of early 20th-century Spain — the kind of place where politicians, industrialists, and the royal family came to take the waters and be seen doing so.

The hotel is still operating and still distinctly grand, though it has aged from exclusive to merely expensive. Non-guests can walk the grounds freely, sit on the terrace for a coffee or glass of wine, and appreciate the incongruity of the setting: a palatial hotel marooned in a pine forest, surrounded by water, reached by a bridge from a small fishing town. The spa facilities are open to day visitors for a fee. A smaller casino and a golf course complete the island's resort infrastructure.
