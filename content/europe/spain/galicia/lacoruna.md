La Coruña (A Coruña in Galician) sits on a rocky peninsula in the far northwest of [Spain](/europe/spain), facing the Atlantic. The city's signature feature is the [Torre de Hércules](/europe/spain/galicia/lacoruna/torre_de_hercules), a Roman lighthouse that has been guiding ships for nearly 2,000 years — the oldest working lighthouse in the world, and a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

The old centre is compact and worth a slow walk: narrow medieval streets, Romanesque churches including the [Church of Santiago](/europe/spain/galicia/lacoruna/church_of_santiago), and a cluster of striking Modernist buildings from the late 19th century, when the city prospered. Picasso spent his childhood here (his father taught at the art school), and the [Casa Museo Picasso](/europe/spain/galicia/lacoruna/casa_museo_picasso) on Calle Payo Gómez preserves the connection. The [Paseo Marítimo](/europe/spain/galicia/lacoruna/paseo_maritimo), one of the longest urban promenades in Europe, wraps around the peninsula with views of the harbour and the open sea.

The city's science museums — the [Domus](/europe/spain/galicia/lacoruna/domus_museum), [Aquarium Finisterrae](/europe/spain/galicia/lacoruna/aquarium_finisterrae), and [Casa de las Ciencias](/europe/spain/galicia/lacoruna/casa_de_las_ciencias) — form one of the better museum complexes in the northwest. The [Plaza de María Pita](/europe/spain/galicia/lacoruna/plaza_de_maria_pita) at the heart of the old town is the place to sit with a glass of Galician white wine and watch the city go about its evening.

La Coruña makes an excellent base for exploring [Galicia](/europe/spain/galicia). The Rías Altas — deep inlets north of the city — are dramatic and uncrowded. The **Costa da Morte** (Death Coast) to the west is wild, windswept and beautiful, named for the shipwrecks that littered its rocks over the centuries.
