---
title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

Lugo runs on the Galician tapas custom: in virtually every bar in the old town, a drink comes with a free tapa, served without asking. The portions are generous by any standard — a croquette, a slice of empanada, a piece of grilled meat, or a small plate of pulpo á feira (octopus with paprika and olive oil on potato). The concentration of bars around [Praza do Campo](/europe/spain/galicia/lugo/praza_do_campo), Rúa da Cruz and Rúa Nova means you can move between five or six places in an evening and eat well without ever ordering food.

The local wine is Ribeiro — a light, slightly fizzy white from the Galician interior — though the Mencía reds from the Ribeira Sacra valley a short drive south are increasingly popular and worth seeking out. Pulpo á feira is the classic local dish; Lugo claims to make the definitive version.
