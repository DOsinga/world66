Monterrei is a wine-producing comarca in the south-east corner of Galicia's Ourense province, right on the Portuguese border. The Valley of Monterrei, watered by the River Tamega and its tributaries, covers the municipalities of Verín, Monterrei, Oímbra, and Castrelo do Val. The valley is one of the warmest and driest corners of Galicia — sheltered from Atlantic rain by mountains to the west — and the light and warmth produce wines that stand apart from the Rías Baixas style.

The [DO Monterrei wineries](/europe/spain/galicia/monterrei/godello_wine) produce whites dominated by Godello and Treixadura — fuller-bodied and more mineral than Albariño, with good ageing potential — and reds from Mencía. The quality has risen considerably in recent years. The [Castle of Monterrei](/europe/spain/galicia/monterrei/castle_monterrei), perched above the valley on a fortified hilltop, is the most impressive medieval complex in the region: three rings of walls enclosing a keep, a 13th-century church, and a Renaissance palace now operating as a Parador hotel.

The [thermal springs](/europe/spain/galicia/monterrei/thermal_springs) at Cabreiroá and Sousas have been drawing visitors since Roman times, and the town of Verín has a tradition of bathing culture that makes a spa stop a natural complement to a wine tasting and a castle visit. This is a valley that rewards a slow couple of days.
