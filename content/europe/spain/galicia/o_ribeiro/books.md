**The Pilgrimage** — Paulo Coelho. Not set in O Ribeiro, but the Camino de Santiago passes through Galicia and Coelho's account of walking it captures the spiritual geography of the region — the granite, the rain, the ancient roads — with the enthusiasm of a first encounter. It will make the landscape feel charged.

**Ramón del Valle-Inclán, *Sonatas*.** Valle-Inclán was born in the Galician province of Pontevedra and these four novellas — lyrical, decadent, deeply strange — are saturated in the atmosphere of inland Galicia: its crumbling pazos, its noblemen gone to ruin, its long memories. No other writer catches the tone of the Galician interior as precisely.

**Manuel Rivas, *The Carpenter's Pencil*.** A Galician journalist and novelist, Rivas set this novel in the aftermath of the Spanish Civil War. It is a quiet, elliptical book about violence, memory, and survival in a small Galician city. Reading it before visiting gives a sense of the weight of recent history that the region still carries.
