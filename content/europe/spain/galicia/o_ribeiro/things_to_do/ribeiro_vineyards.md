The landscape of O Ribeiro is its own attraction. The vineyards climb steep granite slopes above the Miño, Avia, and Arnoia river valleys in small terraced plots — many of them worked by hand because machinery cannot reach them. The vines are mostly trained low on pergola systems, and in summer the canopy of green is dense and cooling.

The DO covers 1,281 hectares across nine municipalities. Driving the back roads between Ribadavia and villages like Beade, Cenlle, and Carballeda de Avia takes you through some of the most quietly beautiful wine country in Spain — no crowds, no grand châteaux, just working farms and the sound of water.

The grape varieties are almost entirely indigenous: Treixadura and Torrontés dominate the whites, Caíño Tinto and Sousón the reds. These are old varieties that have been grown here for centuries and are rarely found anywhere else. A visit to any local winery will demonstrate why they have survived.
