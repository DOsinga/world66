The Castillo de los Templarios dominates the hill above the confluence of the Sil and Boeza rivers, its walls and towers forming one of the most dramatic medieval silhouettes in northern Spain. The castle was built and expanded by the Knights Templar between the 12th and 14th centuries as a way-station and fortress protecting the pilgrimage route to Santiago de Compostela. Walking through the gate towers, along the battlements, and into the inner courts gives you a genuine sense of its scale — this was a full functioning town within walls, not just a watchtower.

Inside, the castle houses a small museum with exhibits on the Templar order, medieval pilgrimage, and local archaeology. The upper battlements give sweeping views across the Bierzo valley and the old quarter of Ponferrada below. Allow two hours at least. The castle is open most days; entry is a few euros and worth every cent.
