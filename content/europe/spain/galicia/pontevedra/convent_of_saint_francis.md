Founded in the 13th century by the Franciscan order, this convent and its church stand near the old riverbank at the edge of Pontevedra's historic quarter. The exterior mixes Romanesque and Gothic elements, and the interior is one of the more atmospheric religious spaces in a city not short of them.

The church is famous for a remarkable medieval tomb — traditionally attributed to Paio Gomes Charinho, admiral to Alfonso X and one of the poets of the Galician-Portuguese lyric tradition. The carved sarcophagus, with its nautical imagery, is worth the detour even if you have no particular interest in medieval history. The convent buildings are not open to visitors, but the church is accessible during normal hours.
