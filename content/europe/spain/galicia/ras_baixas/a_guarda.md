A Guarda sits at the very southwestern corner of Galicia, where the river Miño
meets the Atlantic and the Portuguese border begins. It's a working fishing
town, famous in Spain for its lobster (the Festa da Lagosta is held every
July), with a small but lively harbour and a string of seafood restaurants
along the quayside.

The reason most visitors come is the Castro de Santa Trega, a Celtic
hill-fort excavated on the steep hill that rises behind the town. The
foundations of more than a hundred round stone houses are visible, and the
views from the summit stretch over the Miño estuary into northern Portugal —
worth the steep climb on a clear day.
