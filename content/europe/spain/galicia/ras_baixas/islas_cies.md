The Islas Cíes form part of the Atlantic Islands of Galicia National Park —
a low archipelago at the mouth of the Ría de Vigo, with crescent-shaped white
sand beaches, clear turquoise water, and a permanent population of zero. The
islands are uninhabited, undeveloped, and limited to a fixed number of day
visitors. There is one small campsite and a handful of restaurants near the
ferry dock.

Ferries leave from Vigo, Cangas, and Baiona between mid-spring and early
autumn. Allow a full day — go early, walk to the Faro lighthouse for the
panoramic view, and spend the afternoon on Praia de Rodas. Book the ferry and
permit well in advance; July and August fill weeks ahead.
