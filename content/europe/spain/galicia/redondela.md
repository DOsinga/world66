Redondela is a small town on the inner shore of the Ría de Vigo, in the southwest corner of Galicia. It is best known for its dramatic railway viaducts — the [Viaduct of Madrid](/europe/spain/galicia/redondela/viaduct_de_madrid) and the [Viaduct of Pontevedra](/europe/spain/galicia/redondela/viaduct_de_pontevedra), two 19th-century stone arched bridges that carry the main Vigo–Ourense line high above the town. Their reflection in the ría below is a striking image, and the sound of a train crossing overhead from the streets below is one of those small, memorable travel moments.

The town sits on the [Camino Portugués](/europe/spain/galicia/redondela/camino_portugues), the Portuguese route of the Camino de Santiago, and pilgrim traffic passes through year-round. The Festa da Coca in late June — a midsummer celebration involving a dragon effigy parading through the streets — is one of the livelier local festivals in the province. The [Colexiata de Santiago](/europe/spain/galicia/redondela/colexiata_santiago) at the heart of the old town is the pilgrims' traditional stop.

The ría coast here is mussel and clam country. The *choco* (small cuttlefish) is the local specialty and the subject of its own spring gastronomy festival. Just offshore lies the [Isla de San Simón](/europe/spain/galicia/redondela/isla_san_simon), a small island with a layered history — monastery, quarantine station, Civil War prison — now a protected natural area you can visit by boat in summer. The [historic cemetery of Os Eidos](/europe/spain/galicia/redondela/cemiterio_os_eidos), up on the hill above town, has been voted the most beautiful cemetery in Spain and is worth the short walk. Redondela is a short train or bus ride from [Vigo](/europe/spain/galicia/vigo) and makes a very good day trip.
