The Ribeira Sacra is where the Sil and Miño rivers have carved deep gorges through the hills of inland [Galicia](/europe/spain/galicia). Steep canyon walls drop hundreds of metres to green water, terraced vineyards cling to impossible slopes, and medieval monasteries sit on the high ground above — the "sacred banks" that give the region its name.

The wine is reason enough to visit. The Ribeira Sacra DO vineyards are among the steepest in Europe, worked mostly by hand, producing fresh Mencía reds and mineral Godello whites. But the landscape is the real draw: take the [Sil canyon catamaran](/europe/spain/galicia/ribeira_sacra/sil_canyon_catamaran) through the gorge, drive the narrow rim roads between viewpoints at [Cabezoas](/europe/spain/galicia/ribeira_sacra/viewpoint_cabezoas) and Balcones de Madrid, or walk down through chestnut forest to the [Mosteiro de Santa Cristina de Ribas de Sil](/europe/spain/galicia/ribeira_sacra/mosteiro_santa_cristina).

[Monforte de Lemos](/europe/spain/galicia/ribeira_sacra/monforte_de_lemos) is the main town and a good base — it has a hilltop castle, a parador in a converted monastery, and a Flemish art collection that surprises most visitors. The region is best reached by car from [Ourense](/europe/spain/galicia/ourense) (45 minutes) or Santiago de Compostela (90 minutes).
