The Sea Museum occupies a former slaughterhouse and the ruins of a canning factory on the waterfront at Alcabre — a fitting setting for a museum dedicated to Vigo's relationship with the Atlantic. The permanent exhibition traces the city's maritime history, with a particular focus on the Battle of Rande (1702), when a Spanish treasure fleet was sunk in the ría.

The museum also covers the development of Galicia's fishing industry — the sardine canneries that once employed much of the local population — and includes an aquarium section. The building itself is worth the visit: the ruins of the old canning factory have been integrated into the architecture, with the new glass and concrete structure rising alongside the original stone walls. Alcabre is southwest of the city centre; buses run there from the port.
