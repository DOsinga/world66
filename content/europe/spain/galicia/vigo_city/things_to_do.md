Vigo rewards exploration. The old [Berbés quarter](/europe/spain/galicia/vigo_city/berbes_quarter) is the natural starting point, built around the [Pedra oyster market](/europe/spain/galicia/vigo_city/pedra_oyster_market) where women have shucked oysters at stone counters for generations. From there, [O Castro hill](/europe/spain/galicia/vigo_city/o_castro_vantage_point) rises above the centre with the best views over the ría. The [Quiñones de León Museum](/europe/spain/galicia/vigo_city/municipal_museum) in Castrelos Park anchors the cultural side of the city.

On a clear day, the [Cíes Islands](/europe/spain/galicia/vigo_city/cies_islands) are visible from shore — three granite islands at the mouth of the ría with some of the best beaches in Spain. A summer ferry from the harbour gets you there in under an hour. The [Jules Verne monument](/europe/spain/galicia/vigo_city/jules_verne_monument) near [Porta do Sol](/europe/spain/galicia/vigo_city/porta_do_sol) marks the city's literary connection — the 1702 Battle of [Rande](/europe/spain/galicia/vigo_city/rande_bridge) inspired a key scene in Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Sea.
