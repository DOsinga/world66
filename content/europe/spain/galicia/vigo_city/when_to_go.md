---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

The Atlantic climate keeps Vigo mild year-round but means that rain can arrive at any time. Summers are warm and generally dry — July and August bring the best weather for the Cíes Islands and outdoor eating. The tourist season is concentrated in these months; the islands especially get very busy and visitor numbers are capped by permit.

Spring and autumn are less crowded and the light has a particular quality in September and October — warm enough to sit outside, cool enough to walk comfortably. The sea temperature peaks in August and remains reasonable through September. Winter is mild but wet; the Berbés bars and restaurants fill up with locals and the city feels genuinely lived-in.

The Festa da Reconquista in late March commemorates the expulsion of French troops in 1809 and involves a large historical re-enactment through the old town — worth catching if you're in the area. The city's main summer festival runs in July around the feast of María Auxiliadora, the patroness of the Cíes Islands.
