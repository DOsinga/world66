Albacete is the biggest city in **Castilla-La Mancha**, flat on the sun-baked plains of the region made famous by Cervantes' Don Quixote, halfway between Madrid and Alicante on the high-speed AVE train line. It has none of the picturesque Castilian charm of its smaller neighbours — no medieval centre, no dramatic hilltop fortress — but a handful of handsome 19th-century buildings, wide pedestrian streets, and the excellent [Museum of Albacete](/europe/spain/la_mancha/albacete/museo_provincial) whose collection of Iberian stone sphinxes (the 4th-century BC **Cerrillo Blanco Bulls**) and a complete set of Roman dolls from a child's grave are worth the stop.

Two things the city is genuinely famous for across Spain: **navajas**, the folding pocket knives that have been made here since the 16th century (there is a small knife museum — the [Museo de la Cuchillería](/europe/spain/la_mancha/albacete/museo_de_la_cuchilleria) — in a striking neo-Gothic mansion on the main square), and the annual **Feria de Albacete** in September, one of the oldest and most traditional fairs in Spain, with ten days of bullfights, horse shows, flamenco and the famous Gothic-style fairground [recinto](/europe/spain/la_mancha/albacete/recinto_ferial).

The walk through the centre takes in [Plaza del Altozano](/europe/spain/la_mancha/albacete/plaza_del_altozano), the Italianate glass-roofed [Pasaje de Lodares](/europe/spain/la_mancha/albacete/pasaje_de_lodares), the 16th-century [Cathedral of San Juan Bautista](/europe/spain/la_mancha/albacete/catedral), and the green [Parque Abelardo Sánchez](/europe/spain/la_mancha/albacete/parque_abelardo_sanchez) where the provincial museum sits.
