Twelve restored 16th-century **molinos de viento** stand in a perfect line along the **Cerro Calderico** ridge above the town — white-washed cylindrical towers with conical wooden roofs and four canvas-covered sails. Eleven still have working machinery and several are open to visitors as small museums (one houses the **Casa de los Molineros** with displays on the milling craft, another the tourist office).

Drive or walk up to the ridge for the view; the climb on foot from the town centre takes about 20 minutes. The light is best in late afternoon, when the mills throw long shadows across the plain. Each mill has a name — Bolero, Cardeño, Caballero del Verde Gabán — drawn from local tradition and Cervantes.
