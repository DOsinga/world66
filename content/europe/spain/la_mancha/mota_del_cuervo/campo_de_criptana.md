Campo de Criptana, about 30 km southwest of Mota del Cuervo, has the largest and most famous cluster of La Mancha windmills after Consuegra. Ten mills crown a low ridge above the white-walled town, their sails turning when the wind is right, their reflections visible from the main street below. Three of the mills are believed to date from the sixteenth century and are among the oldest surviving examples in Spain; the rest are eighteenth-century reconstructions, though only a specialist would notice the difference from the outside.

The town of Campo de Criptana itself is worth a walk: the old quarter climbs the hill below the mills through a tangle of narrow streets and whitewashed houses typical of La Mancha at its most photogenic. The flamenco dancer Sara Montiel was born here, and the town celebrates her memory with a small museum. The combination of windmills and old town makes Campo de Criptana a more satisfying stop than Consuegra, which has fewer mills and less character in the town below.
