The Cathedral of Sigüenza is an imposing Romanesque-Gothic fortress-church that took several centuries to build, beginning in 1130 when the bishops of Sigüenza established their seat here. The exterior is massive and military in character — thick walls, square towers, and very little ornament on the north face, which was meant to defend as much as to impress. The south facade is more decorative, with a Romanesque portal of considerable quality.

The interior is much richer. The nave is broadly Romanesque in proportions but Gothic in its upper sections; the decorative programme accumulates over the centuries into something remarkable. The tombs of the bishops of Sigüenza line the chapels, each a chapter of the history of Spanish funerary art. The sacristy has an extraordinary coffered ceiling of painted wood, and the Capilla del Doncel contains the tomb of Martín Vázquez de Arce. The cathedral treasury holds vestments, reliquaries, and liturgical objects accumulated over seven centuries of episcopal use. Guided tours in Spanish depart several times daily.
