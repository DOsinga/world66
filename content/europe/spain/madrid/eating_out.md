---
title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

Madrid's cooking is winter food in a high-altitude city — heavy, meaty, slow-cooked stews built around legumes, offal, and roast lamb. The signature dishes are **cocido madrileño** (a three-course chickpea-and-pork stew traditionally served on Tuesdays), **callos a la madrileña** (tripe stew with chorizo and morcilla), and **bocadillo de calamares** (deep-fried squid in a baguette, sold from any bar around Plaza Mayor).

The eating geography is straightforward. **Cava Baja** in La Latina is the great tapas street — twenty-odd bars in two hundred metres, all good. **Mercado de San Miguel** by Plaza Mayor is a beautiful but touristy gourmet market for oysters and croquetas. The neighbourhood markets of **Mercado de San Antón** (Chueca) and **Mercado de la Cebada** (La Latina) are cheaper and more local. For the city's oldest restaurant try **Sobrino de Botín**, in business since 1725 — the suckling pig is the dish to order.

Eat late: lunch starts at 2 pm, dinner not before 9, and most restaurants do not really fill until 10 or 11. The menú del día — a fixed-price three-course lunch — is the bargain everywhere, usually €12–€18 with wine included.
