The old medieval quarter south-west of Plaza Mayor, narrow-streeted, scattered with small plazas and tucked-away churches, and the centre of Madrid's Sunday tapas culture. The neighbourhood gets its name from a long-vanished hospital founded by the 15th-century scholar Beatriz Galindo, nicknamed La Latina for her command of the language.

Three things to do here: walk the back streets around **Plaza de la Paja**, **Plaza de San Andrés**, and **Plaza de la Cebada** for some of the most atmospheric townscape in central Madrid; eat your way along **Cava Baja** (twenty-odd tapas bars in a single street); and join the Sunday morning **El Rastro** flea market that sprawls across the southern end of the neighbourhood. The basilica of **San Francisco el Grande** and the church of **San Andrés** are both worth ducking into. Metro La Latina or Tirso de Molina.
