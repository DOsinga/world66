Madrid's most genuinely multicultural neighbourhood, spread across the slopes south of the Rastro flea market and east of La Latina. Lavapiés has been the city's immigrant quarter for centuries — first Jewish, then later absorbing successive waves from North Africa, South Asia, China, and Latin America. The result is a dense, energetic mix of tablaos, Indian restaurants, Bangladeshi grocers, and old-school Spanish bodegas all sharing the same steep, narrow streets.

The neighbourhood centres on **Plaza de Lavapiés**, a sunny square flanked by a mix of old pensiones and new bars. The surrounding streets — Calle de la Fe, Calle del Olivar, Calle de Argumosa — are where young Madrileños come to find cheap drinks and tables on the pavement until late. The **Centro de Arte Reina Sofía** anchors the northern edge, and the **Filmoteca Española** on Calle Santa Isabel screens classic and international films almost nightly.
