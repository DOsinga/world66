The private collection of financier-collector José Lázaro Galdiano, housed in the early-20th-century palace he built for it on Calle Serrano. Spread across four floors are some 12,000 pieces — paintings (Goya, Bosch, El Greco, Velázquez, Murillo, Constable), Renaissance bronzes, medieval ivories, Islamic ceramics, Italian majolica, and a famously good collection of jewellery and small metalwork.

It is one of Madrid's most rewarding small museums, and almost never crowded. The Goya rooms include the witches' Sabbath painting **Witches' Flight** and the unsettling early **Aquelarre**. The palace interior — with painted ceilings, French marble, and gilded woodwork — is itself a primary exhibit. Open every day except Tuesdays; free Sunday afternoons. Allow two hours. Metro Núñez de Balboa or Rubén Darío.
