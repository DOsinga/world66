The central square of Malasaña and the neighbourhood's symbolic heart, named for the uprising of 2 May 1808 against Napoleon's occupying forces — the event that kicked off the Peninsular War and is commemorated in Goya's paintings at the Prado. A ruined arch and artillery pieces in the square mark the barracks that stood here at the time.

Today the plaza is a neighborhood meeting point: benches packed with young people on warm evenings, a children's playground on one side, surrounded by terrace bars. On 2 May each year the city holds celebrations here. The neighbourhood pubs and bars on the surrounding streets are among the most concentrated in central Madrid. Metro Tribunal.
