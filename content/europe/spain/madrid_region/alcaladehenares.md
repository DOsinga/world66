Alcalá de Henares is a university city 35 kilometres east of Madrid, and one of the best day trips from the capital. Founded as the Roman city of Complutum and later a medieval Moorish town, it reached its golden age when Cardinal Cisneros established the [Complutense University](/europe/spain/madrid_region/alcaladehenares/university_of_alcala) here in 1499 — making it one of the first planned university cities in the world. The entire historic centre is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

The city is famous as the birthplace of Miguel de Cervantes, author of Don Quixote. His family home on Calle Mayor, now the [Casa Natal de Cervantes](/europe/spain/madrid_region/alcaladehenares/cervantes_birthplace), has been turned into a museum that reconstructs a 16th-century household with period furniture and explanatory exhibits. Nearby, the [Renaissance university building](/europe/spain/madrid_region/alcaladehenares/university_of_alcala) itself is worth visiting for its ornate Plateresque facade and the Paraninfo auditorium where the Cervantes Prize — Spain's most prestigious literary award — is handed out each year by the King.

The [Archbishop's Palace](/europe/spain/madrid_region/alcaladehenares/archbishops_palace) is where Catherine of Aragon was born and where Columbus met Ferdinand and Isabella to plan his second voyage. The [Magistral Cathedral](/europe/spain/madrid_region/alcaladehenares/magistral_cathedral) and the [Corral de Comedias](/europe/spain/madrid_region/alcaladehenares/corral_de_comedias) — the oldest documented theatre in Spain — complete the historic centre's highlights. Alcalá is compact and easily walkable; trains from Madrid Atocha run every 15–20 minutes and take about 45 minutes.
