Melilla is one of two Spanish enclaves on the coast of [Morocco](/africa/morocco), a small autonomous city that has been in Spanish hands since 1497. It sits on the eastern edge of the Rif mountains, facing the Mediterranean.

The old fortress, [Melilla la Vieja](/europe/spain/melilla/melilla_la_vieja), is the main draw — a walled citadel perched on a rocky peninsula with views across the sea toward [Málaga](/europe/spain/andalucia/malaga). Inside its walls you will find the [Museo Municipal](/europe/spain/melilla/museo_municipal) and a handful of small churches. The modern town beyond the walls has an unexpected concentration of [Modernista architecture](/europe/spain/melilla/modernista_architecture), much of it designed by Enrique Nieto, a disciple of Gaudí.

Melilla is also a convenient entry point into Morocco: the border crossing at Beni Enzar is walkable from the city centre, and ferries run to Málaga and Almería.
