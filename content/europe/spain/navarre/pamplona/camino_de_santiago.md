Pamplona is one of the great waypoints on the Camino Francés, the most-walked route of the Camino de Santiago. Pilgrims cross the Pyrenees from Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port, descend to Pamplona through the ancient gate of the Puerta de Francia, and cross the old quarter before continuing west toward [Burgos](/europe/spain/castileandleon/burgos) and ultimately Santiago de Compostela.

The scallop shell waymarks are visible throughout the old town. The municipal pilgrims' hostel on Calle de la Navarrería is one of the most-used on the entire route. Even if you are not walking the Camino, it is worth following the marked trail through the old town — it passes the best buildings and gives a sense of the route that pilgrims have taken for more than a thousand years. The city has a deep hospitality culture built up over centuries of welcoming those on foot.
