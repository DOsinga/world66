La Coruña is a port city on the Atlantic coast of [Galicia](/europe/spain/galicia), built on a peninsula that juts into the sea. The old town clusters around the harbour, while the newer city stretches along a magnificent waterfront promenade — the [Paseo Marítimo](/europe/spain/northernspain/lacorua/paseo_maritimo) — that runs for 13 km around the headland, making it one of the longest seaside walks in Europe. The city has a confident, cosmopolitan feel that sets it apart from quieter Galician towns.

The [Torre de Hércules](/europe/spain/northernspain/lacorua/torre_de_hercules), a Roman lighthouse that has been in continuous use for nearly 2,000 years, stands on the northern tip of the peninsula and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The old town, known as the [Ciudad Vieja](/europe/spain/northernspain/lacorua/ciudad_vieja), is a compact tangle of medieval streets around the Romanesque churches of Santiago and Santa María del Campo. On the waterfront, the [Domus museum](/europe/spain/northernspain/lacorua/domus_museum) and the [Aquarium Finisterrae](/europe/spain/northernspain/lacorua/aquarium_finisterrae) together make up a good science complex within an easy walk of the Torre.

La Coruña is a great eating city. The streets around [Plaza de María Pita](/europe/spain/northernspain/lacorua/placa_de_maria_pita) are lined with tapas bars and restaurants serving fresh Galician seafood — percebes (goose barnacles), pulpo a feira (octopus), and whatever came off the boats that morning. The [Playa del Orzán and Playa de Riazor](/europe/spain/northernspain/lacorua/playa_de_riazor), right in the city centre, are popular surf beaches when the Atlantic swell picks up. The city is also the headquarters of Inditex, the company behind Zara, which has brought a certain commercial energy to the place.
