The Torre de Hércules stands on the northern tip of the La Coruña peninsula, guiding ships into the harbour just as it did when Roman engineers built it in the second century AD. It is the oldest working lighthouse in the world and a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2009.

The climb to the top — 242 steps — is rewarded with extraordinary views over the Atlantic coast in both directions. On a clear day you can see the rugged headlands stretching north toward Cape Prior. The lighthouse grounds below have been landscaped with sculptures and walking paths; the grassy slopes around the base are popular in summer for picnics.

The surrounding area has archaeological remains from the Roman and later periods, including a Celtic castro site nearby. The visitor centre at the base explains the lighthouse's history well. Open year-round; queues can form in summer, especially on weekends.
