On the northern edge of Laguardia, the archaeological site of La Hoya preserves the remains of a Celtic settlement occupied from the Bronze Age through the Iron Age and into the early Roman period. Excavations have uncovered street grids, domestic buildings, and a remarkable quantity of ceramics and metalwork. The site was destroyed by fire around the second century BCE — possibly a Roman military action — and the preserved layers of ash have kept the material record unusually intact.

A small site museum presents the finds from the excavations and explains the daily life of the Iron Age inhabitants of the Ebro valley. Opening times are limited; check with the Laguardia tourist office before making the trip. The ruins themselves can be viewed from a walkway without entering the museum.
