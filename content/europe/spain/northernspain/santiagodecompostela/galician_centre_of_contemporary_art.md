The Galician Centre of Contemporary Art, usually shortened to CGAC, sits on the edge of Santiago's old town beside the convent and gardens of San Domingos de Bonaval. It is the city's strongest stop for contemporary art, with exhibitions that focus on Galician, Spanish, and international work rather than Camino history or cathedral culture.

The building is part of the reason to go. Portuguese architect Alvaro Siza gave it clean granite lines, controlled light, and terraces that open toward the city and the park. Pair it with the neighbouring Museum of the Galician People if you want a half-day around Bonaval, moving from traditional Galician culture into the region's modern artistic conversation.
