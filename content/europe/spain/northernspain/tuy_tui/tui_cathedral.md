story: "The cathedral was built to function as a fortress as much as a church: its towers were designed as lookout points, its walls were thick enough to withstand artillery, and there are gun slits in some sections. For centuries the town alternated between Galician and Portuguese control, and the cathedral was at the centre of the fighting each time. The cloister was only completed in the 16th century, after the border was finally stabilised."
---

The Cathedral of Tui is one of the most distinctive buildings in Galicia — a fortified Romanesque-Gothic church perched on the hill at the top of the old town, its towers giving commanding views over the Miño valley and across the border to Portugal. Construction began in the 12th century and continued for several hundred years, so the building blends Romanesque stonework in the main structure with Gothic additions in the cloister and upper sections.

The interior is impressive: a long nave with good Romanesque carving, a Renaissance cloister, and various chapels added over the centuries. The diocesan museum in the cloister has a collection of religious art and artifacts. But the real reason to visit is the exterior and the views — circle the building and you can see the bridge to Portugal, the walled town of Valença, and the broad sweep of the Miño below.
