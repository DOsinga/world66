Plaza de la Virgen Blanca is the focal point of Vitoria-Gasteiz, a wide square at the base of the medieval hill flanked by arcaded 18th- and 19th-century buildings with distinctive painted balconies. At one end stands the Church of San Miguel, the Gothic church from whose tower the Celedón figure descends during the August fiestas. At the centre of the square is a monument to the Battle of Vitoria (1813), at which Wellington defeated Napoleon's forces and effectively ended French occupation of the Iberian Peninsula.

The square opens onto Los Arquillos on one side and the newer Plaza de España on the other. It is the natural place to start and end any walk through the city, and the bars under the arcades are good for a pre-dinner txakoli.
