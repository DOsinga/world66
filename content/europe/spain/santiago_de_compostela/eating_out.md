---
title: Eating Out
type: section
---

Santiago eats well. The city sits at the confluence of Galicia's two great food traditions — the sea and the interior — and the old town is dense with restaurants that take both seriously. The Rúa do Franco and Rúa da Raíña are the main restaurant streets, running south from the Cathedral toward the market, and while some places here have become tourist traps, the better ones remain excellent.

The dishes to seek out: **pulpo a feira** (octopus dressed with olive oil, coarse salt, and sweet paprika, served on wooden platters); **empanada** (filled pastry, usually tuna or pork, sold by the slice in bakeries and sit-down restaurants alike); **pimientos de Padrón** (small green peppers, most mild but occasionally searing, blistered in oil); and any shellfish from the Galician rias. **Tarta de Santiago** — an almond cake marked with the cross of Saint James — is sold everywhere and is genuinely good when made properly.

Albariño from the Rías Baixas is the wine of choice. Ask for it local if you want something interesting.
