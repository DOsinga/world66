---
title: Getting There
type: section
---

Santiago de Compostela is reasonably well connected for a city of its size, partly because of its status as a pilgrimage destination.

**By air:** Santiago de Compostela Airport (SCQ), about 12 kilometres northeast of the city, has direct connections to Madrid, Barcelona, and several European cities. A bus service runs between the airport and the city centre.

**By train:** The railway station is about 1 kilometre south of the old town — walkable with light luggage, or a short taxi ride. High-speed AVE trains connect Santiago with Madrid (around 2h 30m) and Ourense; regional trains serve the rest of Galicia including Vigo, A Coruña, and Pontevedra. The train from Madrid is the most comfortable option if you are arriving from the capital.

**By bus:** ALSA runs coaches from Madrid and other major cities. The bus station is near the train station, south of the centre.

**On foot:** The vast majority of Camino pilgrims arrive on foot, typically from the west along the Camino Francés. The final stage from O Pedrouzo (20 km) is the most walked stretch of any long-distance route in Europe. If you arrive on the Camino, the route leads directly into the old town and to the Cathedral square.
