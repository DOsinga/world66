Alicante (Valencian: Alacant) is the main city of the Costa Blanca, a busy port and regional capital on Spain's southeastern coast with a genuinely attractive old city behind the tourist facade. The town climbs steeply from the waterfront promenade up to the enormous [Santa Bàrbara Castle](/europe/spain/spanishcostas/alicante/santa_barbara_castle) on Mount Benacantil — that castle, more than anything, is what gives Alicante its distinctive skyline and explains why the city has been fought over for centuries.

The [Explanada de España](/europe/spain/spanishcostas/alicante/explanada_de_espana), with its wave-patterned marble pavement and double row of palms, is the social heart of the city. Below it, the port handles ferries to Ibiza, Formentera, and the Balearics; above it, the streets of El Barrio fill with restaurants in the evenings. The nearby areas of Upper Marina — with the towns of Jávea, Teulada, Benitachell and Calpe — and Lower Marina — including [Benidorm](/europe/spain/spanishcostas/benidorm) and beautiful [Altea](/europe/spain/spanishcostas/altea) — offer more beach options.

The [MARQ archaeological museum](/europe/spain/spanishcostas/alicante/marq) is one of the best in Spain and well worth a morning; the Roman ruins of [Lucentum](/europe/spain/spanishcostas/alicante/lucentum) on the edge of town are a worthwhile extension. The [Basilica of Santa Maria](/europe/spain/spanishcostas/alicante/basilica_santa_maria) and the [MACA contemporary art museum](/europe/spain/spanishcostas/alicante/maca) occupy the same medieval neighbourhood at the foot of the castle. In late June, the city hosts the [Hogueras de Alicante](/europe/spain/spanishcostas/alicante/hogueras_de_alicante) — one of Spain's great summer festivals, ending in the burning of elaborate papier-mâché sculptures.
