Altea is the prettiest town on the Costa Blanca, and one of the few places along this stretch of coast that hasn't been swallowed by resort development. The [Casco Antiguo](/europe/spain/spanishcostas/altea/casco_antiguo) sits on a hill above the bay, its whitewashed lanes climbing to the [Iglesia de Nuestra Señora del Consuelo](/europe/spain/spanishcostas/altea/iglesia_de_nuestra_senora_del_consuelo), a church with glazed blue-tiled domes that have become the symbol of the town. From the church terrace you look down over terracotta rooftops, a marina, and a long pebble beach curving around the bay.

The town has always attracted artists — there is a higher concentration of galleries and studios here than anywhere else on the Costa Blanca. This gives the old quarter a slightly bohemian character that contrasts pleasantly with the surrounding package-holiday belt. The [Tuesday market](/europe/spain/spanishcostas/altea/altea_market) runs weekly on Carrer de la Filarmónica, and craft stalls fill the square outside the church on summer evenings.

Below the hill, the new town stretches along the seafront with a promenade, seafood restaurants, and an easy, unhurried atmosphere. Altea is quieter than [Benidorm](/europe/spain/spanishcostas/benidorm) (just 10 km south) by design: the town has largely resisted high-rise development and keeps its scale human. To the south, the limestone headland of [Serra Gelada](/europe/spain/spanishcostas/altea/serra_gelada) separates Altea's bay from Benidorm and offers good walking and sea-cave kayaking. Three kilometres north, the [Marina Greenwich](/europe/spain/spanishcostas/altea/marina_greenwich) sits exactly on the Prime Meridian — a curiosity worth a detour. And a short drive into the Altea Hills brings you to the [Russian Orthodox Church of Saint Michael](/europe/spain/spanishcostas/altea/russian_orthodox_church), Spain's first purpose-built Orthodox church, assembled by craftsmen who imported everything from the Ural Mountains.
