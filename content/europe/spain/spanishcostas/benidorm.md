Benidorm is Spain's ultimate beach resort — brash, unapologetic, and genuinely fun if you know what you are getting into. Two sweeping sandy beaches, [Playa de Levante](/europe/spain/spanishcostas/benidorm/levante_beach) and [Playa de Poniente](/europe/spain/spanishcostas/benidorm/poniente_beach), bracket the old town on either side, with the Mediterranean stretching blue to the horizon and the [Sierra Helada](/europe/spain/spanishcostas/benidorm/sierra_helada) rising dramatically behind. The beaches are among the finest on the Costa Blanca: wide, clean, and well-organised.

The climate is the best argument for Benidorm. Sheltered by mountains and warmed by a natural bay, it rarely gets cold even in January. This is why northern Europeans — British, Germans, Scandinavians — descend here in winter in enormous numbers, giving the town an odd dual character: a summer chaos of sunburned families and an almost genteel winter migration of pensioners who rent apartments for months at a time.

In summer the place is genuinely packed. If you want quiet Mediterranean beaches, look elsewhere. But if you are after people-watching, good nightlife, excellent seafood in the [old town](/europe/spain/spanishcostas/benidorm/old_town), and reliable sunshine, Benidorm delivers. The original fishing village survives as a compact, maze-like old quarter on the headland between the two beaches — worth an hour of wandering regardless of what you think of the resort around it. The [Balcón del Mediterráneo](/europe/spain/spanishcostas/benidorm/balcon_del_mediterraneo) lookout at the top of the headland gives the best panoramic view of the whole bay.
