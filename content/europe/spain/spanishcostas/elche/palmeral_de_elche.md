The Palmeral de Elche is Europe's largest palm grove, with over 200,000 date palms arranged in groves and gardens across the western edge of the city. The Moors established it sometime around the 10th century, though they were likely improving on earlier cultivation, and it has been maintained ever since. In 2000, UNESCO added it to the World Heritage list, recognising it as a unique survival of an Arab agricultural landscape on European soil. Walking through the denser sections of the grove, the dense canopy shuts out the sky and drops the temperature noticeably — on a summer afternoon in Elche, where the heat is relentless, this matters.

What makes the Palmeral particularly worth your time is not just the palms but the infrastructure around them. The acequia irrigation channels run throughout the grove; some are still in active use, dividing the annual water allocation among different sections according to a system negotiated centuries ago. The palms themselves produce dates — real, commercial dates — and in late autumn you can see the bunches ripening. Many of the female palms have their fronds tied up during the year to bleach them white for use in Palm Sunday processions, a tradition dating back centuries. The Palmeral is not a museum piece; it is a working agricultural landscape that also happens to be beautiful.
