Valencia is Spain's third-largest city but one of its most underrated. It has the Mediterranean climate, the beaches, and the nightlife of Barcelona without the crowds or the prices — and it is the birthplace of paella, which alone is reason enough to visit.

The old city centre is compact and handsome, with the Gothic [Lonja de la Seda](/europe/spain/spanishcostas/valencia/llotja_de_la_seda) (Silk Exchange, a UNESCO World Heritage Site), the [Mercado Central](/europe/spain/spanishcostas/valencia/mercado_central) (one of Europe's largest covered markets), and a [cathedral](/europe/spain/spanishcostas/valencia/valencia_cathedral) that claims to house the [Holy Grail](/europe/spain/spanishcostas/valencia/holy_chalice). The dry bed of the old Turia river has been converted into [a long park](/europe/spain/spanishcostas/valencia/jardines_del_turia) that runs through the city and leads to Santiago Calatrava's spectacular [City of Arts and Sciences](/europe/spain/spanishcostas/valencia/city_of_arts_and_sciences) complex — part [science museum](/europe/spain/spanishcostas/valencia/museu_ciencies_principe_felipe), part [aquarium](/europe/spain/spanishcostas/valencia/oceanografic), part [opera house](/europe/spain/spanishcostas/valencia/palau_de_les_arts), all futuristic white concrete and reflecting pools.

The [beach](/europe/spain/spanishcostas/valencia/malvarrosa_beach) stretches for kilometres along the eastern edge of the city, and the neighbourhood of [El Cabanyal](/europe/spain/spanishcostas/valencia/el_cabanyal) behind it has a village feel with tiled facades and good seafood restaurants. Las Fallas in March is one of Spain's most extraordinary festivals — enormous papier-mâché figures are built over months, displayed for a week, then burned to the ground in a single fiery night of firecrackers and tears.

Nightlife starts very late — do not show up before 1 am or you will think the city is dead. It is not.
