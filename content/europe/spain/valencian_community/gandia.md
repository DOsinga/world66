Gandía is often sold as a beach town, but the better reason to come is the split personality. The wide sand and apartment blocks sit a few kilometres from a proper Valencian city, with everyday shops, orange-country wealth, and one of the strongest Borgia monuments in Spain.

The [Ducal Palace of the Borgias](/europe/spain/valencian_community/gandia/ducal_palace_of_the_borgias) is the headline. It gives the old centre a weight that many coastal resorts lack, and it pairs naturally with the [Collegiate Church of Santa Maria](/europe/spain/valencian_community/gandia/collegiate_church_of_santa_maria), the archaeological museum, and the last surviving tower of the medieval walls. Gandía is not just a strip of summer hotels pretending to be a town.

The beach still matters. [Platja del Nord](/europe/spain/valencian_community/gandia/platja_del_nord) is long, broad, and Spanish-family busy in season, while the quieter northern beaches and dunes keep the coast from feeling entirely overbuilt. Add fideuà, which Gandía claims as its own, and the city makes a strong one-day break from Valencia if you want both old stone and sea air.
