In the central Swedish uplands (Bergslagen), dozens of villages once centred around ironworks, built on a particular Swedish model: the owner provided the buildings, the housing, the school, the church, and the health care — for those who behaved. Today the industry is gone and most of the ironwork buildings are ruins. Engelsberg is the only exception. The [Engelsberg Ironworks](/europe/sweden/engelsberg/engelsberg_ironworks) has been preserved almost completely intact, with machinery still in working condition — the waterwheel, the blower, the hammer, the crusher — alongside the smelting house, the weighing house where charcoal was delivered, the ironmaster's mansion, and the workers' housing.

UNESCO declared it a World Heritage Site for being "an outstanding example of an important European industry from the seventeenth to the nineteenth centuries, with both offices and homes preserved." Unlike many industrial heritage sites that offer empty buildings and information panels, Engelsberg can actually demonstrate the machinery working, which makes the visit surprisingly visceral.

The surrounding Bergslagen landscape rewards more than a single-stop visit. [Åmänningen lake](/europe/sweden/engelsberg/amanningen_lake) a few kilometres south is good for swimming and kayaking, and the [Engelsberg–Norberg heritage railway](/europe/sweden/engelsberg/engelsberg_norberg_railway) runs occasional steam excursions through the mining country. Plan to come in summer when both the ironworks tours and the outdoor activities are at their best.
