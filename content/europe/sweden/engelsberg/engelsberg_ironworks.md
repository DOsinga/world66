Engelsberg Ironworks is one of the best-preserved examples of the European iron industry from the 17th to 19th centuries, and UNESCO has recognised it as such. The complex includes the blast furnace, the hammer forge with its log insulation, the weighing house where farmers brought charcoal, and the ironmaster's mansion — all standing much as they were when the last iron was made here in 1919.

The heart of the visit is the working machinery: the waterwheel drives a blower and hammer that can be demonstrated for visitors, giving a visceral sense of what this industry looked, sounded, and felt like. The workers' housing, the estate church, and the outbuildings complete the picture of a self-contained industrial community where every aspect of life was controlled by the owner.

Guided tours run in summer, typically from June through August. The ironworks is managed by the Ekomuseum Bergslagen and lies about 15 kilometres from Fagersta. Combine it with a drive through the Bergslagen region to see several other, more ruined ironworks in the same landscape.
