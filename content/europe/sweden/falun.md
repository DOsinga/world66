Falun is the capital of Dalarna, a Swedish province that prides itself on being the heartland of traditional Swedish culture. The city is best known for the [Falun Copper Mine](/europe/sweden/falun/falun_mine), a UNESCO World Heritage Site that has been worked since at least the 10th century — making it one of the oldest industrial sites in the world still open to visitors.

At its peak in the 17th century, the mine supplied two-thirds of Europe's copper and made Sweden a major European power. Tours now take you deep into the cool, orange-tinged tunnels past extraordinary rock formations stained by mineral-rich water. The open-pit crater created by a catastrophic collapse in 1687 is still visible above ground. One side-effect of all that copper extraction: the distinctive dark red paint seen on wooden houses and barns across Sweden — "Falun red" — is made from the mine's waste slag, and has been used since at least the 16th century.

Beyond the mine, Falun has [Kristine Church](/europe/sweden/falun/kristine_church), a fine Baroque parish church in the well-preserved old city centre, and the [Dalarnas Museum](/europe/sweden/falun/dalarnas_museum), which traces the region's folk art traditions including the Dala horse. Just outside the city, [Carl Larsson-gården](/europe/sweden/falun/carl_larsson_garden) at Sundborn is the beautifully preserved home of Sweden's most beloved painter. In winter, the [Lugnet sports complex](/europe/sweden/falun/lugnet_sports_compl) hosts World Cup cross-country skiing and ski jumping events.
