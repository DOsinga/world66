  Visby once had 16 churches within its walls, a remarkable concentration for a
  trading town of its size. When Hanseatic wealth dried up and the population
  collapsed after the 1361 Danish sack, the city could no longer maintain them.
  Roofs fell in, walls cracked, and the ruins were simply left standing as the
  town shrank around them. Today the shells of 15 churches — Sankt Lars, Sankt
  Karin, Sankt Nikolai, and the others — are scattered through the old town,
  giving Visby a strange, ruined grandeur unlike any other Scandinavian city.

Inside Visby's city wall, the ruins of 15 medieval churches stand in various states of preservation, ranging from barely-standing fragments to near-complete roofless shells. The most impressive is the ruin of the Franciscan church of Sankt Karin (St Catherine's), dating to around 1250, whose tall Gothic arches are a landmark of the old town. Sankt Lars is used for outdoor concerts in summer. Sankt Nikolai hosted a medieval Dominican monastery.

Walking between the ruins is one of the main pleasures of Visby — each has its own character and history, and the cumulative effect of so many abandoned churches in a small area is genuinely atmospheric. The tourist office can provide a map showing all the ruins. Most are freely accessible, and the interiors that can be entered reward close attention to the carved stonework.
