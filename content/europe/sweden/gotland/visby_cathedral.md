The cathedral of Visby stands at the northern end of the walled city and is visible from some distance outside the walls. The building dates to the 12th century and has been modified many times since, most notably in the 17th century when the twin towers were added. The interior is relatively plain but the building's age and its position among the many ruins give it a particular weight.

The church is surrounded by several of the most striking ruins in Visby. Sankt Lars ruin, a few steps east, is an atmospheric shell used for summer concerts. The whole district around the cathedral repays unhurried wandering — the rose-covered stone walls and narrow lanes are at their best in the warm months.
