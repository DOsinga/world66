Gotska Sandön Lighthouse stands on one of Sweden's most isolated islands, a low, sandy national park in the Baltic Sea north of Gotland. The lighthouse belongs to the island's maritime history, when this lonely shore was a hazard for ships and a place where keepers lived at the edge of a vast, wind-shaped landscape.

For visitors who make it to Gotska Sandön, the lighthouse is a natural walking objective. It adds a human marker to an island otherwise defined by dunes, pine forest, beaches, and silence. The appeal is less architectural detail than remoteness: a working landmark at the end of a long sea crossing.
