Gamla Gefle is the old town district south of the Gavleån river, the only part of central Gävle to survive the catastrophic fire of 1869. It consists of a neighbourhood of low wooden houses from the 18th and early 19th centuries, painted in the traditional ochre, red, and white of Swedish vernacular architecture, set along narrow cobblestoned lanes that follow the original town plan.

The area is residential rather than touristified, which gives it an authentic quality rare in preserved historic districts. The houses are small and tightly packed, many still with their original outbuildings and gardens. The Holy Trinity Church (Heliga Trefaldighets kyrka) anchors the district with its white timber-frame facade. Walking through in the early morning or evening, when residents are out in their gardens, gives a good sense of what Swedish small-town life looked like before industrialisation. The area is about 10 minutes' walk from the city centre.
