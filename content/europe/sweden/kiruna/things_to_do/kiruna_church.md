Kiruna Church is a striking red wooden building completed in 1912, designed by Gustaf Wickman in a style that blends Sámi cultural motifs with National Romantic architecture. It stands 63 metres tall and is one of Sweden's largest wooden buildings. The building's silhouette — a tall central tower flanked by pitched rooflines — is modelled loosely on a traditional Sámi tent (goahti).

The church was built during Kiruna's early industrial boom, funded substantially by LKAB. Its interior is richly decorated with carvings and ironwork; the altar and baptismal font were designed to reflect the town's dual identity as an industrial settlement and a northern wilderness community.

Following the city relocation, the church was disassembled and rebuilt in the new city centre, where it continues to serve as Kiruna's most recognisable landmark.
