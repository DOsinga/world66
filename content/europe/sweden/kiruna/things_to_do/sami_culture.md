The Kiruna area is part of Sápmi, the traditional homeland of the Sámi people that spans northern Norway, Sweden, Finland, and Russia. Swedish Lapland has an estimated 20,000–25,000 Sámi, and the culture is woven into daily life here in ways that are easy to miss if you don't look for them.

The most accessible introduction to Sámi culture is at Jukkasjärvi, where the village's Sámi heritage centre offers demonstrations of traditional crafts, reindeer herding, and joik (the traditional vocal form of Sámi music). The 17th-century wooden church in the village contains an altarpiece depicting the biblical Easter story set in a Lapland winter landscape — an unusual and striking work.

Several operators around Kiruna offer genuine reindeer farm experiences, where Sámi families demonstrate traditional herding techniques, let visitors feed and work with the animals, and explain the ongoing relationship between reindeer herding and modern life. These are not staged performances; many of the families still herd reindeer commercially across the same seasonal routes they always have.

The Sámi parliament (Sametinget) has its offices in Kiruna, a reflection of the city's position at the heart of Sámi country. The Ájtte museum in Jokkmokk (200 km south) is the most comprehensive Sámi cultural museum in Sweden, but for those staying in Kiruna, the local farm experiences offer a more direct encounter.
