Landskrona sits on the western coast of Skåne, halfway between [Helsingborg](/europe/sweden/helsingborg) and [Malmö](/europe/sweden/malmo). Its name means "Land's Crown," and the town earns it — this is a compact, walkable place with a 16th-century citadel, leafy parks, and a working harbour that still feels like a real Swedish town rather than a tourist set piece.

The star attraction is [Citadellet](/europe/sweden/landskrona/citadellet), a remarkably well-preserved Renaissance fortress surrounded by a moat, set in parkland right in the centre of town. South of the harbour, the old fishing village of [Borstahusen](/europe/sweden/landskrona/borstahusen) is worth a wander for its colourful wooden houses and waterfront feel. Landskrona is also one of the greenest towns in Sweden — its many parks make it a pleasant place to spend an afternoon even when the weather is merely Scandinavian.

From the harbour, the [ferry to Ven](/europe/sweden/landskrona/ferry_to_ven) takes 25 minutes and arrives at the island famous as the home of the 16th-century astronomer Tycho Brahe, whose naked-eye observations were so precise that Kepler used them to derive the laws of planetary motion. The island is small enough to cycle around in a few hours and makes for a wonderful day trip. On a clear day, you can see Denmark across the Öresund.
