Lidköping is a small city on the southern shore of Lake Vänern — Sweden's largest lake — at the mouth of the River Lidan. It is a pleasant, unhurried place with a tidy town centre and decent connections to the surrounding countryside.

The main attraction in the area is [Läckö Castle](/europe/sweden/lidkping/lacko_castle), about 25 km north of the city on a small peninsula jutting into Lake Vänern. Built in the 13th century and later transformed into one of Sweden's finest Baroque palaces by Count Magnus Gabriel De la Gardie in the 1650s, Läckö is worth the trip for its setting alone — a gleaming white castle mirrored in the lake, surrounded by nothing but forest and water. The interior has been carefully restored. In summer the castle courtyard hosts the Läckö Slottsopera festival, an outdoor opera series that draws audiences from across Sweden.

Lidköping itself sits at the foot of the [Kinnekulle](/europe/sweden/lidkping/kinnekulle) plateau, a nature reserve known for its wildflowers, layered geology, and cycling trails about 20 km to the east. The town also has the [Rörstrand Museum](/europe/sweden/lidkping/rorstrands_museum) — three centuries of Swedish ceramic design from a manufacturer founded in 1726 — alongside a factory outlet where seconds are sold at a discount.
