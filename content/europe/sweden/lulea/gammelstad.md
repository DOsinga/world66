Gammelstad Church Town is the finest surviving example of a Nordic church town, and its inscription on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1996 was fully deserved. The concept is one that made perfect sense in the vast, sparsely populated north: since parishioners often lived a day's journey away, they needed somewhere to sleep when they came to worship. The solution was to build rows of small red timber cottages around the church, creating a kind of seasonal village that came alive on Sundays and at major festivals.

More than 400 cottages survive in remarkably good condition, most of them still privately owned by the descendants of the families that built them. They are arranged in tight rows along unpaved lanes, low-roofed, functional, and deeply atmospheric. The medieval church at the centre is still an active place of worship. Visitors can tour the church, walk the cottage lanes freely, and visit the open-air museum sections that demonstrate how people lived in this far northern community. The whole site lies about ten kilometres northwest of central Luleå and is easily reached by bus.
