Nyköping is one of Sweden's oldest towns, founded in the 12th century on the estuary of the Nyköpingsån river where it meets the Baltic. It was briefly a royal seat — and the scene of one of medieval Scandinavia's most chilling betrayals: in 1317 King Birger invited his two brothers, Duke Erik and Duke Valdemar, to a Christmas feast at [Nyköping Castle](/europe/sweden/nykping/nykoeping_castle), then had them thrown into the dungeon and starved to death. The castle's round keep, Kungstornet, still stands as a reminder.

Today it is a quiet regional city of around 35,000, with a pleasant old town of wooden buildings and a waterfront that comes alive in summer. The [Sörmland Museum](/europe/sweden/nykping/sormland_museum) occupies the castle buildings and covers the county's history from the Iron Age onward. The riverbank paths along the [Nyköpingsån](/europe/sweden/nykping/nykopingsan_river) make for easy and enjoyable wandering between sights.

Nyköping is best known to many travellers simply as the location of **Stockholm Skavsta Airport**, 5 kilometres outside the city, used mainly by Ryanair as a southern Stockholm hub. That is a pity — the town deserves more than a transit stop. It is small enough to cover in a day but has a genuine, unhurried character that larger Swedish cities have lost. The [Nils Oscar Brewery](/europe/sweden/nykping/nils_oscar_brewery), one of Sweden's most respected craft producers, is based here and welcomes visitors. For those with more time, the [Labro Meadows](/europe/sweden/nykping/labro_meadows) nature reserve on the eastern edge of the municipality is excellent for wildflowers and birds in late spring.
