Öland is a long, slender island off the southeast coast of Sweden, connected to the mainland by a 6 km bridge. On the other side of that bridge is [Kalmar](/europe/sweden/kalmar), the nearest city. The island stretches about 137 km from north to south but rarely exceeds 16 km across, which makes it easy to explore by bicycle or car along the single main road.

What draws people here is the landscape: flat limestone plains called alvar, hundreds of old windmills, wildflowers in spring and summer, and a coastline that flips between rocky shores and sandy beaches. The southern alvar is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, recognized as one of Europe's largest areas of this rare habitat. In summer the island fills with Swedes on holiday; in the off-season it goes wonderfully quiet.

The big sights are spread along the length of the island. [Borgholm Castle](/europe/sweden/oland/borgholm_castle) in the north is the most dramatic ruin in Sweden. [Solliden Palace](/europe/sweden/oland/solliden_palace) nearby is the Swedish royal family's summer residence. At the southern tip, the [Långe Jan lighthouse](/europe/sweden/oland/lange_jan_lighthouse) marks the end of the land. [Eketorp](/europe/sweden/oland/eketorp_fortress), a reconstructed Iron Age fortress in the south, gives a vivid sense of how people lived here 1,500 years ago. The more mysterious [Ismantorp Fortress](/europe/sweden/oland/ismantorp_fortress), hidden in woodland near the centre, is unrestored and wilder. Over 400 [windmills](/europe/sweden/oland/windmills) are scattered across the island — more per square kilometre than anywhere else in Europe.
