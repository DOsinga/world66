The Great Alvar (Alvarplanet) in the south of Öland is a limestone plain of extraordinary ecological importance — one of the largest areas of alvar habitat in Europe and a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2000. The landscape is flat, open, and unlike anything else in Sweden: thin soil over bare limestone, windswept in winter, but covered in a riot of rare wildflowers from May to July, including orchids, pasqueflowers, and thyme.

The alvar is best experienced on foot or by bicycle. The roads that cross it are quiet and largely traffic-free, and you can walk for hours in near silence broken only by wind and birdsong. The combination of the Lange Jan lighthouse at the southern tip and the alvar landscape around it makes for one of the most distinctive half-day walks in Sweden. Bring layers — the wind is persistent.
