Öland has more windmills per square kilometre than anywhere else in Europe, and they are one of the defining images of the island. At the peak in the 19th century, there were over 2,000 working mills on the island; today around 400 survive in varying states of preservation, scattered across the alvar and farmland.

The windmills are particularly concentrated along the eastern side of the island and in the area around Lerkaka, where a row of post mills stands in a field and can be visited up close. Most are not open to enter, but their silhouettes against the flat landscape are the quintessential Öland image. The best way to encounter them is simply to bicycle or drive the eastern road (route 136) from south to north — mills appear every few kilometres, in fields, by roadsides, and occasionally right next to houses.
