In the village of Lovikka, a few kilometres north of Pajala, stands what is claimed to be the world's largest knitted mitten — a monument to the craft tradition that made this tiny Arctic village unexpectedly famous. The Lovikka mitten, invented here in the 1890s, became the cold-weather hand covering of the Scandinavian Arctic and is still produced by hand in the village today.

The statue is a deliberately absurdist piece of local pride — a giant version of an extremely functional object, in a village of a few hundred people above the Arctic Circle. It exemplifies the dry Nordic sense of humour about rurality and remoteness that runs through Mikael Niemi's writing about the Tornedalen. Lovikka is a short drive north of Pajala and can be visited on the way to or from Karesuando.
