Rättvik is one of the classic towns on Lake Siljan, the part of Dalarna that Swedes reach for when they want red cottages, folk dress, midsummer, lake light, and a strong sense of old rural Sweden. The town is small, but it has more than one reason to stop: a 628-metre pier into the lake, a medieval church with old church stables, summer car culture, and Dalhalla, the concert arena carved into a limestone quarry north of town.

The lakefront gives Rättvik its easy charm. [Långbryggan](/europe/sweden/rattvik/langbryggan) runs straight out into Siljan, long enough that the town begins to feel distant before you reach the end. Above the shore, [Rättvik Church](/europe/sweden/rattvik/rattvik_church) and the open-air [Gammelgård](/europe/sweden/rattvik/rattviks_gammelgard) give the place its cultural weight.

Rättvik is best in summer, when Dalhalla is running and the town is busy with lake days, markets, and Classic Car Week. Outside the season it becomes quieter, but the pier, church peninsula, and Siljan landscape still make it a worthwhile stop on a Dalarna loop.
