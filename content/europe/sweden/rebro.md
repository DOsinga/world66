Örebro sits roughly halfway between [Stockholm](/europe/sweden/stockholm) and [Gothenburg](/europe/sweden/gothenburg), at a crossroads that has made it a market town since at least the 13th century. The east-west water route across the great lakes met the north-south land road here, and the town grew up around the traffic.

[Örebro Castle](/europe/sweden/rebro/orebro_castle) is the centrepiece — a medieval fortress on an island in the River Svartån, expanded into a grand Renaissance castle in the 16th century. It still dominates the city centre and is open for guided tours. Along the river, [Wadköping](/europe/sweden/rebro/wadkoping) is an open-air quarter of old wooden houses saved from demolition in the 1960s and reassembled here, named after the fictional town in Hjalmar Bergman's novels.

Örebro is now a university town with a pleasant, walkable centre. The shoe factories that defined the city for a century are all gone. An oddity worth knowing: [Svampen](/europe/sweden/rebro/svampen_water_tower), the world's first mushroom-shaped water tower, was built here in 1958 — there is a copy in Riyadh — and is open for views from the top. The [Saint Nicholas Church](/europe/sweden/rebro/saint_nicholas_church) is the 13th-century heart of the old town.

Birdwatchers should head to [Oset and Rynningeviken](/europe/sweden/rebro/oset_rynningeviken), the marshes where the Svartån flows into Lake Hjälmaren. It is one of Sweden's best urban birding sites, with hides and boardwalks through the wetlands and over 230 recorded species.
