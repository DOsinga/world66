Svampen — Swedish for "the mushroom" — is a 58-metre water tower built in 1958 that became internationally famous as the model for mushroom-shaped water towers worldwide. The original sits in Örebro's northern residential district, easily visible from the city centre, and has a restaurant and viewing platform at the top of the cap — accessed by an internal lift — with views across the flat Närkeplain to the surrounding forests.

The tower is a good example of postwar Scandinavian design pragmatism: a functional infrastructure object designed with enough aesthetic confidence that it became an icon. It's still an active water tower. The restaurant is open in summer; the viewing platform has more limited hours. Worth the short detour if you are curious about the Swedish engineering tradition and want a different view of the city.
