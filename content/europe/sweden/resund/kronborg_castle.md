story: "Kronborg Castle was built in 1420 to control and tax the Öresund passage — every ship going through the strait paid a toll here until 1857, generating revenue that funded the Danish crown. Shakespeare set Hamlet at Elsinore, which is the anglicisation of Helsingør, though there is no evidence he ever visited. The castle became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2000. Its dungeons contain the legendary sleeping figure of Holger Danske, a Viking chieftain who, according to tradition, will wake and defend Denmark if the country is ever in mortal danger."
---

Kronborg Castle at Helsingør, across the strait from the Swedish town of Helsingborg, is one of the most significant Renaissance castles in northern Europe. The ferry from Helsingborg to Helsingør takes 20 minutes, and the castle is a short walk from the Danish terminal. The interior has been restored after a major 17th-century fire and includes a great hall, chapel, and dungeon with the legendary Holger Danske statue. Plan at least two hours for a proper visit.
