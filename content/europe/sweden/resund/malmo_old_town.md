story: "Malmö's old town — Gamla Staden — centres on Stortorget, one of the largest medieval market squares in Scandinavia. The square is flanked by the Renaissance town hall, built in 1546, and by 17th-century merchants' houses in Dutch baroque style, reflecting the period when Malmö was part of Denmark rather than Sweden. Sweden took control in 1658, and the city has carried both cultural inheritances ever since. The pedestrian shopping streets between the square and the central station are genuinely old, with several surviving 16th and 17th-century building facades along Lilla Torg."
---

The centre of Malmö is compact and very walkable. Stortorget, the main square, is surrounded by historic buildings including a 16th-century Renaissance town hall that is still in active use. Lilla Torg, a smaller square around the corner, has outdoor cafes that are busy in any weather and several of Malmö's better restaurants. The distance from Malmö Central Station to Stortorget is about 15 minutes on foot, making it easy to combine with a train connection to or from Copenhagen.
