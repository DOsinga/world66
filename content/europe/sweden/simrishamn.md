Simrishamn is a small fishing port 40 kilometres northeast of Ystad along the Österlen coast — the southeast corner of Skåne known for its farmland, apple orchards, and artists' studios. The town has a population of around 6,500 and a character that mixes a working harbour with an unusually well-preserved medieval centre. The white-painted half-timbered houses and the low-slung skyline make it one of the prettier towns on this stretch of coast.

The centrepiece is **St Nicholas Church**, built in the 12th century and still the most visible landmark in town. The interior is worth visiting for a fine 15th-century carved pulpit and stained glass by the Swedish artist Erik Olson. The waterfront fish smokehouses are a more immediate draw: hot smoked salmon and mackerel sold to take away, best eaten on the quayside while watching the fishing boats unload.

North of the harbour, **Tobisborg** is a quiet bathing area with sandy beaches that draws local families in summer. South of town at **Brantevik** — a smaller fishing village accessible in a short drive — you find a scattering of good restaurants and a harbour so small and still it barely seems real. Combining Simrishamn with a stop at [Ales Stenar](/europe/sweden/ystad/ales_stenar) and the Kåseberga coast makes a full day of Österlen.
