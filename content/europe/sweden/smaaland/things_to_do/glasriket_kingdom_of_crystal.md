Glasriket — the Kingdom of Crystal — is the name given to a stretch of deep forest between Växjö and Kalmar where some twenty glassworks have operated since the 18th century. The two most famous names are Kosta Boda and Orrefors, but villages like Boda, Åfors, Målerås, and Skruf each have their own character and style. Most factories welcome visitors to watch master glassblowers at work, and every one of them has a showroom and outlet shop selling both gallery pieces and seconds at a fraction of retail prices.

The experience of watching molten glass spun and shaped at Kosta or Orrefors is genuinely memorable. The glassblowers work with extraordinary speed and precision, and many factories run hands-on workshops where you can try your own hand at the bench. Outside of summer the workshops can be quieter and more atmospheric — the furnaces glow orange against the winter darkness, and the Christmas season brings special exhibitions and light installations among the trees.

The village of Kosta itself has grown into a small resort, with outlet shopping, a spa hotel, and a newly developed Kosta Boda Art Hotel where the glass art extends into every corridor and room. The surrounding forest roads make for pleasant cycling between the villages, and the whole Glasriket area is compact enough to cover in two or three days.
