Älmhult, a small town in the southern part of Småland, is where Ingvar Kamprad opened the first IKEA store in 1958 — in the original building that still stands on the main street. The IKEA Museum now occupies that building and tells the story of how a farm boy from Småland built the world's largest furniture retailer. Exhibits range from the company's earliest catalogue photographs and flat-pack prototypes to room sets recreated across the decades, showing how Swedish ideas about home and family evolved alongside the brand.

The museum is more engaging than it might sound. Kamprad's own story is inseparable from Småland's culture of self-reliance and frugality — the region's poverty in earlier centuries bred a practical, make-do character that IKEA's whole philosophy reflects. The museum explains this context well, and the archive photographs of mid-century Swedish interiors have genuine historical interest.

Älmhult itself is a quiet town, but the museum draws a steady stream of visitors who pair it with a stop at the adjacent IKEA store — which, fittingly, was the company's original flagship. The town is easily reached by train from Malmö and Gothenburg, and is about an hour's drive from both Växjö and the Glasriket area.
