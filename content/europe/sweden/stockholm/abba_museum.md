  The interactive aspect of the ABBA Museum is taken seriously: each visitor gets a personal card linked to a phone number, and if ABBA member Björn, Benny, Agnetha, or Anni-Frid happens to call the museum while you're inside — which apparently happens — you can pick up the phone and talk to them. The platinum and gold outfits on display had to be replicated for the exhibition because the originals are too fragile to be worn by visitors who try them on.

ABBA The Museum opened in 2013 on Djurgården and is one of the more joyful museums in Stockholm. The exhibits trace the history of Sweden's most successful pop group from their early careers through the Eurovision win in 1974, global domination in the late 1970s, and lasting cultural impact. Original costumes, recording equipment, handwritten lyrics, and gold records fill the space.

The interactive elements set it apart from a standard pop memorabilia collection: you can step into a recording studio, sing with holographic versions of the band members, and try on replica stage outfits. Whether you consider yourself an ABBA fan or not, the sheer scale of what four people from Stockholm achieved makes for a compelling story. Budget one to two hours.
