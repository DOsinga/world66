Sundsvall sits on the coast of northern Sweden, squeezed between two forested hills — [Norra Stadsberget](/europe/sweden/sundsvall/norra_stadsberget) and [Södra Stadsberget](/europe/sweden/sundsvall/sodra_stadsberget) — with the Gulf of Bothnia stretching east. The town burned to the ground in 1888 and was rebuilt entirely in stone, giving the centre an unusually grand look for a city of its size. Locals call it [Stenstan](/europe/sweden/sundsvall/stenstan), the Stone City.

The rebuilt centre is handsome and walkable, with wide streets lined by ornate facades that wouldn't look out of place in a much larger European capital. Storgatan is the main commercial street; the central square, [Stora Torget](/europe/sweden/sundsvall/stora_torget), is the natural starting point for a walk. The [Sundsvalls Museum](/europe/sweden/sundsvall/sundsvalls_museum) inside the restored [Kulturmagasinet](/europe/sweden/sundsvall/kulturmagasinet) warehouses fills in the fire story. For views, climb either Stadsberget — the panorama from the top takes in the city, the harbour, and the [Sundsvall Bridge](/europe/sweden/sundsvall/sundsvall_bridge) over to Alnön.

Sundsvall is a natural stopping point on the drive or train journey up the Swedish coast. The [High Coast](/europe/sweden/the_high_coast) (Höga Kusten), a UNESCO World Heritage site of dramatic uplift coastline, begins just north of the city and makes for an excellent day trip or onward journey.
