Tanum is a municipality in the province of Bohuslän on the northern part of the Swedish west coast. It is best known for its extraordinary concentration of Bronze Age rock carvings — hundreds of panels scattered across the landscape depicting humans, animals, boats, and ritual scenes. These carvings, mostly around 3,000 years old, were declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1994.

The main site at [Vitlycke](/europe/sweden/tanum/vitlycke) is the most accessible, with a museum and walkways between the rock faces. Other carved rocks at [Fossum](/europe/sweden/tanum/fossum), [Litsleby](/europe/sweden/tanum/litsleby), and [Aspeberget](/europe/sweden/tanum/aspeberget) are within easy cycling distance. The surrounding Bohuslän coast offers granite skerries, fishing villages, and excellent sea kayaking, with [Grebbestad](/europe/sweden/grebbestad) the closest base on the water. The small village of [Tanumshede](/europe/sweden/tanum/tanumshede) is the practical hub for visits.
