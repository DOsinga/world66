The Saab Car Museum occupies one of the old NOHAB locomotive factory buildings in the Innovatum area of Trollhättan, a kilometre from the town centre. It holds around 120 cars spanning the full history of Saab production from 1947 to 2011, of which about 70 are on permanent display — the original Ursaaben prototype, standard production models, limited editions, rally cars, and concept vehicles that were never built.

The museum tells the story of a company that was genuinely eccentric: an aircraft manufacturer that decided to build cars as a sideline, ended up making some of the most distinctive vehicles in European motoring, and then was sold, neglected, and finally killed by General Motors over the course of twenty years. For Saab enthusiasts this is a pilgrimage site; for anyone interested in industrial design or Swedish corporate history, it is fascinating in a melancholy way. The museum includes a small cinema showing archive footage.
