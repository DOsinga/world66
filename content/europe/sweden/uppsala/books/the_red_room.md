Strindberg matriculated at Uppsala in 1867 and dropped in and out of the university for years, never finishing his degree but absorbing the late-19th-century academic world that he would attack in *The Red Room* (1879). The novel — a satire of Stockholm bohemian life, told through the misadventures of a young civil-servant-turned-writer — is widely considered the first modern Swedish novel.

The Uppsala chapters are brief but acid: the professors, the petty exam politics, the airless religious orthodoxy. Together with *Doctor Glas* it gives the best portrait we have of the city that the post-Romantic generation needed to escape from.
