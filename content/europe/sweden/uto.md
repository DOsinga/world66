Utö is an island in the southern reaches of the Stockholm archipelago, reached in about two and a half hours from the city by a combination of commuter train and ferry — the boat leaves from Årsta Brygga, itself a short hop on the commuter rail from Stockholm Central. The journey is longer than the quick run out to Vaxholm, but it carries you well beyond the inner suburbs into open archipelago landscape.

The island has been inhabited since the Middle Ages, when iron ore was mined here — some of the oldest mine shafts in Sweden. You can still explore the workings and the old miners' cottages, and an idle windmill on the hill gives the long views over the surrounding skerries that make the climb worthwhile.

For most visitors, though, Utö is about the water. The island offers some of the best swimming in the archipelago: sandy beaches on the south side, smooth granite cliffs on the north, and water that is surprisingly clear. Rent a bike at the harbour and ride the island's quiet roads — a loop of the whole thing takes a couple of leisurely hours, past pine forest, meadows, and small red cottages.

For food, Utö Värdshus is the grand old option, serving Swedish classics in a building that dates to the 1700s, while Båtshaket down by the harbour does simpler fare and is a fine spot for a beer in the sun. In summer the island fills up with day-trippers, so come early on the first boat or stay overnight to get the best of it once the crowds have gone home.
