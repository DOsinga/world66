Vaxholm is a small town on an island just east of Stockholm, and for many visitors it is the gateway to the archipelago — the closest taste of island life you can get without a long journey. Ferries run year-round from the city centre, and the half-hour ride out is half the pleasure: the grand waterfront mansions near central Stockholm give way to smaller, more modest cottages tucked among the rocks and pines as you approach the town.

The town itself is compact and easy to walk. Wooden villas with carved verandas and lace-trimmed gables line the lanes — many built as summer houses in the late 19th century, when steamboats first made the archipelago fashionable. Near the ferry landing you will find craft shops, cafes, and a handful of restaurants along the harbour. Grab a window seat, order some local fish, and watch the boats come and go; there is enough here to fill a relaxed few hours.

Across a narrow strait stands Vaxholm Fortress, a citadel first built in the 1540s to guard the sea approach to Stockholm and rebuilt in its present form in the 19th century. Today it houses a museum of coastal defence, reachable by a short shuttle boat from the town quay, and is well worth the crossing if you have the time.

Vaxholm makes an easy half-day or full-day trip from Stockholm, and works equally well as a first stop on a longer hop deeper into the archipelago. In summer the harbour is busy with pleasure craft and the ice-cream queues are long; off-season it is quieter, windswept, and arguably more atmospheric.
