Vänersborg is a small city of around 40,000 people perched on the southernmost shore of [Lake Vänern](/europe/sweden/vnersborg/lake_vanern_waterfront), Sweden's largest lake. The city is often paired with the nearby industrial city of [Trollhättan](/europe/sweden/trollhttan) — together they are known as "Tvåstad" ("Two Cities") — but Vänersborg has its own quieter, more intimate character.

The [city centre](/europe/sweden/vnersborg/old_wooden_town) has held onto its old wooden houses in a way that is genuinely rare in Sweden, giving the streets a 19th-century atmosphere that feels unhurried and unspoiled. This is a town that chose preservation over redevelopment, and it shows. The [Nabbensberg](/europe/sweden/vnersborg/nabbensberg) park hill, crowned by the city museum, sits above the waterfront and offers the best elevated view over the town and lake.

Vänersborg was the hometown of the poet [Birger Sjöberg](/europe/sweden/vnersborg/birger_sjoberg_park), who gave the city its affectionate nickname: "Little Paris." Before the reorganisation of Swedish regional government, it also served as the administrative capital of Älvsborg County.

The strongest reason to linger here is the natural landscape just outside town: the twin table mountains of [Halleberg](/europe/sweden/vnersborg/halleberg) and [Hunneberg](/europe/sweden/vnersborg/hunneberg) rise abruptly from the plain to the east, carrying one of Sweden's densest populations of wild elk and a network of well-marked forest trails through ancient woodland. The [Vänersborg Museum](/europe/sweden/vnersborg/vanersborg_museum), meanwhile, holds an African natural history and ethnographic collection that is startlingly large for a city this size.
