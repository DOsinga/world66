Birger Sjöberg (1885–1929) was born in Vänersborg and drew much of his inspiration from its streets, personalities, and the particular atmosphere of a small Swedish provincial town at the turn of the 20th century. It was he who gave the city its affectionate nickname "Little Paris" — half ironically, in recognition of its aspirations and self-regard rather than its grandeur.

The park and memorial along the waterfront provides a fitting tribute to the poet. A statue of Sjöberg stands in the parkland near the lake, and the surroundings — with old linden trees and views across Vänern — have the unhurried quality that pervades his poetry. The nearby Nabbensberg hill, with its museum and café, sits above the park and was a favourite location for the poet's townspeople to stroll on Sunday afternoons, a tradition that continues today.
