  In 2014, the Kunstmuseum inherited one of the most controversial art collections in history: the Gurlitt bequest, over 1,500 works assembled by the art dealer Hildebrand Gurlitt and his son Cornelius, suspected of including Nazi-looted art. The museum was given the collection on condition that it investigate every work's provenance — a task that took years and generated international media attention. Several works have since been returned to the families from whom they were stolen.

The Kunstmuseum Bern is the oldest public art museum in Switzerland, founded in 1879, and holds a collection of around 4,000 paintings and 48,000 works on paper. The permanent collection covers Swiss and European art from the 14th century to the present. There is a notable holding of Swiss Expressionism, works by Ferdinand Hodler and Paul Klee (though the main Klee collection is at Zentrum Paul Klee), and a good selection of Impressionist and post-Impressionist canvases.

The museum is in the Länggasse neighbourhood, a short walk north of the main station. The building is a respectable 19th-century civic structure rather than an architectural statement. A connecting walk leads to the Kornhausforum cultural centre and back down to the Old Town. The museum runs occasional evening events and has a decent café.
