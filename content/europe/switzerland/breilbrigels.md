Breil/Brigels is a mountain village in the Surselva district of Graubünden, perched on a sunny terrace at 1,300 metres above the young Rhine River. The village, together with nearby Waltensburg and Andiast, forms a small cluster of traditional Romansh-speaking communities with a distinctly local character. The bilingual name reflects this: Breil is Romansh, Brigels is German.

In winter it functions as a modest [ski resort](/europe/switzerland/breilbrigels/ski_area) — smaller and quieter than the big-name Graubünden resorts, which suits people who prefer uncrowded pistes. In summer it's good walking country, with trails down into the [Rhine gorge](/europe/switzerland/breilbrigels/rhine_gorge_walks) and up toward the high alpine pastures. The [Lag da Breil](/europe/switzerland/breilbrigels/lag_da_breil) is an easy walk from the village. In the valley below, the [Tavanasa Bridge](/europe/switzerland/breilbrigels/tavanasa_bridge) — a Robert Maillart reinforced concrete arch from 1930 — is a quiet masterpiece of engineering rarely visited by tourists. The ruined [Kropfenstein Castle](/europe/switzerland/breilbrigels/kropfenstein_castle), built into a cliff overhang above the Rhine gorge, is a striking medieval survival a short walk away. The landscape is genuinely beautiful, and the relative obscurity keeps prices reasonable.
