---
title: Bars and Cafes
type: section
---

Geneva's nightlife is concentrated on a few specific areas. The **Pâquis** quarter — between the train station and the lake — is the most active and the most varied, with cocktail bars, dance clubs, and the city's red-light street running parallel to the bars. The streets around **Place du Bourg-de-Four** in the old town stay busy in summer when terraces fill up. **Plainpalais** and **Carouge** to the south have a younger, more bohemian crowd — Carouge in particular has a quiet Sardinian charm and a clutch of good wine bars.

For coffee, the old town has several solid cafés along Grand-Rue, but the speciality-coffee scene is centred in the Pâquis and in the streets around the Plainpalais market. Look out for **Boréal Coffee**, with multiple locations across town.

The Swiss tradition of the **aperitif** — a glass of white wine or a vermouth before dinner around 6:30pm — is followed seriously in Geneva. Bars along the lakeside Quai du Mont-Blanc and the Quai Wilson host the post-work crowd; views across the water to the Alps on a clear evening are the reward.

The clubs at the more upmarket end (along the lake and around Pâquis) are notoriously expensive. Free entry is rare; expect to pay for a drink at twice the price you'd pay elsewhere.
