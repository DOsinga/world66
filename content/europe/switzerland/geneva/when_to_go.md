---
title: When to Go
type: section
---

Geneva sits at 375 metres above sea level beside the lake, with a moderately continental climate softened by Lac Léman. Summer is the most popular season — warm, dry, and full of outdoor events — but every season has its own appeal and the city is worth visiting year-round.

**Summer (June–August)** is the high season. Daytime highs hit 25–28°C, with lakeside cafés, summer concerts, the **Fêtes de Genève** music festival on the lake in early August (which culminates in one of Europe's biggest fireworks displays), and easy access to the Alps for day-trip hiking. Evenings are pleasant; the lake breeze keeps the heat manageable.

**Autumn (September–October)** brings cooler temperatures, vineyards in colour, and the **Vendanges** wine harvest celebrations in the Geneva vineyards just outside town. Crowds are thinner. November is grey and damp.

**Winter (December–February)** is cold but not extreme — daytime temperatures hover around freezing. The **Escalade** festival on 11 December commemorates Geneva's 1602 defeat of a Savoyard invasion with torchlit parades, a costumed procession, and the smashing of marzipan cauldrons full of vegetable-shaped chocolates. The surrounding Alps offer some of Europe's best skiing — Chamonix is 90 minutes away by bus, Verbier or Crans-Montana about two hours.

**Spring (March–May)** sees the city emerge from winter. The flowers in the Jardin Anglais bloom; the surrounding vineyards turn green again. Rain is more frequent than summer but less so than autumn.

For the best balance of weather and crowds, aim for **May–June** or **September**.
