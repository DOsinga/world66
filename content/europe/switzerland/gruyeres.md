Gruyères is one of Switzerland's most perfectly preserved medieval towns, perched on a hill above the green valley of the Saane river in the canton of Fribourg. The entire car-free old town sits behind a single gate, its cobbled main street lined with centuries-old stone houses and the flags of the medieval counts of Gruyère. The [Château de Gruyères](/europe/switzerland/gruyeres/chateau_de_gruyeres) dominates the hilltop at the top of the street, and on a clear day the Alps rise dramatically to the south.

The town is internationally famous for two things: cheese and the artist H.R. Giger. The [Maison du Gruyère](/europe/switzerland/gruyeres/maison_du_gruyere), just below the medieval gate, lets you watch Gruyère cheese being made in the traditional way. The [HR Giger Museum](/europe/switzerland/gruyeres/hr_giger_museum), housed in a 15th-century castle, displays the dark surrealist work of the Swiss artist best known for designing the creature in the film Alien. The combination is strange and wonderful — fondue restaurants and biomechanical nightmares in the same cobbled lane.

Day-trippers pour in from Lausanne, Bern, and beyond, which means Gruyères can feel very crowded in summer, especially on weekends. Arrive early or stay overnight; the town empties out once the buses leave, and the atmosphere in the evening is genuinely lovely. The mountain of [Moléson](/europe/switzerland/gruyeres/moleson) rises directly south and can be reached by cable car from nearby Gruyères-Moleson-sur-Gruyères — it offers some of the best views in the region.
