Lausanne tumbles down three steep hills to the shore of Lake Geneva, and that verticality shapes everything about the city. You are always climbing or descending, catching sudden views of the lake and the French Alps beyond. It is a compact place — walkable in theory, breathless in practice — with a medieval old town stacked above a lakefront that feels almost Mediterranean on a summer evening.

The city is best known as the home of the International Olympic Committee, and the [Olympic Museum](/europe/switzerland/lausanne/olympic_museum) down by the lake is genuinely worth the visit. But Lausanne has more going on than that. The [Gothic cathedral](/europe/switzerland/lausanne/cathedrale) is one of Switzerland's finest, the [Collection de l'Art Brut](/europe/switzerland/lausanne/art_brut) is unlike any museum you have seen, and the [Flon](/europe/switzerland/lausanne/flon) district — a former warehouse area below the old town — has become a lively hub of bars, restaurants, and galleries.

Lausanne sits at the heart of the [Lavaux vineyard terraces](/europe/switzerland/lausanne/lavaux), a UNESCO World Heritage landscape of steep vines running along the lakeshore toward [Montreux](/europe/switzerland/montreux). The local Chasselas wines are rarely exported, so this is the place to drink them. The city also makes a good base for exploring the Swiss Riviera, with excellent rail connections along the lake and into the Alps.

The university and the EPFL campus give the city a youthful energy, and there is a surprisingly good food and bar scene for a place this size. Lausanne is not cheap — this is Switzerland — but it rewards a couple of days of wandering.
