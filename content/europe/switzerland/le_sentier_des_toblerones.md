Le Sentier des Toblerones is a waymarked walking trail that runs seventeen kilometres from the shores of Lake Geneva at [Nyon](/europe/switzerland/nyon) up into the Jura foothills to the village of Bassins. It follows the line of triangular concrete anti-tank barriers that the Swiss army installed along this stretch of the Jura during the Second World War — nicknamed Toblerones by Swiss soldiers for their resemblance to the peaked chocolate bar.

The barriers themselves are the main attraction: hundreds of them still stand in place, jutting from fields, gardens, riverbanks, and the edges of forest roads much as they did eight decades ago. Where the line passed through farmland, barriers were set close enough to stop a tank but wide enough apart to let a tractor through. You can enter some of the accompanying pillboxes and look out through their narrow gun slits, which frame oddly peaceful views of the lake below.

The route climbs steadily from the lakeshore through vineyards and woods, crosses the small Promenthouse river valley, and continues up through open meadows with views back down to Lac Léman and across to the French Alps. At clear weather the whole panorama from the Jura rim is exceptional. The path is moderately demanding — a steady uphill all the way — and takes around four hours at a comfortable pace, or three for a brisk walker. Restaurants are scarce above the lake, so bring food and water for the full route.

The trail begins in central Nyon and ends at Bassins, from where buses connect back to Nyon via Saint-Cergue. You can also walk a shorter out-and-back section from Nyon if a full day is more than you want.
