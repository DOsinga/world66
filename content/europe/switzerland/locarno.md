Locarno sits at the northern tip of [Lake Maggiore](/europe/switzerland/locarno/lake_maggiore) in the Italian-speaking canton of Ticino, making it one of the warmest cities in [Switzerland](/europe/switzerland). The [Piazza Grande](/europe/switzerland/locarno/piazza_grande), one of the finest public squares in the country, opens directly onto the lakeshore and every August becomes the open-air cinema of the Locarno Film Festival, one of the oldest and most respected film festivals in Europe.

The old town rises behind the piazza in a tangle of arcaded lanes and pastel-coloured houses. Above the town, the [Santuario della Madonna del Sasso](/europe/switzerland/locarno/madonna_del_sasso) perches on a rocky outcrop reached by funicular, offering views across the lake to the surrounding mountains. The [Cardada-Cimetta](/europe/switzerland/locarno/cardada_cimetta) cable car continues higher still, climbing to over 1,600 metres for panoramic views stretching from the Swiss Alps to the Po Valley on clear days.

The [Visconti Castle](/europe/switzerland/locarno/visconti_castle) at the edge of the old town is where the Locarno Pact was signed in 1925 — the international agreement that briefly made this small Swiss city the centre of European diplomacy. The lake itself, palm trees along the promenade, and the mild climate give Locarno a distinctly Mediterranean feel unusual for Switzerland.
