story: >
  The Vitznau–Rigi railway, which opened in 1871, was the first rack railway in Europe. Its engineers had to solve a problem nobody had solved before: how to build a railway steep enough to climb a mountain that rises nearly 1800 metres. The rack-and-pinion system they developed became the template for mountain railways across the world. Queen Victoria rode it in 1868, three years before it was fully completed.

The Rigi is a 1798-metre mountain that rises sharply from Lake Lucerne to the east of the city. It is sometimes called the Queen of the Mountains, less for its height than for the extraordinary panorama from the summit: on a clear day you can see from Mont Blanc in the west to the Zugspitze in the east, with most of central Switzerland's Alps spread between them. The view is most famous at sunrise and sunset.

Two rack railways climb the mountain — from Vitznau on the lake and from Arth-Goldau to the south — making it accessible without hiking. At the top, a short walk leads to the Rigi Kulm summit. The slopes have gentle walking trails and in winter serve as a modest ski area. The mountain also has a famous thermal spa at Rigi Kaltbad, accessible by cable car from Weggis on the lakeside below.
