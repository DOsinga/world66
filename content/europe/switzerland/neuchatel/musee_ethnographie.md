The Musée d'Ethnographie de Neuchâtel, known as MEN, occupies a nineteenth-century villa on the western edge of the city and has built an international reputation for exhibitions that challenge the conventions of ethnographic display. The permanent collection draws on decades of fieldwork across Africa, the Americas, and Asia, but the temporary exhibitions are what make the museum distinctive.

The approach here is explicitly reflexive — the museum asks not just "what is this culture?" but "who is doing the looking, and from where?" That self-consciousness has produced some remarkable shows: exhibitions on Swiss cheese as cultural mythology, on the tourist gaze, on what counts as exotic when you apply an outsider's eye to your own backyard.

The building is pleasant to wander and the neighbourhood, quieter than the old town, has a residential feel that contrasts with the tourist-facing centre.
