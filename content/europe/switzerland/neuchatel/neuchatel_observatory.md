  For most of the twentieth century, the exact time in Switzerland was set here. The Neuchâtel Observatory broadcast the official Swiss time signal from 1900 until 1975, when the role passed to the Swiss Federal Time Service in Bern. The Neuchâtel Observatory was established in 1858 on a hill east of the city and played a central role in Swiss timekeeping for over a century. The building is architecturally handsome — a classical pavilion with a dome — and the location offers a good view over the city and lake.

The observatory's function is now primarily scientific, but its connection to watchmaking history makes it a meaningful stop for those interested in the industry that shaped the Jura region. Neuchâtel canton contains more watchmakers per capita than anywhere else on earth, and precise time was the foundation of that industry.

The grounds are accessible; interior visits depend on events and guided tours.
