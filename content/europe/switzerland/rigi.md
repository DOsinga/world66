The Rigi is a 1798-metre mountain massif that rises sharply from the eastern shore of Lake Lucerne, hemmed in by water on three sides. It is often called the Queen of the Mountains — less for its height than for the extraordinary panorama from the summit ridge: on a clear day the view runs from Mont Blanc in the west to the Zugspitze in the east, with most of central Switzerland's Alps spread out between them. The view is most famous at sunrise and sunset, when generations of travellers, Mark Twain and Queen Victoria among them, have climbed up to watch the light break over the peaks.

The mountain is reached without any hiking at all. Two rack railways climb it — the Vitznau–Rigi line from the lakeshore, which opened in 1871 as the first mountain rack railway in Europe, and a second line up from Arth-Goldau to the south. A cable car also rises from Weggis on the lake. All of them converge near Rigi Kulm, the summit station, from which a short walk leads to the very top. The engineering history here is a destination in itself: the rack-and-pinion system worked out on these slopes became the template for mountain railways across the world.

Beyond the summit, the Rigi spreads into a gentle plateau of meadows, walking trails, and small resort hamlets. Rigi Kaltbad, reached by cable car from Weggis, has a thermal spa and a network of easy panoramic paths that make it possible to spend a whole slow day up here. In winter the upper slopes serve as a modest, family-scaled ski area, a world away from the big Alpine resorts.

From Lucerne the classic outing combines a boat trip across the lake to Vitznau with the railway up and a different route down, making a loop of the whole massif. Weather is everything: time your visit to a clear day, and check the webcams before you commit, because low cloud can erase the only reason to go.
