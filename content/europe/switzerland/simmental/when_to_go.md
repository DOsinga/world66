---
title: When to Go
type: section
---

Simmental has two distinct seasons worth coming for, and a muddy shoulder period in between that mostly isn't.

**Summer (late June to mid-September)** is the prime walking season. The alpine pastures are open, the Simmental cattle are on the high grazings, and the days are long. July and August can be busy on the main trails but never oppressively so — this is not Grindelwald. The Simme river is at its most inviting for a walk along the banks.

**Winter (mid-December to late March)** draws skiers to Lenk-Simmental, the area's main resort. Lenk's slopes are best suited to intermediate skiers; it lacks the elevation and drama of the major Bernese Oberland resorts but is substantially cheaper and less crowded. Snowshoeing and cross-country skiing are good alternatives across the valley floor and lower slopes.

Spring (April–May) and early autumn (October) bring changeable weather and many closed facilities. The passes may still have snow in April and restaurants run reduced hours. Come in these periods only if you specifically want the valley quiet — which is saying something, since Simmental is never exactly hectic.

There is no single festival that demands you visit at a particular time, but the Alpabzug — when the cattle descend from high summer pastures in late September — happens in villages across the valley and is worth seeing if your timing allows. It is not a tourist performance; it is just what happens.
