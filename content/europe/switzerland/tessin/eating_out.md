---
title: Eating Out
type: section
---

Ticino's food is the happy meeting point of Swiss quality and Italian flavour. Risotto, polenta, and pasta dominate the menus; the local cheeses — formagella, zincarlin — are worth seeking out. The most traditional eating option is the grotto, a casual stone-built restaurant tucked into a hillside, often with long communal tables outdoors. Grottos serve simple, honest food: cold cuts, local cheese, polenta with various accompaniments, and the house wine.

The Ticinese Merlot is a serious wine, underrated outside Switzerland. The canton produces red, white, and rosé from the Merlot grape; the better producers are concentrated in the Mendrisiotto and the Malcantone hills west of Lugano. Wine shops in Lugano stock the full range.

In [Lugano](/europe/switzerland/lugano), the restaurants on the covered Piazza della Riforma are good for people-watching over a coffee or aperitivo. In [Locarno](/europe/switzerland/locarno), the old town around Piazza Grande has a good range of casual restaurants. For the full grotto experience, drive into any of the side valleys — the Verzasca, the Maggia, the Leventina — and look for the hand-painted sign.
