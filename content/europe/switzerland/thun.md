Thun is a small city of around 40,000 at the northwest end of Lake Thun, just 20 minutes by train from [Bern](/europe/switzerland/bern). It is the gateway to the Bernese Oberland — Interlaken, Grindelwald, and the Jungfrau region are all easily reached from here — and has more character than its transit-hub status might suggest.

[Thun Castle](/europe/switzerland/thun/thun_castle), built in 1191, is the dominant feature of the [old town](/europe/switzerland/thun/altstadt). Today a museum, it sits on a hill above the river with views over the rooftops to the lake and the Alps beyond. The old town centre has barely changed in centuries: the Rathausplatz still has its massive 16th-century city hall, and the Hauptgasse is famous for its two-level layout, with shops at street level and a raised pedestrian walkway running along the first-floor arcades. The covered wooden bridges over the Aare and the flower-filled ramparts add to the medieval character.

One sight that most visitors miss entirely: the [Wocher Panorama](/europe/switzerland/thun/wocher_panorama) in the Kunstmuseum — a 360-degree cylindrical painting of Thun as it appeared in 1814, the oldest surviving panorama painting in the world.

[Lake Thun](/europe/switzerland/thun/lake_thun) offers boat trips, swimming, and kayaking in summer. Paddle-steamer cruises connect Thun with [Spiez](/europe/switzerland/spiez) and [Interlaken](/europe/switzerland/interlaken). At the southern end of town, [Schadau Castle](/europe/switzerland/thun/schadau_castle) and its lake-view park make a perfect afternoon walk. On the south shore of the lake, [Hünegg Castle](/europe/switzerland/thun/hünegg_castle) at Hilterfingen is one of the best-preserved 19th-century interiors in Switzerland.
