The Mountaineers' Cemetery is tucked behind the Catholic Church of St. Mauritius in the centre of Zermatt — a small walled enclosure containing the graves of climbers who have died on the surrounding peaks over 150 years. Each headstone tells a short story of an alpine ambition that ended badly.

The graves span the full history of serious alpine climbing: the early Victorian pioneers, the Golden Age alpinists of the 1860s, and more recent climbers from across Europe. Many bodies were recovered years after death from the glaciers that slowly released them. The cemetery is small enough to read every inscription in twenty minutes, and most visitors do. It's free and open at all times.
