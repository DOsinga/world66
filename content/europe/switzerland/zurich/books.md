A few books that help understand Zurich's particular contradictions — Calvinist financial centre and bohemian refuge, banking hub and avant-garde experiment:

**Max Frisch, *Homo Faber* (1957).** Zurich's greatest 20th-century novelist takes a Swiss engineer on a journey across continents that ends in a confrontation with the things he has refused to feel. The Zurich passages early in the book capture the city's well-kept emotional reserve perfectly.

**Hugo Ball, *Flight Out of Time: A Dada Diary* (1927).** First-person account of the launch of the Dada movement at Cabaret Voltaire in 1916. Short, intense, sometimes mystical; gives the bohemian counter-version of wartime Zurich.

**James Joyce, *Ulysses* (1922).** Much of the writing was done in Zurich during Joyce's First World War exile; chapters set in Dublin were drafted in Joyce's flat near the lake. A Joyce-in-Zurich pilgrimage takes in the Foundation, the Pub, and the Fluntern grave — see Carola Giedion-Welcker's biography for context.

**Jean Ziegler, *Switzerland: The Awful Truth* (1976; English ed. 1979).** A polemic by a Geneva sociologist about the Swiss banking system, much of it focused on Zurich. Dated in places, but it changed how a generation of Swiss saw their own financial sector.

**Tom Bower, *Blood Money: The Swiss, the Nazis, and the Looted Billions* (1997).** Forensic investigation of Swiss banking conduct during and after the Second World War, with Zurich as the principal setting. Heavy going but essential context for understanding modern Switzerland.
