Vorokhta is a small mountain resort town in the Ukrainian Carpathians, strung along the Prut River at around 850 metres above sea level. It is best known as the closest town to [Mt. Hoverla](/europe/ukraine/carpathian_mountains/vorokhta/mt_hoverla), the highest peak in Ukraine at 2061 metres, and draws hikers and skiers from across the country.

The town itself is modest — a few streets lined with wooden houses, homestays, and the smell of pine — but the setting is extraordinary. Coniferous and beech forests press in from every side, and the surrounding ridges offer serious hiking in summer and cross-country and downhill skiing in winter at the [Avanhard ski resort](/europe/ukraine/carpathian_mountains/vorokhta/avanhard_ski_resort). The pace is slow, the people are curious and welcoming, and there is very little in the way of commercial tourism. That is the point.

Vorokhta also has a striking [18th-century wooden church](/europe/ukraine/carpathian_mountains/vorokhta/church_of_the_nativity) that is one of the finest examples of Hutsul religious architecture in the region. The Hutsuls — the highlander people of the Ukrainian Carpathians — have a rich craft tradition, and the surrounding villages are a good place to find embroidery, woodcarving, and painted Easter eggs at the [local market](/europe/ukraine/carpathian_mountains/vorokhta/hutsul_crafts).

The [Austro-Hungarian railway viaduct](/europe/ukraine/carpathian_mountains/vorokhta/vorokhta_railway_viaduct) near the station is another landmark worth seeing — an elegant stone arch bridge that once carried Galician aristocrats to their summer retreats and now carries Ukrainian holiday-makers in rattling carriages. The road out of town climbs to the [Yablunytsia Pass](/europe/ukraine/carpathian_mountains/vorokhta/yablunytsia_pass) with wide views across the Carpathian ridges.

In winter the town fills with Ukrainian families on ski holidays. In summer it fills with hikers preparing for or recovering from Hoverla. Both seasons work well.
