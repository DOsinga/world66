Chernihiv is one of the oldest cities in Ukraine — older than Kyiv in the archaeological record, and the capital of a powerful medieval principality before the Mongol destruction of 1239. Today it is a quiet regional city of about 280,000 people on the Desna River, 130 kilometres north of Kyiv, but it contains a concentration of pre-Mongol architecture that is extraordinary: four churches from the 11th and 12th centuries still stand, making Chernihiv one of the best places in Eastern Europe to see medieval Rus construction.

The [Transfiguration Cathedral](/europe/ukraine/chernihiv/transfiguration_cathedral) on Val Hill is the oldest building in Ukraine still in use — begun around 1030 under the Chernihiv prince Mstislav, it is one of the earliest stone buildings in Rus after the construction of the Kyiv Sophia. Beside it, the [Boris and Gleb Cathedral](/europe/ukraine/chernihiv/boris_and_gleb_cathedral) (1123) and the [Piatnytska Church](/europe/ukraine/chernihiv/piatnytska_church) in the lower town (late 12th century) form an almost unbroken sequence of Romanesque-Byzantine architecture that scholars come from across the world to study. The [Catherine's Church](/europe/ukraine/chernihiv/catherines_church) at the edge of the old town is the baroque counterpart — a Ukrainian baroque masterpiece of the 1690s.

For a stranger kind of antiquity, the [Black Grave](/europe/ukraine/chernihiv/black_grave) at Boldina Hill is a 10th-century Viking burial mound — one of the largest in Europe, containing the cremated remains of a warrior prince, his horse, weapons, and treasure. It was excavated in 1872 but the mound itself remains, an unassuming grassy hill in what is now a park.

Chernihiv was badly damaged in the 2022 Russian invasion — the city was under siege for weeks before Ukrainian forces pushed the Russians back. Check current travel conditions before planning a visit.
