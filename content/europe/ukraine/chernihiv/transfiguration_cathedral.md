The Transfiguration Cathedral (Spaso-Preobrazhenskyi sobor) is the oldest building in Ukraine still in active use — a massive five-domed church built around 1030–36 in a period when Kyivan Rus was at its greatest extent. The building predates the more famous St. Sophia in Kyiv by only a few years and uses similar Byzantine construction techniques: thick brick walls, barrel-vaulted interior spaces, and thick stone piers supporting the main dome.

The exterior is austere by later standards — the carved limestone ornament and coloured tile decoration of later centuries are absent here. The interior has been restored and contains a complete early iconostasis. The cathedral stands on Val Hill (Dytynets), the fortified hilltop district that served as the princely citadel of medieval Chernihiv, surrounded by the remains of earth ramparts. Entry is charged. Allow an hour at the cathedral and the immediate hilltop complex.
