Chernivtsi's Jesuit Church was built in the late 19th century, when the city was part of the Habsburg world and Catholic orders were shaping its streetscape. Its tall forms and brickwork add another layer to a city better known for Orthodox, Armenian, and Jewish landmarks.

It is a worthwhile stop while walking the historic centre, especially if you are tracing Chernivtsi's mix of religious architecture.
