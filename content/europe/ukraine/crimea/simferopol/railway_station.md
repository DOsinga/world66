story: "The station was completed in 1951 to replace the wartime ruin of its predecessor. Its 42-metre clock tower has a curiosity: the clock face is divided by symbols of the zodiac instead of numerals. Designed by Alexei Dushkin — better known for some of Moscow's most ornate metro stations — it is one of the finest examples of Stalinist neoclassicism in the south."

A grand Stalinist-era passenger terminal at the western edge of the city centre, with a long colonnaded façade, a tall clock tower, and an interior of marble pillars and chandeliers. The station has been Crimea's main rail gateway for seventy years; before 2014 sleepers from Kyiv, Moscow, and St. Petersburg pulled in here daily.

Service has shifted dramatically with the political situation since 2014, but the building remains worth a few minutes' look even if you are not catching a train. The grand hall faces a small plaza with a statue of Lenin, a few Soviet-era cafés, and a row of taxi drivers offering trips to Yalta and Sevastopol.
