Ai-Petri (1,234 m) is the jagged limestone ridge that looms above Yalta and dominates the skyline from the coast. The name is Greek — it means "Saint Peter" — and the summit has the characteristic look of a Mediterranean limestone massif: bare grey rock, pine forest on the upper slopes, and enormous views.

The easiest ascent is by cable car from the coastal village of Miskhor, 12 km west of Yalta. The ride is spectacular: the first stage climbs through forest, the second hauls up near-vertical rock face to the summit station. At the top, a Crimean Tatar market sells honey, dried fruit, wool felt hats, and grilled meats. The views are extraordinary — the entire southern Crimean coast from Sevastopol to Feodosia on a clear day. If you have time, walk along the ridge; the karst terrain has caves, strange rock formations, and patches of mountain steppe. The descent by cable car is if anything more dramatic than the ascent.
