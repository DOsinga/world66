Livadia Palace was the summer retreat of Tsar Nicholas II and his family, a gleaming white Italian Renaissance structure set in parkland above the sea just 3 km west of Yalta. Built between 1910 and 1911 by the architect Nikolai Krasnov, it replaced an earlier palace on the same site and was intended as the family's most important private residence outside of St. Petersburg.

The palace is now a museum covering two distinct eras: the doomed Romanov dynasty and the fateful Allied summit of 1945. The Tsarist rooms show how the family actually lived — the scale is surprisingly domestic, the rooms comfortable rather than ceremonially grand. The conference rooms on the ground floor preserve the furniture and layout from February 1945. The 18-hectare park, with its formal Italian garden and views over the Black Sea, is worth an hour in itself.
