  Uchan-Su means "flying water" in Crimean Tatar — a name that made more sense in the 19th century than it does today. At 98 meters, it is the highest waterfall in Ukraine, but it flows with full force only in spring when snowmelt from the Crimean Mountains comes down in volume. Uchan-Su tumbles 98 meters down the face of the Crimean Mountains just above Yalta — the highest waterfall in Ukraine, though its drama depends entirely on the season. In spring the flow is impressive, crashing into a pool in the gorge below; by late summer it sometimes dries to a thin curtain of spray.

The waterfall is set in forested mountain terrain about 3 km above the city, reachable by road or by hiking path from Yalta. Most visitors come by marshrutka or taxi; the walk up through the forest takes about an hour and is pleasant in its own right. A café near the waterfall serves the usual hot drinks and grilled meats. Above the falls, the road continues up to the Ai-Petri plateau, and you can combine the waterfall visit with the cable car at Miskhor for a full day in the mountains.
