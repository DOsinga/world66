Kharkiv is Ukraine's second city and cultural capital — a place Ukrainians call the country's "first capital" because it served as the Soviet Ukrainian capital from 1919 to 1934. The city has a distinct character from Kyiv: younger in feel, heavily industrialised during the Soviet era, and home to a large concentration of universities that give it an intellectual energy and a permanently student-heavy street life.

[Freedom Square](/europe/ukraine/kharkiv/freedom_square) (Maidan Svobody) is one of the largest city squares in Europe, ringed by monumental Constructivist and Stalinist architecture — above all the extraordinary [Derzhprom](/europe/ukraine/kharkiv/derzhprom) building, the first major reinforced-concrete structure built in the Soviet Union. The [Annunciation Cathedral](/europe/ukraine/kharkiv/annunciation_cathedral) with its golden domes provides an older counterpoint, and the enormous [Barabashovo Market](/europe/ukraine/kharkiv/barabashovo_market) is a fascinating, slightly overwhelming place to explore. The [Kharkiv Art Museum](/europe/ukraine/kharkiv/kharkiv_art_museum) holds significant collections of Ukrainian avant-garde art from the 1920s, a period when Kharkiv was genuinely one of the creative centres of the Soviet world.

Note: Kharkiv has been severely affected by the conflict in Ukraine that began in 2022. Check current safety conditions before planning any travel.
