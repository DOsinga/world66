The Armenian Cathedral of Lviv, tucked into a narrow street north of Rynok Square, is one of the city's oldest and most atmospheric buildings. Founded by the Armenian merchant community in 1363 and expanded over the following centuries, it combines Gothic structure, Renaissance chapels, and the remarkable early-20th-century frescoes that give the interior its distinctive appearance. The murals, painted by Jan Henryk Rosen between 1925 and 1929, depict saints and biblical scenes in a style influenced by Byzantine icons and Art Nouveau simultaneously.

The adjacent Armenian courtyard — reached through a gate on Virmenska Street — is one of the most peaceful corners of the old town. The cloister-like space with its well and carved stonework is often quiet even in peak season. The cathedral's small treasury contains historical vestments and liturgical objects from the Armenian community's centuries in Lviv.
