**Mykolaiv** (still widely known by its Russian name **Nikolaev**) is a city of around 480,000 people in southern Ukraine, sitting at the confluence of the **Southern Bug** and **Inhul** rivers, about 65 km inland from the Black Sea coast. Founded in 1789 as a Russian naval shipyard by Prince Grigory Potemkin on the orders of Catherine the Great, it grew throughout the 19th and 20th centuries into one of the world's most important shipbuilding cities. The aircraft carriers of the Soviet Black Sea Fleet were assembled in its yards, and the shipyards here later built most of China's first aircraft carriers too. The city is named after **Saint Nicholas**, the patron saint of sailors — a fitting dedication for a place that shaped naval history.

Beyond the industrial legacy, Mykolaiv is a city of wide, shaded boulevards and handsome 19th-century neoclassical architecture. The centre has the quietly confident look of a provincial capital that once mattered enormously. The **Kashperovsky Park** stretches along the Bug riverbank, and the tree-lined streets of the old core retain the geometry of the original military grid laid out by Potemkin's engineers. Beneath one of those parks, archaeologists have uncovered **Dykyi Sad** — a remarkable Bronze Age fortified city dating to around 1300 BC, one of the largest such sites discovered in Eastern Europe.

The city's institutions reflect that long history seriously. The **Museum of Shipbuilding and the Fleet** is one of the finest of its kind, covering two and a half centuries of naval construction. The **Mykolaiv Astronomical Observatory**, founded in 1821, is one of the oldest in the former Russian Empire and still active as a research institution. The city's **Zoo** has a well-earned reputation as one of the best in the former Soviet Union — a real research zoo, not a tourist attraction.

**Since the Russian invasion of Ukraine in February 2022**, Mykolaiv has been close to the front line. The city was heavily shelled in 2022 and sustained significant damage to civilian infrastructure and residential districts. The situation has evolved but the region remains affected by the war. Travel is not recommended without consulting current Ukrainian government guidance and security advisories.
