Poltava sits halfway between [Kharkiv](/europe/ukraine/kharkiv) and [Kyiv](/europe/ukraine/kyiv) in central Ukraine. The city is best known as the site of the 1709 Battle of Poltava, where Peter the Great's forces defeated the Swedish army of Charles XII and their Cossack allies under Hetman Ivan Mazepa — a turning point that established Russia as a European power and sealed Ukraine's fate for two centuries.

The battle's legacy saturates the city. The battlefield south of town has a museum with Peter the Great's outsized uniform (the tsar stood nearly 2.1 metres tall), partially reconstructed earthwork redoubts, and a panoramic rotunda painting of the engagement. In the centre, the city's most distinctive feature is the Poltava Round: a circular garden from which eight avenues radiate outward, laid out in 1802 by architect Mikhail Amvrosimov in a design that makes the Column of Glory — a 22-metre cast-iron commemorative column — visible from every approaching street.

Away from the battle, Poltava has a strong literary identity. Ivan Kotlyarevsky (1769–1838) was born here, and his burlesque retelling of Virgil's Aeneid set in the Cossack world — written in vernacular Ukrainian at a time when the language had no official status — is considered the founding text of modern Ukrainian literature. His memorial house is one of the most visited sites in the city. Nikolai Gogol, who grew up in the nearby village of Hoholeve, set his early comic stories in this part of Ukraine, and his ghost still haunts the surrounding countryside.

The historic churches are worth the walk: the Assumption Cathedral is a fine example of Ukrainian Baroque with pale-blue domes; the Spaska Church is architecturally unique — a stone shell built in the late 18th century to protect a 1706 wooden church inside it.

Since Russia's full-scale invasion in February 2022, Poltava — like all of Ukraine — has been living under wartime conditions. The city has suffered missile strikes. Cultural institutions remain open when conditions allow, but travellers should check current advisories and be prepared for disruption.
