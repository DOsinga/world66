---
title: Eating Out
type: section
---

Uzhhorod sits at the intersection of Rusyn, Hungarian, and Ukrainian food cultures, and the result is a local cuisine unlike anything else in Ukraine. **Bograch** — a thick paprika-and-beef soup made over an open fire, descended from Hungarian gulyás — is the local speciality, sold at the city's annual Bograch Festival in September and on the menus of almost every traditional restaurant year-round. **Banosh** (cornmeal porridge cooked in cream and served with bryndza sheep's cheese and pork cracklings) is the other regional staple, hearty and entirely satisfying after a day in the Carpathians.

Look for restaurants serving **Zakarpattian cuisine** specifically — the term is used to distinguish local Rusyn and Carpathian cooking from generic Ukrainian. Dishes will typically involve forest mushrooms, smoked meats, game in season, and fermented dairy. The city also has a small but genuine café culture with good coffee and excellent pastries, a legacy of the Austro-Hungarian period.
