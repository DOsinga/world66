The Werwolf (*Wehrwolf*) complex at Strizhavka, about 8 kilometres north of Vinnytsia, was one of Hitler's three forward command headquarters on the Eastern Front, used briefly in 1942 and 1943 for military operations against the Soviet Union. The site was built by Organisation Todt using forced labour in great secrecy, with the construction workers reportedly killed after completion to prevent disclosure.

The complex was blown up as German forces retreated in 1944 and today only concrete foundations, bunker walls, and scattered debris remain among the pines. There is minimal formal interpretation on site — the visit is atmospheric rather than educational in the conventional sense. The ruins are accessible by car or taxi, and there is usually a caretaker at the site. A small museum nearby provides historical context. The combination of forest setting, wartime history, and the almost total absence of official signage gives the place a genuinely eerie character.
