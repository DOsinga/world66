Zaporizhzhia is not a soft city. It is a broad, industrial place on the Dnipro, shaped by steel, aircraft engines, Soviet planning, and the river crossing that once marked the land "beyond the rapids." For a traveller, its strength is the way that heavy modern history sits beside one of Ukraine's most important symbolic landscapes.

[Khortytsia](/europe/ukraine/zaporizhzhia/khortytsia) is the reason to come. The island lies inside the city limits, but it feels separate: granite banks, steppe, oak groves, views of the dam, and the memory of the Zaporozhian Cossacks. The recreated [Zaporizhian Sich](/europe/ukraine/zaporizhzhia/zaporizhian_sich) is not an untouched relic, but it gives form to the Cossack world that still matters deeply in Ukrainian identity.

The mainland city adds scale rather than prettiness. [DniproHES](/europe/ukraine/zaporizhzhia/dniprohes) was one of the great engineering projects of the early Soviet period, and the riverfront around it gives the clearest view of Zaporizhzhia's ambition and damage.
