The Holy Trinity Church, built in 1720, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site — part of the collective designation of sixteen wooden tserkvas (Eastern Orthodox churches) of the Carpathian Region spanning Ukraine and Poland. The listing recognises these timber structures as outstanding examples of a regional vernacular building tradition that developed over several centuries.

The church has three wooden naves and a brick sacristy, the latter added when the original wooden predecessor burned in 1717. The interior is dominated by the iconostasis, with approximately 50 icons painted in the early 18th century. The wooden carving, from linden wood, is remarkably fine for a building of this scale. The church now belongs to the Ukrainian Greek Catholic Church and remains in active use — visit in the morning for the best chance of finding it open.
