---
title: Things to Do
type: section
---

Church Stretton's main attraction is the landscape on its doorstep. The Long Mynd is the showpiece — a National Trust moorland plateau cut by deep valleys that locals call batches, each one a different character of walk. Carding Mill Valley is the busiest and most accessible, with a stream to follow and a National Trust tea room at the top. Ashes Hollow and Light Spout Hollow offer quieter alternatives with waterfalls and heather slopes that are almost deserted outside summer weekends.

East of the valley, Caer Caradoc rises sharply to an Iron Age hillfort with open views across Shropshire, Herefordshire, and the Welsh hills beyond. The ascent from the town takes around an hour. The Stretton Hills — a line of volcanic ridges — offer shorter alternatives at Ragleth Hill and The Lawley.

In town, St Laurence's Church is the architectural highlight. It has a rare sheela na gig, a pre-Norman carved figure above the north doorway, and a 12th-century nave that predates the Victorian expansion that gave the town most of its character. The town's former spa credentials survive in a few Victorian villas, though the hydropathic establishments are long gone.
