Coventry is a city defined by reinvention. Devastated by German bombing on the night of 14 November 1940, it was rebuilt in a style that mixed modernist ambition with the scars of what was lost. The result is a city that can feel jarring — brutalist concrete next to medieval fragments — but that is part of what makes it interesting.

The [ruined shell of the old cathedral](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/birmingham_and_west_midlands/coventry/coventry_cathedral) stands open to the sky beside Basil Spence's striking 1962 replacement, connected by a walkway. The contrast between the two is one of the most powerful war memorials in England. Jacob Epstein's [bronze of St Michael](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/birmingham_and_west_midlands/coventry/st_michaels_victory) guards the entrance; inside, the Graham Sutherland tapestry and John Piper's baptistry window are worth the visit alone.

The medieval city was not entirely destroyed. [St Mary's Guildhall](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/birmingham_and_west_midlands/coventry/st_marys_guildhall), where Mary Queen of Scots was held prisoner, survived intact and has a magnificent 14th-century tapestry and carved ceiling. [Spon Street](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/birmingham_and_west_midlands/coventry/spon_street) preserves a row of timber-framed buildings, some relocated from elsewhere in the city. [Ford's Hospital](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/birmingham_and_west_midlands/coventry/fords_hospital) and [Bond's Hospital](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/birmingham_and_west_midlands/coventry/bonds_hospital) are two Tudor almshouses whose courtyards survive in almost original condition. The [Herbert Art Gallery and Museum](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/birmingham_and_west_midlands/coventry/herbert_art_gallery) does a good job of telling the city's story, from Lady Godiva through the watchmaking and bicycle industries to the Blitz and the motor trade.

The [Coventry Transport Museum](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/birmingham_and_west_midlands/coventry/coventry_transport_museum) is one of the most underrated free museums in England — Coventry produced Britain's first production bicycles, the Triumph, the Jaguar, and the Daimler, and the museum makes that history genuinely interesting. [FarGo Village](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/birmingham_and_west_midlands/coventry/fargo_village) on Far Gosford Street is the liveliest eating and drinking destination, with craft beer from [Twisted Barrel Ale](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/birmingham_and_west_midlands/coventry/twisted_barrel_ale) and independent food traders operating from repurposed railway arches.

Coventry was UK City of Culture in 2021, which left a legacy of renovated public spaces and a livelier arts scene than the city had before. It rewards a day or two rather than a quick stop, especially for anyone interested in how cities survive catastrophe and reinvent themselves.
