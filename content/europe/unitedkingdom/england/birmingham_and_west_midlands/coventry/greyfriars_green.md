Greyfriars Green is a small park on the southern edge of the city centre, notable for the skeletal remains of the medieval Franciscan friary that stand at its northern end. The Grey Friars arrived in Coventry in the 1230s and built a priory that survived until the Dissolution of 1539; what remains today is the 14th-century spire and tower of Christ Church, preserved as a picturesque ruin in the middle of the Georgian terraces that surround the green.

The ruins were given new life when the tower was incorporated into a rebuilt Christ Church during the 19th century, and that Victorian church was itself bombed in 1940. Now the spire stands alone, roofless and open, with the green laid out around it. It's a quieter and less visited corner than the cathedral quarter, but worth the short detour for the combination of medieval stonework, Victorian park, and the general improbability of a 700-year-old Franciscan spire standing in the middle of a city centre. The green itself is pleasant on a good day, with mature trees and a fountain.
