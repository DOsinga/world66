Wolverhampton's industrial heritage extends well beyond the Black Country ironworks. The Sunbeam Motor Car Company was one of Britain's most innovative early carmakers — a Wolverhampton firm that built Grand Prix winners and land speed record breakers in the 1920s before the Depression ended its independent existence.

The original factory site at Sunbeamland, just north of the city centre, is largely built over now, but the name survives in the area around Villiers Street. The Wolverhampton City Archives hold records and photographs of the works. For enthusiasts of early motoring history, understanding that one of the pioneers of the modern racing car was built here, in a Black Country factory, is one of the city's less expected historical dimensions.
