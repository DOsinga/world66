Stratford-upon-Avon is one of England's most visited towns, and almost all of that attention flows from a single fact: William Shakespeare was born here in 1564 and died here in 1616. The town is relentlessly dedicated to this connection, with five Shakespeare properties administered by a single charitable trust, a major repertory theatre that has been staging his plays since 1879, and a retail economy oriented almost entirely around literary tourism. It can feel exhausting. But the town is also genuinely attractive — a compact market town on a bend of the Avon, with good Tudor architecture, a pleasant riverside, and a theatre culture that takes its work seriously.

The [Shakespeare's Birthplace](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/birmingham_and_west_midlands/stratforduponavon/shakespeares_birthplace) on Henley Street is the obvious starting point, and the [Royal Shakespeare Theatre](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/birmingham_and_west_midlands/stratforduponavon/royal_shakespeare_theatre) on the riverbank its most impressive building. But the most poignant site is [Anne Hathaway's Cottage](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/birmingham_and_west_midlands/stratforduponavon/anne_hathaways_cottage) in the village of Shottery, fifteen minutes' walk from the town centre — the farmhouse where Shakespeare's wife grew up is one of the best-preserved thatched Tudor buildings in England and feels genuinely connected to real lives rather than tourism mythology.

The [Holy Trinity Church](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/birmingham_and_west_midlands/stratforduponavon/holy_trinity_church), where Shakespeare is buried, stands at the south end of town beside the river. You can see the grave for a small charge to the church fabric fund; it is a plain stone slab with an unusually curt warning against disturbing it. The surrounding churchyard and riverside meadow are among the most pleasant outdoor spaces in the town.

Beyond Shakespeare, Stratford has a good weekend market, some decent restaurants, and a productive cottage-garden and countryside surrounding the town. [Warwick Castle](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/birmingham_and_west_midlands/warwick) is less than ten miles north and easily combined with a Stratford visit.
