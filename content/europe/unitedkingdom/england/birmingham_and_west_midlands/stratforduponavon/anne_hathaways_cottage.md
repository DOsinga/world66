Anne Hathaway's Cottage in the village of Shottery, about fifteen minutes' walk from the town centre, is one of the most beautiful and best-preserved thatched farmhouses in England. The twelve-room thatched house, surrounded by period gardens including an orchard of traditional apple varieties, was the home of the woman who became Shakespeare's wife, and it has been in continuous preservation since its acquisition by the Shakespeare Birthplace Trust. Unlike some of the town-centre Shakespeare properties, this one feels genuinely connected to the period rather than reconstructed for tourism.

The cottage itself dates from the fifteenth century, with later additions, and it remained in the Hathaway family until 1892, meaning it was inhabited for nearly four centuries before becoming a museum. The interior is largely furnished with period pieces, some of which actually belonged to the Hathaway family; the carved oak settle (a wooden bench-chest) in the parlour is believed to be the one on which Shakespeare courted Anne.

The garden is perhaps as rewarding as the cottage itself — the cottage garden plantings are among the finest in Warwickshire, and in summer the combination of thatched roof, timber frame, climbing roses, and kitchen garden vegetables is exactly as appealing as it sounds. The orchard walk adds another dimension in autumn.
