  At its peak in the early 20th century, Walsall produced nearly all of Britain's saddlery and lorinery — the metal fittings for horse harness. The town's craftsmen supplied the cavalry in both World Wars, and Queen Victoria's coronation coach harness was made here. The Walsall Leather Museum tells the story of the town's centuries-old leather trade. Walsall was for two hundred years the most important centre of saddlery and leatherwork in Britain, and the museum occupies a Victorian factory building that was itself once a working saddlery. Demonstrations by working craftspeople are part of the experience — you can watch saddles and bags being hand-stitched using traditional methods.

The displays cover the full range of Walsall's leather output: saddlery, military equipment, bags, belts, and small leather goods. The trade has shrunk dramatically since the 1970s, but a few workshops survive in the town and some of their products are on sale in the museum shop. Entry is free.
