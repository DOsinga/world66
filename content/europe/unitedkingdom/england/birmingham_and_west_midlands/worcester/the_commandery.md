The Commandery is one of the most atmospheric historic buildings in Worcester. The core of the structure dates from around 1200, originally a hospital dedicated to St Wulfstan. By the 16th century it had passed into private hands and become a substantial timber-framed town house, and it served as the Royalist headquarters during the Battle of Worcester in 1651 — the final engagement of the English Civil War.

The building now houses an exhibition covering the Civil War and the broader history of the site over eight centuries. The Painted Chamber, with its 15th-century wall paintings, is particularly striking. The great hall has period furnishings and the whole complex retains a genuine sense of age rather than museum sterility. The garden behind the building is pleasant for a break.

Admission is charged. Check opening times before visiting as the Commandery closes on certain weekdays in the off-season.
