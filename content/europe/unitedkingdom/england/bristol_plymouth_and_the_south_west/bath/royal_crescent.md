story: >
  When John Wood the Younger completed the Royal Crescent in 1774, the rear facades were left entirely plain — the Crescent was designed purely as a spectacle to be viewed from the front. Each of the thirty houses behind the unified facade was finished individually by its owner, which is why the back of the building is a chaotic jumble of different styles and extensions.
---

The most photographed building in Bath, and arguably the finest crescent in Britain. Thirty terraced houses form a sweeping arc of 114 Ionic columns, looking out over a grand lawn that slopes down to Royal Victoria Park. The effect is theatrical and deliberate — John Wood the Younger designed it to be seen from below, the golden limestone glowing in afternoon light.

No. 1 Royal Crescent is open to the public as a museum, faithfully restored to show how a wealthy Georgian family would have lived. The lawn in front is one of the best spots in the city for a picnic when the weather is kind. Walk here from the Circus via Brock Street for the full architectural experience.
