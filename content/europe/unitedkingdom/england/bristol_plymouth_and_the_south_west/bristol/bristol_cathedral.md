Bristol Cathedral stands on College Green at the top of Park Street, its twin towers a landmark from several directions. Founded as a monastery in 1140 and elevated to cathedral status in 1542, it has been continuously built and rebuilt across nine centuries, resulting in an unusually coherent and impressive interior. The 14th-century choir and presbytery are the centrepiece — an example of English Gothic design that was architecturally well ahead of its time.

The cathedral is surrounded by the College Green, a wide open space bordered by the Council House and the remains of the old city. Concerts, services, and occasional events make it a living building rather than a museum piece. Entry is free; donations are welcome. The south transept has a memorial to John Cabot that reinforces Bristol's relationship with the first documented European voyage to North America.
