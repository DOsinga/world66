Pero's Bridge is a pedestrian bascule bridge crossing the harbourside, opened in 1999 and named after Pero Jones — an enslaved man brought to Bristol from the West Indies in the 18th century, who lived and died in the city without freedom. The bridge is distinguished by its two large yoke-shaped counterweights that reference the yokes used to restrain enslaved people; it is a deliberately discomforting piece of public design.

The bridge connects the Bordeaux Quay side of the harbour to the Lloyds Amphitheatre area and is part of the main harbourside walking route. It lifts periodically to allow vessels to pass through to the inner harbour. The naming and the design make it part of Bristol's ongoing reckoning with its slave trade heritage — a subject that has become considerably more prominent since the toppling of the Colston statue in 2020. The nearby Georgian House Museum at 7 Great George Street (where Pero Jones lived as an enslaved man) is a complementary visit.
