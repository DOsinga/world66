Porth Nanven, also known as Cot Valley, is a small beach at the end of a steep valley half a mile south of St Just. It is unusual for a Cornish beach in that the shore is made entirely of large rounded boulders rather than sand — the so-called "dinosaur egg" beach, though the name is a 20th-century invention. The boulders are smooth and varied in colour, and the beach is undeniably striking, though not comfortable for lying on.

The cove is backed by the narrow Cot Valley, which runs inland between old mine workings and scrubby vegetation. It is a good spot for rock pooling and snorkelling in calm conditions — the water here is exceptionally clear. The South West Coast Path passes through and the short walk north along the cliffs to Cape Cornwall is one of the best stretches of the path in the far west. Parking is limited to a small lane above the cove.
