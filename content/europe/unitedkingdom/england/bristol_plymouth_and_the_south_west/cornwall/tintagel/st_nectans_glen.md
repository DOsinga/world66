St Nectan's Glen is a wooded valley a mile east of Tintagel village, reached by a footpath that follows the course of the Rocky Valley stream. The main feature is St Nectan's Kieve — a waterfall that pours through a natural rock basin and then down through a hole in the rock face below. The word "kieve" is Cornish for "round tub" or "bucket," and the formation is immediately why: the water drops into a circular pool before finding its outlet. The setting — mossy rocks, overhanging trees, and the sound of water — is genuinely magical and far quieter than the castle.

The site is privately owned and charges admission. The path to the waterfall is well-maintained, but involves some steps and uneven ground. The walk from Tintagel takes around 30 minutes each way. A cafe and small shop operate at the site in season. People have been tying ribbons, tokens, and crystals to the trees and rocks here for decades, which gives the place an additional layer of atmosphere.
