Truro is the only city in Cornwall and its administrative, commercial, and cultural centre. It sits at the head of the [Truro River](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/bristol_plymouth_and_the_south_west/cornwall/truro/truro_river), a tidal estuary that eventually opens into the Carrick Roads, one of the world's largest natural harbours.

The city's most striking feature is its [cathedral](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/bristol_plymouth_and_the_south_west/cornwall/truro/truro_cathedral) — the Cathedral Church of the Blessed Virgin Mary — built between 1880 and 1910 in a neo-Gothic style that incorporates the south aisle of the earlier parish church of St Mary. Three spires dominate the city centre, visible from the surrounding hills. The city centre is compact and walkable, with Georgian townhouses and Victorian streets lined with independent shops and cafes.

The [Royal Cornwall Museum](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/bristol_plymouth_and_the_south_west/cornwall/truro/royal_cornwall_museum) on River Street holds the finest collection of Cornish minerals in the world, along with strong archaeology and art galleries. The [Hall for Cornwall](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/bristol_plymouth_and_the_south_west/cornwall/truro/hall_for_cornwall) is one of the south-west's main regional theatres. The [Lander Monument](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/bristol_plymouth_and_the_south_west/cornwall/truro/lander_monument) in Lemon Street Gardens commemorates Richard Lander, a Truro-born explorer who was the first European to trace the course of the River Niger.

Truro makes a practical base for exploring central and south Cornwall, and is on the main railway line from London Paddington. Falmouth is a short bus or boat ride away, and the coast at Perranporth and Portscatho is within easy reach.
