The Cathedral Church of the Blessed Virgin Mary is the defining landmark of Truro, its three Gothic spires visible from all directions as you approach the city. It was built between 1880 and 1910 in a neo-Gothic style by architect John Loughborough Pearson, and it remains one of the finest Victorian cathedrals in England — less famous than many, which makes a visit feel less crowded and more rewarding.

The interior is tall, light, and elegantly proportioned. The original 16th-century south aisle of St Mary's has been incorporated into the structure. The Father Willis organ, installed in 1887, is considered one of the finest in the country and occasional recitals are held here. Entry is free, though donations are expected.

The cathedral sits at the heart of the city centre, making it an easy starting point for exploring on foot.
