The Market Place at the heart of Devizes is genuinely impressive — a broad, irregularly shaped square ringed by coaching inns, Georgian townhouses, and the domed Corn Exchange. At its centre stands the Market Cross, topped by the notorious 1753 inscription about Ruth Pierce. The square has been the commercial centre of Devizes since the medieval period, and on Thursdays it still fills with market stalls selling local produce, clothing, and household goods, much as it has for centuries.

The surrounding architecture rewards a slow walk. The Bear Hotel, a former coaching inn on the square, has been trading since at least the 18th century. The Corn Exchange opposite, with its neoclassical façade, dates from 1857. Even on non-market days the square is a good place to sit at one of the pub tables and watch the town going about its business. Devizes has avoided the worst of chain-store homogenisation, and the streets leading off the square still have independent shops.
