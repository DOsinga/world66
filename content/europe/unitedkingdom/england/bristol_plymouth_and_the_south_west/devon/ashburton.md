Ashburton is a small town on the southeastern edge of Dartmoor in Devon, one of the four historic stannary towns where tin was weighed and taxed in the Middle Ages. It sits in a valley where the open moor gives way to green farmland, and it has more character than its size suggests.

The main street is handsome — slate-hung and colour-washed buildings, a few good antique shops, and a handful of independent cafes and restaurants. The town has become something of a foodie destination for its size. The Golden Lion and other pubs serve decent food, and there is a good deli scene.

Ashburton is a practical base for exploring southeastern Dartmoor. [Buckland Beacon](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/bristol_plymouth_and_the_south_west/devon/ashburton/buckland_beacon) and the Dart Valley are all close. The [River Ashburn](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/bristol_plymouth_and_the_south_west/devon/ashburton/river_ashburn) runs through town and down to the Dart, where wild swimming spots are popular in summer. [Buckfast Abbey](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/bristol_plymouth_and_the_south_west/devon/ashburton/buckfast_abbey), a working Benedictine monastery about two miles north, is worth a visit for the remarkable story of its rebuilding as much as for the building itself. [St Andrew's Church](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/bristol_plymouth_and_the_south_west/devon/ashburton/st_andrews_church) at the bottom of the main street is the town's finest medieval building. [Holne Bridge](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/bristol_plymouth_and_the_south_west/devon/ashburton/holne_bridge), a medieval crossing of the Dart, is an easy excursion and a fine starting point for walking in the valley.
