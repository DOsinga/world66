Barnstaple is the main town of North Devon and has been a market town since Saxon times. The covered [Pannier Market](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/bristol_plymouth_and_the_south_west/devon/barnstaple/pannier_market), built in 1855, still operates several days a week selling local produce, crafts, and antiques. Butchers Row, a line of Victorian shops beside the market, is one of the most photographed streets in Devon. [Queen Anne's Walk](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/bristol_plymouth_and_the_south_west/devon/barnstaple/queen_annes_walk), the 18th-century merchants' exchange on the riverside, is another landmark worth seeking out.

The town sits at the head of the Taw estuary and makes a natural base for exploring the North Devon coast. The [Tarka Trail](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/bristol_plymouth_and_the_south_west/devon/barnstaple/tarka_trail), a walking and cycling path named after Henry Williamson's novel, runs from Barnstaple along a disused railway line to Bideford and the coast. The surfing beaches of Croyde and Woolacombe are less than half an hour away, and Exmoor National Park begins just to the east. The [Long Bridge](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/bristol_plymouth_and_the_south_west/devon/barnstaple/long_bridge) across the Taw, in use since the 15th century, connects the town centre to the station and the trail.
