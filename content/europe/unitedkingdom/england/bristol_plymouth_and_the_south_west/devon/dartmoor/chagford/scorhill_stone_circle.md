Scorhill Stone Circle — properly Gidleigh Stone Circle or Steep Hill Stone Circle — stands on the open moorland above the North Teign River, about three miles west of Chagford. It is one of the largest and best-preserved Bronze Age stone circles on Dartmoor, with stones standing up to 1.5 metres high in a ring roughly 27 metres across.

The walk to Scorhill from Chagford takes about an hour across open moorland, following the North Teign River for part of the route. The surrounding landscape includes the remains of further Bronze Age structures: hut circles, field systems, and a ceremonial avenue of stones leading toward the circle. There is a wild, unrestored quality to the site that is quite different from the managed monuments of the main archaeological sites. The return route can be varied to take in Batworthy Corner and the river below.
