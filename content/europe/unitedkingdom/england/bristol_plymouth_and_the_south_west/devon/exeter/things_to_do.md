The Cathedral Close is the natural starting point. [Exeter Cathedral](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/bristol_plymouth_and_the_south_west/devon/exeter/exeter_cathedral) dominates the south side of the city centre, and the close around it is one of the most attractive cathedral precincts in England. [Mol's Coffee House](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/bristol_plymouth_and_the_south_west/devon/exeter/cathedral_close) on the north side of the close is an Elizabethan timber-framed building that has been serving visitors since the 16th century.

Below the streets, the [Underground Passages](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/bristol_plymouth_and_the_south_west/devon/exeter/underground_passages) offer guided tours through medieval water supply tunnels — unique in England. Sections of the [Roman walls](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/bristol_plymouth_and_the_south_west/devon/exeter/roman_walls) survive throughout the city centre, some standing over five metres high.

The [Royal Albert Memorial Museum](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/bristol_plymouth_and_the_south_west/devon/exeter/royal_albert_memorial_museum) on Queen Street is one of England's best regional museums, free to enter and covering natural history, world cultures, and fine art. Down by the water, [Exeter Quayside](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/bristol_plymouth_and_the_south_west/devon/exeter/exeter_quayside) along the Exe makes a good afternoon: old warehouses converted to cafes, kayak and canoe hire, and a pleasant towpath.
