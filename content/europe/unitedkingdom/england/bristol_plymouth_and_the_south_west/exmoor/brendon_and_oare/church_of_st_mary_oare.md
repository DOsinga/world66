Blackmore's Lorna Doone (1869), the novel's climax takes place in this very church: the bride Lorna is shot through the window at the altar by her outlaw cousin Carver Doone on her wedding day. Blackmore based the building exactly on the real church, and the window through which the shot comes is still pointed out to visitors. The church dates from the 12th century and is otherwise entirely unremarkable — which makes the contrast between its modest interior and its fictional drama rather pleasing.

The tiny 12th-century Church of St Mary at Oare sits in a meadow above the East Lyn River, surrounded by ancient yews and sheep-grazed turf. It holds perhaps eighty people. Were it not for R. D. Blackmore's novel *Lorna Doone*, published in 1869, almost no one outside the parish would know it existed.

Blackmore used this church as the setting for the novel's dramatic climax. Today it is the most visited building in an otherwise roadless part of Exmoor — a strange kind of literary pilgrimage. The interior is simple: white-washed walls, box pews, a small gallery. The windows are clear glass, and the view of the moorland outside is better than any stained glass could be.
