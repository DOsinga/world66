Newton Abbot is a market town in South Devon, inland from Torbay and sitting at the head of the Teign estuary. It is primarily a commercial and transport hub for the surrounding area — the railway junction was historically important, and the town still serves as a practical base for exploring [Dartmoor](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/bristol_plymouth_and_the_south_west/devon/dartmoor), the Teign Valley, and the Torbay coast.

The [weekly market](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/bristol_plymouth_and_the_south_west/newtonabbot/newton_abbot_market) is a lively affair — a market town since 1220 and still trading on Wednesdays — and the centre has decent shops and cafes around [St Leonard's Tower](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/bristol_plymouth_and_the_south_west/newtonabbot/st_leonards_tower), the medieval stub on which William of Orange's declaration was first read in 1688. Just outside the centre, [Bradley House](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/bristol_plymouth_and_the_south_west/newtonabbot/bradley_house) is a remarkably intact 15th-century manor, and the [Templer Way](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/bristol_plymouth_and_the_south_west/newtonabbot/teign_estuary_trail) follows the Teign estuary out to the sea.

Dartmoor's open moorland begins just a short drive inland.
