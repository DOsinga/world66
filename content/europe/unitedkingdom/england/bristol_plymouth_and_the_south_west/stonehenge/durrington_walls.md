Durrington Walls is not as visually obvious as Stonehenge, but it is one of the keys to understanding the prehistoric landscape around it. This huge Neolithic henge once enclosed a settlement and ceremonial complex, tying the famous stone circle to a much broader world of timber circles, feasting, processions, and seasonal gathering.

The site rewards imagination more than photography. Walk the earthworks, read the landscape, and think of Stonehenge not as an isolated monument but as part of a ritual geography stretching across the chalk. For travellers with any interest in archaeology, it is a serious upgrade to the standard Stonehenge visit.
