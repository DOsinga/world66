  Durlston was assembled in the 1860s by George Burt, a Swanage-born stone merchant who had made a fortune in London and bought up the headland to turn it into an educational pleasure park. The Great Globe, the lettered seats with carved Bible verses, and the lighthouse all date from Burt's improving vision.

Durlston Country Park is 320 acres of clifftop and meadow at the southern end of Swanage, with the best coastal walking on the Isle of Purbeck. The South West Coast Path runs through the park and links a series of features: Durlston Castle (a Victorian folly housing a cafe and visitor centre), the Great Globe, Anvil Point Lighthouse, and the Tilly Whim Caves stone quarries below the cliff. Wildflower meadows in early summer, dolphins offshore from May to September, peregrine falcons on the cliff face.

The visitor centre at Durlston Castle is the best starting point — free parking nearby, maps, a cafe, and a viewing telescope on the terrace pointed at the Channel. Allow three to four hours to do the headland properly on foot.
