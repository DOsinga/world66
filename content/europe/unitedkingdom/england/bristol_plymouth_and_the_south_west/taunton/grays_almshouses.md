Gray's Almshouses are a terrace of six small almshouses built in 1635 by the wealthy cloth merchant Robert Gray for the relief of "ten poor single women" of Taunton. They survive almost unchanged: a stone façade of mullioned windows and slate-hung gables, the founder's coat of arms above the central doorway.

The almshouses are still in residential use, so visitors should be discreet. The exterior is the point — a textbook example of early seventeenth-century English charitable architecture, and unusually intact. The founder's tomb, with a recumbent stone effigy, can be seen inside the Minster church a hundred metres away.
