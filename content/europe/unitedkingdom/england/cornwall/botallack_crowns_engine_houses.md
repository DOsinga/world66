The Crowns Engine Houses at Botallack are the single most photographed image of Cornwall's mining past: two roofless granite engine houses stacked down the cliff face, close enough to the sea that storm waves have been known to reach them, built to pump and haul from tin and copper workings that once extended far out under the seabed. They're part of the Cornwall and West Devon Mining Landscape, a UNESCO World Heritage Site recognising the region's outsized role in the Industrial Revolution.

The mine closed in 1914, but the setting made it an obvious filming location centuries of erosion couldn't improve on — the BBC's Poldark used Botallack extensively for Ross Poldark's fictional Wheal Leisure. A coastal path loop from the National Trust car park takes in the engine houses, the cliffs, and the wider scatter of mining ruins along this stretch of coast, known locally as the Tin Coast.
