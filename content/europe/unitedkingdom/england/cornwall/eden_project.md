The Eden Project fills a former china clay quarry near St Austell with two enormous geodesic biomes — transparent bubble-clusters of hexagonal panels — that between them recreate a tropical rainforest and a Mediterranean landscape, planted with more than a million specimens from around the world. From a distance the whole site looks faintly extraterrestrial, an odd, futuristic thing to find dropped into a Cornish china clay pit.

It opened in 2001 as both a visitor attraction and a working education charity focused on plants, sustainability, and humanity's relationship with the natural world, and it has become one of Cornwall's biggest draws, busy with families and school groups especially in summer. The rainforest biome is properly hot and humid — expect to peel off a layer inside — while the Mediterranean biome is a gentler, drier walk through olive groves and vines.
