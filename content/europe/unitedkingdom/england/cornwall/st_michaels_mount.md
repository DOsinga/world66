St Michael's Mount is a small tidal island in Mount's Bay, crowned by a medieval castle and topped with subtropical terraced gardens, connected to the village of Marazion by a cobbled causeway that disappears under the sea twice a day. Cross on foot at low tide, or take the ferry across when the water's in — either way, the approach across open sand or water to a castle on a rock is most of the point.

The Mount has been a place of pilgrimage and fortification since at least the medieval period, and has belonged to the St Aubyn family since the 17th century, who still live in part of the castle. It's often compared to France's Mont Saint-Michel, which shares both the tidal-island setup and a dedication to the same saint. Allow half a day: the climb up through the gardens, the castle rooms, and the small harbour village at the base are all worth the time, and the tide table decides your schedule either way.
