Cornwall's landscape does a lot of the work for its strangest attractions: a castle that becomes an island twice a day, mining ruins balanced on cliffs above the Atlantic, a stone circle with a folklore excuse for its own existence, and a former china clay quarry turned into something that looks faintly extraterrestrial. Add a castle ruin tangled up in Arthurian legend and you have a county that rewards the odd detour as much as the obvious coast path.

None of these need much explanation once you're standing in front of them — Cornwall's strangeness tends to be visual first, historical second.
