Tintagel Castle sits split across a rocky headland and a near-island on Cornwall's rugged north coast, the ruins of a 13th-century castle built partly to cash in on a legend that was already old by then: that this was the birthplace of King Arthur, a story first written down by Geoffrey of Monmouth in the 12th century. Archaeology has found something more historically solid underneath the legend — the remains of a high-status early medieval settlement, possibly a stronghold of the kings of Dumnonia, with imported Mediterranean pottery suggesting real long-distance trade.

Whatever you make of Arthur, the setting does the rest of the work: waves breaking against sheer cliffs on both sides, a scattering of stone footings across the headland, and Merlin's Cave accessible below at low tide. It's a steep, weather-exposed site — good boots and a coat are not optional.
