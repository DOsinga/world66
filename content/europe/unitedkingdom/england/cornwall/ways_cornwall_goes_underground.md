Cornwall's most memorable places are often the ones below ground level rather than on top of it. At low tide you can walk clean through Merlin's Cave, a 90-metre sea tunnel running beneath Tintagel's castle headland, daylight visible at both ends. Near St Just, Carn Euny's fogou is a corbelled underground passage built more than 2,000 years ago for a purpose nobody has ever agreed on — cold storage, ritual, refuge — reached by ducking through a low stone opening into total darkness.

The clifftop tin and copper mines take the theme further still. At Levant, shafts once ran more than a mile out under the seabed, close enough that miners could hear the Atlantic breaking directly overhead; the beam engine that pumped them is the last of its kind in the world still running under steam. Geevor, next door, lets you follow a guided tunnel into the same mining landscape. Add St Nectan's Glen, where a waterfall drops clean through a hole worn in solid rock, and Cornwall's underground side turns out to be as strange as anything on its coast path.
