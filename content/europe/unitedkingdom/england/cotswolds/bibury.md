Bibury is a small village on the River Coln that the designer William Morris is said to have called the most beautiful village in England — a claim it has never quite lived down, for better and worse. The reason is Arlington Row, a terrace of steeply gabled stone cottages built in 1380 as a monastic wool store and converted into weavers' cottages in the 17th century; it's one of the most photographed rows of buildings in the country, and one of the most recognisable images of the Cotswolds generally.

Beyond Arlington Row, Bibury is a proper working village rather than a museum piece: a trout farm on the Coln, the Church of St Mary with Saxon origins, and water meadows where the river runs shallow and clear over gravel. It gets busy — coach parties and photographers cluster on the little bridge opposite Arlington Row through the middle of the day — so an early morning or late afternoon visit gets you the village with far fewer people in the frame.
