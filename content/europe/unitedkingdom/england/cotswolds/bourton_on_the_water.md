Bourton-on-the-Water earns its "Venice of the Cotswolds" nickname honestly: the River Windrush runs shallow and wide directly through the middle of the village, crossed by a string of low, flat stone footbridges, with grass verges running right down to the water instead of railings. It's one of the most visited villages in the Cotswolds, and it shows — the wide main street fills with coach parties and ice-cream queues in summer.

The village's oddest attraction is the Model Village, a scale replica of Bourton itself, built in the 1930s at one-ninth scale from the same Cotswold stone — a village modelling itself, tucked behind a pub garden. There's also a motor museum and a bird park nearby. Bourton works best either very early, before the crowds arrive, or as a stop on a longer loop through the quieter villages nearby.
