Broadway takes its name literally: a wide, grass-fringed high street of golden-stone houses, wider than most Cotswold villages ever bother to be, lined with tea rooms, antique shops, and the odd genuinely old coaching inn. It's long been nicknamed the "Jewel of the Cotswolds," and it drew an early wave of Victorian artists and, later, Hollywood: the actors Gordon Selfridge and Mary Pickford both had houses here at different times.

On the hill above the village stands Broadway Tower, a castellated folly built in 1798 for the Earl of Coventry, supposedly at the suggestion of his wife, who wondered whether a beacon on the hill would be visible from her house in Worcester some miles away. William Morris later used it as a country retreat, and on a clear day the tower's viewing platform reportedly looks over parts of sixteen counties. The tower and the surrounding country park make for a short, steep walk up from the village with one of the best views in the Cotswolds at the top.
