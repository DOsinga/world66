Castle Combe sits in a wooded valley on the Wiltshire edge of the Cotswolds, a single street of golden stone cottages running down to a 14th-century market cross and a packhorse bridge over the By Brook — small enough to see in twenty minutes, and often called, with real competition for the title, the prettiest village in England. Part of the effect is deliberate: the village has resisted new building, satellite dishes, and visible road markings for decades specifically to keep the look intact.

That unspoiled look has made it a favourite film location — the 1966 Doctor Dolittle and Steven Spielberg's War Horse both shot here, along with several period dramas — and the village leans into it without quite becoming a theme park. There's a church with fine tombs of the Scrope family, a historic coaching inn, and the by-then-obligatory stream of visitors photographing the market cross. Park outside the village and walk in; there's nowhere for cars in the centre and nowhere would want them there anyway.
