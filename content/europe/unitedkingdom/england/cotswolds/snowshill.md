Snowshill is a tiny, largely traffic-free village of honey-stone cottages around a small green — pleasant enough on its own terms, but the real reason to come is Snowshill Manor, a Tudor house that its last owner, the architect and collector Charles Paget Wade, filled with more than 22,000 objects before donating the lot to the National Trust in 1951. Suits of Samurai armour, bicycles, musical instruments, clocks, toys, weavers' tools, model ships — Wade collected by craftsmanship and colour rather than logic, and arranged it all in deliberately atmospheric, dimly lit rooms he didn't even live in himself, preferring a cottage in the grounds.

The Arts and Crafts garden around the manor, designed by Wade with a series of linked outdoor "rooms," is worth the visit on its own. The village itself has one pub and no shop to speak of — come for the manor and the walking, not for anywhere to eat lunch.
