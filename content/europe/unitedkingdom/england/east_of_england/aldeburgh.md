Aldeburgh is a small town on the Suffolk coast, set behind a long shingle beach where fishing boats still sell the day's catch from huts above the tide line. It has the old-fashioned look of an English seaside town, but its identity is sharper than that: music, sea wind, good fish, and a constant awareness that the North Sea has taken bites out of the coast before.

Benjamin Britten and Peter Pears made Aldeburgh one of Britain's great music towns. Their home, [The Red House](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/east_of_england/aldeburgh/the_red_house), is now a museum and archive, while Maggi Hambling's [Scallop](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/east_of_england/aldeburgh/scallop) sculpture on the beach has become the town's most argued-over landmark.

The older town is still there too. The [Moot Hall](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/east_of_england/aldeburgh/moot_hall) is a rare Tudor timber-framed public building by the sea, and the [Martello Tower](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/east_of_england/aldeburgh/martello_tower) at the southern end of town marks the old fear of Napoleon's invasion fleet.

Aldeburgh is easy to underrate if you only drive through for lunch. Stay on foot: walk the beach, buy smoked fish, look at the old streets behind the front, and let the place be spare and salty rather than pretty in the usual postcard way.
