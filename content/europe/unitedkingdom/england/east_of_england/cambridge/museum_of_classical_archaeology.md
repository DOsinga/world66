The Museum of Classical Archaeology is a compact but serious Cambridge collection, built around plaster casts of Greek and Roman sculpture. It is especially good for seeing famous ancient works side by side, without the crowds or scale of a national museum.

The museum suits travellers who like universities as working places rather than postcard backdrops. It sits within the Faculty of Classics, so opening times can feel academic, but the payoff is a quiet room of gods, athletes, emperors, and fragments that helps explain why classical art shaped so much of Cambridge’s intellectual world.
