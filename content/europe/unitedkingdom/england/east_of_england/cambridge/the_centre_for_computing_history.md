The Centre for Computing History is one of Cambridge's most approachable specialist museums, especially for visitors who want something more tactile than college courtyards and manuscript cases. Its collections cover home computers, games consoles, calculators, software, and the broader story of how digital technology entered ordinary life.

The best part is that much of the museum is interactive: machines are displayed as things to try, not just objects behind glass. It is a good choice for families, gamers, engineers, or anyone who wants a modern-history stop on the east side of the city.
