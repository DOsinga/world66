Hitchin is a well-preserved market town in Hertfordshire, about 35 miles north of London. Its medieval street plan survives largely intact, centred on a handsome [Market Place](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/east_of_england/hitchin/market_place) that still hosts a twice-weekly market under the watch of the Italianate [Corn Exchange](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/east_of_england/hitchin/corn_exchange) clock tower. The town has an active independent shopping scene — coffee shops, bookshops, and delis tucked into buildings that go back centuries — alongside the usual high street chains.

The single most surprising thing in Hitchin is [St Mary's Church](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/east_of_england/hitchin/st_marys_church), one of the largest parish churches in England — bigger than many cathedrals — set on the river Hiz a few minutes from the square. The [North Hertfordshire Museum](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/east_of_england/hitchin/north_hertfordshire_museum) and the unusual Lancasterian schoolroom at the [British Schools Museum](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/east_of_england/hitchin/british_schools_museum) are both worth an hour. In summer (late June to mid-August), the lavender fields at [Hitchin Lavender](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/east_of_england/hitchin/hitchin_lavender) on the edge of town pull crowds from across the south-east.

It sits between [Stevenage](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/east_of_england/stevenage) and [Letchworth Garden City](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/east_of_england/letchworth), and is easy to reach by train from London King's Cross in under 40 minutes.
