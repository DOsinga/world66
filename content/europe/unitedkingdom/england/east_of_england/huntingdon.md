Huntingdon is a small market town on the River Great Ouse in Cambridgeshire, best known as the birthplace of Oliver Cromwell. The [Cromwell Museum](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/east_of_england/huntingdon/cromwell_museum), housed in the former grammar school where he studied, tells the story of the man who overthrew a king and briefly turned England into a republic.

The town has a handsome [medieval bridge](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/east_of_england/huntingdon/old_bridge_huntingdon) connecting it to Godmanchester across the river, and the surrounding Ouse valley is flat, green, and good for walking and cycling. The vast traditional flood meadow of [Portholme](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/east_of_england/huntingdon/portholme_meadow), one of the largest in England, stretches between the town and the river. [Hinchingbrooke House](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/east_of_england/huntingdon/hinchingbrooke_house), the former Cromwell family home on the western edge of town, opens occasionally for visitors. Huntingdon is a practical stop on the way to or from [Cambridge](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/east_of_england/cambridge), about twenty minutes north by train.
