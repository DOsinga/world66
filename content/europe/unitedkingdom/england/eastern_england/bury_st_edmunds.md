Bury St Edmunds is one of Suffolk's best market towns: prosperous without feeling smug, historic without being embalmed. Its centre is compact, handsome, and unusually coherent, with Georgian shopfronts, old coaching inns, a working market, and the smell of brewing never very far away.

The town grew around the shrine of St Edmund, the martyred Anglo-Saxon king who gave Bury its name. The medieval abbey was once one of the richest in England; today the broken flint walls of [Bury St Edmunds Abbey](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/eastern_england/bury_st_edmunds/bury_st_edmunds_abbey) sit in public gardens beside [St Edmundsbury Cathedral](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/eastern_england/bury_st_edmunds/st_edmundsbury_cathedral), [St Mary's Church](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/eastern_england/bury_st_edmunds/st_marys_church), and the Norman Tower.

This is a town for unhurried wandering. Start at Angel Hill, drift through the abbey gardens, look into [Moyse's Hall](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/eastern_england/bury_st_edmunds/moyses_hall), then end with a pint at [The Nutshell](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/eastern_england/bury_st_edmunds/the_nutshell) or the [Old Cannon Brewery](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/eastern_england/bury_st_edmunds/old_cannon_brewery). It makes an easy day trip from [Cambridge](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/eastern_england/cambridge) or [Ipswich](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/suffolk/ipswich), but it is pleasant enough to justify a night.
