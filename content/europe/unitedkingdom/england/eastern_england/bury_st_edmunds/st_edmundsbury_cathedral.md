St Edmundsbury Cathedral stands beside the abbey gardens and is more interesting than its relatively young cathedral status suggests. The church grew out of the medieval parish church of St James, then became the cathedral for Suffolk in the 20th century.

Inside, it is bright, ordered, and welcoming, with good woodwork and a sense of active use rather than museum stillness. The completed tower gives the building a confident profile over the abbey precinct, and the setting makes the whole cathedral quarter one of the finest small urban ensembles in East Anglia.
