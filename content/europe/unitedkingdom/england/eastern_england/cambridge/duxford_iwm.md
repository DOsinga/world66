  Duxford was an operational RAF base during the Battle of Britain in 1940, and the aircraft that flew from here genuinely intercepted German bombers over southeastern England. The most famous of those pilots was Douglas Bader, who flew from Duxford with both legs amputated, wearing prosthetics. The airfield is now Europe's largest air museum; the Spitfires parked in the hangars are the real thing.

IWM Duxford, about 15 kilometres south of Cambridge, is the largest aviation museum in Europe and one of the best in the world. The site is a former RAF aerodrome — operational from the First World War through the Cold War — and holds over 200 aircraft in a series of enormous hangars and outdoor displays. The collection includes Spitfires, Hurricanes, and Lancaster bombers from the Second World War, Cold War jets, a Concorde, and a large American Air Museum focusing on USAAF operations from British bases.

Duxford also holds regular air shows in which many of the aircraft fly. Admission is expensive (around £25 adults) but the scale is exceptional — allow a full day. Buses from Cambridge city centre serve Duxford village; the museum is a short walk from the stop.
