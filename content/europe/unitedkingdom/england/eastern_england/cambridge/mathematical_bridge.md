The Mathematical Bridge at Queens' College is a wooden footbridge across the Cam, built in 1749 and reconstructed twice since. It is called the Mathematical Bridge because its arches are constructed from straight timbers arranged in a pattern of tangent lines rather than curved beams — an elegant piece of engineering that produces a curved effect from entirely straight components. Local legend, entirely unfounded, credits Isaac Newton with designing it without screws or bolts.

The bridge is most easily viewed from the riverbank or from a punt. Queens' College charges admission to enter the grounds during term time, but you can see the bridge from the river or from the lane alongside it.
