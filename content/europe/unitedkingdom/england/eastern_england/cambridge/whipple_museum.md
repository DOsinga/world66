  Robert Whipple, chairman of Cambridge Instrument Company, donated his collection of historic scientific instruments to the university in 1944. Among them are astrolabes from the 14th century, a quadrant used by the Duke of Florence in the 16th century, and mathematical instruments owned by John Flamsteed, the first Astronomer Royal. The collection traces 600 years of humans trying to measure the world precisely.
---

The Whipple Museum on Free School Lane, tucked between the history faculty and the old Cavendish Laboratory, holds one of the finest collections of historic scientific instruments in the world. Astrolabes, theodolites, microscopes, telescopes, calculating machines, and navigational instruments fill room after room — most of them genuinely beautiful objects as well as working tools.

The collection spans from the medieval Islamic world through the Scientific Revolution to the early 20th century. Given that Cambridge was central to much of the science of that period — Newton, Darwin, Maxwell, Rutherford, Crick and Watson all worked here — the museum sits in an appropriate place. Free entry; open Monday to Friday 12:30pm–4:30pm.
