Grays is a Thames-side town in the borough of Thurrock, Essex, sitting on the north bank of the river about 30 kilometres east of central London. It is a working town rather than a tourist destination, but it has a few things worth stopping for if you are passing through or exploring the Thames estuary.

Grays Beach Riverside Park offers pleasant walks along the river with views across to [Tilbury Fort](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/eastern_england/grays/tilbury_fort) on the opposite bank — one of the best-preserved artillery forts in England, where Queen Elizabeth I famously addressed her troops before the Spanish Armada in 1588. The [State Cinema](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/eastern_england/grays/state_cinema), a striking 1938 Art Deco building on Clarence Road, is one of the finest surviving examples of its type in Essex, though it is no longer in use as a cinema. Just north of the town centre, the [Grays Thurrock Chalk Pit](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/eastern_england/grays/grays_thurrock_chalk_pit) is a Site of Special Scientific Interest that has yielded Ice Age mammal fossils — mammoth, woolly rhinoceros, cave lion — and is now a small nature reserve.

On the western edge of town, [Chafford Gorges Nature Park](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/eastern_england/grays/chafford_gorges) is a 200-acre former quarry reserve managed by the Essex Wildlife Trust, with chalk grassland, orchids, and reptiles in a surprisingly dramatic landscape. The town is well connected by rail, with c2c trains reaching Fenchurch Street station in central London in about 35 minutes.
