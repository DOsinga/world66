Luton is a large town of about 220,000 people in Bedfordshire, 30 miles north of London. It is primarily a transit hub — Luton Airport handles around 18 million passengers a year, serving many budget carriers — but the town itself has more to it than its reputation suggests.

The [Wardown Park Museum](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/eastern_england/luton/wardown_park_museum), set in a Victorian villa by the River Lea, has an unexpectedly good collection covering local history and the **hat-making industry**, for which the town was once famous. The [Stockwood Discovery Centre](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/eastern_england/luton/stockwood_discovery) south of town combines a period garden, craft galleries, and a collection of horse-drawn vehicles. [Luton's covered market](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/eastern_england/luton/luton_market) is one of the more diverse in the southeast, reflecting the town's large South Asian and Caribbean communities — best experienced by walking through [Bury Park](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/eastern_england/luton/bury_park) along Dunstable Road. Out of town, the chalk escarpment of [Dunstable Downs](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/eastern_england/luton/dunstable_downs) and the country house at [Luton Hoo](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/eastern_england/luton/luton_hoo) are both worth half a day.

Most visitors pass through without stopping, which is understandable — the town centre has seen better days architecturally — but it rewards a couple of hours if you are already passing through on the way to the airport.
