The medieval parish church of Luton, a substantial Grade I-listed building on Church Street in the town centre. **St Mary's** was largely built in the 13th and 14th centuries on the site of an earlier Saxon church (a fragment of which is preserved in the south porch). The chequer-work flint and stone exterior, the unusual octagonal **baptistery**, the 14th-century carved oak chancel screen, and the Wenlock Chapel with its surviving medieval wall paintings make it one of the more interesting parish churches in the home counties.

Free to enter; volunteer guides on weekday afternoons. About a 10-minute walk from the railway station. Combine with a wander around the surrounding St George's Square and Town Hall.
