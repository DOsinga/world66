story: "Luton was for nearly two centuries the centre of the English **straw plait and hat-making industry** — at its peak in the late 19th century the town produced around 70 million hats a year, employing a third of the local workforce, mostly women working from home. The Wardown collection preserves the looms, examples, and trade records of a craft that defined the town's identity."

The town's main museum, set in a flamboyant Victorian villa in the lakeside Wardown Park beside the River Lea. The collection covers Luton from prehistory (Iron Age finds from local hillforts) through the Roman period, the medieval village, and the explosive growth of the 19th and 20th centuries as the town became first a hat-making, then a Vauxhall-car-making centre.

The hat-making galleries are the highlight — looms, machinery, plaiting tools, and an extensive collection of hats (some by Stephen Jones, who is from Luton). There is also a regimental gallery for the Bedfordshire and Hertfordshire Regiment. The villa itself, with its ornate plasterwork and stained glass, is worth the visit alone. Free entry; closed Mondays.
