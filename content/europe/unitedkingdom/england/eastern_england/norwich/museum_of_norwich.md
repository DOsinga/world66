The Museum of Norwich at the Bridewell tells the history of Norwich as a trading and manufacturing city, housed in a 14th-century merchant's house that was later used as a prison for women and vagrants (a "bridewell" — a term for a house of correction). The building itself is one of the oldest surviving flint houses in the city.

The museum covers Norwich's textile trade — the Worsted cloth that made the city rich in the medieval period — its manufacturing history, the role of the Strangers (Dutch and Walloon refugees) in the economy, and the decline of traditional industries in the 20th century. There are good exhibits on the local mustard industry and Colman's mustard, which is practically synonymous with Norwich. Entry is charged.
