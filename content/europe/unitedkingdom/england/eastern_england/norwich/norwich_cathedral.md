  The cathedral spire at 96 metres is the second-tallest in England, after Salisbury. It was rebuilt after the original wooden spire was struck by lightning in 1362 and collapsed — the replacement was built in the new Perpendicular Gothic style, entirely different in character from the Norman nave below it. The result is an architectural conversation between two centuries, one still talking to the other.
---

Norwich Cathedral is one of the best-preserved Norman cathedrals in England, begun in 1096 and largely complete by the 13th century. The nave is enormous — 139 metres long — and lit by Norman clerestory windows that fill the pale stone with an even, calm light. The ceiling bosses in the nave and cloister are an extraordinary collection of carved medieval narrative: scenes from the Bible, mythological figures, and everyday medieval life, carved in oak and painted, over a thousand of them in the cloister alone.

The Cathedral Close, enclosed by medieval walls with two surviving gate towers, is a world apart from the city outside. Lawns, old flint buildings, and the sound of rooks replace the city noise. It is one of the most peaceful spaces in Norwich and worth lingering in. The close is freely accessible and the cathedral welcomes visitors; a donation is requested.
