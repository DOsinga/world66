The Dutch Cottage is a small circular thatched building dating from around 1621, one of only two surviving examples of this building type in England. It was built by Dutch workers — specialists in land drainage — who were brought to Essex in the early 17th century to drain the Thames marshes, and constructed in a style typical of the Low Countries. The building served as a tollhouse for the weekly market that has been held in Rayleigh since the medieval period.

The cottage is a listed building managed by Rochford District Council and is occasionally open for guided visits. Even when closed, the exterior — round walls, conical thatched roof, low entrance — is worth the short detour from the town centre. It sits on Crown Hill, a few minutes' walk from Rayleigh Mount and the windmill. The Rayleigh Town Museum can provide details of opening times.
