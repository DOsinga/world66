---

Rayleigh's historic core is compact and walkable. [Rayleigh Mount](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/eastern_england/rayleigh/rayleigh_mount) is the surviving earthwork of a Norman castle built shortly after 1066, now managed by the National Trust and freely accessible. The views from the top of the motte give a clear sense of why the site was chosen — the ridge looks out over the Thames estuary flatlands for miles.

In the grounds of the mount, [Rayleigh Windmill](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/eastern_england/rayleigh/rayleigh_windmill) is a restored tower mill from 1809 that worked commercially until 1867 and now operates as a free museum, open on selected weekends. On Crown Hill a few hundred metres away, the [Dutch Cottage](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/eastern_england/rayleigh/dutch_cottage) is a round thatched building from 1621 — one of only two surviving examples in England, built by the Dutch drainage workers who reclaimed the Essex marshes. The [Rayleigh Town Museum](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/eastern_england/rayleigh/rayleigh_town_museum) in the town centre covers local history from the Norman period to the present, staffed by volunteers.

The 15th-century Holy Trinity church at the top of the high street has medieval features worth seeing, and the market area below retains its pre-war character. The town is easily reached from Southend-on-Sea (20 minutes by train) or London Fenchurch Street (50 minutes).
