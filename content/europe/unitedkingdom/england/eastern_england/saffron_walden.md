Saffron Walden is an old Essex market town that kept more of its centre than the commuter belt usually allows. Timber-framed houses, pargeted plasterwork, narrow lanes and a proper market square give it a strong East Anglian character, while the name recalls the saffron crocus trade that made the town rich in the Tudor period.

The great sight is [Audley End House](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/eastern_england/saffron_walden/audley_end_house), a Jacobean mansion large enough to pull visitors from Cambridge and London. But the town itself deserves time too: [Bridge End Garden](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/eastern_england/saffron_walden/bridge_end_garden) is a lovely restored Victorian garden, and the [Fry Art Gallery](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/eastern_england/saffron_walden/fry_art_gallery) gives a focused look at artists with deep Essex connections.

Saffron Walden is not a big day out in the way Cambridge is. It is better as a compact, satisfying excursion: walk the market streets, visit the garden, then give Audley End the slower attention it deserves.
