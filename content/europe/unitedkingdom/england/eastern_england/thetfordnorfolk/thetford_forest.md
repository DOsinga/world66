At around 19,000 hectares, Thetford Forest is the largest lowland pine forest in Britain, planted from the 1920s onwards on the ancient heathland of the Brecks. Managed by Forestry England, it wraps around the western and northern sides of Thetford and extends across the county border into Suffolk. The main visitor hub is High Lodge Forest Centre, three kilometres west of town off the B1107, where you can hire bikes, pick up trail maps, and find a café and toilets.

The cycling trails are the forest's main attraction — there are colour-graded routes for all abilities, from gentle family loops to red and black graded mountain bike trails. Walkers have their own network of paths. The forest is home to fallow and roe deer, red squirrels (rarer and best spotted on quiet weekday mornings), nightjars in summer, and crossbills that feed in the pine canopy. The Go Ape high-wire adventure course at High Lodge is popular with families.

Away from the trails, the forest rewards independent exploration. The ancient Peddars Way long-distance path crosses the northern section, and the forest edges give way to patches of open Breckland heath — a rare and botanically rich habitat found almost nowhere else in Britain.
