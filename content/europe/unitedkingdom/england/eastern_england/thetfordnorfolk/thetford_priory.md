  Thetford Priory was the chosen burial place of the Mowbray dukes of Norfolk, 
  one of the most powerful noble families in medieval England. When the last 
  Mowbray duke died without a male heir in 1476, the dukedom passed to the 
  Howards — who promptly moved their patronage elsewhere. The priory was 
  dissolved by Henry VIII in 1540, and the ducal tombs were scattered. 
  The 3rd Duke of Norfolk tried to rescue them; his own fate was barely 
  better — he was in the Tower awaiting execution when Henry VIII died in 
  1547, and was saved only because the new king signed no death warrants 
  on his first day.

The ruined gatehouse and soaring arches of Thetford Priory stand in a meadow beside the Little Ouse, free to visit and largely empty on most days. Founded in 1103 as a Cluniac monastery, this was one of the wealthiest religious houses in East Anglia before the Dissolution. The scale of what remains — the long nave walls, the prior's lodging, the great gatehouse — gives a clear sense of how large the original complex was.

English Heritage maintains the ruins and there is an interpretive display near the entrance. The setting beside the river is particularly peaceful, with the old meadow and the willows making it a pleasant place to linger even if you have no interest in medieval architecture.
