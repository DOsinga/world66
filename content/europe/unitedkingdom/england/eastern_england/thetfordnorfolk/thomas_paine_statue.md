A gilded bronze statue of Thomas Paine stands on King Street in the centre of Thetford, looking every inch the revolutionary — quill in one hand, a copy of *The Rights of Man* in the other. The statue, by sculptor Charles Wheeler, was unveiled in 1964 and paid for partly by American donors who regarded Paine's ideas as foundational to the United States.

Paine was born a few streets away in 1737 and lived here until his early thirties, when he sailed for Philadelphia on the recommendation of Benjamin Franklin and almost immediately changed the course of history. The juxtaposition of the radical gilded figure and the quiet English market town around him is quietly funny. The Ancient House Museum, a short walk away, has the most complete exhibition on his life and work.
