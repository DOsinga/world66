Waltham Cross takes its name from one of the twelve Eleanor Crosses erected by Edward I in 1291 to mark the resting places of his wife's funeral cortege on its journey from Lincoln to London. The [cross](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/eastern_england/walthamcross/eleanor_cross) still stands in the town centre, heavily restored but one of only three originals to survive. It is an extraordinary piece of medieval craftsmanship to find in what is now a busy commuter town on the northern edge of London.

Nearby [Waltham Abbey](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/eastern_england/walthamcross/waltham_abbey), just across the River Lea, is worth a visit — the abbey church has a Norman nave and is reputedly the burial place of King Harold, killed at the Battle of Hastings in 1066. The [Lee Valley Regional Park](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/eastern_england/walthamcross/lee_valley_park) stretches south from here along the river, with nature reserves, walking trails, the white-water course built for the [2012 Olympics](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/eastern_england/walthamcross/lee_valley_white_water), and the remains of the [Royal Gunpowder Mills](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/eastern_england/walthamcross/royal_gunpowder_mills). West of the town centre, [Cedars Park](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/eastern_england/walthamcross/cedars_park) sits on the grounds of the demolished royal palace of Theobalds.
