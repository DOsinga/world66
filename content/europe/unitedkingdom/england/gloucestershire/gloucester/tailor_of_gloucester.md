  Beatrix Potter visited her cousin in Gloucester in 1897 and heard the story of a local tailor named John Prichard who, returning to his shop after a holiday, found a waistcoat almost finished — leaving only a note from his two assistants explaining they had no more cherry-coloured twist. Potter transformed this into *The Tailor of Gloucester*, which she privately published in 1902 and considered her personal favourite of all her books. She set it in this specific building, which is why the shop is identifiable today.

College Court, just outside the Cathedral close, is one of Gloucester's most photogenic medieval lanes — a narrow cobbled alley lined with timber-framed buildings. At one end stands the small shop that Beatrix Potter used as the model for *The Tailor of Gloucester*, one of her most loved stories. The building now houses a modest museum dedicated to the book and its origins, along with a gift shop selling Potter merchandise.

The museum is small — a couple of rooms — and the displays are fairly standard: illustrations, editions of the book, context on Potter's connection to Gloucester. But the building itself and its setting in College Court are genuinely charming, and if you're already visiting the Cathedral (50 metres away), it's a natural detour. Worth 20 minutes.

The shop is at 9 College Court, Gloucester, GL1 2NJ.
