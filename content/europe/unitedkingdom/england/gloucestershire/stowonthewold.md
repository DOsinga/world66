Stow-on-the-Wold is one of the most famous of the Cotswold towns, perched at the highest point on the ancient Fosse Way in northeastern Gloucestershire. [The large market square](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/gloucestershire/stowonthewold/the_market_square) — once used for huge sheep fairs that could see twenty thousand animals change hands in a day — is now ringed by antique shops, tea rooms, and honey-coloured stone buildings. The narrow alleys running into it, called "tures," were built to funnel livestock; now they channel tourists.

[St Edward's Church](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/gloucestershire/stowonthewold/st_edwards_church) anchors the north end of the square, its north door famously framed by two ancient yew trees. The church served as a prison for Royalist soldiers captured at the [Battle of Stow-on-the-Wold](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/gloucestershire/stowonthewold/battle_of_stow) in March 1646 — the last pitched battle of the English Civil War, fought on ground just north of the town.

Stow is also England's most concentrated antique-dealing centre, with around forty dealers operating in a town of two thousand people. The [antique shops](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/gloucestershire/stowonthewold/antique_shops) along Sheep Street and Church Street range from single-room specialists to large multi-dealer centres. The town makes a good base for exploring the northern Cotswolds: Bourton-on-the-Water is a short drive south, and the rolling countryside between here and Chipping Norton is quintessential English landscape.
