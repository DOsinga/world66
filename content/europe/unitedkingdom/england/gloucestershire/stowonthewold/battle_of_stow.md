  The Battle of Stow-on-the-Wold on 21 March 1646 was the final pitched battle of the English Civil War. The last Royalist field army in England surrendered in the market square that afternoon.

A mile north of the town centre, on flat ground near Donnington, the last significant battle of the English Civil War was fought in March 1646. Sir Jacob Astley commanded around three thousand Royalist troops and was attempting to reach King Charles I at Oxford; Parliamentary forces intercepted him here. Astley's army was overwhelmed, and the remnants were driven into the town where fighting continued in the streets.

There is no formal battlefield site to visit, and the ground is farmland, but a short walk north along the Fosse Way gives a good sense of the terrain. Back in town, St Edward's Church holds the aftermath in memory — it served as a prison for the survivors, and the churchyard contains some of those who didn't survive. A small exhibition in the town covers the Civil War period.
