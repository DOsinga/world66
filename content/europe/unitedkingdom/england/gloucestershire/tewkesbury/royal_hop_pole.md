  The Royal Hop Pole appears in Charles Dickens's *The Pickwick Papers* (1836), where Mr Pickwick and his companions stop here for dinner on their way to Birmingham. Dickens describes the party eating well and drinking better, and the inn has traded on this literary connection ever since. Whether Dickens actually stayed here or used it only as a convenient fictional staging post is a matter of mild local dispute.
---

The Royal Hop Pole is a historic coaching inn on the main street, one of Tewkesbury's more atmospheric drinking spots. The building dates from the medieval period and retains its old bones behind a Georgian facade. The bar is comfortable and unpretentious, and the inn serves food throughout the day.

Its main claim to fame is the Dickens connection — the inn features in *The Pickwick Papers* and the association is faithfully maintained in the decor and marketing. This kind of literary tourism can be either charming or tiresome depending on your tolerance, but the pub itself is worth a visit regardless of the Pickwick angle.
