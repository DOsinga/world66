Ambleside is the Lake District town that best balances scenery with usefulness. It sits just above Waterhead, at the northern end of [Windermere](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/lakedistrict/windermere), with stone streets full of outdoor shops, walkers drying waterproofs in pub corners, and fells rising almost immediately behind the roofs.

The town is not as grand as Keswick or as polished as Bowness, but it is wonderfully placed. You can walk to [Stock Ghyll Force](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/lakedistrict/ambleside/stock_ghyll_force) before breakfast, take a steamer from [Waterhead Pier](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/lakedistrict/ambleside/waterhead_pier) down the lake, climb [Wansfell](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/lakedistrict/ambleside/wansfell), or follow the lanes toward [Rydal Mount](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/lakedistrict/ambleside/rydal_mount) and [Rydal Water](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/lakedistrict/ambleside/rydal_water).

Ambleside also has more cultural weight than its size suggests. The [Armitt Museum and Library](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/lakedistrict/ambleside/armitt_museum) preserves local art, manuscripts, and Beatrix Potter material; the tiny [Bridge House](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/lakedistrict/ambleside/bridge_house) is one of the most photographed buildings in the Lakes; and the remains of [Ambleside Roman Fort](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/lakedistrict/ambleside/ambleside_roman_fort) sit quietly in a field near the lake.

Use Ambleside if you want to walk rather than just look at the Lake District. It is busy in summer and wet more often than visitors like to admit, but the trade-off is simple: from here, half-day walks, lake boats, Wordsworth sites, and high-level routes all start within easy reach.
