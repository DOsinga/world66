Belle Isle is the largest island on Windermere and sits directly opposite the Bowness promenade, close enough to see clearly from the shore. The island is private and not accessible to visitors, but its unusual round house — built in 1774 and one of the first circular houses in England — makes it a talking point on every lake cruise.

The island is thickly wooded and the house peeks through the trees, more visible in winter when the leaves are down. It appears in several of Wordsworth's writings and in the background of various lake paintings. Seeing it from a cruise boat, with the wooded western shore behind it and the fells beyond, gives a good sense of the scale and character of Windermere.
