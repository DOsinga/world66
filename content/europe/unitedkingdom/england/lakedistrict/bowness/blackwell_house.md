story: "Blackwell was designed by M.H. Baillie Scott and completed in 1900 as a holiday home for a Manchester brewery owner. It is considered one of the finest surviving examples of Arts and Crafts domestic architecture in Britain — notable not only for the quality of its interior craftsmanship but for the fact that it has survived almost unaltered. For much of the 20th century it was used as a school and offices, which paradoxically helped preserve it from modernisation. It was restored and opened to the public in 2001."

Blackwell is one of the great surprises of the Lake District — an Arts and Crafts masterpiece sitting just two miles south of Bowness with sweeping views over Windermere. Designed by Mackay Hugh Baillie Scott in 1900, the house is a showcase of the movement's ideals: beautiful handcrafted interiors, light-filled rooms, and a deep integration of the building with its natural setting.

The main hall, with its extraordinary carved frieze and stained glass, is the highlight, but every room repays careful attention. The house is now managed by Lakeland Arts and admission is reasonable. The café in the old kitchen is a pleasant place to stop, and the views from the terrace on a clear day are among the best in the southern Lake District.
