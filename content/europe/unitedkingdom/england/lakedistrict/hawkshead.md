Hawkshead is one of the prettiest villages in the Lake District — a tight cluster of whitewashed cottages, narrow flagged lanes, and medieval yards that have changed surprisingly little in 300 years. It sits in a shallow valley between [Esthwaite Water](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/lakedistrict/hawkshead/esthwaite_water) and Coniston Water, and on a quiet morning before the coaches arrive it is genuinely lovely.

The village has two famous literary associations. William Wordsworth attended the [grammar school](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/lakedistrict/hawkshead/hawkshead_grammar_school) here from 1779 to 1787, and the school building is now a small museum. Beatrix Potter lived nearby at [Hill Top Farm](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/lakedistrict/hawkshead/hill_top) in Near Sawrey, and her original drawings and manuscripts are displayed at the [Beatrix Potter Gallery](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/lakedistrict/hawkshead/beatrix_potter_gallery) in the village centre. Both draw large numbers of visitors in summer.

As a base for the Lake District, Hawkshead works well if you have a car. You are within easy reach of [Coniston](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/manchester_liverpool_and_north_west/lakedistrict/coniston_1), [Grasmere](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/manchester_liverpool_and_north_west/lakedistrict/grasmere), and the western fells. Public transport is limited to an infrequent bus service, so without a car your options are restricted to the village itself and the ferry across Windermere from [Bowness](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/manchester_liverpool_and_north_west/lakedistrict/bowness).
