High Street takes its name from the Roman road that once ran along its broad summit ridge, linking the fort at Galava (modern [Ambleside](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/lakedistrict/ambleside)) with forts further north near Brougham. At 828 metres it is the highest point in the eastern fells, and unlike most Lakeland summits it is flat and wide enough that a road across it made practical sense.

For centuries after the Romans left, the summit kept a second life as a gathering point: shepherds from the surrounding valleys held an annual fair on the ridge, complete with horse racing, wrestling, and sheep-trading, on a section still marked on maps as Racecourse Hill. The standard walk climbs from Mardale Head or Hartsop and follows the old road along the ridge for long stretches — one of the few places in the Lake District where you are, quite literally, hiking a motorway.
