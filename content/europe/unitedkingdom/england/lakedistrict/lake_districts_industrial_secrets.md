The Lake District sells itself on lakes, fells, and poets, which makes it easy to miss that this was also a working industrial landscape for most of its history. Keswick's pencil factory hid a tiny compass and a rolled map of Germany inside special pencils issued to RAF aircrew in the Second World War — alongside the 7.91-metre novelty pencil that is somehow the museum's other headline exhibit. Up at Coniston, spoil heaps and collapsed adits mark copper workings that ran from Roman times to the 1850s, when the mines employed several hundred men, some of them children.

None of this fits the postcard version of the Lakes, and that's the point: a bobbin mill that supplied Lancashire's cotton mills, a quarry turned heritage railway, and a volunteer-run corn mill in Eskdale are reminders that this was farmed, mined, and manufactured country long before it became a national park.
