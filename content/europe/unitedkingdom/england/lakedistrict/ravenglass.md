Ravenglass is the only village in the Lake District national park that lies on the Cumbrian Coast — a single street of whitewashed cottages running down to a triple-river estuary on the Irish Sea. The shingle foreshore is pleasant though not outstanding; the real reason to come is everything else within a mile of it.

The village is the terminus of the [Ravenglass and Eskdale Railway](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/lakedistrict/ravenglass/ravenglass_eskdale_railway), a preserved 15-inch narrow-gauge steam line that runs seven miles up Eskdale — one of the loveliest of the Lake District valleys, and a back way in to Scafell Pike, the highest point in England. The [Roman bath house](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/lakedistrict/ravenglass/roman_bath_house) of [Glannoventa](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/lakedistrict/ravenglass/glannoventa), with walls four metres high, stands a short walk inland. [Muncaster Castle](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/lakedistrict/ravenglass/muncaster_castle) and [Muncaster Mill](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/lakedistrict/ravenglass/muncaster_mill) lie a mile or two up the road.
