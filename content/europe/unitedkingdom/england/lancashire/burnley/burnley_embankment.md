The Burnley Embankment carries the Leeds and Liverpool Canal through the heart of Burnley on a dramatic Victorian causeway, rising up to 18 metres above street level. Walking the towpath gives you an unusual elevated perspective over the town's rooftops and the surrounding hills.

The structure dates to the late 18th century and was a major feat of canal engineering for its time. The Leeds and Liverpool Canal itself is one of the longest in Britain (127 miles), linking Yorkshire with the Lancashire coast, and this section through Burnley was one of the engineering challenges that made it famous. The towpath is a pleasant walking and cycling route, particularly westward where it enters more rural countryside.
