Towneley Hall is a 14th-century country house on the southern edge of Burnley, now run as a free public art gallery and museum by the council. The building has been continuously modified over six centuries, with Gothic, Elizabethan, and later additions layered onto the medieval core, making the architecture itself a kind of historical document.

The collection inside is impressive for a municipal gallery: Old Masters (including a Spagnoletto), English portraits, decorative arts, and a good local history section covering Burnley's textile industry. The parkland surrounding the hall — some 60 acres of ancient woodland, meadow, and a lake — is open freely and popular with local walkers. Free entry to both house and grounds.
