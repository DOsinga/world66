Bradford made its fortune on wool. By the mid-19th century it was the wool capital of the world, a grimy, prosperous mill town that drew workers from across Europe and, later, from South Asia. The industry unravelled after World War II, and Bradford has been reinventing itself ever since — with mixed but often interesting results.

The city punches above its weight culturally. The [National Science and Media Museum](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/leeds_sheffield_and_yorkshire/bradford/national_science_and_media_museum), right in the city centre, is one of the best of its kind in Britain, and Bradford was named a UNESCO City of Film in 2009, the first in the world. The Victorian Gothic [Wool Exchange](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/leeds_sheffield_and_yorkshire/bradford/wool_exchange) and [Bradford City Hall](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/leeds_sheffield_and_yorkshire/bradford/bradford_city_hall) survive in good condition near the central [City Park](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/leeds_sheffield_and_yorkshire/bradford/bradford_city_park), and the densely listed [Little Germany](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/leeds_sheffield_and_yorkshire/bradford/little_germany) merchants' quarter is one of the best-preserved 19th-century textile-exporters' districts in Britain. On the southern edge of the Pennines, the surrounding landscape is dramatic: the Yorkshire Moors that inspired Emily Brontë lie just to the north, and the village of Haworth — where the Brontë family lived — is within easy reach.

Bradford's South Asian community, one of the largest in Britain, has transformed the city's food scene. The restaurant strip along Manningham Lane and [Mumtaz](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/leeds_sheffield_and_yorkshire/bradford/mumtaz) on Great Horton Road are genuine destinations; the older [Karachi Restaurant](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/leeds_sheffield_and_yorkshire/bradford/karachi_restaurant) on Neal Street has been serving the same kitchen since 1965. The city has a complicated history of racial tension alongside genuine multiculturalism — both are part of its story.

The suburb of Saltaire, a few kilometres north, is a World Heritage Site: a complete Victorian model village built by the wool magnate Sir Titus Salt in the 1850s. [Salt's Mill](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/leeds_sheffield_and_yorkshire/bradford/salts_mill_saltaire) at its centre now houses an extraordinary collection of David Hockney paintings, a fine café, and design shops. It is one of the most rewarding half-days in the north of England.
