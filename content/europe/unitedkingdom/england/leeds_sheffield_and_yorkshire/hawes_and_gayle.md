Hawes is the main market town at the head of Wensleydale in the [Yorkshire Dales](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/leeds_sheffield_and_yorkshire/yorkshire_dales), and the nearby hamlet of Gayle sits just a few minutes' walk to the south. At 260 metres above sea level, it is one of the highest market towns in England, and on the days when cloud sits on the surrounding fells and the stone buildings darken in the rain, it is also one of the most atmospheric.

The town centre is more interesting than most Dales towns of its size. Within a short walk of the market place you will find the Wensleydale Creamery (where the famous crumbly white cheese is made and sold direct), the Dales Countryside Museum in the old station yard, and W.R. Outhwaite & Son — one of England's last working ropemakers, where rope is still made on Victorian machinery in the same building as it has been since 1905. The Tuesday market is small but real.

Gayle, just down the lane, is quieter and has the wonderful Gayle Mill: thought to be the oldest structurally intact cotton mill in England, built around 1784 and now restored as a working watermill with a woodland business attached. A kilometre and a half north of Hawes, the hamlet of Hardraw guards access to Hardraw Force — England's highest single-drop waterfall, reached through the Green Dragon Inn.

The Pennine Way long-distance path passes through Hawes, bringing walkers from all over Britain. The town is well placed for exploring upper Wensleydale and the surrounding fells, with Pen-y-ghent, Ingleborough, and Great Shunner Fell all within a day's walking.
