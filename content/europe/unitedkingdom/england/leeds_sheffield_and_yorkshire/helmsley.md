Helmsley is a handsome stone-built market town on the southern edge of the [North York Moors](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/leeds_sheffield_and_yorkshire/north_york_moors), about 25 miles north of [York](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/leeds_sheffield_and_yorkshire/york). It is the kind of place that rewards an unhurried day: a broad market square ringed by independent shops, butchers, and a particularly good deli, with a weekly Friday market that has been held here since the 12th century. The town makes an easy and satisfying base for exploring the moors, and combines a market town, a ruined castle, and one of England's finest abbeys within a few miles.

Helmsley Castle, an English Heritage property just off the square, is a substantial Norman keep with later additions and good displays on the Duncombe family who owned it through the post-medieval period. Beside it lies Duncombe Park, a grand 18th-century house set in landscaped grounds and ancient parkland. The town also marks the western end of the Cleveland Way, the long-distance footpath that loops around the moors to the coast, so you will often see walkers setting out or arriving with packs.

Three miles west, in a narrow wooded valley, stand the ruins of [Rievaulx Abbey](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/leeds_sheffield_and_yorkshire/north_york_moors/rievaulx_abbey) — among the most complete and atmospheric Cistercian remains in England. The nave and chancel walls still rise to a considerable height, and the monks chose the valley deliberately for its remoteness and beauty. The combination of a sunny afternoon, a valley of ruins, and the silence is hard to beat in the north of England. A walk along the ridge above brings you to the Ionic temple at Rievaulx Terrace (National Trust), which offers the famous views down into the valley and adds an hour or so to the day.
