Huddersfield has one of the most distinguished South Asian food cultures in the north of England, a legacy of the Pakistani and Bangladeshi communities that came to work the textile mills from the 1950s and 1960s. The cooking here has a seriousness and depth that goes well beyond the generic curry house menu: karahi dishes, slow-cooked haleem, freshly baked roti, and the sort of regional Pakistani cooking that takes time to find even in the larger cities.

The concentration of restaurants is strongest around the town centre and in the Asian quarter near the university, but good places are scattered across the suburbs too. Viaduct Street and the surrounding streets have been a focus for South Asian food since the 1970s. The quality is consistently high — the local community demands it, and the competition is fierce enough to keep standards honest. It is the kind of food culture that develops when a community cooks for itself rather than for visitors.

For newcomers, the safest approach is to look for restaurants that are full of Pakistani families on a Friday or Saturday evening. A tandoori mixed grill or a proper karahi — the dish is cooked and served in the blackened iron pan it was made in — is the right starting point.
