Ingleborough is the most distinctive of the Yorkshire Three Peaks — a flat-topped plateau at 723 metres that dominates the western Dales skyline for miles in every direction. From Ingleton, the ascent follows the steep western flank through limestone outcrops and upland grassland, gaining height quickly on clear ground. The approach from the village is the most direct route and takes around three to four hours return.

The summit plateau is large and windswept, with the remains of an Iron Age hillfort visible as low earthen banks around the perimeter. On a clear day, the views extend to the Lake District fells to the northwest, Morecambe Bay to the west, Pen-y-Ghent and Whernside to the east, and — on exceptional days — to Snowdonia. The summit is frequented by cloud and wind; waterproofs and proper footwear are necessary even in summer.

The Yorkshire Three Peaks challenge — Whernside, Ingleborough, and Pen-y-Ghent in a single day — begins and ends in Horton in Ribblesdale but passes close to Ingleton. Completing it typically requires about twelve hours and a well-conditioned level of fitness. Ingleborough alone from Ingleton is a more reasonable half-day walk.
