  Limestone pavement — the geological feature that defines Twistleton Scars
  — forms when glaciers strip a surface of soil and vegetation down to bare
  rock. Rainwater then dissolves along the natural cracks in the limestone

Twistleton Scars is the limestone pavement that extends from the slopes of Ingleborough to the north of Ingleton, a stretch of open, almost lunar terrain quite different from the green dales and wooded gorges below. The pavement — sheets of grey rock divided into blocks by deep crevices called grykes — is one of the best examples in the Yorkshire Dales and largely unvisited compared to the more famous pavement above Malham Cove.

Walking across limestone pavement requires care: the surface is uneven and the grykes can be ankle-deep or deeper. Good footwear is essential. The reward is a strange, quiet landscape that changes character dramatically with the light — pale and blinding in midday sun, silver and moody under cloud, occasionally dramatic under a low winter sky. The grykes shelter an unusual range of plants including hart's tongue fern, herb Robert, and — in some sections — wild garlic.

Access is from the road on the north side of Ingleton toward Chapel-le-Dale. The pavement sits above and to the east, reached by open tracks. No admission is charged; the area is within the Yorkshire Dales National Park.
