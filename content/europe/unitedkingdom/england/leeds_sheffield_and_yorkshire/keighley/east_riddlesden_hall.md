East Riddlesden Hall is a 17th-century manor house on the north bank of the River Aire, a mile east of Keighley's centre, now in the care of the National Trust. The building dates from the 1640s and is a good example of West Yorkshire vernacular architecture — solid gritstone, mullioned windows, and a domestic scale that feels genuinely inhabited rather than palatial.

The real gem of the site is the medieval tithe barn alongside the house — one of the finest surviving examples in the north of England, with a magnificent timber roof structure. The interior of the house is modestly furnished in period style. The grounds run down to the river, and the combination of the barn, the hall, and the riverside walk makes for a pleasant half-day. The hall is within walking distance of the Leeds-Liverpool Canal towpath.
