  Every evening at 9 p.m., a hornblower in historical costume sounds a horn at each corner of the market square obelisk — a ceremony that has taken place continuously for over a thousand years. The original Wakeman was responsible for keeping the night watch; any citizen who was robbed between the sounding of the horn and dawn could claim compensation. The tradition has continued unbroken through wars, plague, and industrial revolution.

Ripon's market square is one of the most handsome in the north of England, flanked by Georgian townhouses and dominated by a tall obelisk erected in 1781. The Wakeman's House, on the corner of the square, is a late medieval timber-framed building that houses a small museum about the history of the city and the horn-blowing ceremony. It is one of the oldest domestic buildings still standing in Ripon.

The square comes alive on Thursday mornings with a traditional market, and the Saturday market is one of the larger ones in the area. The nine o'clock horn-blowing ceremony is genuinely atmospheric on a quiet weekday evening — this is not a tourist performance staged for visitors but a ritual that the city has simply never stopped observing.
