Scarborough is a classic seaside resort on the east coast of Yorkshire. The old town is built around a massive headland that juts out into the North Sea, dividing the town into a north side and a south side — each with its own character and its own beach.

The North Bay is relatively unspoiled and popular with surfers. The best walk in town is the [Marine Drive](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/leeds_sheffield_and_yorkshire/scarborough/marine_drive) — a Victorian seafront road that circles the base of the headland — continuing out to Scalby Mills and the Sea Life Centre. The South Bay is more commercialised, with amusement arcades, ice cream parlours, and fish and chips keeping most people happy. [Victorian funicular railways](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/leeds_sheffield_and_yorkshire/scarborough/scarborough_funiculars) connect the clifftop Esplanade to both beaches.

At the top of the headland is [Scarborough Castle](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/leeds_sheffield_and_yorkshire/scarborough/scarborough_castle) — a 12th-century fortress on a site that has been defended since the Iron Age. The climb is steep but the views are exceptional. Below, on the South Bay shore, the [Scarborough Spa](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/leeds_sheffield_and_yorkshire/scarborough/scarborough_spa) complex is the grand Victorian building that grew from the mineral spring that started the whole resort story in the 17th century.

The [Grand Hotel](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/leeds_sheffield_and_yorkshire/scarborough/grand_hotel) — the largest hotel in Europe when it opened in 1867 — still dominates the Esplanade skyline. Anne Brontë died there in 1849 and is buried in the churchyard below the castle. The [Stephen Joseph Theatre](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/leeds_sheffield_and_yorkshire/scarborough/stephen_joseph_theatre) — where Alan Ayckbourn has been premiering his plays for six decades — gives Scarborough a cultural life well above what most seaside towns can manage. The [Scarborough Art Gallery](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/leeds_sheffield_and_yorkshire/scarborough/scarborough_art_gallery), in an Italianate villa on the Crescent, has a good collection of Yorkshire coast paintings and is free to enter.
