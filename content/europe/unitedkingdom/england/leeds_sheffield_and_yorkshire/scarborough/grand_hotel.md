The Grand Hotel dominates the Scarborough skyline as it has since 1867, when it opened as the largest hotel in Europe and one of the largest buildings in the world. The building is a massive Victorian confection in yellow brick, with four towers, ornate ironwork, and a scale that was intended to impress — and still does.

The hotel continues to operate, though its condition and ownership have varied over the decades. Even if you are not staying, it is worth walking up to the terrace in front of the building for the view over the South Bay, which is one of the classic Scarborough vistas. The building is Grade II* listed. Anne Brontë died here in 1849 and is buried in St Mary's churchyard below the castle — a pilgrimage point for Brontë fans.
