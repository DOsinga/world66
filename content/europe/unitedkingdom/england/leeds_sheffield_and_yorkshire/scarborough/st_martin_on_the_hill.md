St Martin-on-the-Hill sits above Scarborough's South Cliff, away from the seafront amusements, and rewards anyone interested in Victorian church art. The building was designed in the 1860s, but the reason to go inside is the decoration: stained glass associated with the Pre-Raphaelite circle, painted panels, and a richly worked interior that feels unusually ambitious for a seaside parish church.

It pairs well with a walk along the Esplanade and the quieter South Cliff gardens. Even if churches are not your usual priority, St Martin's gives Scarborough a layer beyond beach, harbour, and castle: the resort as a serious 19th-century town with money, patrons, and a taste for high Victorian drama.
