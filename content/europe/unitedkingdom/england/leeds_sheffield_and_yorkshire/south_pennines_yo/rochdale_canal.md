story: >
  The Rochdale Canal was the first trans-Pennine canal to be completed, opening in 1804. At its busiest it carried coal, wool, and cotton across the watershed. It fell into disrepair in the mid-20th century but was restored and reopened for navigation in 2002 — one of the longest canal restorations in British history. The lock-keepers' cottages along the route are largely original.
---

The Rochdale Canal runs through the heart of the South Pennines, following the valley floor from Sowerby Bridge through Hebden Bridge, Todmorden, and on to Rochdale in Lancashire. The towpath makes for easy, level walking through the industrial landscape — mill buildings, lock chambers, stone bridges, and old warehouses line the route, with moorland rising steeply on both sides.

The full length through the Calder Valley is about 12 miles and can be walked in a long day, or broken into shorter sections by catching a train between the valley towns. Hebden Bridge to Todmorden (about 5 miles) is a particularly attractive stretch. The canal is navigable and sees some narrowboat traffic in summer, which adds to the atmosphere.
