Todmorden's town hall is an extraordinary piece of Victorian civic confidence. Built in 1875 to designs by John Gibson, the Italianate structure rises at the heart of the town with a giant portico of Corinthian columns and a pediment filled with allegorical sculpture representing the cotton and wool trades that paid for it. The scale is deliberately overwhelming — the mill owners who commissioned it wanted their market town to look like it meant business.

The building is Grade I listed and still in use as a venue. It is best viewed from the market place, where the full facade comes into focus and the columns frame the surrounding hillsides. Evening light picks out the detail of the stonework particularly well.
