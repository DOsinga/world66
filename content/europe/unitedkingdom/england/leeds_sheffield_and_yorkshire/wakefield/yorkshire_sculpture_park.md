story: "The park opened in 1977 in the grounds of the eighteenth-century Bretton Hall estate, founded by Peter Murray as the first sculpture park of its kind in the UK. Henry Moore had been born nearby in Castleford; he visited often, and his estate has since lent the park substantial holdings of his work, set in the rolling fields that resemble the landscapes he grew up in."

The Yorkshire Sculpture Park covers five hundred acres of rolling parkland six miles south-west of Wakefield, and is one of those rare places where landscape and art genuinely enhance each other. Henry Moore bronzes occupy the upper sheep pastures; Andy Goldsworthy walls and stones run through the woods; a series of indoor galleries — including a former chapel, a curved glass underground gallery, and the much-praised Weston building from 2019 — host substantial rotating shows.

You can spend a full day here and still not see everything. Entry to the park itself is free; you pay for parking and for any ticketed exhibition. On a clear day with deer grazing between the sculptures it is hard to think of a better free day out in England. Allow at least four hours and bring sturdy shoes — the trails are uneven and parts of the park are a real walk from the entrance.
