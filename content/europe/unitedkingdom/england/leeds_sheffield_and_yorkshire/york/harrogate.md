Harrogate is 20 miles west of York — a pleasant 45-minute train journey — and makes a very good day trip. The town grew wealthy in the 18th and 19th centuries as a spa resort, and the Victorian buildings along the Stray (a 200-acre open common around the town centre) and in the shopping streets retain a confident prosperity. The Turkish Baths — a Victorian Moorish-style suite of hot rooms and plunge pools — are still operating and worth an afternoon.

The Royal Horticultural Society's garden at Harlow Carr, on the edge of town, is one of the RHS's four major gardens and is particularly good in spring and early summer. The Bettys branch in Harrogate is the original, opened in 1919, and slightly less crowded than the York branch on most days. The town has good independent restaurants and a farmers' market that draws produce from across the Dales. Trains from York run frequently throughout the day.
