Whitby is one of the most compelling day trips from York, 45 miles to the northeast on the North Sea coast. The town sits at the mouth of the River Esk, with the old fishing harbour and the newer West Cliff facing each other across the water. Above the old town, reached by 199 steps, stand the ruins of Whitby Abbey — a 13th-century Benedictine church dissolved by Henry VIII, whose exposed clifftop position has given it a photographic familiarity out of proportion to its actual dimensions. The combination of the abbey ruins, the gothic graveyard, and the harbour below is quite distinctive.

Bram Stoker spent several holidays in Whitby and drew on the setting extensively for Dracula (1897): the Russian ship runs aground at Whitby harbour, Dracula arrives in the form of a large dog, and Lucy Westenra sleeps in the clifftop churchyard. The Dracula connection sustains a modest industry, and the twice-yearly Whitby Goth Weekend brings several thousand people in Victorian mourning dress to the town. Whitby jet — a black gemstone formed from compressed wood — has been mined locally since the Bronze Age and is still carved and sold here; the jet jewellery shops on Church Street are genuine rather than souvenir operations. Direct buses run from York; the scenic alternative is the train to Scarborough and a bus from there.
