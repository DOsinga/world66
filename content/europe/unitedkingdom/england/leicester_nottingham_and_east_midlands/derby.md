Derby has a strong claim to being the birthplace of the Industrial Revolution. The [Derby Silk Mill](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/leicester_nottingham_and_east_midlands/derby/derby_silk_mill), built on the River Derwent in 1721, was one of the world's first factories, and the [Derwent Valley Mills](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/leicester_nottingham_and_east_midlands/derby/derwent_valley_mills) stretching north to Cromford are a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Today the city is better known as the home of Rolls-Royce and a gateway to the [Peak District](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/peakdistrict).

The city centre has a handsome [Cathedral Quarter](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/leicester_nottingham_and_east_midlands/derby/cathedral_quarter) built around [Derby Cathedral](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/leicester_nottingham_and_east_midlands/derby/derby_cathedral), a good market hall, and the [Derby Museum and Art Gallery](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/leicester_nottingham_and_east_midlands/derby/derby_museum_and_art_gallery), which holds the largest collection of Joseph Wright of Derby paintings — dramatic candlelit scenes of 18th-century science and industry. The [QUAD arts centre](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/leicester_nottingham_and_east_midlands/derby/quad_arts_centre) brings contemporary exhibitions and cinema.

Derby is not a tourist destination in the usual sense, but it makes a practical base for the southern Peak District, and the industrial heritage trail along the Derwent Valley is genuinely fascinating — a walk through the origins of the modern world. The [Cathedral Quarter](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/leicester_nottingham_and_east_midlands/derby/cathedral_quarter) and [Sadler Gate](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/leicester_nottingham_and_east_midlands/derby/sadler_gate) give the city a liveable centre with good independent restaurants, bars, and the oldest pub in the city — [Ye Olde Dolphin Inne](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/leicester_nottingham_and_east_midlands/derby/ye_olde_dolphin_inne), which has been serving ale since around 1530.
