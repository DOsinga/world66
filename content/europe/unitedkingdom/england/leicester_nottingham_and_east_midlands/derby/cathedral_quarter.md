The Cathedral Quarter is the historic heart of Derby, a compact network of streets running north from the Market Place to the Cathedral. Iron Gate, Sadler Gate, and Queen Street are its main axes — all pedestrianised or traffic-restricted — and they contain the city's best concentration of independent shops, restaurants, bars, and historic buildings. The architecture is a mix of Georgian town houses, Victorian commercial buildings, and a few genuine medieval survivors, including the timber frame of the Old Bell Hotel on Sadler Gate.

The Cathedral itself, at the top of Iron Gate, is the anchor. The surrounding streets reward a slow walk: the ironwork on the gates and railings is unusually fine, there are several small courtyards and alleys that open unexpectedly off the main streets, and the pace is calmer than the modern shopping centre to the south. This is the part of Derby worth spending time in.
