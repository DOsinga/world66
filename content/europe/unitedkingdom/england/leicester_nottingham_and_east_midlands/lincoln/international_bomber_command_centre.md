The International Bomber Command Centre stands on a ridge outside Lincoln, with a tall memorial spire aligned toward the cathedral and the wartime airfields of Lincolnshire. It commemorates the aircrew and civilians connected to RAF Bomber Command, while the exhibition spaces explain the human cost behind the statistics.

It is a sobering visit rather than a machinery museum. The best reason to go is the balance of memorial, archive, and landscape: from the site you can understand why this part of eastern England became so central to the air war.
