Northampton is a large market town in the East Midlands that has been making shoes since the Middle Ages — at its peak it supplied boots to the British Army, and a handful of quality shoemakers still operate here today. The town's history is older than that: it was a major medieval centre with a royal castle where parliaments were held, though almost nothing survives of either.

What does survive is a scattering of fine churches, including the [Church of the Holy Sepulchre](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/leicester_nottingham_and_east_midlands/northampton/holy_sepulchre), one of only four round churches left in England, built by a Crusader knight in about 1100. [St Peter's Church](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/leicester_nottingham_and_east_midlands/northampton/st_peters_church) on Marefair is another Norman gem of comparable quality. The market square is one of the largest in the country, overlooked by [All Saints' Church](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/leicester_nottingham_and_east_midlands/northampton/all_saints_church) with its statue of Charles II. The [Northampton Museum and Art Gallery](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/leicester_nottingham_and_east_midlands/northampton/northampton_museum) holds one of the world's largest shoe collections, a fitting monument to the town's industrial heritage.

Northampton is not a place many tourists seek out, but it makes a practical base for visiting [Althorp House](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/leicester_nottingham_and_east_midlands/northampton/althorp_house) (the Spencer family home where Diana, Princess of Wales, is buried) and the [Rushton Triangular Lodge](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/leicester_nottingham_and_east_midlands/northampton/rushton_triangular_lodge) — one of England's most extraordinary follies, built as a coded Catholic monument in the 16th century.
