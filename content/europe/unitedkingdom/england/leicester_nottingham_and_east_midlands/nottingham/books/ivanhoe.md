Scott's 1819 novel is the book that fixed the modern image of Robin Hood: a Saxon outlaw resisting Norman oppression in Sherwood Forest, in alliance with a returning Richard the Lionheart. The Robin Hood of the medieval ballads was an altogether shiftier figure; Scott invented the noble-rebel version that has dominated ever since, including in Nottingham's own marketing. Read it for what it did, not necessarily for what it is.
