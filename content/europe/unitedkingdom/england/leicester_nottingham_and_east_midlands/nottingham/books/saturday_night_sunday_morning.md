Sillitoe's 1958 novel follows Arthur Seaton, a 22-year-old lathe operator at the Raleigh bicycle factory in Nottingham, through his weekends of drinking, fighting and sleeping with other men's wives. It's a foundational text of British "kitchen-sink" realism — angry, funny, and unillusioned about the working-class city it describes. Half a century on, it still reads as a more honest portrait of Nottingham than anything the tourist board would prefer.
