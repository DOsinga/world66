London's markets do different jobs depending on the day and the neighbourhood, and the good ones reward showing up for the right one. Borough Market under the London Bridge railway arches has been trading in some form since the twelfth century and is still the place to eat your way through a Saturday; Portobello Road turns into a mile of antiques dealers and vintage stalls on the same day; and Columbia Road only exists as a flower market at all between eight and three on Sunday mornings, when a single Shoreditch street disappears under buckets of blooms.

The common thread is that none of these are curated retail experiences dressed up as markets — Leadenhall's Victorian arcade, Camden's canalside stalls, and Electric Avenue's Caribbean grocers all grew out of genuine trade, and it still shows in the crowd, the haggling, and the produce on offer.
