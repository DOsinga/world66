Woolf's 1925 novel follows Clarissa Dalloway over a single day in post-WWI London as she prepares for an evening party. She walks through Westminster, crosses the park, passes through Mayfair — the London she moves through is still, largely, there. The novel pioneered the stream-of-consciousness technique and remains one of the finest portraits of London as a psychological landscape, a city that holds its history invisibly in its streets.
