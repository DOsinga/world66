The obvious risk with a list like this is padding it with anywhere that merely looks old. These five clear that bar with room to spare: the Prospect of Whitby has stood on the Thames at Wapping since around 1520, with Samuel Pepys and Turner both among its regulars; Ye Olde Cheshire Cheese was rebuilt within a year of the Great Fire and has barely been touched since; and the Lamb and Flag has been slinging pints down its Covent Garden alley since the seventeenth century, unbothered by the crowds a hundred yards away.

What ties them together isn't just age but continuity — these are pubs where the building, the trade, and often the crowd have carried on with only minor interruption for centuries, so a pint here comes with the accumulated texture of the city rather than a themed approximation of it.
