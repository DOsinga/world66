Battersea Power Station spent fifty years as a decommissioned coal-fired power plant before its four chimneys were rebuilt around a shopping and residential district — the old boiler house is full of shops now instead of turbines. The Roundhouse in Camden started life in 1847 as a shed for turning railway engines around and has been a music venue since the 1960s, with a guest list running from Jimi Hendrix and Pink Floyd to today's touring acts. Wilton's Music Hall did the reverse journey: built as a Victorian music hall, it spent decades as a rag warehouse and mission hall before reopening as a performance space, deliberately left unrestored.

Underneath Smithfield Market, the Victorian cold storage rooms that once kept meat fresh are now Fabric, one of London's most respected nightclubs. And a mile of Bermondsey's railway arches, built to carry trains over the streets below, now house a strip of craft brewery taprooms that fills up every Saturday morning. London rarely demolishes redundant infrastructure — it just waits for someone to find a new use for it.
