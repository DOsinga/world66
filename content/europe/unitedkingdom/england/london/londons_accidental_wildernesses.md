Most of London's best nature isn't in a park that was ever designed to be one — it's land the city stopped using for something else. Walthamstow Wetlands turned a set of working Thames Water reservoirs into the largest urban wetland reserve in Europe, still supplying drinking water on one side of the fence and packed with herons and cormorants on the other. Woodberry Wetlands did the same trick on a smaller scale in Hackney, and the London Wetland Centre out in Barnes converted old reservoir land beside the Thames into lagoons and reed beds that pull in migrating waterbirds every winter.

Smaller pockets do the same thing at a different scale: Camley Street Natural Park is a pond and meadow squeezed onto former railway land beside the canal at King's Cross, Greenwich Peninsula Ecology Park is a wetland tucked into the redevelopment around the O2, and Sydenham Hill Wood is a genuine relic of the ancient woodland that covered this part of London before any of it was built on. None of them were meant to stay wild — they just never got developed, and the wildlife moved in.
