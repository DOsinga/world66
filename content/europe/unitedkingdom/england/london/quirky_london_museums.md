London has enough major museums that the small, strange ones tend to get overlooked, which is a shame, since they're often the more memorable visit. An architect's house preserved exactly as he left it, stuffed with antiquities and a genuine Egyptian sarcophagus. A cabinet-of-curiosities collection above a Hackney bar. A surgical amphitheatre tucked in the roof of a church, unused and forgotten for decades before its rediscovery.

None of these need a full day, most are free or cheap, and all of them reward the kind of curiosity that a big national collection can't quite satisfy.
