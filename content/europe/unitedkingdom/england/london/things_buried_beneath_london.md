London has an odd habit of building over its own history instead of clearing it away, so a lot of the city's strangest sights are the ones you'd walk straight past on the pavement above. A Roman temple to Mithras, found in postwar rubble and rebuilt in a glass case under a bank's headquarters. A Roman gladiatorial arena, discovered by accident in the basement of an art gallery. A stretch of the old city wall still standing near the Barbican, and a battered block of stone bricked into a Cannon Street wall that nobody can quite explain.

Two more recent layers: a Piccadilly line station that has sat disused since 1994, occasionally opened for torch-lit tours, and a narrow-gauge railway that spent seventy years hauling the capital's post under Mount Pleasant before anyone outside the sorting office ever rode it. Between them, they make the case that London's real history museum is underfoot, not on a plinth.
