story: "The Earl of Derby was imprisoned in the pub immediately before his execution in Bolton's market place in 1651, during the aftermath of the English Civil War. According to local tradition, the chair he sat in while awaiting his fate is still in the building. The execution followed the Storming of Bolton in 1644, when Royalist forces under Prince Rupert took the town and killed several hundred parliamentary defenders — an event bitter enough in local memory that it was known as the Bolton Massacre."

Ye Olde Man & Scythe on Churchgate is one of the oldest licensed premises in England, with records of a public house on this site dating to 1251. The current building dates largely from the seventeenth century — a timber-framed structure with low ceilings and small windows characteristic of the period — though it has been modified and restored over the centuries. The interior retains exposed beams, uneven floors, and the general atmosphere of a building that has absorbed several centuries of use.

The pub serves real ales alongside standard pub food, and its age and historical associations make it a popular stop on any tour of Bolton's town centre. The chair supposedly used by the Earl of Derby on the morning of his execution is displayed inside, surrounded by appropriate historical notes that will either add to your enjoyment of a pint or leave you entirely unmoved depending on your tolerance for this kind of heritage theatre. The location on Churchgate, a short walk from the town hall and market, makes it a natural stopping point between sights.
