Moyse's Hall is one of the most remarkable buildings in Bury St Edmunds, and one of the rarest in England: a Norman domestic building that has survived essentially intact since around 1180. It stands at the top of the market square, its round-headed windows and flint-and-stone construction distinct from everything around it. Over eight centuries it has been used for almost every conceivable purpose — dwelling, jail, workhouse, police station — before becoming a museum.

The collections focus on local archaeology and history, and include material from prehistoric Suffolk, the Roman period, Anglo-Saxon finds, and the town's medieval history. The Red Barn Murder exhibit — documenting the 1828 case in which William Corder murdered Maria Marten and tried to hide the body in a barn — is among the more arresting displays, including a book bound in Corder's own skin. Free entry.
