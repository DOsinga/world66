St Edmundsbury Cathedral occupies the site of the Abbey's parish church of St James, which was elevated to cathedral status in 1914 when the Diocese of St Edmundsbury and Ipswich was created. The building is a curious hybrid: a 16th-century nave, substantially remodelled and extended through the 20th century, and finally crowned by the Millennium Tower — a modern Gothic lantern tower completed in 2005 that gives the skyline the emphasis it had lacked.

The interior is worth more than a passing look. A succession of additions has produced a building that is richer than you might expect from the outside: medieval stonework, Victorian fittings, and the elegant new nave ceiling painted in blue and gold. The Norman Tower immediately adjacent — the original bell tower for the old Abbey, and one of the finest Norman gateways in England — is technically the cathedral's belfry. It is freely accessible during normal visiting hours and worth a good look both from inside and out.
