Chester Cathedral occupies the northeast corner of the city within the walls, a large sandstone building that has been in continuous religious use for nearly a thousand years. The current structure is mostly fourteenth-century Gothic, though elements of the original Norman building survive in the north transept.

The highlight is the choir: the medieval oak stalls, dating from around 1380, are among the finest surviving sets of Gothic woodcarving in England. The misericords — the hinged ledges that allowed monks to rest while standing — are carved with a catalogue of medieval imagination: jousting knights, mythological beasts, domestic scenes, and grotesque faces. The cloister garden is peaceful and free to enter; it is one of the quieter spots in a busy city. The cathedral asks for a voluntary donation rather than charging admission.
