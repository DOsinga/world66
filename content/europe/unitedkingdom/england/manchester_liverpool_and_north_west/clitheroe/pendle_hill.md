  In 1652, the Quaker founder George Fox climbed Pendle Hill and experienced the vision that convinced him to begin his ministry. He wrote: "I was moved of the Lord to go atop of it; and when I came atop of it I saw Lancashire sea." The hill had already been associated with the witch trials of 1612, when twelve people from the villages below were accused and ten hanged. Fox's vision added a layer of religious significance that persists among Quakers, who still make pilgrimages here.

Pendle Hill (557 metres) is the unmistakable landmark of the Ribble Valley — a long, flat-topped moorland mass that looms over Clitheroe and the surrounding villages. The walk to the summit from Barley, on the southern side, takes about 90 minutes at a comfortable pace. The path is well-marked and the terrain is open moorland: boggy in places after rain but generally manageable.

The hill is inseparable from the Pendle Witch Trials of 1612, when nineteen people were accused of witchcraft; twelve were tried at Lancaster Assizes and ten hanged. The surrounding villages — Barley, Newchurch, Roughlee, Sabden — trade on this history, and a walking trail connects them. The summit views on a clear day reach to the Lake District, the Yorkshire Dales, and the Irish Sea.
