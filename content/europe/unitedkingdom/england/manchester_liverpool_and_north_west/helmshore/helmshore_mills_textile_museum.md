Helmshore Mills Textile Museum occupies two original mill buildings on the River Ogden that together span the full story of the Lancashire textile industry. Higher Mill, the older building, contains water-powered fulling machinery — great wooden hammers that pound and compact freshly woven cloth — that dates from the late 18th century and still runs on its original wheel. Whitaker's Mill next door houses an extraordinary collection of spinning machinery, including massive self-acting mules that can still be set in motion during demonstrations.

The museum does something most industrial history sites fail to achieve: it makes the machinery feel alive. Watching the mules extend and retract, drawing thread from roving into fine yarn, gives a real sense of the physical scale and rhythmic power of the industrial revolution. The guides who run the demonstrations know their subject well and can explain the technology clearly. This is an excellent museum for children and adults alike — allow two to three hours. Check opening times in advance as the museum has had seasonal closures.
