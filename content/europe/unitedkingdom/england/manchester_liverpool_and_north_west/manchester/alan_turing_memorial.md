The Alan Turing Memorial in Sackville Gardens, a short walk from Canal Street, shows the mathematician seated on a park bench, holding an apple — a reference to the manner of his death. The statue was unveiled in 2001, fifty years after Turing was convicted for the "crime" of being gay by the same government whose codebreaking operation he had effectively won the Second World War for.

Turing's foundational contributions to computing and artificial intelligence were worked out in Manchester: he joined the University of Manchester in 1948, and it was on the Manchester Mark 1 computer that much of his theoretical work was first implemented. The memorial is in the Gay Village, close to the canal, and is tended carefully. It has become a place of pilgrimage for computing enthusiasts and human rights advocates alike. The apple at his side is both biographical fact and symbol.
