Bolton-by-Bowland is a quiet, attractive village in the Ribble Valley on the southern fringe of the Forest of Bowland, an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty in Lancashire. The village is centred on a broad green with a set of old stocks, overlooked by the medieval [church of St Peter and St Paul](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/manchester_liverpool_and_north_west/pendle/bolton__by__bowl/st_peter_and_st_paul_church). It is the kind of place where very little happens, and that is precisely the appeal.

The Forest of Bowland is one of England's least-visited upland landscapes, a rolling expanse of heather moorland, deep valleys, and scattered farms. Walking is the main reason to come here. The village makes a good starting point for circular walks along the [Ribble Way](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/manchester_liverpool_and_north_west/pendle/bolton__by__bowl/ribble_way_walking) or up onto the fells, where you are likely to see curlews, lapwings, and hen harriers. [Gisburn Forest](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/manchester_liverpool_and_north_west/pendle/bolton__by__bowl/gisburn_forest), four kilometres to the north, offers mountain biking trails and walks through conifer woodland.

The Coach and Horses pub on the green serves decent food and local ales. Bolton-by-Bowland is small enough that you can see everything in half an hour, but the countryside around it rewards a longer stay. The larger market town of Clitheroe, with its Norman castle and good independent shops, is about 10 kilometres to the south.
