Crook Hall is a 13th-century manor house and walled garden complex in a quiet corner of Durham, about a fifteen-minute walk north of the cathedral along the river. The house has been added to across the centuries — a medieval hall, a Jacobean wing, a Georgian section — and the layering gives it the quality of having grown organically rather than been designed. The gardens are exceptional for a private property of this size: a formal walled garden, a moat garden, a maze, and wild-garden areas that attract significant birdlife.

The hall and gardens are open to visitors seasonally (typically April to September). The café in the gardens serves good home-cooked lunches and teas. It's less visited than the cathedral and castle, which gives it a pleasant unhurried atmosphere.
