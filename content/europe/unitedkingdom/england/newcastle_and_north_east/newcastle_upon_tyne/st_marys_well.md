story: St Mary's Well in Jesmond was, in the late medieval period, one of the most visited Marian shrines in northern England — second only to Walsingham. The pilgrimage routes that converged on it gave the surrounding hamlet of Jesmond its 13th-century name (Jesemuth, "the mouth of the brook with the Jesus").
---

A modest, easily missed survival in suburban Jesmond — a square stone well-head sunk into the ground near the ruins of a medieval chapel of St Mary. In the 14th and 15th centuries the site drew pilgrims from across the north of England seeking miracle cures. The Reformation broke its formal cult, and the well has been quietly here ever since.

Easy detour from a walk through Jesmond Dene. Free, no facilities. The information board next to it gives the medieval context.
