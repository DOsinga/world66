Alnwick (pronounced "Annick" — don't say it as written) is the traditional county town of Northumberland, and it punches well above its weight for a market town of 8,000 people. The two great draws are the castle and the garden. [Alnwick Castle](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/newcastle_and_north_east/northumberland/alnwick/alnwick_castle) is one of the grandest medieval castles in England — the seat of the Percy family, Dukes of Northumberland, for over 700 years, and familiar to millions as the exterior of Hogwarts in the early Harry Potter films. [The Alnwick Garden](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/newcastle_and_north_east/northumberland/alnwick/the_alnwick_garden), created from the late 1990s by the Duchess of Northumberland, is one of the most ambitious new gardens in Britain — and contains, among its features, a Poison Garden planted exclusively with plants that can kill you.

The town itself is pleasant without being spectacular: a market square, a mediaeval gateway at Bondgate, and a Georgian street grid behind it. But [Barter Books](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/newcastle_and_north_east/northumberland/alnwick/barter_books), housed in the old Victorian railway station, is worth making a detour for on its own terms — one of the largest second-hand bookshops in Britain, with a model railway running along the ceiling and open fires in winter. It is also the place where a copy of the original "Keep Calm and Carry On" poster was found in 2000, unwittingly launching what became a global merchandising phenomenon.

Alnwick makes a good base for Northumberland coast and countryside. The magnificent [Dunstanburgh Castle](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/newcastle_and_north_east/northumberland/alnwick/dunstanburgh_castle) is eight miles down the coast, reachable by a coastal walk from Craster. Warkworth, with its own fine castle, is seven miles south. The National Trust's Cragside — the first house in the world to be lit by hydro-electricity — is in the hills above Rothbury, about twelve miles to the west.
