The Warkworth Hermitage is one of the strangest and most atmospheric sights in Northumberland: a small medieval chapel and priest's house cut directly into a sandstone cliff on the north bank of the River Coquet, accessible only by rowing boat from the south bank.

English Heritage operates the short ferry crossing during summer months (April to September). The interior of the rock-cut chapel is surprisingly elaborate — a vaulted ceiling, carved altar niches, and a figure of the Virgin all survive. The site was used as a place of private worship by the Percy family, who endowed a hereditary hermit-priest to serve it. The combination of the river crossing, the cliff setting, and the carving makes it one of the most memorable short visits in northern England.
