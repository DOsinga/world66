  The first Wearmouth Bridge, built in 1796, was the first iron bridge in England after Ironbridge itself, and at the time of its construction it had the widest single span of any iron bridge in the world. The current bridge replaced it in 1929 and was engineered by Mott, Hay and Anderson, later responsible for the Forth Road Bridge. The railway bridge running parallel to it — the King Edward Bridge — opened in 1879 and has the unusual feature of a swinging central span.

The current Wearmouth Bridge carries the main road across the Wear between the city centre and the north bank where the university and National Glass Centre stand. The view from the bridge up and down the river is good, taking in the university campus, the wharves, and the curve of the river toward the sea. The bridge is a reasonable piece of interwar engineering, though not a match for the spectacular spans at Newcastle.

Alongside the road bridge, the older railway bridge can be seen swinging open occasionally to allow river traffic. Walking across the road bridge — a five-minute stroll — gives the best general view of the riverside and is the natural route between the city centre and the north bank attractions.
