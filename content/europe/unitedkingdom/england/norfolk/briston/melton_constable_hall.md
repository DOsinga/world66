story: "Built for Sir Jacob Astley in 1664–87, Melton Constable Hall is one of the earliest and finest Carolean country houses in England, and reputedly the inspiration for the Sotherton Court of Jane Austen's Mansfield Park. The Astleys lived here for three centuries before selling in 1948; the hall passed through several hands and slid into decline. In the 1980s it was used as a location for the Joseph Losey film The Go-Between, set on its own neglected lawns."
---

The hall stands behind iron gates on the road between Briston and Melton Constable, a square red-brick block with a hipped roof and a balustraded parapet. It is privately owned and not open to the public, but the exterior is easy to see from the road, and the long avenue of trees leading to it is a clue to how grand the seventeenth-century landscape once was.

The associated parkland is part of the same estate, the village of Melton Constable was rebuilt by the Astleys as a model railway town in the 1880s, and the church of St Peter sits within the park. Combined, they make a striking essay on what a Norfolk estate village looked like in the heyday of country-house England.
