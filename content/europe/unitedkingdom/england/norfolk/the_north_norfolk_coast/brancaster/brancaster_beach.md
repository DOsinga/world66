Brancaster Beach is one of the great Norfolk beaches — wide, flat, backed by dunes, and at low tide almost impossibly expansive. The sand stretches for miles in either direction with nothing much to interrupt it, and the sky above the North Sea is huge in a way that rewards simply standing and looking.

The beach is reached on foot from the village or from the National Trust car park to the north. Swimming is possible but the currents and tides on this coast demand respect; the beach shelves very gradually and the tide goes out a long way. Dogs are welcome year-round on most of the beach. At dusk or dawn in winter, with a low sun and perhaps a line of oystercatchers working the tideline, this is one of the finest stretches of coast in England.
