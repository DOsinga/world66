---
title: 'When to Go'
type: section
---

The North Norfolk Coast is a year-round destination, but the character of the place changes dramatically by season. Spring and autumn are the best times for birdwatching — spring brings migrating waders and passerines arriving from Africa, while autumn is peak passage season when rarities turn up regularly and the numbers of birds moving through can be extraordinary. The RSPB reserve at Titchwell is particularly productive in both seasons.

Summer is the time for beach-going, and Brancaster beach in July and August is as good as English coastal beaches get. The dunes are warm, the sand is wide, and the sky is enormous. The village and coast road can get congested at peak summer weekends, but nothing like the levels of traffic that affect the south coast.

Winter is the secret season for those who know the coast. Brent geese and other wildfowl arrive in large numbers, the crowds thin to almost nothing, and the low winter light across the marsh and sea has a quality that summer light simply lacks. The pubs are warm and the walks are empty.
