Cley and Salthouse Marshes are the reason many travellers know the village at all. Norfolk Wildlife Trust bought the reserve in 1926, and its pools, reedbeds, grazing marsh, and shingle edge have become a serious birdwatching site.

Avocets, bitterns, marsh harriers, spoonbills, wigeon, and lapwings are among the birds that bring people here. Even if you are not a birder, the visitor centre and hides give you a fine way to understand the north Norfolk coast: flat, exposed, watery, and full of small movements.
