North East England runs from the Tees to the Scottish border, and from the North Sea up into the Pennines. It is the least visited corner of England, which surprises most people who come, because it has some of the country's best sights: the most dramatic stretch of [Hadrian's Wall](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_east/hadrians_wall), a coast lined with castles, two of the great early Christian sites of Europe, and [Durham](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_east/durham) cathedral on its rock above the Wear.

[Newcastle](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_east/newcastle_upon_tyne) is the regional capital and the best base: a handsome Victorian centre, the bridges over the Tyne, a famous night out, and the Metro out to the seaside at [Tynemouth](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_east/tynemouth). [Sunderland](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_east/sunderland) has the National Glass Centre and Souter Lighthouse on the coast, and [Hartlepool](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_east/hartlepool) has its historic quay and HMS Trincomalee.

The **Northumberland coast** is the reason many people come, and it rewards a few days. Castles stand on almost every headland, from [Warkworth](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_east/warkworth) and the market town of [Alnwick](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_east/alnwick) to [Bamburgh](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_east/bamburgh), and offshore are the puffins and seals of the [Farne Islands](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_east/farne_islands) and the tidal [Holy Island](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_east/holy_island) of Lindisfarne. At the top of the coast, [Berwick-upon-Tweed](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_east/berwickontweed) still has its Elizabethan walls. See the [Northumberland Coast](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_east/northumberland_coast) for the whole stretch.

Inland the country empties out. [Northumberland National Park](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_east/northumberland_national_park) and the Cheviot Hills, reached from [Wooler](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_east/wooler), are the least visited national park in England, and [Kielder](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_east/kielder) has a huge reservoir in a huge forest under some of the darkest skies in Europe. To the south-west the [North Pennines](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_east/north_pennines) have high moors and the waterfall of High Force. Up by the border, [Ford and Etal](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_east/ford_and_etal) are two estate villages near the battlefield of Flodden. West of Newcastle, [Consett](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_east/consett) sits on the edge of the moors at the top of the old railway paths.

The region was an engine of the Industrial Revolution: coal, shipbuilding and the first railways. George Stephenson was born at Wylam, and Joseph Swan lit his house in Gateshead with electric light before Edison. The mines and yards have closed, but museums like Beamish, the Discovery Museum and the Washington F Pit keep that history.
