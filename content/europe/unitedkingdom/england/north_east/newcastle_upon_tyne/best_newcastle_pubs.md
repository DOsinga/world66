Newcastle's best pubs are not on the Bigg Market, and most of them are not even in the city centre. The Crown Posada is the one to see first — a narrow, deep Victorian room on the Side with two Stephen Adam stained-glass windows and a strict no-television rule — while the Free Trade Inn has the single best view in town, a beer garden on a ridge above the Ouseburn looking straight at the King Edward VII Bridge. The Cumberland Arms, a short steep walk further up the same lane, is the headquarters of the city's folk-music scene, and the two together make an obvious crawl.

Closer to the centre, the Bridge Hotel pairs unspoilt Victorian mahogany with a terrace cantilevered out over the Castle Garth, and the Broad Chare turns proper British cooking — kedgeree, suet puddings, a Sunday lunch that books out weeks ahead — into a reason to visit a pub instead of a restaurant. The Tyne and the Cluny, both tucked under the Ouseburn's railway viaducts, round it out with live music and beer gardens built into the industrial architecture, which is Newcastle's drinking scene in one sentence: unpretentious, characterful, and built into whatever structure was already standing.
