Newcastle rewards looking up, down, and sideways rather than just straight ahead. Above a back door behind the cathedral, a fanged, glossy-black gargoyle called the Vampire Rabbit has been snarling down at passers-by since the 1890s for reasons nobody has ever satisfactorily explained. West of the centre, the stone footprint of a Roman temple to a half-forgotten Celtic god survives, improbably, in the back gardens of a 1930s housing estate. In suburban Jesmond, a plain stone well-head marks what was once one of northern England's busiest pilgrimage sites, second only to Walsingham.

The city's Brutalist Civic Centre keeps its own secret in plain sight — startling bronze seahorses jutting from the corners of a 1968 tower block — while the burial ground at Ballast Hills, built from the stones ships dumped before loading coal, and the fragments of medieval town wall tucked behind Chinatown round out a city whose past keeps surfacing in places nobody thinks to look.
