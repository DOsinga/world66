Newcastle has a habit of getting there first without making much noise about it. Robert Stephenson's High Level Bridge opened in 1849 as the world's first bridge to stack a railway on top of road traffic, a design later copied across Europe. Thirty years later, the reading room at the Lit and Phil became the first public building on Earth lit by Joseph Swan's incandescent bulb, three years before the equivalent was demonstrated in the United States. Then in 1894, Charles Parsons built Turbinia, the world's first turbine-driven steamship, and in 1897 crashed her, uninvited, into a Royal Navy review at more than twice the speed of anything the Admiralty owned — the orders followed within the year.

The habit didn't stop with the Victorians. The Tyne Bridge went up in 1928 as the working prototype for the design later scaled up for Sydney Harbour, and the Gateshead Millennium Bridge opened in 2001 as the world's first tilting bridge, rotating shut like an eyelid rather than lifting or swinging. Taken together, they're a reminder that an unassuming, hard-working city has spent the best part of two centuries quietly setting the technical benchmark for everyone else.
