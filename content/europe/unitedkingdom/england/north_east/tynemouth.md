Tynemouth sits where the Tyne meets the North Sea, twenty-five minutes from central Newcastle on the Metro. It is a handsome place of Georgian and Victorian terraces, with a busy front street of cafés, pubs and independent shops, and it gets crowded on sunny weekends.

The headland is taken up by the ruins of [Tynemouth Priory and Castle](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_east/tynemouth/tynemouth_priory_and_castle), a monastery inside a fortress with gun batteries that guarded the river mouth until the Second World War. Below, the North Pier runs half a mile out to its lighthouse.

North of the headland, [Longsands](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_east/tynemouth/longsands) is a wide Blue Flag beach and the best surfing spot on this coast. King Edward's Bay, a small sandy cove under the priory, is more sheltered. At weekends the [market in Tynemouth station](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_east/tynemouth/tynemouth_market), under the restored Victorian iron-and-glass canopy, sells crafts, books and street food.
