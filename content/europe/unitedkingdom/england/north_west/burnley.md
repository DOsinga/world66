Burnley is a post-industrial town in Lancashire, set among the Pennine hills about 25 miles north of Manchester. Its Victorian textile and coal heritage is still very much visible in the built environment — mill buildings, canal infrastructure, and the handsome [Burnley Embankment](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_west/burnley/burnley_embankment), which carries the Leeds and Liverpool Canal on a dramatic causeway above the town centre.

[Towneley Hall](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_west/burnley/towneley_hall), a free country house museum and gallery on the southern edge of town, is consistently overlooked relative to its quality. The most distinctive landmark in the area is the [Singing Ringing Tree](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_west/burnley/singing_ringing_tree), a wind-powered sound sculpture on the Pennine moors above Crown Point that produces an eerie, resonant hum and has dramatic views across Lancashire.

[Burnley FC](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_west/burnley/turf_moor) has played at [Turf Moor](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_west/burnley/turf_moor) since 1883 and is one of the most historically significant clubs relative to its town's size in English football. [Moorhouse's Brewery](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_west/burnley/moorhouses_brewery) has been making real ales here since 1865. The [Burnley Mechanics](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_west/burnley/burnley_mechanics) is the main cultural venue, with a consistent programme of theatre, comedy, and music.
