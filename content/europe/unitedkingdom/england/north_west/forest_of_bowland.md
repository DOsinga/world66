The Forest of Bowland is a large area of high moorland and deep, quiet valleys in the north of Lancashire, protected as a National Landscape. It is a "forest" in the old sense of a royal hunting ground, not a wood: the tops are open heather and blanket bog, the haunt of hen harriers, curlews and red grouse, and the valleys are dotted with stone villages and farms.

The classic way through it is the [Trough of Bowland](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_west/forest_of_bowland/trough_of_bowland), a narrow road over a pass between Lancaster and [Clitheroe](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_west/clitheroe). Dunsop Bridge, in the middle, claims to be the village nearest the geographical centre of Great Britain. Villages such as [Bolton-by-Bowland](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_west/bolton_by_bowland), [Downham](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_west/downham) and Slaidburn make good bases for walks, and [Pendle Hill](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_west/pendle/pendle_hill) is part of the same protected area. On the south-western edge, [Beacon Fell](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_west/forest_of_bowland/beacon_fell) is an easy country park with views to the coast.
