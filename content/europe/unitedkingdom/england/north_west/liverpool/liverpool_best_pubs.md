Liverpool takes its pubs seriously enough that John Lennon once said the thing he missed most about being a Beatle was a quiet pint at the Philharmonic — a Victorian gin palace so ornate that visitors queue to see the marble-clad toilets. Around the corner, Peter Kavanagh's is a warren of snugs painted by a landlord who settled his decorators' bills in free drink for life, and the Roscoe Head is one of only five pubs in the country to appear in every edition of CAMRA's Good Beer Guide since it began in 1974.

The Beatles connections run just as deep without needing to be manufactured: Ye Cracke is where a young John Lennon drank between art college classes, and the Grapes on Mathew Street was the band's actual local when the alcohol-free Cavern Club next door wouldn't serve them. Add the smugglers'-tunnel history under the Baltic Fleet's dockside bar, and the throughline is a city where the pub was always as much the story as the beer.
