Some of Liverpool's best interiors don't advertise themselves. Beneath an office building near Exchange Flags, fifty rooms of a Second World War command bunker sat sealed and dust-covered for over forty years after staff simply locked the doors in 1945. On a quiet residential street, a plain frontage gives no hint of the Moorish Revival splendour inside Princes Road Synagogue. At Peter Kavanagh's, a former landlord who refused to pay his decorators in cash instead gave them free drinks for life, and the murals they left behind still cover every snug room in the pub.

The throughline is Liverpool's habit of putting the real story one layer beneath the surface: the Philharmonic Dining Rooms hides marble urinals admired as a tourist attraction in their own right, the Baltic Fleet sits over smuggling tunnels that once ran to the docks, and St Luke's — a roofless shell gutted by German firebombs in 1941 — is, on any given night, one of the liveliest event spaces in the city. None of it looks like much from the street. All of it rewards actually going in.
