Manchester's industrial wealth built more pubs than it knew what to do with, and a surprising number of the best ones are still standing more or less as built. The Peveril of the Peak wraps an entire freestanding building in green-and-cream Victorian tiles; a few streets over, the Britons Protection pours from a list of 360 whiskies under murals of the Peterloo Massacre that happened outside its door. Mr Thomas's Chop House has been feeding Cross Street since 1867, and the Marble Arch's sloped mosaic floor was already old when the pub started brewing its own beer in the back room.

None of these are gastropubs dressed up for a weekend crowd — they're working pubs that happened to survive with their original fittings intact, in a city that spent the postwar decades knocking most of the rest down. Order a pint, look up at the ceiling, and you're standing in more Manchester history than most of the museums manage to hold.
