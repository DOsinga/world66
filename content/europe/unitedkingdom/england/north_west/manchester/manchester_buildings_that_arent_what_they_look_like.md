Manchester grew rich fast and built accordingly, which means the city is full of buildings whose grandeur was aimed at something other than what they're used for today. The neo-Gothic facade on Deansgate that so resembles a medieval church is in fact a library holding a Gutenberg Bible; the Victorian cotton exchange down the road now has a steel-and-glass theatre pod suspended inside its trading hall. A public swimming baths was built to look like a civic monument, tiled and stained-glassed as if it were a town hall rather than somewhere to get wet, and the world's oldest railway station now holds a science museum instead of a single train.

What unites them is a very Mancunian instinct: never build something merely functional when you could dress it up as something loftier. The result is a city where the most rewarding thing to do with a grand facade is walk through the door and see what's actually inside.
