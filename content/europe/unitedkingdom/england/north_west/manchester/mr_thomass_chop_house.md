Thomas Studd opened this chop house on Cross Street in 1867, on the site of an old Georgian townhouse whose narrow footprint the building still keeps. Cross Street was the main artery of Victorian Manchester, and the business — Studd's brother ran the similarly named Sam's Chop House nearby — quickly became one of the most popular addresses in the city.

The current building dates from 1901, designed by Robert Walker with one of Manchester's first cast-iron frames behind decorative terracotta and Accrington brick. It is Grade II listed and holds a place on CAMRA's National Inventory of Historic Pub Interiors, one of only a handful of Manchester pubs to do so. The menu still runs to proper British chop-house food — steak and kidney pudding, black pudding, potted meats — eaten at close-set tables under a high tiled ceiling.
