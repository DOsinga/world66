A pub has stood on this corner of Great Bridgewater Street since 1806, when its name and its business came from recruiting soldiers for the Napoleonic Wars. The building that survives today was remodelled in the 1930s and is now Grade II listed, with a terrazzo-tiled corridor, a moulded ceiling, and a rare hatch through which the front bar still serves two separate rear rooms.

The pub sits close to St Peter's Field, and interior murals depict the 1819 Peterloo Massacre — unconfirmed local legend holds that some of the wounded were carried inside and laid out on the bar. None of that gets in the way of the drinking: the Britons Protection is best known today for a whisky list running past 360 bottles, alongside a solid range of cask ales.
