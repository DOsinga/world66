Built in 1888 on the site of an older brewery tap on Rochdale Road, the Marble Arch has an interior CAMRA rates as being of outstanding national historic importance: a sloped mosaic-tiled floor, a barrel-vaulted ceiling, and glazed brickwork running the length of the room. It survived under a series of ordinary names — including the Wellington — until a 1980s freehouse takeover let it start brewing on the premises.

That back-room brewery became Marble Brewery, now one of the best-regarded cask ale producers in the north of England, though production has since moved to a larger site nearby. The Marble Arch remains its flagship pub, with the full seasonal range on tap and a room worth a long look even between pints.
