Morecambe is an old seaside resort on the shore of Morecambe Bay, a few miles from [Lancaster](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_west/lancaster). Its great asset is the bay itself: a vast sweep of sands, channels and salt marsh, with the Lakeland fells on the far side and sunsets that draw people to the promenade every clear evening. The bay is also dangerous, with fast tides and quicksand, and should only be crossed on foot with the official guide (see [crossing the bay](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_west/morecambe/cross_bay_walk)).

On the front is the white curve of the Midland Hotel, an Art Deco landmark of 1933, and nearby the statue of [Eric Morecambe](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_west/morecambe/eric_morecambe_statue), the comedian who took his stage name from the town. The promenade is good for walking and cycling, and is part of a cycle route along the bay.
