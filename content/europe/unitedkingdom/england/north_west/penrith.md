Penrith is a busy market town built of red sandstone, just outside the north-eastern corner of the [Lake District](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_west/lakedistrict) and a few minutes from the M6. It has been a crossroads for two thousand years: the Romans had a fort at Brougham, the medieval town grew up around a market and a church, and the ruined [castle](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_west/penrith/penrith_castle) by the station was built against Scottish raids.

The centre is a tangle of small market squares, with old coaching inns and independent shops and butchers, and Ullswater is only five miles away down the A592. South of the town are two of Cumbria's best castles: [Brougham Castle](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_west/penrith/brougham_castle) beside the River Eamont, and [Lowther Castle](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_west/penrith/lowther_castle), a romantic shell with restored gardens. North-east, in the Eden valley, is [Long Meg and Her Daughters](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/north_west/penrith/long_meg), one of the largest stone circles in England.
