Marlowe was born in Canterbury in 1564 and educated at the King's School in the cathedral precincts. *Doctor Faustus* — the story of a scholar who sells his soul to the devil in exchange for twenty-four years of knowledge and power — is his most enduring play, written shortly before his death in 1593. The verse is faster and more visceral than Shakespeare's; the ending, where Faustus realises the deal is real and tries fruitlessly to repent, is one of the most genuinely frightening scenes in English drama.

Reading it in Canterbury makes the connections legible: the cathedral's medieval certainty, the Renaissance city's emerging doubt, the small grammar school by the cloister where Marlowe first encountered Latin. There are several editions; the New Mermaids paperback is a good introduction with reliable notes.
