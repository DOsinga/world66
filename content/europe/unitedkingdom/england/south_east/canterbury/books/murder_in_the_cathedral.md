Eliot's 1935 verse drama tells the story of Thomas Becket's last weeks — his return from exile in France, his confrontations with the four tempters who try to dissuade him from the path that will end in his murder, and the killing itself in the cathedral's north-west transept. It was written specifically to be performed in the Canterbury Cathedral chapter house, where it premiered.

Short, stark, and largely in verse, it remains the best literary entry point to understanding why Canterbury became a pilgrimage city. The play is still performed in the cathedral roughly every other year — worth timing a visit around.
