---
title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

Canterbury eats well for a small cathedral city — partly because of the two universities, partly because the surrounding Kent countryside grows good produce and the coast is fifteen minutes away. **The Goods Shed** at Canterbury West railway station is the standout: a permanent indoor farmers' market with a restaurant on a mezzanine, serving cooking that is plainly very good but kept honest by what is at the stalls below.

For something more relaxed, **Tiny Tim's Tearooms** does the proper version of cake and pots of tea on the High Street, and **The Refectory Kitchen** in the cathedral lodge — yes, in the precincts — turns out surprisingly modern lunches with views of the towers. Kent's strong Indian-restaurant tradition is well represented; **The Ambrette** on the High Street is the most ambitious of the lot.

For pub food, **The Parrot** in St Radigund's Street is the local choice — a Tudor building, low ceilings, and a kitchen that takes itself a degree more seriously than the average gastropub.
