Crawley is a post-war New Town in West Sussex, designated in 1947 and built over the following decades to relieve housing pressure on London. Most visitors know it only as the town next to [Gatwick Airport](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/crawley/gatwick_airport), which sits just north of the borough and dominates the local economy. If you are flying in or out of Gatwick, Crawley offers cheaper hotels and more choice in restaurants than the airport itself.

The new town development was thorough but not total. The [Old Town](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/crawley/old_town) — a genuine medieval High Street with timber-framed buildings, a fifteenth-century coaching inn, and old pubs — survived intact and sits a short walk south of the station. Anyone with a few hours to spare between flights will find more here than the airport hotels suggest. [Worth Church](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/crawley/worth_church), in a village now absorbed into the eastern suburbs, is one of the finest Saxon churches in England, with nave walls dating to around 950 AD.

[Tilgate Park](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/crawley/tilgate_park) on the southern edge of town gives 260 acres of woodland, a large lake, a nature centre, and a walled garden — the best open space in the borough and a good place to clear your head after a long flight. Six kilometres south at Handcross, [Nymans Garden](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/crawley/nymans_garden) is one of the National Trust's finest gardens in the south-east, with outstanding seasonal plantings and romantic ruins at its centre.
