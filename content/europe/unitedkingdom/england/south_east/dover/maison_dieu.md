The Maison Dieu — House of God — was founded in 1203 by Hubert de Burgh as a hostel for pilgrims making their way from the Channel to the shrine of Thomas Becket at Canterbury. For centuries it was one of the most important stopping-points on the Canterbury pilgrim route; Edward I, Henry VIII and Napoleon III all passed through. The great hall dates from the 13th century, and the building retains its medieval bones despite later additions.

Today it forms the core of Dover Town Hall. The interior includes Victorian stained glass and a collection of portraits and ceremonial objects. Guided tours are available during the week, and the building is particularly atmospheric when illuminated for evening events.
