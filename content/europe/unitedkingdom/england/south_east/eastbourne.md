Eastbourne sits on the Sussex coast where the South Downs meet the English Channel, a Victorian seaside resort that still feels like one — in the best possible way. The [seafront](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/eastbourne/seafront_promenade) is grand: a long promenade, carpet gardens, a bandstand, and a [pier](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/eastbourne/eastbourne_pier) that has survived fires and storms since 1870.

The real draw is the landscape. [Beachy Head](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/eastbourne/beachy_head), the highest chalk sea cliff in Britain, rises 162 metres just west of town. The view from the top is genuinely dramatic — white chalk dropping vertically to the sea, with the red-and-white lighthouse far below. The [Seven Sisters](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/eastbourne/seven_sisters) chalk cliffs continue westward toward [Seaford](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/seaford), and the walk along them is one of the finest coastal paths in England. The [Belle Tout Lighthouse](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/eastbourne/belle_tout_lighthouse) halfway along the cliffs has its own remarkable story.

The town itself is pleasant without being exciting. The [Towner Gallery](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/eastbourne/towner_gallery), a striking modernist building near the station, has the largest collection of Eric Ravilious in the country. The [old town](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/eastbourne/old_town), slightly inland, has more character than the seafront — independent shops, a few decent pubs, and the medieval church of St Mary. The [Devonshire Park](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/eastbourne/devonshire_park) complex hosts the Eastbourne International tennis tournament each June, a week before Wimbledon.
