  Fort Blockhouse has been defending the mouth of Portsmouth Harbour since the 15th century — originally a simple earthwork, later rebuilt in brick and stone as gun technology improved. It was the main home of the Royal Navy's submarine service from 1905 until 1999, when the submarines moved to Faslane in Scotland. HMS Dolphin, the shore establishment built around the fort, trained generations of submariners in the 20th century.

Fort Blockhouse stands at the tip of the peninsula where Gosport's waterfront meets the entrance to Portsmouth Harbour. The fort itself is still in military use and not publicly accessible, but the Haslar sea wall running west from the fort is open and makes for one of the best walks in the area.

The sea wall follows the shoreline for about two kilometres, with wide views across the Solent toward the Isle of Wight on one side and back over the harbour mouth to Old Portsmouth on the other. The path is flat, exposed, and genuinely bracing on a windy day — this is the open water of the eastern Solent, not a sheltered estuary. At the southern end, near Fort Monckton, the seagrass beds are visible through the clear water at low tide, and common terns nest here in summer.
