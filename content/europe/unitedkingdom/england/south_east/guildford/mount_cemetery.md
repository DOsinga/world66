A late-Victorian cemetery on a hill west of the town centre, set out in 1856 with serpentine paths and rows of yew trees. The view from the upper terraces across to the cathedral is one of the better quiet panoramas of Guildford.

Lewis Carroll's grave is the cemetery's main draw — a plain white-marble cross at plot J/F1, near the upper boundary, with a small plaque added in 1932 by the Lewis Carroll Society. Carroll spent his last years with his sisters at The Chestnuts on Castle Hill, and died in Guildford during the influenza epidemic of January 1898.
