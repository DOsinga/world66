  The Covered Market was established by an Act of Parliament in 1774, when the city council decided to clear the market stalls that had been cluttering Oxford's streets since medieval times. The stalls were relocated into this purpose-built structure between High Street and Market Street, and the same basic layout — permanent shops within a covered arcade — has operated continuously ever since, making it one of the oldest indoor markets in Britain.
---

The Covered Market, accessible from High Street, Cornmarket, and Market Street, is a genuine working market with permanent shops and stalls in a large covered Victorian structure. Unlike many "covered markets" that have been sanitised into food courts, Oxford's still does what a market should: it has independent butchers, fishmongers, cheese shops, florists, a dedicated cobbler, and cafes that have been trading in the same spot for decades.

The market is best visited on a weekday morning when the serious shopping is happening. Ben's Cookies, with their warm chocolate-chip cookies sold by weight, has been operating here for decades and draws a constant queue. Several cafes serve good coffee and breakfast in the market's narrow passages. Christmas is an especially good time to visit: the stalls get decorated, the food shopping intensifies, and the whole place smells of pine needles and cheese. The market is free to enter.
