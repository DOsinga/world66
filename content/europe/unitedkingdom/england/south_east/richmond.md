Richmond is one of the most appealing places to spend a day within striking distance of central London — far enough from the centre to feel genuinely unhurried, close enough that you don't need to plan much. The town itself is attractive, with a good high street, a riverside walk along the Thames, and [Richmond Green](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/richmond/richmond_green) providing a handsome open space a few minutes from the station.

The real prize is [Richmond Park](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/richmond/richmond_park). At 2,500 acres, it is the largest of London's royal parks — big enough that deer herds roam freely and the city disappears entirely from view. The park has been used for royal hunting since Charles I enclosed it in the 1630s, and from King Henry's Mound on its north-western edge there is a protected sightline all the way to St Paul's Cathedral, seventeen miles away. On a clear day this is one of the best views in greater London.

[Kew Gardens](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/richmond/kew_gardens) lies just to the north-east and is easily combined with a Richmond visit. The riverside pubs at the foot of [Richmond Hill](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/richmond/richmond_hill) are packed on summer evenings and worth finding early. Along the river south of town, [Ham House](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/richmond/ham_house) preserves one of the finest 17th-century interiors in England — the towpath walk there is as rewarding as the house itself.
