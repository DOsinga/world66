Seaford is the quieter Sussex seaside town at the western end of the Seven Sisters country. It does not have Eastbourne's resort polish or Brighton's noise, and that is a large part of the appeal: a long shingle beach, a practical town centre, and chalk downland rising almost immediately to the east.

The great reason to come is the coast. From town you can walk up to [Seaford Head](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/seaford/seaford_head) for the classic view of the [Seven Sisters](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/eastbourne/seven_sisters), then continue to [Cuckmere Haven](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/seaford/cuckmere_haven) where the river winds through the valley to the sea. It is one of the cleanest chalk-and-sea landscapes in southern England.

Seaford town itself is modest: a seafront promenade, a [Martello tower museum](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/seaford/seaford_museum), a few pubs and cafes, and enough railway convenience to make it an easy day trip. West of town, [Tide Mills](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/seaford/tide_mills) adds a stranger, ruined note between Seaford and Newhaven.

Use Seaford as a walking base rather than a bucket-and-spade resort. The beach is wide and open, but the real reward is to climb out of town, look back over the Channel, and then drop toward the Cuckmere valley.
