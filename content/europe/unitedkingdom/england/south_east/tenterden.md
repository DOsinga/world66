Tenterden is one of the more convincing small towns in the Kentish Weald: broad High Street, weatherboard houses, brick Georgian fronts, antique shops, and countryside that still feels close at hand. It is not a grand cathedral city or a seaside resort, which is part of the appeal. The day here is slower and more local.

The headline draw is the [Kent and East Sussex Railway](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/tenterden/kent_and_east_sussex_railway), a preserved steam line that runs from Tenterden Town station through the Rother Valley toward Bodiam. That alone gives the town a proper excursion. Add a tasting or vineyard walk at [Chapel Down](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/tenterden/chapel_down), and Tenterden starts to feel like a compact version of modern Kent: heritage on one side, English wine on the other.

[Smallhythe Place](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/tenterden/smallhythe_place), just outside town, gives the visit a more personal historical note. It was once a shipbuilding settlement on a vanished waterway, then the home of the actress Ellen Terry. Tenterden is best as a relaxed day from elsewhere in Kent or East Sussex rather than a destination to build a whole trip around.
