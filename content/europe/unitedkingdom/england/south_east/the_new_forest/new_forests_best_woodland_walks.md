None of these routes will test your legs — the New Forest is famously flat, and that's exactly the point. The Northern Commons, on the quiet edge of the park near Ringwood, give you open heath and pony-grazed pasture with room to spread out. Bolderwood does the opposite: proper old woodland, a public deer-viewing platform, and a good chance of seeing fallow deer without leaving the car park behind for long. The short stroll to the Knightwood Oak, a 500-year-old tree with a 7-metre girth known as the Queen of the Forest, is about as low-effort as a woodland walk gets, and still worth doing.

Blashford Lakes swaps trees for water — a set of old gravel pits, now a serious birdwatching reserve with level paths and hides for winter migrants — while the walk out to the Rufus Stone, the iron marker deep in the woods near Brook where King William II died in 1100, pairs an easy path with a genuinely strange piece of history. Together they cover the Forest's range: heath, ancient woodland, wetland, and a story you won't get anywhere else.
