Royal Tunbridge Wells is a Georgian spa town in the Weald of Kent, surrounded by some of the most scenic countryside in south-east England. The town earned its "Royal" prefix from Edward VII and has been associated with genteel respectability ever since, famously lending its name to the letter-writing "Disgusted of Tunbridge Wells."

[The Pantiles](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/tunbridge_wells/the_pantiles) is the town's architectural centrepiece, a colonnaded walkway dating from the seventeenth century that was the fashionable promenade for visitors taking the waters at the [chalybeate spring](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/tunbridge_wells/chalybeate_spring). The spring still flows and you can taste the iron-rich water for free. The Pantiles is now lined with independent shops, restaurants and a weekly farmers' market.

The town sits at the edge of the High Weald, an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty. The rocks on [Tunbridge Wells Common](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/tunbridge_wells/tunbridge_wells_common), dramatic sandstone outcrops in the middle of the town, are popular with climbers and walkers. [High Rocks](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/tunbridge_wells/high_rocks), just outside town, is the bigger and more serious climbing venue, and the [Spa Valley Railway](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/tunbridge_wells/spa_valley_railway) runs heritage steam trains out into the Wealden countryside. [Penshurst Place](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/tunbridge_wells/penshurst_place), six miles north-west, makes the best day trip for its medieval hall and walled garden.

Tunbridge Wells is about 50 minutes by train from London Charing Cross.
