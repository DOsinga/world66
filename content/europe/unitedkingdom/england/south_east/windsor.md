Windsor exists because of its castle, and the castle is reason enough to come. [Windsor Castle](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/windsor/windsor_castle) is the oldest and largest occupied castle in the world, a royal residence for nearly a thousand years, and it dominates the town from every angle. The State Apartments, [St George's Chapel](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/windsor/st_georges_chapel) (where Henry VIII is buried alongside Charles I and Jane Seymour), and the changing of the guard are all worth your time.

The town itself is pleasant if unremarkable — a compact centre of Georgian and Victorian buildings sloping down from the castle to the Thames. The [Long Walk](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/windsor/long_walk) stretches three miles south from the castle through [Windsor Great Park](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/windsor/windsor_great_park), a vast royal parkland that is open to the public and perfect for a long walk or a run. Look out for the [Crooked House](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/windsor/crooked_house) on the High Street and the absurd shortness of [Queen Charlotte Street](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/windsor/queen_charlotte_street) beside it.

Across the [bridge](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/windsor/windsor_bridge) is Eton, home to the famous [school](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/windsor/eton_college). The high street is worth a stroll — all old bookshops and tailors. Further afield, [Runnymede](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/windsor/runnymede) is where King John sealed the Magna Carta in 1215, and [Legoland Windsor](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_east/windsor/legoland_windsor) is the family attraction on the south-west edge of town. Windsor is an easy day trip from [London](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/london), about 50 minutes by train from Paddington via Slough.
