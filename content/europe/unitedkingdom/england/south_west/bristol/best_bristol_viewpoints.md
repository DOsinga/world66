Bristol is built on a gorge and a set of hills, so the good views are never far, and none of them need a ticket to a tower block. Brunel's Clifton Suspension Bridge is the obvious one — 75 metres of nothing between you and the Avon below — but the Clifton Observatory does it with more theatre, projecting a live, silent image of the same gorge onto a table inside a darkened room. From Cabot Tower on Brandon Hill you get the whole city at once, harbour to hills, with Wales visible on a clear day.

The quieter versions are just as good. Ashton Court's deer park and Blaise Castle's folly-topped woodland both look back at Bristol from a comfortable distance, and Leigh Woods, across the gorge from Clifton, gives you the view most visitors never think to flip around and take. Together they cover the same city from every angle — height, distance, and the theatrical trick of the camera obscura — which is more interesting than picking just one.
