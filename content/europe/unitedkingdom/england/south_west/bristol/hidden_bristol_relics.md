Central Bristol hides a surprising amount if you slow down enough to look. Four squat brass pedestals outside the Corn Exchange, worn nearly smooth by centuries of use, mark the spot where merchants once had to conclude deals in cash on the spot — supposedly the origin of the phrase "paying on the nail." A few streets over, a network of sandstone caves burrows under Redcliffe, quarried for glassmaking sand in the medieval period and later pressed into service as an air-raid shelter, with a smugglers' legend attached that nobody can quite confirm. Up on the Downs, a windmill-turned-observatory still projects a live, silent image of the Avon Gorge onto a table inside a darkened room, using mirrors and lenses installed in 1828.

The oddities aren't all pretty. A blunt concrete shot tower interrupts the harbour's restored warehouses like a fist through a photograph, a wartime anti-aircraft battery still stands on the hill above Stoke Park, and the shell of a medieval church stands roofless in Castle Park, gutted in the Blitz and left standing as a memorial rather than rebuilt. None of it needs a ticket or a gift shop — just a willingness to look twice at a doorway, a hillside, or a patch of grass that turns out to have a story attached.
