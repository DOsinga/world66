St Mary Redcliffe stands on the south bank of the Avon, its 292-foot spire visible for miles. It is one of the finest Gothic churches in England — not a cathedral, but built at cathedral scale by the merchants of Bristol who could afford it in the 14th and 15th centuries. The nave and chancel are exceptional: the vaulting is intricate, the windows generous, and the proportions make the space feel both grand and intimate.

The church has strong connections to John Cabot, who prayed here before setting out for North America in 1497. It also has a famous chaotic pendulum installed in the mid-20th century, whose irregular swinging has attracted visitors and physicists alike. The churchyard and surrounding area retain some of the atmosphere of medieval Redcliffe; the tram rail embedded in the churchyard wall (blown there during a Second World War air raid) is worth finding. The church is free to visit.
