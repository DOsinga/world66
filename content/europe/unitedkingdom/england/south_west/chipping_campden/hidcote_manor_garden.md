Hidcote Manor Garden was created from 1907 onwards by Lawrence Johnston, an American-born horticulturalist who bought the bare hilltop farm and spent decades planting one of the most innovative gardens in England. Johnston divided the garden into a series of enclosed "rooms", each with its own character and planting scheme, separated by high hedges of yew, holly, and hornbeam. The idea of the garden as a sequence of outdoor rooms became enormously influential on 20th-century garden design.

The planting is exceptional — Johnston was an obsessive plant collector who brought back specimens from expeditions to South Africa, China, and the Alps — and the structural bones of the garden hold up well even in winter. In summer the colour combinations in the individual rooms are studied and intense. The garden is at its best in June and July.

Hidcote is National Trust and one of the most visited gardens in England; arrive early or out of season. Kiftsgate Court Garden, immediately adjacent and separately run, contains the original Kiftsgate rose — the largest rose in England — and is worth combining with Hidcote in a single visit.
