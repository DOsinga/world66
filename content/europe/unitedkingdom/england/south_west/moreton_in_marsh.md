Moreton-in-Marsh is a long, low market town strung along the Roman Fosse Way — the A429 runs straight through the centre on the same line it has followed for two thousand years. It is not the most picturesque of the Cotswold towns, but it has one significant advantage: a direct rail connection to London Paddington, making it the most accessible gateway to the northern Cotswolds for visitors without a car.

The Tuesday market is one of the largest in the Cotswolds and worth seeing: a mix of local produce, household goods, and craft stalls spreading along the High Street. The Redesdale Market Hall (1887) at the centre of the street is the architectural focal point.

From Moreton, a car or bicycle opens up the surrounding country quickly. Chipping Campden is 8 miles north, Stow-on-the-Wold is 4 miles south, and Batsford Arboretum — an exceptional tree collection with a garden centre — is just 2 miles west. The Four Shires Stone, a mile or so from town, is the point where Worcestershire, Warwickshire, Gloucestershire, and the former Oxfordshire met.

The town itself is pleasant without being remarkable — good pubs, the usual run of Cotswold shops, and a relaxed pace. It works well as a day-trip destination in its own right or as a staging point for exploring deeper into the region.
