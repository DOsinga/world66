Batsford Arboretum was planted from 1886 by Algernon Freeman-Mitford (grandfather of the Mitford sisters) on the slopes above the Evenlode valley near Moreton-in-Marsh. Freeman-Mitford had served as secretary at the British legation in Japan and returned fascinated by Japanese garden design; the arboretum reflects this with Japanese maples, bamboo groves, and a large bronze Buddha brought back from Tokyo.

The collection covers 55 acres and contains around 1,500 labelled trees, with particular strength in Japanese maples, magnolias, and flowering cherries. The autumn colour is exceptional, particularly in October when the maples turn. The spring display of cherry blossom and magnolias is equally good.

The arboretum is privately owned and maintained to a high standard. It is less crowded than Westonbirt and the hillside setting, with views across the Evenlode valley to the surrounding countryside, gives it a different character from the more formal national arboretum. A garden centre and falconry centre are attached to the estate.
