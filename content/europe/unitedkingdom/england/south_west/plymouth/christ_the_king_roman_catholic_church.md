Christ The King Roman Catholic Church is a postwar city-centre church opened in 1962 and built in sandstone brick. Its main interest is architectural: it was the last work of Sir Giles Gilbert Scott, whose career also produced Liverpool Cathedral, Battersea Power Station, Waterloo Bridge, and the red telephone box.

The church is a worthwhile detour if you enjoy modern ecclesiastical architecture or Scott's work. Visit respectfully, and avoid service times if you only want to look at the building.
