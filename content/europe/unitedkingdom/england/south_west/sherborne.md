Sherborne is one of Dorset's handsomest inland towns, built from warm ochre hamstone on the edge of the Blackmore Vale. It has the scale of a small market town, but the architectural weight of somewhere that mattered for a long time.

The centre is anchored by [Sherborne Abbey](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/bristol_plymouth_and_the_south_west/dorset/sherborne/sherborne_abbey), whose fan vaulting would be enough reason to stop. A little east, the ruins of [Sherborne Old Castle](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/bristol_plymouth_and_the_south_west/dorset/sherborne/sherborne_old_castle) and the later [Sherborne Castle](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/bristol_plymouth_and_the_south_west/dorset/sherborne/sherborne_castle) tell a compact story of bishops, Sir Walter Raleigh, and the Civil War.

Sherborne is not loud or fashionable. Its pleasure is in a slow day: abbey, almshouse, old streets, castle gardens, and a decent train connection that makes it easy to reach from Salisbury, Yeovil, or Exeter.
