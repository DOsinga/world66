Watchet is a small harbour town on the Somerset coast at the eastern edge of Exmoor. Its ancient [harbour](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_west/watchet/harbour), now supplemented by a modern marina, has been in use since Saxon times. The town is said to be where Samuel Taylor Coleridge was inspired to write "The Rime of the Ancient Mariner" — a [statue of the mariner](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_west/watchet/ancient_mariner_statue) stands on the esplanade.

The harbour area is the main draw, with colourful houses along the quay and good views across the Bristol Channel to the Welsh coast. The [Market House Museum](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_west/watchet/market_house_museum) on the quay and the [Boat Museum](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_west/watchet/boat_museum) near the station are small but worthwhile. Watchet is also a stop on the [West Somerset Railway](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_west/watchet/west_somerset_railway), a heritage steam line that runs to Minehead and Bishops Lydeard. For a short walk, the Saxon-era cliff fort at [Daw's Castle](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/south_west/watchet/daws_castle) is a kilometre west along the coast path.
