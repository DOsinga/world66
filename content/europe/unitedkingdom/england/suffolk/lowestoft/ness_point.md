Ness Point, also called Lowestoft Ness, holds the distinction of being the most easterly point in England, Great Britain, and the United Kingdom. It is a headland on the North Sea coast, marked with a compass rose pavement, a large marker post, and a wind turbine that has become something of a local landmark.

There is nothing grand about the site itself — it is an industrial edge of the harbour rather than a dramatic clifftop — but the symbolic weight of standing at the furthest eastern tip of Britain gives the visit its appeal. The walk along the clifftop from the town centre takes about twenty minutes, passing the South Pier and the outer harbour before reaching the point. On a clear day you can see the wind farms on the horizon.
