story: >
  Sudbury Priory was founded around 1114 as a cell of the Abbey of Westminster, making it one of the oldest religious houses in Suffolk. It was dissolved by Henry VIII in 1538 during the general dissolution of the monasteries, but substantial sections of the medieval fabric survived by being converted to domestic use — the same pragmatic recycling of monastic buildings that preserved so many similar structures elsewhere in England.
---

The remains of Sudbury's Dominican priory stand in a quiet corner of the town, incorporated into later buildings. Enough medieval fabric survives to give a sense of the original scale — a sizeable establishment that reflected Sudbury's importance as a wool-trade centre in the fourteenth century.

The site is not a formal visitor attraction — the buildings are in private use — but the exterior is visible and worth a look for those interested in medieval architecture. Combined with St Gregory's Church, it gives a sense of the substantial religious life that the medieval town supported.
