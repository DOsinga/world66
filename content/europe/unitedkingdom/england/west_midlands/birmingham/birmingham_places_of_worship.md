Birmingham's reputation runs to canals, factories, and red brick, so the Dhamma Talaka Pagoda comes as a genuine surprise: a white-and-gold Burmese Buddhist stupa rising beside Edgbaston Reservoir, built by monks rather than industrialists. It sits a few miles from St Chad's, Augustus Pugin's Gothic Revival Catholic cathedral and one of the first raised in England since the Reformation, and from St Philip's, a Baroque Anglican cathedral whose windows are the work of Edward Burne-Jones.

Keep going and the map only widens: a working mosque serving one of Britain's largest Muslim communities, a Sikh gurdwara in the Jewellery Quarter, a Hindu temple tucked into an ordinary residential street. None of these are museum pieces — they are active congregations, so visit as a guest rather than a tourist — but together they say more about who actually lives in Birmingham today than any factory tour does.
