The Ancient High House is Stafford's headline sight and one of the largest surviving timber-framed town houses in England. Built in the late 16th century, it rises above Greengate Street in a stack of black-and-white gables, with a slightly improbable height that makes the modern shops around it look temporary.

Inside, the building works as a small local-history museum. The rooms cover Stafford life from the Civil War to the Edwardian period, and the house itself is the main exhibit: creaking floors, exposed beams, narrow staircases, and the sense that a whole slice of Elizabethan town life has been lifted above the pavement.
