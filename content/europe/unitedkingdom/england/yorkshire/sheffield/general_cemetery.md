Sheffield General Cemetery is one of the city's most atmospheric green spaces: part burial ground, part urban woodland, part open-air history book. Its monuments, chapels, catacombs, and winding paths date from the Victorian period, when Sheffield's industrial growth brought wealth, reformers, dissenters, and civic ambition.

It is a good counterpoint to the more obvious city-centre sights. You can walk here from the Ecclesall Road area and spend half an hour among weathered stone, mature trees, and views back toward the city. The cemetery rewards slow wandering rather than checklist sightseeing.
