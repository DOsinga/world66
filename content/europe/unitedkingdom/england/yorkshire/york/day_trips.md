York's position in the Vale of York makes it a good base for day trips in several directions. Most destinations are within an hour by car, and many are reachable by rail or bus.

To the west, [Harrogate](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/yorkshire/harrogate) is a prosperous Victorian spa town about 20 miles away — a pleasant 45-minute train journey — with the RHS garden at Harlow Carr, the original Bettys tea room, and the still-working Turkish Baths.

North of the city, [Helmsley](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/yorkshire/helmsley) is a handsome market town with a Norman castle, good food shops, and the ruins of Rievaulx Abbey a few miles west. It sits on the southern edge of the [North York Moors](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/yorkshire/north_york_moors), the national park whose heather plateaus turn purple in late summer and whose steam-hauled North Yorkshire Moors Railway runs from Pickering to the coast.

That coast is best reached at [Whitby](/europe/unitedkingdom/england/yorkshire/whitby), 45 miles northeast, where a working fishing harbour sits beneath the clifftop ruins of Whitby Abbey and the Dracula connections that draw Bram Stoker pilgrims and the twice-yearly Goth Weekend.

Closer to hand, Castle Howard — one of England's grandest baroque houses, familiar from Brideshead Revisited — lies just 25 kilometres northeast and easily fills a day on its own.
