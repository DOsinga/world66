Douglas is the capital of the Isle of Man and home to about a third of the island's population. It wraps around a wide bay on the east coast and is where most visitors arrive, either by ferry or from the airport a few miles to the south.

The town has a long Victorian promenade lined with guesthouses and hotels, and the [horse-drawn trams](/europe/unitedkingdom/manisleof/douglas/douglas_promenade) that run along it in summer have been operating since 1876. The [Manx Museum](/europe/unitedkingdom/manisleof/douglas/manx_museum) on Kingswood Grove is the best place to get an overview of the island's history, from its Norse settlers to the internment camps of World War II. The [Gaiety Theatre](/europe/unitedkingdom/manisleof/douglas/gaiety_theatre), designed by Frank Matcham, is one of the finest surviving Victorian theatres in the British Isles. Out in the bay, the [Tower of Refuge](/europe/unitedkingdom/manisleof/douglas/tower_of_refuge) sits on its reef like a miniature castle.

Douglas is the starting point for the [Isle of Man TT course](/europe/unitedkingdom/manisleof/douglas/tt_course) and the hub for the island's heritage railways — the steam railway south to Port Erin and the electric railway north to Ramsey both depart from [Douglas Railway Station](/europe/unitedkingdom/manisleof/douglas/douglas_railway_station). The [Tynwald](/europe/unitedkingdom/manisleof/douglas/tynwald), the world's oldest continuous parliament, is a short walk from the promenade.
