The Manx Museum in Douglas is the national museum of the Isle of Man and the best place to understand the island's history before exploring further. The collection covers Norse settlement (the island has one of the highest concentrations of Viking-age artefacts in the British Isles), the Manx language, the TT races, and the island's Celtic Christian heritage, including an extraordinary collection of carved stone crosses.

The Norse gallery is particularly strong — the Isle of Man was a significant Viking stronghold in the 9th and 10th centuries, and the island's parliament, Tynwald, derives its name from the Old Norse thing-völlr (assembly field). The museum is free to enter and is well organised for a half-day visit.
