The Tower of Refuge stands on a partially submerged reef in the middle of Douglas Bay — a small, turreted stone castle that rises dramatically from the sea at high tide. It was built in 1832 as a shelter for sailors shipwrecked on the notorious Conister Rock, which has claimed many vessels over the centuries. From the promenade it looks like a fairy-tale castle, and at high tide when only the turrets show above water, the effect is particularly striking.

The tower is not accessible to visitors, but it's impossible to miss from the promenade, especially at high tide. On a clear day it makes a compelling focal point for the bay, and photographers are drawn to it in the golden hour light.
