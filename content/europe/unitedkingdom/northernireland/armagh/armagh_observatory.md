The Armagh Observatory was founded in 1790 by Archbishop Richard Robinson as part of his ambition to transform Armagh into a great intellectual centre. It has been in continuous operation ever since — 235 years of astronomical observation from the same hill — making it one of the longest-running observatories in the world still producing active research.

The grounds, known as the Astropark, are open to the public and contain scale models of the solar system, stone sundials, and sculptures exploring astronomical themes. The adjacent Planetarium hosts regular public shows covering the night sky, space exploration, and astronomical history. The combination of working research facility and public outreach is unusual and works well.

The observatory archive holds weather records going back to 1795, among the longest climate records in Ireland. Guided tours of the historic dome and telescope are available; check the website for schedule and booking, as they fill quickly. The grounds are open daily without charge; the Planetarium shows have admission fees.
