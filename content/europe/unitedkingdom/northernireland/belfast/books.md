title: Books

**Say Nothing** by Patrick Radden Keefe — the best book written about the Troubles, a narrative non-fiction account centred on the murder of Jean McConville in 1972 and its long aftermath. Gripping, balanced, and deeply researched, it gives a clearer picture of what the conflict actually felt like for those who lived through it than any academic history.

**Eureka Street** by Robert McLiam Wilson — the funniest and most humane novel set in Belfast, written in 1996 just as the first ceasefire took hold. Two friends navigate the absurdities of a city on the edge of peace, and Wilson writes with extraordinary sympathy and wit. The best introduction to Belfast through fiction.

**The Boy in the Striped Pyjamas** is not set in Belfast, but **Across the Barricades** by Joan Lingard — set in the Falls and Shankill during the Troubles — is worth reading before a trip to understand the sectarian geography of the city. Originally written for young adults, it remains sharp and moving.
