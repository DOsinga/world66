Newry straddles the border between Northern Ireland and the Republic in all but name — the frontier is just a few miles south, and the town has always been a crossing point. It sits in the gap between the [Mourne Mountains](/europe/unitedkingdom/northernireland/newry/mourne_mountains) and [Slieve Gullion](/europe/unitedkingdom/northernireland/newry/slieve_gullion), with the [Newry Canal](/europe/unitedkingdom/northernireland/newry/newry_canal) (the oldest summit-level canal in the British Isles) running through the centre.

The town has reinvented itself since the Troubles. The Buttercrane and Quays shopping centres draw cross-border shoppers, but the older streets around Hill Street and the cathedral quarter have more character. [Newry Cathedral](/europe/unitedkingdom/northernireland/newry/newry_cathedral), built in 1829, was the first Catholic cathedral built in Ireland after Catholic Emancipation. The [Town Hall](/europe/unitedkingdom/northernireland/newry/newry_town_hall), a striking granite building on a bridge over the canal, is worth a look, and [Bagenal's Castle](/europe/unitedkingdom/northernireland/newry/bagenals_castle) — a Tudor tower house that was for centuries hidden inside a later bakery — now houses the town museum.

For walkers, the Mournes are the main draw — Slieve Donard, the highest peak in Northern Ireland, is less than half an hour's drive east.
