The Ulster American Folk Park, a few kilometres north of Omagh on the road to Gortin, is the most compelling heritage attraction in County Tyrone. It tells the story of Irish emigration to America — one of the defining movements of the 19th century — through a landscape of reconstructed buildings that visitors walk through in sequence.

The journey begins with the old world: thatched farmhouses, a forge, a schoolhouse, and a weaver's cottage representing rural Ulster before the emigrant ships. A full-scale replica of an 18th-century ship forms the centrepiece, and crossing through it brings you to the new world: log cabins, a Pennsylvania farmstead, and the frontier America where generations of Irish emigrants remade their lives. The actors in period costume do not overperform; the buildings themselves do the work.

The park is particularly strong on the emotional reality of emigration — the poverty that drove people out, the uncertainty of the crossing, and the strangeness of arrival. Budget at least two hours; the grounds are larger than they look on the map.
