Britain does secondhand and independent bookshops better than almost anywhere, and the best of them are destinations in their own right rather than places you just happen to pass. This list runs from a former Victorian railway station stacked to the rafters with second-hand stock, to a bookshop at the end of a Highland dirt track, to a Marylebone gallery arranged so you can browse a country's shelf and its guidebooks in one place.

What they share is a refusal to be generic: a personalised recommendation service over champagne, a hydraulic-jacked underground cavern, a whole town built around the trade. None of these are chain stores, and all of them reward a slow, unhurried browse rather than a quick in-and-out.
