The UK's oldest pubs tend to come with a claim attached — oldest license, oldest cellar, oldest continuously poured pint — and the claims rarely stand up to scrutiny, since almost every fire, rebuild, and boundary change in British history has given some pub or other an excuse to call itself the original. What they do have, reliably, is centuries of accumulated strangeness: a pub built into thousand-year-old sandstone caves, one so small it fits a handful of drinkers at a time, another with a presidential non-inhaling anecdote attached to a wall plaque.

This list favours pubs with a genuinely odd physical setting or story over ones that are merely old — caves, cellars, covered passageways, and the kind of low doorways that catch out anyone over six feet.
