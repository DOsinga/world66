Ayr is a market town on the Firth of Clyde in South Ayrshire, about 50 kilometres south of Glasgow. It is the largest town in Ayrshire and has a long seafront promenade backed by a sandy beach. The town's greatest claim on the traveller's attention is its connection to Robert Burns, Scotland's national poet, who was born in the nearby village of Alloway in 1759 — now effectively part of greater Ayr.

The Burns Heritage Park on the town's southern fringe centres on Burns Cottage, the small whitewashed birthplace of the poet, along with the Tam o' Shanter Experience visitor centre, the Burns Memorial, and the famous Brig o' Doon — the bridge across which Tam o' Shanter rode to escape the witches in Burns's poem of that name. For Burns fans, this is the essential pilgrimage site.

A short drive north of Ayr, Culzean Castle is one of Scotland's finest country houses: an 18th-century Robert Adam design set dramatically on a clifftop above the Firth, surrounded by a large country park run by the National Trust for Scotland. Membership of the Trust gives free entry to all their properties, which can make financial sense even on a short Scottish trip.
