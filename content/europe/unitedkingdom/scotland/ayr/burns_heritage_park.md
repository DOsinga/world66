The Burns National Heritage Park in Alloway brings together the key sites associated with Robert Burns in a single visit: Burns Cottage and the adjoining museum, the ruined Auld Kirk of Alloway (where the witches dance in "Tam o' Shanter"), the Brig o' Doon (the bridge Tam o' Shanter crosses to escape them), the Burns Monument, and the Tam o' Shanter Experience visitor centre. It is possible to walk between all of them in an afternoon.

The Brig o' Doon itself — a 15th-century humpback bridge over the River Doon — is well-preserved and still carries foot traffic. The setting is pleasant: the bridge over the river, a formal garden on one side, the ruined church on the other. The Burns Monument, a neoclassical rotunda on a hill above the bridge, has a small museum inside and gives views across the Ayrshire countryside. The whole area feels like a genuine piece of landscape imbued with literary history rather than a manufactured heritage attraction.
