  Greenan Castle was built in the early 17th century by John Kennedy of Baltersan, a branch of the Kennedy family who had dominated South Ayrshire for centuries. It was abandoned within a few decades — possibly after a dispute over ownership — and was already a ruin by the 18th century. Local legend holds that it was burned by Cromwell's troops, though there is no firm historical evidence for this. The tower now tilts visibly towards the cliff edge, and the local authority periodically assesses whether it is safe to approach.

Greenan Castle is a ruined 17th-century tower house perched on a headland three miles south of Ayr centre, accessible on foot along the beach. The castle is dramatically placed right on the cliff edge — parts of the masonry have already fallen into the sea, and what remains leans at a discernible angle. The approach walk along Ayr Beach is pleasant, and the views from the headland, on a clear day, extend south to Ailsa Craig and Kintyre.

The castle is not managed or maintained by any heritage body — it is simply a ruin in a dramatic location, freely accessible. Take care near the cliff edge. The walk from the town centre takes about an hour each way along the beach; by road it is three miles south on the A719.
