The Town House on Banff's Low Street is the civic centrepiece of the old burgh: a tall, harled Georgian building dating from 1796, topped with a steeple containing one of the oldest civic bells in Scotland. It served as council chambers, courtroom, and jail in a single compact package, and the basement still preserves cell doors from its time as a tolbooth.

Today the building houses a council-run heritage and registration office. The exterior is the main draw — it anchors a tight cluster of 18th-century townhouses around Plainstones, the cobbled square in front, which gives Banff its claim to be one of the finest small Georgian towns in Scotland.
