Not much of Kindrochit is left: just the ground floor of a tower, about 19 by 13 metres, on the bank of the Clunie Water in the village centre. But it was once an important place. King Robert II used it as a hunting seat and issued charters here most years between 1371 and 1388, and in 1390 Malcolm Drummond was licensed to build a new tower on the site.

The ruins were restored in 2013–14 and are free to visit at any time. The name means "bridge end", and the castle guarded the crossing that the whole village grew up around.
