---
title: When to Go
type: section
---

The Cairngorms are a year-round destination, though each season has its demands. Summer (June to August) is the classic walking and wildlife season: long daylight hours (up to 18 hours in June), mild temperatures on the plateau, and the best chance of seeing ospreys, dotterel, and ptarmigan. July and August are busiest, particularly in Aviemore and on the most popular paths.

Spring (April to May) is quieter, with calving reindeer, capercaillie display season in the pinewoods, and the last snow on the high tops. Autumn (September to October) brings superb colour to the forests and moorland, the deer rut on the open hillside, and noticeably fewer visitors. The light in September is extraordinary — long golden evenings with the heather still in purple bloom.

Winter offers skiing and snowboarding on Cairn Gorm when the snow lies, and a stark, demanding landscape on the high plateau. The Cairngorms can be genuinely arctic in January — temperatures below -20°C have been recorded on the summit plateau — and winter hill walking here demands full mountaineering competence. The lower forest walks are fine in any weather.
