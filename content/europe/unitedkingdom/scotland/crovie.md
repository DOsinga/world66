Crovie (pronounced "Crivie") is a single row of cottages wedged between cliff and sea on the Moray Firth coast of Aberdeenshire. Once a busy fishing village, it is now almost entirely holiday homes — but the setting is extraordinary and the peace absolute.

There is no road into the village itself; you park at one end and walk. The cottages face directly onto the shore, close enough to feel the spray in a storm. In summer the bay produces spectacular sunsets; in winter the Northern Lights are sometimes visible. Dolphins are regularly seen offshore, and you may spot minke whales from a boat.

Just east of the village, [Troup Head](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/crovie/troup_head) is an RSPB reserve with one of mainland Britain's few gannet colonies, along with puffins, guillemots, razorbills, and cormorants. The nearby village of [Gardenstown](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/crovie/gardenstown), connected to Crovie by a cliff path, has a small harbour and a pub. A little further east lies [Pennan](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/crovie/pennan), Crovie's near-twin and famous as the village from the 1983 film *Local Hero*. On the cliff above Gardenstown, the ruined kirkyard of [St John's Church, Gamrie](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/crovie/st_johns_church_gamrie) gives a long view across Gamrie Bay.
