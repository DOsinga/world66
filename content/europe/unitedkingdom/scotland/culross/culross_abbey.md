story: "Culross Abbey was founded in 1217 by Malcolm, Earl of Fife, on a site associated with Saint Mungo (Kentigern), the patron saint of Glasgow. According to legend, Mungo was born here in the 6th century and raised by Saint Serf, who educated him in the monastery that preceded the abbey. The story has Mungo performing his first miracle in Culross — restoring a robin to life after other boys killed it to blame the act on him. The four symbols of Glasgow's coat of arms (a robin, a fish, a bell, and a tree) all derive from miracles attributed to Mungo in stories connected to this area."
---

The Cistercian abbey founded at Culross in 1217 is now largely in ruins, but the surviving choir and tower of the medieval church are still in use as the parish church of Culross — a continuity of worship on this site spanning eight centuries. The ruins of the nave and cloister buildings are preserved in the hillside above the village and are freely accessible.

The walk up from the village through the churchyard to the abbey takes about ten minutes and rewards with good views over the Firth of Forth and the Ochil Hills beyond. The parish church itself (the former choir of the medieval building) contains several fine carved medieval and Renaissance monuments, including the alabaster tomb of Sir George Bruce — builder of Culross Palace — and his family. Allow time to look at the older gravestones in the churchyard, many of them with elaborate Scots Renaissance carving.
