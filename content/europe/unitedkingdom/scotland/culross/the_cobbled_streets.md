The cobbled streets of Culross are among the best-preserved examples of a pre-industrial Scottish town layout in existence. Unlike many 'heritage' villages where the historic core is a small preserved island surrounded by modern development, Culross is almost entirely intact — the whitewashed houses, the crow-stepped gables, the carved doorways, and the irregular roof lines are genuine 16th and 17th century buildings, not reconstructions.

The most interesting street is the Back Causeway, which runs up the hill behind the Town House. Its distinctive feature is the row of raised central stones — called the crown of the causeway — that were reserved for the town's elite while commoners walked in the lower channels on either side. This is not a modern legend: the right to walk the crown was legally defined and enforced. The street makes for one of the most interesting short walks in Fife, passing several well-preserved merchant's houses with original carved stonework and inscribed lintels.
