The capital of Scotland, Edinburgh is one of the world's great cities — a compact, walkable capital built on volcanic rock and Georgian ambition, where the medieval Old Town and the planned New Town face each other across a valley that was once a loch. Few cities pack so much history, architecture, and sheer drama into such a small space.

The [Royal Mile](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/edinburgh/theroyalmile) runs from [Edinburgh Castle](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/edinburgh/edinburghcastle) at the top to the [Palace of Holyroodhouse](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/edinburgh/palace_holyroodhouse) at the bottom — the whole spine of the Old Town in a single kilometre. Off either side, closes and wynds plunge into the tenement blocks that once housed the entire city, rich and poor, in the same stairwells. The [Scottish Parliament](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/edinburgh/scottish_parliament) sits at the bottom of the Mile, its extraordinary Catalan-designed building facing Holyrood. [Greyfriars Kirkyard](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/edinburgh/greyfriars) is the Old Town's best half-hour: ancient gravestones, the Covenanters' Prison, the tomb of Greyfriars Bobby.

[Holyrood Park](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/edinburgh/holyroodpark) with its Arthur's Seat volcano is one of the great city parks in Europe, and [Calton Hill](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/edinburgh/calton_hill) to the east gives the best panoramic view of the whole city without much effort. [Princes Street Gardens](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/edinburgh/princes_street_gardens) in the valley below the castle are Edinburgh's drawing room — open, green and free. The [National Museum of Scotland](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/edinburgh/national_museum) on Chambers Street is free and among the best national museums in Britain; the [National Gallery](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/edinburgh/national_gallery) on the Mound is equally free and equally excellent.

The New Town — [Stockbridge](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/edinburgh/stockbridge), George Street, the Georgian terraces of the northern slopes — is Edinburgh at its most elegant. Leith, the former port to the north, is Edinburgh at its most interesting to eat in.

In August, the city transforms for the **Festivals** — the Fringe, the International Festival, the Book Festival, the Military Tattoo — and becomes for three weeks the most culturally concentrated place on earth.
