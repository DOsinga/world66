The Anatomical Museum at the University of Edinburgh holds historic teaching collections of anatomical specimens, life masks, and death masks. Its best-known exhibit is the skeleton of William Burke, one of the Burke and Hare murderers whose crimes were tied to Edinburgh's 19th-century anatomy trade.

The museum is still part of a working teaching area, so opening is limited and visits usually need to be booked ahead. It is most worthwhile for visitors with an interest in medical history or Edinburgh's darker scientific past.
