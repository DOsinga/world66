*Europe > United Kingdom > Scotland > Falkirk*

Falkirk sits between Edinburgh and Glasgow in Scotland's central belt, a town that punches well above its weight when it comes to engineering spectacles. It is best known for the [Falkirk Wheel](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/falkirk/falkirk_wheel), a rotating boat lift that remains the only one of its kind in the world, and [The Kelpies](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/falkirk/the_kelpies), two enormous steel horse heads that have become one of Scotland's most recognisable landmarks since their installation in 2013.

The town has a long history tied to Scotland's industrial and military past. The Antonine Wall, Rome's northernmost frontier, passed through this area — and two pivotal battles of Scottish independence were fought on Falkirk's fields. The later industrial revolution left its mark too: the Union Canal and the Forth and Clyde Canal converged here, and reconnecting them with the famous Wheel was one of Scotland's great engineering achievements of the early 21st century.

[Callendar House](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/falkirk/callendar_house), standing in its own park at the edge of town, is worth an afternoon. The house dates from the 14th century and holds a local history museum inside rooms furnished in period style. Further afield, the [Dunmore Pineapple](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/falkirk/dunmore_pineapple) — a folly built in the shape of a stone pineapple — is one of Scotland's more eccentric architectural jokes, sitting quietly in the countryside just north of town.

Falkirk is an easy day trip from Edinburgh, around 30 minutes by train. Most visitors come for the Wheel and the Kelpies, but there is enough history and walking to justify a full day. The town itself is a working Scottish burgh rather than a tourist destination, which gives it an unvarnished authenticity that more polished places lack.
