Most traffic along Loch Ness takes the busy A82 on the northwest shore, racing between Fort William and Inverness. Foyers sits on the quieter southeast side, reached by a single-track road that offers lovely views across the water without the lorries.

The village itself is tiny — barely a shop — but the [Falls of Foyers](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/foyers_loch_ness/falls_of_foyers) are worth the stop. A short, well-maintained path leads down through woodland to two waterfalls, the lower one dropping into a gorge. It is a safe walk with children and makes a welcome break from driving. Below the falls, the lower village sits right on the [loch shore](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/foyers_loch_ness/loch_ness).

A few kilometres north along the same road sits [Boleskine House](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/foyers_loch_ness/boleskine_house), once home to the occultist Aleister Crowley and later to Led Zeppelin's Jimmy Page — now a roofless shell after two fires. Hidden between the upper and lower villages is the [Foyers hydropower scheme](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/foyers_loch_ness/foyers_hydro), the first commercial hydroelectric station built in Britain.

If the Loch Ness monster is your main interest, the exhibition centres at Drumnadrochit are on the opposite shore. But for a peaceful stretch of the Highlands without the crowds, the southeast road through Foyers is hard to beat.
