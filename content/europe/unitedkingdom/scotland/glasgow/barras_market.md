  The Barras was founded in the 1920s by Maggie McIver, a fierce market trader from the East End who built her stalls into a covered market empire. She is commemorated by a bronze statue at the market entrance. The Barras developed its own subculture — a reputation for knockoff goods, second-hand everything, and the possibility of buying something stolen — that the city has never quite managed to shake, though the market itself is considerably more law-abiding than its legend suggests.

The Barras is Glasgow's historic flea market and street market, running every weekend (Saturday and Sunday) in the East End between the Gallowgate and London Road. It is one of the oldest markets in Scotland and the cultural heart of the East End — a place to find second-hand goods, vintage clothing, records, tools, and assorted curiosities alongside a certain amount of new merchandise of uncertain origin.

The experience of walking through the Barras on a Saturday morning — the hawkers, the banter, the accents, the improbable juxtapositions of what's for sale — is irreducibly Glaswegian and worth doing even if you are not looking to buy anything. The surrounding streets have several good cafes and Scottish breakfast places. The Barras is free to enter and runs roughly 8am to 4pm on weekends.
