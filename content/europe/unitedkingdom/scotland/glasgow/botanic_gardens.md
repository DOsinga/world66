The Glasgow Botanic Gardens sit at the top of Byres Road in the West End, a 27-acre collection of plants, glasshouses, and wide lawns that Glasgow University has maintained since the 18th century. The Kibble Palace, a vast Victorian cast-iron glasshouse donated to the city by engineer John Kibble in 1873, is the most spectacular structure — an enormous domed conservatory filled with ferns, cycads, and statuary, one of the largest surviving Victorian glasshouses in the world.

The gardens are free, open daily, and popular with students and families at any time of year. The River Kelvin flows through the lower section and can be followed on a pleasant riverside walk south toward Kelvingrove Park. In good weather the lawns fill with people from the surrounding West End streets; in bad weather, which is more common, the Kibble Palace provides warm shelter.
