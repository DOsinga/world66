  The Duke of Wellington equestrian statue outside GoMA has been wearing a traffic cone on its head since at least the 1980s, a tradition of anonymous civic irreverence that has become one of Glasgow's most recognised symbols. The council has repeatedly tried to remove the cones; the cones keep returning. In 2013, a campaign to have the cone listed as a protected cultural feature of the city attracted tens of thousands of signatures.

The Gallery of Modern Art occupies the Royal Exchange building on Royal Exchange Square, an imposing neoclassical structure originally built as a mansion in 1778, converted into an exchange, and now housing contemporary art. The collections on four floors focus on Scottish and international contemporary work, with a particular emphasis on socially engaged art and work by living artists.

The building's ground floor is airy and accessible — a good free space to shelter from the rain while looking at rotating exhibitions. The Duke of Wellington statue outside, perpetually cone-hatted, is as much a reason to visit as the art inside.
