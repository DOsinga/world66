Holmwood House is one of Glasgow's best small architecture visits, designed by Alexander "Greek" Thomson in the 1850s for paper magnate James Couper. From outside it looks calm and classical; inside, the restored colour schemes, stencilling, and carefully controlled proportions show why Thomson matters well beyond local architectural history.

It is south of the centre, so it takes a little intention to reach, but that helps keep it quieter than Glasgow's headline museums. Go if you are interested in design, domestic architecture, or the city's 19th-century confidence. Holmwood is not large, but it is precise, original, and very Glaswegian in its seriousness.
