Helmsdale is a small fishing village on the east coast of Sutherland, two hours north of [Inverness](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/inverness) by road or rail. It is a quiet place now, with a harbour, a river mouth, stone cottages and the A9 sweeping past, but its history is unusually heavy for a village of this size.

The village was laid out in the early 19th century for people pushed off inland farms during the Highland Clearances. That story is told well at [Timespan](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/helmsdale/timespan), while the [Emigrants Statue](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/helmsdale/emigrants_statue) above the river gives the same history a more emotional form. Helmsdale also had a short gold rush in 1869, and you can still pan for gold in the Kildonan burns with the proper local permits.

The best reason to stop here is the combination: a working coastal village, strong Clearance history, Iron Age sites nearby, and lonely cliff walks north towards Caithness. [Badbea](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/helmsdale/badbea) and [Ousdale Broch](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/helmsdale/ousdale_broch) are both close enough for a half-day outing by car, and together they make Helmsdale more than just a pause on the North Coast 500.
