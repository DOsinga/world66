  The Battle of Culloden lasted less than an hour on the morning of 16 April 1746. Around 1,500 Jacobite soldiers were killed, and the Highland clan system was effectively destroyed in its aftermath. The government forces lost fewer than 50 men. The battlefield is haunting even on a clear day — on a raw Scottish morning with low cloud, it is one of the most affecting places in Britain.

Culloden Moor lies just 5 kilometres east of Inverness city centre, making it one of the most accessible major battlefields in Britain. The National Trust for Scotland operates an excellent visitor centre here with detailed accounts of the battle, the Highland culture that was suppressed in its wake, and the fate of the survivors.

The moor itself is partially reconstructed to show how it looked in 1746 — trees have been cleared and a section of the old road restored. Clan headstones mark the mass graves where the Jacobite dead were buried. The spot where Bonnie Prince Charlie watched his army destroyed is marked, as is the Cumberland Stone — the rock from which the Duke of Cumberland allegedly watched the slaughter. Allow at least two hours to do the site justice.
