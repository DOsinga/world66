title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

Inverness has improved significantly as a restaurant city over the past decade. Scottish seafood and game are the strengths — the Highland larder of smoked salmon, venison, langoustines, and Orkney beef is as good as anywhere in Britain, and a handful of restaurants make the most of it.

**What to eat**

Cullen skink — a thick smoked haddock chowder — is the archetypal Highland soup and appears on many menus. Haggis, neeps, and tatties is the national dish and worth trying properly, not from a tourist trap. Fresh langoustines from the West Coast, available in season, are outstanding.

**Where to eat**

The city centre around Church Street and Academy Street has the greatest concentration of restaurants. There are good options for most price ranges, and the food quality has risen considerably.
