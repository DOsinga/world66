Islay — say "eye-la" — is the whisky island. Around 3,200 people live here, and between them they run nine or ten working distilleries, the count shifting as old names are revived and new ones open. The ones on the south coast make the heavily peated, smoky, seaweed-and-iodine malts that people either love or can't stand. Even if you don't drink, the distilleries shape the island: whitewashed warehouses on the shore, the smell of malting barley in Port Ellen, and more than 15,000 visitors squeezing in for the Fèis Ìle festival every May.

The island is mostly low, green grazing land and heather, with the hills piled up along the east coast facing [Jura](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/jura). **Port Ellen** in the south is the main ferry port and the start of the walk to the three Kildalton distilleries — [Laphroaig](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/islay/laphroaig_distillery), [Lagavulin](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/islay/lagavulin_distillery) and Ardbeg. **Bowmore**, the capital, was laid out as a planned village in the 18th century with the [Round Church](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/islay/kilarrow_round_church) at the top of Main Street and its distillery on the shore of Loch Indaal. Across the loch, **Port Charlotte** is the prettiest village, a row of white cottages named after Lady Charlotte Campbell, with the Museum of Islay Life and Bruichladdich just up the road.

Islay has much more history than its size suggests. The [Kildalton Cross](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/islay/kildalton_cross) is the finest early Christian cross in Scotland, and at [Finlaggan](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/islay/finlaggan) the Lords of the Isles ruled the western seaboard from two small islands in a freshwater loch. On the cliffs of the Oa the [American Monument](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/islay/american_monument) remembers two troopships lost off the coast in 1918.

In winter the birds take over. Tens of thousands of barnacle geese and Greenland white-fronted geese arrive from the Arctic, and [Loch Gruinart](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/islay/loch_gruinart) is the place to watch them. CalMac ferries sail from Kennacraig on Kintyre to Port Ellen and Port Askaig in about two hours; there are also flights from Glasgow. Bring a car or bike if you can — the bus runs weekdays only — and from Port Askaig a small council car ferry makes the short crossing to Feolin on Jura.
