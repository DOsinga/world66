From the 13th to the 15th century, two small islands at the north end of Loch Finlaggan were the centre of the Lordship of the Isles — the Clan Donald dynasty that ruled the Hebrides and much of the west coast almost as a kingdom of its own. It's hard to believe today: there are only low ruins, grazing sheep, and wind on the water.

A walkway leads out to Eilean Mòr, the main island, where you can trace the hall, a chapel and later grave slabs. The smaller Council Island beyond, where the Lords' council met, is usually not accessible. A small visitor centre by the loch, run by the Finlaggan Trust, has finds from the excavations and opens in summer; the island itself can be visited any time.
