The Kildalton Cross stands in the churchyard of a roofless medieval chapel at the end of the shore road beyond Ardbeg. Carved from a single slab, probably in the second half of the 8th century, it is closely related to the crosses on [Iona](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/iona) and is often called the finest surviving Celtic cross in Scotland — still standing where it was made to stand, with its bosses, serpents and Virgin and Child weathered but legible.

The site is in the care of Historic Environment Scotland and open at all times, free. It's a beautiful drive out past the three distilleries; combine it with the walk or a tasting, but keep the designated driver in mind.
