Kirkcudbright sits near the mouth of the River Dee in southwest Scotland, where the Solway coast turns quiet and green. It is pronounced roughly "kuh-KOO-bree", which is worth knowing before you ask for directions. The town still has a working harbour, painted Georgian houses, and a small-street centre that feels more lived-in than polished.

Its claim to fame is art. In the late 19th and early 20th centuries, painters linked to the Glasgow Boys and Scottish Colourists came here for the light, the coast, and the cheaper life away from Edinburgh and Glasgow. E. A. Hornel made [Broughton House](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/kirkcudbright/broughton_house) his home, and the town kept attracting galleries long after the original colony faded.

For a small town, Kirkcudbright makes a satisfying day: [Broughton House](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/kirkcudbright/broughton_house), [Kirkcudbright Galleries](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/kirkcudbright/kirkcudbright_galleries), the ruins of [MacLellan's Castle](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/kirkcudbright/maclellans_castle), the harbour, and the short walk out toward St Mary's Isle. You need a car for the wider Galloway coast, but the town itself rewards slow wandering.
