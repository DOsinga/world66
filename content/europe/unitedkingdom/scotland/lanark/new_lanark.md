New Lanark is the prime reason to come to Lanark: a cotton-mill village founded in 1785 and later made famous by Robert Owen's social reforms. The sandstone mill buildings stand in a tight bend of the River Clyde, with workers' housing, schoolrooms, machinery, and interpretation that explains why this planned industrial community mattered far beyond Scotland.

It can feel polished in parts, but the setting saves it from becoming a museum set. Step away from the visitor centre and the river, mills, and steep wooded sides of the valley do much of the work.
