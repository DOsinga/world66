St Michael's Parish Church stands beside Linlithgow Palace and shares its lochside position. The medieval church dates from the 15th century and is one of the finest examples of Scottish late Gothic architecture. It has been in continuous use as a parish church since its foundation. The 1964 aluminium crown that tops the tower — added to replace a medieval crown that collapsed in the 19th century — remains controversial among architectural conservatives, but it gives the church a distinctive modern silhouette that identifies it clearly from a distance.

The interior is spacious and well maintained. The nave, aisles, and choir are all substantial, and the medieval stonework throughout is of high quality. The church often has information available about its history. Combined with the palace next door, a visit here makes a good couple of hours in Linlithgow.
