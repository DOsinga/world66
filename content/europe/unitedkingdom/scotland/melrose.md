Of the abbey towns in the Scottish Borders, Melrose is possibly the prettiest, tucked at the foot of the Eildon Hills with the River Tweed nearby. It is a small, friendly place -- more village than town -- but it punches well above its weight for things to see.

[Melrose Abbey](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/melrose/melrose_abbey) is the main draw, founded in the twelfth century by Cistercian monks and once part of a great monastery. The ruins are atmospheric and beautifully carved, and the abbey holds a particular claim to fame: the heart of Robert the Bruce is buried here, in a lead casket discovered during excavations in 1921. The abbey sits on the [Borders Abbeys Way](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/melrose/borders_abbeys_way), a 65-mile circular trail linking four historic abbeys -- Melrose, Jedburgh, Kelso, and Dryburgh -- all built during the reign of King David I.

Nearby, [Abbotsford House](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/melrose/abbotsford) was the home of Sir Walter Scott and is well worth the short detour, while the old Roman site at [Trimontium](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/melrose/trimontium) has an exhibition covering the fort that once stood at the foot of the Eildons. Two National Trust for Scotland gardens — [Priorwood](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/melrose/priorwood_garden) and [Harmony](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/melrose/harmony_garden) — round out the attractions. Climb [Eildon Hill](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/melrose/eildon_hill) for a 360-degree view over the Tweed valley. For its size, Melrose offers a remarkably full day out.
