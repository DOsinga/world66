story: >
  Thomas of Erceldoune — Thomas the Rhymer — was a 13th-century poet and
  alleged prophet from nearby Earlston whose verse predicted the death of
  Alexander III. According to legend he met the Queen of the Fairies under the
  Eildon Tree on this spot, and lived with her in fairyland for seven years
  before being returned to the human world with the gift of true prophecy. The
  stone, erected by a local historical society in 1929, marks the supposed
  meeting place.
---

A modest engraved stone in a roadside layby on the B6359, the Rhymer's Stone is more a curiosity than a sight in its own right — but the legend behind it is one of the oldest and most evocative pieces of Borders folklore. It commemorates Thomas the Rhymer's mythical encounter with the Queen of the Fairies, a story that has worked its way into ballads, novels, and Scottish nationalist iconography across seven centuries.

Worth a five-minute stop if you are driving the back road over the Eildons; combine with the climb to Eildon North summit, which begins not far away.
