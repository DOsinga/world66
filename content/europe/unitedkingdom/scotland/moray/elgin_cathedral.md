story: >
  Known as the "Lantern of the North," Elgin Cathedral was consecrated in 1224 and considered one of the finest in Scotland. In 1390, the Wolf of Badenoch — Alexander Stewart, illegitimate son of Robert II — burned it to the ground as revenge for being excommunicated by the Bishop of Moray. The ruin you see today was never fully rebuilt, and Stewart was eventually forced to perform public penance for the act, walking barefoot to a church while carrying a candle.
---

The ruined 13th-century cathedral that once made Elgin the ecclesiastical capital of northern Scotland. Even in its broken state — roofless nave, truncated towers, shattered windows — the scale and ambition of the building is striking. The west front retains much of its carved decoration, and the chapter house, with its central column and ribbed vault, is the best-preserved interior space.

The cathedral was consecrated in 1224 and expanded repeatedly over the next two centuries. Today Historic Environment Scotland manages the site, and a small visitor centre provides context for what you are looking at. Admission is charged. Open daily, hours vary by season.
