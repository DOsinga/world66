  Elgin Museum, founded in 1836, is one of the oldest independent museums in Scotland and houses one of the world's most significant collections of Permo-Triassic fossils — creatures from the period just after the worst mass extinction in Earth's history, when life was slowly reconquering a devastated world. The reptile fossils from the Moray coast were found in the 1820s and 1830s and helped establish the science of vertebrate palaeontology.
---

The Elgin Museum on High Street is a Victorian gem — purpose-built in 1842 with an Italianate facade and a collection that has been accumulating ever since. The displays cover local natural history and Pictish carved stones, but the standout holding is the palaeontology collection: fossils of early reptiles and amphibians from the Permian and Triassic periods found in the red sandstones of the Moray coast, some of which are among the rarest in the world.

The museum is run by the Moray Society and is staffed largely by volunteers who know their collection well. Admission is small. Open Tuesday to Friday and Saturday morning. The Victorian cases, crowded with bones, stones, and curiosities, give the place the atmosphere of a nineteenth-century cabinet of wonders.
