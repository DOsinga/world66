  A good whisky spends years in an oak cask, and the cask has more influence on the final flavour than almost any other factor. The Speyside Cooperage at Craigellachie is one of the few places in the world where you can watch coopers making and repairing the barrels by hand — croze cutting, hooping, firing the stave, driving the chime. The craft has changed little in 200 years, and a skilled cooper can still produce eight barrels per day.
---

At the Speyside Cooperage in Craigellachie, visitors watch from a gallery above the main workshop floor as coopers dismantle, repair, and rebuild oak casks — the containers in which all Scottish whisky matures. The work is skilled, loud, and physically demanding: hammers ring against metal hoops, staves are shaved and fitted by hand, and the burning of the new wood to make barrels flexible fills the building with smoke and a sweet caramel smell.

The cooperage supplies barrels to many of the major Speyside distilleries, and the visitor centre explains clearly why the cask matters so much to whisky — American oak versus European oak, ex-bourbon versus ex-sherry maturation. Admission is charged; there is also a shop. Open Monday to Friday year-round, with limited Saturday hours in summer.
