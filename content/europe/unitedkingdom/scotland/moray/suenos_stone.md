  Sueno's Stone is over six metres tall and carved on all four faces with intricate Pictish scenes — a narrative battle sequence on one side, geometric knotwork on the others. One of the largest and most elaborately carved Pictish monuments in Scotland, standing 6.5 metres tall on the outskirts of Forres. The stone is carved on all four sides, with the main face depicting a battle scene in extraordinary detail: warriors on horseback, rows of headless corpses, and a triumphal arch presiding over the carnage. Historians have proposed a range of interpretations — a victory over Viking raiders, a conflict between Pictish kingdoms — but no consensus exists.

The stone is protected inside a purpose-built glass enclosure managed by Historic Environment Scotland. Admission is free, and the site is accessible at all times. It is signposted from the centre of Forres and is a short walk from the car park on the main road.
