New Lanark is a compact mill village on the River Clyde, built around water-powered cotton mills and later made famous by Robert Owen's social reforms. It is a rare industrial heritage site where the ideas matter as much as the buildings.

The restored mills, workers' houses, schoolroom, machinery, and village store show how Owen tried to make factory life more humane in the early 19th century. The place can feel tidy, but the stone buildings, steep valley, and river keep it grounded.

The best visit combines the [New Lanark Mills](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/new_lanark/new_lanark_mills) with a walk to the [Falls of Clyde](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/new_lanark/falls_of_clyde). The falls, wooded gorge, ruined viewpoints, and old power landscape turn New Lanark from a single museum stop into a strong half-day destination.
