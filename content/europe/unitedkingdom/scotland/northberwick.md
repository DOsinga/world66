North Berwick is a small seaside town on the East Lothian coast of Scotland, about 40 km east of Edinburgh and easily reached by train in under an hour. It has an excellent beach, a compact and pleasant town centre, and the [Bass Rock](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/northberwick/bass_rock) offshore — a massive volcanic plug that holds one of the largest gannet colonies in the world, with over 150,000 birds visible from the shore and reachable by boat in summer.

The [Scottish Seabird Centre](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/northberwick/scottish_seabird_centre) on the [harbour](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/northberwick/north_berwick_harbour) is a good starting point, with live camera feeds to the gannet colony and boats that circle the rock. [Tantallon Castle](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/northberwick/tantallon_castle), a dramatically sited 14th-century ruin on the clifftops a few kilometres east, looks directly at the Bass Rock and is one of the most impressive castle sites in Scotland. The conical hill of [North Berwick Law](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/northberwick/north_berwick_law) above town gives a 30-minute climb to the best view on the Forth, and the [West Links](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/northberwick/north_berwick_west_links) golf course is one of the oldest and most copied in the world. The town has a few good restaurants and the kind of prosperous independent-shop character typical of the East Lothian coast. North Berwick is genuinely one of the best day trips from Edinburgh and deserves more attention than it usually gets.
