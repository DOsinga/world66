Birsay sits on the north-west corner of Mainland [Orkney](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/orkney), where the land runs out into cliffs, tidal rocks, and big Atlantic weather. The village itself is small, but the cluster around it is strong: a causeway walk to the [Brough of Birsay](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/orkney/birsay/brough_of_birsay), a ruined Renaissance palace, an old bere mill, and a farm museum that still smells faintly of peat smoke.

The great pleasure here is the way Orkney's layers sit close together. You can cross to Pictish and Norse remains at low tide, come back to the roofless [Earl's Palace](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/orkney/birsay/earls_palace_birsay), and then drive a few minutes inland to [Kirbuster Farm Museum](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/orkney/birsay/kirbuster_farm_museum), where the old stone bed and central hearth make the prehistoric houses at Skara Brae feel less remote.

Do not treat the tide as a detail. The Brough is only reachable for a short window either side of low water, and the shore looks more forgiving than it is. With a car or bicycle, Birsay makes an excellent half-day from Kirkwall or Stromness; without one, bus times are sparse enough that the plan has to come first.
