A spectacular Iron Age site on the north coast of Mainland, the Broch of Gurness is a circular drystone tower from around 200 BCE surrounded by an entire small village of cellular stone houses. Brochs are a distinctively Scottish Iron Age form — fortified towers up to 13 metres high with double walls — and Gurness is the most complete example of a broch settlement in Orkney.

The site is on a low promontory overlooking the Eynhallow Sound, with the island of Rousay opposite. Allow an hour and a half. There is a small visitor centre with interpretive displays. Closed in winter.
