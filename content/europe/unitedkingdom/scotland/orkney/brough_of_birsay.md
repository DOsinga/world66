The Brough of Birsay is a small grass-covered tidal island just off the northwest corner of Mainland Orkney. It is reached via a concrete causeway that is uncovered for two or three hours either side of low tide; check tide tables before crossing.

Once on the island, the ruins of a Norse village, a twelfth-century church, and the foundations of a Pictish predecessor sit on the seaward side. A symbol-stone with Pictish carvings (now a replica; the original is in the National Museum of Scotland) marks the earliest known phase. Beyond the ruins, the cliffs hold puffins in summer and the views back across to Mainland are superb. Allow three hours including the walk, more if the puffins are about.
