The most exciting current archaeological dig in Britain, the Ness of Brodgar is a vast Neolithic complex still being excavated on the narrow strip of land between the Ring of Brodgar and the Stones of Stenness. The site has revealed a major ceremonial centre dating from around 3300 BCE — long walls, painted stones, decorated pottery, and a great hall larger than anything else known from Neolithic Britain.

The dig runs for six weeks each summer (usually July and early August), with daily tours led by the archaeologists. Outside the season the site is covered with protective tarpaulin and is much less interesting. If your visit coincides with the dig, do not miss it.
