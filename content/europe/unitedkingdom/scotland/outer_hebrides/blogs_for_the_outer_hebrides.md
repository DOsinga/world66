The Outer Hebrides are written about mostly as a postcard. These three write from inside: the islands' tourism body on the empty east of Lewis that visitors heading for Callanish never see, a Stornoway-born writer's essays on island life now, and Uist's community site, in Gaelic and English.

They lead to a ruined church at Point, a headland for whales, the beach where one writer's family came from, and the Madonna on the hill above South Uist.
