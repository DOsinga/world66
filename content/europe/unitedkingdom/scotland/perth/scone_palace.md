story: >
  Every Scottish king from Kenneth MacAlpin in the 9th century to Charles II in 1651 was crowned at Scone, sitting on the Stone of Destiny. When Edward I carried the stone to Westminster Abbey in 1296, he believed he had taken the heart of Scottish sovereignty with it. In 1950, a group of Scottish students stole the stone back from Westminster and drove it to Scotland, where it was kept for four months before being returned. The stone is now in Edinburgh, but Scone remains the place where Scotland's royal history is most tangibly present.
---

Scone Palace sits on the site of the ancient Moot Hill, where Scottish kings were crowned for over a millennium. The present palace, built in the early 19th century on medieval foundations, belongs to the Earl of Mansfield and is open to visitors from April to October. The state rooms contain a serious collection of furniture, clocks, porcelain, and Vernis Martin (French lacquered objects) built up over centuries by the Murray family.

The grounds are worth as much time as the interior. The Murray Star Maze, planted with different varieties of beech, is one of the more interesting mazes in Scotland. A significant collection of conifers was established in the 19th century from seeds brought back from North America. Allow two to three hours for palace and grounds. It's 2 miles north of Perth city centre; easily reached by taxi, or there's a bus from the town centre.
