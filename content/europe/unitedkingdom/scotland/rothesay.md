Rothesay is the main town of the Isle of Bute, curled around a wide bay on the Firth of Clyde. In its Victorian and Edwardian heyday this was where Glasgow went "doon the watter": steamers crowded the pier, a hydropathic hotel took the cure-seekers, an electric tramway ran across the island to the beach at [Ettrick Bay](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/rothesay/ettrick_bay), and the Winter Gardens and the 1938 Pavilion filled with music-hall stars. Cheap package holidays ended all that in the 1960s, and Rothesay has been quietly reinventing itself ever since.

What's left is a handsome, slightly faded seaside town of tall sandstone tenements and villas along the bay, with a surprising amount of history at its centre. The ruined [Rothesay Castle](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/rothesay/rothesay_castle), circular and moated, was taken by Norse raiders in the 13th century and later became a favourite residence of the Stewart kings; it's why the heir to the throne is still the Duke of Rothesay. By the ferry terminal, the [Victorian Toilets](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/rothesay/victorian_toilets) are a genuine attraction, and the modernist Pavilion on Argyle Street has been undergoing a long restoration.

Bute itself is small and gentle, split by the Highland Boundary Fault into hillier country in the north and farmland in the south. Its great sight is [Mount Stuart](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/rothesay/mount_stuart), the extravagant Gothic Revival seat of the Marquesses of Bute, a few miles south of town. Further south still, the ruined [St Blane's Church](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/rothesay/st_blanes_church) sits in a hollow on the site of an early monastery, and the west coast has sandy bays looking across to the mountains of Arran. The 30-mile West Island Way crosses the island for walkers.

Getting here is easy: a CalMac ferry crosses from Wemyss Bay, where trains connect to Glasgow Central, and a second, very short crossing links Rhubodach in the north of Bute with Colintraive on the Cowal peninsula. It makes Rothesay one of the simplest island escapes from Glasgow.
