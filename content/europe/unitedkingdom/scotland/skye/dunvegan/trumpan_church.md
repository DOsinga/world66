story: "In May 1578 a raiding party of MacDonalds from Uist landed at Ardmore, walked up to Trumpan and set fire to the church while a MacLeod congregation was inside at Sunday service. All but one of the worshippers were burned alive. The survivor ran to Dunvegan and raised the alarm; the MacLeods unfurled their Fairy Flag and caught the MacDonalds on the beach before they could re-embark. The retribution was complete — the MacDonald raiders were killed and buried under the dyke at the shore in what is still called the Battle of the Spoiled Dyke."

The ruined church at Trumpan stands on a windy headland above Ardmore Bay on the Vaternish peninsula, north of Dunvegan. Only the walls remain, but the churchyard is still in use and the setting is striking — open sea on three sides and the Outer Hebrides visible on a clear day.

In the churchyard is the so-called "trial stone" — a thin pillar with a hole through which an accused person was required to insert a finger in the dark. If they hit the hole first time, innocence was assumed. A short walk in either direction from the church gives long views over the bay and the cliffs.
