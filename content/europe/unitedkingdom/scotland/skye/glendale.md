Glendale is a remote crofting township at the western tip of [Skye](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/skye), seven miles west of [Dunvegan](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/skye/dunvegan) in the Duirinish peninsula. The area is associated with the MacLeod clan and with an 1882 land reform dispute — the "Battle of the Braes" — when local crofters resisted eviction and helped spark the legislation that eventually protected Scottish tenants' rights.

Today Glendale draws visitors for its scenery and its unusual attractions. The [Glendale Toy Museum](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/skye/glendale/glendale_toy_museum) has an eclectic collection of antique and vintage toys from around the world. Craft studios and a community cafe round out the options. The peninsula's dramatic sea cliffs — [Waterstein Head](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/skye/glendale/waterstein_head) and [Neist Point lighthouse](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/skye/glendale/neist_point_lighthouse), the westernmost point on Skye — are within easy reach and offer some of the best walking and sea-watching on the island. Minke whales and basking sharks are sometimes visible from the cliffs in summer. Across the moor at Colbost, the [Three Chimneys](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/skye/glendale/the_three_chimneys) restaurant and the [Colbost Croft Museum](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/skye/glendale/colbost_croft_museum) sit a stone's throw apart — a study in contrasts.
