The British Golf Museum occupies a modern building beside the Royal and Ancient Clubhouse, just back from the 18th green of the Old Course. Its permanent collection covers the history of golf from its Scottish origins through to the modern professional game, and it is genuinely well-done — not just a repository of old clubs and balls, but an attempt to put golf's development in social and cultural context.

The collection includes early clubs and featherie balls (hand-stitched leather spheres stuffed with compressed feathers, used before the gutta-percha ball appeared in 1848), early rulebooks, championship trophies, and memorabilia from every era of the Open Championship. The section on women's golf and the history of the Curtis Cup is particularly good. There is a hands-on putting area and some interactive exhibits that work rather better for adults than the children's-museum versions at most sporting museums.

For anyone with a serious interest in golf, the museum is at least two hours. For casual visitors, an hour covers the highlights comfortably. Entry is around £9 for adults; the combination ticket with the St Andrews Cathedral and Castle is not available here, but a golf pass covering the museum and other sites is sold at the R&A. Check the museum website for the exhibition schedule — the temporary exhibitions are often the best reason to visit.
