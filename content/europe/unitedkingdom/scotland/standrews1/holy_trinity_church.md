Holy Trinity Church on South Street is the town's main parish church and has been at the centre of St Andrews's religious life since 1412. The current building is mostly 15th-century, though heavily restored in the 19th century; the tower is original medieval work and visually anchors the mid-section of South Street.

The church's interior is unusually interesting for a Scottish parish church. It contains a notable marble monument to Archbishop Sharp, the Episcopalian archbishop murdered by Covenanters in 1679 — his assassination in his coach near St Andrews, in front of his daughter, became a cause célèbre of the religious conflicts of the period. The monument, erected by his son, shows Sharp in full episcopal robes.

John Knox preached his first sermon in the reform cause from Holy Trinity's pulpit in 1547, setting out his programme for the Presbyterian church. The church is open for visitors during the day and the tourism material explaining the building's history is better than average. It is easy to visit in combination with a walk down South Street.
