St Andrews harbour is a compact working harbour at the eastern end of town, below the cathedral. The stone piers enclose a small tidal basin where lobster pots, fishing boats, and small pleasure craft share the space. It is not a postcard harbour in the polished, tourist-oriented way of some Scottish fishing villages — it still does actual fishing — and that makes it more interesting.

The harbour was crucial to St Andrews from medieval times onward; pilgrims arriving by sea to visit the cathedral's relics landed here, and the burgh's prosperity depended on coastal trade. The **East Sands** beach stretches south from the harbour mouth and is a quieter, smaller alternative to the more famous West Sands — good for swimming in summer, with some shelter from the wind when the north is blowing.

The path along the harbour wall and up toward the castle provides the best coastal views from the headland. At low tide, rock pools appear below the castle cliff. The fish and chip shops on the harbour front range from indifferent to good; the queue outside the better ones tells you which is which.
