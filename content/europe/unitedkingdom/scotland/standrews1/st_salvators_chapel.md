St Salvator's Chapel is the surviving centrepiece of St Salvator's College, founded in 1450 by Bishop James Kennedy, and the most architecturally distinguished building in St Andrews outside the cathedral ruins. Its square tower is a landmark visible from much of the town, and the chapel interior — with its fan-vaulted ceiling, medieval stalls, and the tomb of Bishop Kennedy — is the finest intact medieval interior in St Andrews.

The chapel is part of the University of St Andrews and is still used for regular services, graduation ceremonies, and university occasions. The red gown-wearing student body swirls around it on Sunday evenings and during formal academic events. Visiting outside service hours is generally possible; check the university calendar, as access is sometimes restricted during term ceremonies.

Bishop Kennedy's tomb is one of the most elaborate pieces of 15th-century funeral sculpture in Scotland. The bishop is shown in full vestments; the canopy above the tomb was once more elaborate still, with gold and enamel work that has not survived. Outside the chapel, the cobblestone monogram of Patrick Hamilton has become one of the most photographed spots in town — the student superstition around it is taken more seriously than you might expect.
