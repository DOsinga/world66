  The opening sequence of Chariots of Fire — the one with the runners on the beach, set to Vangelis — was filmed on West Sands in 1981. The film is set in 1924 and shows the British Olympic team training in Cambridge, but the actual location is St Andrews. The beach was chosen for its length and the quality of its light. A small plaque near the beach car park commemorates the filming.

West Sands is a two-mile sweep of sand running north from the edge of the Old Course Links, backed by dunes and with views across the bay toward the harbour. It is large enough to feel genuinely uncrowded even in summer, which is a relief given the small size of the town behind it. The sand is clean and the tidal range is substantial, so the beach doubles in width at low tide.

The beach is freely accessible and has a car park at the southern end. It is popular with dog walkers, joggers, and families throughout the year. The sea here is the North Sea, which means the water temperature stays cold even in August — committed swimmers use it year-round, but most visitors are content with a walk along the tideline.

The walk from the town centre takes about twenty minutes heading northwest past the Royal and Ancient clubhouse and along the edge of the Links. Combined with a return via the coastal path on the far side, taking in the castle and cathedral headlands, it makes for a satisfying two-hour circuit of the town's western and northern edges.
