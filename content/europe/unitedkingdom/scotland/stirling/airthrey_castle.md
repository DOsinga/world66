Airthrey Castle is a small Gothic Revival country house designed in 1791 by Robert Adam for Robert Haldane of Airthrey. It is one of Adam's last commissions — he died the following year — and the only one in central Scotland. The exterior is a compact, picturesque assemblage of round towers, battlements and pointed windows; the interior was reworked in the 19th century and the original Adam plan has been heavily altered.

The house and its surrounding parkland were bought by the new [University of Stirling](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/stirling/university_of_stirling) in the 1960s, and the castle now contains university offices. The grounds are open to the public and include the 17-acre Airthrey Loch, a number of mature specimen trees (including a 200-year-old yew and a tulip tree), and a small walled garden.

It sits at the eastern end of the university campus. There is no formal admission to the castle interior, but the exterior and grounds are worth a stop on a campus walk.
