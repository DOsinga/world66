The Bannockburn Heritage Centre is about 3 kilometres south of Stirling town centre, on the site where Robert the Bruce defeated Edward II's English army in June 1314. The centre, operated by the National Trust for Scotland and significantly upgraded in 2014 for the battle's 700th anniversary, is one of the more sophisticated heritage operations in Scotland — its handling of a complex and contested history is notably good.

The centrepiece is a 3D battle simulation that allows visitors to follow the two days of fighting, including the movements of cavalry, schiltron formations, and the famous rout on the second day. The exhibition provides context for the war of independence and the political situation that led to the battle, treating the evidence honestly rather than mythologising. An equestrian statue of Robert the Bruce stands at the rotunda outside.

The site itself is modest: there are no visible earthworks or ruins, and the land is now farmland with some interpretation markers. The heritage centre is the attraction; those who want to see the actual ground of the battle should walk around the marked circuit. Worth half a day combined with the castle.
