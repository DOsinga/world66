The Bannockburn Heritage Centre sits at the edge of the battlefield where Robert the Bruce defeated Edward II of England on 23–24 June 1314 — a victory so complete that it effectively secured Scottish independence until the Treaty of Union nearly four hundred years later. The centre, run by the National Trust for Scotland, was redesigned in 2014 for the battle's 700th anniversary and replaces a description-heavy old museum with a 3D digital battlefield experience and a "Battle Game" where visitors take command of the Scottish or English forces.

The interpretation is unusual for a heritage site — there are very few physical artefacts on display, because almost no contemporary objects survive from the battle. Instead the centre uses motion-captured actors, virtual reality and a circular battle floor projection to convey what the day was like. Outside, the Pilkington Jackson equestrian statue of Bruce — bronze, monumental, sword raised — stands in front of the centre, and a stone rotunda marks what tradition holds was Bruce's command post.

Booking is recommended in summer for the timed Battle Game sessions. The site is two miles south of Stirling city centre, easily reached by local bus or a 40-minute walk along the Borestone Road.
