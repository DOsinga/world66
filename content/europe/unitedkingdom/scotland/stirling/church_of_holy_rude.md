The Church of the Holy Rude is the medieval parish church of Stirling, standing immediately below the castle on the edge of the old town. It has been a place of worship since the twelfth century, and the current building — largely fifteenth-century — is a substantial Gothic structure with one of the few surviving medieval oak barrel-vaulted roofs in Scotland. The nave is impressively austere, the columns solid and high, the light falling through plain glass in a way that seems appropriate to the austere Calvinist tradition that took over the building in the Reformation.

The church carries considerable historical weight. John Knox himself preached from the pulpit here on multiple occasions, and the building was central to the religious and political life of a town that was for long periods the effective capital of Scotland. The choir at the east end dates from the fifteenth century and is a particularly good example of late Scottish Gothic.

Open to visitors during the day, usually without admission charge. The churchyard extending around the building is full of interesting stones and offers a good angle on the castle above.
