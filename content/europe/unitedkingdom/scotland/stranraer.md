Stranraer sits on Loch Ryan near the far southwestern tip of [Scotland](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland). For much of the 20th century it was known above all as a ferry port for Northern Ireland, but the big sailings now use Cairnryan a few miles north, leaving Stranraer with a quieter harbour and a slightly bypassed feel.

The town itself is modest, but it makes a practical base for the Rhins of Galloway: Portpatrick on the west coast, the gardens and beaches to the south, and the ferry terminals to Belfast and Larne. The setting is good, with the water of Loch Ryan on one side and low green country behind.

Stranraer's best local sights are small-scale: the [Castle of St John](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/stranraer/castle_of_st_john), the town museum, harbour walks, and easy access to ruined abbeys, gardens, and long-distance walking routes. It is not a place to rush across Scotland for, but it is useful and likeable if your route already brings you this far west.
