Thurso is the north-coast town most travellers meet on the way to Scrabster and the Orkney ferry, but it deserves more than a glance from a car window. It faces the Pentland Firth with a broad sandy bay, cold-water surf, old Caithness stonework, and a raw edge-of-mainland feel that is very different from the Highland postcard further south.

The town's best surprise is the surf. [Thurso Beach and Thurso East](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/thurso/thurso_beach_and_thurso_east) draw serious surfers when the North Atlantic swell is working, and even non-surfers can enjoy the bay, river mouth, and view towards the ruins of Thurso Castle. A few streets inland, the old town around the river still hints at the fishing settlement that came before the planned streets.

For history, [Old St Peter's Kirk](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/thurso/old_st_peters_kirk) is a roofless medieval church ruin with a stronger sense of age than many tidier monuments. West of town, [Holborn Head](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/thurso/holborn_head) gives Thurso its best walk: a short clifftop circuit from Scrabster past lighthouse, sea stacks, blowholes, and big Pentland Firth views. Add [Wolfburn Distillery](/europe/unitedkingdom/scotland/thurso/wolfburn_distillery), the mainland's northern whisky outpost, and Thurso becomes a good one-day stop rather than just the place before the boat.
