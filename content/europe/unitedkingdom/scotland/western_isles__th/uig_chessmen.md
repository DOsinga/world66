The Uig peninsula on the west coast of Lewis is where the Lewis Chessmen were discovered in 1831 — 93 carved Norse chess pieces now counted among the most important medieval artefacts ever found in Scotland. The figures, which include a queen biting her shield and a knight on horseback, were carved in the 12th century, almost certainly in Norway. The site itself is a large sandy bay backed by dunes, with no marker beyond a small roadside interpretation panel.

The bay is worth visiting regardless of its archaeological significance. Uig Sands is one of the larger beaches on Lewis, with wide sands exposed at low tide and views across to North Uist. A museum in the village of Uig — the Uig Heritage Centre — displays replicas of the chessmen and explains the discovery in detail. To see originals, you need to travel to Edinburgh or London.
