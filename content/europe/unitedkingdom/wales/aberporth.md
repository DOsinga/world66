Aberporth is a small seaside village on Cardigan Bay in Ceredigion, with two sandy beaches tucked below the road and the Wales Coast Path running over the cliffs. It was once a herring-fishing and coastal trade village; today it is a quiet beach stop with enough scenery to justify more than a quick look.

The beaches are the centre of the visit. At low tide, rock pools open up, children spread across the sand, and the cliff path gives wide views along the bay. Bottlenose dolphins are often seen offshore in this part of Cardigan Bay, especially in warmer months.

Aberporth is not a big resort and should not be judged like one. Its appeal is simple: swim, watch the water, walk the coast path toward Tresaith or Mwnt, then settle into a pub meal before moving on.
