Anglesey — Ynys Môn in Welsh — is a large island off the northwest coast of Wales, connected to the mainland by two famous bridges over the Menai Strait: Thomas Telford's suspension bridge of 1826 and Robert Stephenson's Britannia Bridge carrying the railway. Despite the easy crossing, the island has a character quite distinct from the Welsh mainland. It is flatter, wider, and more agricultural than Snowdonia just across the water, with a long coastline of bays, sandy beaches, and sea-cliffs that makes it one of Wales's best coastal destinations.

The island has been inhabited for thousands of years and has an unusual density of ancient monuments. [Din Lligwy](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/anglesey/din_lligwy) on the northeast coast preserves a Neolithic burial chamber, a walled Roman-era settlement, and a medieval chapel within a few hundred metres of each other. Holy Island, at the western tip, contains Holyhead Mountain with Iron Age hut circles on its summit — modest in height but commanding remarkable views. The island was also the last stronghold of the Druids before the Roman conquest.

[Beaumaris](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/anglesey/beaumaris) on the eastern shore is the most architecturally distinguished town on the island, with its concentric Edward I castle — considered the most technically perfect of its kind in Britain. The port of [Holyhead](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/anglesey/holyhead) is the main ferry terminal for Ireland and has a Roman fort intact in the town centre, with seabird cliffs at South Stack nearby. The island is small enough to explore thoroughly in a few days and is a straightforward detour from the main tourist circuit of Snowdonia.
