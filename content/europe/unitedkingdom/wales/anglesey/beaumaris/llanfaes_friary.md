The Franciscan friary of Llanfaes was founded in 1237 by Llywelyn the Great in memory of his wife Joan, who is buried in St Mary's, Beaumaris. It became one of the wealthiest Franciscan houses in Wales and the burial place of several members of the Welsh royal house of Gwynedd.

Almost nothing remains. Henry VIII dissolved the friary in 1538 and the stone was carried off to build local houses; the site is now part of a private farm a mile north of Beaumaris, with a single ruined wall and a fragment of arch the only surface trace. The interest is in the story — the medieval town of Llanfaes that once existed here was forcibly cleared by Edward I in 1295 to make way for Beaumaris, and the friary is the last visible echo of that vanished community.
