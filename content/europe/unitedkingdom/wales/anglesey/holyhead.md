Holyhead — Caergybi in Welsh — sits at the far western tip of Anglesey, and most people pass through on their way to the Irish ferries. But there's reason to linger.

Behind the town rises [Holyhead Mountain](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/anglesey/holyhead/holyhead_mountain) — modest in height, but the only real hill on the whole island. The coastal cliffs around it are spectacular. At [South Stack](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/anglesey/holyhead/south_stack_lighthouse), the RSPB runs a seabird centre where, during breeding season, you can watch guillemots, razorbills, and puffins nesting on the cliff faces. Choughs — once virtually extinct in England — have been spotted here too, part of a slow recolonisation from their Welsh strongholds.

In the town itself, the parish [church of St Cybi](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/anglesey/holyhead/st_cybis_church) sits inside the remarkably complete walls of a small late-Roman fort, [Caer Gybi](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/anglesey/holyhead/caer_gybi) — one of the few Roman fortifications in Britain to survive almost intact. The Victorian engineering of the harbour, including the longest [breakwater](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/anglesey/holyhead/holyhead_breakwater) in the United Kingdom, is a reminder of when Holyhead was the busiest packet port between London and Dublin.
