In the centre of Holyhead, the parish church of St Cybi sits inside the walls of a small late-Roman fort built around AD 300 as a defence against Irish sea raiders. Three of the four corner towers and a long stretch of curtain wall survive, complete with the irregular Roman bonding-courses of stone tiles. It is one of only a handful of Roman forts in Britain where the walls are still standing to anything like their original height.

The fort was given to St Cybi, a 6th-century Welsh missionary, after the Romans left, and the medieval church grew up inside the walls — the name "Caer Gybi" simply means "Cybi's Fort." The result is the unusual experience of walking through a Roman gate to reach a medieval church.
