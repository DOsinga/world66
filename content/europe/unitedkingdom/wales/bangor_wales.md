Bangor is one of the smallest cities in the United Kingdom, tucked between the mountains of Snowdonia and the Menai Strait in north Wales. A population of around 20,000 — about a third of them students at Bangor University — gives the place an energy that belies its size.

The city's origins stretch back to the 6th century, when a monastery was founded here by Saint Deiniol. The [Bangor Cathedral](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/bangor_wales/bangor_cathedral) that eventually replaced it still stands in the city centre, one of the oldest continuously occupied Christian sites in Britain. Bangor's setting is its real asset: the Menai Strait separates the city from Anglesey, crossed by the [Menai Suspension Bridge](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/bangor_wales/menai_suspension_bridge) — Thomas Telford's 1826 engineering masterpiece — and the peaks of Snowdonia rise to the south. [Garth Pier](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/bangor_wales/garth_pier), restored and stretching 450 metres into the strait, is a pleasant walk with views across to Anglesey.

The National Trust's [Penrhyn Castle](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/bangor_wales/penrhyn_castle) just east of the city is the most impressive single sight — a vast Victorian mock-Norman mansion built on the profits of Jamaican sugar and Welsh slate. For most visitors, Bangor also serves as a base for exploring the surrounding area — Snowdonia's walking trails, the castles of Caernarfon and Conwy, and the beaches of Anglesey are all within easy reach.
