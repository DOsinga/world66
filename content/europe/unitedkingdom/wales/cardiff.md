Cardiff only became the capital of [Wales](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales) in 1955, but it has grown into the role with impressive speed. The city has a genuinely European feel — compact, walkable, and with a cultural life that punches well above the weight of its 350,000 population.

[Cardiff Castle](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/cardiff/cardiff_castle) dominates the city centre — a Roman fort, Norman keep and Victorian Gothic fantasy all on the same site. The Marquess of Bute's lavishly decorated interiors are worth the tour. Nearby, the [National Museum Cardiff](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/cardiff/national_museum_cardiff) houses one of the best Impressionist collections outside Paris, alongside an excellent natural history section.

The [Cardiff Bay](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/cardiff/cardiff_bay) waterfront, once the gritty docklands of Tiger Bay that shipped Welsh coal to the world, has been completely transformed. The [Wales Millennium Centre](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/cardiff/wales_millennium_centre) (the opera house) is a striking piece of architecture, and the [Senedd](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/cardiff/senedd) (Welsh Parliament building) is open to visitors. The bay itself is now a freshwater lake, ringed by restaurants and bars.

For rugby fans, the [Principality Stadium](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/cardiff/principality_stadium) in the heart of the city centre is something close to a cathedral. On match days the entire city revolves around it. The [Victorian arcades](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/cardiff/cardiff_arcades) weaving through the city centre — more covered shopping streets per square kilometre than anywhere in Britain — make for excellent wet-weather wandering.
