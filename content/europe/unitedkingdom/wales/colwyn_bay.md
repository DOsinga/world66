Colwyn Bay (Welsh: *Bae Colwyn*) is a Victorian seaside resort on the north Welsh coast, sitting between [Llandudno](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/llandudno) and the Conwy estuary. It grew up in the 1860s when the railway arrived, and a long row of guesthouses and bay-windowed villas still gives the town its character. The promenade has been overhauled with a new sea wall and a sandy beach has been re-laid, making it a more attractive place to walk along the front than it was a decade ago.

The town itself is modest — a high street, a few cafes, the small Victorian streets running back from the bay — but it has two distinct draws. The first is the [Welsh Mountain Zoo](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/colwyn_bay/welsh_mountain_zoo), set on a wooded hillside above the town, with chimpanzees, snow leopards and a celebrated daily display of free-flying eagles and condors. The second is the [Harlequin Puppet Theatre](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/colwyn_bay/harlequin_puppet_theatre) at neighbouring Rhos-on-Sea, Britain's oldest purpose-built marionette theatre, which has been staging weekend shows in the same little auditorium since 1958.

Around the bay there is more than first appears. [Bryn Euryn](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/colwyn_bay/bryn_euryn), a limestone hill rising behind the town, gives one of the best panoramas of the north Wales coast, and on its shoulder lie the ruins of [Llys Euryn](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/colwyn_bay/llys_euryn), a 15th-century hall built on the site of a much older Welsh court. The tiny [Chapel of St Trillo](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/colwyn_bay/st_trillo_chapel) on the seafront at Rhos-on-Sea seats six people and is said to be the smallest chapel in Britain.

Colwyn Bay is not a holiday in itself, but it is a comfortable base for the [Conwy](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/conwy) castle and walled town, [Llandudno](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/llandudno) with its pier and Great Orme, and the eastern fringe of [Snowdonia](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/snowdonia). A coastal cycle path runs all the way along the front, linking these towns flat and traffic-free.
