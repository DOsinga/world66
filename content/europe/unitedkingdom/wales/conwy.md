Conwy is one of the best-preserved medieval walled towns in Britain. The [town walls](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/conwy/town_walls), built by Edward I in the 1280s as part of his ring of fortresses across north Wales, still encircle the old town almost completely — you can walk the full circuit of the battlements and look down into the streets below. [Conwy Castle](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/conwy/conwy_castle) itself is an impressive hulk of towers and great halls perched above the estuary, and along with the walls it forms a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

The town is small enough to cover on foot in a morning, with a compact centre of old stone streets, a good [quayside](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/conwy/conwy_quayside), and the claim to fame of [the Smallest House in Britain](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/conwy/smallest_house) (genuinely tiny, on the quay). [Plas Mawr](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/conwy/plas_mawr), the Elizabethan townhouse near the castle, and [Aberconwy House](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/conwy/aberconwy_house), the only surviving medieval merchant's home in town, are both worth an hour of your time. Thomas Telford's elegant [suspension bridge](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/conwy/conwy_suspension_bridge) sits in the shadow of the castle walls — a remarkable survival from 1826.

Conwy sits on the A55, the main north Wales coastal route, and is a natural stopping point between [Llandudno](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/colwyn_bay) and [Bangor](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/bangor_wales). The Holyhead ferry to Dublin is a few miles further west, making Conwy a convenient overnight stop on the Ireland crossing. The [Conwy Valley Railway](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/conwy/conwy_valley_railway) runs south from Llandudno Junction through spectacular scenery into [Snowdonia](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/snowdonia).
