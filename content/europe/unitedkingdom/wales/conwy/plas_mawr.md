  Plas Mawr was built between 1576 and 1585 by Robert Wynn, an ambitious Welsh merchant who had spent years at the courts of Europe. The elaborate plasterwork ceilings, decorated with heraldic devices, animals, and mythological figures, were designed to impress visitors and signal his wealth and connections. The house changed hands many times after Wynn's death and was for decades divided into tenements before its careful restoration in the 1990s.

Plas Mawr — "Great Hall" in Welsh — is reckoned to be the best-preserved Elizabethan townhouse in Britain, and after a thorough restoration by Cadw it looks much as it would have done when Robert Wynn completed it in the 1580s. The painted plasterwork is extraordinary: richly decorated ceilings and overmantels fill every room with heraldic badges, grotesque figures, and bold geometric patterns in colours that were carefully reproduced from the original pigments.

The house sits in the middle of the walled town, an easy walk from the castle, and the audio guide (included in the admission price) does a good job of bringing the domestic life of an Elizabethan household to life. It is one of the most underrated historic interiors in Wales — the kind of place that rewards slow looking rather than a quick walk-through. Allow an hour.
