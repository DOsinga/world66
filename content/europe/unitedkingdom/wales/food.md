---
title: Food
type: section
---

Welsh food is farmhouse food, built on lamb, dairy and the sea. It has come a long way in recent decades, with good producers and markets, and a crop of restaurants cooking local ingredients.

The dish everyone should try is **cawl**, a slow broth of lamb or beef with leeks and root vegetables, best eaten with bread and a piece of cheese on the side. **Welsh rarebit** is cheese melted with ale and mustard on toast. Salt-marsh lamb from the Gower and the estuaries is a speciality.

The coast adds **laverbread** (*bara lawr*), seaweed cooked down to a dark purée and traditionally fried with oatmeal for breakfast, and **cockles** from Penclawdd on the Gower. Halen Môn sea salt from Anglesey is sold all over Britain.

For something sweet there are **Welsh cakes**, small griddle cakes with currants that are sold warm in markets, and **bara brith**, a tea-soaked fruit loaf. **Caerphilly** is the classic Welsh cheese, crumbly and mild. **Glamorgan sausages** are a meatless version made with cheese and leeks.
