*Europe > United Kingdom > Wales > Hay-on-Wye*

Hay-on-Wye is a small Welsh border town that made itself famous with books. It sits on the River Wye, just inside Bannau Brycheiniog, and its streets are packed with second-hand bookshops, specialist shelves, maps, prints, and the sort of browsing that can swallow a whole afternoon.

The town's literary identity is not just decorative. Richard Booth helped turn Hay into the world's first modern book town in the 1960s, and the Hay Festival later made it a serious stop on the British cultural calendar. Even outside festival weeks, the place has a strong rhythm: bookshops on Lion Street, a Thursday market, small galleries, and walkers coming in muddy from the Wye or Offa's Dyke Path.

[Hay Castle](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/hay_on_wye/hay_castle) gives the centre real weight. The medieval fortification and later mansion reopened as an arts and learning centre after a long restoration, so it now works as both monument and cultural venue. When you need air, [The Warren](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/hay_on_wye/the_warren) is an easy riverside walk from town, with the Black Mountains rising beyond the fields.

Hay is not a big-sight town, and that is part of the appeal. It is best for a slow day: buy more books than you meant to, climb through the castle, walk down to the Wye, and stay long enough to feel the border-country pace.
