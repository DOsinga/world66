title: Highlights
type: section
---

Wales is small enough to cross in an afternoon and varied enough to fill a month. These are the reasons to go.

**The mountains of Eryri.** [Snowdonia](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/snowdonia) has the highest peaks south of the Scottish Highlands, and Yr Wyddfa (Snowdon) is the busiest mountain in Britain. You can walk it from [Llanberis](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/llanberis), take the rack railway, or climb one of the quieter peaks around it. [Betws-y-Coed](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/betwsycoed) is the forest gateway, and Capel Curig the climbers' crossroads.

**Edward I's castles.** Caernarfon, Conwy, Harlech and Beaumaris, built to hold down the north after the conquest of 1283, are together a World Heritage Site. [Caernarfon](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/caernarfon) is the grandest, [Conwy](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/conwy) still has its complete town walls, and [Harlech](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/harlech) sits on a crag above the sea. In the south, Caerphilly is one of the largest castles in Britain.

**The Pembrokeshire coast.** The [national park](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/pembrokeshire_coast_national_park) is Britain's only coastal one: cliffs, coves and surf beaches along a 186-mile coast path. [St Davids](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/st_davids), Britain's smallest city, is the base for the boats to Ramsey Island and for coasteering, which was invented here. In early summer the puffins come back to Skomer. [Tenby](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/tenby) is the classic walled seaside town.

**Gower and the south coast.** [Gower](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/gower), just west of [Swansea](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/swansea), was Britain's first Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty. Rhossili Bay is one of the great beaches of Britain, and there are small coves all along the south side.

**The Brecon Beacons.** The [Brecon Beacons](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/brecon_beacons) (Bannau Brycheiniog) are long grassy ridges rising to Pen y Fan. To the south are the waterfalls and caves of Waterfall Country, and to the east the Black Mountains above [Hay-on-Wye](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/hay_on_wye), the town of books.

**Cardiff.** The capital, [Cardiff](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/cardiff), has a castle in its centre, Victorian shopping arcades, the redeveloped Bay and a rugby stadium in the middle of town. On a match day the whole city turns out.

**Welsh, spoken.** Welsh is a living language, the everyday tongue across much of the north and west. Hearing it in a shop in Caernarfon or a pub on the Llŷn is part of the trip.
