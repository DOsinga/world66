The Great Orme (Welsh: *Y Gogarth*, meaning "the dark place") is a limestone headland that rises sharply from the sea at the northern end of Llandudno. It dominates the town and provides a clear visual anchor for everything around it. The summit reaches 207 metres, and on a clear day the view takes in the Isle of Man, the Lake District fells across the Liverpool Bay, and the whole north Wales coast.

The headland is a country park, with a network of walking paths, the [Great Orme Mines](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/llandudno/great_orme_mines) Bronze Age copper workings, the Marine Drive toll road around the cliffs (4.5 miles, with several pull-offs for the view), and a substantial population of feral Kashmir goats. The summit has a visitor centre, a café, a small church (St Tudno's), and an artificial ski slope and toboggan run. The cliffs on the western and northern faces are nationally important seabird breeding sites; guillemots, razorbills and kittiwakes nest in numbers between March and July.

You can reach the summit by the [Great Orme Tramway](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/llandudno/great_orme_tramway), the [Llandudno Cable Car](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/llandudno/cable_car), by car along Marine Drive, or on foot from the town centre (about 45 minutes uphill). Allow at least half a day for the whole headland. The Marine Drive is one-way clockwise from Llandudno and costs a small toll per car.
