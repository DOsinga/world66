Kendrick's Cave is a small natural cave in the limestone on the lower slopes of the [Great Orme](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/llandudno/great_orme), about 100 metres above the town. It was discovered in 1879 by Thomas Kendrick, a stonemason who was using the cave as his workshop and found the floor full of bone material when he started levelling it. The subsequent excavations produced one of the most important Upper Palaeolithic finds ever made in Britain.

The cave was used both as a burial site and as a workshop for several thousand years between about 13,000 and 9,000 BC. The most famous finds — an engraved horse jawbone, perforated bear and deer teeth strung as necklaces, and human remains — are now in the British Museum, but the cave itself is still accessible from a steep footpath off the Marine Drive. The cave is small (about 6 metres deep) and unlit, so a torch is useful; it is grilled off at the entrance but visible from outside.

Access is on foot only, by the path up from Church Walks. Allow an hour for the visit including the walk. The cave is a peaceful, atmospheric spot with excellent views over Llandudno Bay.
