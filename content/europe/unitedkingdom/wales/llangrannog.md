Llangrannog is a small seaside village on the Ceredigion coast, tucked into a steep-sided valley where two streams reach the beach. It is not a town of grand sights. Its pull is simpler: a sandy cove, a second beach reached at low tide, cliffs rising on both sides, and a coast path that gives you the full Cardigan Bay view within minutes of leaving the village.

The beach is split visually by [Carreg Bica](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/llangrannog/carreg_bica), a tooth-shaped sea stack with a local giant legend attached to it. Around the headlands, the Wales Coast Path leads toward [Ynys Lochtyn](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/llangrannog/ynys_lochtyn), one of the prettiest short walks on this part of the coast. In good weather the sea can look almost Mediterranean; in rough weather the same cliffs make it feel very Welsh indeed.

Come for a half day from [Aberystwyth](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/aberystwyth), Cardigan, or the wider Ceredigion coast. Llangrannog earns its place because the beach, the rock, and the cliff walking are all in one compact, memorable pocket.
