title: Things to Do
type: section
---

The Llŷn Peninsula rewards those who explore it slowly. The big draw is the coastline — some of the cleanest beaches in Wales, dramatic headlands, and the pilgrimage island of Bardsey sitting off the tip. But the peninsula also has architectural surprises at its eastern edge: the fantastical Italian village of Portmeirion and the narrow-gauge Ffestiniog Railway at Porthmadog.

[Bardsey Island](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/lleyn_peninsula/bardsey_island) is the spiritual heart of the peninsula — a place of extraordinary atmosphere, known for centuries as the "Island of 20,000 Saints." Reaching it requires a boat from Aberdaron and some cooperation from the weather. [Porth Oer](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/lleyn_peninsula/porth_oer) (Whistling Sands) on the north coast delivers one of Wales's most distinctive beach experiences: the dry sand actually squeaks underfoot. [Portmeirion](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/lleyn_peninsula/portmeirion), just east of the peninsula, is an Italianate folly village that must be seen to be believed — and the [Ffestiniog Railway](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/lleyn_peninsula/ffestiniog_railway) at Porthmadog is the finest narrow-gauge line in Britain.

For history, [Criccieth Castle](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/lleyn_peninsula/criccieth_castle) stands on a promontory over the sea, and [St Hywyn's Church in Aberdaron](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/lleyn_peninsula/st_hywns_church_aberdaron) was the last resting place for pilgrims before the crossing to Bardsey.
