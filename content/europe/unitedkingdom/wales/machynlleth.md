Machynlleth (pronounced roughly mak-UN-khleth) is a small market town in mid-Wales, sitting at the head of the Dyfi valley on the edge of Snowdonia National Park. It punches above its weight for a town of its size, with a clock tower at its centre, a Wednesday street market, and a genuinely interesting history: Owain Glyndŵr, the last native Prince of Wales, held his parliament here in 1404.

Most visitors today come for the [Centre for Alternative Technology](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/machynlleth/centre_for_alternative_technology) (CAT), just a couple of miles north up the Dyfi valley. Founded in the 1970s, it's a working demonstration site for renewable energy, sustainable building, and organic growing — worth a half-day especially if you have kids in tow.

The town itself is a decent base for the surrounding area. [Cadair Idris](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/machynlleth/cadair_idris), one of Wales's finest mountains, is accessible to the south, and the [Mach Loop](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/machynlleth/mach_loop) — a low-level military flying route through the valleys — draws aviation enthusiasts from across the country. [Cors Dyfi](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/machynlleth/cors_dyfi) nature reserve on the estuary to the south is home to nesting ospreys — the first wild breeding pair in Wales in 400 years. The [Bron Yr Aur](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/machynlleth/bron_yr_aur) cottage nearby is where Led Zeppelin wrote much of their acoustic material in 1970. The Cambrian rail line connects Machynlleth to [Aberystwyth](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/aberystwyth) to the west and Shrewsbury to the east.
