Bala sits at the northeast end of Llyn Tegid, Wales's largest natural lake, with the hills of southern Eryri rising around it. It is small, Welsh-speaking, and outdoorsy in a way that feels distinct from the busier Snowdon villages to the north.

The lake is the obvious anchor. [Llyn Tegid](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/snowdonia/bala/llyn_tegid) gives Bala its view, its sailing, and much of its summer life. [Bala Lake Railway](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/snowdonia/bala/bala_lake_railway) follows the shore by narrow-gauge steam train, while the [National White Water Centre](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/snowdonia/bala/national_white_water_centre) on the Tryweryn makes the town one of Britain's better white-water bases.

Bala is not polished in the resort sense, which is part of the appeal. Use it for lake time, rafting, walking, and a quieter Eryri base where Welsh is part of the everyday soundscape rather than a decorative sign.
