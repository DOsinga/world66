Swansea — Abertawe in Welsh — is pretty and pretty ugly at the same time. The old industrial port and the bombed-out 1950s reconstruction sit alongside a revived [Maritime Quarter](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/swansea/maritimequarter), a long stretch of sand at [Swansea Bay](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/swansea/swansea_bay), and the wide-open hills and cliffs of the Gower Peninsula a few miles further west.

Swansea is far more Welsh-feeling than [Cardiff](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/cardiff). You will hear *Yr Iaith* — the language — spoken in the markets, the chapels and the older suburbs, and the city has a stronger sense of being its own place. It is also Dylan Thomas's city: he was born in the [Uplands](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/swansea/uplands) at [5 Cwmdonkin Drive](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/swansea/dylan_thomas_birthplace), played as a child in [Cwmdonkin Park](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/swansea/cymdonkinpark), drank in the bars of Wind Street and remembered the city all his life.

The compact centre runs from [Castle Square](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/swansea/castlesquare), with the ruins of [Swansea Castle](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/swansea/swansea_castle), through [Swansea Market](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/swansea/swanseamarket) — the largest indoor market in Wales — down to the [National Waterfront Museum](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/swansea/national_waterfront_museum), the [Dylan Thomas Centre](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/swansea/dylan_thomas_centre) and the marina. The [Glynn Vivian Art Gallery](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/swansea/glynn_vivian) holds a surprisingly strong collection of Welsh and international art.

Beyond the centre, the bayside cycle and bus route runs five miles west to [Mumbles](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/swansea/mumbles), the Victorian seaside village under [Oystermouth Castle](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/swansea/oystermouth_castle). Further on, the Gower Peninsula — Britain's first Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty — has Rhossili Bay, the tidal causeway out to Worm's Head, and a dozen other beaches and cliff walks that more than justify the trip to South Wales.
