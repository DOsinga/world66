Tintern is a small village on the Welsh bank of the River Wye, north of Chepstow, and it would be easy to overstate the village itself. The reason it belongs on a travel map is the setting: a tight green valley, a ruined Cistercian abbey, and walks that climb quickly to views over the river bend.

[Tintern Abbey](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/tintern/tintern_abbey) is the headline. Its roofless church has been drawing travellers, poets, painters, and day-trippers since the early Wye Tour made this valley one of Britain's first fashionable scenic routes. Wordsworth, Turner, and generations of less famous visitors all found the same thing here: stone arches open to sky, with the wooded valley pressing close around them.

The village also works well on foot. The [Wye Valley Greenway](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/tintern/wye_valley_greenway) follows the old railway alignment, [The Old Station](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/tintern/the_old_station) gives that railway history a gentle stop, and [The Devil's Pulpit](/europe/unitedkingdom/wales/tintern/the_devils_pulpit) rewards a steep climb with the classic view down to the abbey.
