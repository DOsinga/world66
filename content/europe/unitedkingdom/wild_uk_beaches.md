The UK's coastline runs to thousands of miles, and a surprising amount of it is still empty even in high summer — you just have to be willing to walk, take a causeway, or catch a ferry to get there. This list skips the crowded seaside-town beaches for the ones backed by dunes, pinewoods, or nothing at all: a vast Norfolk strand shared with grazing cattle, a Hebridean beach with water the colour of the Caribbean, a Welsh bay guarded by a ruined castle on its own headland.

None of these have a promenade, an arcade, or a chip shop within easy reach — pack a lunch, check the tide times, and expect the sand mostly to yourself.
