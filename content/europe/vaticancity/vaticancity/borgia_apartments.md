A suite of six rooms on the floor below the Raphael Rooms, decorated for the Borgia pope Alexander VI by Pinturicchio and his workshop between 1492 and 1494. After Alexander's death, the rooms were closed off — the Borgia name had become so toxic that subsequent popes preferred not to live in their predecessor's chambers — and they remained largely sealed for centuries.

The frescoes are dense, colourful, and full of Borgia symbolism: the family bull appears in coats of arms; the figures bear the faces of Borgia children and their entourage. The most famous panel is the *Disputation of St. Catherine* in the Hall of the Saints, where Catherine debates with the philosophers and is widely believed to bear the face of Lucrezia Borgia.

The apartments now form part of the modern religious art collection. They sit just before the Sistine Chapel on the standard museum route — easy to miss if you are pushing through, but worth slowing down for.
