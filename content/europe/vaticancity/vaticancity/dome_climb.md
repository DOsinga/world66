The dome of St. Peter's was designed by Michelangelo and completed (with modifications) by Giacomo della Porta in 1590. At 136 metres from the floor of the basilica to the top of the cross, it is the tallest dome in the world. From inside the basilica, the dome rises above the high altar; the inscription around the base — *TU ES PETRUS ET SUPER HANC PETRAM AEDIFICABO ECCLESIAM MEAM* — is two metres tall.

The climb to the top is one of the great Roman experiences. A lift takes you to the roof terrace, from which 320 steps lead up through the inner shell of the dome to a viewing balcony at the lantern. The stairs spiral inside the curving wall and tilt with the dome; in places the passages narrow to single-file with handrails on either side. Not recommended for the claustrophobic.

The view from the top is unmatched — the whole of Rome laid out, with the Vatican Gardens behind you and the city in front. Tickets are bought at the entrance inside the basilica portico. Combine with a basilica visit; the dome climb cannot be done independently.
