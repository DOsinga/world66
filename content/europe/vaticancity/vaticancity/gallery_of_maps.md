A long, brilliantly lit corridor on the third floor of the Vatican Museums, lined with 40 large topographical maps of Italy frescoed onto the walls between 1580 and 1583. Pope Gregory XIII commissioned them from the Perugian geographer and cosmographer Ignazio Danti.

Each panel shows a region of Italy — the maps are oriented so that the corridor itself runs along the spine of the country, with the Tyrrhenian coast on one wall and the Adriatic on the other. The detail is extraordinary: cities are shown in elevation, ships sail in the seas, classical battles are depicted in the surrounding cartouches. The barrel-vaulted ceiling above is gilded with stuccoes and scenes from Italian church history.

The gallery comes near the end of the museum route, shortly before the Sistine Chapel — and is one of the rooms where guided groups tend to stop and explain things, so it can be a bottleneck. Walk slowly enough to read the maps, even if you can't pause.
