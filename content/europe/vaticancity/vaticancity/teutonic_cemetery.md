A small German-speaking enclave tucked just south of St. Peter's Basilica, with a cemetery that has been in continuous use since around 799, when Charlemagne reportedly granted the site to a Frankish religious community. The Campo Santo Teutonico is the burial ground of the College of German Priests in Rome and is bordered by a tiny baroque church, Santa Maria della Pietà.

The cemetery is roughly the size of two tennis courts but feels much larger because of the dense planting of cypresses, oleanders and roses. Tombs include those of priests, religious sisters, and a handful of notable expatriates from Austria, Germany, the Netherlands, Belgium and Switzerland — eligibility for burial is based on connection to the German college rather than nationality alone.

To enter, you need to ring at the small Sant'Anna or Petriano gate and explain you wish to visit (the gatekeeper has discretion). Dress neatly and behave as you would in any active cemetery. Mass is celebrated in the chapel several times a week.
