The 25-metre red granite obelisk in the centre of St. Peter's Square was quarried at Aswan around 2,000 BC and shipped to Rome by Caligula in 37 AD to decorate his private circus on the Vatican Hill. It is the only obelisk in Rome that has never fallen — it has stood continuously for nearly two thousand years.

A bronze cross was added to the top in 1586, with a small reliquary said to contain a fragment of the True Cross. The four bronze lions at the base are by Prospero Antichi. The pavement around the obelisk is marked with a wind rose and the points of the compass, and at midday a shadow line on the ground turns the whole square into a vast sundial.
