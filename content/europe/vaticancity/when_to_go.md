---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

Vatican City shares Rome's Mediterranean climate — mild rainy winters and hot dry summers — but the deciding factor for a visit is crowds, not weather.

**Spring (April–May)** and **autumn (September–October)** are ideal: pleasant temperatures, the basilicas are bearable without air conditioning, and the queues are at least manageable.

**Summer (June–August)** is hot — over 30°C is common — and St Peter's Square in midday sun is brutal. The Vatican Museums are slow-moving and stuffy.

**Winter (November–March)** is quiet, with cool damp days but rarely freezing. The Christmas Mass at St Peter's and the lighting of the tree and nativity in late December draw large crowds; otherwise December–February is the cheapest and least crowded period.

**Avoid Easter week and the days around Christmas** unless the religious dimension is the reason for your trip. The Vatican Museums close on Sundays except for the last Sunday of the month, when entry is free and queues run for hours.

The **Wednesday Papal Audience** in St Peter's Square (or the Paul VI Hall in winter) draws crowds year-round; book free tickets in advance via the Prefecture of the Papal Household.
