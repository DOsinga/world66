Antigua and Barbuda is a twin-island nation in the northern Leeward Islands, known primarily for its beaches — 365 of them, so the tourism boards claim, one for each day of the year. Most visitors come for [English Harbour](/northamerica/antiguaandbarbuda/englishharbour), the restored 18th-century Royal Navy dockyard that is now a yacht marina and one of the most charming historic sites in the Caribbean, and for the sailing — Antigua Sailing Week each April is one of the world's great regattas.

[St. John's](/northamerica/antiguaandbarbuda/stjohns), the capital, is a small city with a covered market and a pastel-coloured waterfront. [Barbuda](/northamerica/antiguaandbarbuda/barbuda), 48km to the north, is quieter, flatter, and almost entirely undeveloped — large frigate bird sanctuary, pink-tinted beaches, and very little else. Hurricane Irma devastated Barbuda in 2017 and the island was fully evacuated; rebuilding has been slow.
