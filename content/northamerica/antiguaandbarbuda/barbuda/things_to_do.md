---
title: "Things to Do"
type: section
---

Barbuda's attractions are almost entirely natural. The beaches on the western shore are among the finest in the Caribbean — long, virtually empty, and backed by scrub rather than resort hotels. The Codrington Lagoon frigate bird colony is one of the great wildlife spectacles of the region. Beyond those two headline experiences, the island rewards slow exploration: limestone caves, salt ponds, and the ruins of 18th-century colonial structures scattered across the scrubland.

Guided boat tours into the Codrington Lagoon mangroves are the standard way to see the frigates. Local boatmen can be arranged through the few guesthouses on the island or by asking in Codrington village. Half a day is enough for the lagoon; a full day lets you combine it with a drive to the eastern beaches and the Barbuda Highlands.
