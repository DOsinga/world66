Clarence House is a Georgian colonial mansion built in 1804 on the hill above Ordnance Bay in English Harbour, originally constructed to receive the Royal Navy's senior officers and their guests — including, on at least one occasion, the Duke of Clarence (later William IV), who served in the Caribbean as a naval officer. The house was subsequently used as a summer residence by Antigua's governors, and Prince Charles and Princess Diana stayed here on their 1985 tour of the Caribbean.

The building has been preserved with period furnishings and is open as a museum and event space. The architecture is typical of British colonial construction in the tropics: a two-storey wooden structure with wide verandas to catch the breeze, set in a garden with views over the harbour.
