---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

Antigua and Barbuda enjoy one of the sunniest, driest climates in the eastern Caribbean. The islands sit slightly outside the wettest band of the trade-wind belt, and Antigua's 365 beaches see less rain than many of their neighbours.

**Dry season (December–April)** is high season for good reason. Warm days (27–29°C), steady trade winds, blue water, and reliably sunny weather. The flagship event is **Antigua Sailing Week** in late April — one of the world's premier regattas, with the harbour at English Harbour packed with classic yachts.

**Shoulder (May–June and November)** offers similar weather with much lower prices and far fewer cruise passengers in the capital.

**Wet/hurricane season (July–October)** is wetter but still mostly sunny. The hurricane window peaks in September; Barbuda was devastated by Hurricane Irma in 2017 and is still recovering.

**Antigua Carnival** runs the last week of July into the first week of August — the islands' biggest cultural festival.

**Frigatebird nesting** in Codrington Lagoon, Barbuda — one of the world's largest colonies — peaks in the dry season; chicks are visible **September–April**.
