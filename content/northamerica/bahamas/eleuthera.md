Eleuthera is a long, narrow island in the Bahamas, stretching about 180 km from north to south but rarely more than 2 km wide. It's one of the Out Islands — quieter and less developed than Nassau or the resort towns of the Exumas — and known for some of the finest pink-sand beaches in the Caribbean.

The island has three main towns: [Governor's Harbour](/northamerica/bahamas/eleuthera/governors_harbour) in the centre, which has the only airport with regular direct flights from the US; [Dunmore Town](/northamerica/bahamas/eleuthera/dunmore_town) on Harbour Island just off the north tip, the most visited and most expensive corner of the island; and [Rock Sound](/northamerica/bahamas/eleuthera/rock_sound) in the south. Each section has its own character. Harbour Island is famous for its [pink beach](/northamerica/bahamas/eleuthera/pink_sands_beach) and upmarket hideaways; the central island around Governor's Harbour is more low-key and local; the southern tip near [Cape Eleuthera](/northamerica/bahamas/eleuthera/cape_eleuthera_ruins) is almost completely undeveloped.

Pineapple farming once defined the island's economy and the annual Pineapple Festival is still held in [Gregory Town](/northamerica/bahamas/eleuthera/gregory_town) each June. The [Glass Window Bridge](/northamerica/bahamas/eleuthera/glass_window_bridge), at the island's narrowest point, lets you see the Atlantic's deep blue and the pale turquoise Bight of Eleuthera at the same time from a single short bridge.
