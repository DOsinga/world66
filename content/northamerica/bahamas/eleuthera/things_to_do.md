---
title: "Things to Do"
type: section
---

Eleuthera is mostly about beaches and water — but for an island this size there is more variety than people expect. The headline sight is **Harbour Island's Pink Sands Beach**, three miles of pale rose-tinted sand on the north-eastern tip. The **Glass Window Bridge** at the island's narrowest point gives the iconic Eleuthera view: deep Atlantic blue on one side, pale turquoise Bight of Eleuthera on the other.

Beyond the famous bits, the island rewards slow exploration. The **Ocean Hole** at Rock Sound is a deep inland blue hole connected to the sea, full of fish. The **Cape Eleuthera Resort Ruins** are an atmospheric reminder of failed 1970s glamour, slowly being reclaimed by the bush. **Preacher's Cave** in the north is where Captain William Sayle and his "Eleutheran Adventurers" sheltered after a 1648 shipwreck — the founding event of Bahamian Protestantism. The **Lighthouse Beach** at the southern tip and the **Surfer's Beach** north of Gregory Town are among the wildest stretches.

For activity, this is excellent water — the **Devil's Backbone reef** off the north shore, the **Current Cut** between North Eleuthera and Current Island, and the Atlantic-side breaks for surfers. The annual **Pineapple Festival** in Gregory Town (early June) is the local cultural highlight.
