  The underwater caves at Lucayan National Park form one of the longest known underwater cave systems in the world — more than 36 kilometres of mapped passages. The caves were used by the Lucayan Taino people, whose bones and artefacts have been found in the dry upper chambers, providing some of the most complete evidence of their culture anywhere in the Bahamas.

About 40 kilometres east of Freeport, Lucayan National Park packs six distinct ecosystems into 40 acres: ocean holes, tidal creeks, mangrove forests, rocky coppice, pine forest, and beach. The underwater cave system is the headline attraction — experienced divers can enter the caves through two ocean holes, while non-divers can explore the upper dry chambers on a boardwalk that winds through the mangroves.

The tidal creek can be explored by kayak — rentals are available nearby — and the mangroves are excellent for birds, including herons, ospreys, and a variety of waders. Gold Rock Beach at the park's southern end is one of the finest beaches on Grand Bahama: a long crescent of pale sand, usually uncrowded, backed by dunes and pine forest. Combine Lucayan with a stop at the small community of Freetown on the way back for conch fritters at a local stand.
