A 100-acre national park less than a kilometre from Freeport's city centre, the Rand Nature Centre is a manageable introduction to the flora and fauna of Grand Bahama. Nature trails wind through mature pine forest, passing ponds where herons and shorebirds feed, and the interpretive displays identify the native plants along the way — over 130 species grow here.

The main attraction for most visitors is birds. The Bahama parrot is the star, but the site also reliably produces West Indian woodpeckers, Cuban emerald hummingbirds, and various warblers during migration. A visit in the early morning is worthwhile — the birds are active and the heat is manageable. The centre is run by the Bahamas National Trust, and the modest entry fee goes towards conservation.
