Nassau is the capital of the Bahamas, sitting on New Providence Island in the northwest of the archipelago. It's a city of real contrasts: colonial-era pastel buildings and cobbled streets in the old [downtown](/northamerica/bahamas/nassau/downtown), massive cruise ship terminals crowding the waterfront, and a string of resort hotels strung along [Cable Beach](/northamerica/bahamas/nassau/cable_beach) to the west.

Most visitors come for the beaches, and they deliver. The water is genuinely turquoise, the sand is white, and the sea temperature is warm most of the year. [Cabbage Beach](/northamerica/bahamas/nassau/cabbage_beach) on [Paradise Island](/northamerica/bahamas/nassau/paradise_island) is the picture-postcard one; the giant [Atlantis](/northamerica/bahamas/nassau/atlantis_paradise_island) resort and its [aquarium tunnels](/northamerica/bahamas/nassau/the_dig) sit just behind it.

But Nassau has more going on than beach time. The old town is walkable, with the colonial pink-and-white core around [Parliament Square](/northamerica/bahamas/nassau/parliament_square), [Fort Fincastle](/northamerica/bahamas/nassau/fort_fincastle) on its hill, the slave-cut [Queen's Staircase](/northamerica/bahamas/nassau/queens_staircase), a proper [Straw Market](/northamerica/bahamas/nassau/straw_market), and a handful of good museums — the [Pirates of Nassau](/northamerica/bahamas/nassau/pirates_of_nassau) and the sober [Pompey Museum of Slavery](/northamerica/bahamas/nassau/pompey_museum) chief among them.

The food capital is [Arawak Cay](/northamerica/bahamas/nassau/arawak_cay) — a strip of open-air seafood shacks where Nassau itself eats and where the conch salad is prepared in front of you. The [Junkanoo](/northamerica/bahamas/nassau/junkanoo_parade) festival in December and January is one of the Caribbean's great street spectacles — loud, colorful, and entirely the Bahamian thing.
