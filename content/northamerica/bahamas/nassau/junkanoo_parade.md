  Junkanoo's origins lie in the very limited freedom that enslaved Africans had during the Christmas season: three days off, no work, and permission to drum. The Bahamians built an art form out of that gap and have not let it go since. The biggest costumes — "leads" — can weigh over 90 kilograms and are worn by a single dancer for the eight-hour parade.

Twice a year, on **Boxing Day** (December 26) and **New Year's morning**, downtown Nassau closes for the **Junkanoo parade**. Thousands of performers in costumes built from coloured crepe paper, cardboard, and wire process along Bay Street to the rhythm of goatskin drums, cowbells, and brass — competing in formal categories for cash prizes, but mostly drumming for the sake of it.

The parade starts around 2 am and continues until well after dawn; the costumes are best seen at sunrise when the gold-leaf and feathers catch the light. Grandstand tickets are available from the Bahamas Tourist Office; the street is free but loud. A smaller **Junkanoo Summer Festival** runs Saturday nights in July at Arawak Cay for those who cannot be in Nassau over Christmas.
