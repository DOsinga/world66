  The 65 steps were carved out of solid limestone by enslaved African labourers in the late 18th century, providing the only direct route from downtown to Fort Fincastle. They were renamed in honour of Queen Victoria after the abolition of slavery in the British Empire, an act she signed into law.
---

A narrow passage cut through limestone, with sheer rock walls rising twelve metres on either side, leads up from West Hill Street to the foot of Fort Fincastle. A small artificial waterfall trickles down one side; ferns and tropical plants grow from the cracks. It is genuinely cool in the cleft even at midday, which is half the reason cruise passengers walk it.

The steps are a few minutes' walk south from Bay Street. Vendors at the bottom sell water, straw hats, and unwelcome hair-braiding services. The history is heavier than the average cruise-port attraction — slaves cut the rock; the name commemorates the woman who freed them — and worth a moment's thought before climbing.
