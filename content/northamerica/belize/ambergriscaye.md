Ambergris Caye is the largest and most visited of Belize's cayes, running 56 km along the northeastern coast. The main town, San Pedro, has grown from a quiet fishing village into Belize's main tourist hub, with resorts, dive shops, restaurants, and bars lining its sandy streets. Golf carts are the primary transport — cars are rare on the island.

The reef is the main attraction. [Hol Chan Marine Reserve](/northamerica/belize/ambergriscaye/hol_chan_marine_reserve) and [Shark Ray Alley](/northamerica/belize/ambergriscaye/shark_ray_alley), just south of the island, offer some of the most accessible reef diving and snorkelling in the Caribbean: nurse sharks, rays, and hundreds of reef fish in clear, calm water. More experienced divers head further out to the Blue Hole and Turneffe Islands.

[San Pedro](/northamerica/belize/ambergriscaye/san_pedro_town) gets busy and can feel overdeveloped compared to neighbouring [Caye Caulker](/northamerica/belize/cayecaulker). But the beach is good, the diving is excellent, and the range of accommodation — from budget guesthouses to upscale resorts — means it suits most budgets. Water taxis and domestic flights connect to Belize City multiple times daily. For a more secluded beach, [Secret Beach](/northamerica/belize/ambergriscaye/secret_beach) on the western side of the island has calm, shallow turquoise water and a handful of beach bars.
