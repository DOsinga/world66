---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

Belize City sits in the tropics and has a classic wet/dry seasonal split. The dry season runs roughly November to April — warm, clear, and good for diving, snorkelling, and inland wildlife. February and March are often the most reliably dry months. Humidity is high year-round, but sea breezes moderate the heat in the city.

The wet season (May–October) brings afternoon downpours, higher humidity, and the threat of hurricanes. Hurricane season peaks in September and October. The city has been badly damaged before — Hurricane Hattie in 1961 caused such destruction that the capital was moved inland to Belmopan — so if you're in the region during peak season, keep an eye on weather systems.

Even in the wet season, mornings are often clear and travel is quite feasible. Prices are lower and the tourist sites emptier. The reef is always there.
