  The building was designed to evoke a Mayan temple — low, monumental, and set within a formal plaza. When the capital was relocated inland after Hurricane Hattie in 1961, the government chose this site and commissioned architecture meant to signal permanence and indigenous identity, even as most Belizeans refused to follow and stayed in Belize City.
---

The home of Belize's bicameral parliament — the House of Representatives and the Senate — sits at the heart of Belmopan's government quarter. The building is deliberately reminiscent of Mayan architecture, with a low stepped profile and a ceremonial plaza in front. It is the physical embodiment of the new capital's founding ambition.

Public access is limited, but the exterior and the plaza around it are worth a look. The surrounding government complex, built largely in the 1960s and 1970s, has a certain retro-functionalist quality — an entire capital conceived from scratch, all at once. Few countries have attempted that experiment and survived it.
