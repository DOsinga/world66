Corozal is a small, quiet town on the northern coast near the Mexican border, overlooking [Corozal Bay](/northamerica/belize/corozaltown/corozal_bay_waterfront). It's not a major tourist destination but is pleasant enough — laid-back, relatively safe, and useful as a stopping point if you're crossing into Mexico at Chetumal.

The town has a Maya heritage that runs deep. The town itself sits on top of the ancient city of Chactemal, whose surviving ruin at [Santa Rita](/northamerica/belize/corozaltown/santa_rita_ruins) is a 20-minute walk from the centre, and the rare late pre-Classic site of [Cerros](/northamerica/belize/corozaltown/cerros_maya_site) is a short boat ride across the bay. The [Corozal House of Culture](/northamerica/belize/corozaltown/corozal_house_of_culture) on the waterfront, housed in the old 1886 market building, holds the local history exhibits.

Modern Corozal was effectively founded by Yucatec Maya refugees fleeing the Caste War of Yucatan in the late 1840s, and Spanish remains the main language on the streets — alongside Belizean Kriol and English. The town was rebuilt after Hurricane Janet flattened most of it in 1955, which is why the streets are wide and the buildings mostly low and modern.

Buses run regularly to [Belize City](/northamerica/belize/belizecity) (about three and a half hours via Orange Walk), and the Mexican border at Santa Elena/Subteniente López is 15 km north.
