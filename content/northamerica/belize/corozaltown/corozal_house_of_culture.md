Housed in the old market building on the seafront — an iron-roofed wooden structure dating from 1886 and one of the few colonial buildings in Corozal to survive Hurricane Janet in 1955 — the House of Culture serves as a small community museum and cultural centre.

The permanent display covers Corozal District history: the influx of Yucatec Maya refugees during the Caste War of Yucatan in the 1840s and 1850s (which essentially founded the town), the sugar industry that long dominated the local economy, and the Maya archaeology of the area. Rotating exhibitions cover Belizean art, photography, and craft.

It's small, and admission is free. The historic building itself is reason enough to drop in for half an hour.
