Crooked Tree is a lagoon village in northern Belize where the attraction is not the town grid but the water and birds around it. The village sits inside the [Crooked Tree Wildlife Sanctuary](/northamerica/belize/crooked_tree/crooked_tree_wildlife_sanctuary), one of the easiest places in the country to see wetland birdlife without committing to a full jungle expedition.

The sanctuary is the reason to come. In the dry season, birds gather around the shrinking lagoons in large numbers, and the jabiru stork is the headline species. You can see plenty from the village edges, but a [lagoon boat trip](/northamerica/belize/crooked_tree/lagoon_boat_trips) with a local guide gives a much better chance of seeing the wider wetland.

Crooked Tree is rough around the edges in useful ways. Roads can turn ugly after rain, the pace is slow, and the village still feels like a place where people live beside the sanctuary rather than perform it for visitors. Add the remote Maya site of [Chau Hiix](/northamerica/belize/crooked_tree/chau_hiix), and Crooked Tree easily earns a day from Belize City, Orange Walk, or the Northern Highway.
