St. George's is Bermuda's old town, settled in 1612 and still tight with lanes, churches, stone buildings, and harbour edges. It is the place on the island where Bermuda's long Atlantic history is easiest to feel under your feet rather than read on a panel.

The town is small enough to walk without a plan, but it has real depth. [King's Square](/northamerica/bermuda/st_georges/kings_square) gives you the centre, [St. Peter's Church](/northamerica/bermuda/st_georges/st_peters_church) carries four centuries of Anglican history, and the [Unfinished Church](/northamerica/bermuda/st_georges/unfinished_church) adds a romantic ruin that would feel overdone if it were not real. Up the hill and out toward the coast, [Fort St. Catherine](/northamerica/bermuda/st_georges/fort_st_catherine) explains why this end of Bermuda mattered militarily.

It is a strong day even for travelers based elsewhere on the island: morning in the streets and museums, lunch near the harbour, then a walk or bus out to Tobacco Bay or the fort. Hamilton is busier, but St. George's is where Bermuda feels oldest.
