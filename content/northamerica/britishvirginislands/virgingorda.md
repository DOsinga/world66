Virgin Gorda is the third largest of the [British Virgin Islands](/northamerica/britishvirginislands) and the most distinctive — a long, dragon-shaped sliver of granite and limestone running for ten miles from the airport at the south end to North Sound at the top. Christopher Columbus sighted it in 1493 and named it the "fat virgin" because of the round, reclining profile of the central hill (Gorda Peak), which from the sea genuinely does look like a sleeping body. The island is more spacious and less crowded than Tortola and rewards visitors with two of the most extraordinary natural features in the Caribbean.

The southern third is where most people stay. [Spanish Town](/northamerica/britishvirginislands/virgingorda/spanish_town) (also called The Valley) is the small administrative centre, with the ferry dock, the airport, the main supermarket, and a strip of low-rise shops and bars. A 20-minute walk south of town is [The Baths](/northamerica/britishvirginislands/virgingorda/the_baths) — Virgin Gorda's most famous attraction and one of the great Caribbean landscapes — a beach scattered with house-sized granite boulders that form natural pools, grottoes and ladder-climbing routes through the rock. The path through the boulders to neighbouring Devil's Bay is one of the best short walks in the West Indies. Just south is the [Copper Mine National Park](/northamerica/britishvirginislands/virgingorda/copper_mine), a 19th-century Cornish mining ruin perched on the cliffs.

The middle of the island is dominated by [Gorda Peak National Park](/northamerica/britishvirginislands/virgingorda/gorda_peak_national_park) — a 265-acre reserve of subtropical dry forest, with the highest point on Virgin Gorda (414 metres) at its centre. The summit is reached by an easy 45-minute trail and gives one of the best panoramas in the BVI. Most of the road north from Spanish Town runs along the east coast and passes a string of secluded beaches — Savannah Bay, Pond Bay, Mahoe Bay — that are usually empty.

[North Sound](/northamerica/britishvirginislands/virgingorda/north_sound), the wide protected bay at the top of the island, is the principal sailing destination in the BVI. The road runs out at Gun Creek; the rest of North Sound — including the resort enclaves of Leverick Bay, the [Bitter End Yacht Club](/northamerica/britishvirginislands/virgingorda/bitter_end_yacht_club), Saba Rock and the private islands of Necker and Mosquito — is accessed by boat. North Sound is sheltered on three sides and ringed by reefs, with consistent trade winds and outstanding snorkelling. It is where many BVI charter boats end up.
