In 1907 and 1908, Mary Schaffer — a Quaker woman from Philadelphia who had spent years botanising in the Rockies — led an expedition on horseback deep into unmapped territory, guided by Indigenous Stoney Nakoda maps drawn from memory. She was searching for Maligne Lake, a place described in oral accounts but unknown to white maps. She found it. Her account of the journey is modest, precise, and beautifully observed, and a reminder that the mountains were well known to their original inhabitants long before the railway arrived.
