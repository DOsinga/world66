The second volume of Berton's epic account of the Canadian Pacific Railway follows the building of over 2,000 miles of track through the Rockies and across the prairies in just five years — exactly half the contracted time. It is a story of engineering audacity, brutal labour, financial near-ruin, and the political maneuvering that held a nascent Canada together. Berton writes popular history at its best: vivid, paced like a novel, and grounded in human stories. The railway created Banff National Park — it was the CPR that lobbied to protect the hot springs that had drawn its workers. Understanding that connection transforms how you look at the town.
