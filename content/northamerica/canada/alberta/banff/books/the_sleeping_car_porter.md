Baxter is a Black Canadian working as a sleeping-car porter on the transcontinental railway in 1929, saving to attend dentistry school, trying to be invisible to passengers who treat him as furniture. When a mudslide stalls the train in the Rockies for two days, things begin to unravel. Winner of the 2022 Giller Prize, the novel uses the trapped train as a container for a wider story about race, sexuality, and what it cost to build and ride the Canadian Pacific. It is the finest fiction to emerge from the railway that created Banff, and essential reading before you travel through the mountains by rail.
