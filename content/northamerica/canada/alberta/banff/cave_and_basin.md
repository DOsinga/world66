The hot springs discovered in 1883 by three Canadian Pacific Railway workers — and the direct cause of Canada's national parks system being established. The sulphur springs bubble up at around 31 degrees Celsius in a cave that has been visited since the 1880s, when wooden bathing pools were built for the arriving tourists. The pools closed to swimmers in 1993 due to degradation of the unique warm-water ecosystem that had developed in the outflow channel — including several endemic species found nowhere else. The site is now a museum and national historic site, with exhibits on the parks history and guided tours of the cave and original outdoor pool.
