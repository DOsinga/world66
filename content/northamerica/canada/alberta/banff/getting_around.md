---
title: "Getting Around"
type: section
---

Banff town itself is walkable — most restaurants, bars, and shops are within a few blocks of each other on Banff Avenue and the surrounding streets. The Roam Transit system runs electric buses within Banff and between Banff, Lake Louise, and Canmore; it is free to use within the town zone and inexpensive for longer routes.

A rental car is the most practical option for exploring beyond the immediate town — the Bow Valley Parkway, Icefields Parkway, Lake Louise, Moraine Lake, and Johnston Canyon all require driving or joining a bus tour. Moraine Lake Road is closed to private vehicles in peak summer season (June to October); access is by shuttle or Parks Canada reservation.

Cycling is excellent on the Bow Valley Parkway (a quieter alternative to the Trans-Canada) and around the Vermilion Lakes. Bike rentals are available from several shops on Bear Street.

In winter, ski shuttles connect Banff Avenue hotels to the three ski resorts (Lake Louise, Sunshine Village, and Mt. Norquay). Cars are fine but the shuttle makes parking at the resorts unnecessary.
