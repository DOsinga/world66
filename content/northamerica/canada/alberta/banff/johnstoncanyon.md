story: >
  The canyon is named for William "Billy" Johnston, a prospector who camped at
  the creek head in 1885 hoping to pan gold from the canyon silt. He found
  none and moved on, but the name stuck. The metal catwalks bolted to the
  canyon walls — which feel precarious but have been here since the 1910s —
  were built specifically to give tourists access to the waterfalls that the
  narrow gorge would otherwise make unreachable. At the Ink Pots above the
  upper falls, six cold springs push glacial sediment to the surface
  year-round, keeping the pools a vivid aquamarine even in winter.

A self-guided interpretive trail follows Johnston Creek through a narrow limestone gorge, passing two dramatic waterfalls before opening onto the Upper Canyon. The full return walk to the Ink Pots is 11.2 km (7 mi). Six cold springs bubble out of the earth year-round, their glacial sediment turning the pools a vivid aquamarine. Can be busy in summer — early morning is the best time to go.
