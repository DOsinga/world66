Crowsnest Pass is a string of old coal towns along Highway 3 in south-western [Alberta](/northamerica/canada/alberta), pressed between the Rockies and the British Columbia line. It is not a single tidy town so much as a mountain corridor: Bellevue, Hillcrest, Frank, Blairmore, and Coleman, each with pieces of mining history and big country around them.

The place has one unforgettable sight. At [Frank Slide](/northamerica/canada/alberta/crowsnest_pass/frank_slide_interpretive_centre), Turtle Mountain broke loose in 1903 and sent a vast field of limestone over part of the town below. The rubble still lies across the valley, making the disaster feel less like a story than a landscape.

Crowsnest Pass is strongest if you like industrial history with mountain air. [Bellevue Underground Mine](/northamerica/canada/alberta/crowsnest_pass/bellevue_underground_mine) takes visitors into a former coal mine, [Leitch Collieries](/northamerica/canada/alberta/crowsnest_pass/leitch_collieries) preserves the ruins of an ambitious early-1900s operation, and the [Crowsnest Museum](/northamerica/canada/alberta/crowsnest_pass/crowsnest_museum) ties the scattered communities together.

It is a good spare day from Waterton Lakes, Pincher Creek, or a Highway 3 road trip. Come expecting history, walks, and views rather than a polished resort town.
