Edmonton is the capital city of Alberta and sits further north than Moscow — a fact that shapes everything about it, from the design of its buildings to the particular intensity of its summer festival season. It is a city of about one million people, built on both banks of a deep river valley, with a cultural life that repeatedly surprises visitors who arrive expecting a resource industry town and nothing more.

The [North Saskatchewan River Valley](/northamerica/canada/alberta/edmonton/north_saskatchewan_river_valley) is the city's defining feature — North America's largest urban park system, a continuous ribbon of trails and ravines cutting through the middle of the city. Above it, the downtown cultural quarter clusters around [Churchill Square](/northamerica/canada/alberta/edmonton/churchill_square): the [Royal Alberta Museum](/northamerica/canada/alberta/edmonton/royal_alberta_museum) (the largest in western Canada), the [Art Gallery of Alberta](/northamerica/canada/alberta/edmonton/art_gallery_of_alberta) in its dramatic stainless steel building, and the [Winspear Centre](/northamerica/canada/alberta/edmonton/winspear_centre) concert hall. South of the river, the [Alberta Legislature Building](/northamerica/canada/alberta/edmonton/legislature_building) occupies the original site of Fort Edmonton, and [Fort Edmonton Park](/northamerica/canada/alberta/edmonton/fort_edmonton_park) nearby recreates the city's history across four historical eras.

Edmonton is known internationally for its summer festivals — the [Edmonton International Fringe Theatre Festival](/northamerica/canada/alberta/edmonton/edmonton_fringe_festival) in August is the second-largest fringe in the world after Edinburgh, and the [Edmonton Folk Music Festival](/northamerica/canada/alberta/edmonton/edmonton_folk_music_festival) draws an audience that camps on a hillside above the river valley for four days. The city has a deeply embedded culture of outdoor events that runs from May through September as compensation for the winters.

[West Edmonton Mall](/northamerica/canada/alberta/edmonton/west_edmonton_mall) is impossible to ignore — for two decades it was the largest mall in the world, and it contains an amusement park, a water park, an ice rink, and a replica of Columbus's Santa Maria alongside the 800 shops. Sports anchor the rest of the city's identity: the Edmonton Oilers, whose four Stanley Cup championships in the 1980s under Wayne Gretzky define the city's sporting mythology, play at [Rogers Place](/northamerica/canada/alberta/edmonton/rogers_place) in the Ice District.

[Old Strathcona](/northamerica/canada/alberta/edmonton/old_strathcona) — the neighbourhood south of the river along Whyte Avenue — is where the independent restaurants, cafés, bars, and live music venues concentrate. It is the most animated part of the city on an ordinary evening, and becomes extraordinary during Fringe.
