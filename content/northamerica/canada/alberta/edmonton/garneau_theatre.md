The Garneau Theatre on 109 Street in Old Strathcona has been showing films since 1940, making it one of Edmonton's oldest operating cinemas. The building is a study in Art Moderne design — the streamlined, functional evolution of Art Deco that was fashionable in the years just before the Second World War. The curved corners, the horizontal banding, and the distinctive backlit marquee are all intact.

The Garneau screens a mix of independent films, foreign-language cinema, and classics alongside mainstream releases — a programming style that has kept it relevant in a multiplex age. The interior retains period details that most cinema refurbishments would have long since ripped out. It is small by modern standards, with a single screen, which makes it feel like going to the cinema felt sixty years ago.

The surrounding neighbourhood, Old Strathcona, is Edmonton's most interesting district — independent restaurants, coffee shops, the Fringe Theatre, and a street life that the downtown core often lacks. The theatre sits at the heart of it.
