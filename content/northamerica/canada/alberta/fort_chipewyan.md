*North America > Canada > Alberta > Fort Chipewyan*

Fort Chipewyan, usually shortened to Fort Chip, sits on the western edge of Lake Athabasca beside [Wood Buffalo National Park](/northamerica/canada/alberta/wood_buffalo_national_park). It is one of Alberta's oldest European-founded settlements, but its story is not only European: Cree, Dene, and Metis communities have shaped this place for far longer than the fur-trade plaques suggest.

The town is remote in a way that changes the visit. In summer you arrive by air or water; in winter, by ice road when conditions allow. That isolation helps preserve the feeling of a northern lake settlement, with the Canadian Shield underfoot, huge water at the edge of town, and a history tied to canoes, trading posts, missions, and the routes north.

The [Fort Chipewyan Bicentennial Museum](/northamerica/canada/alberta/fort_chipewyan/fort_chipewyan_bicentennial_museum) is the right place to start. From there, walk the historic church sites and the Lake Athabasca shore, then use local guidance if you are heading into Wood Buffalo or out on the water. Fort Chip is not a casual roadside stop, but if you are already in the region, it has a strong enough identity to deserve the day.
