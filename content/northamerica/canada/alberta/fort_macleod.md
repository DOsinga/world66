Fort Macleod is one of southern Alberta's better small-town stops, with a real historic spine and a UNESCO site close enough to shape the whole visit. It began as a North-West Mounted Police post, and that frontier history still gives the town its strongest identity.

The essential trip is [Head-Smashed-In Buffalo Jump](/northamerica/canada/alberta/fort_macleod/head_smashed_in_buffalo_jump), west of town, where the Blackfoot and other Plains peoples used the cliffs for thousands of years. In town, the [Fort Museum](/northamerica/canada/alberta/fort_macleod/fort_museum_of_the_north_west_mounted_police) covers the NWMP story, [Historic Main Street](/northamerica/canada/alberta/fort_macleod/historic_main_street) preserves a handsome run of sandstone and brick buildings, and the [Empress Theatre](/northamerica/canada/alberta/fort_macleod/empress_theatre) still anchors the centre.

Fort Macleod works well as a day from Lethbridge, Calgary, or Waterton country. It is small, but the combination of Blackfoot history, prairie policing, and intact main-street architecture is strong enough to justify the stop.
