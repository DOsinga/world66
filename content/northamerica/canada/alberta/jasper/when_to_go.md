---
title: When to Go
type: section
---

July and August are peak season: everything is open, the weather is reliably warm, and the park is genuinely crowded. Parking at major sights like Maligne Lake and the Athabasca Glacier fills before noon. June and September are significantly quieter and often just as beautiful, with the added bonus of wildlife more active at roadsides and trails less packed with people.

Late September and October bring the elk rut, when bulls move into the townsite and surrounding meadows and their bugling calls carry through the valley at dawn. It is one of the more primal experiences available within walking distance of a Tim Hortons.

Jasper has been designated a Dark Sky Preserve, one of the largest in the world, making autumn and winter exceptional for stargazing. The Milky Way is clearly visible on clear nights away from the townsite. Winter brings its own pleasures — backcountry skiing, frozen canyon walks, and a fraction of the summer crowds — but requires proper preparation. The Icefields Parkway can close without notice in severe weather, and winter driving demands experience and equipped vehicles.
