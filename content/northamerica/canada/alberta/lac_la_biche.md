*North America > Canada > Alberta > Lac La Biche*

Lac La Biche is a small northern Alberta hamlet on a big lake of the same name, about 220 km northeast of [Edmonton](/northamerica/canada/alberta/edmonton). It is not a polished resort town, and that is part of the appeal: the place still feels tied to Cree, Metis, fur-trade, fishing, and forestry history rather than to weekend-cottage gloss.

The strongest reason to stop is the lake country around town. [Sir Winston Churchill Provincial Park](/northamerica/canada/alberta/lac_la_biche/sir_winston_churchill_provincial_park) protects island forest and beaches just east of the hamlet, while [Lakeland Provincial Park](/northamerica/canada/alberta/lac_la_biche/lakeland_provincial_park) is wilder backcountry farther east. Both make Lac La Biche a good base if you want canoeing, birding, winter skiing, or a quiet day by the water.

The human history is just as important. [Lac La Biche Mission](/northamerica/canada/alberta/lac_la_biche/lac_la_biche_mission), founded in the 1850s, preserves one of western Canada's important Oblate mission sites, and the Portage College museum in town holds a serious collection of Indigenous art and artifacts. Come for a day if you are already travelling through Alberta's northeast; stay longer if the lake and boreal forest are the point of the trip.
