Medicine Hat sits in the dry southeast corner of Alberta, perched above the South Saskatchewan River valley. Rudyard Kipling famously described it as a city "with all hell for a basement" — a reference to the vast natural gas reserves that lie beneath it and gave the city its nickname, the Gas City.

The setting is dramatic prairie: wide skies, coulees, and the river cutting through town. The [Medalta](/northamerica/canada/alberta/medicinehat/medalta_potteries) historic clay district preserves the old pottery factories that once made Medicine Hat a ceramics centre, and the riverside parks — [Police Point](/northamerica/canada/alberta/medicinehat/police_point_park) to the north, [Echo Dale](/northamerica/canada/alberta/medicinehat/echo_dale_regional_park) to the west — are pleasant for walking and (in summer) swimming. The [Esplanade Arts Centre](/northamerica/canada/alberta/medicinehat/esplanade_arts_centre) is the city's main cultural building, and the giant [Saamis Tepee](/northamerica/canada/alberta/medicinehat/saamis_tepee) standing alongside the Trans-Canada Highway is the most photographed landmark.

Medicine Hat is also one of the sunniest cities in Canada, with more annual sunshine than most Mediterranean capitals — a fact worth knowing for travellers passing through the prairies. **Dinosaur Provincial Park**, one of the richest dinosaur fossil sites in the world, is about an hour and a half's drive northwest, and **Cypress Hills Interprovincial Park** is an hour southeast.
