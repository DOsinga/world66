The Canadian Pacific Railway station was completed in 1906 in a confident Edwardian style — red brick, a clay-tile roof, and a long open platform looking out to the prairie. It is a federally protected heritage station and one of the better-preserved early-twentieth-century CPR buildings in southern Alberta.

There is still a Via Rail stop here (the Canadian, three times a week), and the building functions as a working station rather than a museum piece. Wander the platforms and the small waiting room; the architecture, the patina, and the long horizon east and west are the point.
