Peace River is one of the few prairie towns where the landscape does most of the talking. The town sits far below the surrounding plateau, squeezed into a steep valley where the Peace, Smoky, and Heart rivers meet. From above, it looks almost hidden; from below, the valley walls make the place feel much more remote than the highway map suggests.

The best first stop is [Sagitawa Lookout](/northamerica/canada/alberta/peace_river/sagitawa_lookout), south of town, where the river confluence and the long curve of the settlement make sense at once. In town, the [Peace River Museum](/northamerica/canada/alberta/peace_river/peace_river_museum) tells the fur-trade and settlement story, while [Twelve Foot Davis Gravesite](/northamerica/canada/alberta/peace_river/twelve_foot_davis_gravesite) gives the town its best-known local character and another strong valley view.

Peace River is still a small northern service town, not a polished resort. That is part of the appeal. Drive the [Shaftesbury Trail](/northamerica/canada/alberta/peace_river/shaftesbury_trail) along the river, watch the light move across the banks, and the place becomes less a stop on the road north than a good day in the Peace Country.
