100 Mile House is a small town in the Cariboo region of [British Columbia](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia), named for its position on the old Cariboo Wagon Road, 100 miles north of Lillooet (Mile Zero). It is a ranching and forestry town that serves as a gateway to some of the best cross-country skiing and lake fishing in the province.

The area around 100 Mile House has over 100 lakes within a short drive, making it a popular destination for [trout fishing in summer and ice fishing in winter](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/100milehouse/cariboo_lakes). The town bills itself as the "Handcrafted Log Home Capital of North America," and you will see evidence of this in the impressive log buildings around the area. The [100 Mile Marsh](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/100milehouse/mile_house_marsh), right on the edge of town, is a good spot for birdwatching, with nesting grebes, herons, and various waterfowl.

In winter, the Cariboo region receives reliable dry snow, and the [100 Mile Nordic ski trails](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/100milehouse/nordic_skiing) — some of the finest groomed cross-country terrain in British Columbia — attract serious skiers from across the province. The town is a convenient stop on the drive between [Kamloops](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/kamploops) and the northern Cariboo. [Guest ranches](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/100milehouse/guest_ranches) in the surrounding hills offer horseback riding and a taste of the ranching life that still defines this part of British Columbia.
