Cache Creek is a small highway town in the dry Thompson country of British Columbia. On first sight it looks like a fuel-and-food stop between Kamloops, the Cariboo, and the Fraser Canyon, and in a practical sense that is exactly what it is. The reason to give it more time is the landscape around it: sagebrush hills, old wagon-road ranches, fossil beds, and small desert parks.

The best single stop is [Historic Hat Creek](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/cache_creek/historic_hat_creek), a preserved Cariboo Wagon Road roadhouse and ranch north of town. East of Cache Creek, the [McAbee Fossil Beds](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/cache_creek/mcabee_fossil_beds) hold Eocene fossils in dry, exposed hills. [Juniper Beach Provincial Park](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/cache_creek/juniper_beach_provincial_park) and [Marble Canyon Provincial Park](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/cache_creek/marble_canyon_provincial_park) add river, lake, canyon, and desert ecology.

This is not a polished destination. It is best for travellers already driving the interior roads who want the country to become more than a view through the windshield. With a car and a spare day, Cache Creek gives you a compact version of the southern Interior's ranching, road-building, fossil, and dryland stories.
