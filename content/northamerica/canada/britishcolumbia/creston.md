Creston sits in a broad valley in southeastern British Columbia, between the Selkirk and Purcell mountains and just north of the Idaho border. It is a small town, but not a blank one: wetlands, orchards, farms, wineries, a brewery, and the Crowsnest Highway give it a clear Kootenay identity.

The main reason to stop is the [Creston Valley Wildlife Management Area](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/creston/creston_valley_wildlife_management_area), one of the province's great wetland landscapes. Boardwalks, dyke trails, viewing towers, and marshes make it easy to spend several hours with binoculars, especially during migration seasons.

In town, the [Creston Museum](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/creston/creston_museum) gives useful local context, and the [Columbia Brewery](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/creston/columbia_brewery) marks the town's most famous export: Kokanee beer. Creston works best for travellers already crossing the southern Kootenays, but if you like birds, fruit stands, quiet roads, and mountain-rimmed valleys, it earns its day.
