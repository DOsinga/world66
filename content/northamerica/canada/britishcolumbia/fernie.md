Fernie sits in the Elk Valley of southeastern British Columbia, with the Three Sisters ridge rising above a compact brick downtown. It began as a coal town, burned almost completely in 1904 and again in 1908, then rebuilt in brick and stone. That accident of fire gives the centre much of its charm today: old bank buildings, the courthouse, the museum, and a main street that still feels like a working mountain town rather than a resort mall.

The mountain is the other reason to come. [Fernie Alpine Resort](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/fernie/fernie_alpine_resort) has five big bowls, deep snow, and enough steep terrain to keep good skiers busy for days. In summer the same valley shifts to hiking and mountain biking, with [Mount Fernie Provincial Park](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/fernie/mount_fernie_provincial_park) close to town and the old-growth cedar trails around [Island Lake Lodge](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/fernie/island_lake_lodge) just up the side valley.

Fernie is not as polished as Whistler, and that is part of the point. The town still has miners, forestry workers, ski bums, families, and long-time locals sharing the same streets. For a traveller crossing the southern Rockies, it is a strong one-day stop: walk the heritage centre, spend an afternoon on the trails, and stay for the mountain-town bars and cafes if the weather turns.
