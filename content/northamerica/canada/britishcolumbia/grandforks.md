Grand Forks sits at the confluence of the Kettle and [Granby](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/grandforks/granby_river) rivers in the Boundary Country of southeastern [British Columbia](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia). It is a small, quiet town with a distinctive heritage — Doukhobor settlers from Russia arrived in the early 1900s, and their influence is still visible in the community halls at the [Hardy Mountain village](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/grandforks/hardy_mountain_village), the excellent borscht and perogies served at local restaurants, and the surrounding farmland.

The town makes a convenient stop on the back roads between the Okanagan and the Kootenays. The semi-arid climate is surprisingly warm in summer, and the surrounding hills are good for hiking and mountain biking. The Badger Trail along [Christina Lake](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/grandforks/christina_lake) offers panoramic views on an easy 2.3 km walk, while the Granby River Trail follows the east bank of the river through old-growth forest into Granby Provincial Park. The [Trans Canada Trail](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/grandforks/trans_canada_trail) runs along old railway lines east and west of town, and the Granby and Kettle rivers are pleasant for canoeing and kayaking. A handful of small wineries have appeared in the valley in recent years.
