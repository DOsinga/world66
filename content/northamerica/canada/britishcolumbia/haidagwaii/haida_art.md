Haida visual art is one of the most distinctive artistic traditions in the world: a formline-based system of curved black lines and ovoid shapes that represents creatures and beings in a way that is immediately recognisable as Haida but allows infinite individual expression. The tradition was nearly lost after the devastating smallpox epidemics of the 19th century, but its revival since the 1960s — led by artists like Bill Reid, Robert Davidson, and later Jim Hart and others — has been one of the great cultural recoveries of modern times.

The primary place to encounter Haida art on the islands is the [Haida Heritage Centre](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/haidagwaii/haida_heritage_centre) at Skidegate, which has a carving shed where you can watch artists at work. Several galleries in Queen Charlotte (Daajing Giids) sell original work — prints, silver jewellery, argillite carvings, and cedar bentwood boxes — directly from Haida artists. Buying directly from the artists ensures they receive fair payment. Haida art is also produced in Masset, where the carver Robert Davidson lives and maintains a studio.
