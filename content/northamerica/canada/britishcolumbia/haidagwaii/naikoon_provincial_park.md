Naikoon Provincial Park occupies the northeast corner of Graham Island, encompassing the long wild beaches of the East Beach and the boreal forest of the McIntyre Bay area. The park is named after Naikoon — the Haida word for "long nose," referring to the point at the northern tip of the park. It is large, remote, and spectacular, with ancient Sitka spruce draped in lichen, boggy forest floors, and long sweeps of windswept beach that see almost no foot traffic.

The East Beach extends for roughly 100 kilometres from Tlell to Rose Spit at the island's northern tip. The Naikoon backcountry trail follows this beach, taking 4-6 days; hikers must be fully self-sufficient as there are no services and no cell coverage. The beach also makes an excellent day walk from Tlell or from the campground at Agate Beach. The wreck of the Pesuta is accessible on a short trail from the highway; the walk takes about an hour each way through spruce forest to the beach.
