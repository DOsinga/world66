Fort Langley National Historic Site is the most important historical site in the [Vancouver](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/vancouver) region and well worth the 45-minute drive from the city. The fort has been reconstructed on its original footprint and staffed with costumed interpreters who demonstrate how the Hudson's Bay Company operated its fur trade and provisioning business in the mid-19th century. The cooperage — where barrels were made for packing salted salmon — is one of the few original structures remaining.

The fort's position at the confluence of the Salmon River and the Fraser made it the hub of the fur trade north of the Columbia River for decades. The surrounding village of Fort Langley is pleasant to walk through afterwards: antique shops and heritage buildings line Glover Road, and the waterfront along the Fraser River is a good place to sit and watch the river traffic. Costumed interpreters at the fort are genuinely knowledgeable — go on a weekday if you want less company.
