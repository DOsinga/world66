New Westminster is western Canada's oldest city and was the first capital of [British Columbia](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia). It sits along the north bank of the Fraser River, about 25 km east of [Vancouver](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/vancouver), and is easily reached by SkyTrain.

The waterfront area around the Quay is the main draw, with the [River Market](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/newwestminster/river_market), views across the Fraser and a boardwalk along the shore. The city's [Columbia Street historic district](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/newwestminster/columbia_street) retains a number of early twentieth-century commercial buildings, and the residential neighbourhoods on the hill above have fine Victorian and Edwardian houses around [Queen's Park](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/newwestminster/queens_park). The [Irving House Historic Centre](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/newwestminster/irving_house) is the best-preserved Victorian home in the province. New Westminster feels like a distinct city rather than a Vancouver suburb — it has its own character, its own downtown, and a proud sense of its history as the original capital. It also has a stronger claim than anywhere else to the title of Canada's lacrosse capital, a case well made at the [Canadian Lacrosse Hall of Fame](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/newwestminster/canadian_lacrosse_hall_of_fame).
