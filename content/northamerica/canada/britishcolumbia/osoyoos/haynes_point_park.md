story: >
  The park was renamed from Haynes Point to sw̓iw̓s in 2015 — sw̓iw̓s being the Syilx word for the narrow spit of land. Judge John Carmichael Haynes, the original namesake, was a 19th-century gold commissioner who acquired vast land holdings in the Okanagan, including most of the land around Osoyoos Lake. The renaming was part of a broader effort to restore Indigenous place names in BC.

A narrow sandspit jutting about a kilometre east into Osoyoos Lake, with campgrounds and day-use beach areas along both sides. The lake here is the warmest in Canada on average, and the shallow water heats up fast in summer — usually swimmable by late May, and comfortable well into September.

The park is a favourite campground for families, and reservations in July and August are essential. Day visitors get the beach without the crowds, particularly early morning. The spit gives good views back to the town and the dry hills behind it. Wildlife includes western painted turtles basking on logs, ospreys fishing, and occasional American white pelicans. No development beyond the campground and basic facilities — this is a place to swim, lie in the sun, and watch the lake.
