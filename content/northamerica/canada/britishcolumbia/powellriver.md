Powell River sits on the upper Sunshine Coast, facing Vancouver Island across the Strait of Georgia but cut off from the rest of the mainland road system. You arrive by ferry or small plane, and that extra effort is part of the point: the town feels coastal, self-contained, and a little out of the regular British Columbia loop.

The strongest reason to stop is the old company town. Powell River's Historic Townsite was laid out from 1910 for the pulp and paper mill, and it survives with an unusual amount of its early plan intact: workers' houses, civic buildings, the mill above the water, and the Patricia Theatre, one of Canada's great old cinemas. It is not polished heritage-theme scenery. It still feels like a working town that happened to keep its bones.

The landscape around town is better than the town itself. Inland Lake has a gentle loop trail and good swimming, while the qathet Canoe Route and the Sunshine Coast Trail push deep into forest, lakes, and low mountains behind Powell River. If you have come north from Vancouver looking for the coast beyond the commuter towns, Powell River is where the journey starts to feel properly far away.
