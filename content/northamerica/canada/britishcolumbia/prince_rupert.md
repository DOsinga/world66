Prince Rupert sits on Kaien Island on British Columbia's North Coast, closer in spirit to Alaska than to Vancouver. Ferries, fishing boats, container ships, fog, ravens, and steep forested hills all crowd around a compact harbour city.

This is not a polished resort town. Prince Rupert is a working port with a strong Tsimshian presence and a dramatic wet-coast setting. The [Museum of Northern British Columbia](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/prince_rupert/museum_of_northern_british_columbia) is the best first stop, especially for Northwest Coast art and local history.

The city also works as a base for the coast around it. The [Butze Rapids Trail](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/prince_rupert/butze_rapids_trail) gives you rainforest and tidal water close to town, while the [North Pacific Cannery](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/prince_rupert/north_pacific_cannery) near Port Edward makes the salmon industry feel tangible rather than abstract. If you are already moving along the Inside Passage or the Yellowhead Highway, Prince Rupert is worth more than a transit night.
