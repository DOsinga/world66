---
title: "Sidney Spit Marine Park"
type: poi
---

Sidney Spit is a long sandy peninsula extending from the northern tip of Sidney Island, about two kilometres east of Sidney across Miners Channel. A seasonal passenger-only ferry (typically running May through September) departs from the Sidney pier and makes the crossing in around 20 minutes, dropping visitors at one of the finest sandbars on the BC coast.

The spit itself is a narrow finger of white sand with shallow, calm water on one side — warm enough for swimming in summer — and views across the strait in every direction. The park is part of the Gulf Islands National Park Reserve and is managed by Parks Canada. Behind the beach, a forested interior has walking trails, and the island is home to a population of fallow deer introduced in the early twentieth century. Kayakers frequently paddle over from the Sidney waterfront.

There are no services on the island beyond basic facilities at the park, so bring food and water. Timing your visit around the ferry schedule is essential. The combination of easy accessibility from Sidney and genuine wilderness character makes Sidney Spit one of the most rewarding half-day excursions in the southern Gulf Islands.
