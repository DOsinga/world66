---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

Terrace has a maritime-influenced climate softened by the Coast Mountains: cool wet winters and short warm summers. Snow falls heavily from November through March but rarely accumulates much in the city itself — the snow stays in the mountains, where it feeds Shames Mountain's famous depth.

**Summer (June to August)** is the obvious season — long days, swimming at Lakelse Lake, and the main salmon runs on the Skeena. Daytime temperatures sit in the low twenties Celsius, occasionally pushing thirty.

**Autumn (September to early November)** is the best window if you've come to fish or to see the country at its most dramatic — the cottonwoods turn yellow, the rivers are still running, and the steelhead are in. Bring waterproofs; it rains a lot.

**Winter (December to March)** is for skiers. The town empties out, the days are short, but the Shames snowpack is unmatched.

Avoid mid-October if you can — it tends to be the wettest stretch of the year, and the salmon runs are starting to wind down.
