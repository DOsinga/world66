Tofino sits at the far western edge of Vancouver Island, where the road runs out and the Pacific begins. It is one of Canada's most dramatic coastal settings: ancient temperate rainforest, massive swells rolling in from the open ocean, and wide sandy beaches that attract surfers from around the world. [Long Beach](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/tofino/long_beach), within Pacific Rim National Park, is one of the longest beaches in Canada and the centre of the surf scene.

The town itself is small — only a few thousand permanent residents — but has punched well above its weight in terms of restaurants and wilderness lodges. [Whale watching](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/tofino/whale_watching) is a major draw from March to October, when grey whales and orcas pass through [Clayoquot Sound](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/tofino/clayoquot_sound). Sea kayaking around the islands of the Sound, [Hot Springs Cove](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/tofino/hot_springs_cove) accessible only by boat or seaplane, and old-growth forest trails through [Meares Island](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/tofino/meares_island) round out the options.

Tofino's weather is famously wet — it receives around 3,300mm of rain a year. The storm-watching season from November to February brings a different kind of visitor: people who come specifically to watch the waves crash against the shore from the warmth of a lodge.
