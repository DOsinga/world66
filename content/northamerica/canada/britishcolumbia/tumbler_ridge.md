*North America > Canada > British Columbia > Tumbler Ridge*

Tumbler Ridge was built in the early 1980s as a coal town, then found a second identity in the cliffs, canyons, waterfalls, and dinosaur tracks around it. The surrounding country is now the Tumbler Ridge UNESCO Global Geopark, the only one in western Canada, and the town has become a base for unusually accessible palaeontology and northern hiking.

The dinosaur story is the hook. Start at the [Dinosaur Discovery Gallery](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/tumbler_ridge/dinosaur_discovery_gallery), then walk out to [Flatbed Creek](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/tumbler_ridge/flatbed_creek_dinosaur_tracks), where trackways in the creek bed helped change the town's future. The landscape is just as strong: [Kinuseo Falls](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/tumbler_ridge/kinuseo_falls) is the headline, and smaller hikes like [Quality Falls](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/tumbler_ridge/quality_falls) make good half-day trips.

This is not a polished mountain resort. Roads can be rough, distances are long, and some sites need local advice before you go. That is also why Tumbler Ridge works: with one spare day in the Peace region, you can see something here that looks and feels unlike the standard northern highway stop.
