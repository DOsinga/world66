*North America > Canada > British Columbia > Vancouver Island*

Vancouver Island is British Columbia in its salt-sprayed, forested form: a long Pacific island where old-growth cedar, sheltered harbours, surf beaches, and mountain roads sit surprisingly close together. [Victoria](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/victoria) gives the island a polished southern gateway, but the best of the island starts once you leave the capital and let the roads get slower.

The west coast is the wild side. [Pacific Rim National Park Reserve](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/vancouverisland/pacific_rim_national_park) protects Long Beach, the Broken Group Islands, and the start of the West Coast Trail, while [Tofino](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/tofino) and Ucluelet make useful bases for surfing, whale watching, storm watching, and long walks in rain gear. Inland, [Cathedral Grove](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/vancouverisland/cathedral_grove) gives you an easy look at the kind of ancient forest that once covered much of the island.

The east coast is gentler and easier to travel. [Nanaimo](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/nanaimo), Parksville, Qualicum Beach, Courtenay, and the [Comox Valley](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/comox_valley) line up along ferry routes, beaches, farmers markets, and mountain access. The north is quieter again, with Cape Scott, Telegraph Cove, and Port Hardy rewarding travellers who like long drives and fewer people.

Do not try to "do" Vancouver Island as a day trip from Vancouver. The ferries are part of the experience, but distances are real; Victoria to Tofino is a full half-day drive. Give the island time and it becomes one of Canada's most satisfying slow-travel regions.
