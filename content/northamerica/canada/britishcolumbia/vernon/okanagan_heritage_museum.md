The Vernon Museum and Archives, in the Civic Centre building downtown on 30th Avenue, covers the history of the North Okanagan from Indigenous occupation through the fur trade era, the gold rush, the arrival of the railways, and the development of the fruit-growing industry that still defines the valley. The collection includes historical photographs, Indigenous artifacts, and reconstructed pioneer-era rooms.

The building also houses a small art gallery with rotating exhibitions of local and regional work. Not the largest regional museum in BC, but reasonably done and a good rainy-day option. Admission is by donation. The archives are accessible to researchers by appointment.
