  Silver Star's distinctive painted Victorian buildings — fire-engine red, canary yellow, bright blue — were the idea of resort founder Gordon Ackroyd, who in the late 1950s decided the mountain village should look like a Gold Rush town. He built the first structures himself and insisted every business that moved in match the aesthetic. The colour scheme has been maintained ever since.

Silver Star Mountain, 22 km east of Vernon, is one of British Columbia's top-ten ski resorts and a fully functional mountain village open year-round. In winter the ski area has 132 runs, two separate mountain faces (Vance Creek and Putnam Creek), and consistent dry powder from November through April. The vertical drop of 760 m is not enormous by BC standards, but the variety of terrain and the relative lack of crowds compared to Whistler make it a favourite for families and intermediate skiers.

In summer the resort reinvents itself as a mountain bike destination. Over 70 km of singletrack trails fan out through the forest, ranging from beginner-friendly cross-country loops to challenging downhill runs for which the gondola provides uplift. The village itself — a cluster of brightly painted Victorian buildings around a central plaza — is pleasant to wander regardless of season. Accommodation, restaurants, and ski/bike rental are all available on site.
