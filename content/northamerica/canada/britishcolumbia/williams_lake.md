Williams Lake sits in the central Cariboo, where Highway 97 meets the road west into the Chilcotin. It is a working forestry and ranching town, not a polished mountain resort, and that is its charm. The landscape opens into dry grasslands, lakes, river valleys, and cattle country.

The town's big identity marker is the [Williams Lake Stampede](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/williams_lake/williams_lake_stampede), one of Canada's best-known rodeos. Outside Stampede weekend, [Museum of the Cariboo-Chilcotin](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/williams_lake/museum_of_the_cariboo_chilcotin), [Scout Island](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/williams_lake/scout_island), and the [Williams Lake River Valley Trail](/northamerica/canada/britishcolumbia/williams_lake/williams_lake_river_valley_trail) give enough for a relaxed day.

The best reason to stop is context. Williams Lake explains the Cariboo better than a roadside pullout does: ranching, Secwepemc and Tsilhqot'in country, gold-rush routes, rail history, and the start of the long westward road toward Bella Coola.
