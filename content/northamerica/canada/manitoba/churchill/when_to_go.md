---
title: When to Go
type: section
---

Churchill has three distinct seasons for visitors, each centred on a wildlife event. The choice of when to go is essentially a choice of which animal you want to see.

**July (beluga season)** is the warmest and most comfortable time to visit. Temperatures reach 15–20°C, the tundra is in full bloom, and thousands of beluga whales fill the Churchill River estuary. Kayaking, boat tours, and snorkelling are all possible. Mosquitoes can be ferocious in early July.

**October–November (bear season)** is the most famous time. Polar bears congregate along the coast waiting for Hudson Bay to freeze, and tundra buggy tours take visitors out among them. Temperatures drop to well below zero by November, so pack seriously warm clothing. The season peaks in late October.

**January–March (aurora season)** brings the best northern lights displays. Temperatures regularly fall to -30°C or colder, and the town is quiet. Churchill Northern Studies Centre offers research-focused winter programming. Dog-sledding, snowshoeing, and snowmobiling fill the days.

Churchill is accessible only by air (scheduled flights from Winnipeg) or by train (the Via Rail service from Winnipeg, a 36-hour journey). Book transport and accommodation well in advance for bear season.
