Flin Flon is the kind of northern town that makes no sense until you stand in it: streets climbing over hard Canadian Shield rock, lakes appearing between houses, mine works on the skyline, and a name borrowed from an Edwardian adventure novel. It sits on the Manitoba-Saskatchewan border, but its mood is more boreal frontier than prairie.

The town's best sights are small and local rather than polished. Start with the [Flin Flon Station Museum](/northamerica/canada/manitoba/flinflon/flin_flon_station_museum) for mining history, then look for the [Flinty statue](/northamerica/canada/manitoba/flinflon/flinty_statue), the [Hundred Stairs](/northamerica/canada/manitoba/flinflon/hundred_stairs), and the odd above-ground sewer boxes that double as wooden walkways in places where bedrock would not let pipes go underground.

Flin Flon is also a lake town. [Ross Lake](/northamerica/canada/manitoba/flinflon/ross_lake) sits in the middle of things, with boardwalk walking and rocky views, while [Bakers Narrows Provincial Park](/northamerica/canada/manitoba/flinflon/bakers_narrows_provincial_park) gives you the larger boreal-lake version a short drive southeast.

Come if you like northern places with real texture. Flin Flon is not a postcard town, and that is much of the appeal: it is practical, strange, hard-edged, and surrounded by water and spruce.
