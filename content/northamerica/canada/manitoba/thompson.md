Thompson is the largest city in northern Manitoba, a mining town of about 13,000 people built by Inco in the late 1950s after the discovery of one of the world's largest nickel deposits. The city sits on the edge of the boreal forest, surrounded by lakes, rivers, and muskeg — a landscape that is strikingly empty in every direction.

For travellers, Thompson functions mainly as a gateway. The train to [Churchill](/northamerica/canada/manitoba/churchill) — the famous polar bear capital on Hudson Bay — departs from here, and many visitors stop overnight on their way north. The town itself has the practical amenities of a northern hub (fuel, supplies, accommodation) but limited tourist appeal beyond the [Spirit Way wolf-mural walk](/northamerica/canada/manitoba/thompson/spirit_way) through downtown and the small [Heritage North Museum](/northamerica/canada/manitoba/thompson/heritage_north_museum). In winter, the [Northern Lights](/northamerica/canada/manitoba/thompson/northern_lights) are spectacular, and the surrounding wilderness — [Paint Lake](/northamerica/canada/manitoba/thompson/paint_lake) and [Pisew Falls](/northamerica/canada/manitoba/thompson/pisew_falls) chief among the close-in options — offers fishing, canoeing, and wildlife viewing in the warmer months.
