---
title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

Winnipeg's food scene is more ambitious than its modest reputation suggests. The city's extraordinary ethnic diversity — Ukrainian, Filipino, Icelandic, Métis, Chinese, South Asian, and more — feeds a restaurant landscape with genuine depth.

**Corydon Avenue** (Little Italy) has the highest density of restaurants in the city, from old-school Italian trattorias to contemporary bistros. **Osborne Village** mixes Thai, Greek, Japanese, and North African options in a walkable strip of old houses and storefronts. **Chinatown** on King Street offers classic Cantonese dim sum. **St. Boniface** across the river has the best French dining in western Canada.

For Ukrainian food, the **North End** is the place — perogies are a civic institution. The market stalls at **The Forks** are good for a quick lunch. For a pure Winnipeg experience, the **Salisbury House** diner chain has been serving "nips" (small burgers) since 1931, and **Bridge Drive Inn** on Jubilee Avenue is the city's favourite for ice cream.
