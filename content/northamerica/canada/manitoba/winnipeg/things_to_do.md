---
title: "Things to Do"
type: section
---

Winnipeg's standout sight is the **Canadian Museum for Human Rights** — the first national museum of Canada built outside Ottawa and the first museum in the world dedicated exclusively to human rights. Nearby, **The Forks** is the city's beating heart: a 54-acre public space at the junction of the Red and Assiniboine rivers with a market hall, restaurants, a skatepark, and in winter the world's longest naturally frozen skating trail.

The **Exchange District** is a National Historic Site of ornate Edwardian brick warehouses that survived because Winnipeg's boom ended abruptly in 1914 — the city never modernised them. Today they house galleries, studios, the warehouse theatre scene, and the annual Fringe Festival. The **Manitoba Museum** holds the full-scale replica of the Nonsuch, the 1668 ketch whose successful voyage to Hudson Bay launched the fur trade. The **Ukrainian Cultural and Educational Centre** (Oseredok) houses one of the finest Ukrainian art collections outside Ukraine.

For performing arts, the **Royal Manitoba Theatre Centre** is Canada's oldest English-language regional theatre. The **Centennial Concert Hall** is home to the Winnipeg Symphony Orchestra and the Royal Winnipeg Ballet. The **Burton Cummings Theatre** (restored 1906 Walker Theatre) hosts touring shows and is beautiful in its own right.
