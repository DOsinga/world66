---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

Winnipeg has an extreme continental climate — one of the most extreme of any major city in the world. January averages around -16°C but regularly drops below -30°C, with wind chill making it feel colder still. Summers are genuinely warm and sunny, with July averaging 26°C and some days pushing 35°C.

**June to August** is the prime visiting season. The days are long, the parks are full, and the city runs its best events: **Jazz Winnipeg** (June), **Winnipeg Fringe Theatre Festival** (July, the second-largest fringe in North America), and **Folklorama** (August, two weeks of multicultural pavilions — over 40 countries represented in pavilions across the city). The riverbanks come alive, the Forks fills with people, and outdoor patios open across the city.

**September and October** offer pleasant shoulder weather and lower prices. The elms are spectacular in autumn colour.

**Winter (December–March)** is for the committed. Temperatures regularly hit -30°C to -40°C with wind chill. But the city doesn't shut down: The Forks operates the world's longest naturally frozen skating trail (up to 10 km along the frozen Red and Assiniboine rivers), warming huts appear along the route, and the Red River Mutual Trail is a genuine winter spectacle when conditions allow. The **Festival du Voyageur** in February — a celebration of French-Canadian and Métis heritage in St. Boniface — is one of the best reasons to visit in winter.

Pack proper cold-weather gear for any winter visit. The Winnipeg Walkway (indoor skyway system) connects much of downtown and makes winter navigation more bearable.
