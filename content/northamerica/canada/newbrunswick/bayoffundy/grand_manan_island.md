Grand Manan Island sits in the Bay of Fundy, south of the New Brunswick mainland and close enough to Maine that the weather, fog, and tides feel larger than the map suggests. The island is not polished. It is a working place of small fishing communities, wharves, dulse drying, ferry schedules, and long quiet roads between cliff edges and coves.

The eastern side holds most of the settlements, while the western coast is wilder, with high basalt cliffs and fewer easy approaches. A spare day here is best spent moving slowly: arrive by ferry at North Head, walk out to [Swallowtail Lighthouse](/northamerica/canada/newbrunswick/bayoffundy/grand_manan_island/swallowtail_lighthouse), then drive south for beach walks, birding, and the trails of [Anchorage Provincial Park](/northamerica/canada/newbrunswick/bayoffundy/grand_manan_island/anchorage_provincial_park).

Grand Manan is also one of the better places in Atlantic Canada to feel the natural history of the Bay of Fundy at close range. The [Grand Manan Whale and Seabird Research Station](/northamerica/canada/newbrunswick/bayoffundy/grand_manan_island/whale_and_seabird_research_station) gives context to the water around you, and the local museum fills in the island's fishing, shipwreck, lighthouse, and birding stories. It is worth the ferry ride if you like edges of maps and places where the sea still runs the day.
