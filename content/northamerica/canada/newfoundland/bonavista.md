Bonavista sits at the northern tip of its peninsula, where Newfoundland's fishing history, Cabot legends, and cold Atlantic scenery all press together. It is one of the island's great outports: saltbox houses, old churches, wooden flakes for drying cod, and lanes that still feel shaped by the harbour rather than by a planner's map.

The town leans hard into the story of John Cabot, who is said to have made landfall nearby in 1497 and called out "O buona vista" after weeks at sea. Whether or not that exact line survived history intact, the feeling is easy to understand at [Cape Bonavista Lighthouse](/northamerica/canada/newfoundland/bonavista/cape_bonavista_lighthouse), where cliffs, puffins, whales, and icebergs turn the cape into one of Newfoundland's most theatrical viewpoints.

Bonavista is also good on foot. The ground is gentle, traffic is light, and old names like Mockbeggar, Rolling Cove, and Bakeapple Marsh still cling to different corners of town. Come in summer if you want open museums, boat trips, and wildlife tours. In the quieter months, the place is more local and more closed, but the sea does not become any less dramatic.
