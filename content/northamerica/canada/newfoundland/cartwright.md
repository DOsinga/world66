Cartwright is a tiny coastal village on Sandwich Bay in southern Labrador. It is not an easy place to reach, and that is most of the point: this is an end-of-the-road settlement where the Trans-Labrador Highway gives way to sea routes, gravel distances, fishing history, and wide empty country.

The village has a long Inuit and mixed Inuit history, later layered with the 18th-century trading post of George Cartwright and Hudson's Bay Company commerce. Today it is small and practical, with limited services, but it has a strong Labrador identity and a remarkable setting.

Cartwright clears the W66 bar because of what lies around it: [Mealy Mountains National Park Reserve](/northamerica/canada/newfoundland/cartwright/mealy_mountains_national_park), the long sandy [Wonderstrands](/northamerica/canada/newfoundland/cartwright/wonderstrands), Eagle River salmon country, and the view back over Sandwich Bay from [Flagstaff Hill](/northamerica/canada/newfoundland/cartwright/flagstaff_hill). Do not come casually. Come prepared, with fuel, confirmed beds or camping, and a healthy respect for distance.
