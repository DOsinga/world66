Rencontre East is a tiny Newfoundland outport on Fortune Bay, with no road connection and a population small enough that the ferry arrival still feels like an event. The town motto, "Isolated and Loving It", is not a tourism slogan so much as a useful warning: this is not a place for a quick drive-through or a full menu of services. You come by passenger ferry from Bay L'Argent or Pool's Cove, walk the dirt lanes, and adjust to a village where ATVs do the work that cars do elsewhere.

That isolation is the reason to come. Houses sit tight to the harbour, breakwaters hold back the sea, and the hills behind town rise quickly toward ponds, rough trails, and Rencontre Lake. The old fishing economy is now mixed with salmon aquaculture, but the feel is still of a south-coast Newfoundland settlement that refused resettlement and kept its own rhythm.

For travellers already working along the south coast, Rencontre East is one of the more memorable detours in Newfoundland. It asks for planning, weather patience, and a willingness to bring your own expectations down to village scale. In return you get a ferry-only outport, good walking, cold water, and a hard edge of coast that feels much farther from the road system than the map suggests.
