Inuvik sits at the end of the Dempster Highway in Canada's Northwest Territories — the northernmost point you can reach by public road in North America until you turn onto the [Tuktoyaktuk Highway](/northamerica/canada/northwestterritories/inuvik/tuktoyaktuk_highway) and continue another two hours to the Arctic Ocean. It is a small town of about 3,000 people on the [Mackenzie River Delta](/northamerica/canada/northwestterritories/inuvik/mackenzie_delta), built in the 1950s as an administrative centre and now serving as the main hub for the western Arctic.

The town is above the treeline and above the Arctic Circle. In summer the midnight sun shines for 56 days straight, and in winter the sun disappears for 30 days. The igloo-shaped [Our Lady of Victory Church](/northamerica/canada/northwestterritories/inuvik/our_lady_of_victory_church) is the town's most distinctive building, but the [Community Greenhouse](/northamerica/canada/northwestterritories/inuvik/community_greenhouse) — built into a converted hockey arena and growing tomatoes 200 km above the Arctic Circle — is the more remarkable story. The small [Midnight Sun Mosque](/northamerica/canada/northwestterritories/inuvik/midnight_sun_mosque), trucked and barged 4,000 km from Winnipeg in 2010, sits at the north edge of town. The [Great Northern Arts Festival](/northamerica/canada/northwestterritories/inuvik/great_northern_arts_festival) in July draws Inuit, Gwich'in, and circumpolar artists from across the Arctic.

Most visitors come for the Dempster Highway itself — one of the great drives in North America, 740 kilometres of gravel road across Arctic tundra from Dawson City in the Yukon — or for the [northern lights](/northamerica/canada/northwestterritories/inuvik/aurora_viewing), which are reliably visible on clear nights from late August through April. In summer, the boat tours of the Mackenzie Delta are the best way to get out beyond the town.
