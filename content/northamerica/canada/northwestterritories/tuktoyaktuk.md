Tuktoyaktuk, usually shortened to Tuk, sits on the Beaufort Sea coast beyond the treeline, where the Inuvik-Tuktoyaktuk Highway reaches the Arctic Ocean. It is a small Inuvialuit hamlet, but it is not just a dot at the end of a road: this is the only Canadian Arctic Ocean community connected to the rest of the country by public road, and the landscape around it is unlike anywhere farther south.

The strongest reason to come is the meeting of tundra, sea, and ice-cored hills. The [Pingo Canadian Landmark](/northamerica/canada/northwestterritories/tuktoyaktuk/pingo_canadian_landmark) protects a cluster of pingos west of town, including Ibyuk Pingo, and the highway itself gives broad views across lakes and low Arctic ground. In town, the [traditional sod house](/northamerica/canada/northwestterritories/tuktoyaktuk/traditional_sod_house), the [Our Lady of Lourdes Schooner](/northamerica/canada/northwestterritories/tuktoyaktuk/our_lady_of_lourdes_schooner), and the northern end of the Trans Canada Trail give a quick but real sense of how people lived, moved, and adapted here.

Most visitors come from [Inuvik](/northamerica/canada/northwestterritories/inuvik) as a long day trip or as the last leg of the Dempster Highway drive. Services are limited and weather changes fast, but if you have come this far north, Tuk is worth the extra road time.
