Yellowknife sits directly under the auroral oval — the ring around the magnetic pole where the northern lights are most frequently and intensely visible. The city averages 240 aurora-visible nights per year, more than almost anywhere else in Canada with practical visitor infrastructure. The prime season runs from late August through April, when the nights are dark enough; peak months are September through March.

Several local operators offer aurora tours that combine transport out of town (away from city light pollution), warm gear, and knowledgeable guides who can explain the science. Many run from dedicated viewing shelters or heated teepees on the tundra, so the experience is more comfortable than simply standing in minus-30 weather. Aurora forecasts are widely available online and most guides monitor them closely. If you are in Yellowknife between late August and April and the skies are clear, do not go to bed early.

story: "The auroral oval over Yellowknife is positioned by Earth's magnetic field, not its geographic location. Yellowknife lies about 400 kilometres south of the Arctic Circle but sits almost directly beneath the oval because the magnetic pole is offset from the geographic one. This is why cities farther north, like Inuvik, sometimes see fewer displays — they can be inside the oval rather than under its edge, where the lights are most intense."
