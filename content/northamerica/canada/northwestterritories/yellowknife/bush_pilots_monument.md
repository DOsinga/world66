The Bush Pilots Monument stands on a rocky promontory in Old Town, overlooking Yellowknife Bay and the floatplane base below. It honours the pilots who opened up the Canadian North in the 1920s and 1930s — men who flew fragile aircraft across vast, unmapped wilderness to deliver supplies, evacuate the sick, and connect remote communities that had no other link to the outside world. Without them, much of Canada's northern development, including Yellowknife's own gold rush, would not have been possible.

The monument is more than a memorial: it is one of the best viewpoints in the city. From the rock, you look out across the bay to the houseboats and islands of Old Town, with the float base directly below and the towers of New Town rising to the west. On a clear summer evening, the whole scene glows in the long subarctic light.

story: "One of the most celebrated bush pilots commemorated here is Punch Dickins, who in 1928 made the first flight over the Barren Grounds — covering 8,000 kilometres of unmapped tundra north of the treeline. He navigated entirely by dead reckoning, with no radio and no emergency services within hundreds of miles."
