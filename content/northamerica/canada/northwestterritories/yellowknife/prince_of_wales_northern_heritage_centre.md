The Prince of Wales Northern Heritage Centre is the territorial museum of the Northwest Territories, and easily the most important cultural institution in Yellowknife. The collections tell the deep human story of the North — from Indigenous Dene and Métis heritage spanning thousands of years to the rush of European fur traders, miners, and bush pilots who transformed the region from the eighteenth century onward. Artifacts, photographs, and oral histories are woven together to give a genuine sense of how people have lived in one of the world's most extreme environments.

The museum also covers natural history, with displays on the geology, wildlife, and ecology of the subarctic and taiga. Special exhibitions rotate throughout the year. The Centre sits on a hill overlooking Frame Lake, a short walk from downtown, and admission is free — one of the best deals in any Canadian capital city.

story: "The museum takes its name from Prince Charles, who visited Yellowknife in 1975 during the Northwest Territories' centennial celebrations. The name was assigned before he became King, and the plaque at the entrance still reads 'Prince of Wales.'"
