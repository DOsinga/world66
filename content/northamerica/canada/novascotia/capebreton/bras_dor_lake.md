The Bras d'Or is a vast inland sea that divides Cape Breton Island nearly in two. At 1,100 square kilometres it is larger than many Canadian provinces' entire lake systems, and its mixture of fresh and salt water supports an extraordinary variety of wildlife. Bald eagles nest along almost every shore. Osprey fish the shallows. The deeper channels hold abundant Atlantic salmon, and blue herons are almost omnipresent.

The best base for exploring the Bras d'Or is Baddeck, a small town on the northern shore with a working marina, sailing equipment hire, and direct access to the Alexander Graham Bell National Historic Site. Bell's summer estate at Beinn Bhreagh stands on a headland across the water (private property, but visible from the lake). The historic site in town is excellent on its own — the breadth of Bell's interests beyond the telephone, from kite design to hydrofoils, is genuinely surprising.

Kayaking and sailing are the best ways to see the lake. The Bras d'Or is recognized as a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve. The Mi'kmaq have inhabited these shores for at least 4,000 years and consider the lake sacred.
