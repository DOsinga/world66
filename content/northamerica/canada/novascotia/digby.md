Digby is a small working town on the Bay of Fundy, best known for scallops and the ferry from Saint John. It is not polished, and that helps: the waterfront still belongs to boats, weather, and restaurants selling the catch that made the town famous.

The town has a simple day-trip shape. Walk [Digby Harbour](/northamerica/canada/novascotia/digby/digby_harbour), look into Loyalist and shipping history at the [Admiral Digby Museum](/northamerica/canada/novascotia/digby/admiral_digby_museum), and eat scallops by the water. If you have a car, the stronger natural drama is farther down Digby Neck at [Balancing Rock Trail](/northamerica/canada/novascotia/digby/balancing_rock_trail), where basalt cliffs and Fundy tides make the trip feel much larger than the town.

Digby is worth a day if you are already moving along Nova Scotia's west coast or arriving by ferry. It is less convincing as a standalone detour from Halifax, but as a Fundy-side stop with seafood and a working harbour, it earns its place.
