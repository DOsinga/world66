The Nova Scotia Museum of Natural History sits on Summer Street, a short walk from the Public Gardens. It is one of the province's better natural history museums, covering the geology, wildlife, and Indigenous history of Nova Scotia across well-designed galleries. The Mi'kmaw culture gallery is particularly strong, with objects, languages, and oral histories from one of the oldest continuous cultures in Atlantic Canada. The natural history halls cover the birds, mammals, and ecosystems of Nova Scotia, including the province's surprisingly diverse marine life.

There is a live animal exhibit with local species, which makes the museum a reliable choice with children. The honeybee observation hive is a longtime favourite — a working colony behind glass where you can watch the bees go about their business. Entry is modestly priced. The museum is open year-round, Tuesday to Sunday, and is often quieter than the waterfront attractions, which can be crowded in summer. It pairs well with the Public Gardens, which are a few minutes' walk away.
