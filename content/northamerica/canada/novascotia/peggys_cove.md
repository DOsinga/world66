Peggy's Cove is a very small fishing village on the Atlantic shore southwest of [Halifax](/northamerica/canada/novascotia/halifax). It would be easy to dismiss it as one lighthouse and a crowd of cameras, but the setting is stronger than that: weathered granite sloping into cold water, fishing sheds around a tight cove, and surf that can turn from pretty to lethal in seconds.

The [Peggy's Point Lighthouse](/northamerica/canada/novascotia/peggys_cove/peggys_point_lighthouse) is the icon, and it is one of Canada's classic coastal images. The village itself is worth a slow walk too, especially early or late in the day when tour buses have thinned and the working harbour feels less like a postcard.

Peggy's Cove is best treated as a half-day from Halifax or a stop on the South Shore drive toward [Lunenburg](/northamerica/canada/novascotia/lunenburg). Go for the rocks, the weather, and the stark little harbour. Stay well back from wet black rock; people have been swept into the sea here.
