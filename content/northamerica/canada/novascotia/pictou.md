Pictou sits on a sheltered harbour on Nova Scotia's Northumberland Shore, a small town with a bigger place in the province's origin story than its size suggests. It calls itself the birthplace of New Scotland because the ship Hector landed Scottish settlers here in 1773, beginning a wave of Highland migration that shaped northern Nova Scotia and Cape Breton.

The town is easy to take in on foot. The waterfront has the replica Hector, small museums, fishing boats, and enough old wooden buildings to keep the place from feeling over-polished. It is not as grand as Lunenburg or as busy as Halifax, but it has a direct, working-harbour feel and a strong Scottish memory.

Pictou also works as a practical stop on the way to the Prince Edward Island ferry at Caribou. Give it a few hours for the harbour and museums, or stay longer if you want a quieter base for beaches and small towns along the Northumberland coast.
