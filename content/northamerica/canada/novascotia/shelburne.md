Shelburne is a small harbour town on Nova Scotia's South Shore, quieter than [Lunenburg](/northamerica/canada/novascotia/lunenburg) but with a sharper historical edge than its size suggests. In the 1780s it became one of British North America's largest Loyalist settlements, and nearby Birchtown was one of the most important Black Loyalist communities in Canada.

The town's travel appeal is concentrated along the old waterfront. [Shelburne's Museums by the Sea](/northamerica/canada/novascotia/shelburne/shelburnes_museums_by_the_sea) pull together the county museum, the [Dory Shop Museum](/northamerica/canada/novascotia/shelburne/dory_shop_museum), and the [Ross-Thomson House and Store](/northamerica/canada/novascotia/shelburne/ross_thomson_house_and_store), so a short walk covers shipbuilding, Loyalist trade, and local archives.

Shelburne is not a big-ticket destination, but it is a good South Shore stop for travellers who like working harbours, old wooden buildings, and complicated colonial history. The [Black Loyalist Heritage Centre](/northamerica/canada/novascotia/shelburne/black_loyalist_heritage_centre) in Birchtown gives the visit its strongest context, and turns Shelburne from a pretty waterfront into a place with a story worth hearing.
