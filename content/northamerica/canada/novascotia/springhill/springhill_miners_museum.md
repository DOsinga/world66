  Springhill's mining history is not a single tragedy. The town remembers the 1891 disaster, a major underground fire in 1916, the 1956 explosion, and the 1958 bump that killed 75 miners and drew international attention to a small Nova Scotia coal town.

Springhill Miners Museum is the strongest reason to stop in town. Guided tours take you into the mine setting and explain how coal shaped Springhill's economy, politics, families, and grief.

The underground element makes the story immediate. Wear sensible shoes, expect cool temperatures below ground, and leave time for the surface exhibits rather than treating the tour as a quick novelty.
