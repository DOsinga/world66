**Wolfville** is a small Annapolis Valley town with more personality than its size suggests. Acadia University gives it a bookish, energetic centre, while the farms and vineyards around town sit on land shaped by some of the highest tides in the world.

The strongest reason to come is the landscape around **Grand-Pré**. The Acadian dykelands, the memorial church, and the broad sweep of the Minas Basin tell a story that is both beautiful and painful: fertile reclaimed marshes, French settlement, and the 1755 deportation of the Acadians. It is one of the few places in Nova Scotia where the view itself carries that much history.

Wolfville also works well as a gentle food-and-wine stop between [Halifax](/northamerica/canada/novascotia/halifax) and the western valley. You can walk the Acadia campus, browse Main Street, taste local whites and sparkling wines, and be out on the Harvest Moon Trail or the Fundy shore within minutes. It is not a big-city destination, but it rewards a slow day.
