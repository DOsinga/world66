Iqaluit is the capital of [Nunavut](/northamerica/canada/nunavut) and the gateway to the Eastern Arctic. Formerly known as Frobisher Bay, it sits on the southeastern coast of Baffin Island and is home to about 8,000 people — a growing city by Arctic standards, but still a place where the tundra begins at the edge of town and polar bears occasionally wander through the outskirts.

[St Jude's Cathedral](/northamerica/canada/nunavut/iqaluit/st_judes_cathedral), shaped like an igloo and built after the original burned down in 2005, is the most distinctive building and an important Anglican centre for the entire Canadian Arctic. The [Nunatta Sunakkutaangit Museum](/northamerica/canada/nunavut/iqaluit/nunatta_sunakkutaangit_museum) holds a worthwhile collection of Inuit artefacts and prints in a former Hudson's Bay Company warehouse. The [Legislative Assembly of Nunavut](/northamerica/canada/nunavut/iqaluit/legislative_assembly) is open to visitors and worth seeing for its unusual consensus-based design.

[Sylvia Grinnell Territorial Park](/northamerica/canada/nunavut/iqaluit/sylvia_grinnell_park) is a 30-minute walk from the centre, with hiking, fishing, and archaeological sites going back thousands of years. For the more ambitious, Iqaluit is the jumping-off point for trips to Auyuittuq National Park on the Cumberland Peninsula and, for the truly committed, Quttinirpaaq on Ellesmere Island — one of the most remote places on Earth.
