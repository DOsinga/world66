Nunatta Sunakkutaangit Museum (the name means "our heritage" in Inuktitut) holds the most accessible collection of Inuit artefacts, sculptures, and prints in Iqaluit. Founded in 1969 in a former Hudson's Bay Company building, it focuses on the history and material culture of the Nunavut region, with a particular emphasis on the pre-contact and early contact period.

The collections include tools, clothing, hunting equipment, and examples of the carving and printmaking traditions that Inuit artists developed — and for which the Cape Dorset workshops became internationally celebrated in the 20th century. The museum is small but well-curated and gives useful context for understanding the region before heading out into the tundra. Open Tuesday through Saturday; admission is modest.
