Rankin Inlet is a Kivalliq hub on the west coast of Hudson Bay, remote by southern Canadian standards but central to travel across mainland [Nunavut](/northamerica/canada/nunavut). It began its modern life beside a nickel mine in the 1950s and now works as a government, transport, and service centre for the region. The town is windy, practical, and very Arctic: gravel roads, wide sky, tundra at the edge of town, and light that changes the mood of the place by the hour.

The strongest reason to linger is the mix of art and land. [Matchbox Gallery](/northamerica/canada/nunavut/rankin_inlet/matchbox_gallery) is tied to Rankin's unusual role in Inuit ceramics, while the [Kivalliq Regional Visitor Centre](/northamerica/canada/nunavut/rankin_inlet/kivalliq_regional_visitor_centre) gives a useful introduction to local wildlife, crafts, and travel conditions. North of town, [Iqalugaarjuup Nunanga Territorial Park](/northamerica/canada/nunavut/rankin_inlet/iqalugaarjuup_nunanga_territorial_park) protects tundra, river country, and archaeological traces of Thule and Caribou Inuit camps.

Rankin is not a casual detour; there are no roads from southern Canada, and weather can change plans quickly. But if you are already moving through Nunavut, it rewards a spare day with a real sense of the Kivalliq coast rather than just another airport stop.
