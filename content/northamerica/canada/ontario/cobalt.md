Cobalt is a small northern Ontario town with a big industrial story. In the early 1900s, silver discoveries here triggered one of Canada's great mining rushes, and the town still carries the hard edges of that boom: headframes, mine buildings, steep streets, and old commercial blocks squeezed into rocky ground.

The reason to come is the **Cobalt Mining District National Historic Site**, not a single polished museum. The best visit combines the Mining Museum, the Heritage Silver Trail, the old railway station, and a slow look at how close the mines were to daily town life.

Cobalt is not pretty in the usual lakeside Ontario way, and that is part of its value. It feels like a working mining camp that never quite became a theme park. If you are driving through Temiskaming country and like industrial history, it is worth the stop.
