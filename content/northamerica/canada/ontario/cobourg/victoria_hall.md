  When Victoria Hall opened in 1860 the Prince of Wales — the future Edward VII — was sent to open it personally. The tiny town of Cobourg had spent the equivalent of years of municipal revenue on a courtroom modelled on London's Old Bailey, and there was, at the time, a serious if optimistic plan to make Cobourg the capital of the new province of Ontario.

Victoria Hall is the building that defines Cobourg. The neoclassical sandstone front, with its pillared portico and clock-towered cupola, sits four-square on King Street and looks more like a small provincial parliament than a town hall — which is more or less what its 1860 builders had in mind. The interior holds a concert hall, the council chamber, and a courtroom modelled on the Old Bailey in London, still in occasional ceremonial use.

The building is open to the public on weekdays and for events. The ground-floor Art Gallery of Northumberland (free entry) is worth a quick walk-through for its Canadian art collection and rotating shows. Concerts in the upstairs Concert Hall are an experience in themselves — the acoustic is exceptional for a town this size. Look up at the ceilings: the trompe-l'œil plasterwork is original.
