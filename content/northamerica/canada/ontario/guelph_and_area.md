Guelph is a university city with a strong arts scene and some of the best-preserved limestone architecture in Ontario. Settled in the 1820s, the downtown still has a handsome Victorian core built in the local grey stone, centred on the [Basilica of Our Lady Immaculate](/northamerica/canada/ontario/guelph_and_area/basilica_of_our_lady) — an imposing neo-Gothic cathedral visible from most of the city. The University of Guelph gives the place energy and keeps the music and gallery scene lively for a city of its size.

The [Guelph Farmers' Market](/northamerica/canada/ontario/guelph_and_area/guelph_farmers_market) has operated since the city's founding in 1827, one of the oldest continuous markets in Ontario. [McCrae House](/northamerica/canada/ontario/guelph_and_area/mccrae_house), birthplace of the poet who wrote "In Flanders Fields," is a small but affecting museum near the Speed River. The [Old City Hall](/northamerica/canada/ontario/guelph_and_area/old_city_hall), a National Historic Site, anchors the limestone streetscape around the river. The [River Run Centre](/northamerica/canada/ontario/guelph_and_area/river_run_centre) hosts the Guelph Jazz Festival in October — one of the better music events in the region.

The Hillside Festival, held at Guelph Lake in late July, is a long-running folk and roots music weekend with a loyal following across Ontario. The surrounding area has some good day-trip options. Elora, 25 km to the northwest, has a dramatic limestone gorge and an attractive 19th-century village. The covered bridge at West Montrose — built in 1881 and still used by Mennonite horse-and-buggy traffic — is one of the last of its kind in Ontario. St. Jacobs, in the heart of Mennonite country near Waterloo, is worth a morning at the farmers' market.
