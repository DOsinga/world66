story: "When Abitibi Power and Paper laid out Iroquois Falls in 1913, it followed the progressive company town model then in vogue: workers' housing built to a higher standard than typical mill housing, with proper streets, a recreation hall, a library, and civic amenities. The grid it imposed is still legible in the town's layout. Walking the original residential blocks — particularly along Second and Third Avenues near the original mill site — gives a clear sense of how deliberately this community was constructed from nothing, in the middle of the boreal forest, within a decade."
---

The residential core of Iroquois Falls preserves one of the most intact early-20th-century company town layouts in Ontario. The streets of workers' cottages, civic buildings, and recreational facilities built by Abitibi Power and Paper between 1913 and 1930 still stand largely unchanged. A self-guided walking route can be picked up at the museum.
