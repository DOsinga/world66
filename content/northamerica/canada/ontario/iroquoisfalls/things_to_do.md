---

Iroquois Falls is not a destination in the conventional sense — it is a place you visit to understand how northern Ontario was built, and because the landscape around it is genuinely wild and accessible. The Abitibi River, which runs through town, offers good paddling and fishing for walleye and pike. Rene Brunelle Provincial Park, 15 kilometres south, has a campground, a beach on Lake Commando, and boreal forest trails that see almost no one on a weekday.

The town itself rewards a slow walk through the residential grid. The company town layout — identical workers' cottages on measured lots, a community hall, a school, a recreational centre all placed with deliberate care by Abitibi Power and Paper — is unusually intact for early-20th-century industrial planning. The Iroquois Falls Museum, in the town's former fire hall, documents both the mill's history and the lives of the people who ran it.
