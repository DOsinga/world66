  Bobcaygeon was immortalised in The Tragically Hip's 1999 song "Bobcaygeon," written after singer Gord Downie spent time there following a difficult period. The town embraced the association with characteristic Canadian self-deprecation — there is a festival, a mural, and a bridge named in the band's honour.

Bobcaygeon is one of the prettiest towns in the Kawartha Lakes region, sitting at the junction of Sturgeon and Pigeon Lakes on the Trent-Severn Waterway. The main lock in the centre of town is the focal point of summer activity: boats queue up to pass through, ice cream is consumed, and the waterfront buzzes with a pleasant cottager energy.

The downtown has shops, galleries, and restaurants in old limestone buildings. The surrounding area is excellent for canoeing and kayaking on the connected lakes, and fishing for walleye and bass is taken seriously by the local community. Bobcaygeon is about 40 kilometres north of Lindsay — allow an hour of driving on cottager-clogged summer roads.
