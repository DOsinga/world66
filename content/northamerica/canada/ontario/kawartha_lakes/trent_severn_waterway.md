story: >
  The Trent-Severn Waterway was completed in 1920 after 87 years of construction — one of the longest infrastructure projects in Canadian history. It was built to connect the Bay of Quinte on Lake Ontario to Georgian Bay on Lake Huron, replacing a grueling overland portage route. By the time it opened, the railways had made it commercially obsolete, so it became a pleasure waterway almost immediately.
---

The Trent-Severn Waterway is a 386-kilometre canal and river system that passes through the heart of Kawartha Lakes, threading through a chain of lakes linked by 44 locks. Parks Canada manages the waterway as a National Historic Site, and its locks are among the most photogenic in Canada — stone chambers where pleasure boats rise and fall on their way through the lake chain.

Lindsay sits on the waterway. You can watch boats lock through at the town centre, rent a canoe or kayak and paddle a section of it, or catch a larger vessel through the Peterborough Lift Lock (a short drive east), which is the highest hydraulic lift lock in the world. The canal walking trails along the waterway banks make for easy cycling and walking through farmland and cottage country.
