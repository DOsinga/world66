Kenora sits on the north shore of [Lake of the Woods](/northamerica/canada/ontario/kenora/lake_of_the_woods), a vast freshwater lake straddling the Ontario-Manitoba border with over 14,000 islands and some of the best fishing in central Canada. The town is small — around 15,000 people — but it punches above its weight as a summer destination, drawing cottagers and anglers from Winnipeg (two hours west) and Thunder Bay.

The waterfront along the lake is Kenora's best feature: the [Harbourfront](/northamerica/canada/ontario/kenora/harbourfront) with its boardwalk and shops, a beach at [Norman Park](/northamerica/canada/ontario/kenora/norman_park_beach), and the dock for the [M.S. Kenora](/northamerica/canada/ontario/kenora/ms_kenora) cruise boat. [Husky the Muskie](/northamerica/canada/ontario/kenora/husky_the_muskie), a giant fibreglass fish statue, is the town's beloved landmark and the obligatory photo stop. In summer the lake is the point — fishing for walleye, muskie, and lake trout, canoeing between islands, or just swimming off a dock at [Coney Island](/northamerica/canada/ontario/kenora/coney_island). In winter the lake freezes solid enough for ice fishing and snowmobiling.

The town itself is unpretentious: a few blocks of Main Street shops, decent restaurants for a place this size, and the kind of relaxed pace that comes from being genuinely remote. Kenora is the only Ontario town in the Central time zone, a geographic oddity that underlines just how far west you are.
