Muskoka Heritage Place is an open-air living history museum on the edge of Huntsville that reconstructs life in the Muskoka district from the 1860s to the early 20th century. A cluster of restored and replica buildings — a blacksmith shop, a general store, a schoolhouse, a farmstead — are staffed by interpreters in period dress who demonstrate the trades and daily routines of early settlers.

The museum also houses a small collection of steam-powered machinery and a working sawmill. The highlight for many visitors is a ride on a narrow-gauge steam train that circles the property. The indoor portion of the museum includes artefacts from the Indigenous peoples of the region and from the first wave of European settlers who arrived after the Crown Survey of the 1860s. Open late June to early September, Tuesday to Sunday.
