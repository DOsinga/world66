Niagara Region is the compact corner of southern [Ontario](/northamerica/canada/ontario) where Canada does spectacle and small pleasures in the same afternoon. The obvious draw is [Niagara Falls](/northamerica/canada/ontario/niagrafalls), but the region is more than a waterfall stop: it has vineyard roads, War of 1812 battlefields, canal towns, lake beaches, and a surprisingly good food-and-wine scene packed into a short drive.

The Niagara River forms the eastern edge, cutting a gorge between Canada and the United States before spilling over the falls. North along the parkway, [Niagara-on-the-Lake](/northamerica/canada/ontario/niagara_on_the_lake) is the polished counterpoint to the neon around the falls, with theatre, inns, and wineries. Inland, [St. Catharines](/northamerica/canada/ontario/stcatharines) is the practical urban centre, [Welland](/northamerica/canada/ontario/welland) is built around the canal, and [Fort Erie](/northamerica/canada/ontario/fort_erie) looks across the river to Buffalo.

The best way to see Niagara is not to rush straight to the brink and leave. Walk the [Niagara Glen](/northamerica/canada/ontario/niagara/niagara_glen), ride the [Whirlpool Aero Car](/northamerica/canada/ontario/niagara/whirlpool_aero_car), watch a freighter climb through the [Welland Canal Lock 3](/northamerica/canada/ontario/niagara/welland_canal_lock_3), then spend the evening in wine country. It is busy, commercial, and sometimes shamelessly touristy, but the geography is strong enough to survive all that.
