  Fort George was captured by American forces in May 1813 after a combined land and naval assault. The British abandoned the position and withdrew, but retook the fort in December of the same year when they recaptured the Niagara frontier. By then, American troops retreating south had burned the town of Newark (now Niagara-on-the-Lake) to the ground on a freezing December night, leaving hundreds of civilians homeless.

Fort George is a National Historic Site at the northern edge of town, a British military post originally built between 1796 and 1799 and reconstructed after being destroyed in the War of 1812. The fort saw direct action in 1813, when American forces captured it after a naval bombardment; the British retook it later that year.

The reconstruction is detailed and well-maintained. Costumed interpreters demonstrate musket drills, cooking, and camp life. The officers' quarters, guardhouse, blockhouses, and powder magazine are all restored to their 1812-era appearance. Fort George makes an excellent half-day and is particularly worthwhile for anyone interested in Canadian or American military history. It sits on the banks of the Niagara River with views across to Fort Niagara in New York State.
