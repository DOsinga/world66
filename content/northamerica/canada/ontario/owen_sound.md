Owen Sound sits at the head of a long inlet of Georgian Bay, where harbour, limestone escarpment, and small-city Ontario meet. It is not as famous as the Bruce Peninsula towns to the north, but it has enough of its own shape to reward a spare day: waterfalls above town, a working waterfront, and a strong link to Canadian landscape painter Tom Thomson.

The escarpment gives Owen Sound its best scenery. Inglis Falls drops 18 metres over the Niagara Escarpment just south of town, and Harrison Park links forest paths, streams, and the Bruce Trail within the city. Down by the harbour, the old rail and shipping story is still visible in the grain elevators and waterfront streets.

Owen Sound's cultural anchor is the Tom Thomson Art Gallery, which makes more sense here than almost anywhere else: Thomson grew up in the area, and the Georgian Bay country around town helps explain the hard edges and northern light in his paintings. Use Owen Sound as a base for nearby Bruce Peninsula trips, but give the town itself at least an afternoon.
