story: >
  The centrepiece of the museum is a Handley Page Halifax Mk VII heavy bomber — one of only two surviving in the world. Halifax NA337 was shot down over a Norwegian lake in 1945 while serving with the Royal Air Force. It lay on the lake bottom for five decades until sonar located it in 1981. A painstaking recovery effort brought it to the surface in 1995, and after years of restoration it was unveiled at CFB Trenton in 2005. It is the only airworthy-era Halifax with most of its original metal intact.
---

Canada's national museum of air force history sits on the grounds of CFB Trenton, the largest Royal Canadian Air Force base in the country. The collection spans from the earliest days of Canadian military aviation in the First World War right through to modern jet aircraft, told through restored aircraft, uniforms, photographs, and personal accounts of Canadian airmen and women.

The indoor exhibition hall holds the extraordinary Halifax bomber alongside a Bristol Bolingbroke, a de Havilland Mosquito, and several other carefully restored aircraft. Outside, a large open-air park displays fighter jets, transport planes, and helicopters that visitors can walk among freely. Admission to the outdoor park is free; the indoor halls have a modest charge. The museum also maintains an extensive archive and research library for those tracing a relative's service history.
