story: >
  The Trent–Severn Waterway took 87 years to build, from 1833 to 1920 — a record of indecision, funding crises, and changing engineering fashions. Construction began at Bobcaygeon as a commercial venture and inched northward and southward for nearly a century before the route was finally completed. Lock 1 at Trenton, the southern terminus, was one of the last sections finished, making it both the start and the end of a very long story.
---

Lock 1 marks the southern end of the Trent–Severn Waterway, the 386-kilometre canal-and-lake route that connects Lake Ontario at Trenton to Georgian Bay at Port Severn. This is where boats enter or exit the waterway system, rising 5.4 metres in the first lock before beginning (or ending) a journey through 44 locks, two marine railways, and a hydraulic lift lock that remain remarkable pieces of 19th- and early 20th-century engineering.

In summer the lock is busy with pleasure cruisers, sailboats, and the occasional houseboat working their way through. Parks Canada operates the lock station, and the surrounding waterfront park has been cleaned up considerably over the years — there is a pier, picnic tables, and good views across the Trent River. You don't need a boat to appreciate it: the lock operation itself is worth watching, and the adjacent park makes a pleasant spot to eat lunch watching the traffic go through.
