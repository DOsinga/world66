St. Joseph Island sits in the north channel of Lake Huron, southeast of Sault Ste. Marie, with a bridge to the mainland but a mood that still feels insular. It is cottage country, farm country, and maple syrup country: quiet roads loop through hardwoods, shoreline villages, and old family farms.

The island's best reason to come is the mix of soft scenery and hard history. [Fort St. Joseph](/northamerica/canada/ontario/st_joseph_island/fort_st_joseph) was once Britain's western military post on the upper Great Lakes, and its ruins now sit in a grassy Parks Canada site at the island's southern tip. The [St. Joseph Island Museum](/northamerica/canada/ontario/st_joseph_island/st_joseph_island_museum) fills in the pioneer and farming story.

This is not a place for a packed checklist. Come for a slow drive around Highway 548, a museum stop, a walk at the fort, and maple syrup if you are here in season. In late September and October the hardwoods give the island its prettiest weeks.
