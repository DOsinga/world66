High Park is Toronto's largest urban park at 161 hectares, running along the western edge of the city near the lake. It manages to contain a surprising amount of variety: natural oak savannah, a ravine, an outdoor swimming pool, a small zoo with free admission, tennis courts, a children's playground, and Grenadier Pond (named for the British soldiers who once fished here), which becomes a skating rink in a cold winter.

The park is most famous for its Japanese cherry blossoms each spring — an event that now draws tens of thousands of visitors on peak weekends. In summer, the Dream in High Park outdoor Shakespeare festival performs in the park's natural amphitheatre (evenings, pay-what-you-can). At the south end, Colborne Lodge is a well-preserved 19th-century house museum that belonged to the park's original owner, John George Howard.

The park is accessible from High Park station on the Bloor-Danforth subway line. Bloor Street runs along the northern boundary; cyclists can access it from the Martin Goodman Trail along the waterfront.
