The Toronto Reference Library on Yonge Street, designed by Raymond Moriyama and opened in 1977, is the largest public reference library in North America. The central atrium is extraordinary — five stories of walkways and balconies surrounding a sunlit central void, with a waterfall and pool at the base. The architecture is one of the most successful library designs of the 20th century: it manages to be grand without being intimidating.

The collection runs to about four million items, including a notable Arthur Conan Doyle archive and significant historical photograph collections. Unlike many major reference libraries, it is open to anyone — you do not need a library card or academic credentials to walk in, use the materials, or sit in the reading rooms.

Free WiFi and comfortable seating make it a popular working spot for freelancers and students. It is directly on the Yonge subway line (Bloor-Yonge station), one stop from the ROM and within walking distance of Yorkville.
