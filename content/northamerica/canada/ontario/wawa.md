Wawa is a small lakefront town on the Trans-Canada Highway, 216 km north of Sault Ste. Marie. It is a mining town, but better known as a base for outdoor and wilderness adventures — boating, hiking, fishing, hunting, and exploring the vast Canadian Shield country that stretches in every direction.

"Wawa" means "wild goose" in Ojibway, and the town has leaned into this with a giant Canada [Goose monument](/northamerica/canada/ontario/wawa/wawa_goose) standing over the highway junction. The steel statue went up in 1963, replacing an earlier plaster version that couldn't handle the Northern Ontario weather. It was the brainchild of a local businessman who wanted to draw travellers off the Trans-Canada and into town after the highway's last link was completed in 1960, bypassing the downtown area. The goose worked — it remains one of the most photographed roadside landmarks in Canada.

Beyond the monument, Wawa is a useful base for exploring [Lake Superior Provincial Park](/northamerica/canada/ontario/wawa/lake_superior_provincial_park) just to the south, paddling and swimming on [Wawa Lake](/northamerica/canada/ontario/wawa/wawa_lake), and stopping at [Magpie High Falls](/northamerica/canada/ontario/wawa/magpie_high_falls) on the way out of town.
