Naufrage is a tiny bayside village in Kings County on Prince Edward Island's northeast coast, the kind of quiet PEI that visitors come looking for — farm country, red sand beaches, and clear Atlantic water. Seals are a regular sight from the shore in the morning. The name comes from the French word for shipwreck — this stretch of coastline was dangerous for sailing ships in the age before a lighthouse.

The [Naufrage Lighthouse](/northamerica/canada/princeedwardisland/naufrage/naufrage_lighthouse) stands on the harbour point and the [harbour](/northamerica/canada/princeedwardisland/naufrage/naufrage_harbour) is a working lobster fishery with a boardwalk through a salt marsh conservation area. The [beaches](/northamerica/canada/princeedwardisland/naufrage/pei_red_sand_beaches) here are uncrowded even in high summer. The nearby [Greenwich section of PEI National Park](/northamerica/canada/princeedwardisland/naufrage/greenwich_national_park), about 15 kilometres west, has one of the finest dune and beach systems on the island.
