Baie-Comeau sits on the north shore of the St. Lawrence near the mouth of the Manicouagan River, about 420 km northeast of [Québec City](/northamerica/canada/quebec/quebeccity). It is not a postcard old town. It is a working Côte-Nord city, shaped by paper mills, aluminium, hydro power, port traffic, and the long road toward Labrador.

That industrial edge is part of the story. The modern town grew quickly after a paper mill was built in 1936, then expanded with the hydroelectric projects on the Manicouagan and Outardes rivers. The city still feels split between Baie-Comeau proper and Hauterive, so a car helps.

For travelers, Baie-Comeau is a useful North Shore base. Route 138 follows the St. Lawrence, ferries cross to Matane on the south shore, and Route 389 begins here before pushing north toward Fermont and the Trans-Labrador Highway. The coast, boreal forest, and big-river country around town are the real draw.
