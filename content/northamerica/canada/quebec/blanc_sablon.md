Blanc-Sablon is the far eastern end of Quebec: a small municipality of three villages where the province meets Labrador and the Strait of Belle Isle. It is remote, windy, and practical, with ferries, fishing history, low houses, and a coast that feels closer to Newfoundland than to Montreal.

The place clears the bar because its identity is strong. The [Blanc-Sablon National Historic Site](/northamerica/canada/quebec/blanc_sablon/blanc_sablon_national_historic_site) protects evidence of thousands of years of human use on this shore. Offshore, [Baie de Brador Migratory Bird Sanctuary](/northamerica/canada/quebec/blanc_sablon/baie_de_brador_migratory_bird_sanctuary) shelters seabird colonies, including puffins. Above Lourdes-de-Blanc-Sablon, [Mont Parent](/northamerica/canada/quebec/blanc_sablon/mont_parent) gives the simplest sense of the headlands, bays, and big northern water.

Do not come expecting a polished resort town. Blanc-Sablon is for travellers already moving along the Lower North Shore, the Labrador road, or the Newfoundland ferry route who want to stop and understand where they are.
