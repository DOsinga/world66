Fermont is a remote iron-mining town in northern Québec, near the Labrador border and far up Route 389 from Baie-Comeau. It is not pretty in the usual sense, but it is fascinating: a planned subarctic company town built around one huge idea, the long building everyone calls "Le Mur", or the Wall.

The Wall is Fermont's defining feature. It holds apartments, services, shops, a hotel, schools, and sports facilities, and it was placed to shield the rest of town from brutal north winds. In winter, that design is not a curiosity but a survival strategy. The town's connection to [Labrador City](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Labrador_City) and the Mont-Wright mine gives it a frontier-industrial feel quite different from the St. Lawrence valley version of Québec.

Visitors usually come because they are driving the long road between the Côte-Nord and Labrador, working in mining, or looking for northern hunting, fishing, and outdoor space. Fermont is a practical stop, but the [Wall](/northamerica/canada/quebec/fermont/le_mur) and the scale of the [Mont-Wright mining complex](/northamerica/canada/quebec/fermont/mont_wright_mining_complex) make it more than a fuel-and-sleep dot on the map.
