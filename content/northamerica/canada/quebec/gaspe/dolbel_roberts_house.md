Dolbel-Roberts House is a restored historic home now used as a small museum about communities at the eastern end of the Gaspe Peninsula. Its exhibits cover Mi'kmaq history, New France settlers, later fishing families, and newer immigrant communities.

The museum is also useful for understanding the controversial expropriations that created Forillon National Park in 1970, when local families were forced from their homes.
