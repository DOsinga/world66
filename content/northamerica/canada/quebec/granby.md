Granby is a pleasant mid-sized city of around 70,000 people in the **Eastern Townships** (Cantons-de-l'Est) of southern Quebec, about 80 km east of Montreal. It grew up as a 19th-century English-speaking textile town and still carries some of that Anglo-Quebec heritage in its red-brick downtown and the surrounding dairy-farm country, though today the population is overwhelmingly francophone.

Granby is best known for its [zoo](/northamerica/canada/quebec/granby/granby_zoo) — one of the largest in Canada, with more than 1,500 animals in natural enclosures and an adjoining water park, set on forested grounds just outside town. It draws hundreds of thousands of visitors each year and is one of the most popular family destinations in Quebec. Beyond the zoo, [Yamaska National Park](/northamerica/canada/quebec/granby/yamaska_national_park) on the town's edge has a reservoir lake, bike trails, and beaches that get busy on summer weekends.

The surrounding Cantons-de-l'Est wine country and the small villages of the Eastern Townships make excellent day trips. Dunham, 30 km south, is the centre of Quebec's wine region, with a cluster of small vineyards along Chemin de Dunham. The [Musée d'art de Granby](/northamerica/canada/quebec/granby/musee_dart_de_granby) in the downtown offers a look at contemporary Quebec art if you want something quieter after a day at the zoo.
