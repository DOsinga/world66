Havre-Saint-Pierre is a small harbour town on Quebec's far North Shore, where the Gulf of St Lawrence feels wide, cold, and very alive. It is remote by southern Quebec standards, but not vague: fishing boats, Acadian family names, Innu culture, and the pale limestone islands offshore give it a clear sense of place.

The reason to come is the [Mingan Archipelago National Park Reserve](/northamerica/canada/quebec/havre_saint_pierre/mingan_archipelago_national_park_reserve). Boats leave from town for islands of sea-carved monoliths, low boreal forest, puffins, seals, and cold-water plants that look almost Arctic. It is one of eastern Canada's stranger coastal landscapes, and Havre-Saint-Pierre is the most practical base for seeing it.

The town itself rewards a slow half day before or after the boat. Visit the [Maison de la Culture Roland-Jomphe](/northamerica/canada/quebec/havre_saint_pierre/maison_de_la_culture_roland_jomphe), walk the harbour, and drive east to [Cap Ferré](/northamerica/canada/quebec/havre_saint_pierre/cap_ferre) for open Gulf views. This is not a polished resort town; it is better than that if you like working coasts, long light, and places that still feel far from the centre.
