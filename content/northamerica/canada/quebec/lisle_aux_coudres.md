L'Isle-aux-Coudres is a low, inhabited island in the St. Lawrence River, just offshore from the Charlevoix coast. It feels close to the mainland, because the ferry crossing from Saint-Joseph-de-la-Rive takes only about fifteen minutes, but the mood changes as soon as you roll off the boat: orchards, tidal flats, old chapels, mill buildings, and a ring road that keeps returning to the river.

The island works best as a slow day. Cycle the loop road, stop at the windmill and watermill, taste cider from island fruit, and leave time for the river viewpoints on the west and east ends. It is not a place of grand monuments, but it has a strong island identity and a rhythm that makes sense only once you are moving at bicycle speed.

L'Isle-aux-Coudres is especially good if you are already in Charlevoix and want a day that is not another mountain lookout or art-gallery stop in Baie-Saint-Paul. Bring layers: the St. Lawrence is broad here, and even summer sun can come with a sharp wind.
