Mont-Tremblant is the best ski resort in eastern Canada and one of the top ten in North America. It sits in the Laurentian Mountains about 140km north of [Montréal](/northamerica/canada/quebec/montreal), an easy two-hour drive. The resort has grown from a small ski village into a full year-round destination, with a pedestrian village at the base of the mountain that fills with visitors in both winter and summer.

The [ski mountain](/northamerica/canada/quebec/mont_tremblant_que/ski_mountain) has 102 trails, 14 lifts and a vertical drop of 645 metres. The north side is steeper and more challenging; the south side gets more sun and is better for beginners and intermediates. Snowfall is reliable — the Laurentians average about 350cm per year — and snowmaking covers the main runs. The ski season typically runs from late November to mid-April.

In summer, Mont-Tremblant transforms into a hiking, cycling and water sports destination. The mountain itself is climbable on foot or by gondola, and [Lac Tremblant](/northamerica/canada/quebec/mont_tremblant_que/lac_tremblant) and the surrounding lakes offer kayaking, canoeing and swimming. The village hosts music festivals through the summer, including a popular blues and jazz festival in August. [Mont-Tremblant National Park](/northamerica/canada/quebec/mont_tremblant_que/mont_tremblant_national_park) begins just north of the resort and offers exceptional canoeing and hiking in a far quieter setting. The historic [Circuit Mont-Tremblant](/northamerica/canada/quebec/mont_tremblant_que/circuit_mont_tremblant) — a legendary road racing circuit that hosted early Canadian Grand Prix races — is worth a detour for motorsport enthusiasts.
