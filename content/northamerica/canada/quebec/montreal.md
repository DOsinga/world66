Montreal is the beating heart of French-speaking North America and one of the most culturally rich cities on the continent. Built on an island in the St. Lawrence River, it blends European charm with North American energy in a way no other city quite manages. The locals call it *joie de vivre* -- joy of living -- and you will feel it in the packed terrasses on summer evenings, the [underground city](/northamerica/canada/quebec/montreal/underground_city) humming through winter, and the seemingly endless cycle of festivals that keeps the streets alive year-round.

The city is a genuine melting pot. French is the mother tongue, but English is everywhere, and waves of immigration from Haiti, North Africa, Italy, Greece, Portugal, and Latin America have layered the culture and especially the food. You will hear conversations shift between languages mid-sentence. This is not Paris and it is not New York -- it is something entirely its own.

Montreal's neighborhoods each have a distinct personality. The cobblestoned streets of [Old Montreal](/northamerica/canada/quebec/montreal/old_montreal) feel like stepping into a French port town, with the [Basilique Notre-Dame](/northamerica/canada/quebec/montreal/notre_dame_basilica) and the [Old Port](/northamerica/canada/quebec/montreal/vieux_port) as anchors. The [Plateau Mont-Royal](/northamerica/canada/quebec/montreal/plateau_mont_royal) is bohemian and walkable, lined with colorful row houses, independent bookshops, and some of the best restaurants in the city. [Mile End](/northamerica/canada/quebec/montreal/mile_end) is the creative quarter — bagel shops, coffee roasters, street art, and music venues. Downtown has the skyscrapers, the shopping, and the underground city that connects 33 kilometers of tunnels, malls, and metro stations. [The Village](/northamerica/canada/quebec/montreal/the_village) is one of North America's largest LGBTQ+ neighborhoods, especially vibrant during summer when Sainte-Catherine Street becomes a pedestrian zone.

You cannot visit Montreal without trying a smoked meat sandwich — thick-cut, peppery, piled on rye bread with mustard. [Schwartz's](/northamerica/canada/quebec/montreal/schwartz_smoked_meat) on Saint-Laurent has been serving them since 1928 and the line out the door is part of the experience. The other essential is poutine — fries, cheese curds, and gravy — best experienced at [La Banquise](/northamerica/canada/quebec/montreal/poutine_rapide), open 24 hours with 30 varieties. On summer Sundays, the [Tam-Tams](/northamerica/canada/quebec/montreal/tam_tams) drum circle at the base of [Mount Royal](/northamerica/canada/quebec/montreal/mount_royal) is one of the city's most joyful and spontaneous rituals.

The city is a major transportation hub with direct flights from across North America and Europe into Trudeau International Airport (YUL), and easy highway and rail connections to [Toronto](/northamerica/canada/ontario/toronto), [Ottawa](/northamerica/canada/ontario/ottawa), [Quebec City](/northamerica/canada/quebec/quebeccity), and the US northeast.
