Montreal has produced an extraordinary range of literature in both French and English. These are the books that will help you understand the city.

**Beautiful Losers** by Leonard Cohen (1966). Cohen's second novel is set in Montreal and wanders across the city and through Quebec history in the way only Cohen could — surreal, erotic, and dense with Catholic imagery. Not an easy read but an essential one for understanding the city's creative obsessions.

**The Watch that Ends the Night** by Hugh MacLennan (1959). MacLennan's novel follows a Montreal man through the Depression years and the Spanish Civil War. Better than his more famous *Two Solitudes*, it captures the texture of Montreal's English-speaking community and its relationship to the city's French majority.

**Cockroach** by Rawi Hage (2008). Hage's Montreal novel follows an unnamed immigrant narrator through the streets and rooming houses of a winter city — dark, funny, and alive to the alienation of the immigrant experience. Hage himself is a Lebanese-Canadian writer who spent years in Montreal.

**Five Roses** by Alice Zorn (2016). A novel set in the neighbourhoods of Pointe-Saint-Charles and Little Burgundy as they gentrify — two women, two storylines, and a vivid sense of the neighbourhoods that existed before the coffee shops arrived.

**Montreal Stories** by Mavis Gallant. Gallant was born in Montreal in 1922 and left Canada for Paris in 1950, but her fiction returns obsessively to the city of her childhood — its class divisions, its cold winters, and the particular loneliness of its Anglo-Protestant society. The stories are among the finest short fiction written about the city.
