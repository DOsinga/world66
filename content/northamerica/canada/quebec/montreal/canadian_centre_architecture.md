The Canadian Centre for Architecture is one of the most serious architecture museums in the world — a research institution, a library, and a public gallery combined in a building that is itself a statement. The main structure wraps around the Shaughnessy House, a restored 1874 grey limestone mansion; the new wings curve around it to form internal gardens and a sculpture garden on the street. The whole ensemble is one of the finest pieces of contemporary architecture in Montreal.

The exhibitions focus on architectural history, urbanism, and the relationship between buildings and cities — serious and intellectually demanding, but not inaccessible. The library holds one of the largest collections of architectural drawings, photographs, and archives in the world. The sculpture garden on the south side of the building, accessible from the street, is free and features eccentric architectural follies by Melvin Charney. Worth combining with a visit to the Musée des Beaux-Arts, a short walk away.
