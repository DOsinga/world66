Montreal's Little Italy — centred on boulevard Saint-Laurent north of avenue Mozart — is one of the most genuine ethnic neighbourhoods in the city. The Italian community that settled here in the early 20th century built cafés, grocers, pastry shops, and social clubs that still anchor the neighbourhood today. The best espresso in Montreal comes from this quarter: Caffè Italia on Saint-Laurent has been serving dense, old-school shots since 1956, and the handful of rival cafés on the same stretch maintain the same standard.

The neighbourhood's crown jewel is the Marché Jean-Talon, the largest outdoor market in North America, which lies at its southern end. Around it, specialty food shops — pasta makers, Italian delis, cheese importers — supply both home cooks and restaurant chefs. On summer evenings, the terrasses on boulevard Dante and the side streets fill with Italian families and the neighbourhood takes on an easy Mediterranean rhythm that feels unlike anywhere else in the city.
