Mile End is the neighbourhood that feels most distinctly Montreal in the 21st century — a palimpsest of Jewish, Greek, Portuguese, and French-Canadian history overwritten by artists, musicians, and tech workers, and somehow still holding most of those layers at once. Boulevard Saint-Laurent (the Main) runs through it, flanked by the independent shops and restaurants that have made this one of the most creative urban stretches in Canada.

The famous bagel bakeries — St-Viateur and Fairmount, open 24 hours — have been on the same streets since the 1950s and produce a distinctly different product from their New York counterparts: sweeter, denser, wood-fired. The debate over which is better is a local religion. Rue Saint-Viateur and avenue Bernard are the neighbourhood's main pedestrian streets, lined with coffee shops, bookshops, record stores, and restaurant terrasses. The Hasidic Jewish community centred around Parc Avenue coexists alongside the hip cafés in a way that still surprises first-time visitors.
