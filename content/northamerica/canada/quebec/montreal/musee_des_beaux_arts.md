The Musée des Beaux-Arts de Montréal is the largest art museum in Canada and one of the great collections in North America. Founded in 1860, it has grown across five interconnected pavilions on Sherbrooke Street West, spanning the street with an underground passage. The permanent collection covers everything from pre-Columbian artefacts and Québécois decorative arts to Rembrandt, Monet, Picasso, and a strong representation of contemporary Canadian artists.

The Michal and Renata Hornstein Pavilion of Peace focuses on world cultures and international art, while the Liliane and David M. Stewart Pavilion houses international design and decorative arts. Temporary exhibitions are consistently ambitious — the museum has hosted blockbuster shows on major artists and themes drawn from European institutions. The Bourgie Concert Hall inside the museum hosts chamber music series. Permanent collections are free on Wednesday evenings; temporary exhibitions are ticketed.
