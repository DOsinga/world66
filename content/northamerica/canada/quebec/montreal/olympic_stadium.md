The Olympic Stadium is one of the most recognizable buildings in Canada — and one of the most controversial. Designed by French architect Roger Taillibert for the 1976 Summer Games, its curving concrete form and 175-metre inclined tower are unmistakable on the east-end skyline. The stadium seats 56,000 and has hosted baseball (the Expos, until 2004), football, concerts, and trade shows, though it has never quite found a permanent raison d'être since losing its baseball team.

The tower — the world's tallest inclined tower — has an observation deck reached by a funicular cable car. On a clear day you can see the Laurentian mountains to the north and the St. Lawrence to the south. Even if you find the stadium itself uninspiring, the view is worth the price of admission. The complex sits next to the Biodome, Botanical Garden, and Planetarium, making this entire corner of the east end a half-day destination on its own. Reach it by metro to Pie-IX or Viau stations.
