Natashquan sits near the end of the long road along Québec's Côte-Nord, where the Gulf of St. Lawrence, Innu country, Acadian fishing history, and Québec song all meet in a small place. It is remote enough that arrival still feels like an event.

The village is famous across Québec as the birthplace of poet and singer Gilles Vigneault, but it is not a one-name pilgrimage. [Les Galets](/northamerica/canada/quebec/natashquan/les_galets) preserves the old fishing sheds by the sea, [La Vieille École](/northamerica/canada/quebec/natashquan/la_vieille_ecole) tells local stories in a restored schoolhouse, and the shore has the big, empty northern feel that makes the drive worthwhile.

Natashquan is a spare-day place only if you are already far along the Côte-Nord. But if you are, do not rush through. The village has a strong identity, and its best sights belong to this exact edge-of-the-road setting rather than to a generic coastal stop.
