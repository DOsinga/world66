Percé is the showpiece village of the Gaspé Peninsula, stretched along the Gulf of St. Lawrence with [Percé Rock](/northamerica/canada/quebec/perce/perce_rock) just offshore. It is touristy in summer, sometimes shamelessly so, but the setting earns the attention: cliffs, sea birds, fishing history, and one of Québec's most recognisable natural landmarks.

The classic day starts on the waterfront, watching the light move across the rock, then takes a boat out toward [Bonaventure Island](/northamerica/canada/quebec/perce/bonaventure_island). The island's northern gannet colony is the real surprise; the birds are close, loud, and far more memorable than a distant nature-viewing tick on a list.

Percé is also more than its postcard view. The [Percé UNESCO Global Geopark](/northamerica/canada/quebec/perce/perce_geopark) climbs behind town on Mont Sainte-Anne and Mont Blanc, with trails, lookouts, and a glass platform high over the coast. Come between late spring and early autumn if you want the boats, trails, and village services working at full strength.
