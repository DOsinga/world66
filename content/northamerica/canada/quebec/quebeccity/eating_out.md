---
title: "Eating Out in Québec City"
type: section
---

Québec City has one of the best food scenes in Canada. The cuisine is rooted in French tradition but distinctly Québécois — rich, seasonal, and more generous with maple syrup, pork and cheese than anything you'd find in France. The city has a concentration of excellent restaurants in the Old City and the Saint-Jean-Baptiste neighbourhood.

**Rue Saint-Jean** (both inside and outside the old walls) is the best street for restaurants. **Rue du Petit-Champlain** in the Lower Town is touristy but has some genuinely good places alongside the tourist traps.

Local dishes to try: **tourtière** (a spiced meat pie traditionally made with pork or game), **cretons** (a spreadable pork pâté), **tarte au sucre** (a sweet maple-sugar tart), and poutine, which is taken very seriously here.

**Restaurant Champlain** at the Château Frontenac (418 692-3861; 1 rue des Carrières) is one of the most celebrated dining rooms in the city — formal, expensive, and excellent. [Aux Anciens Canadiens](/northamerica/canada/quebec/quebeccity/eating_out/aux_anciens_canadiens) on rue Saint-Louis serves traditional Québécois cooking in the oldest house in the Old City (built 1675). [La Bûche](/northamerica/canada/quebec/quebeccity/eating_out/la_buche) takes the sugar shack tradition into a lively Old City setting. [L'Ardoise](/northamerica/canada/quebec/quebeccity/eating_out/lardoise) in the Lower Town is the best place for mussels. For the best poutine in the city, [Chez Ashton](/northamerica/canada/quebec/quebeccity/eating_out/chez_ashton) near the Parliament Building is the local standard. Outside the tourist orbit, [Le Clocher Penché](/northamerica/canada/quebec/quebeccity/eating_out/le_clocher_penche) in Saint-Roch does excellent seasonal Québécois bistro food at honest prices.
