The oldest commercial district in North America — founded in 1608, continuously occupied since. Petit-Champlain occupies the narrow strip between the cliff face and the St. Lawrence River in the Lower Town, reached from above by the funicular or the vertiginously named Escalier Casse-Cou (Breakneck Stairs). The streets are genuinely 17th-century in layout, if not always in building fabric; stone walls and copper-roofed doorways give the neighbourhood a density and texture rare in North America.

The area is touristy — there's no avoiding it — but it avoids the worst excesses by maintaining a mix of independent shops, decent restaurants, and local artisan studios alongside the souvenir trade. In winter, the neighbourhood takes on a fairytale quality: ice sculptures, string lights, and snow making the narrow streets even more improbable.
