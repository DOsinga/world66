Rimouski is the main city of Quebec's Lower St. Lawrence, set where the river has already widened into something that feels almost like sea. It is not a postcard village; it is a working regional centre, with a university, a port, and long views across cold water. That gives it more life than many stops on Route 132.

The best reason to pause is [Pointe-au-Pere Maritime Historic Site](/northamerica/canada/quebec/rimouski/pointe_au_pere_maritime_historic_site), just east of town, where a tall lighthouse, the Onondaga submarine, and the Empress of Ireland story turn the St. Lawrence into a serious piece of maritime history. In the other direction, [Bic National Park](/northamerica/canada/quebec/rimouski/bic_national_park) protects coves, cliffs, salt marsh, and seal-haunted shoreline.

Rimouski itself is easy to like on a spare day. Walk the waterfront, take the boat to [Ile Saint-Barnabe](/northamerica/canada/quebec/rimouski/ile_saint_barnabe) in summer, then spend an hour at the [Rimouski Regional Museum](/northamerica/canada/quebec/rimouski/rimouski_regional_museum) if the weather turns. It works best for travellers moving slowly along the south shore of the St. Lawrence rather than racing toward the Gaspe Peninsula.
