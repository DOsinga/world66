Rivière-au-Tonnerre is a tiny fishing village on Québec's North Shore, far enough east that the road itself starts to feel like an expedition. It is not a polished destination, and that is the appeal: crab boats, rocky coves, big Gulf of St. Lawrence skies, and a river whose falls gave the village its thunderous name.

The two best stops are simple and strong. [Saint-Hippolyte Church](/northamerica/canada/quebec/riviere_au_tonnerre/saint_hippolyte_church) is an all-wood village church with a remarkable carved interior, built when there was no road connection. Inland, [Manitou Falls](/northamerica/canada/quebec/riviere_au_tonnerre/manitou_falls) gives the place its natural drama.

This is a Route 138 stop, not a city break. Come if you are driving the Minganie coast and want the North Shore at human scale: a boardwalk, a church, a waterfall trail, and the feeling of being a long way from the St. Lawrence corridor.
