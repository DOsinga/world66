Rouyn-Noranda is a mining city in far western Quebec, closer in feel to Northern Ontario than to the St Lawrence corridor. Copper and gold built it, and the Horne smelter still gives the city a blunt industrial edge. That edge is part of the appeal: this is not a polished resort town pretending the north is easy.

What makes Rouyn-Noranda worth a day is the mix of mining history, lakefront, and culture. [Magasin général Dumulon](/northamerica/canada/quebec/rouyn_noranda/magasin_general_dumulon) takes you back to the 1920s boom years, [Lac Osisko](/northamerica/canada/quebec/rouyn_noranda/lac_osisko) gives the centre its waterfront, and the [Musée d'art de Rouyn-Noranda](/northamerica/canada/quebec/rouyn_noranda/musee_dart_de_rouyn_noranda) shows the city's contemporary side. In the surrounding municipality, [Parc national d'Aiguebelle](/northamerica/canada/quebec/rouyn_noranda/parc_national_daiguebelle) adds the big boreal landscape.

The city also has an outsized festival calendar, especially for music and film. If you are already in Abitibi-Témiscamingue, Rouyn-Noranda is the place that best explains the region's mix of mining, forest, French-speaking culture, and northern distance.
