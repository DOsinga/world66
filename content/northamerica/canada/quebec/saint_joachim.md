Saint-Joachim is a small village on the Côte-de-Beaupré east of [Québec City](/northamerica/canada/quebec/quebeccity), where the St. Lawrence flats meet the Laurentian escarpment. It is not a town of urban sights, but its setting is strong: marshes, cliffs, old farms, and the great shoulder of Cap Tourmente rising above the river.

The two big reasons to come are outside the village centre. [Cap Tourmente National Wildlife Area](/northamerica/canada/quebec/saint_joachim/cap_tourmente_national_wildlife_area) is famous for snow geese during migration and has trails through marsh, forest, and hillside. [Canyon Sainte-Anne](/northamerica/canada/quebec/saint_joachim/canyon_sainte_anne) adds a more dramatic outing, with a high waterfall, suspension bridges, and short walks into the gorge.

Saint-Joachim also has one of the older agricultural stories on the Côte-de-Beaupré. The land here helped feed early New France, and the [Route de la Nouvelle-France](/northamerica/canada/quebec/saint_joachim/route_de_la_nouvelle_france) still ties the village to the farmsteads, churches, and river views east of Québec City.
