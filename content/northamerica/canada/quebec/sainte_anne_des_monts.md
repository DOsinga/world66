Sainte-Anne-des-Monts sits on the north shore of the Gaspé Peninsula, where the St. Lawrence estuary meets the road into the Chic-Choc Mountains. It is a small French-speaking town, but the setting gives it real pull: sea on one side, high inland mountains on the other.

Most travelers stop here for [Gaspésie National Park](/northamerica/canada/quebec/sainte_anne_des_monts/gaspesie_national_park), whose road begins in town and climbs toward Mont Albert and Mont Jacques-Cartier. The park is the main reason to make the trip, with alpine tundra, serious hiking, and a rare caribou herd south of the St. Lawrence.

The town itself adds enough to justify a base rather than a fuel stop. [Exploramer](/northamerica/canada/quebec/sainte_anne_des_monts/exploramer) gives the waterfront a useful museum and sea-life focus, the quay is good at sunset, and [La Martre Lighthouse](/northamerica/canada/quebec/sainte_anne_des_monts/la_martre_lighthouse) makes a worthwhile coastal drive east. Sainte-Anne-des-Monts is quiet, but it has a clear identity: Gaspé sea air, mountain weather, and a working edge rather than resort polish.
