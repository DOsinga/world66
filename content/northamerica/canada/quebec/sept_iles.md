Sept-Îles is the main city on Quebec's far North Shore, a long drive east of Baie-Comeau where Route 138, iron ore, Innu culture, and the Gulf of St Lawrence meet. It is remote without being tiny: ships load at the port, a railway runs north into the interior, and the bay opens around the seven islands that gave the city its name.

The town is worth a spare day if you have come this far along the coast. Start with the [Shaputuan Museum](/northamerica/canada/quebec/sept_iles/shaputuan_museum) and the [Regional Museum of the North Shore](/northamerica/canada/quebec/sept_iles/regional_museum_of_the_north_shore), then walk the old trading-post ground at [Vieux-Poste](/northamerica/canada/quebec/sept_iles/vieux_poste). The best outdoor time is around the bay: [Bois-Joli Creek Garden](/northamerica/canada/quebec/sept_iles/bois_joli_creek_garden), [Parc Aylmer-Whittom](/northamerica/canada/quebec/sept_iles/parc_aylmer_whittom), and the Pointe Noire shore all make the cold-water setting feel close.

Sept-Îles is not polished resort Quebec. It is a working northern port with beaches, spruce, hard weather, and enough culture to make a stop here feel like more than a refuelling break.
