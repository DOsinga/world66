Trois-Rivières sits on the St. Lawrence between [Montréal](/northamerica/canada/quebec/montreal) and [Québec City](/northamerica/canada/quebec/quebeccity), and it is much more than a fuel stop between the two. Founded in 1634, it is one of the oldest cities in Québec, with a small old quarter, a working-class river identity, and several museums that do not feel generic.

The city is strongest when it tells its industrial and institutional history. [Forges du Saint-Maurice](/northamerica/canada/quebec/trois_rivieres/forges_du_saint_maurice) preserves the remains of Canada's first ironworks. [Boréalis](/northamerica/canada/quebec/trois_rivieres/borealis) turns the old paper industry into a good museum, and the [Old Prison](/northamerica/canada/quebec/trois_rivieres/old_prison_of_trois_rivieres) gives the city one of Québec's more memorable guided visits.

Trois-Rivières is not as polished as Québec City, and that is part of the appeal. Come for a day if you are driving the St. Lawrence corridor; stay longer if you want an honest old river city with enough history to reward slow wandering.
