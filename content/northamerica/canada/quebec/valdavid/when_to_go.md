---
title: When to Go
type: section
---

Val-David has two distinct peak seasons and a quieter shoulder season in between.

**Winter (December to March)** brings snow and the full P'tit Train du Nord trail transforms into a cross-country ski route. Snowshoeing, ice skating on the village rink, and quiet evenings by the fire in the village's many small cafes make this a genuine winter destination rather than just a fallback option.

**Summer (June to September)** is the season for cycling the trail, rock climbing, and the village's outdoor arts events. The 1001 Pots festival in July draws ceramic artists and collectors from across Quebec, filling the village for two weeks with exhibitions and workshops. The Jardins de Rocailles is at its best in early summer when the alpine flowers are in bloom.

**Autumn (October to November)** brings the foliage. The Laurentians turn dramatically in October, and Val-David is close enough to Montréal that the village fills up on weekends. Midweek visits in October are generally quieter and the light is excellent.

Spring mud season (April to early May) is the least appealing time to visit.
