Battlefords Provincial Park sits on the south shore of Jackfish Lake, about 40 km north of North Battleford. The park's headline is its sand beach — 4 km of it, long and soft, and on warm summer afternoons crowded with day visitors from across northwestern Saskatchewan, all the way out to Lloydminster on the Alberta border. It is the most accessible piece of waterfront for a wide stretch of farming country, and it earns its popularity.

Beyond the beach, the park gives you the full prairie-summer package: tent and RV campgrounds with good facilities, an 18-hole golf course, fishing on Jackfish Lake for walleye, perch, and northern pike, and well-marked hiking trails through boreal-edge poplar and spruce forest. The forest transitions noticeably as you move away from the lake, and the trails are quiet enough that you will often have them to yourself even on busy beach days.

Winter turns the park into a cross-country skiing and snowshoeing destination, and ice fishing on Jackfish Lake is a local tradition. If you are visiting outside the summer beach rush, the campground mood drops away and you get the park largely to yourself. Arrive early on hot July and August weekends if you want a decent stretch of beach — the parking lots fill by mid-morning.

The park is an easy return day trip from North Battleford, but it also makes a sensible overnight stop if you are driving the Yellowhead Highway corridor and want to break up the journey with a swim and a proper night outdoors.
