La Ronge is where Saskatchewan changes character. South of here, the road has already left most of the prairie behind; around town, the Canadian Shield begins to show itself in rock, spruce, islands, and cold northern lakes. The result is less a sightseeing town than a practical base for wilderness.

That base is useful. [Lac La Ronge Provincial Park](/northamerica/canada/saskatchewan/la_ronge/lac_la_ronge_provincial_park) begins at the edge of town and opens into almost 100 lakes, old fur-trade canoe routes, campgrounds, and long forest trails. [Nut Point Trail](/northamerica/canada/saskatchewan/la_ronge/nut_point_trail) is the local hike to know if you want a serious day on foot without disappearing into the backcountry.

The wider area has one exceptional heritage anchor: [Holy Trinity Anglican Church](/northamerica/canada/saskatchewan/la_ronge/holy_trinity_anglican_church) at Stanley Mission, the oldest standing building in Saskatchewan. It is not a casual walk from town, but it belongs to the same Churchill River travel world that makes La Ronge worth considering.
