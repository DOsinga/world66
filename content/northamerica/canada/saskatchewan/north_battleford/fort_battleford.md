story: Fort Battleford was the North-West Mounted Police post that became the focal point of the 1885 Northwest Resistance — the brief, brutal conflict between the Métis and Cree and the Canadian government. After the conflict, eight Cree men were hanged here in the largest mass execution in Canadian history, a fact the modern site does not flinch from.
---

The fort stands on a bluff above the South Saskatchewan and the Battle River confluence, in the town of Battleford rather than its larger neighbour. Five of the original NWMP buildings still stand inside the palisade, including the commanding officer's residence, barracks, and the trading store, with period interpretation throughout.

The site does an unusually frank job of presenting multiple perspectives on the resistance — Métis, Cree, settler, and Mounted Police accounts all appear in the exhibits and the staff tours. Allow at least half a day, and time the visit for a guided tour if you can; the human history is the main draw, not the buildings.
