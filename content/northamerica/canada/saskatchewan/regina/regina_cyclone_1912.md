story: The Regina Cyclone of June 30, 1912 killed 28 people, injured hundreds, and destroyed or damaged 2,500 buildings in under three minutes — the worst tornado in Canadian history. The cyclone touched down in the south end, crossed Wascana Creek, and destroyed the area around Victoria Park before lifting. Contemporary photographs of the aftermath show the downtown core levelled as completely as any wartime bombing, and the rebuilding that followed gave Regina much of its current architectural character.
---

The Regina Cyclone of 1912 remains the deadliest tornado in Canadian history, and while the physical evidence is difficult to find — the city rebuilt quickly and thoroughly — the story shapes understanding of the downtown core. Most of the buildings visible around Victoria Park and the central business district were built in the immediate post-cyclone reconstruction, which explains why they share a similar Edwardian and early modernist vocabulary.

The cyclone museum is not a major attraction, but the information available at the Saskatchewan Archives and the Regina Public Library local history room tells the story in detail. Walking downtown with the cyclone in mind changes how you see the buildings: many of the structures that look like they should be older are in fact 1912-1915 reconstructions. The event is commemorated with a plaque in the downtown area, and the Saskatchewan Archives holds a comprehensive photographic record of the destruction and rebuilding.
