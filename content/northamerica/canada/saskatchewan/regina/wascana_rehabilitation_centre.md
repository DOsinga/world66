The greatest free spectacle in Regina requires no admission, no queue, and no particular planning — just stepping outside at the right moment and looking up. The prairie sky over southern Saskatchewan is extraordinary: the absolute flatness of the terrain in every direction means the sky occupies a larger fraction of your visual field than anywhere in a more topographically varied landscape, and the light at sunset does things that painters from elsewhere find difficult to believe.

Summer evenings in Wascana Park, with the lake reflecting the sky and the Legislative Building silhouetted against it, are the best setting for this experience. Winter nights, on the clear cold evenings that follow prairie storms, bring the northern lights well enough south to be visible from the city. There is no formal attraction here — just the sky and the scale of the prairie and the understanding that this is what people have been looking at for thousands of years from this particular patch of ground.
