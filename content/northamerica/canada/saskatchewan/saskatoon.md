Saskatoon sits on the banks of the South Saskatchewan River in the heart of the prairies, about 520 km southeast of Edmonton and 780 km northwest of Winnipeg. Seven bridges span the river within city limits, giving the city its unofficial nickname — the "City of Bridges." With a population of around 250,000, it is the largest city in Saskatchewan and an agricultural and university hub for the surrounding region.

The riverbank is the best part of the city. The [Meewasin Valley Trail](/northamerica/canada/saskatchewan/saskatoon/meewasin_valley_trail) follows both sides of the South Saskatchewan for over 80 km, making it excellent for walking and cycling. The [Ukrainian Museum of Canada](/northamerica/canada/saskatchewan/saskatoon/ukrainian_museum_of_canada) is worth a visit — it has one of the largest Ukrainian art and artifact collections in the country, reflecting the strong immigrant heritage of the prairies. [Wanuskewin Heritage Park](/northamerica/canada/saskatchewan/saskatoon/wanuskewin_heritage_park), just north of the city, preserves 6,000 years of Northern Plains Indigenous culture and became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2023.

Saskatoon has a compact, walkable downtown with good cafes and restaurants along 2nd Avenue. The [Broadway District](/northamerica/canada/saskatchewan/saskatoon/broadway_district), across the river from downtown, is a lively strip of independent shops, bars, and restaurants that gives the city some character. The [Delta Bessborough](/northamerica/canada/saskatchewan/saskatoon/delta_bessborough) railway hotel anchors the riverfront with its château-style towers. Summers here are hot and sunny; winters are brutally cold. If you're visiting in summer, the farmers' market on Saturday mornings at Riversdale is one of the best in the province.
