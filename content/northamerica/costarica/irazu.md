Irazú is the highest volcano in Costa Rica at 3,432 metres, and unusually you can drive to the top — the road runs from [Cartago](/northamerica/costarica/cartago) right to the crater rim, which makes it the easiest high-altitude volcano visit in the country and, on a clear morning, one of the few places said to show both oceans at once.

The main crater holds a lake whose colour shifts between green and rust depending on the mineral load, in a grey moonscape of ash where almost nothing grows. It erupted through 1963 and 1964, covering the Central Valley in ash for months, beginning on the day President Kennedy arrived on a state visit.

Go early and in the dry season. By ten in the morning the cloud has usually come up the mountain and the crater is a white wall; it is also genuinely cold at the top, which catches people out.
