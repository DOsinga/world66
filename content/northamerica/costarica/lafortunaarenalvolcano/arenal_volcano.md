  On 29 July 1968, after four centuries of dormancy, Arenal erupted without warning. The initial blast destroyed three villages and killed at least 87 people; the entire flank of the volcano on the town side collapsed. The eruption continued intermittently for 42 years. When it stopped in 2010, the perfect cone was left standing in unusual silence — geologists classify it as resting, not extinct.

Arenal Volcano is the reason La Fortuna exists as a tourist destination. Its perfectly symmetrical cone rises 1,670 metres above the Alajuela plain and dominates every view in town. From 1968 until 2010 it was one of the most active volcanoes in the Americas, producing nightly lava flows visible from the road that drew visitors from around the world. Since 2010 it has been quiet.

The best way to experience the volcano is through the [Arenal Volcano National Park](/northamerica/costarica/lafortunaarenalvolcano/arenal_volcano_national_park), which has trails through the lava fields from the 1968 and 1992 flows. The park entrance is about 15 km from La Fortuna on the road toward Nuevo Arenal. Clear mornings give the best views of the cone — cloud typically builds around the summit by late morning. The summit itself is off-limits to hikers. At its base, the thermal springs and [Tabacón resort](/northamerica/costarica/lafortunaarenalvolcano/tabacon_hot_springs) are fed by geothermally heated water.
