  Manuel Antonio is the most visited national park in Costa Rica despite being the country's smallest, at just 16 square kilometres. It was nearly developed as a housing estate in the 1970s before a group of local residents and international conservation advocates lobbied the government to protect it. The developer had already cleared some land; you can still see the ecological boundary between the secondary growth and the older primary forest.

Costa Rica's smallest and most visited national park, protecting a peninsula of primary rainforest, coastal cliffs, and a string of perfect coves. Four beaches, two hiking trails through the forest canopy, and a resident population of wildlife make it one of the most rewarding parks in Central America for a first-timer.

The capuchin monkeys are the most visible residents — bold, clever, and habituated to tourists, they are the ones most likely to raid your bag if you turn your back. Sloths are harder to spot: look for the dark mass in a cecropia tree, slow-moving or motionless. Scarlet macaws, toucans, and various raptors are regular sightings. Hire a guide at the entrance for the first visit — they know where the animals are.

Daily visitor numbers are capped. Book tickets online at least a few days ahead in high season (December–April). Bring water and snacks; no food or drink is sold inside. The park closes Mondays for maintenance.
