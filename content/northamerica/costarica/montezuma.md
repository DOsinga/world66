Montezuma is a small, bohemian beach village at the southern tip of Costa Rica's Nicoya Peninsula. It attracts a mix of backpackers, surfers, yoga practitioners, and nature lovers drawn by its waterfalls, rocky coastline, and laid-back atmosphere. The village itself is tiny -- a handful of streets with restaurants, surf shops, and a weekly organic market in the park.

The main draws are natural. The [Montezuma Waterfalls](/northamerica/costarica/montezuma/montezuma_waterfalls) — a series of three cascades with swimmable pools — are a short walk from town. The [beach](/northamerica/costarica/montezuma/montezuma_beach) alternates between sand and volcanic rock, and the surf is decent if not world-class. [Cabo Blanco Absolute Natural Reserve](/northamerica/costarica/montezuma/cabo_blanco_reserve), the oldest protected area in Costa Rica, sits at the tip of the peninsula about 10 kilometres south. The area has a well-established expat community, and the food scene is better than you would expect for a village this small.
