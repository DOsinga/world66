  The unusual white sand is what sets Flamingo apart from most Pacific coast beaches in Costa Rica, which typically have dark volcanic sand. The white colour comes from pulverised shells and coral rather than volcanic rock — a coastal anomaly that, combined with the sheltered bay, made this stretch appealing to early developers and explains the relatively upscale character of the town compared to its neighbours.

Playa Flamingo is one of the few genuinely white-sand beaches on Costa Rica's Pacific coast. The bay is sheltered and the waves are gentle, which makes it suitable for swimming and for launching kayaks and stand-up paddleboards directly from the shore. The beach is about a kilometre long, relatively uncrowded compared to the surf beaches further south, and backed by palms and the occasional beachfront restaurant.

Kayaks and stand-up paddleboards can be rented on the beach. Catamaran tours depart in the morning, typically running north along the coast or out toward the Catalina Islands, with a snorkelling stop and drinks on board. The sunsets here are among the better ones on the Guanacaste coast — the bay faces southwest and the light on the Pacific from the hillside above town is genuinely spectacular on clear evenings.
