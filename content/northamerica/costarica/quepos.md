Quepos is a small fishing town on Costa Rica's central Pacific coast, best known as the gateway to [Manuel Antonio National Park](/northamerica/costarica/manuelantonio). The town itself has a scruffy, likeable energy — a handful of funky bars and local restaurants line the main streets downtown, and the fishing fleet still operates from the harbour. South of downtown, the modern [Marina Pez Vela](/northamerica/costarica/quepos/marina_pez_vela) hosts the sport-fishing boats and the more polished dining.

The road from Quepos to Manuel Antonio climbs a forested ridge with increasingly upscale hotels and restaurants along the way, including the conspicuous [El Avion](/northamerica/costarica/quepos/el_avion) — a restaurant built around the fuselage of an old CIA cargo plane. At the end of the road is Manuel Antonio itself: a beautiful white-sand beach backed by rainforest, with the national park entrance just to the left. The park is small but spectacular, with monkeys, sloths, and iguanas visible on even a short visit.

Quepos is also one of the top [sport-fishing](/northamerica/costarica/quepos/sport_fishing) destinations in Costa Rica. Sailfish run from December through April, marlin bite hardest from November to April, and snapper, tuna, dorado, and roosterfish can be caught year-round. Charter boats range from around $600 per day for a smaller vessel to $1,500 for a larger one — book direct with boat owners for the best price, since hotels and agents add commissions.
