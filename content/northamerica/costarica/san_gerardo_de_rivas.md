San Gerardo de Rivas is a small village in the Talamanca mountains of southern [Costa Rica](/northamerica/costarica), and the starting point for the climb up Cerro Chirripó, the country's highest peak at 3,820 metres. There is not much to the village itself — a handful of lodges, a few restaurants, and a ranger station — but that is beside the point. You come here to hike.

The trail to [Cerro Chirripó](/northamerica/costarica/san_gerardo_de_rivas/cerro_chirripo) is about 20 kilometres each way, with 2,300 metres of elevation gain. Most people take two days, spending a night at the basic shelter near the top. The reward is a view that on clear mornings takes in both the Pacific and the Caribbean simultaneously — one of the few places on earth where this is possible. Permits are limited to 60 hikers per day, so book through the national park service well ahead of your trip.

Even if you are not climbing Chirripó, the area has real appeal. The [Cloudbridge Nature Reserve](/northamerica/costarica/san_gerardo_de_rivas/cloudbridge_nature_reserve) is a private cloudforest reserve a short walk from the village, open on a donation basis with good quetzal sightings in season. Natural [hot springs](/northamerica/costarica/san_gerardo_de_rivas/hot_springs) sit 15 minutes' walk north, particularly welcome after the descent. The village itself sits at about 1,350 metres, so the climate is noticeably cooler than the lowlands.
