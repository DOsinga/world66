San José is the capital and beating heart of Costa Rica, sitting in the fertile Central Valley at around 1,100 metres elevation — high enough to enjoy a mild, spring-like climate year-round. It is inevitably a transit hub for travellers moving between the coasts and the highlands, but the city rewards a day or two in its own right.

The centre is walkable and lively. The [Plaza de la Cultura](/northamerica/costarica/sanjose/plaza_de_la_cultura) is the social nucleus: street performers, shoeshine vendors, and the underground entrance to the extraordinary [Gold Museum](/northamerica/costarica/sanjose/goldmuseum), which houses the finest pre-Columbian gold collection in Central America. Nearby, the [National Theater](/northamerica/costarica/sanjose/nationaltheater) — funded by 19th-century coffee barons — is one of the most ornate buildings in Central America and offers daily tours. The [National Museum](/northamerica/costarica/sanjose/nationalmuseum), in a bullet-scarred former fortress, is worth a couple of hours.

Beyond the sights, San José is best experienced through its public spaces. The [Mercado Central](/northamerica/costarica/sanjose/mercado_central) near Avenida Central is a dense, fragrant labyrinth of butchers, spice sellers, and cheap sodas (the local casual restaurants). The city's parks and plazas — particularly [Parque Morazán](/northamerica/costarica/sanjose/parque_morazan) and [Parque España](/northamerica/costarica/sanjose/parque_espana) — fill with office workers at lunchtime and families in the evenings. Joséfinos are direct and opinionated; conversations about politics, football, and the state of the roads start easily.

The [Barrio Amón](/northamerica/costarica/sanjose/barrio_amon) neighbourhood, northeast of the centre, has the best concentration of Victorian and Art Nouveau architecture from the coffee-boom era, and a growing number of boutique hotels and restaurants occupy its old mansions. [La California](/northamerica/costarica/sanjose/la_california) and [Barrio Escalante](/northamerica/costarica/sanjose/barrio_escalante), east of the centre, are where San José's restaurant and nightlife scene has evolved in recent years. On the western edge of the city, [La Sabana Metropolitan Park](/northamerica/costarica/sanjose/la_sabana_park) offers the best green space in the capital, with the [Museum of Costa Rican Art](/northamerica/costarica/sanjose/museum_of_costa_rican_art) housed in the former airport terminal at its entrance.
