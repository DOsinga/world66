Santa Teresa runs along a single dirt road behind a very long beach at the southwestern tip of the Nicoya Peninsula, and for a place this awkward to reach it is remarkably busy. The surf is the reason: a consistent beach break that works most of the year, forgiving enough at the northern end for a first lesson and heavy enough at Playa Hermosa and Mal País to be taken seriously.

The town has changed enormously in twenty years, from a fishing village with one road to a strip of surf camps, yoga shalas, smoothie bars and restaurants that would not be out of place in a much larger town. Prices have followed. It remains, on a weekday out of season, a beautiful and very slow place.

Getting here is the filter. The road in is unpaved and potholed, and the usual route is the ferry from [Puntarenas](/northamerica/costarica/puntarenas) to Paquera and then an hour and a half by road; the alternative is a small plane into Tambor.
