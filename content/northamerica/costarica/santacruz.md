Santa Cruz is the main service town for the Guanacaste province in northwestern Costa Rica. It is not a beach destination itself, but it sits within easy reach of some of the country's best Pacific coast beaches — Playa Junquillal, Playa Tamarindo, and Playa Flamingo are all within 30-50 km. The town provides shopping, banks, and services for a wide area and works well as a base for exploring the Nicoya Peninsula coast.

Newly paved roads and growing commercial development have made this corner of Guanacaste increasingly accessible. The community is a blend of Costa Rican families and a small international contingent. Several local artists open their studios to visitors, and the area is known for its distinctive Chorotega ceramic tradition — pre-Columbian pottery styles that survive in local workshops. For family holidays, the nearby blue-flag beaches are recognised for their cleanliness, safety, and community-led turtle and monkey conservation projects.

Santa Cruz is also known as the "Folklore Capital of Costa Rica" — a title earned through its folk music, dance, and cultural festivals. The town's [Parque Central](/northamerica/costarica/santacruz/parque_central) is the social hub, and the [Iglesia de Santa Cruz](/northamerica/costarica/santacruz/iglesia_santa_cruz) dominates the square. For pottery, the village of Guaitil (15 km east) preserves the [Chorotega ceramic tradition](/northamerica/costarica/santacruz/chorotega_ceramics) in active workshops.
