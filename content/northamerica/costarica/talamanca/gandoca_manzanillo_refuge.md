The Gandoca-Manzanillo Wildlife Refuge covers the southernmost stretch of Costa Rica's Caribbean coast, protecting beaches, coral reefs, mangroves, and lowland forest in a single block that runs almost to the Panamanian border. The village of Manzanillo marks the practical end of the road, beyond which a coastal trail continues through the refuge to Punta Mona — a three-hour walk each way through increasingly wild jungle.

The main reason to come between March and July is the leatherback turtle nesting season. Gandoca beach receives fewer turtles than Tortuguero to the north, but that means fewer crowds too. Guided night-walks to watch nesting are organised through ANAI, the local conservation association. Dolphins are frequently seen in the lagoon near Gandoca. The reef offshore from Manzanillo is among the least-disturbed in the region — local dive operators rate it consistently above the more famous Cahuita reef for coral health and fish diversity.
