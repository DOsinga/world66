Tortuguero is a small, roadless village on Costa Rica's northern Caribbean coast, squeezed between a black-sand beach and a maze of jungle canals. It is one of the country's great wildlife bases: boats replace cars, rainforest presses close to the water, and sea turtles come ashore on the protected beach in season.

The village itself is simple and sandy, with guesthouses, tour offices, small restaurants, and a slow Caribbean rhythm. The point is not urban charm. The point is access: dawn canoe trips through the canals, guided night walks, turtle nesting tours, and the short hop to [Cerro Tortuguero](/northamerica/costarica/tortuguero/cerro_tortuguero) for a view over the flat green coast.

[Tortuguero National Park](/northamerica/costarica/tortuguero/tortuguero_national_park) is the main reason to come. Its canals and lagoons are rich with monkeys, caimans, basilisk lizards, toucans, herons, and kingfishers. The beach is internationally important for green turtles, with leatherback and hawksbill turtles also nesting in the wider area. Come prepared for rain, mosquitoes, and slow travel. Tortuguero is remote by Costa Rican standards, but that remoteness is exactly what makes it memorable.
