Turrialba is a town in the Central Valley about 60 km east of San José. It sits in a productive agricultural valley and is the main gateway for rafting the [Río Pacuare](/northamerica/costarica/turrialba/rio_pacuare), one of the best whitewater rivers in Central America (Class III–IV rapids through a jungle gorge). For more technical paddlers, the [Río Reventazón](/northamerica/costarica/turrialba/white_water_rafting) nearby offers Class IV–V runs.

The [Turrialba Volcano](/northamerica/costarica/turrialba/turrialba_volcano), northeast of town, has been intermittently active since 2010, with ongoing ash emissions. The park around it is periodically closed; check current status before visiting.

Turrialba is also close to the [Guayabo National Monument](/northamerica/costarica/turrialba/guayabo_monument), Costa Rica's most significant pre-Columbian archaeological site, with stone causeways, aqueducts, and foundations dating back 3,000 years. On the eastern edge of town, the [CATIE botanical garden and research campus](/northamerica/costarica/turrialba/catie) offers a quieter counterpoint — tropical forest, experimental plantations, and excellent birdwatching.
