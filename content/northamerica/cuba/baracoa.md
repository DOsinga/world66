Baracoa is the oldest Spanish settlement in [Cuba](/northamerica/cuba), founded in 1511 and cut off from the rest of the island by mountains for centuries. That isolation is part of its appeal — reaching Baracoa means crossing the spectacular [La Farola highway](/northamerica/cuba/baracoa/la_farola), a winding road carved through the Sierra del Purial that is one of the great drives in the Caribbean.

The town itself is small, laid-back, and set between a dramatic flat-topped mountain called [El Yunque](/northamerica/cuba/baracoa/el_yunque) and a curving bay. The streets are quiet, the architecture colonial but crumbling — the [cathedral](/northamerica/cuba/baracoa/co_cathedral) on the Plaza Independencia holds the Cruz de la Parra, plausibly the only surviving cross planted by Columbus in 1492. Three Spanish fortifications guard the town: [Matachín](/northamerica/cuba/baracoa/fort_matachin) and [La Punta](/northamerica/cuba/baracoa/fort_la_punta) along the seafront, and [Seboruco](/northamerica/cuba/baracoa/hotel_castillo) on the hill above. The food is different from the rest of Cuba — coconut-based dishes and cucurucho, a sweet coconut confection wrapped in palm leaves.

The surrounding area has some of Cuba's best hiking, river swimming, and untouched beaches, particularly [Playa Maguana](/northamerica/cuba/baracoa/playa_maguana) to the northwest, the [Yumurí canyon](/northamerica/cuba/baracoa/yumuri_canyon) to the east, and the UNESCO-listed [Alejandro de Humboldt National Park](/northamerica/cuba/baracoa/alejandro_humboldt_park) west of town.
