Santiago de Cuba is Cuba's second city, but it often feels like the real capital — fiercer, louder, and more Caribbean than Havana. Wedged between the Sierra Maestra mountains and the sea, the city climbs steeply through a tangle of narrow streets where Afro-Cuban rhythms pour from open doorways and the heat is relentless.

This is the birthplace of the Cuban Revolution — Fidel Castro launched his first attack on the [Moncada Barracks](/northamerica/cuba/santiagodecuba/moncada_barracks) here in 1953 — and the cradle of son, the music that became salsa. The city's cultural identity draws on African, Spanish, and French-Haitian roots, producing a mix you will not find anywhere else on the island. Santiago's [carnival](/northamerica/cuba/santiagodecuba/when_to_go), held each July, is the wildest in Cuba: a week of congas, rum, and dancing in the streets that makes Havana's celebrations look restrained.

The old centre is compact enough to explore on foot, though the hills will test your legs. [Parque Céspedes](/northamerica/cuba/santiagodecuba/parque_cespedes) is the social heart of the city, enclosed by the Cathedral, the Casa de Diego Velázquez (said to be the oldest house in Cuba), and the Hotel Casa Granda. The [Casa de la Trova](/northamerica/cuba/santiagodecuba/casa_de_la_trova) on nearby Calle Heredia is the essential stop for live son music — sessions run daily. The [Museo Bacardí](/northamerica/cuba/santiagodecuba/museo_bacardi) holds the oldest public collection in Cuba, a personal and eccentric trove assembled by the rum-heir-turned-independence-fighter Emilio Bacardí.

Beyond the city, the [Castillo del Morro](/northamerica/cuba/santiagodecuba/castillo_del_morro) — a UNESCO-listed 17th-century fortress guarding the harbour entrance — has some of the best sunset views in Cuba. The [Santa Ifigenia Cemetery](/northamerica/cuba/santiagodecuba/santa_ifigenia_cemetery) is where both José Martí and Fidel Castro are buried, their tombs a study in contrasting revolutionary styles. The [Museo de la Lucha Clandestina](/northamerica/cuba/santiagodecuba/museo_lucha_clandestina) on the hill above town covers the urban resistance that supported Castro's guerrillas in the Sierra Maestra.
