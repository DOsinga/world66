The bay runs on a timetable set by the wind. Surfers take the morning, when the sea is glassy and the swell is cleanest; windsurfers get the early afternoon as the trades fill in; and from about two o'clock the bay belongs to the kiteboarders. Most kite schools teach in **Cabarete Bay** in front of town, also called Bozo Beach, where the outer reef keeps the water near the shore calm for learners and advanced riders go out to the reef. **Kite Beach**, a kilometre west, loses most of its sand in summer, so it is a poor place to launch for beginners then.

The wind season runs roughly December to August, strongest from January to April and again in high summer. **Playa Encuentro**, four kilometres west, has the waves — best December to March — and is where the surf schools teach.

Off the water, the **27 Charcos de Damajagua** is the standard day trip: a guided climb up a river canyon, then jumping and sliding back down through the pools. Go with a licensed guide from the visitor centre rather than an unofficial one at the roadside; the drops need someone who knows the water level.
