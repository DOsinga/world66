---
title: Highlights
type: section
---

**The Zona Colonial in Santo Domingo.** The first cathedral, the first university, the first paved street in the Americas — and not a museum but a lived-in quarter where the sixteenth-century buildings have bars on their roofs. Walk Calle Las Damas at dusk.

**Humpback whales at Samaná.** Between mid-January and mid-March some two thousand humpbacks come into Samaná Bay to calve and breed, one of the largest gatherings anywhere. Boats go out from the town; the regulations on approach distance are strict and worth respecting.

**Kiteboarding at Cabarete.** The wind builds every afternoon like clockwork and the bay fills with kites. Even if you never get on a board, the beach at four o'clock is a spectacle.

**Pico Duarte.** At 3,098 metres, the highest mountain in the Caribbean — a two- or three-day walk through pine forest from Jarabacoa, cold at night, and a genuine surprise on an island people associate with beaches.

**Bahía de las Águilas.** Eight kilometres of white sand with nothing built on it, inside a national park at the far south-western corner. Getting there takes a boat or a bad road, which is exactly why it is still like that.

**The diving at Bayahibe.** Wrecks, walls and the protected reefs of the Parque Nacional del Este, with the caves and Taíno pictographs on Saona thrown in.

**Merengue and bachata.** Both were born here, and both are everywhere — from the colmado on the corner with its speaker in the doorway to the clubs of Santiago. This is the most reliable pleasure in the country and it is free.
