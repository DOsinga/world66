Higüey is the capital of La Altagracia province and the gateway city for the eastern Dominican Republic. Most travelers blow through here on their way to Punta Cana and the beach resorts, but the city has one genuinely significant attraction: the [Basílica de Nuestra Señora de la Altagracia](/northamerica/dominicanrepublic/higuey/basilica_altagracia), a striking modernist cathedral completed in 1971 that is the most important pilgrimage site in the country. On January 21st each year, hundreds of thousands of Dominicans converge here for the feast of the Virgin of Altagracia, the patron saint of the nation.

The [old sanctuary of San Dionisio](/northamerica/dominicanrepublic/higuey/santuario_san_dionisio) next to the basilica dates to the sixteenth century — one of the oldest churches in the Americas — and houses the original venerated image. The contrast between the two buildings, old colonial stone and ambitious modern concrete, is worth taking in.

Higüey is a busy, somewhat chaotic Dominican city that runs on its own rhythms. The streets around the [mercado municipal](/northamerica/dominicanrepublic/higuey/mercado_municipal) are crowded and noisy, the traffic is creative, and the food stalls sell cheap and filling comida criolla. It is not a tourist town, which is part of its appeal. If you want a glimpse of everyday Dominican urban life away from the resort bubble, Higüey delivers that honestly.
