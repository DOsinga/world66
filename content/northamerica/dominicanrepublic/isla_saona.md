Isla Saona is the largest island off the southeastern Dominican Republic coast, sitting at the tip of the country where the Caribbean Sea meets the Atlantic. Long, flat, and fringed with coconut palms, it forms part of the Parque Nacional del Este — a designation that has kept hotels off the island and the beaches in largely pristine condition.

The most famous stop en route is the Natural Pool, a shallow sandbar in the middle of the open ocean, knee-deep and stretching for what feels like kilometres. Catamarans pull up alongside and passengers wade out into turquoise water with nothing but open sea in every direction. Starfish are visible in the shallows at several points around the island; dolphins are occasionally spotted near the catamarans in transit.

The main beach on the island is a wide arc of fine white sand with calm, clear water — less crowded than its reputation suggests, partly because the island is large and the beach spreads out considerably. Mano Juan, the small village at the western end, is worth a short walk: painted wooden houses on stilts line a calm lagoon, and the contrast with the day-trip world just minutes away is striking. The interior is dry tropical forest with good birdlife if you have time to push past the beach.

Tours leave from Bayahibe, roughly two hours west of Punta Cana, and from La Romana. The catamaran trips typically include the Natural Pool stop, lunch on the island, and an open bar — the format is sociable rather than solitary. If you want a quieter experience, speedboat charters are available and some operators offer early departure times before the main catamaran crowd arrives. Come on a weekday if you can: weekends bring larger groups and the island feels busier.
