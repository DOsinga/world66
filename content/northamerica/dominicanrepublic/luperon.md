Luperon is a small fishing town on the north coast of the Dominican Republic, about 25 kilometres west of Puerto Plata. It is best known among sailors for its deep, protected natural harbour — one of the safest hurricane holes in the Caribbean — which attracts long-term cruisers from around the world. The rest of tourism largely passes it by, which is precisely what makes it worth visiting.

The town itself is quiet and unhurried, far from the all-inclusive resort strip. [Playa Grande](/northamerica/dominicanrepublic/luperon/playa_grande), just minutes from the centre, is a beautiful stretch of white sand with clear, warm water and few visitors. The surrounding countryside is lush and green, and the atmosphere is about as far from mass tourism as you can get on this coast.

Nearby, the ruins of [La Isabela](/northamerica/dominicanrepublic/luperon/la_isabela) — Christopher Columbus's first permanent settlement in the Americas, founded in January 1494 — are one of the most historically charged sites in the entire Caribbean. Most visitors to the Dominican Republic never make it out here, which means you often have the archaeological park almost to yourself. The [Malecón](/northamerica/dominicanrepublic/luperon/malecon) at dusk, with fishing boats returning and the anchored sailboats swinging on their chains in the protected bay, is one of those unpretentious, genuinely local scenes that travel writers usually say are impossible to find.
