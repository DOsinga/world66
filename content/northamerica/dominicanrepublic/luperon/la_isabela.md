La Isabela is one of the most historically significant sites in the Americas — the first permanent European settlement in the Western Hemisphere, founded by Christopher Columbus in January 1494. It sits on a low limestone terrace overlooking the sea about 45 kilometres west of Luperon, and it draws surprisingly few visitors given what happened here.

The archaeological park preserves the foundations of Columbus's house, a small church, a warehouse, and part of the defensive wall. A museum on site displays artifacts from the excavations: pottery, tools, coins, and objects that illustrate how the colonists lived and how quickly the settlement failed. Outside the park, the village of El Castillo — essentially built on top of the colonial ruins — surrounds the site.

The setting is beautiful: a sweep of ocean, low cliffs, and the green hills of the Cordillera Septentrional behind. To get here from Luperon you'll need a car or motoconcho — it's about an hour on a rough road. Allow two to three hours for the site and museum. The combination of historical weight and spectacular isolation makes it one of the most affecting places on the north coast of the Dominican Republic.
