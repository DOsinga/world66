The Malecón is the long seafront promenade that runs along Puerto Plata's north shore, connecting the port area in the west to the approach to Fort San Felipe at the far end. It is the city's main social artery in the evenings: people walk it, vendors sell food and drinks from carts and small kiosks, and the open-air bars along the strip fill steadily from sundown. During the day it is quieter, useful mainly as a way to reach the fort on foot with sea air alongside.

The ocean side of the Malecón faces directly onto the Atlantic, and the water here is noticeably rougher than the resort beaches further east — this is not a swimming spot, but the spray and the sound are part of the appeal. The road itself carries through-traffic, so the promenade side is a narrow strip between the carriageway and the sea wall. At the western end, the approach to Fort San Felipe involves a small park area with benches and old cannon; at the eastern end, the Malecón transitions into the port zone. The stretch between these two points is about 1.5 kilometres and takes twenty minutes to walk at an easy pace.
