Parque Central is the hinge around which Puerto Plata's daily life turns. In the mornings it fills with school groups and commuters cutting through; by mid-morning, vendors selling coconut water and fried snacks set up around the perimeter; in the evenings, families come out to sit under the trees and listen to whatever is happening on or near the painted iron bandstand at the centre. It is neither grand nor dramatic, but it is genuine — the kind of park where people actually use it.

The bandstand itself is the park's most photographed object, an ornate Victorian ironwork gazebo painted white and pale green that would not look out of place in a small English market town. It was built during the late 19th century when Puerto Plata was a prosperous tobacco port and the local merchant class was importing both European goods and European aesthetic tastes. The surrounding benches and lamp posts are from the same era. On the park's south side, the Cathedral of St Philip the Apostle closes the view; on the north, a row of Victorian-era commercial buildings with ground-floor shops and colonnaded walkways line the street. Spend time here before moving on to the fort or the museum — the park gives the city its context.
