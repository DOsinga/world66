Punta Cana is the Dominican Republic's premier resort zone, occupying the country's southeastern tip where the Atlantic Ocean meets the Caribbean Sea. The combination — palm-lined beaches stretching for 50 km, a coral reef close enough to the shore to keep the waters calm, warm year-round temperatures, and an international airport that brings direct flights from Europe and North America — makes it one of the most visited places in the Caribbean.

This is primarily an all-inclusive resort destination, and it makes no pretence of being otherwise. The resorts are large, the beaches are magnificent, and the activities on offer — snorkelling, scuba diving, parasailing, windsurfing, deep-sea fishing, ATV tours, zip-lines, horseback riding — are plentiful. The reef runs close enough to shore that snorkelling from the beach is possible at many points. [Bávaro Beach](/northamerica/dominicanrepublic/punta_cana/bavaro_beach) is the quintessential stretch of the coast; [Macao Beach](/northamerica/dominicanrepublic/punta_cana/macao_beach) to the north has no reef and genuine surf. More serious diving heads to [Isla Saona](/northamerica/dominicanrepublic/punta_cana/isla_saona), where starfish fields and turquoise shallows provide a different kind of underwater experience, and to Isla Catalina.

Punta Cana is honest about its trade-offs: if you want to understand Dominican culture, this is not the right base. The local population works in the resorts but largely lives outside the zone. [El Cortecito](/northamerica/dominicanrepublic/punta_cana/el_cortecito), the old fishing village at the centre of the strip, gives a glimpse of pre-resort life and has the best independent restaurants. For those who want cultural immersion alongside the beach, day trips to La Romana, Altos de Chavón, and [the Basílica in Higüey](/northamerica/dominicanrepublic/punta_cana/higuey_basilica) give a fuller picture of the country. [Hoyo Azul](/northamerica/dominicanrepublic/punta_cana/hoyo_azul) and the [Indigenous Eyes Ecological Park](/northamerica/dominicanrepublic/punta_cana/indigenous_eyes) offer nature escapes within the resort zone itself. For those who want good weather, good water, and a relaxed week, Punta Cana does it efficiently. One practical note: the mosquitos at night can be ferocious, so bring repellent.
