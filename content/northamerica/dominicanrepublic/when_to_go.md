---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

The Dominican Republic has a tropical climate, but the country is varied enough that "best season" depends on what you want. The Caribbean south coast is drier and hotter than the Atlantic north; the Cordillera Central interior is markedly cooler.

**High season (December–April)** is the obvious time to come. Dry, sunny, low humidity, and outside the hurricane window. Christmas, New Year, and Easter Week (Semana Santa) are extremely busy and expensive — book ahead.

**Shoulder (April–June)** is excellent value: temperatures still pleasant, the spring rains modest, and prices much lower. Whale watching off the **Samaná Peninsula** runs January through mid-March, when humpbacks calve in the bay.

**Wet season (May–November)** brings afternoon showers — usually short — and rising humidity. The interior mountains and Jarabacoa stay cool and green.

**Hurricane season (June–November)** peaks August through October. Direct hits are rare but significant storms do happen; check forecasts before booking and consider travel insurance with hurricane provisions.

**Carnival** runs through February, with the biggest celebrations in **Santo Domingo, La Vega**, and **Santiago**. **Merengue Festival** takes over Santo Domingo's Malecón in late July.
