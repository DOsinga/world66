Two streets of board hire, pupusa stands and bars, ending at a black-sand beach with a rock shaped like a pig lying offshore — that is El Tunco, and it is where most people who come to El Salvador to surf end up sleeping. The wave in front of town is a right-hand point over rock and cobble that works nearly year-round, best on the mid to high tide, and it gets crowded because it is the most accessible good wave on the coast.

It is a small place with an outsized weekend. Midweek it is surfers, coffee and not much else; from Friday night the bars fill with people out from San Salvador, forty minutes away, and the music runs late. If that is not what you want, [El Zonte](/northamerica/elsalvador/lalibertad) ten minutes west is quieter, and the serious wave — Punta Roca — is at [La Libertad](/northamerica/elsalvador/lalibertad) in the other direction.

Beginners are well served: the beach break at the western end is forgiving, boards rent by the hour on every corner, and lessons are cheap and plentiful. Experienced surfers should know that the take-off is over rock, not sand, and reef booties are not a bad idea.

The sunsets are the other reason people stay longer than they planned. The coast faces due west, and the whole town walks down to the rocks for it.
