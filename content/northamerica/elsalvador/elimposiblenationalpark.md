El Imposible National Park protects one of El Salvador's most important remaining forests, a steep slice of Pacific-slope wilderness in Ahuachapan department. The name sounds theatrical, but it comes from a real problem: the old gorge crossing here was once so dangerous for pack animals and coffee transport that locals called it impossible.

The park is not a quick roadside viewpoint. It is for travellers who want heat, forest, birds, river crossings, and trails that feel genuinely removed from the town-and-beach circuit. [El Imposible Forest Trails](/northamerica/elsalvador/elimposiblenationalpark/el_imposible_forest_trails) are the main draw, usually done with a local guide.

Most visitors approach from the Ruta de las Flores side, especially [Tacuba](/northamerica/elsalvador/elimposiblenationalpark/tacuba), or from the lower coastal plain around [San Francisco Menendez](/northamerica/elsalvador/elimposiblenationalpark/san_francisco_menendez). If you have your own transport, the park combines well with the western highlands, but do not underestimate distances on rough rural roads.
