La Libertad is the old Pacific port of El Salvador and the easiest beach escape from [San Salvador](/northamerica/elsalvador/sansalvador). It is not pretty in a polished resort sense. The town is busy, salty, and practical, with fishing boats, seafood stalls, surfboards on bus roofs, and traffic rolling along the coast road. That is exactly why it works.

The centre of gravity is the malecón around the [Muelle Turístico](/northamerica/elsalvador/lalibertad/muelle_turistico), where fishermen bring in the morning catch and seafood restaurants compete for the lunch crowd. Next door, [Sunset Park](/northamerica/elsalvador/lalibertad/sunset_park) has given the waterfront a family-friendly evening scene, with rides and Pacific views that are best just before dark.

Surfers use La Libertad as the gateway to the country's strongest wave corridor. [Punta Roca](/northamerica/elsalvador/lalibertad/punta_roca) is the serious right-hand point break at the edge of town, while [El Tunco](/northamerica/elsalvador/lalibertad/playa_el_tunco), [El Sunzal](/northamerica/elsalvador/lalibertad/playa_el_sunzal), and [El Zonte](/northamerica/elsalvador/lalibertad/playa_el_zonte) sit farther west along the coast. Even if you do not surf, the black-sand beaches, cheap grilled fish, and warm Pacific evenings make this one of the easiest places in El Salvador to slow down.

Come during the week if you want space. Weekends and Semana Santa bring half the capital to the coast, and the beaches can feel more like a festival than a retreat.
