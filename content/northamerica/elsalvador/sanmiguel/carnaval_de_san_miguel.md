Every November, San Miguel throws the biggest street carnival in Central America. For several days — typically the third week of November — the streets around the city centre fill with hundreds of thousands of people, floats, live music stages, and enough cumbia and salsa to make sleep difficult. The Carnaval de San Miguel is the defining event of the city's calendar and attracts visitors from across El Salvador, Honduras, and beyond.

The carnival is noisy, crowded, and entirely participatory — there is no real separation between performers and spectators. Improvised food stalls line every block. The main parade route runs through the central avenues and can take hours to complete. Hotel rooms fill up well in advance; book at least a few weeks ahead if you plan to attend. If you are anywhere in eastern El Salvador in November and do not come to San Miguel for the carnival, you will regret it.

The festival is tied to the feast of Nuestra Señora de la Paz, which falls on 21 November, but the street celebrations typically run across the surrounding weekend and days.
