  Coatepeque fills a volcanic caldera formed by a series of eruptions between 57,000 and 72,000 years ago. The lake is privately ringed by weekend homes of San Salvador's wealthy families, which limits public beach access somewhat, but the view from the rim road is extraordinary — a deep blue oval of water enclosed by steep forested walls, with the Santa Ana Volcano rising behind.

Lake Coatepeque is one of the most beautiful natural features in El Salvador — a deep crater lake about 25 km south of Santa Ana that glows a vivid blue-green in good light. The water is warm and the lake is good for swimming; several lakeside restaurants offer access to the water along with fish and seafood.

To reach the lake, take the road from El Congo junction on the Panamericana. Several comedores along the water serve freshwater fish dishes. The view from the rim road before you descend to the lake is worth stopping for. About 25 km from central Santa Ana.
