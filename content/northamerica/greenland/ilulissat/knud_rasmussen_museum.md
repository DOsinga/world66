  Knud Rasmussen was born in Jakobshavn (now Ilulissat) in 1879 to a Danish father and a Greenlandic-Inuit mother. He grew up speaking Greenlandic as a first language, which gave him an extraordinary ability to move among Arctic Inuit communities and earn their trust. His fifth Thule Expedition (1921–24) crossed the entire North American Arctic from Greenland to Alaska — the longest dog sled journey in history — collecting oral histories, stories, and artifacts from communities that had never had contact with Europeans.

The childhood home of Knud Rasmussen — the great Greenlandic-Danish explorer, ethnographer, and dog sledder — has been preserved as a small but worthwhile museum. Rasmussen was born in Ilulissat in 1879 and went on to make some of the most ambitious expeditions in Arctic history, traveling by dog sled across the entire Arctic and documenting Inuit culture with a sensitivity and depth rare for his era.

The museum is intimate and personal — photographs, equipment, and artifacts from his expeditions fill the wooden rooms of what is genuinely a historic building. For anyone interested in Arctic exploration or Inuit culture, it is essential.
