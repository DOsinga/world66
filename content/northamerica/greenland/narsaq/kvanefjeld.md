Kvanefjeld is the mountain above Narsaq, rising to around 830 metres and offering the best views in the area — out over Tunulliarfik Fjord, the surrounding mountains, and on clear days across to the Greenland ice sheet. The ascent takes two to three hours from town and is manageable for reasonably fit walkers; the terrain is tundra and rock, without marked trails, so basic navigation skills help.

The mountain is known geologically for its unusual mineral deposits, including the pink gemstone tuttupit (discovered here and named after the reindeer hunters who found it) and significant deposits of uranium and rare-earth elements. You can hunt for tuttupit crystals in the rock fractures — small, dark red stones that turn brighter in sunlight and that were for decades found only on this mountain. No special equipment is needed; look in cracks and on fresh rockfaces.
