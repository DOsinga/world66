Narsarsuaq is a small settlement in southern Greenland, sitting at the head of the Tunulliarfik fjord. It is barely a town — a few hundred residents, a hotel, and an airport — but it serves as the main gateway to the Norse ruins and hiking landscapes of the South Greenland region.

The airport was built by the Americans during World War II as part of the Blue West One base, and the valley still has the character of a place shaped by logistics more than community. The surroundings, however, are spectacular: blue icebergs calve from the Qooroq glacier into the fjord, the hillsides turn red and orange in late summer, and the Norse settlement at Brattahlid — where Erik the Red established his farm around 985 AD — is just a short boat ride away across the fjord.

Air Greenland flies once a week from Copenhagen (twice in summer), and Air Iceland operates frequent seasonal service from Reykjavik. Domestic flights connect to Nuuk four days a week. Boat transfers to nearby settlements including Qaqortoq and Narsaq can be arranged locally.
