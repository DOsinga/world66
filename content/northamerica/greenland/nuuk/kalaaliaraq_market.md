  The market's Danish name — Brættet, meaning "the plank" — refers to the wooden tables on
  which hunters traditionally laid out their catch for sale. In the nineteenth century, the
  market was the primary economic interface between the Inuit hunting economy and the colonial
  administration: hunters sold seal, narwhal, and Arctic char here, and the proceeds were
  used to buy goods from the colonial store. The arrangement was intended to integrate the
  Inuit into a cash economy; it succeeded, while also making the Inuit dependent on goods
  that could only be obtained from Danish merchants.

The Kalaaliaraq Market, known in Danish as Brættet, is an outdoor market in the old colonial quarter where hunters and fishermen sell fresh local produce: seal meat, Arctic char, reindeer, musk ox, and whatever else has been caught or hunted that week. This is not a tourist market — it is where Nuuk residents buy fresh local food that is often unavailable in the supermarkets.

The market operates on no fixed schedule; it is open when hunters have something to sell, which typically means weekday mornings. If you are not planning to buy, watching the market from a respectful distance is worthwhile as a genuine window into how the hunting economy still functions in modern Greenland. Seal meat and narwhal (muktuk) are among the products on sale; both are legal and culturally significant Inuit foods.
