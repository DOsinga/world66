Qaanaaq is one of the northernmost towns in the world, a settlement of about 600 people on the shore of Inglefield Bredning in the far northwest of Greenland. The town was established in 1953 when the inhabitants of the original Thule settlement were relocated to make way for the expansion of the American air base at Pituffik (Thule Air Base), about 100 kilometres to the south.

Despite its remoteness, Qaanaaq is a functioning town with a school, hospital, and small hotel. The [Qaanaaq Glacier](/northamerica/greenland/qaanaaq/qaanaaq_glacier) is less than an hour's walk away, and the surrounding landscape is Arctic in the most absolute sense — sea ice, ice caps, and midnight sun in summer, polar darkness in winter. The Inughuit people who live here maintain some of the most northerly hunting traditions in the world, still working teams of [Greenland dogs](/northamerica/greenland/qaanaaq/dog_sledding) across the sea ice each spring.

The town itself has a small [museum](/northamerica/greenland/qaanaaq/qaanaaq_museum) in the former house of the explorer Knud Rasmussen. The fjord in front of it — [Inglefield Bredning](/northamerica/greenland/qaanaaq/inglefield_bredning) — is one of the few places in the world where narwhal and beluga share the same waters. A short boat ride or sled trip north along the coast brings you to [Siorapaluk](/northamerica/greenland/qaanaaq/siorapaluk), the world's northernmost civilian settlement.

Getting to Qaanaaq requires flying via Ilulissat or Upernavik on Air Greenland's small domestic network. Access was previously complicated by the need to transit through the Pituffik base area, but a new airport at Qaanaaq has improved connections. Accommodation is limited, so book ahead.
