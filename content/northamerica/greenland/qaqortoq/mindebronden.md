The Mindebrønden — literally the Memorial Fountain — sits in the heart of Qaqortoq's central square and serves as the town's de facto meeting place. The stone and bronze monument dates from 1932 and was constructed to mark the bicentennial of the town's founding as a colonial trading post. The square around it is lined with brightly coloured wooden houses, the kind of Scandinavian palette — red, yellow, ochre — that looks almost surreally vivid against the grey rock and pale sky of southern Greenland.

The fountain is the natural start and end point for any walk through town. On warm summer days, locals sit on the surrounding benches and children play in the square. From here the town fans out in every direction: the museum is a short walk uphill, the old church is visible to the northwest, and the tannery is just below.
