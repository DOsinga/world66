story: "Behind the main museum building stands an exact replica of a Greenlandic turf house from the early 1900s. Turf houses were the standard housing for Greenlandic families well into the twentieth century — thick-walled, dark, and warm, they were engineered for survival in conditions that would make modern construction crumble. Stepping inside the replica is a visceral reminder of how recently and how dramatically life here changed."

Qaqortoq Museum — Kalaallit Nunaanni Katersugaasiviit in Greenlandic — is the primary cultural institution in southern Greenland. The collection covers both Inuit and Norse history, with particular strength in pre-contact Inuit material culture: hunting tools, clothing, kayak equipment, and everyday objects that tell the story of a people who lived successfully in one of the world's most unforgiving environments for centuries.

The Viking and Norse holdings include artifacts from the area's medieval Norse settlements, which flourished between roughly 985 and 1408 AD before mysteriously disappearing. Carved bones, iron tools, and decorative objects make up much of the collection. The museum also holds colonial-era photographs and items relating to the Danish administration of Greenland — a history that is still complicated and contested.
