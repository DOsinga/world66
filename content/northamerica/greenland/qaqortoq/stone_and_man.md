Stone and Man is an extraordinary open-air sculpture project that transformed Qaqortoq into an outdoor gallery. Beginning in 1993, sculptors carved directly into the rocks and cliff faces of the town — not onto imported stone, but into the living bedrock. The result is a set of over thirty sculptures distributed across the town's hills and streets, each emerging from its geological setting as if it had always been there.

The subjects range from abstract forms to faces, animals, and mythological figures drawn from Greenlandic oral tradition. A map available from the tourist office or the museum guides you to the major pieces, but part of the pleasure is stumbling across a carved face in a rock wall you walk past every day. The project has become one of the defining cultural experiences of Qaqortoq, and arguably the most ambitious art project ever undertaken in Greenland.
