Sisimiut is Greenland's second-largest town, set just north of the Arctic Circle where rocky coast, sled-dog country, and open tundra meet. It has a stronger outdoor identity than its size suggests: hikers finish the Arctic Circle Trail here, winter travellers head out by dog team or snowmobile, and summer visitors use it as a base for fjords, mountains, and old Inuit sites.

The town itself is worth a day. [Sisimiut Museum](/northamerica/greenland/sisimiut/sisimiut_museum) sits in the old colonial quarter and gives useful context for trade, whaling, kayaking, and the Saqqaq finds from the region. Just west of the harbour, [Sallinnguit](/northamerica/greenland/sisimiut/sallinnguit) is a short walk into thousands of years of settlement history.

The landscape takes over quickly. [Nasaasaaq](/northamerica/greenland/sisimiut/nasaasaaq) rises southeast of town and gives serious hikers a hard, memorable mountain day, while the [Arctic Circle Trail](/northamerica/greenland/sisimiut/arctic_circle_trail) begins or ends at Sisimiut with 160 km of tundra between here and Kangerlussuaq. Nearby, Aasivissuit-Nipisat adds UNESCO weight to the region's long hunting history.

Sisimiut is not a polished Arctic resort. That is part of the appeal. It is a working Greenlandic town with enough culture for a slow day and enough wild country around it to keep an active traveller busy much longer.
