Carriacou is the largest of Grenada's sister islands, lying about 37 kilometres north of the main island. It is small enough to drive across in twenty minutes, unhurried enough that goats outnumber cars on many roads, and interesting enough to justify staying a few days rather than just passing through. The island has a population of around 8,000 and a character quite distinct from Grenada itself — more Scottish, more African, more self-contained.

The Scottish connection is most visible in the village of [Windward](/northamerica/grenada/carriacou/windward) on the eastern coast, where the descendants of Scottish boatbuilders still construct wooden fishing sloops and trading schooners on the beach using centuries-old techniques. The annual [Carriacou Regatta](/northamerica/grenada/carriacou/windward/regatta) in August brings these locally built boats together for racing — it is the island's biggest event and draws visitors from across the Caribbean. The African roots run just as deep: the Big Drum Dance, a ceremony that acknowledges ancestral nations from West Africa, is performed at significant life events and preserved nowhere else in quite the same form.

Hillsborough is the main town, with a small waterfront, a market, and the boat landing for the ferry from Grenada. The beaches on the sheltered western side are calm and clear; Sandy Island, a small cay offshore, is surrounded by reef and reachable by water taxi. Diving and snorkelling around the reefs are excellent, with good visibility and a healthy marine population.
