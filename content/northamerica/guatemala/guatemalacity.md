Guatemala City is the capital and by far the largest city in Central America (excluding Mexico), with a metropolitan population over 3 million. It sits in a wide valley at about 1,500 metres elevation, giving it a mild climate year-round.

Most travellers pass through rather than linger, but the capital has more to offer than it gets credit for. The Zona 1 historic centre has the [Palacio Nacional de la Cultura](/northamerica/guatemala/guatemalacity/national_palace) — the green volcanic-stone palace now open as a museum — the [Metropolitan Cathedral](/northamerica/guatemala/guatemalacity/cathedral_of_guatemala_city), and the vast [Mercado Central](/northamerica/guatemala/guatemalacity/mercado_central) underground market beneath the cathedral. The [Kaminaljuyú](/northamerica/guatemala/guatemalacity/kaminal_juyu) pre-Classic Maya site in Zona 7 is surrounded by housing but archaeologically significant. The modern Zonas 9 and 10 have good restaurants and the excellent [Popol Vuh Museum](/northamerica/guatemala/guatemalacity/popol_vuh_museum) and [Ixchel Museum of Indigenous Dress](/northamerica/guatemala/guatemalacity/ixchel_museum), both on the Francisco Marroquín university campus. The [National Museum of Archaeology and Ethnology](/northamerica/guatemala/guatemalacity/national_museum_of_archaeology) in Zona 13 has the best state collection of Maya stelae and jade. The bizarre [Mapa en Relieve](/northamerica/guatemala/guatemalacity/mapa_en_relieve) in Zona 2 — a 1,800 square metre topographic model of the entire country built in 1904 — is one of the strangest attractions in Central America.

Safety is a genuine concern in certain areas — Zone 1 at night, the bus terminals, and peripheral neighbourhoods. The tourist zones (Zonas 9, 10, 13, 14) are considerably safer. Use Uber rather than street taxis.

[Antigua](/northamerica/guatemala/antiguaguatemala) is 45 km to the west by shuttle (about 1 hour), and most travellers base themselves there rather than in the capital.
