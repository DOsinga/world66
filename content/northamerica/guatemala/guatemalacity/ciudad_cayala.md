Ciudad Cayalá is a privately managed town-centre development in Zona 16, built as a clean, controlled, walkable alternative to Guatemala City's harder-edged streets. Its pale arcades, courtyards, plazas, shops, restaurants, offices, and apartments borrow from colonial urbanism and New Urbanist planning, creating a district that feels more like a stage set than an organic neighbourhood.

That artificiality is exactly why it is interesting. Some travellers will find Cayalá too polished and commercial; others will enjoy having a safe evening stroll, dinner, or coffee in a city where public space can be complicated. Either way, it says a great deal about contemporary Guatemala City: its security anxieties, its appetite for orderly public life, and its willingness to build a new version of the old city from scratch.
