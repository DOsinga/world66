At 3,020 metres, Volcán San Pedro is the smallest of the three volcanoes framing the lake — which makes it the most manageable. The trailhead sits at the edge of San Pedro La Laguna town, and the ascent takes around three to four hours, with a similar time back down. You must hire a local guide through the community-run entrance booth; this is both a safety requirement and a direct contribution to the local economy.

The route climbs steeply through coffee and avocado farms, then into pine and oak forest, then cloud forest near the top. On clear mornings — go early, before clouds build by mid-morning — the summit offers a full panoramic view of the caldera: the lake directly below, Volcán Tolimán and Volcán Atitlán to the east, and the Pacific plain visible on the southern horizon. Wear warm layers; the temperature drops sharply with altitude.
