Livingston has no road to it. Everything arrives by boat, and most of what there is to do leaves the same way: up the gorge to [Río Dulce](/northamerica/guatemala/rio_dulce) through the limestone walls, along the coast to the Siete Altares — a staircase of falls and pools in the forest — and out to Playa Blanca, the only white sand within reach.

The town itself is Garífuna, which makes it unlike anywhere else in Guatemala: a Caribbean creole town at the mouth of a Guatemalan river, with its own language, its own drumming and *tapado*, the coconut and seafood stew that is the thing to eat here.

The agencies on the main street also sell the international crossings — Punta Gorda in Belize, Omoa in Honduras — which run on demand rather than to a timetable.
