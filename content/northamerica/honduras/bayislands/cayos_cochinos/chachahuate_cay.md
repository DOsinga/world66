story: Chachahuate is barely 200 metres long — a strip of sand with palm trees, fishing boats, and around 100 Garifuna inhabitants whose ancestors arrived on Roatán in 1797 after the British exiled them from St Vincent. They still speak Garifuna at home, fish from dugout canoes, and bury their dead on the larger island a few kilometres away.
---

Chachahuate is the only permanent settlement in the Cayos Cochinos and the most photographed picture-postcard Caribbean cay you are likely to set foot on. Wooden shacks sit a few metres from the water on all sides; nets dry over coconut palms; chickens wander between the boats. The villagers cook fresh-caught fish over open fires and rent out simple cabins and hammocks for the night.

This is a working community, not a resort. Bring your own water if you are staying — there is no fresh source on the cay, and supplies arrive by boat from the mainland. Most visitors come for a few hours as part of a snorkelling day trip from La Ceiba or Roatán, but staying a night turns the visit into something genuinely memorable. After the day-trippers leave around four in the afternoon the island goes quiet, and the sunsets are about as good as the Caribbean gets.
