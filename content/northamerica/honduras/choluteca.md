Choluteca is a mid-sized city in southern Honduras, close to the borders with Nicaragua and El Salvador. It sits in a broad agricultural valley on the Choluteca River, and the surrounding area is one of the hottest in Central America — temperatures regularly exceed 35°C in the dry season. The city is a transport hub for travellers crossing between the three countries, and most people pass through without stopping. Those who do stop find a genuine colonial centre and some of the best street food in Honduras.

The [Cathedral of the Immaculate Conception][catedral_inmaculada] and the adjacent Parque Central are the social heart of the city. The [Casa Valle][casa_valle] museum preserves the home of José Cecilio del Valle, one of the authors of Central American independence and Choluteca's most celebrated historical figure. The city is known throughout Honduras for its baleadas — flour tortillas stuffed with beans, cheese, and meat — available from street stalls across the centre for next to nothing.

Choluteca's best feature is its proximity to the [Gulf of Fonseca][gulf_of_fonseca], a Pacific bay shared between Honduras, El Salvador, and Nicaragua. From the port at Coyolito, boats cross to Isla del Tigre, a volcanic island with the colonial town of Amapala and beaches that see almost no tourists. The surrounding mangroves and the Punta Ratón peninsula are important nesting grounds for olive ridley sea turtles.
