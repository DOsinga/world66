The beach at Corozal runs directly in front of the village, with coconut palms leaning over the sand and wooden fishing boats pulled up at the water's edge. It is uncrowded even on weekends — this is not a destination tourists tend to discover, and the community remains genuinely local. The sea is calm and suitable for swimming.

On weekend evenings, punta drumming often drifts across from the village — the deep, rapid rhythm of the Garifuna tradition that has UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage status. If you happen to be here for a community celebration or the annual Garifuna Settlement Day on 26 November, the beach becomes the centre of the festivities.

story: "The Garifuna arrived on the Central American coast in 1797, exiled by the British from St. Vincent after a failed uprising. They were set ashore at Roatán with almost nothing. Within a generation they had settled along the Caribbean coast from Honduras to Belize and Guatemala, carrying their language, music, and cassava-based food culture intact."
