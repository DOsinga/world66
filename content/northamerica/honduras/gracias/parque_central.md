  Gracias holds the distinction of having been the first capital of the Audiencia de los Confines — the Spanish colonial authority that governed all of Central America — from 1544 to 1549. The choice of Gracias was politically shrewd: its mountain location made it defensible and central. The plaza still has the slightly formal air of a place that once mattered more than its current size suggests.
---

The main square of Gracias is the calm heart of a town that still moves at a pre-modern pace. Benches fill with residents in the evenings, shoeshine men work the corners, and the colonial church of San Marcos faces the square with the unhurried gravity of something built to last centuries — which it has. The park is small but well-kept and serves as the best spot to simply sit and watch highland Honduran life pass.

Three colonial churches sit within easy walking distance of the plaza: San Marcos, Las Mercedes, and the smaller Ermita de San Sebastián. None contains remarkable interior art, but the weathered exteriors and their placement on cobblestone streets give the town its character. The market, a block from the plaza, opens early and sells local produce, baleadas, and handicrafts.
