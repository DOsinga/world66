  Pico Bonito is part of the Mesoamerican Biological Corridor and harbours an estimated 20 to 50 jaguars — among the densest jaguar populations in Central America. The park was established on 1 January 1987, at the stroke of the new year, which gives it an oddly auspicious founding date.

Pico Bonito National Park begins almost at La Ceiba's city limits, rising from the coast through tropical forest to cloud forest above 2,000 metres. The park's name — "Beautiful Peak" — refers to the summit of Pico Bonito at 2,435 metres, which looms dramatically behind the city and is visible from the beachfront. The park covers 564 square kilometres and is one of Honduras's most important protected areas.

The lower reaches of the park along the Cangrejal River are the most accessible for day visitors. Trails from the lodge area lead to waterfalls, swimming holes, and bird-rich forest where you have a realistic chance of spotting toucans, motmots, and several species of hummingbirds. More serious hikers can arrange multi-day treks into the upper forest — a guide is necessary and should be arranged in advance through lodges near the park entrance.
