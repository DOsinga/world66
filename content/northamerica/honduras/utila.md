Utila is the smallest and cheapest of the Bay Islands, 11 km long and sitting about 30 km off the north coast of Honduras. It has a well-earned reputation as one of the best-value places in the world to learn to [scuba dive](/northamerica/honduras/utila/diving) — prices for PADI open-water courses are lower here than almost anywhere comparable — and the diving itself is excellent.

The island sits on the Mesoamerican Barrier Reef with over 50 dive sites, including walls, caves, and wrecks. [Whale sharks](/northamerica/honduras/utila/whale_shark_diving) move through the waters around Utila regularly, particularly from March to May, making it one of the more reliable places in the world to encounter them.

Above water, the highlights are short and sweet: a hike up [Pumpkin Hill](/northamerica/honduras/utila/pumpkin_hill) to the volcanic high point, a visit to the [Iguana Research Station](/northamerica/honduras/utila/iguana_research_station) where the endemic swamper iguana is bred, and a boat trip out to [Water Cay](/northamerica/honduras/utila/water_cay) and the colourful stilt-house cays on the southern reef. Dinner runs to wood-fired pizza at the [Mango Inn](/northamerica/honduras/utila/mango_inn) and drinks in the mosaic-covered [Treetanic Bar](/northamerica/honduras/utila/treetanic).

The population is a distinctive mix: English-speaking Bay Islanders with British and Caribbean heritage, mainland Hondurans, and a steady stream of long-term expat residents. The atmosphere is relaxed and unpretentious — this is not a resort island. The main settlement, Utila Town (also called East Harbour), is the only town, and most of what you need is within easy walking distance.

The ferry from [La Ceiba](/northamerica/honduras/laceiba) takes about an hour and runs several times a day.
