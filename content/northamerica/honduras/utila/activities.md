---
title: Diving
type: section
---

Utila is one of the cheapest places in the world to learn to dive. A PADI Open Water course on the island costs a fraction of what you would pay in most of the Caribbean, and the standard package usually throws in a few days of accommodation. The compromise is that the schools are busy and the classes can be large — it pays to ask around for a smaller, slower-paced operator if that matters to you.

The diving itself is genuine. Utila sits on the southern end of the Mesoamerican Barrier Reef, with more than fifty named sites within a short boat ride. Wall dives, swim-throughs, sponge gardens, and a couple of wrecks all sit in warm, clear water. Visibility is good year round and the reef is healthier here than around busier Roatán.

The best-known operators include Utila Dive Centre, Underwater Vision, Alton's, and Parrots — all long-running, all reputable. Whale shark trips, deep specialty courses, and free-diving are widely available.
