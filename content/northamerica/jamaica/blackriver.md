The town was the first place in Jamaica to get electricity, in 1893, paid for by the logwood dye trade that made it rich — and the Georgian and Victorian merchant houses along the waterfront, some restored and some sagging, are what is left of that.

The reason most people come is the river itself. The **Black River Great Morass** behind the town is the largest wetland in Jamaica, some 250 square kilometres of mangrove and sawgrass, and the boat trips upriver go looking for American crocodiles — there are a few hundred in the system, and on a good morning you see several without much trouble. The water is dark from the peat rather than dirty, which is where the name comes from.

Inland, **YS Falls** is the best of the island's waterfalls to actually swim at: seven cascades in private farmland, with pools, a rope swing and far fewer people than Dunn's River. **Appleton Estate**, the rum distillery, is twenty minutes further up the same valley and has been making rum since 1749.

The **Pelican Bar**, a shack on stilts on a sandbar a kilometre out to sea, is reached by boat from here or from [Treasure Beach](/northamerica/jamaica/treasurebeach) along the coast.
