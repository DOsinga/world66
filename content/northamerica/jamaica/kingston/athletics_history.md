  Jamaica produces Olympic sprinting champions at a per-capita rate that has no equivalent in world sport. The country of three million people has won 26 Olympic gold medals in track and field alone. Jamaican track and field culture is centred on Kingston's National Stadium and the Inter-Secondary Schools Boys and Girls Championships — known as Champs — held every March. Champs is one of the most intensely followed domestic sporting events in the Caribbean: 60,000 people in the stadium, live television coverage, schools competing with the seriousness of professional sports franchises. It is the event that identifies Jamaican sprinting talent and the social context that explains why Jamaica disproportionately produces world-class sprinters.

For visitors with any interest in athletics, attending Champs in March is an extraordinary experience — not for international stars but for the spectacle of an entire nation's school system competing at elite level. The National Stadium also hosts international athletics meetings, including the annual Jamaica International Invitational, which draws world-class athletes from February through spring. For context on Jamaica's sporting achievements, the Jamaica Olympic Association building on Arthur Wint Drive holds a small exhibition.
