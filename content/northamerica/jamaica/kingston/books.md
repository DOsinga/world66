**Roger Mais, *Brother Man* (1954)** — Set in a Kingston yard in the Rastafarian milieu of the early independence era. One of the finest novels in Caribbean literature, and the most vivid fictional portrait of Kingston's inner-city communities.

**V.S. Reid, *New Day* (1949)** — The panoramic Jamaican novel, tracing three generations of a Jamaican family from the Morant Bay Rebellion of 1865 to the eve of independence. Reid was the first Jamaican to write a major novel in a distinctly Jamaican voice.

**Colin Channer, *Waiting in Vain* (1998)** — A contemporary Kingston novel of love and music, capturing the city's uptown creative world in the 1990s with the kind of insider detail only a native Kingstonian could provide.

**Clinton Hutton and Lloyd Bradley, *Bass Culture: When Reggae Was King* (2000)** — The definitive history of Jamaican popular music from its roots in mento and American R&B through the peak years of roots reggae. More thorough and less hagiographic than most reggae histories.

**Diana McCaulay, *Huracan* (2012)** — A historical novel set during the 1692 earthquake that destroyed Port Royal, written by one of Jamaica's most significant contemporary novelists. Vivid and researched.
