  Half Way Tree takes its name from a cotton tree that served as a landmark halfway along the old road between Kingston and the mountain estates — a stopping point for travellers and traders in the colonial era. The tree is long gone, but the name stuck, and the commercial square at the heart of the neighbourhood retains a vitality that New Kingston's more corporate streets lack.
---

Half Way Tree is the major commercial node of Uptown Kingston — a dense, busy area around the main square where Half Way Tree Road meets Constant Spring Road and Dunrobin Avenue. It functions as the hub of the public transport system for the northern half of the city, and at peak hours the square and surrounding streets achieve a concentrated urban energy unlike anywhere else in Kingston except Downtown.

The area is primarily commercial — pharmacies, markets, fast food, mobile phone shops, minibus stops — but it has a genuine street life and the square retains the character of a place that has functioned as a meeting point for over a century. The clock tower in the centre of Half Way Tree Square is the most recognisable landmark. For visitors, Half Way Tree is most useful as a transit hub for reaching other parts of the city by route taxi, but it rewards a brief stop to absorb how Kingston's everyday commercial life actually operates.
