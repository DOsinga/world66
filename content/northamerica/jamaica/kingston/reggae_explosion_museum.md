The Reggae Explosion museum in Marketplace on Constant Spring Road is the most comprehensive overview of Jamaican popular music in Kingston — covering the full arc from mento through ska, rocksteady, reggae, and dancehall in roughly chronological order. The presentation is more serious than the typical commercial music museum: it focuses on the specific social and economic conditions that shaped each genre, the role of sound systems, and the recording studios that served as the technical backbone.

The museum is particularly strong on the period before Bob Marley became the international face of Jamaican music — the mento and ska years of the late 1950s and early 1960s, when Jamaican musicians were absorbing and transforming American R&B in ways that have influenced global music ever since. For visitors who arrive in Kingston already knowing reggae well, the museum offers context and detail that deepens rather than repeats what they know.
