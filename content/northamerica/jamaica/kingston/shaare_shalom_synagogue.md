Shaare Shalom Synagogue on Duke Street in Downtown Kingston is one of just five sand-floored synagogues in the world, and the only one in the Western Hemisphere. The building dates from 1912 — a reconstruction of an earlier synagogue on the same site — and the Jewish community in Jamaica traces its presence on the island to the 1490s, when Sephardic Jews fleeing the Spanish Inquisition arrived with Columbus's expeditions and subsequent settlements.

The interior is remarkable: the floor is covered in sand, and the white walls, wooden pews, and brass fittings give it an austere elegance that sets it apart from more elaborate religious architecture. The Jamaica Jewish community has dwindled to a few hundred families, and the building functions as both a working synagogue and a museum of Jamaican Jewish history. Visits outside of services are possible; arrangements are best made in advance through the Jewish Community of Jamaica.
