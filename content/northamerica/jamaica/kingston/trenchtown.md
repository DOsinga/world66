Trench Town is the neighbourhood in West Kingston that gave birth to reggae. In the 1950s and 60s, thousands of rural Jamaicans migrated to Kingston and settled in the new government-built housing estates here — concrete blocks and zinc-roofed yards that had replaced the old shanties. The compressed social energy of these yards, mixed with American R&B filtering in through radio, produced ska, rocksteady, and eventually reggae.

The Trench Town Culture Yard on First Street preserves the government yard where Bob Marley lived in the early 1960s, sharing a room with Bunny Wailer and later Desmond Dekker. The site is managed as a museum and community space, with local guides who explain both the history and the present reality of the neighbourhood. Visiting requires a guide — going alone and wandering is not advisable — but organised tours are available and worthwhile for anyone seriously interested in reggae history.
