Halse Hall is one of the best-preserved Jamaican great houses still standing, set in cane fields south of the town. The current building dates mainly from the 18th century, raised on a heavy stone basement of Spanish workmanship, and was restored in the 1970s by the bauxite company Alcoa as part of its corporate compound.

Tours arranged in advance walk through the panelled rooms and the basement. The grounds give a quiet sense of how a wealthy plantation worked in the colonial period — sugar, slavery, and the long shadow of both.
