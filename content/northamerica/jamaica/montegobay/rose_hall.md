  The "White Witch" story — that Annie Palmer, the 19th-century mistress of Rose Hall, murdered three husbands and many enslaved lovers before being killed herself in 1831 — is mostly invented. It was popularised by Herbert de Lisser's 1929 novel and a 1973 Johnny Cash song. The historical Annie Palmer was real but rather unremarkable; the legend stuck because the house was abandoned for almost a century, falling to ruin and overgrown, and seemed to demand a ghost story.

Rose Hall is the most famous Great House in Jamaica — a tall, white Georgian-Caribbean mansion on a low rise above the coast road east of Montego Bay. Built in the 1770s by John Palmer, a wealthy English plantation owner, it commanded a sugar estate worked by hundreds of enslaved Africans. Restored to a high standard in the 1960s by Texan businessman John Rollins, it now operates as a historic-house museum.

The main interest is the architecture — the wide verandas, the formal rooms, the period furniture — and the building's place in Jamaican legend as the haunt of Annie Palmer, the "White Witch of Rose Hall". Two tours run: a daytime historical tour and a much-hyped evening ghost tour. The day tour is the better visit. The grounds and the views over the coast are worth the trip even if you skip the house itself.
