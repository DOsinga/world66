title: "Books"

**A High Wind in Jamaica** by Richard Hughes (1929). Not set in Negril but the most gripping novel about Jamaica's past as a crossroads of piracy, empire, and childhood innocence. Hughes's story of children captured by pirates has an unsettling moral depth entirely at odds with its adventure-story surface. Read it for the Jamaica that existed before the beach resorts.

**The Harder They Come** by Michael Thelwell (1980). The novelisation of Perry Henzell's classic film, following a young man from the countryside who arrives in Kingston with dreams and ends up a fugitive outlaw. Thelwell's prose captures the sound of Jamaican English and the brutal economics of the island's music industry. The best introduction to the culture that produced reggae.

**Wide Sargasso Sea** by Jean Rhys (1966). Set mostly in Jamaica and Dominica in the years after emancipation, this is the story of Bertha Mason — the first Mrs Rochester of Jane Eyre — told from her own perspective. Rhys's prose is incandescent; the Jamaican landscape, its heat and wildness, becomes a character. Essential reading for understanding the island's colonial history through a personal lens.

**My Brother** by Jamaica Kincaid (1997). A memoir by the Antiguan-born writer about returning to care for a brother dying of AIDS. Kincaid is forensic about the Caribbean's relationship with its colonial past and with death; the book ranges far beyond its ostensible subject. A short, concentrated read that stays with you.
