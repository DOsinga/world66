Port Royal sits at the tip of the Palisadoes spit that encloses Kingston Harbour. In the 17th century it was one of the largest cities in the Americas — a notorious pirate haven and trading post where Henry Morgan and other buccaneers brought their plunder. In 1692 a massive earthquake dropped much of the city into the harbour, killing thousands instantly. The sunken city and its cargo are still down there.

What remains is a small, historically rich village with [Fort Charles](/northamerica/jamaica/portroyal/fort_charles) — Jamaica's oldest standing fort, built in 1655 — and the [Giddy House](/northamerica/jamaica/portroyal/giddy_house), a former artillery store that tilted 45 degrees in the 1907 earthquake and stayed that way. The [underwater ruins](/northamerica/jamaica/portroyal/sunken_city) are protected as one of the great archaeological sites of the Caribbean. The 18th-century [St Peter's Church](/northamerica/jamaica/portroyal/st_peters_church) and remains of the [Royal Navy Dockyard](/northamerica/jamaica/portroyal/jamaica_dockyard) complete the historic core, and a short boat ride takes you to [Lime Cay](/northamerica/jamaica/portroyal/lime_cay), a small sand cay popular for swimming.

Getting here: a ferry runs regularly from the Ocean Boulevard terminal in Kingston. The ride takes about 20 minutes and is cheap. It's an easy half-day trip from Kingston.
