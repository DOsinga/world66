Spanish Town, 20 km west of Kingston along the main highway, was Jamaica's capital for over 300 years — from the Spanish founding in the 1530s until Kingston took over in 1872. Almost no visitors come here, which is a pity.

[Emancipation Square](/northamerica/jamaica/spanishtown/emancipation_square) at the centre of town is one of the finest colonial ensembles in the Caribbean. The ruins of the King's House (the former governor's residence, burned in 1925), the handsome [Rodney Memorial](/northamerica/jamaica/spanishtown/rodney_memorial), the old House of Assembly, and the Court House frame a formal square that gives some sense of the town's former importance.

The [Jamaica Archives](/northamerica/jamaica/spanishtown/jamaica_archives) are housed in the old House of Assembly complex, holding the most complete records of Jamaican history anywhere, including birth, death, and marriage records going back to the 17th century. A few blocks away, the [Phillippo Baptist Church](/northamerica/jamaica/spanishtown/phillippo_baptist_church) commemorates the missionary James Phillippo, one of the key figures behind emancipation and the free village movement.

Spanish Town today is a working-class city with a higher crime rate than its colonial grandeur might suggest. Visit in the daytime, stay in the central area, and use it as a half-day excursion from Kingston rather than a base.
