Les Fonds Blancs — "the white shallows" — is a stretch of shallow lagoon on Martinique's east coast, between Le François and Le Vauclin. Patches of white sand rise just below the surface of the Caribbean here, creating knee-deep pools of clear turquoise water in the open sea, far from any shore. The effect is disorienting and beautiful: you step off a boat and find yourself standing in the middle of the ocean with the water barely reaching your waist.

The most famous sandbar in the area is the Baignoire de Joséphine — Josephine's Bath — named for Napoleon's empress, who was born on Martinique. The legend that she bathed here as a child is almost certainly invented, but the sandbar is real enough: a wide oval of white sand barely submerged in luminous water, ringed by nothing but open sea and distant mangroves.

Boat operators run morning excursions from the Baie du Simon near Le François, typically including a long soak on the sandbars, a ti'punch or two, and a Creole lunch on board with zouk playing from the speakers. Most trips take three to four hours and set out early — by mid-morning the best light on the water is already shifting. The excursions are popular with both visitors and Martinicans; weekends can be crowded in high season.

The surrounding lagoon is also good for snorkelling at the sandbar edges, where the sandy shallows drop quickly into deeper water and seagrass beds. If you want a quieter version of the experience, ask your boat operator about less-visited sandbars further south toward Le Vauclin.
