Arandas sits high in the Los Altos de Jalisco, surrounded by blue agave fields, grain farms, and the red soils that give highland tequila its softer, fruitier reputation. It is not as polished as Tequila town and it is a long day from [Guadalajara](/northamerica/mexico/guadalajara), but that is part of the appeal: Arandas still feels like a working tequila town first and a visitor stop second.

The town's strongest draw is the tequila culture. [Destileria La Altena](/northamerica/mexico/arandas/destileria_la_altena) is one of the respected old names in traditional production, tied to labels such as Tapatio, El Tesoro, and Tequila Ocho. Visits are not a walk-up museum experience, so arrange ahead if tequila is the reason you are coming.

The centre is compact and easy to walk. The huge neo-Gothic [Parroquia de San Jose Obrero](/northamerica/mexico/arandas/parroquia_san_jose_obrero) dominates one side of town, while the older [Parroquia de Santa Maria de Guadalupe](/northamerica/mexico/arandas/parroquia_santa_maria_guadalupe) brings the pink-stone, painted-ceiling version of Los Altos church architecture. Between them are bakeries, tequila shops, and the [Mercado Municipal](/northamerica/mexico/arandas/mercado_municipal), where the visit gets more everyday and more useful.

Arandas works best for travelers following Jalisco's tequila trail, or for anyone who would rather see a highland distillery town than another big-city suburb. Come with time to linger, not with a checklist.
