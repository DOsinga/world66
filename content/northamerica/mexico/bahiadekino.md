Bahia de Kino is where the Sonoran Desert meets the Sea of Cortez -- a quiet beach town with none of the disco-and-resort glitz of Cabo or Cancun. The sunsets alone are worth the trip.

The town splits into two halves. [Kino Viejo Fishing Harbour](/northamerica/mexico/bahiadekino/kino_viejo_harbour) is the heart of the old Mexican fishing village — authentic and unhurried. Kino Nuevo is the gringo side, with beachfront houses and a long sandy strip facing [Isla Tiburón](/northamerica/mexico/bahiadekino/isla_tiburon), the largest island in Mexico and a biosphere reserve managed by the Seri people. Both are relaxed, friendly places where the pace of life slows to a crawl.

The [Museo de los Seris](/northamerica/mexico/bahiadekino/museo_seri) in Kino Viejo offers a good introduction to the Comcáac people, whose ironwood carvings are the town's most distinctive souvenir. Accommodations range from moderate to expensive. For supplies, head to Miguel Aleman (locals call it Calle Doce), the nearest shopping town where you can find most everything you need.
