Northern elephant seals breed on Isla Guadalupe's beaches in winter, alongside the Guadalupe fur seal, which is the island's own species. Both are why great whites patrol offshore.

The recovery is the remarkable part. Nineteenth-century sealers took northern elephant seals down to perhaps twenty animals, all of them on this island; the fur seal was twice declared extinct. Mexican protection from 1922 — among the earliest marine protection anywhere — let both rebuild, and the northern elephant seal population is now in the hundreds of thousands across its range, every one descended from that remnant.

Landing on the island is restricted and the colony is normally seen from the water. The genetic bottleneck leaves the species with almost no variation, which is of ongoing interest to biologists and of some concern for its future.
