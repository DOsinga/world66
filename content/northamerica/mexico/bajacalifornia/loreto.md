Loreto is the Baja town that still feels like a town. It sits between the Sea of Cortez and the Sierra de la Giganta, with a low waterfront, a mission square, fishing boats, and islands just offshore in the national marine park.

The town has real history. [Mision de Nuestra Senora de Loreto Concho](/northamerica/mexico/bajacalifornia/loreto/mision_de_nuestra_senora_de_loreto_concho) was founded in 1697 and became the first permanent Spanish mission on the California peninsula. The old centre is small enough to walk, especially between the mission, plaza, and [Malecon de Loreto](/northamerica/mexico/bajacalifornia/loreto/malecon_de_loreto).

The sea is the stronger reason to stay. [Bahia de Loreto National Park](/northamerica/mexico/bajacalifornia/loreto/bahia_de_loreto_national_park) protects islands, reefs, dolphins, sea lions, and seasonal whales; [Isla Coronado](/northamerica/mexico/bajacalifornia/loreto/isla_coronado) is the classic day trip. Loreto is not as busy or polished as Los Cabos, and that restraint is a large part of its appeal.
