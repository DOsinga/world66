The valley makes about three-quarters of Mexico's wine, and visiting it means driving between fifty-odd wineries scattered across gravel roads, most of which want a **reservation** rather than a drop-in. Decide in advance or you will spend the day at the three with a car park.

**Book a driver.** The distances are short, the roads are unlit and unsigned, and the Baja drink-driving enforcement is real. Operators run from Ensenada, Tijuana and San Diego; the Ensenada ones are cheaper and start later.

What to taste: the valley's best wines are usually the **Bordeaux reds** on the El Porvenir side and the increasingly good **nebbiolo**, which Baja grows better than it has any right to. A number of younger producers work with minimal intervention and wild yeast.

The food is as much of a reason to come as the wine. The valley's open-air kitchens — **Deckman's**, **Fauna**, **Finca Altozano**, **Corazón de Tierra** — cook Baja produce over wood and are booked weeks ahead at weekends.

Harvest, the **Fiestas de la Vendimia**, runs through August and the first half of September and fills everything. Spring and late autumn are quieter and the valley is greener.
