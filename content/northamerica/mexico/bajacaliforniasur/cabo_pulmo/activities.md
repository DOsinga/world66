Everything here is the reef, and the reef is a **national marine park** where all fishing has been banned since 1995. That is why it is worth the drive.

**Diving** is the main event. The sites are five to twenty minutes out by panga: granite ridges with hard coral growing over them, cleaning stations, and — most famously — a resident school of bigeye jacks numbering in the thousands, dense enough that divers swim into the middle of it and lose the surface. Bull sharks are regular, giant grouper are back, mantas pass through and whale sharks turn up in season. Most sites are between ten and thirty metres, so this is open-water diving, not technical.

**Snorkelling** works too, because a good deal of the coral is shallow. Trips run to the inner reef and to the **sea lion colony at Los Frailes**, the headland that closes the park at its southern end.

Every boat needs a park permit, sites have mooring buoys and visitor caps, and nothing is touched or fed. There are four or five operators in a village of a few dozen people; all of them are small.

Bring cash. There is no bank and no petrol station, and the nearest of either is in La Ribera.
