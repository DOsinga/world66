Loreto sits between the Sierra de la Giganta and the **Bay of Loreto National Park** — five islands and the water around them, protected since 1996 and listed by UNESCO.

**Coronado** has a white sand spit and a sea lion colony; **Danzante** and **Carmen** have cliffs dropping into clear water. Boats, kayaks and paddleboards all go out from the town waterfront, and the bay is calm enough that a novice paddler is not taking a risk. Diving is uncrowded and the fish are large.

In winter, Loreto is the usual base for the drive across the peninsula to **Magdalena Bay**, where grey whales calve between January and March after swimming down from the Arctic. The mothers there regularly bring their calves alongside the boats to be touched — behaviour recorded nowhere else at this scale, and the reason that trip is worth the three hours in a van.

Inland, the **Misión San Javier**, an hour up a mountain road, is the best-preserved of the Baja missions, and rock art sites in the sierra can be visited with a registered guide.
