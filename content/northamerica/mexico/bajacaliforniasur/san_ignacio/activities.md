San Ignacio is a date-palm oasis with a Jesuit mission church in the middle of the Vizcaíno desert, and it is the gateway to two things that are worth crossing a continent for.

**Laguna San Ignacio**, sixty kilometres southwest down a dirt road, is where grey whales calve between roughly mid-January and mid-April after swimming down from the Bering Sea. It is the lagoon where the whales approach the boats — mothers pushing calves up to be touched, a behaviour first recorded here in 1972 and now passed between generations of whales. Boat numbers are capped by the biosphere reserve and all trips go through the permitted camps.

The **rock art of the Sierra de San Francisco**, north of town, is the other. Hundreds of painted shelters, some with human figures three metres tall, made by people the Cochimí could only say were giants, and dated to as much as seven thousand years ago. It is a UNESCO World Heritage site, access is by mule with a registered INAH guide and a permit, and the better sites are a two-day ride in. Arrangements are made in San Ignacio or in the village of San Francisco de la Sierra.
