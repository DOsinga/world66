  Bacanora was illegal in Mexico from 1915 until 1992, banned by the Sonora state government as part of a broader prohibition on distilled agave spirits. Production continued anyway in the hills — clandestine stills that local families had been running for generations. The spirit only received a Denominación de Origen in 2000, finally giving legal status to something people had been making and drinking here for centuries.
---

Bacanora is the agave spirit of Sonora — older than tequila's commercial dominance, more local than most mezcal, and almost entirely unknown outside the region. It is distilled from the Agave pacifica (locally called the yaquiana), which grows wild in the hills around Banamichi. The production process is traditional: the piñas are roasted in earthen pits, fermented with natural yeast, and distilled in copper or clay pot stills. Ask at your accommodation to arrange a visit to a local producer — this is not a formal tourist experience, but something closer to meeting a family at their work.
