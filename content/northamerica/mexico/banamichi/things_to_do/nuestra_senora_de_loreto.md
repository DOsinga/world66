  The church was established by Jesuit missionaries in 1639 as part of a network of missions along the Sonora River. When the Jesuits were expelled from New Spain in 1767, the local Opata population — who had built and maintained the church — continued the traditions themselves. The building standing today incorporates walls from that original 17th-century structure.
---

The parish church on the north side of Hidalgo Plaza is the oldest standing building in Banamichi and the architectural anchor of the entire town. Built by Jesuit missionaries and the Opata people who lived here, it has been in continuous use for nearly four centuries. The facade is simple by colonial Mexican standards — whitewashed adobe with a modest bell tower — which makes it feel more like a working church than a monument. Step inside for the cool air and the carved wooden retablo behind the altar.
