Calpulalpan sits in western Tlaxcala, close to the border with the State of Mexico and the old routes between the central highlands and the Gulf. It is a modest city, but it has a sharper historical identity than its size suggests: pre-Hispanic trade routes, Franciscan-era religious buildings, and the nearby archaeological site of [Tecoaque](/northamerica/mexico/calpulalpan/tecoaque).

The centre is quiet and provincial, with the [Parish of San Antonio de Padua and former convent](/northamerica/mexico/calpulalpan/san_antonio_de_padua) as its main landmark. The stronger reason to come is Tecoaque, a site that connects the late pre-Hispanic world with the violent first years of the Spanish conquest in a way few places do.

Calpulalpan makes sense as a focused detour if you are moving between [Mexico City](/northamerica/mexico/mexicocity), [Teotihuacan](/northamerica/mexico/teotihuacan), and Tlaxcala. It is not a broad sightseeing base, but the archaeology is distinctive enough to justify a stop.
