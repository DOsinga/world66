Campeche is the quiet third of the Yucatán peninsula. It has no Caribbean coast and no resorts, and what it has instead is the best-preserved walled city in Mexico and the biggest Maya ruin in the country.

[San Francisco de Campeche](/northamerica/mexico/campeche/san_francisco_de_campeche) was fortified in the seventeenth century against pirates — the walls, two forts and eight bastions are still there — and the grid inside is painted in sherbet colours and kept immaculately. It became a UNESCO site in 1999 and remains oddly empty of visitors.

**Calakmul**, in the far south near the Guatemalan border, was one of the two superpowers of the Maya classic period and fought Tikal for two centuries. It sits inside a 7,000 km² biosphere reserve; the climb up Structure II puts you above a canopy with jaguar, howler monkeys and ocellated turkey underneath, and almost nobody else on the site.

[Edzná](/northamerica/mexico/campeche/edzna) is closer to the capital and easier — a five-storey palace-pyramid and a drainage system that tells you how the city survived in a place with no rivers.

[Champotón](/northamerica/mexico/campeche/champoton) is where the Spanish were first beaten back by the Maya, in 1517, and [Isla Aguada](/northamerica/mexico/campeche/isla_aguada) sits on the lagoon where dolphins come in.
