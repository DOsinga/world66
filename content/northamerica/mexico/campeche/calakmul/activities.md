**The site itself** opens at eight. Structure II and Structure I can both be climbed, which is now unusual in Mexico, and the view from the top of either is the reason people come — unbroken forest with other pyramids standing out of it.

There is a **second entrance fee** at the reserve gate on Highway 186, before the sixty-kilometre spur road, in addition to the site admission. Bring cash; there is no card machine and no bank for an hour.

**Wildlife** is the other half. The road in at dawn is the best chance in Mexico of seeing an ocelot or a tapir crossing, and howler monkeys are reliable at the site. Guides who bird or who track will find you far more than a guide who recites dynastic history, and this is one of the few places in Mexico where that distinction is worth paying for. Jaguars are present and are not seen.

Nearby, the **Río Bec** sites — Becán with its dry moat, Chicanná with a doorway built as a monster's mouth, Balamkú with its stucco frieze — can be strung into the same trip and are usually empty.

The bat cave at **Volcán de los Murciélagos**, near Conhuás, empties at dusk: two to three million bats spiralling out of a sinkhole for the better part of an hour.
