  Construction of the cathedral began in 1540, making it one of the oldest in the Americas. It took 164 years to complete — a span that explains the mix of architectural styles, from the earliest Renaissance stonework to the Baroque towers added in the early 18th century. The towers are asymmetrical; the south tower was never finished to match the north.
---

The Cathedral of Our Lady of the Immaculate Conception anchors the east side of the Plaza Principal and has been the religious centre of Campeche since the city's founding. The façade, in a restrained Baroque style, is one of the more elegant church fronts in the Yucatán Peninsula. Inside, the high nave is cool and peaceful, and the main altar is worth seeing despite later alterations.

The cathedral is still actively used and is most atmospheric during morning masses. The plaza in front is the natural meeting point of the city — shaded benches, wandering vendors, and in the evenings a crowd of locals that reminds you Campeche is a living city, not just a heritage site.
