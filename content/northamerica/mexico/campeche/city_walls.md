Campeche is one of the only fortified cities in the Americas whose colonial-era walls survive nearly intact. The hexagonal rampart enclosing the historic centre was built between 1686 and 1704 in response to a devastating pirate raid, and today it forms one of the most complete examples of Spanish colonial military engineering in existence.

Eight of the original bastions survive and can be explored — several have been converted into small museums covering the city's history, archaeology, and natural history. The Baluarte de San Carlos on the northwest corner houses the City Museum; the Baluarte de la Soledad on the seafront side contains Mayan stelae salvaged from various sites. Walking the circuit of the walls takes about an hour and gives the best overview of the city's layout and its relationship with the Gulf of Mexico.
