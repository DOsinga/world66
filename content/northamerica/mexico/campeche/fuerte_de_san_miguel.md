The finest of Campeche's forts, perched on a hill about 3 km south of the historic centre with wide views over the city and the Gulf of Mexico. The Fuerte de San Miguel was built in the late 18th century as the primary defensive anchor for the fortification system, and its moat, drawbridge, and cannon positions are well preserved.

Inside, the museum is exceptional: six jade funeral masks from the royal tombs at Calakmul, along with other pre-Columbian objects recovered from Maya sites across the Campeche region. The collection includes ceramics, jade jewellery, and funerary objects of striking quality. This is one of the best Maya archaeology collections in Mexico outside of Mexico City — and the combination of the museum with the fort architecture and the views from the gun platforms makes it the single best half-day visit in Campeche.
