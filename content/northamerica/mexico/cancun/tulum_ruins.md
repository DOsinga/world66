The Mayan walled city of Tulum sits on a 12-metre limestone cliff above the Caribbean, with the white sand of one of the most photogenic beaches in Mexico curving away below. The complex was a coastal trading port between roughly 1200 and 1500, and one of the last cities the Maya built before the Spanish conquest. The signature image is **El Castillo**, the small temple perched right on the cliff edge.

The site is small and crowded — most tour buses arrive between 10 a.m. and 2 p.m., so come early or late. Plan to combine the visit with a swim at the beach below (a path leads down from the ruins) or at one of the cenotes in the surrounding area. The town of Tulum, 4 km inland, has its own restaurants and boutique hotels and has become a destination in its own right. About a two-hour drive south of Cancun.
