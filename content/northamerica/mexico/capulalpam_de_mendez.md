Capulalpam de Méndez is a small Zapotec village high in Oaxaca's Sierra Norte, usually shortened to Capulalpam. It has fewer than 1,500 residents, a cool mountain climate, and the kind of zócalo where sitting still for half an hour feels like part of the trip.

The village became Oaxaca's first Pueblo Mágico, but its appeal is not polished. It is a place of forests, communal tourism, traditional medicine, old mining history, and a 16th-century church looking over the valley. The Traditional Medicine Center is the most distinctive stop, with herbal treatments, massage, and temazcal rooted in local Indigenous practice.

Capulalpam works best as a slow day from [Oaxaca](/northamerica/mexico/oaxaca), or as a base for a night in the Sierra Norte. The road from Ixtlán is narrow and mountain-curved, so leave time and do not treat it as a quick urban side trip.
