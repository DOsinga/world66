Catazajá is a small, lowland Chiapas town on the edge of a broad lagoon system, about half an hour from [Palenque](/northamerica/mexico/palenque). It is not a town of churches, plazas and colonial polish. Its reason to exist for travellers is water: boatmen, fish restaurants, evening light on the lagoon and the chance, never guaranteed, of seeing manatees in the wetland.

The [Catazajá Lagoon System](/northamerica/mexico/chiapas/catazaja/catazaja_lagoon_system) is an ecological conservation area and a refuge for birds, crocodiles, turtles, howler monkeys and manatees. Local trips usually leave from the [malecón](/northamerica/mexico/chiapas/catazaja/malecon_and_playas_de_catazaja), where the town feels more like a fishing village than a formal attraction.

Catazajá is best treated as a slow half-day from Palenque, especially if you want wildlife and water after the ruins. It is too modest to build a whole trip around, but on a spare day it can be much better than another cafe in town.
