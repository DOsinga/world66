Chihuahua is the biggest state in Mexico and mostly empty: high desert, cattle country, and along its western edge the **Sierra Tarahumara**, where a system of gorges deeper and longer than the Grand Canyon cuts into the range.

The [Copper Canyon](/northamerica/mexico/chihuahua/coppercanyon) is the reason to come, and the **Chepe** railway from Los Mochis to Creel is one of the great train journeys — 650 kilometres, 37 bridges, 86 tunnels, and a climb from sea level to 2,400 metres. The canyons are home to the **Rarámuri**, who are known outside Mexico for long-distance running and inside it for having kept their distance from the state for four centuries.

[Chihuahua city](/northamerica/mexico/chihuahua/chihuahua_city) is the capital, with Pancho Villa's house — now a revolution museum — and the cell where Hidalgo was held before his execution.

[Ciudad Juárez](/northamerica/mexico/chihuahua/ciudadjuarez) faces El Paso across the Rio Grande and is one of the busiest land crossings in the world. It had a grim reputation in the cartel years; the city today is calmer than the stories suggest, but it is a working border city rather than a destination.

[Nuevo Casas Grandes](/northamerica/mexico/chihuahua/nuevocasasgrandes) is the base for **Paquimé**, the adobe trading city that linked Mesoamerica to the American Southwest.
