Chihuahua is a working northern city rather than a sight in itself, and most of what people arrange here points west, into the Sierra Madre.

The **Chepe** — the Chihuahua al Pacífico railway — starts here and is one of the great train journeys anywhere: thirty-seven bridges, eighty-six tunnels, and a climb from the high desert into pine forest and out over [Copper Canyon](/northamerica/mexico/chihuahua/coppercanyon). The scenic stretch is west of [Creel](/northamerica/mexico/chihuahua/creel), so an eastbound traveller wanting it in daylight should start from Los Mochis rather than here. Seats sell out in high season.

In the city, the history is the Revolution. **Quinta Gameros** is an art-nouveau mansion with its original interiors; **Quinta Luz**, Pancho Villa's house, is now a museum with the bullet-holed car he was assassinated in; and the **Palacio de Gobierno** holds the cell where Hidalgo was held before his execution in 1811.

Agencies here sell the sierra as a package — the train ticket, the transfers and the nights in Creel or Divisadero — which is a reasonable way to do it if you would rather not assemble a journey across the Sierra Madre yourself.
