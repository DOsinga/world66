A lumber town of a few thousand people at 2,300 metres in the Sierra Tarahumara, strung along the railway line, and the place almost everyone uses as a base for [Copper Canyon](/northamerica/mexico/chihuahua/coppercanyon).

Creel is not pretty in the way the Bajío towns are pretty. It is a working place: timber yards, a wide main street, pickup trucks, pine smoke in the cold months. What it has is position. The **Chepe** stops here, the roads to the canyon rim and down into it start here, and the Rarámuri communities of the surrounding sierra come here to trade.

The **Rarámuri** — Tarahumara in the Spanish name, though Rarámuri is what they call themselves — have lived in these canyons for centuries, and in Creel you will see them. The town's visible economy includes the sale of their pine-needle baskets and woven belts; the less visible one is that the sierra is poor and the summers have been getting drier. Buy from the makers rather than from a shop, and do not photograph people without asking.

Around town, within an hour by road or on a rented bike: the **Lago de Arareco**, a quiet reservoir among pines; the **Valle de los Hongos** and **Valle de las Ranas**, fields of eroded rock shaped like mushrooms and frogs; the **Cusárare waterfall** with a Jesuit mission church beside it; and the **Recohuata hot springs** at the bottom of a steep descent.

It is cold here in winter and it snows. The canyon floors, a couple of thousand metres below, are subtropical in the same week — which is the strangest thing about this part of Mexico and the reason October and November, clear at both elevations, are the months to come.
