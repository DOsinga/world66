title: Activities
type: section
---

Creel is a base, not a destination, and the operators here sell two sorts of day.

The short ones circle the town: the **Lago de Arareco**, the eroded rock fields of the Valle de los Hongos and Valle de las Ranas, the **Cusárare** waterfall and mission, and the **Recohuata hot springs** at the bottom of a steep walk down. Most of these can be done on a rented bike or a horse as well as in a van.

The long ones go to the canyon proper. **Divisadero**, an hour west, is the viewpoint where three canyons meet and where the adventure park has a cable car and the longest zip line in the world. **Batopilas**, five hours south down a road that drops 1,800 metres in switchbacks, is a silver town at the bottom of a canyon with a ruined cathedral in the jungle nearby — it is a two-day trip, not a day trip, whatever is advertised. **Basaseachi**, Mexico's second-highest waterfall, is the other long day, north-west.

Trips to **Rarámuri communities** are sold here too. The good ones go by arrangement with the community and leave money in it; the bad ones treat a village as a viewing gallery. Ask the operator who they work with and what the community gets.

The **Chepe** passes through Creel twice a day in each direction. Seats sell out in high season and on the first-class service, and the stretch west of here towards Divisadero and El Fuerte is the one worth being awake for.
