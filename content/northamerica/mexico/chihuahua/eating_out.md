---
title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

Chihuahua is beef country — the state produces some of the best cattle in Mexico, and the local cuisine makes no apologies for it. **Carne asada** is eaten at almost every meal, from street tacos to formal restaurants. The Mennonite community in the surrounding countryside supplies excellent cheese — **queso Chihuahua**, a mild, semi-soft yellow cheese that melts beautifully, is found everywhere and exported across Mexico.

**Restaurante La Casa de los Milagros** on Calle Victoria is a converted 19th-century mansion with good regional cooking in atmospheric surroundings. **Mesón de Catedral**, just off the Plaza de Armas, does reliable northern Mexican classics — burritos, enchiladas, and carne asada — in a traditional setting popular with both locals and visitors. For something faster, the Mercado Central has stalls serving menudo (tripe soup, traditionally eaten as a hangover cure on Sunday mornings) and other local dishes at low prices.
