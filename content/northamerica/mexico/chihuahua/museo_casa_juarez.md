A well-preserved 19th-century building in the city centre that served as the residence and office of Benito Juárez during his years of exile in Chihuahua. The rooms are kept in period style, with original furniture, documents, and artefacts from Juárez's time here. The museum tells the story of the French Intervention and Juárez's determined resistance — a chapter of Mexican history that resonates strongly in the north, where he found refuge when the capital was occupied.

The house is a short walk from the Plaza de Armas, easily combined with a visit to the cathedral.
