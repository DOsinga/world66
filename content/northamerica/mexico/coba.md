Cobá is a Maya archaeological site in the jungle west of Tulum, set around lakes and old white roads called *sacbe'ob*. It is less manicured than Chichén Itzá and less coastal than Tulum, which is exactly why it is worth the inland detour. You move through trees, not open plazas, and the scale of the old city reveals itself gradually.

The headline is [Nohoch Mul](/northamerica/mexico/coba/nohoch_mul), one of the great pyramids of the Yucatán, but the site is more than one climb or one photo. The [main archaeological zone](/northamerica/mexico/coba/coba_archaeological_zone) includes ball courts, stelae, temples, and a web of sacbe roads that once linked Cobá to other Maya cities.

Cobá also works as a full day because the archaeology is surrounded by water and forest. After the ruins, swim at [Cenote Choo-Ha](/northamerica/mexico/coba/cenote_choo_ha) or continue north to [Punta Laguna Nature Reserve](/northamerica/mexico/coba/punta_laguna_nature_reserve), where community guides lead visitors into spider monkey and howler monkey habitat.
