The Revillagigedo archipelago sits some four hundred kilometres off the Colima coast: four volcanic islands — **Socorro**, San Benedicto, Clarión and Roca Partida — with no inhabitants beyond a small naval garrison, and a marine park of 148,000 km² around them, the largest in North America. UNESCO listed it in 2016.

Divers call it Socorro, and it is on most serious lists of the best big-animal diving in the world. The draw is the **giant oceanic manta**, with wingspans past seven metres, which approach divers and appear to seek out the bubbles. Alongside them: schooling hammerheads, silvertips, Galápagos and silky sharks, bottlenose dolphins that initiate contact, and humpback whales between January and March, often audible underwater before they are visible.

**You can only come by liveaboard.** Boats leave from Cabo San Lucas — a crossing of 24 to 30 hours — and trips run eight to eleven days, from November to May. There is no day diving, no shore access and no tourism of any other kind; landings on the islands are generally prohibited.

Conditions are open ocean: currents, surge, and water from 21 to 27°C. Operators normally require an advanced certification and a reasonable number of logged dives, and they are right to.
