story: The Volcan de Colima, also called the Volcan de Fuego, is one of the most active volcanoes in North America. It has erupted more than forty times since the Spanish arrived and remains restless today — major explosions in 2015 and 2017 sent ash columns several kilometres into the sky and prompted evacuations of surrounding villages.
---

You don't climb the active Volcan de Fuego — it is genuinely dangerous and the exclusion zone is enforced — but you can drive to a series of viewpoints on the road north out of the city that put you within a few kilometres of the cone. The classic outlook is at La Yerbabuena, before the closure barriers, where on a clear morning you can see steam venting from the crater.

Local tour operators run half-day trips that combine the volcano viewpoints with the village of Comala, the source of the regional ceramics tradition. Mornings are best — afternoon cloud usually wraps the summit by lunchtime.
