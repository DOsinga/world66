Comalcalco is a small city in Tabasco's humid cacao country, about an hour from [Villahermosa](/northamerica/mexico/villahermosa). Its modern streets are useful rather than beautiful, but the place earns a page because the surrounding landscape ties together three strong threads: Maya archaeology, chocolate, and the wetlands of the Gulf lowlands.

The main reason to come is the [Comalcalco archaeological site](/northamerica/mexico/comalcalco/comalcalco_archaeological_site), the westernmost major Maya city and one of the strangest in the Maya world. Instead of limestone, its temples and palaces were built from fired clay bricks, many marked with figures, glyphs, or fingerprints from the people who made them.

Comalcalco also sits in one of Mexico's important cacao-growing areas. [Hacienda La Luz](/northamerica/mexico/comalcalco/hacienda_la_luz) turns that history into a visitable chocolate stop, while the local market gives a more ordinary taste of Tabasco: tropical fruit, regional cheeses, and simple food stalls. With a car or taxi, the colourful church in Cupilco and the Rio Playa wetlands turn the city into a worthwhile day from Villahermosa rather than just a ruins stop.
