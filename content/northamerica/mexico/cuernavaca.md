Cuernavaca, the capital of Morelos state, sits just south of [Mexico City](/northamerica/mexico/mexicocity) at about 1,500 metres, low enough that its climate is noticeably warmer and more pleasant than the capital. It has been called "The City of Eternal Spring" for good reason — the weather is mild year-round, and Mexico City residents have been escaping here since the Aztec emperors did the same.

The [Palacio de Cortés](/northamerica/mexico/cuernavaca/palacio_de_cortes), built on the ruins of an Aztec pyramid by Hernán Cortés in the 1520s, dominates the central [Zócalo](/northamerica/mexico/cuernavaca/zocalo) and houses a regional museum with murals by Diego Rivera. The [cathedral](/northamerica/mexico/cuernavaca/cuernavaca_cathedral), one of the oldest in the Americas, is another remnant of the early colonial period. Emperor Maximilian later built a summer retreat here, and the terraced gardens of the [Jardín Borda](/northamerica/mexico/cuernavaca/jardin_borda) remain one of the most pleasant spots in town. The [Museo Robert Brady](/northamerica/mexico/cuernavaca/museo_robert_brady) — a private collection of extraordinary range assembled by an American artist over three decades — is the city's most surprising attraction.

Cuernavaca is also one of the most popular places in [Mexico](/northamerica/mexico) to study Spanish. Dozens of immersion schools operate here, typically combining half-day classes with homestay accommodation. The city makes a good base for day trips to [Taxco](/northamerica/mexico/cuernavaca/taxco), the silver-working town in the hills to the south, and to the archaeological sites of [Xochicalco](/northamerica/mexico/cuernavaca/xochicalco) and [Tepoztlán](/northamerica/mexico/cuernavaca/tepoztlan). Closer to the centre, the pre-Aztec double-pyramid of [Teopanzolco](/northamerica/mexico/cuernavaca/teopanzolco) sits in a quiet neighbourhood park barely a kilometre from the zócalo.

From Mexico City, comfortable buses run frequently from the Terminal del Sur and take about an hour.
