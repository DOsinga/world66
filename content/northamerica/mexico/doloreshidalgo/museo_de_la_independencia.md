The Museo de la Independencia occupies the old jail in Dolores Hidalgo, a fitting setting for a town that calls itself the cradle of Mexican independence. Exhibits focus on the events around 1810, when Miguel Hidalgo's call to revolt turned this small town into a national symbol.

It is strongest when visited with the parish church and Museo Casa de Hidalgo, because the three sights make the independence story feel grounded in actual streets and buildings. The museum is not large, but it gives useful context before you step back into a town where nearly every central landmark points to the same historical moment.
