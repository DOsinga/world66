El Fuerte is a small colonial city in northern Sinaloa, set by the Río Fuerte and best known to travelers as a more appealing Chepe railway stop than Los Mochis. It has a shaded plaza, old mansions, a fort museum, Yoreme Mayo cultural roots, and enough history to stand on its own rather than simply acting as a Copper Canyon gateway.

The town began as San Juan Bautista de Carapoa in the 16th century and took its present name from a 1610 fort built during conflict with the Zuaque and Tehueco peoples. Later it became an important trading centre and, briefly, the capital of the old state of Occidente. That layered frontier history is still visible around the plaza and in the stories attached to the fort.

El Fuerte is at its best from November to April, when the heat is easier and the railway logistics are kinder. Use it as a slow overnight before or after the canyon train, but give the town itself time: the [El Fuerte Museum](/northamerica/mexico/el_fuerte/el_fuerte_museum), [Plaza de Armas](/northamerica/mexico/el_fuerte/plaza_de_armas), and [Cerro de la Máscara](/northamerica/mexico/el_fuerte/cerro_de_la_mascara) justify the stop.
