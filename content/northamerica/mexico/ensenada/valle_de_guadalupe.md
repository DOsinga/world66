The Valle de Guadalupe, about 30 kilometres northeast of Ensenada along Highway 3, is Mexico's premier wine-growing region and one of the most interesting food destinations on the Pacific coast of North America. The valley floor, with its Mediterranean climate and sandy granite soils, supports over 100 small wineries, many with tasting rooms open to visitors.

The best way to experience the valley is to drive the main road, stop at a few wineries, and eat at one of the outdoor restaurants that have made the region famous. Places like Corazón de Tierra and Malva serve food that pairs Baja ingredients — local seafood, Ensenada olive oil, valley vegetables — with the wines grown within view of the table. Book ahead for weekend lunch in summer; it fills up. The harvest season from August to October is particularly lively, with festivals and special events at many producers.
