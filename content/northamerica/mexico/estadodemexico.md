The State of México encircles Mexico City on three sides and is where the capital goes at weekends. It is also where the capital's own story starts: **[Teotihuacán](/northamerica/mexico/estadodemexico/teotihuacan)**, the largest city in the Americas at its height around 500 AD, stands an hour north-east, and nobody knows for certain who built it. The Aztecs found it already abandoned and gave it the name — "the place where the gods were created".

[Toluca](/northamerica/mexico/estadodemexico/toluca) is the capital, the highest state capital in Mexico at 2,660 metres, with a botanical garden housed under stained glass. [Valle de Bravo](/northamerica/mexico/estadodemexico/valle_de_bravo) on its reservoir is the weekend town — sailing, paragliding, and the monarch butterflies wintering in the forests above from November to March.

[Tepotzotlán](/northamerica/mexico/estadodemexico/tepotzotlan) has the finest Jesuit church interior in the country; [El Oro](/northamerica/mexico/estadodemexico/el_oro_de_hidalgo) is a mining town that built itself a French-style theatre with the proceeds; [Ixtapan de la Sal](/northamerica/mexico/estadodemexico/ixtapan_de_la_sal) has been a spa since before the Spanish.

[Naucalpan](/northamerica/mexico/estadodemexico/naucalpan) is part of the capital's sprawl rather than a destination in itself.
