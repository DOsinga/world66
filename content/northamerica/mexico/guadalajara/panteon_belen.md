  The cemetery's most famous resident is a figure known as "the vampire of Guadalajara" — a man buried in a mausoleum from which, according to legend, a fig tree grew through his body and pressed against the sealed door. The Panteón de Belén is Guadalajara's oldest surviving cemetery, established in the 1840s to receive victims of the cholera epidemics that swept the city. The grounds have a haunted quality: elaborate 19th-century mausoleums, crumbling angels, and overgrown tombs of wealthy colonial families. The combination of genuine historical interest and ghost stories has made it a popular site for day-of-the-dead visits and evening tours.

The cemetery is a 15-minute walk north of the historic centre. It functions as both an active cemetery and a public monument — visitors wander freely during daylight hours.
