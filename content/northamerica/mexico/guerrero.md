Guerrero holds two of Mexico's older destinations and some of its most difficult territory.

[Acapulco](/northamerica/mexico/guerrero/acapulco) was the first Mexican resort, the place Hollywood flew to in the 1950s, and its bay is still one of the most dramatic settings for a city anywhere. It has been through a great deal since — decline, cartel violence, and Hurricane Otis in 2023, which hit the city as a category 5 with almost no warning. The cliff divers at La Quebrada still work.

[Taxco](/northamerica/mexico/guerrero/taxco) is the opposite: a silver town poured down a mountainside, white houses and red roofs, the Santa Prisca church built by a single eighteenth-century mining fortune, and silversmiths in every third doorway. It is among the prettiest towns in Mexico.

[Ixtapa-Zihuatanejo](/northamerica/mexico/guerrero/ixtapazihuatanejo) pairs a planned resort with the fishing town it grew beside.

**Be realistic about safety.** Parts of Guerrero — particularly the interior, the Costa Grande road and Chilpancingo — carry the highest travel warnings any Mexican state receives, and have done for years. The destinations above are visited normally; overland travel between them, especially after dark, is where advice gets firm.
