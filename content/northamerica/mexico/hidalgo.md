Hidalgo is the state nearest Mexico City that tourists most often skip, which is to its advantage.

**[Tula](/northamerica/mexico/hidalgo/tula_de_allende)** was the Toltec capital, and its *atlantes* — four-metre basalt warriors standing on a pyramid — are among the most striking figures in Mesoamerican art.

The hills around [Pachuca](/northamerica/mexico/hidalgo/pachuca), the capital, were silver country worked by Cornish miners from the 1820s. They brought the pasty, which survives as the *paste*, sold everywhere in the state and now filled with mole and beans as often as meat; they also brought football, which arrived in Mexico here. [Mineral del Chico](/northamerica/mexico/hidalgo/mineral_del_chico) sits in a national park of rock formations above the town.

The north breaks into the **Huasteca**, where the Barranca de Metztitlán and the thermal rivers around [Tecozautla](/northamerica/mexico/hidalgo/tecozautla) and [Ixmiquilpan](/northamerica/mexico/hidalgo/ixmiquilpan) have made the state a weekend destination for hot springs. [Huichapan](/northamerica/mexico/hidalgo/huichapan) is where the first independence anniversary was celebrated, in 1812, while the war was still being fought.

Hidalgo is also *pulque* country — the fermented agave drink that preceded beer and is being revived.
