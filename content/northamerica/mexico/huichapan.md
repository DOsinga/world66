Huichapan is a small Pueblo Magico in western Hidalgo, close to the Queretaro border. It has more weight than its size suggests: a handsome colonial centre, several old churches in pink stone, a local chapter in Mexico's independence story, and the enormous Saucillo Aqueduct crossing a canyon outside town.

The centre is compact. The [Parroquia San Mateo Apostol](/northamerica/mexico/huichapan/parroquia_san_mateo_apostol), the Guadalupe chapel, El Chapitel, and the archaeology and history museum all sit within a few blocks of the plaza. It is an easy place to walk slowly, especially in the morning before the heat builds.

The real surprise is [El Saucillo Aqueduct](/northamerica/mexico/huichapan/saucillo_aqueduct), built in the eighteenth century and rising 44 metres above the ravine. That one structure gives Huichapan a proper day-trip anchor, while the churches and small museums fill out the rest of the visit. It works best for travellers already moving through Hidalgo, Queretaro, or the hot-spring towns nearby.
