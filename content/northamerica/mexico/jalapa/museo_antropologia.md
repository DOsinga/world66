If Xalapa has one unmissable sight, this is it. The Museo de Antropología — known locally as the MAX — holds the second largest archaeological collection in Mexico after the National Museum in Mexico City, and the finest collection of Olmec art anywhere. Seven colossal Olmec heads anchor the entry gallery, but the depth of the collection is the real story: Olmec, Huastec, Totonac and Remojadas pieces fill a long ramp of galleries that steps gradually down through 3,000 years of Gulf Coast civilisations.

The building itself is a calm, low-slung modernist structure of white marble and basalt by Edward Durell Stone, with planted courtyards opening off the galleries — it has aged unusually well. Allow at least two hours, ideally three. Open Tuesday to Sunday, with a modest entrance fee; well captioned in Spanish, less so in English.
