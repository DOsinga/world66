Much of what the world pictures as Mexican is from Jalisco: mariachi, tequila, the *charro* on horseback, the wide-brimmed hat. The state treats that inheritance as a working culture rather than a museum piece.

[Guadalajara](/northamerica/mexico/jalisco/guadalajara) is the second city and the least touristed of Mexico's great ones — a colonial centre of enormous plazas, Orozco's murals burning across the ceiling of the Hospicio Cabañas, and the mariachi trade still conducted in Plaza de los Mariachis. [Tlaquepaque and Tonalá](/northamerica/mexico/jalisco/guadalajara), now swallowed by the city, remain the craft towns.

[Tequila](/northamerica/mexico/jalisco/tequila) is a real town an hour out, surrounded by the blue agave fields that are themselves a UNESCO site, with distilleries from industrial to artisanal open to visitors.

On the coast, [Puerto Vallarta](/northamerica/mexico/jalisco/puertovallarta) is the established resort — old town, a long malecón, and the Sierra Madre dropping into the bay behind it. South of it the Costalegre empties out towards [Barra de Navidad](/northamerica/mexico/jalisco/barradenavidad); north, the bay villages of [Yelapa](/northamerica/mexico/jalisco/yelapa) and [Las Ánimas](/northamerica/mexico/jalisco/lasanimas) are reachable only by boat.

Inland, **Lake Chapala** — Mexico's largest — has drawn North American retirees to [Ajijic](/northamerica/mexico/jalisco/ajijic) for decades, and the mountain town of [Tapalpa](/northamerica/mexico/jalisco/tapalpa) is the weekend escape from Guadalajara.
