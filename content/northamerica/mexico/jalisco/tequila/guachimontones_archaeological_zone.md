Guachimontones is the archaeological surprise of the Tequila valleys: a West Mexican site built around circular stepped platforms rather than the rectangular pyramids most travellers expect from Mesoamerica. The site belongs to the Teuchitlán tradition and sits near the town of Teuchitlán, within the wider UNESCO-listed agave landscape associated with Tequila.

The circular monuments are the reason to go, but the setting matters too. From the platforms you look across fields, low hills, and the blue-agave country that defines the region today. A small museum helps explain why the architecture is so unusual, and the site pairs naturally with Tequila for travellers who want culture beyond distillery tours.
