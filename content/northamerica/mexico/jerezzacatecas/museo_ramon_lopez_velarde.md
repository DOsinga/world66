The childhood home of Ramón López Velarde has been preserved as a small museum dedicated to the poet's life and work. López Velarde, born here in 1888, went on to write "Suave Patria," a lyrical meditation on Mexican identity that became one of the most celebrated poems in the Spanish language. The house itself is an excellent example of a 19th-century provincial Mexican home — tiled floors, a central courtyard, thick walls to keep out the heat.

The museum displays personal effects, manuscripts, photographs, and editions of his work, with explanatory texts in Spanish. Even for visitors who don't read Spanish, the building and its quiet courtyard offer a vivid sense of life in provincial Zacatecas before the revolution. It is the one sight in Jerez that earns a proper stop rather than a passing glance.
