Los Mochis sits at the junction of the Pacific coast and the Sierra Madre, and the local seafood reflects that geography. Topolobampo Bay shrimp are the standout — large, sweet, and sold everywhere in the city, from market stalls to white-tablecloth restaurants. The regional ceviche uses local shrimp, tomato, onion, chilli, and lime and is among the best versions of the dish in Sinaloa.

The area around the central market and along Calle Leyva has the highest concentration of marisquerías (seafood restaurants), ranging from plastic-table spots selling shrimp cocktails to more polished establishments. Ordering a combination platter is the best way to sample multiple preparations. Aguachile — raw shrimp cured in lime juice with cucumber and chilli — is a local speciality worth trying if you're confident about the seafood's freshness.
