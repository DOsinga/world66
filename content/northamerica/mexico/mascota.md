Mascota sits in the pine-covered mountains of western Jalisco, far enough from Puerto Vallarta to feel like a different country. The town is small, whitewashed, and easy to walk, with a cool highland climate and the slow rhythm of a ranching valley.

The centre is the reason to come. [Templo Inconcluso de la Preciosa Sangre](/northamerica/mexico/mascota/templo_inconcluso_de_la_preciosa_sangre) is the standout: a 19th-century church left as open stone walls, more ruin than parish, and strangely beautiful for it. Around it are the main plaza, the [Parroquia de Nuestra Senora de los Dolores](/northamerica/mexico/mascota/parroquia_de_nuestra_senora_de_los_dolores), and small restaurants where the town still feels lived-in rather than staged.

Mascota also has real depth for a town this size. The [Museo Arqueologico de Mascota](/northamerica/mexico/mascota/museo_arqueologico_de_mascota) gives context to the older cultures of the valley, while the river and surrounding hills make it a good slow stop between Puerto Vallarta, Talpa de Allende, and the inland Jalisco towns.
