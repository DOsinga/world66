The Monumento a la Patria stands at a roundabout on the Paseo de Montejo, about halfway along the boulevard, and is one of the most ambitious public sculptures in Mexico. The monument takes the form of a large hemicycle — a curved wall faced with an intricate relief carving that runs the full length of the structure. The imagery traces Mexican history from pre-Hispanic cultures through the conquest, independence, reform, and revolution, with particular emphasis on Maya civilisation and Yucatecan identity.

Up close, the carving is extraordinary: figures emerge from the stone in varying levels of relief, some nearly fully three-dimensional, others flat against the background. The scale and detail are remarkable given that the monument was the work of essentially one man over eight years. The central element is a large national coat of arms above a map of Mexico. The monument is freely accessible at all times; it is particularly dramatic at night when lit from below. It receives far fewer visitors than the sites around the Plaza Mayor and deserves more attention.
