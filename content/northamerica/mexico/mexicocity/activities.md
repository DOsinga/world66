Mexico City is a city you walk and eat rather than one you book, but a few things are worth arranging.

**Food tours** are the best value. The street food here is the best argument for the city and the thing visitors are most nervous about; a guide who knows which stall has been in the same doorway for thirty years solves that in an afternoon. The good operators walk and use the metro.

**Lucha libre** at the Arena México on a Tuesday or Friday is genuinely a spectacle, and the tours exist mostly to handle the tickets and get you there and back — you can also simply buy a ticket at the box office.

**Xochimilco**, in the far south, is what is left of the lake the city was built on: canals, flat-bottomed trajineras poled between chinampas, mariachis for hire, and — behind it — a working agricultural system a thousand years old and the last wild habitat of the axolotl. Go on a weekday if you want the canals rather than the party.

Out of town: **Teotihuacán**, an hour northeast, with balloons at sunrise; **Taxco**, the silver town; **Puebla** and **Cholula**; **Valle de Bravo**; and, from late October to March, the long day east to the **monarch butterfly** sanctuaries.
