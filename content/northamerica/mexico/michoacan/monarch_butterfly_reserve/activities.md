title: Activities
type: section

There is one activity here and it lasts from **late October to March**. Outside those months the forest is empty and the sanctuaries are shut.

**Four colonies** are open to visitors. **El Rosario**, above Ocampo in Michoacán, is the largest and busiest; **Sierra Chincua**, nearer Angangueo, is quieter and the walk is gentler; **Cerro Pelón** and **El Capulín**, on the Estado de México side, are the least visited and the hardest to reach. All of them sit between 2,400 and 3,600 metres, and the air at that altitude is thin and cold in the shade even when the valley is warm.

**Go between eleven and two.** The butterflies cluster on the oyamel trunks overnight in great brown masses and do not move until the sun reaches them; when it does, they lift off in their millions, and the sound — a soft rushing like distant rain — is the thing people remember. Arrive at nine and you will see bark.

Entry is by **local guide**, which is not optional and is the point: the forests are owned by the ejidos who stopped logging them, and the guide fees and entrance money are what makes protecting the trees worth more than cutting them. Guides are hired at the gate and know where the colonies have settled, which moves from year to year.

Horses are available for the climb at El Rosario and Sierra Chincua. Do not touch butterflies on the ground, and stay on the path — a good proportion of the ones underfoot are alive.
