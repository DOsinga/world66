Morelia is a UNESCO-listed colonial city built in pink cantera stone, and the best thing to do in it is walk — the cathedral, the aqueduct's two hundred and fifty arches, the Palacio Clavijero, the sweet shops of the Mercado de Dulces.

What makes it a base is the rest of Michoacán. The **monarch butterfly sanctuaries** are two to three hours east and run from late October to March. **Pátzcuaro** and its lake, with the island of Janitzio and the P'urhépecha villages around the shore, are an hour southwest, and are the heart of Mexico's **Day of the Dead** observance on the night of 1 November — book months ahead or do not try. **Paricutín**, the volcano that came out of a cornfield in 1943 and buried a village to its church steeple, is three hours west and reached on horseback from Angahuan.

Morelia's own calendar is worth checking: the **Festival Internacional de Música** in November and the film festival in October both fill the city.
