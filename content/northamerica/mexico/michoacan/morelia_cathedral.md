The Cathedral of Morelia is one of the finest baroque churches in Mexico, a pink stone structure that anchors the city's UNESCO-listed historic centre and faces onto the main plaza. It is built from the local pink quartzite that gives Morelia its distinctive warm colour, and its proportions — monumental without being crushing — make it one of the most photographed buildings in western Mexico.

Inside, the cathedral retains its 18th-century organ, extensively restored and still used for regular concerts. The interior combines baroque altarpieces with a neoclassical renovation of the nave undertaken in the early 19th century. The plaza in front of the cathedral is the social heart of Morelia — lined with portales housing cafés and restaurants, and busy at all hours. Morelia lies about 330 km west of Mexico City and is very easily reached; it makes a worthwhile overnight stop or longer.
