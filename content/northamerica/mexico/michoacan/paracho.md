Paracho de Verduzco, usually just called Paracho, is a Purépecha town in the highlands of [Michoacán](/northamerica/mexico/michoacan), north of Uruapan. It is famous across Mexico for guitars: workshops, small shops, and family luthiers fill the centre with classical guitars, vihuelas, guitarrones, bajo sextos, and other stringed instruments.

The craft tradition is not a tourist invention. The Purépecha plateau has long been known for woodworking, and the colonial-era village specialisation system associated with Vasco de Quiroga helped turn Paracho toward instrument making. Today the town calls itself the world capital of the guitar, and on a market day it feels less like a slogan than a working fact.

Paracho is at its liveliest during the Feria de la Guitarra in early August, when concerts, workshops, competitions, and instrument stalls take over the town. At quieter times, the pleasure is simpler: walk Avenida 20 de Noviembre and the streets around the plaza, step into workshops, ask questions, and listen for the difference between a souvenir guitar and a serious handmade instrument.
