Tzintzuntzan ("place of the hummingbirds" in P'urhépecha) was the capital of the P'urhépecha empire — one of the great pre-Columbian civilisations of Mexico and the only major power that successfully held off Aztec expansion in the century before the Spanish arrived. The site is on the northern shore of Lake Pátzcuaro, about 18 km from Pátzcuaro town.

The yácatas — the distinctive stepped platform pyramids unique to P'urhépecha architecture — survive on a terrace overlooking the lake and are the main archaeological sight. Five of these truncated pyramid-platforms remain, their stonework partially restored, with views across the lake that help explain why this location was chosen for the empire's capital. Below the archaeological zone, the 16th-century Franciscan convento built partly with stones from the destroyed pyramids adds a layer of colonial history. The craftwork market in the village sells the lacquerware and woven goods for which this area of Michoacán is famous.
