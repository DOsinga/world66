Morelos is the smallest state in Mexico after the capital, a compact but varied region due south of Mexico City in the central highlands. Its capital is Cuernavaca, historically known as the "City of Eternal Spring" for its mild climate — a quality that made it the preferred weekend retreat of Mexico City residents since Aztec times and through the colonial period, when Hernán Cortés built his [palace here](/northamerica/mexico/morelos/palace_of_cortes).

The state is best known for Cuernavaca and for the archaeological site of [Xochicalco](/northamerica/mexico/morelos/xochicalco), a pre-Columbian fortified hilltop city that flourished between the fall of Teotihuacán and the rise of the Toltecs. The village of [Tepoztlán](/northamerica/mexico/morelos/tepoztlan), set dramatically below volcanic cliffs, has a famous pyramid on the ridge above the town and a well-preserved Dominican monastery in the centre. The region's warm valleys produce sugar cane, rice, and flowers, and the local cuisine — carnitas, rice dishes, and various mole preparations — is worth seeking out.
