El Conde is a pre-Columbian archaeological site in the municipality of Naucalpan, preserving the remains of a small ceremonial platform and pyramid associated with the Teotihuacan cultural sphere. The site dates to the Preclassic and Classic periods (roughly 200 BCE–650 CE) and was part of the extensive settlement network that surrounded Teotihuacan during its peak.

The remains are modest — a partially excavated platform and associated structures — and the site operates as an open-air museum with explanatory signage. It is not on the standard tourist circuit and tends to be quietly visited, which makes it an interesting stop for those with a genuine interest in pre-Columbian archaeology. The context of a major Teotihuacan-period settlement this close to modern Mexico City's northwestern edge is itself thought-provoking.
