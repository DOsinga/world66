Los Remedios National Park occupies the volcanic hills at the western edge of Naucalpan and provides one of the largest green spaces accessible from the northwestern Mexico City metropolitan area. The park covers around 400 hectares of scrub and woodland, with footpaths, cycling routes, and areas for picnicking. On weekends it draws large numbers of Mexico City residents looking for accessible outdoor space.

The sanctuary of the Virgen de los Remedios, at the park's edge, is historically significant and architecturally interesting — a colonial-era church with baroque embellishments that sits atop a pre-Columbian pyramid base. The combination of colonial religious architecture built directly over indigenous ceremonial structures is characteristic of early colonial Mexico and is plainly visible here. The views from the sanctuary terrace extend over the metropolitan area.
