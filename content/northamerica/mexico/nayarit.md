Nayarit is small, green and mostly coastal, and the stretch immediately north of Puerto Vallarta has become the most fashionable beach in Mexico.

[Sayulita](/northamerica/mexico/nayarit/sayulita) led it — a surf village that filled with boutiques and taco stands and is now busy year-round — with San Pancho next door quieter, and [Punta Mita](/northamerica/mexico/nayarit/puntamita) on its headland at the expensive end. The [Marietas Islands](/northamerica/mexico/nayarit/marietasislands) offshore hold the much-photographed hidden beach inside a bomb crater, visitor numbers now strictly capped.

[San Blas](/northamerica/mexico/nayarit/sanblas) further north was a Spanish naval port and is now a birding destination of the first rank — the boat trip up La Tovara through the mangroves is the classic. Bring repellent; the *jejenes* are famous.

[Mexcaltitán](/northamerica/mexico/nayarit/mexcaltitan) is an oval island town in a lagoon, its streets flooding in the wet season, and is claimed by some to be Aztlán — the place the Aztecs set out from.

Inland, [Tepic](/northamerica/mexico/nayarit/tepic) is the capital and the gateway to **Huichol** (Wixárika) country in the sierra, whose yarn paintings and beadwork are the state's best-known craft.
