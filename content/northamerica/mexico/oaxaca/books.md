**Zapotec Science: Farming and Food in the Indian Villages of Oaxaca** by Roberto González Villareal — an anthropological study of traditional Zapotec agriculture and food systems that gives a deep grounding in how the land and culture of the valley relate to each other. More readable than it sounds.

**In the Shadow of Tlaloc: Life in a Mexican Village** by Leigh Binford — a close study of a Mixtec community in the Oaxacan sierra, covering land, labour, and daily life. Good preparation for understanding the living indigenous culture you will encounter in the valley villages.

**Like Water for Chocolate** by Laura Esquivel — set across the border in northern Mexico but the most evocative literary treatment of Mexican food culture. Read it before you go; you will taste the cooking differently.

**The Lacuna** by Barbara Kingsolver — a novel that moves between Mexico (with Frida Kahlo and Diego Rivera as characters) and Virginia; the Mexican sections are vivid and historically grounded, though Oaxaca itself barely features.
