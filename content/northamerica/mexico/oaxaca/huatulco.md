Huatulco is Mexico's planned resort that did not get out of hand. The development agency took the coast in 1984 with the same powers that produced Cancún, but kept the building heights low, the hotel zone small, and some seventy per cent of the land as national park. It shows.

The point of the place is the **nine bays** — Santa Cruz, Chahué, Tangolunda and six more, several reachable only by boat — strung along a coast of coral-headed coves. The **snorkelling and diving** are the best on this stretch of Pacific: visibility is decent most of the year, the reefs are shallow and easy, and La Entrega bay can be swum from the beach.

[La Crucecita](/northamerica/mexico/oaxaca/huatulco) is where people actually live and eat, a few minutes inland from the hotels, with a plaza and a market that keep normal Oaxacan prices.

Inland the Copalita river runs down from the sierra for rafting, and the coffee fincas of Pluma Hidalgo are an hour up the mountain.

Huatulco is a long way from [Oaxaca city](/northamerica/mexico/oaxaca/oaxaca_de_juarez) — seven hours by road over the sierra, or a short flight — and most visitors come straight in by air.
