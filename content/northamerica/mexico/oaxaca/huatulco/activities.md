Huatulco is nine bays and thirty-six beaches on a stretch of coast that was planned as a resort and then mostly left alone, with a **national park** covering both the land behind and the water in front.

The **bay tour** is the standard day: a boat or catamaran out of Santa Cruz or Marina Chahué, stopping at La Entrega for snorkelling, then Maguey, Cacaluta and San Agustín. Several bays can only be reached by water. The reef at La Entrega and San Agustín is modest by Caribbean standards but alive and shallow.

From **December to March** humpback whales calve offshore and the whale-watching trips are genuine rather than hopeful. Dolphins, turtles and rays are around all year.

Inland, the **Copalita** gives the best rafting in southern Mexico, class III through the dry season and harder in summer. The sierra above the coast grows coffee at altitude and the fincas take visitors, and the Copalita archaeological site at the river mouth is quiet and worth an hour.
