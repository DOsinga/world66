Oaxaca city is small and the valley around it is not, and almost everything is a half or full day out.

**Monte Albán**, twenty minutes up a hill, is a Zapotec capital built on a mountain top that was levelled to take it — occupied for thirteen centuries, abandoned around 850 AD, and still one of the great archaeological sites of the Americas. Go early; there is no shade.

The valley circuit strings together the **Tule tree** at Santa María (the thickest trunk of any tree on earth), the weaving village of **Teotitlán del Valle**, the stone-mosaic palaces of **Mitla**, and the petrified waterfall at **Hierve el Agua** — mineral springs that have left calcified cascades down a cliff, with pools at the top. Hierve el Agua is on communal land and has been closed by the community more than once in disputes over who collects the entrance money; check it is open the week you go.

**Mezcal** is the other reason people come. The palenques around **Santiago Matatlán** distil in clay or copper from agave roasted in an earth pit, and a visit to a family palenque is a different experience from a tasting room in town — you see the pit, the horse-drawn tahona and the still. Any agency here sells the trip.

In the city itself: the **Mercado 20 de Noviembre** and its smoke-filled meat corridor, the Santo Domingo cultural centre and its ethnobotanical garden, and the food. Oaxaca is the best eating in Mexico and most of it costs very little.
