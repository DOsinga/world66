The Convento de San Pedro y San Pablo is the reason Teposcolula belongs on a travel map. Built by Dominican friars in the 16th century, it is huge for such a small town, with a sober church, arcaded convent buildings, and an open chapel designed for large outdoor congregations.

Look closely at the scale of the atrium and chapel. This was mission architecture built for a newly conquered Mixtec population, not a parish church for a small Spanish town. Inside, the fresco of the martyrdom of Saint Peter is the detail most visitors remember.
