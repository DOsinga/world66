  The story goes that in 1943, a group of US military wives from Fort Duncan
  arrived at the Victory Club restaurant after closing time. Cook Ignacio
  "Nacho" Anaya improvised a quick snack from what was available: fried
  tortilla chips, Colby cheese melted on top, and sliced jalapeños. He named
  the dish after himself — "Nacho's especiales" — and it spread from there.
  His son Ignacio Anaya Jr. maintained the story his whole life, and the city
  of Piedras Negras has embraced it so fully that they hold an International
  Nacho Festival every October. The International Nacho Day on November 6th
  is also attributed to Piedras Negras.

A bronze statue in the city centre commemorates Ignacio Anaya and his accidental culinary invention. The statue shows Anaya holding a plate of nachos, and it attracts a steady trickle of visitors who want a photograph with the man who fed the world's sports bars and cinema lobbies. The location is on Avenida Zaragoza near the central plaza.

Whether or not the story is entirely accurate — other claimed birthplaces exist — Piedras Negras has fully committed to the claim and builds a small local identity around it. The International Nacho Festival in October is a lively affair with cooking competitions and live music, and if you happen to be passing through then, it is worth stopping for.
