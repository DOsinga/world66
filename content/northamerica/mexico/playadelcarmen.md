Playa del Carmen is the main town of the Riviera Maya, about 68 km south of Cancun along the Caribbean coast of the Yucatan Peninsula. It started as a small fishing village and departure point for ferries to Cozumel, and while it has grown considerably since the 1990s, it retains a more manageable scale than Cancun. The [beach](/northamerica/mexico/playadelcarmen/playa_del_carmen_beach) is excellent — long, white-sand, with clear water — and the reef offshore is part of the Great Maya Reef, the second-largest in the world.

The town centres on [Quinta Avenida](/northamerica/mexico/playadelcarmen/quinta_avenida) (Fifth Avenue), a pedestrianised street running parallel to the beach, lined with restaurants, bars, and shops. It is pleasant to walk but increasingly touristy; the same souvenirs sold there can be found for half the price on the side streets. If you want to stay close to a lively scene without being locked into a resort, Playa is a good base — you can walk to the beach, catch a ferry to [Cozumel](/northamerica/mexico/playadelcarmen/cozumel) for the day, and reach [Tulum](/northamerica/mexico/playadelcarmen/tulum) or [Chichén Itzá](/northamerica/mexico/playadelcarmen/chichen_itza) by bus.

The diving is the main reason many visitors come. Cozumel, a 35-minute ferry ride away, has some of the clearest water and richest reef diving in the Americas. The [cenotes](/northamerica/mexico/playadelcarmen/cenotes) — freshwater sinkholes fed by underground rivers — are scattered throughout the surrounding jungle and make for memorable snorkelling and diving, with visibility sometimes exceeding 100 metres. [Río Secreto](/northamerica/mexico/playadelcarmen/rio_secreto), an extraordinary underground cave system south of town, offers guided wading tours through stalactite chambers. The eco-park at [Xcaret](/northamerica/mexico/playadelcarmen/xcaret), built around a Maya harbour site, is expensive but genuinely impressive for a full day.

Few visitors realise that [ancient Maya ruins](/northamerica/mexico/playadelcarmen/xaman_ha_ruins) lie just blocks from Quinta Avenida. Avoid the all-inclusive resorts if you can — a small hotel in town costs a fraction of the resort price and gives you freedom to eat where you want, explore the neighbourhood, and actually see Mexico.
