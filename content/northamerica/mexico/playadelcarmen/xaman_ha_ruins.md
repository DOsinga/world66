  Xaman-Há means "water of the north" in Yucatec Maya — the site functioned as a waypoint on the coastal trading route that connected Tulum with Xcaret and other ceremonial centres. 
  The ruins were inhabited when the first Spanish explorers sailed past this stretch of coast in the early 16th century, making them one of the few Maya sites that was still an active settlement at the time of contact.

Most visitors walking Quinta Avenida have no idea that a genuine Maya site sits a few blocks away. The Xaman-Há ruins are scattered through what is now the Playacar gated residential development on the south side of town, reached via the main gate off the highway. The ruins are modest — a handful of small platforms and structures — but their setting amid manicured gardens and the fact that few tourists find them makes a visit rewarding.

The site was part of the coastal Maya trading network and dates to the Postclassic period (900–1500 CE). Plaques explain the significance of the structures. Access is usually possible on foot through the Playacar entrance, though it is worth asking at the gate. Entry to the ruins themselves is free.
