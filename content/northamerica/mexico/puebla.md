Puebla was founded in 1531 as a Spanish city on empty ground, deliberately placed between Mexico City and the sea, and it has been a conservative, devout, well-fed place ever since.

[Puebla de Zaragoza](/northamerica/mexico/puebla/puebla_de_zaragoza) has a UNESCO centre of tiled façades — *talavera*, the glazed ceramic the city has made since the sixteenth century — around seventy churches, and the dish that carries its name: *mole poblano*, a sauce of chillies and chocolate among thirty-odd ingredients. The battle of 5 May 1862, in which a Mexican force beat the French army outside the city, is why Cinco de Mayo exists.

[Cholula](/northamerica/mexico/puebla/cholula) next door has the largest pyramid by volume in the world, which looks like a hill with a church on top because that is what the Spanish made of it.

The volcanoes **Popocatépetl** and **Iztaccíhuatl** stand on the western border; Popocatépetl is active and smokes most days.

The **Sierra Norte** in the north of the state is a different country — cloud forest, Nahua and Totonac villages, and towns like [Cuetzalan](/northamerica/mexico/puebla/cuetzalan) and [Pahuatlán](/northamerica/mexico/puebla/pahuatlan) where the market day is still the week's event. [Tehuacán](/northamerica/mexico/puebla/tehuacan) in the dry south is where maize was first domesticated.
