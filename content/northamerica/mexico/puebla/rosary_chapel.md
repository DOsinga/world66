  The chapel was completed in 1690 after 40 years of construction. Every surface — walls, ceiling, arches — is covered in gilded stucco, with no bare stone visible anywhere. It is often called the eighth wonder of the world, which is hyperbole, but it is one of the most extraordinary Baroque interiors in the Americas.
---

Inside the Church of Santo Domingo, this side chapel is an explosion of gilded stucco: saints, angels, heraldic symbols, floral motifs, and allegorical figures cover every surface from floor to dome. The effect is overwhelming — not garish but genuinely magnificent in its ambition. Work on the chapel began around 1650 and took four decades; the result is the high point of Mexican Baroque.

The main church of Santo Domingo, which you pass through to reach the chapel, is also worth time: more restrained, but still richly decorated with fine altarpieces. Entry to both is free; the chapel draws crowds in the middle of the day, so arrive early or late in the afternoon. The facade of Santo Domingo on 5 de Mayo is itself one of the finest colonial exteriors in the city.
