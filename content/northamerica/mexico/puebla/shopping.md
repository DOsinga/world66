---
title: "Shopping"
type: section
---

Puebla is one of the best cities in Mexico for craft shopping, and unlike many tourist destinations the goods are the real thing: Talavera pottery made in workshops that have operated for centuries, traditional sweets from family confectioneries, antiques in a dedicated neighbourhood market.

**Talavera pottery** is the flagship purchase. Workshops are concentrated on **Calle 18 Poniente**, northwest of the historic centre, where half a dozen operations — from small family studios to large factory-showrooms with guided tours — can be visited in an afternoon. Prices reflect genuine quality: authentic Talavera carries a certification seal, uses tin-oxide glaze, and is hand-painted by artisans. Cheaper imitations sold at market stalls are usually mass-produced in other regions.

**El Parián** is the craft market on the northeast corner of the Zócalo, housed in a 19th-century arcaded building. It sells pottery, textiles, leather goods, and souvenirs. Quality varies widely; the Talavera sold here is mostly tourist grade, but it's a good place to browse for smaller gifts and compare prices.

**6 Oriente** (the street running east from the cathedral) is lined with traditional sweet shops selling Puebla's signature confections: **camotes** (sweet potato paste rolled in sugar), **rompope** (a rich egg-and-cinnamon liqueur sold by the bottle), **muéganos** (fried dough with piloncillo), and candied fruits. Buy from the small family shops rather than the chain operations.

**Barrio de los Sapos** (Frog Quarter), a short walk south of the Zócalo, is the antiques district. Permanent shops sell colonial furniture, vintage silver, ceramics, and art; outdoor stalls fill the Plazuela de los Sapos on weekends. Sunday morning is the best time to visit.
