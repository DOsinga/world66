The Zócalo is the heart of Puebla's historic centre and one of the finest central plazas in Mexico. Ringed by colonial arcades (portales) sheltering cafes, restaurants, and shops, the square feels genuinely alive at all hours: shoe-shiners, vendors, families on benches, and the perpetual soundtrack of pigeons and street musicians.

The south side is dominated by the cathedral, whose twin bell towers frame every view. The north side is occupied by the Palacio Municipal; the east by the Casa de la Cultura. The 17th-century Baroque fountain in the centre is one of the oldest in Mexico. Unlike many Mexican zócalos, this one has always been kept clear of permanent stalls and market structures — a policy that gives it a more formal, ceremonial character. It is at its best on weekend evenings, when the portales fill for dinner and the square lights up.
