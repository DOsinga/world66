A small archipelago about 8 kilometres off Punta Mita, designated a UNESCO biosphere reserve and one of the most important seabird breeding sites on the Pacific coast of Mexico. Blue-footed boobies, brown pelicans, and frigatebirds all nest here in large numbers. Underwater the islands have spectacular reef systems with manta rays, sea turtles and dolphins regularly sighted.

The famous Hidden Beach (Playa del Amor) inside a collapsed cavern is the headline draw, but visits are now strictly regulated — only 116 visitors per day with permits, and only certified operators are allowed to enter. Most boat tours from Puerto Vallarta or Punta Mita combine snorkelling at the outer reefs with a panga ride past the cavern openings rather than landing inside.
