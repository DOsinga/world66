Akumal is a bay with seagrass in it, and green turtles come to eat the seagrass. That is the whole attraction and it is a good one.

Since 2016 the bay has been a **federal fishing refuge** with enforced rules, which exist because the turtles were being chased by several hundred unguided snorkellers a day. You enter the protected zone only with a licensed guide, wearing a life jacket, at a maximum of six swimmers per guide, for about forty-five minutes. Sunscreen is prohibited. Touching a turtle is prohibited. Entering alone is illegal and fined. The rules are a nuisance and the bay is measurably better for them.

North of the bay, **Yal-Ku lagoon** is where an underground river surfaces and meets the sea: brackish, calm, full of fish, and snorkellable without a boat. South of town the reef is good and quiet, and the **cenotes** of the Dos Ojos system are fifteen minutes up the highway.

Turtle nesting season runs roughly May to October, with hatchlings from July; the beach is patrolled and nests are marked.
