Reynosa is a major border city of about 650,000 people in the state of Tamaulipas, sitting directly across the Rio Grande from McAllen, Texas. Like many Mexican border cities, it has a split personality — a busy industrial economy driven by maquiladoras and cross-border trade, and a reputation for cartel-related violence that has made international headlines. Travelers should check current security advisories carefully before visiting, as conditions can change quickly.

That said, Reynosa is a real, working city with a bustling centro, a large commercial zone along the highway to [Monterrey](/northamerica/mexico/monterrey), and a border-crossing culture that is fascinating in its own right. The [Zona Centro market](/northamerica/mexico/reynosa/zona_centro_market) has a lively sprawl of stalls where you can find everything from hand-tooled boots to pirated electronics, and the [Plaza Principal](/northamerica/mexico/reynosa/plaza_principal) has a few decent restaurants and the imposing Catedral de Nuestra Señora de Guadalupe.

Reynosa is primarily a transit point — either for crossing to or from Texas via the [McAllen–Hidalgo International Bridge](/northamerica/mexico/reynosa/getting_there), or for the drive south to [Monterrey](/northamerica/mexico/monterrey), which is about three hours away through increasingly dramatic mountain scenery. The city's maquiladora belt, which lines the highway out of town, shows another face of border life: the global assembly lines that stitch together cheap labour and free-trade rules. If you are crossing the border here, keep your wits about you and stick to well-traveled routes.
