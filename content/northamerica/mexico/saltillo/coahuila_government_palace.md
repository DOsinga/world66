The Coahuila State Government Palace, known locally as the Palacio Rosa (Pink Palace), stands on the Plaza de Armas directly facing the cathedral. Built in the neoclassical style with a distinctive pink facade, it houses state government offices and a series of murals depicting scenes from Coahuilan and Mexican history — particularly the state's role in the Mexican Revolution and its ties to Francisco I. Madero, who was born in Coahuila.

The building is open to visitors during business hours and entry is free. The interior courtyard offers a calm contrast to the busy plaza outside, and the murals are worth a slow look for anyone interested in Mexican history.
