The Plaza de Armas is the historical heart of Saltillo, flanked by the cathedral and the government palace and lined with benches, trees, and a central bandstand. It is a thoroughly Mexican public square — active from morning to night, with vendors selling esquites and elotes, families out for an evening walk, and frequent events and performances on weekends.

The plaza was laid out in the colonial era on the traditional Spanish grid plan and has retained its central role in city life. Surrounding streets have colonial-era arcades with cafes and shops. The square comes most alive on weekend evenings and during festivals, when performances of music and dance take over the central kiosk.
