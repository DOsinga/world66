The Cathedral of Santiago de Saltillo stands on the Plaza de Armas and is widely considered one of the finest baroque churches in northern Mexico. The ornate churrigueresque facade is covered in intricate carved stone — sunbursts, columns, and figurative ornament all executed in warm pink cantera. The twin towers are visible from much of the historic centre and make a striking landmark.

The interior is cooler and quieter, with a vaulted nave and a series of side chapels dedicated to various saints. The cathedral has been the religious heart of Coahuila since the colonial era. It is free to enter and open to visitors throughout the day, though you should respect services in progress.
