San Joaquín sits high in the Sierra Gorda of Querétaro, a cool mountain town where the air is sharper than in the Bajío plains below. It is one of Mexico's Pueblos Mágicos, but the draw is not a polished colonial centre. The reason to come is the landscape around town: forested hills, caves, and pre-Hispanic ruins tied to old mining and trade routes.

The strongest anchor is [Ranas Archaeological Zone](/northamerica/mexico/san_joaquin/ranas_archaeological_zone), a hilltop site with ball courts, terraces, and views over the sierra. [Grutas Los Herrera](/northamerica/mexico/san_joaquin/grutas_los_herrera) gives the visit a very different texture, with limestone chambers and formations under the hills. [Campo Alegre](/northamerica/mexico/san_joaquin/campo_alegre) is the easy green escape for picnics and cool-weather wandering.

San Joaquín also has a strong musical identity. The town is known for huapango, the fast-footed regional son huasteco dance, and hosts an annual national huapango contest. It is a small place, but if you are already in Querétaro and want a mountain day with archaeology rather than another colonial plaza, it earns the trip.
