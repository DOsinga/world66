San Blas is a small fishing and surfing town on the Pacific coast of Nayarit state, about 70 km north of Puerto Vallarta. It has a slow, colonial-era character and sees far fewer tourists than the resorts to the south. The town was an important colonial port — [Fort Basilio](/northamerica/mexico/sanblas/fort_basilio) on the hill above the river mouth is a romantic ruin, and Henry Wadsworth Longfellow set his poem *The Bells of San Blas* here.

The coast around San Blas is wild and rewarding: excellent [surf breaks](/northamerica/mexico/sanblas/surfing) at Matanchen Bay, estuaries and mangrove lagoons full of [birds](/northamerica/mexico/sanblas/birding) — this is one of the best birding sites in Mexico, with over 500 species recorded in the area. The [boat trip to La Tovara](/northamerica/mexico/sanblas/la_tovara), a freshwater spring reached through mangrove channels teeming with herons and crocodiles, is the classic excursion. The local dish, [pescado zarandeado](/northamerica/mexico/sanblas/zarandeado), is one of the best things to eat on the Pacific coast. The famous no-see-um gnats (jejenes) can be ferocious, particularly in the evenings — bring repellent.
