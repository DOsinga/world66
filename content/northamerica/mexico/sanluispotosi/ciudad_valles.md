A hot, flat sugar town on the Río Valles in the eastern lowlands of San Luis Potosí, with nothing much in it and everything around it. Ciudad Valles is where you sleep and hire a guide for the **Huasteca Potosina** — a limestone country of turquoise rivers, waterfalls and caves that is the best adventure destination in central Mexico and still largely visited by Mexicans rather than foreigners.

The water is the point and it is genuinely turquoise, for the same reason it is at Semuc Champey or Plitvice: limestone dissolved in the river precipitates out and the suspended calcium scatters blue light.

Within an hour or two: the **Cascadas de Micos**, a staircase of falls you jump down in a wetsuit and helmet; **Tamul**, a 105-metre waterfall reached by punting a boat two hours upstream against the current; the **Sótano de las Golondrinas**, a 376-metre vertical shaft out of which tens of thousands of swifts spiral every dawn; the **Puente de Dios** at Tamasopo; and the river at **Tamasopo** itself. South, towards [Xilitla](/northamerica/mexico/sanluispotosi/xilitla), is Edward James's concrete surrealist garden in the cloud forest.

Come between **November and May** if you want the rivers at their most blue; the summer rains turn them green and brown and close some of the sites outright. It is hot and humid all year.
