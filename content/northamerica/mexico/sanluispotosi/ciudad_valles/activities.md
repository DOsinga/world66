Everything here is water, and the colour of it changes with the season. **November to May** is the dry window: rivers turquoise, levels low, every site open. From **June to October** the rains turn the water green or brown, raise the rivers, and close some of the waterfall jumps and the Tamul boat trip for days at a time. Operators will tell you this if you ask.

**Cascadas de Micos** is the one most people do first — a chain of waterfalls you descend by jumping, with a wetsuit, a helmet and a guide, the biggest drop around twelve metres. **Tamul**, at 105 metres the highest fall in the state, is reached by punting a wooden boat two hours up the Santa María against the current, which is slower and better than it sounds.

The **Sótano de las Golondrinas** at Aquismón is a sinkhole 376 metres deep and wide enough to drop the Chrysler Building into. Swifts and green parakeets spiral out of it at dawn in their tens of thousands and spiral back at dusk. Experienced cavers rappel it; everyone else watches from the lip.

Also within reach: the **Puente de Dios** and the river at Tamasopo, the **Cascada de Tamasopo**, **Minas Viejas** and **El Salto** in the north near El Naranjo, rafting on the Tampaón, and [Xilitla](/northamerica/mexico/sanluispotosi/xilitla) with Las Pozas.

Guides should hold **NOM-09** certification, the Mexican standard for adventure-tourism guiding. Ask. The jumps and the rappels here are real and the river does drown people.
