Xilitla is a coffee town in cloud forest on the southern edge of the Huasteca, and the reason to come is **Las Pozas**: thirty-odd hectares of jungle in which the English poet and patron Edward James spent twenty years and a fortune building concrete surrealist structures — staircases to nowhere, columns shaped like flowers, doorways and pavilions, all now half-reclaimed by the forest and threaded by waterfalls and pools. It is the strangest place in Mexico. Visitor numbers are capped by timed ticket.

The **Sótano de las Huahuas**, nearby, is the other swift cave — less famous than Las Golondrinas and with the same dawn and dusk spiral of birds.

From here the rest of the Huasteca Potosina is within reach: the waterfalls at **Tamul** and **Micos**, the river at **Tamasopo**, and the rafting on the Tampaón, all of which the Xilitla operators run. The dry season, **November to May**, is when the rivers run turquoise.

Xilitla's own hills grow coffee at altitude and the town is cooler and wetter than the lowlands an hour north.
