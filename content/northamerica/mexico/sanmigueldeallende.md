San Miguel de Allende is a colonial city in the highlands of Guanajuato state, at an elevation of around 1,900 metres, and it is one of the most polished small cities in Mexico. The historic centre was given UNESCO World Heritage status in 2008, and the recognition has brought a steady influx of foreign residents and artists who have made it a well-serviced place to visit — good restaurants, galleries, and language schools everywhere — without quite losing its Mexican character.

The centrepiece is El Jardín, the main square, overlooked by the pink neo-Gothic spires of La Parroquia de San Miguel Arcángel. The church was redesigned in the late 19th century by a self-taught local stonemason who, according to local legend, based his drawings on postcards of European Gothic cathedrals. The result is unmistakably Mexican despite its borrowed inspiration. The streets fanning out from the square are colonial and largely carless — cobbled, hilly, and lined with churches, mansions, and art galleries.

San Miguel was a centre of the Mexican War of Independence, and one of its heroes, Ignacio Allende, was born here. The house is now a museum. The city also has a tradition of creative education that dates to the postwar era, when American GIs on the GI Bill came to study art here and stayed. That bohemian lineage gives the place a slightly different flavour from purely touristic colonial cities.

The climate is mild year-round, the food is good, and the Festival Internacional de Música draws classical musicians in August. If you are anywhere in central Mexico, it is worth the detour.
