Sayulita is a small surf town on Mexico's Pacific coast, about 40 kilometers north of [Puerto Vallarta](/northamerica/mexico/puertovallarta). It has the kind of laid-back character that draws people in for a day and keeps them for a week: colorful buildings, a long sandy beach, reliable surf, good tacos, and a [town square](/northamerica/mexico/sayulita/sayulita_plaza) where the main activity is sitting and doing nothing in particular.

It isn't undiscovered — the cobblestone streets fill up on weekends and the high season brings crowds — but outside peak periods Sayulita remains genuinely unhurried. It works well as a base for exploring the Riviera Nayarit coast, or simply as a few days' escape from the noise of Puerto Vallarta.

The [surf break](/northamerica/mexico/sayulita/surf_lessons) here is consistent and beginner-friendly, which makes Sayulita a good place to take a first lesson. More experienced surfers will want to head to [La Lancha](/northamerica/mexico/sayulita/la_lancha) nearby for faster, less-crowded waves. When the surf is flat, [boat trips to the Marietas Islands](/northamerica/mexico/sayulita/marietas_islands) fill the gap — the hidden beach inside the sea cave and the blue-footed boobies on the cliffs are worth the 30-minute crossing.

The social life of the town rotates around the [Plaza Principal](/northamerica/mexico/sayulita/sayulita_plaza) and the [flag-draped Calle Delfines](/northamerica/mexico/sayulita/calle_delfines). The [Friday farmers market](/northamerica/mexico/sayulita/mercado_del_pueblo) on Avenida Revolución is excellent for provisions and prepared food, running from November through April. The walls of the back streets are covered in [murals](/northamerica/mexico/sayulita/street_murals), much of it influenced by Wixáritari (Huichol) art traditions that run deep in this part of Nayarit.
