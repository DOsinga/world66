The Wixáritari (commonly called Huichol) people from the mountains of Jalisco and Nayarit have a strong presence in Sayulita's craft market, and their work is among the most visually compelling indigenous art in Mexico. The two main forms are beadwork — tiny glass beads pressed into wax to create densely patterned animals, masks, and ceremonial objects — and yarn paintings, where wool is pressed into beeswax to create bright, intricate pictures on flat boards.

Quality varies enormously. The best pieces are made by Wixáritari artisans using traditional methods and can take weeks to complete; cheaper versions are machine-made imitations or quick tourist items with little connection to the tradition. A few shops in Sayulita sell authenticated pieces directly from Wixáritari artists — these cost more but are worth it if you want something genuinely made.

Look for pieces where the beadwork is dense and even under a magnifying glass, with no visible wax between beads. In yarn paintings, look for crisp edges and complex symbolic content rather than generic rainbow patterns. Prices for serious pieces run from several hundred to several thousand pesos.
