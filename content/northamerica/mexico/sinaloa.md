Sinaloa feeds much of Mexico — tomatoes, maize, and the largest fishing fleet on the Pacific coast — and it exports *banda*, the brass-heavy music that is the state's other main product.

[Mazatlán](/northamerica/mexico/sinaloa/mazatlan) is the destination: a nineteenth-century old town restored around its opera house, a 21-kilometre malecón, and a carnival in February that is among the largest in the world. It is a working port as well as a resort, which keeps it honest.

[Los Mochis](/northamerica/mexico/sinaloa/losmochis) exists mainly as the Pacific end of the **Chepe railway** into the [Copper Canyon](/northamerica/mexico/chihuahua/coppercanyon), and [El Fuerte](/northamerica/mexico/sinaloa/el_fuerte), a colonial town upriver, is the better place to board it.

[Culiacán](/northamerica/mexico/sinaloa/culiacan) is the state capital and a serious city. It is also the home of the cartel that bears the state's name, and its security situation can deteriorate quickly and without warning — the shutdowns of 2019 and 2024 closed the city for days. Travellers rarely have reason to go.

[Mocorito](/northamerica/mexico/sinaloa/mocorito) is a quiet *pueblo mágico* inland, and [Bahía de Topolobampo](/northamerica/mexico/sinaloa/bahia_de_topolobampo) is the ferry port for La Paz across the Gulf of California.
