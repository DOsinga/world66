title: Activities

---

Mazatlán is a working Pacific port with a restored nineteenth-century old town at one end and a long hotel strip at the other, and the best way around it is a **pulmonía** — the open-sided taxis built on Volkswagen running gear that exist in this city and nowhere else.

On the water: **sport fishing** is the old reputation, with sailfish and marlin offshore, and the fleet is serious. Boats run out to the **Islas Tres Hermanas** off the malecón for snorkelling and birds, and to **Isla de la Piedra**, the long quiet beach across the harbour reached by a five-minute panga from the Playa Sur embarcadero.

Inland, the colonial mining villages of **Concordia** and **Copala** are an hour up into the sierra and are the standard half-day out.

Mazatlán's own spectacle is free: the **clavadistas**, cliff divers who jump from a platform on the malecón at Glorieta Sánchez Taboada, timing it to an incoming swell. And **Carnaval**, in February, is the third-largest in the world and fills the entire city.
