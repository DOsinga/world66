Tamaulipas runs along the Rio Grande from the Gulf to the Nuevo León border, and most people who pass through it are crossing between countries rather than stopping.

[Tampico](/northamerica/mexico/tamaulipas/tampico) in the south is the exception worth making. It is an oil port whose boom years left a centre of New Orleans-style ironwork balconies, now partly restored, and it has a genuine Gulf food culture — *jaiba*, crab in every form, and the *torta de la barda*.

[Nuevo Laredo](/northamerica/mexico/tamaulipas/nuevolaredo) and [Reynosa](/northamerica/mexico/tamaulipas/reynosa) are border cities and among the busiest commercial crossings on the continent: trucks, factories and bridges rather than destinations.

**El Cielo**, a cloud-forest biosphere reserve inland, is the state's natural draw — a place where temperate and tropical species overlap, with serious birding.

**Be clear-eyed about this state.** Tamaulipas carries the highest level of travel advisory issued for anywhere in Mexico, from several governments, and has done for more than a decade. The warnings concern organised crime, roadside kidnapping and carjacking, and they apply to the highways as much as the cities. If you are driving to or from the border, this is the state where route and timing matter most.
