Tapachula is the big, hot, restless city of Mexico's far southern Pacific coast, close to Guatemala but not quite on the border. It is the capital of the Soconusco, a humid strip of cacao, banana, mango, and coffee country squeezed between the ocean and the Sierra Madre de Chiapas.

The city is not pretty in the way [San Cristóbal de las Casas](/northamerica/mexico/sancristobaldelascasas) is pretty. Its interest is more practical and regional: Guatemalan buses, Pacific trade, migrants moving north, old coffee wealth, Chinese and German family names in the cemetery, and a centre that still gathers around Parque Miguel Hidalgo. Tapachula feels Central American as much as Mexican.

The reason to stay a day is [Izapa](/northamerica/mexico/tapachula/izapa), one of the oldest major archaeological sites in southern Mexico, where carved stelae and grassy mounds sit under the slopes of Volcán Tacaná. The [Museo Arqueológico del Soconusco](/northamerica/mexico/tapachula/museo_arqueologico_del_soconusco) in town gives those finds context, while the surrounding coffee road climbs into cooler fincas above the city.

Tapachula is still a border and transport city, so it rewards realistic expectations. Come for Izapa, Soconusco coffee, and the feeling of being at the hinge between Mexico and Central America, not for a polished colonial weekend.
