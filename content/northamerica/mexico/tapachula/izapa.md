Izapa is Tapachula's strongest reason to detour. The site began long before the Classic Maya cities and reached its height in the first millennium BC, leaving carved stelae, altars, plazas, and low mounds in the hot green countryside northeast of town.

It is not a towering ruin like Palenque. The appeal is older and quieter: stone monuments, volcanic soil, cacao country, and a culture often described as a bridge between Olmec and Maya worlds. Go early, bring water, and pair it with the museum in Tapachula.
