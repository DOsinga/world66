Tecomán is a working agricultural city near the Colima coast, best known in Mexico as a lime capital rather than as a polished beach town. That is part of the point: the city is practical, hot, and sometimes rough-edged, while the interesting travel happens along the beaches, lagoons, and seafood palapas south and west of town.

The strongest reason to come is [Boca de Pascuales](/northamerica/mexico/tecoman/boca_de_pascuales), a serious surf beach with heavy Pacific waves and a simple village feel. [Playa El Real](/northamerica/mexico/tecoman/playa_el_real) and [Playa Tecuanillo](/northamerica/mexico/tecoman/playa_tecuanillo) give you other versions of the same coast, with dark sand, open water, birds, and enramadas serving fish and shrimp.

Tecomán is not a destination for aimless strolling after dark; pay attention to local safety advice and use taxis when needed. But if you are already in Colima or Manzanillo and want a day of surf, coconuts, lagoons, and a less packaged Pacific coast, it has a clear identity.
