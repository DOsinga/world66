Caesar's Restaurant Bar occupies a restored space on Avenida Revolución that has been serving the salad that Caesar Cardini accidentally invented here in 1924. The restaurant closed and reopened multiple times over the decades, but the current incarnation takes the history seriously — the Caesar salad is still prepared tableside by waiters who mix the dressing in a large bowl and build the salad in front of you, following the original technique.

The salad itself is the reason to come. It's made with the traditional recipe: whole romaine leaves dressed with emulsified egg, lemon juice, Worcestershire sauce, Parmesan, and house-made croutons. The experience is theatrical and the result is better than most versions you've tasted. The rest of the menu is solid Baja-influenced Mexican, but secondary.

The restaurant is busiest at lunch and early dinner. Reservations are accepted but walk-ins are usually fine outside peak weekend hours.
