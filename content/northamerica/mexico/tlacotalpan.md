Tlacotalpan sits low on the Papaloapan River, a small Veracruz town painted in bright Caribbean colors and wrapped around arcaded streets, palms, and river light. It is one of Mexico's more graceful colonial towns, but it feels less polished than the famous highland showpieces. The heat, the river, and the music keep it firmly on the Gulf coast.

The historic center is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, protected for its unusual river-port layout and its long rows of one- and two-story houses with colonnades, red-tile roofs, inner courtyards, and strong color. Most visitors come for the architecture, then stay longer than planned because the town is easy to wander by foot or bicycle.

Tlacotalpan is also tied to son jarocho, the Afro-Caribbean and Spanish-rooted music of Veracruz, and to the festival of the Virgin of Candelaria. Around late January and 2 February, the town fills with processions, fandango, horsemen, and church rituals. Outside festival time it is quieter, best treated as a slow overnight or a long day from [Veracruz](/northamerica/mexico/veracruz).
