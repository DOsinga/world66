Tlaxcala is the smallest state in Mexico and the one with the most awkward place in the national story: the Tlaxcalteca had held out against the Aztecs for a century, and in 1519 they allied with Cortés. Without them the conquest would have gone differently, and Mexican history has never quite forgiven them.

[Tlaxcala de Xicohténcatl](/northamerica/mexico/tlaxcala/tlaxcala_de_xicohtencatl) is among the smallest state capitals, with an ochre-and-red main square, a sixteenth-century Franciscan monastery whose wooden ceiling is Moorish, and a vast mural in the government palace telling the state's version of events.

**Cacaxtla**, nearby, has the best-preserved pre-Hispanic murals in Mexico — battle scenes in blue and red pigment, painted around 700 AD and only discovered in 1975.

[Tlaxco](/northamerica/mexico/tlaxcala/tlaxco) is haciendas and fighting-bull ranches; the state breeds much of Mexico's bull stock, and its *charreada* tradition runs deep. [Calpulalpan](/northamerica/mexico/tlaxcala/calpulalpan) sits on the old road to Mexico City.

The state's carnival, with its *huehues* in carved wooden masks of pink-faced Spaniards, is among the most distinctive in the country.
