Stephens, an American diplomat, travelled the Yucatán Peninsula in 1841–42 with the English artist Frederick Catherwood, visiting forty-four Maya sites including Tulum. His two-volume account, illustrated with Catherwood's astonishing drawings, was the first serious description of the ruins for a non-Maya audience and effectively founded modern Mayan archaeology. The Tulum chapter — the city is reached by canoe in a state of complete jungle abandonment — is one of the most evocative pieces of travel writing of the century.
