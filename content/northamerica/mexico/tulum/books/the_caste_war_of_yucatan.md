The standard history of the long Maya rebellion that began in 1847 and continued, in pockets of the southern Yucatán, until 1933. Tulum was at the heart of the rebel territory: the cult of the Talking Cross at Chan Santa Cruz drew its legitimacy in part from the ancient temples on the coast. Reed's account is exhaustive but readable, and it explains why the Yucatán still feels culturally distinct from the rest of Mexico.
