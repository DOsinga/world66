Valle de Bravo is a lakeside mountain town west of [Mexico City](/northamerica/mexico/mexicocity), where whitewashed streets and red-tile roofs drop toward Lake Avandaro. It is popular with weekend visitors from the capital, but the setting is strong enough to survive the crowds: water, pine-covered hills, waterfalls, and high launch points for paragliders.

The town has two personalities. Around the center, you get churches, craft shops, steep lanes, and the waterfront promenade. Across the lake and into Avandaro, the mood turns more outdoorsy and moneyed, with marinas, forest trails, restaurants, and adventure operators. On a clear afternoon, paragliders circling down over the lake are part of the skyline.

Valle is also one of the better bases for seeing the monarch butterfly migration in season. Piedra Herrada lies east of town, and in winter the hike up through the fir forest can end with whole branches trembling orange and black. Come midweek if you can; on holiday weekends, the charm is still here, but you share it with half of Mexico City.
