Catemaco sits on a volcanic crater lake in **Los Tuxtlas**, the northernmost tropical rainforest in the Americas and a biosphere reserve. The combination — rainforest at the latitude of the Bahamas — is why the biology here is strange and why the forest is worth more than the lake.

**Boats** leave from the malecón for the islands: Isla de los Monos, where a colony of Thai macaques brought for research in the 1970s now lives free; Isla de las Garzas with its herons; and the shore springs. A basic circuit takes about an hour.

The **reserves** along the north shore run guided forest walks with mineral springs, mud baths and temazcal. Inland, the waterfalls at **Salto de Eyipantla** are a short drive, and the biological station at Los Tuxtlas can be visited by arrangement.

Catemaco is also Mexico's **brujería** town, and the first Friday in March brings a national gathering of curanderos. Some of it is serious and traditional, a good deal of it is sold to visitors, and the difference is not obvious from outside. Treat it with the same scepticism you would bring anywhere.
