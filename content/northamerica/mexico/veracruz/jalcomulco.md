A village of a few thousand people in a hot green valley an hour from [Xalapa](/northamerica/mexico/veracruz/xalapa), on a river that comes down off the Cofre de Perote and runs to the Gulf. It is the centre of whitewater rafting in Mexico, and that is essentially all it is.

The river is the **Río Antigua**, called the **Río Pescados** along the stretch beside the village. It is runnable all year — unusual in Mexico, where most rivers are seasonal — because the Veracruz slope catches rain off the Gulf for most of the year. The standard commercial run is **class III** with a few class IV drops in the wet season, takes two to three hours, and is sold to people who have never been in a raft.

Outfitters line the road in and keep camps along the bank. Most sell the river as part of a weekend with rappelling, ziplines, a temazcal and food, which is how Mexican visitors from Veracruz city and Mexico City usually take it. Weekends are busy and midweek is not.

A suspension footbridge crosses the river at the village and is the best place to look at it before committing. Beyond the rafting there is not much here: a plaza, a church, some good river fish, and heat.
