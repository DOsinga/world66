**Rafting** is the reason Jalcomulco exists on a traveller's map. The commercial run on the Río Pescados section of the Antigua is class III with a few harder drops when the river is up, runs year-round, and is sold to complete beginners with a guide in every boat.

Water levels change everything. In the **dry months, roughly January to May**, the river is lower, friendlier and more technical. In the **wet season, June to October**, it is faster, pushier and occasionally closed outright after a storm — a responsible operator will cancel, and one that will not is telling you something about itself. Check that the company has a national tourism registry number and that guides carry rescue certification; both are normal here and both are worth asking about.

The same outfitters sell **rappelling** down the canyon walls, ziplines across the river, canyoning in the side streams, mountain biking, and **temazcal** steam ceremonies. Camps along the bank do the whole thing as a weekend package with meals.

The **Barranca Grande** and the Pescados canyon upstream are the harder water, for people who already paddle. Downstream, the Antigua runs out through coffee country to the Gulf at La Antigua, where Cortés's men built the first Spanish settlement on the mainland.
