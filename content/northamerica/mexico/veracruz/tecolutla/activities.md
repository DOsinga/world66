Tecolutla is a quiet Gulf beach town at a river mouth, and the thing that makes it worth the detour is the **turtles**.

The **Campamento Tortuguero Vida Milenaria**, on the beach at the edge of town, has been protecting Kemp's ridley and green turtle nests here since 1974 — the camp is the work of Fernando Manzano, known locally as Papá Tortuga, and it releases tens of thousands of hatchlings a year. Nesting runs roughly April to August and releases happen from around June into the autumn, at dusk, on the beach. The camp has no working website; you turn up or ask in town.

Boats go upriver into the **mangroves** from the embarcadero — the usual circuit takes about an hour and covers the estuary and the so-called pirate cave — and out to the sandbar at the river mouth.

The beach itself is Gulf beach: brown sand, warm flat water, almost entirely Mexican holidaymakers, and busy at Easter and in July and August. [El Tajín](/northamerica/mexico/veracruz/el_tajin), the Totonac city with the Pyramid of the Niches, is an hour inland and is the other reason to be here.
