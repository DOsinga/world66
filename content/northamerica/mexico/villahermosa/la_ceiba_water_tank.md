  The ceiba tree is sacred in Maya cosmology — it is the World Tree, the axis connecting the underworld, the earth, and the heavens. Decorating a water tank with ceiba imagery was a way of asserting Tabasco's indigenous cultural heritage at a time — the early 20th century — when Mexican muralism and indigenismo were making pre-Columbian symbols part of the national identity. The water tank became, inadvertently, a work of public art.

The La Ceiba water tank is a piece of public infrastructure that acquired an unexpected cultural dimension. The tank is decorated with imagery of the ceiba tree — the sacred World Tree of Maya cosmology — in a style that blends Art Nouveau decorative sensibility with pre-Columbian symbolism. It's an oddity in the best sense: a mundane water tower turned into a cultural statement.

Atlas Obscura notes this as a quirky landmark, and it does have a certain appeal as an example of Mexico's 20th-century project of incorporating indigenous imagery into public life. It's a minor sight, but worth a look if you're already in the area.
