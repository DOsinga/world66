The state of Yucatán is the cultural heart of the peninsula and the half of it that was never built for tourists. There is no coastal strip of hotels here; there is limestone, henequén country, and more Maya spoken than anywhere else in Mexico.

[Mérida](/northamerica/mexico/yucatan/merida) is the capital and one of the most liveable cities in the country — a grid of colonial houses, Sunday streets closed to cars, and a tradition of free music in the plazas every night of the week.

The **ruins** are the draw. [Chichén Itzá](/northamerica/mexico/yucatan/chichen_itza) is the famous one and the most crowded; [Uxmal](/northamerica/mexico/yucatan/uxmal) in the Puuc hills is the better building, and the Puuc route beyond it runs to sites where you may be alone. [Izamal](/northamerica/mexico/yucatan/izamal), painted entirely yellow, has a monastery built on a flattened pyramid.

**Cenotes** are everywhere — thousands of them, from tourist-managed swimming holes to collapsed openings down a track — and the ring of them across the north of the state traces the rim of the Chicxulub crater, where the asteroid landed.

On the coast, [Progreso](/northamerica/mexico/yucatan/progreso) is where Mérida swims, and [Río Lagartos](/northamerica/mexico/yucatan/rio_lagartos) and [El Cuyo](/northamerica/mexico/yucatan/puerto_el_cuyo) sit on the Ría Lagartos reserve, where the flamingos breed.

[Valladolid](/northamerica/mexico/yucatan/valladolid) in the middle makes a far better base for Chichén Itzá than the coast does.
