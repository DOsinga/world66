The Malecón is the city's 7-kilometre waterfront promenade running along the Gulf of Mexico. Modern, wide, and well-maintained, it was developed in the early 2000s and transformed what had been an industrial shoreline into a pedestrian esplanade with benches, bicycle paths, food stalls, and unobstructed views of the Gulf at sunset.

Campeche's sunsets over the water are genuinely spectacular — the Gulf is flat to the west and the sky frequently turns vivid shades of orange and pink. The malecón fills with strolling families, joggers, and cyclists in the evening. Vendors sell regional snacks and cold drinks. It's one of those simple pleasures — sitting facing the water as the light fades — that travel guides struggle to justify but which makes a place feel like itself.
