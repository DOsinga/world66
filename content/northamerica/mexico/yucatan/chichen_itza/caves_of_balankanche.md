The Caves of Balankanche are a Maya ritual site a short drive from Chichen Itza, where underground chambers were used for offerings long after the great city above ground had flourished. The cave passages open into rooms with stalactites, altars, and ceramic remains, giving a very different sense of Maya sacred geography from the pyramids and plazas nearby.

A guided visit is the usual way in, both for safety and for context. The atmosphere is humid and enclosed, and the lighting can feel theatrical, but the archaeological importance is real. Balankanche is best treated as a complement to Chichen Itza: less visually grand, but more intimate and eerie.
