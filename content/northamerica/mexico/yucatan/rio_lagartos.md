Rio Lagartos is a small fishing village on the north coast of the Yucatan Peninsula, best known for the flamingo colony in the surrounding biosphere reserve. Thousands of Caribbean flamingos breed in the shallow lagoons and salt flats of the [Ría Lagartos Biosphere Reserve](/northamerica/mexico/yucatan/rio_lagartos/ria_lagartos_biosphere_reserve) — [boat tours](/northamerica/mexico/yucatan/rio_lagartos/flamingo_boat_tour) to see them are the main reason visitors come. The reserve also supports over 300 bird species and extensive mangrove habitat.

The village itself is quiet and unpretentious — a handful of streets, simple seafood restaurants, and a waterfront where fishing boats come and go. It makes a good base for boat tours into the lagoon system, including crocodile-spotting trips and visits to [Las Coloradas](/northamerica/mexico/yucatan/rio_lagartos/las_coloradas) — the extraordinary pink salt pans about 20 kilometres east. The [sport fishing](/northamerica/mexico/yucatan/rio_lagartos/sport_fishing) is excellent — tarpon, snook, and jacks in the protected lagoon waters.

Rio Lagartos is about three hours from Mérida via Tizimín. Chichén Itzá and other Yucatán ruins are within a couple of hours' drive.
