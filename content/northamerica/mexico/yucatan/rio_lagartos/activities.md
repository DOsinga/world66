Río Lagartos is a working fishing village on the north coast of Yucatán, at the edge of a **biosphere reserve** that covers sixty thousand hectares of shallow lagoon, mangrove and low dry forest.

**Flamingos** are the reason people come. They are present all year and concentrate between roughly **April and September**, when thousands nest along the ría. Boats go out from the village waterfront in the early morning, when the light is best and the birds are feeding; midday trips find them further off and more easily disturbed. Approach distances are set by the reserve.

The same trip usually covers **crocodiles** in the mangrove channels, the **baño maya** — a mud bath from the lagoon bed that visitors are invited to apply to themselves — and the salt ponds at **Las Coloradas**, where the water runs pink. Entering the pink water is prohibited and fined; it is an industrial salt works, not a lagoon.

Birders should know this is one of the best sites in Mexico: more than three hundred species recorded, and the full Gulf shorebird passage in winter. Bring binoculars and ask for a guide who birds rather than one who drives.

**San Felipe**, ten minutes west, and **El Cuyo**, an hour east, are the quieter villages on the same coast.
