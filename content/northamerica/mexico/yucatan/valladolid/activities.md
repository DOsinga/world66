title: Activities
type: section
---

Valladolid is a colonial town on top of a Maya one, and its own attractions are modest — a convent, a long main street, the **Cenote Zací** in the middle of town. What makes it worth basing yourself here is what is within an hour.

**Chichén Itzá** is forty minutes west. Staying in Valladolid rather than on the coast means arriving at opening time, before the Cancún coaches and before the heat; by eleven the site is a different and worse experience.

**Ek Balam**, half an hour north, is the better ruin for some tastes: smaller, quieter, with a stucco frieze of winged figures preserved under a collapsed wall, and a main pyramid you are still allowed to climb.

The **cenotes** are everywhere. Dzitnup and Samulá, both a few kilometres out, are caverns with a shaft of light falling into clear water; Suytun has the famous stone platform; Oxman and Hubikú are set in old haciendas. The crowded ones are crowded by mid-morning and the countryside has dozens that are not.

Further out: **Río Lagartos** for the flamingos, **Cobá** in the jungle to the southeast, and **Izamal**, the yellow town, to the west.
