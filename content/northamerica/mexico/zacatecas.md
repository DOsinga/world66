Zacatecas is high, dry and built out of pink cantera stone, and for two centuries it produced a fifth of the world's silver.

[Zacatecas city](/northamerica/mexico/zacatecas/zacatecas_city) sits in a ravine at 2,400 metres beneath the **Cerro de la Bufa**, with a cathedral whose façade is among the most elaborately carved in the Americas. A cable car crosses the city from the hill to the mine; the **Mina El Edén** can be walked through, and part of it is a nightclub, several levels underground. The Rafael Coronel museum holds thousands of masks in a ruined convent.

[Jerez](/northamerica/mexico/zacatecas/jerez) is a cowboy town of arcaded streets and the birthplace of the poet Ramón López Velarde. [Sombrerete](/northamerica/mexico/zacatecas/sombrerete) is another silver town, with the Sierra de Órganos behind it — rock columns that stood in for the American West in a long run of Mexican films.

[Fresnillo](/northamerica/mexico/zacatecas/fresnillo) still works the largest silver mine in the world. [Juchipila](/northamerica/mexico/zacatecas/juchipila) lies in a canyon in the south, where the Caxcan resisted the Spanish in the Mixtón war.

**La Quemada**, a fortified hill city on the road south, was a frontier outpost between Mesoamerica and the nomadic north.
