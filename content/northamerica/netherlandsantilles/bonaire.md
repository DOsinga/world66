Bonaire is a small, flat island in the southern Caribbean, part of the ABC islands alongside Aruba and Curaçao. It sits just 80km north of Venezuela and well outside the hurricane belt, which keeps its waters calm and its skies reliably clear. The island became a special municipality of the Netherlands in 2010 — technically the closest thing to Dutch soil in the western hemisphere.

What draws most visitors is the reef. Bonaire's coral is among the healthiest in the Caribbean, and the entire coastline is a [marine park](/northamerica/netherlandsantilles/bonaire/bonaire_marine_park). You can wade in from the beach with a tank and be on the wall within minutes — no boat required for most sites. Divers have been making pilgrimages here since the 1970s, and the dive infrastructure shows it: every dive shop is efficient, the sites are well-marked, and locals treat the reef as something to be proud of. The classic wreck dive is the [Hilma Hooker](/northamerica/netherlandsantilles/bonaire/hilma_hooker), sitting in 30 metres of water off the southwest coast.

Away from the water, Bonaire is quiet and unpretentious. [Kralendijk](/northamerica/netherlandsantilles/bonaire/kralendijk), the capital, is a small port town of pastel-painted buildings and open-air restaurants, with the 17th-century [Fort Orange](/northamerica/netherlandsantilles/bonaire/fort_orange) on the seafront. The north of the island is [Washington-Slagbaai National Park](/northamerica/netherlandsantilles/bonaire/washington_slagbaai), a wilderness of cactus scrub and flamingo breeding grounds, and the old village of [Rincon](/northamerica/netherlandsantilles/bonaire/rincon). In the south, the [Pekelmeer salt pans](/northamerica/netherlandsantilles/bonaire/salt_pans) still operate, and pink flamingos wade in the shallows alongside the solar evaporation pools. The shallow turquoise [Lac Bay](/northamerica/netherlandsantilles/bonaire/lac_bay) on the southeast coast is one of the best windsurfing destinations in the world.
