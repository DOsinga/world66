Bonaire is the quietest of the ABC islands, a dry strip of cactus country wrapped in protected Caribbean water. Diving shapes travel here, but not in the usual resort-island way: numbered yellow stones beside the coast mark shore entries, pickup trucks carry tanks in the back, and much of the best reef lies only a short swim from the road.

The [Bonaire National Marine Park](/northamerica/netherlandsantilles/bonaire/bonaire_marine_park) protects the sea around the main island and [Klein Bonaire](/northamerica/netherlandsantilles/bonaire/klein_bonaire). First-time visitors should start with an easy snorkel or dive, then work up to the reef wall at [1000 Steps](/northamerica/netherlandsantilles/bonaire/1000_steps), the fish-filled pillars of [Salt Pier](/northamerica/netherlandsantilles/bonaire/salt_pier), or the [Hilma Hooker wreck](/northamerica/netherlandsantilles/bonaire/hilma_hooker). The freedom is real, but so is the responsibility: pay the nature fee, use the marked entries, and do not stand on coral.

[Kralendijk](/northamerica/netherlandsantilles/bonaire/kralendijk), usually called Playa, is the practical base and the only place that feels even slightly urban. Its low waterfront is pleasant once the cruise crowds leave. [Rincon](/northamerica/netherlandsantilles/bonaire/rincon), tucked into a northern valley, carries more of the island's cultural life through its food, festivals, and places such as [Mangazina di Rei](/northamerica/netherlandsantilles/bonaire/mangazina_di_rei).

The two road loops are as important as the towns. Northbound, the coast passes 1000 Steps and [Gotomeer](/northamerica/netherlandsantilles/bonaire/gotomeer) before reaching [Washington-Slagbaai National Park](/northamerica/netherlandsantilles/bonaire/washington_slagbaai), a long, rough drive through cactus scrub, beaches, salt lakes, and the island's highest ground. Southbound, the road runs between open sea and working [salt pans](/northamerica/netherlandsantilles/bonaire/salt_pans), past the [slave huts and obelisks](/northamerica/netherlandsantilles/bonaire/slave_huts_and_obelisks) that make the human cost of that landscape impossible to ignore.

On the east coast, shallow [Lac Bay](/northamerica/netherlandsantilles/bonaire/lac_bay) catches the trade wind and supports mangroves as well as windsurfing. The island is expensive, public transport is close to nonexistent, and a car is hard to avoid. It is also easy to understand: allow the reef to set the pace, give the north and south a day each, and do not treat everything above water as an interval between dives.
