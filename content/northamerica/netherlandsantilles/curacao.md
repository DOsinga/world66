Curaçao gives you calm Caribbean water without asking you to spend the whole trip inside a resort. It is the largest of the ABC islands, lying just off Venezuela, and has been a constituent country within the Kingdom of the Netherlands since 2010. The land is dry, windy, and covered in cactus rather than coconut palms.

The capital, [Willemstad](/northamerica/netherlandsantilles/curacao/willemstad), is the island's cultural and visual centre. Its old quarters sit on both sides of the Sint Annabaai: pastel Dutch gables line the [Handelskade](/northamerica/netherlandsantilles/curacao/willemstad/handelskade), the [Queen Emma Bridge](/northamerica/netherlandsantilles/curacao/willemstad/queen_emma_bridge) swings aside for ships, and Otrobanda's steep lanes carry the history of a port shaped by trade and slavery. This is a working Caribbean capital, not a heritage set built for cruise passengers.

West of the city, [Bandabou](/northamerica/netherlandsantilles/curacao/bandabou) narrows into cactus hills and old plantation land. Small beaches such as Grote Knip and Playa Lagun sit in limestone coves along the sheltered coast, often with a reef close enough to reach from shore. Inland, Christoffelberg rises above the scrub; on the north coast, waves hit the cliffs at Shete Boka with enough force to make swimming unthinkable.

[Bandariba](/northamerica/netherlandsantilles/curacao/bandariba), east of Willemstad, is less dramatic but rewards a slower circuit around Spanish Water and Caracas Bay. Fort Beekenburg, the shallow Tugboat wreck, herb gardens, and dry open countryside show a side of Curaçao that disappears if you stay between the capital and the beach clubs.

Farther offshore, [Klein Curaçao](/northamerica/netherlandsantilles/curacao/klein_curacao) is an uninhabited scrap of sand, rock, and reef reached by boat from the main island. The crossing can be rough, but the long white beach, clear water, abandoned lighthouse, and shipwrecks make it a rewarding full-day excursion.

Papiamentu is the language of everyday life, alongside Dutch, English, and Spanish. The same mix runs through the food: goat stew, cactus soup, fried fish, Dutch cheese, and Indonesian and Surinamese dishes all belong at the table. Curaçao is busier than Bonaire and less polished than Aruba. That slight roughness is part of its appeal, but so is its independence: rent a car, leave Willemstad, and the island opens up quickly.
