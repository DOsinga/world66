title: Food

---

Curaçaoan food is built from movement: West African techniques, Dutch cheese, Iberian and Venezuelan ingredients, and later Indonesian and Surinamese arrivals. The result is hearty rather than delicate, with stews, fried fish, cornmeal, plantain, and plenty of chilli relish.

Start with *kabritu stobá*, goat slowly cooked with tomato and spices, or *karni stobá*, the beef version. *Keshi yená* turns a shell of Edam or Gouda into a baked casing for seasoned meat, olives, capers, and raisins. *Sòpi kadushi* is a green cactus soup with a slippery texture that divides visitors; try it before deciding. *Funchi* is the everyday cornmeal side, while *tutu* mixes cornmeal with black-eyed peas.

For a quick breakfast or snack, look for a *pastechi*, a crescent of fried dough filled with cheese, meat, or fish. Along the west coast, simple places serve the day's catch with funchi, plantain, and *pika*, the local hot relish. In [Willemstad](/northamerica/netherlandsantilles/curacao/willemstad), [Marshe Bieu](/northamerica/netherlandsantilles/curacao/willemstad/marshe_bieu) is the most useful introduction because several cooks serve traditional dishes under one roof.
