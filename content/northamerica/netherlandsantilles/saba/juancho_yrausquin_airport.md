Saba's airport is one of the great curiosities of Caribbean aviation. The runway is just 400 metres long — barely enough for a small turboprop, and well within the perimeter fence of most international airports — and it sits on a headland at Flat Point, with the sea at one end and a cliff face at the other. The Winair Twin Otter that operates the route from Sint Maarten handles it with practiced ease, but first-time passengers tend to grip their armrests.

The approaches are what make Yrausquin memorable: arriving from Sint Maarten, the plane drops sharply between the cliffs before touching down and braking hard; departing, it accelerates toward the cliff, lifts, and banks steeply over the water. The whole sequence, arrival to gate, takes about three minutes. Aviation enthusiasts sometimes fly in specifically for the experience. If you are nervous about small planes, this is not the island for you — but if you find it thrilling, Saba delivers on arrival before you've even unpacked.
