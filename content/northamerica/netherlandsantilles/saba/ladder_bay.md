Ladder Bay is a steep anchorage on the island's leeward side, notable mainly for the 800 stone steps that once formed Saba's only connection between the sea and the inhabited interior. Before Josephus Lambert Hassell completed The Road in 1947, these steps were how everything arrived on the island — cargo, mail, livestock, machinery — all carried or hoisted by hand up the near-vertical cliff. The steps date to the 17th century and are still intact, offering a strenuous descent to a small pebble beach at the water's edge.

The hike down is genuinely steep and the steps are uneven; good footwear and a steady head for heights are useful. At the bottom, the swimming is decent in calm weather, though the bay is exposed to swells and there are no facilities. The real reason to come is the historical weight of the place — standing at the top of those 800 steps and imagining generations of Sabans moving an entire island's worth of commerce up and down them puts the island's self-reliant character in tangible context.
