---
title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

Bluefields food is Caribbean rather than Nicaraguan — the staples are rice and beans cooked together in coconut milk (rondón rather than gallo pinto), fried fish, green plantains, and seafood prepared simply. The Creole kitchen draws on the same tradition as coastal cooking from Honduras to Panama, with coconut a constant presence. Fish soup made with root vegetables and coconut milk — rondón in its full sense — is the dish most specific to the Caribbean coast and worth seeking out.

There are no fine dining establishments and not many tourist restaurants. Eating well in Bluefields means finding the places where locals eat: small comedores (casual lunch spots) around the market serving large plates of fish, rice, and plantain for very little money, and houses with hand-painted signs advertising seafood. The waterfront area has a few more formal options that cater to passing visitors. Lobster and shrimp from the Caribbean are excellent and inexpensive by international standards — eat them here rather than in Managua, where they arrive overpriced and at a distance from the sea.
