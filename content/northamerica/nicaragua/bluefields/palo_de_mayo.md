Palo de Mayo is the defining cultural event of Bluefields, an Afro-Caribbean festival held through the entire month of May that fills the streets with music, dancing, and the kind of collective public celebration that the Pacific side of Nicaragua simply doesn't do. The festival has deep roots in the spiritual and agricultural traditions of the West African diaspora that came to the Caribbean coast via slavery and subsequent Afro-Caribbean migration, filtered through two centuries of Caribbean Creole culture. The maypole dancing at its centre is the visible core, but the whole month is one long performance.

The peak of the festival — with the biggest public dances, the best music, and the most visible street celebrations — falls in the last week of May. Every neighbourhood puts on its own events; community groups spend months preparing costumes and choreography. The music is a mix of Creole styles — May Pole rhythms, punta, soca, reggae — played live or from speakers at volume. Visitors during May are welcomed as participants rather than spectators; this is not a festival staged for tourism. The rest of the year, the bar and music scene in Bluefields gives a quieter sense of what the Caribbean coast sounds like.
