---
title: "Things to Do"
type: section
---

Bluefields is the capital of Nicaragua's South Caribbean Coast Autonomous Region, and it operates by a different logic from the Pacific side of the country. The language on the street is Creole English as much as Spanish; the music is reggae and soca rather than marimba; and the town's physical situation — surrounded by a vast lagoon, accessible only by plane or boat — gives it an isolation that sharpens its Caribbean character. The journey to get here is part of the experience: most visitors fly from Managua or take the bus to Rama and continue by fast panga boat down the Escondido River and across the lagoon.

The main things to do in Bluefields are relational rather than monument-based: walking the waterfront, eating well, listening to music, and taking boat trips to the surrounding lagoon and its islands. The May festival Palo de Mayo (also called Maya May) is the single biggest draw — an Afro-Caribbean celebration rooted in fertility rites brought by enslaved Africans, involving weeks of dancing, music, and public revelry that transforms the town. Outside festival season, the town is quieter but the lagoon excursions, the Rama Cay community, and Pearl Lagoon to the north make for a genuine Caribbean experience.
