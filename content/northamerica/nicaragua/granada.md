Granada sits on the northwestern shore of Lago de Nicaragua, Central America's largest lake, with the [Volcán Mombacho](/northamerica/nicaragua/granada/mombacho) rising behind it. It is one of the oldest Spanish colonial cities in the Americas and one of the best-preserved — the [cathedral](/northamerica/nicaragua/granada/cathedral), the main square, and the colourful facades along [Calle La Calzada](/northamerica/nicaragua/granada/la_calzada) give the centre a coherent character that many similarly promoted colonial cities have lost.

The [Parque Central](/northamerica/nicaragua/granada/parque_central) anchors the city. Street vendors sell vigorón — yuca topped with pork rinds and curtido — wrapped in banana leaves, and horse-drawn carriages still carry passengers through the streets. The lake is close enough to feel present; boat trips to the [isletas](/northamerica/nicaragua/granada/isletas) just offshore make for a good half-day out.

The [Museo de Granada](/northamerica/nicaragua/granada/museo_granada), set in a former Franciscan convent, holds pre-Columbian stone statues from Isla Zapatera — among the finest indigenous sculpture in Central America. The [abandoned Hospital San Juan de Dios](/northamerica/nicaragua/granada/hospital_san_juan) and [Iglesia de La Merced](/northamerica/nicaragua/granada/iglesia_la_merced), whose tower offers one of the best views in the city, are also worth seeking out.

Granada is small enough to walk in an afternoon and lively enough to reward a couple of nights.
