Ometepe is an island in Lake Nicaragua, formed by two volcanoes — [Concepción](/northamerica/nicaragua/isla_ometepe/concepcion_volcano) and [Maderas](/northamerica/nicaragua/isla_ometepe/maderas_volcano) — joined by a low isthmus to create an hourglass-shaped landmass of 276 square kilometres. The name comes from the Nahuatl words for "two hills." It is one of the most striking islands in Central America: perfectly symmetrical Concepción rising to 1,610 m at the north end, the older, forested Maderas at 1,394 m in the south.

Concepción is still active and has erupted numerous times since 1880; climbing it requires a guide and a good level of fitness. Maderas is quieter, its crater holding a lagoon, and the ascent through cloud forest is a full-day affair. Both volcanoes can be climbed from the town of [Altagracia](/northamerica/nicaragua/isla_ometepe/altagracia) (Concepción) or Balgüe (Maderas).

Beyond the volcanoes, the island offers [kayaking on the Río Istián](/northamerica/nicaragua/isla_ometepe/kayaking_istian), swimming in [Ojo de Agua](/northamerica/nicaragua/isla_ometepe/ojo_de_agua) — a natural spring-fed pool — [petroglyphs](/northamerica/nicaragua/isla_ometepe/petroglyphs) scattered across the farmland, and a pace of life that makes the mainland feel very far away. The two main settlements are [Moyogalpa](/northamerica/nicaragua/isla_ometepe/moyogalpa), the ferry port on the western shore, and Altagracia, the larger town to the north-east. Ferries run from San Jorge on the mainland, taking about an hour.
