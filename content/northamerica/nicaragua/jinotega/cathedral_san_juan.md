  The Diocese of Jinotega was only established in 1982, making it one of the youngest Catholic dioceses in Central America. The cathedral was elevated to its current status at the same time, though the building predates the diocese by several decades. During the Contra War of the 1980s, Jinotega was a front-line city and the cathedral served as a sanctuary for civilians caught in the fighting.

The Cathedral of San Juan stands on the main park at the centre of Jinotega, the most prominent building in a town that is otherwise defined by single-storey commercial streets and the surrounding mountains. The white painted facade is straightforward colonial-style construction, not remarkable architecturally, but the setting — with the cloud forest peaks visible in every direction above the town rooflines — gives it a dramatic backdrop.

The interior is cool and quiet, with a painted ceiling and modest decorations. The cathedral is the seat of the Diocese of Jinotega and holds regular masses attended by townspeople from across the region. The main park in front is the social centre of town in the evenings, with food stalls, children, and the particular unhurried energy of a highland provincial city.
