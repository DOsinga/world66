The Casa Museo Rubén Darío is the house where Nicaragua's national poet spent his adolescence, now preserved as a museum. Darío is revered in Nicaragua with an intensity that surprises many visitors — his face appears on banknotes, streets and schools bear his name, and his tomb in the cathedral is a genuine place of pilgrimage.

The museum is small but well-curated: personal objects, photographs, manuscripts, and first editions fill the rooms where the young Darío wrote his early poems. For anyone interested in Latin American literature, this is a pilgrimage stop. For everyone else, it is at least an introduction to a writer whose influence stretched far beyond Nicaragua — he reshaped how the Spanish language was used in poetry, and writers from García Lorca to Borges acknowledged the debt.
