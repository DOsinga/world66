Comedores are the backbone of eating in Managua — small, informal eateries, often a single room with a few plastic tables and a handwritten menu on a chalk board, serving the standard Nicaraguan working lunch: gallo pinto (rice and beans fried together with onion and sweet pepper), grilled chicken or pork, salad, plantains, and a cup of fresh fruit juice. A full plate costs $2–4.

They are found throughout the city, but the densest concentrations are around the markets — particularly the Mercado Huembes and Mercado Oriental — where the clientele are market workers and bus drivers eating quickly between shifts. The food is filling and the turnover is high, which means the grill is always busy and the dishes are fresh. For travellers on a tight budget, a midday comedor lunch is the most economical way to eat well in Managua.
