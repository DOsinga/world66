The nacatamal is Nicaragua's definitive festive food: a large corn-dough dumpling filled with pork, rice, potatoes, tomatoes, olives, and a mint leaf, wrapped in banana leaves and steamed for several hours. It is heavy, complex, and deeply satisfying — a meal that takes most of a day to prepare properly. In Managua, Sunday morning is nacatamal time.

On Sunday mornings throughout the city, vendors sell nacatamales from large pots outside their homes or at market stalls, and Managuan families line up to buy them for the traditional late morning meal. They are eaten with white bread (pan de molde) and sweet black coffee. The nacatamal is both a home cooking tradition and a street food, and eating one on a Sunday morning in Managua is as close to experiencing local ritual as most visitors will get. Look for the hand-written signs or the steam rising from large covered pots — they appear regularly in residential neighbourhoods.
