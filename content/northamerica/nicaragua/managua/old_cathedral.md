The Catedral de Santiago — universally known as the Old Cathedral — is one of the most striking ruins in Central America. Completed in 1938, it dominated the old city centre until the 1972 earthquake left its thick concrete walls so severely cracked that it was judged irreparable. Rather than demolish it, Managua preserved the shell and opened it to visitors as a kind of outdoor monument to the disaster that reshaped the city.

Walking inside the roofless nave feels genuinely eerie: tropical vegetation pushes through the cracked floor, pigeons nest in the bell towers, and the original tile work and statues remain in place under the open sky. At night the ruin is dramatically floodlit, visible from across the lakefront plaza. It is paired on the same block with the National Palace of Culture, making this corner of the old centre the most evocative spot in Managua.
