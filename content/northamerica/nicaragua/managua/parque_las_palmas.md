Parque Las Palmas is one of the older surviving green spaces in Managua, a broad public park in the western part of the city with tall palm trees, football pitches, and a small outdoor theatre. On weekends it fills with families, vendors, and neighbourhood life in a way that gives a genuine sense of how Managuans use their city.

The park is nothing spectacular — it is a lived-in urban park rather than a designed attraction — but that is exactly what makes it worth a short visit. There are benches under the palms, food vendors selling quesillos and fritanga, and children playing football on any flat surface available. It is a good place to sit and watch the city going about its business, particularly on Sunday mornings when the pace is slower than the rest of the week.
