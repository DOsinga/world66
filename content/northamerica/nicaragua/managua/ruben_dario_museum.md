Nicaragua's greatest cultural export is Rubén Darío (1867–1916), the poet from Metapa (now renamed Ciudad Darío in his honour) who effectively invented the Modernismo movement in Spanish-language literature and influenced every major Spanish poet who followed him. His influence extended to García Lorca, Pablo Neruda, and Jorge Luis Borges, each of whom acknowledged debts to Darío's innovations in rhythm and imagery.

The small museum dedicated to Darío within the National Palace of Culture complex holds personal effects, manuscripts, first editions, and correspondence, as well as photographs and a biography in both Spanish and English. It is a compact but carefully assembled collection that gives genuine insight into how a provincial Nicaraguan became the most important figure in Spanish-language poetry. The museum is modest in scale but the subject matter rewards it; Darío is genuinely fascinating, and his life — wandering Europe, writing in poverty, celebrated and then forgotten and celebrated again — was as vivid as his verse.
