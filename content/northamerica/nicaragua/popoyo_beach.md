Popoyo is Nicaragua's best-known surf break, a powerful reef break on the southwestern Pacific coast that draws surfers from across Central America. The [Popoyo Reef](/northamerica/nicaragua/popoyo_beach/popoyo_reef) is consistent, with offshore winds most mornings during the dry season (November to April), and it works at all tides though low tide produces the heaviest barrels.

The area around Popoyo is still fairly undeveloped — a scattering of surf lodges, small restaurants, and rental houses spread along a dusty road behind the coast. That is part of the appeal: no high-rises, no crowds outside the peak surf season, and a pace of life that revolves around the tides. Beyond the main break there are several other spots within walking or short driving distance, including [Playa Sardinas](/northamerica/nicaragua/popoyo_beach/playa_sardinas) and the beach break at [Guasacate](/northamerica/nicaragua/popoyo_beach/guasacate_beach), which is better for beginners.

North of the surf zone, the [Chacocente Wildlife Reserve](/northamerica/nicaragua/popoyo_beach/chacocente_reserve) protects one of Nicaragua's most important sea turtle nesting beaches, where olive ridley turtles arrive by the thousands during the nesting season.

Popoyo is about two hours south of Managua on paved roads, or roughly an hour and a half from the colonial city of [Granada](/northamerica/nicaragua/granada). Most visitors base themselves here for the surfing, but the nearby town of [San Juan del Sur](/northamerica/nicaragua/sanjuandelsur) — about an hour south along the coast — offers more dining and nightlife options.
