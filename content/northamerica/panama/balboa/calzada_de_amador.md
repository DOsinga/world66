The Amador Causeway is a 6-kilometre breakwater built by the US military from excavation spoil from the Canal. It connects the mainland to three small islands — Naos, Culebra, and Flamenco — and runs along the Pacific entrance to the Canal, giving you views of ships waiting to transit or departing. Today the causeway is lined with restaurants, bars, a marina, and rental bike stands.

The Biomuseo, designed by Frank Gehry, sits on Isla Amador and houses a serious natural history museum about the formation of the Isthmus of Panama and its impact on global biodiversity — the meeting of North and South American species is one of the most consequential biological events of the past 3 million years. The building alone is worth the trip: a collision of multicoloured canopies visible from across the bay.

Cycling the causeway at sunset, with container ships silhouetted against the Pacific sky, is one of the more pleasurable hours available in Panama City.
