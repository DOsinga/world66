Cerro Ancón rises 199 metres above Panama City on the edge of Balboa, topped by a Panamanian flag visible from much of the city. The hill is covered in protected tropical forest and is home to a surprising amount of wildlife given its urban location — sloths, toucans, titi monkeys, and agoutis can all be seen by patient walkers on the well-maintained trail to the summit.

The walk from the base to the summit takes about 45 minutes; the path is paved and steep in places but manageable. From the top, you get a panorama across both the Pacific approaches to the Canal and the skyscrapers of Panama City's Punta Pacífica rising beyond. Early morning is the best time — cooler, less crowded, and the wildlife is active. The hill was a US military restricted zone until the Canal handover in 1999 and retains some of its orderly character from that era.
