The Río Chiriquí Viejo runs down the western flank of Volcán Barú and forms the border with Costa Rica for the lower part of its course. It is one of the better whitewater rivers in Central America — class III and IV rapids through a forested canyon, with good water levels from May through December.

Several outfitters in Boquete and Volcán run day trips on the lower section, with put-ins around Paso Ancho or Volcán. The upper section near Cerro Punta is steeper and reserved for experienced kayakers. The river is also under threat from a series of hydroelectric dam projects that have already eliminated some of the historic rapids; check current conditions before booking.
