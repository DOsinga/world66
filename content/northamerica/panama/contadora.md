Contadora is the most developed island in the Perlas Archipelago, a scattered group of islands in the Gulf of Panama about 70 km from Panama City. Its name comes from the old pearl trade — this was where [pearls from the archipelago were counted before export](/northamerica/panama/contadora/things_to_do/pearl_counting_station). Later the island became a retreat for the wealthy, and in 1979 it briefly served as refuge for the deposed Shah of Iran — an episode that triggered the Iran hostage crisis. In the early 1980s, diplomats from four Latin American nations met here to begin [the peace process](/northamerica/panama/contadora/things_to_do/contadora_peace_process) that would help end Central America's civil wars. Today it is a popular weekend escape from the capital and a growing draw for international visitors.

The island is small enough to walk across in twenty minutes. It has nine named beaches: [Playa Cacique](/northamerica/panama/contadora/things_to_do/playa_cacique) is the most accessible, [Playa Larga](/northamerica/panama/contadora/things_to_do/playa_larga) the most beautiful, and [Playa de las Suecas](/northamerica/panama/contadora/things_to_do/playa_de_las_suecas) the quietest. The clear warm water makes for excellent [snorkelling and diving](/northamerica/panama/contadora/things_to_do/snorkelling_and_diving) around the rocky headlands, with parrotfish, angelfish, stingrays, and nurse sharks as regular company. Between July and October, [humpback whales](/northamerica/panama/contadora/things_to_do/whale_watching) migrate through the surrounding channel on their way from Antarctic feeding grounds. Air Panama connects Contadora to Panama City in about 30 minutes with several flights daily, and a ferry service runs for those with more time.
