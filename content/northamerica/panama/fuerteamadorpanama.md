Fuerte Amador is a causeway at the Pacific entrance to the Panama Canal, connecting the mainland of Panama City to a string of small islands — Naos, Culebra, Perico, and Flamenco. It takes its name from Manuel Amador Guerrero, the first president of Panama, and was built from the rock excavated during the Canal's construction. Until 1999 it was a US military base; today it is one of Panama City's main leisure destinations.

The causeway is about three kilometres long, and the main activity is biking or walking its length while watching container ships queue to enter the Canal. Views of the Panama City skyline across the water are excellent, and at the far end on Flamenco Island the Fuerte Amador Resort and Marina has restaurants, bars, and a yacht basin. The Smithsonian Tropical Research Institute operates a marine exhibition centre on Naos with displays on Panama's extraordinary biodiversity.

Day trips to Isla Taboga, known as the Island of Flowers, depart from the Calypso Ferry Dock near the causeway entrance. This 30-minute ferry ride brings you to a quiet fishing village with colonial churches, hibiscus-lined paths, and decent beaches — a worthwhile half-day from the city. Fuerte Amador is a 15-minute taxi ride from the historic Casco Viejo district and makes a natural base for exploring both.
