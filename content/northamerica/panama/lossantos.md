Los Santos is a province on the Azuero Peninsula in southern Panama, the heartland of Panamanian rural culture. This is where the country's traditions run deepest — the pollera dress, the sombrero pintao, and the folk dances that define the national identity all originate here. If you visit during Carnival, the [celebrations in Las Tablas](/northamerica/panama/lossantos/carnival_las_tablas) are among the wildest and most genuine in Central America — the rivalry between the town's two halves makes this feel nothing like a tourist performance.

Outside festival time, Los Santos is a quiet, agricultural region of dry grasslands, cattle ranches, and small colonial towns. [La Villa de los Santos](/northamerica/panama/lossantos/la_villa_de_los_santos) holds significant historical importance as the site of Panama's first declaration of independence. The coastline along the Pacific has empty beaches and decent surf, particularly around [Pedasí](/northamerica/panama/lossantos/pedasi) and Playa Venao. [Isla Iguana](/northamerica/panama/lossantos/isla_iguana), a short boat ride from Pedasí, has pristine coral reefs and a large frigatebird colony. The region sees far fewer tourists than Panama City or Bocas del Toro, which is part of its appeal.
