A small but moving museum on Calle 24 Este, dedicated to the West Indian workers — overwhelmingly from Barbados, Jamaica, Trinidad, and Martinique — who provided the bulk of the labour for digging the Panama Canal. Roughly 20,000 of them died of yellow fever, malaria, and accidents during construction; tens of thousands more stayed and made Panama, particularly Colón and Panama City, their home.

The museum is housed in an old wooden Antillean-style mission church and covers the recruitment of West Indian workers in the 1900s, the segregated "Silver Roll" / "Gold Roll" wage system, the slum housing in El Chorrillo, the long fight for citizenship rights in independent Panama, and the eventual emergence of Afro-Panamanian culture. Labels in Spanish; a small bookshop has good background reading. One hour.
