A striking concrete dome on a hill 11 km northeast of Panama City, near the airport. The Bahá'í House of Worship — one of only nine in the world — is shaped like a giant white egg, with curved external buttresses and a series of small lozenge-shaped windows that admit slivers of light into the otherwise quiet, undecorated interior.

There are no clergy, no altar, no fixed services — Bahá'í temples are open to people of all faiths for silent prayer or meditation. The setting alone is worth the trip: the temple sits above a quiet residential neighbourhood with views of the canal entrance and the city beyond. Open every day; about 30 minutes by taxi from Casco Viejo. Best combined with a visit to **Panamá Viejo** since both are on the eastern side of the city.
