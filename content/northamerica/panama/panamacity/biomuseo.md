A Frank Gehry-designed museum at the Pacific entrance to the canal, dedicated to the natural history of Panama and the moment, three million years ago, when the rising of the isthmus joined the Americas and changed the climate of the entire planet. The crumpled, brightly coloured roof is visible from across the bay and from the windows of every flight landing at Tocumen.

Inside, eight permanent galleries cover everything from the geological formation of the isthmus to the Great American Biotic Interchange (the moment armadillos walked north and horses walked south) to the present-day biodiversity of Panama. The aquariums are excellent — Pacific and Caribbean tanks side by side, demonstrating how different the two oceans now are. The exhibits are bilingual (Spanish/English) and aimed at adults; allow two hours. Combine with a walk along the Amador Causeway.
