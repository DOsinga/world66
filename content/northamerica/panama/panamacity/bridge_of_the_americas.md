The 1,654-metre cantilever bridge that carries the **Pan-American Highway** over the Pacific entrance to the canal, immediately west of the city. The bridge — opened in 1962 — was for forty years the only road link between Panama City and the western half of the country (and, by extension, the rest of Central America); the parallel Centennial Bridge upstream now carries most of the highway traffic, but the Bridge of the Americas remains a defining piece of the Panama City skyline.

It is impressive from below — you can drive or walk along the Cinta Costera and the Amador Causeway and watch ships pass under it. The bridge itself has a narrow pedestrian walkway, but it's not pleasant: traffic is heavy, the road is high above the water, and the security around the canal is tight. Better photographed than walked.
