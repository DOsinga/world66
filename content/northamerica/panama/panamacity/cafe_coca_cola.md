The oldest still-operating café in Panama City, on the eastern side of Plaza Santa Ana between Casco Viejo and the modern centre. Café Coca-Cola is a low-ceilinged, formica-tabled, fluorescent-lit working-class diner that has been serving breakfast, sandwiches, and stronger drinks to the same neighbourhood for nearly 150 years.

The food is honest and very cheap — fried fish with rice and beans, beef sancocho, ham and cheese sandwiches — and the place is more interesting for its continuity than for any particular dish. Ernesto "Che" Guevara reportedly drank coffee here in the 1950s on his way through to Cuba. Go for breakfast.
