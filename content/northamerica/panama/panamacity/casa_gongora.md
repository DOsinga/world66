A 17th-century stone-and-tile colonial house on Avenida B in Casco Viejo, one of the oldest surviving buildings in the new city. Casa Góngora was built around 1760 — the heavy stone walls and barred lower windows are typical of early colonial Panama, designed for security and to survive the regular fires that plagued the city.

The building is now a cultural centre with free art exhibitions, occasional jazz concerts in the courtyard, and a small museum room about Casco Viejo's history. It is most interesting for the architecture itself — the original stone vaulting in the ground floor and the wooden roof structure upstairs. Free; closed Mondays. Twenty minutes is enough.
