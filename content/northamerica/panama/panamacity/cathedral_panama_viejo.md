The most visible surviving structure of the original 1519 Panama City. The cathedral tower is a square stone pile, four storeys high, standing alone in the middle of the **Panamá Viejo** ruins. It was built around 1626 — the only part of the cathedral that survived Henry Morgan's raid largely intact, because the explosion that destroyed the rest of the city didn't fully topple it.

A modern wooden staircase inside takes you to the top, where there is a small viewing platform. The view is the whole archaeological park spread out below — the foundations of churches, monasteries, the bishop's palace, the city plaza — with the modern Panama City skyline visible across the bay. Included with admission to the Panamá Viejo site.
