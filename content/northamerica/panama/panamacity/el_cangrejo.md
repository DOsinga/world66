The first of the modern high-rise neighbourhoods, developed in the 1950s and 60s as the city expanded north from the old centre. El Cangrejo ("the crab") is a tight grid of mid-rise apartment buildings, hotels, casinos, restaurants from a dozen different countries, and the bars and nightclubs of **Calle Uruguay**. It is also home to a large Jewish community and a substantial Chinatown along **Vía España**.

For travellers, El Cangrejo is the most practical place to stay outside Casco Viejo — central, cheap, well-connected by metro at Iglesia del Carmen and Vía Argentina stations. The food is unexpectedly varied: Lebanese on Vía Argentina, Italian along Calle 51, Chinese along Vía España, and a few of the city's best specialty-coffee bars. It is not pretty — the architecture is mostly grim 1970s concrete — but it is functional and lively.
