---
title: Getting around
type: section
---

Panama City's traffic is bad — seriously bad at rush hour — but the city is manageable once you understand the geography. The key areas for visitors are the **Casco Viejo** (far western tip of the bay), the modern downtown and **Marbella/El Cangrejo** hotel districts (central), **Miraflores Locks** (northwest, 10km), **Panamá Viejo ruins** (eastern edge, 6km), and the **Amador Causeway** (just southwest of the Casco). None of these are walking distance from each other.

The **Panama Metro** has two lines and is cheap, clean, and efficient for the corridors it covers. Line 1 runs east–west through central Panama City connecting Albrook (the main bus terminal) to the eastern residential areas, passing through **Cinco de Mayo** near the Casco and several central stations. It does not serve the Casco Viejo directly; the nearest station (5 de Mayo) is about 20 minutes' walk. Line 2 extends east toward the new city. The Metro is worth using for longer cross-city trips.

**Taxis** are ubiquitous, inexpensive, and the default for most visitors. As a rule, agree on the price before getting in, or use **Uber**, which removes all price negotiation and is widely available. Uber is generally a few dollars more than a taxi but operates predictably and tracks your ride.

The Casco Viejo itself is entirely walkable — most of the relevant streets are within a 10-minute walk of each other. The **Amador Causeway** is good for cycling; bicycle rental is available near the causeway entrance. **Traffic** is worst 7–9am and 5–7pm on weekdays; if you are visiting the Canal or the ruins, aim to arrive before 10am or after 3pm.
