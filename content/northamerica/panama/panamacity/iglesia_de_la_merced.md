A small, Baroque Mercedarian church on Avenida Central, one block west of Plaza de la Independencia. The stone facade — with its three arches, twin square towers, and pediment — is the oldest piece of architecture in the new city, dating to the 1620s in its previous location at Panamá Viejo. The rest of the building was reconstructed after the move.

The interior is plain but atmospheric: a wooden ceiling, a few colonial altarpieces, and the simple geometry of a working parish church. Mass is celebrated daily. Free to enter, and a good first stop for understanding the physical continuity between the old and new cities.
