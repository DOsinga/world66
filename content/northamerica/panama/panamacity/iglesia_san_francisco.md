The Franciscan church and adjoining convent on the east side of Plaza Bolívar, founded shortly after the new city itself. The current building dates from the late 18th century — earlier versions were destroyed by repeated fires — and the adjacent convent is where Simón Bolívar convened the **1826 Amphictyonic Congress**, the first formal attempt to unite the newly independent Spanish-American republics.

The interior of the church is plain by colonial standards — whitewashed walls, simple wooden altarpieces — but the building's importance is historical rather than visual. The convent next door is now Panama's **Ministry of Foreign Affairs**; the upstairs **Sala Bolivariana** preserves the original congress hall and is sometimes open to visitors.
