A small white church on Avenida A in Casco Viejo with what is probably the most famous single object in Panama City: the **Altar de Oro**, a Baroque altarpiece thickly covered in gold leaf that nearly fills the back of the nave. The altar's origin is the original Panamá Viejo cathedral and the survival story (above) is one of the city's favourite legends — probably embroidered but possibly true.

The church itself is humble: simple white walls, a barrel-vaulted wooden ceiling, a few minor altarpieces. Free to enter; modest dress required. Five minutes is enough if you only want to see the altar; the church is two blocks west of the cathedral.
