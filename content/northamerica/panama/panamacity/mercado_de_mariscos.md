The seafood market on the western edge of Casco Viejo, where the Pacific fishing fleet lands its catch each morning. Downstairs is a wet market with whole fish, prawns, octopus, oysters, and conch piled on ice — go in the early morning to see the auction. Upstairs is a roof full of plastic-table seafood restaurants and, at the entrance, a long row of stalls selling **ceviche** by the cup for two or three dollars.

The ceviche is the reason most travellers come: corvina, octopus, or mixed, marinated in lime, onion, and culantro, served in a styrofoam cup with crackers. Eat it standing on the pavement looking out over the bay. The restaurants upstairs are unpretentious but excellent — order whole fried snapper, patacones, and a cold beer. Avoid the lunch rush from cruise-ship passengers.
