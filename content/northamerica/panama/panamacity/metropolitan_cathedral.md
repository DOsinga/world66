The cathedral on Plaza de la Independencia is the visual anchor of Casco Viejo: a wide stone facade flanked by two square white bell towers, topped with mother-of-pearl inlay that glitters when the sun catches it. Construction began in 1688, only fifteen years after the founding of the new city, and was not completed until 1796 — over a century — which says something about the pace of colonial Panama.

Inside, the cathedral is austerely beautiful, with a high vaulted nave, marble floors, and a baroque altar. Pope Francis celebrated Mass here during World Youth Day in 2019, after which the cathedral was thoroughly restored. The square outside is the geographic heart of Casco Viejo and a good place to sit with a coffee and watch the neighbourhood come and go.
