The easiest way to see the canal in operation: a four-storey visitor centre with viewing platforms right above the two-step Miraflores locks, where ships are raised or lowered 16 metres between Miraflores Lake and the Pacific. The visitor centre has an excellent museum on four floors covering the geology, history, construction, and biology of the canal, an IMAX film, a restaurant with a balcony over the locks, and bilingual narration as each ship passes through.

Time the visit. Ships transit on a roughly fixed schedule — northbound (Pacific to Atlantic) in the morning, southbound in the afternoon — and there are gaps of an hour or more between lockings. Check the canal authority's website for the day's schedule; aim to be on the viewing deck for an arrival. The first time you see a 300-metre container ship slide into a chamber with only a metre of clearance on either side is genuinely arresting. Allow two to three hours, including the museum.
