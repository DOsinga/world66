The Panamanian presidential palace, built in 1673 (the same year the new city was founded) and remodelled into its current Moorish-influenced form in 1922. The Palacio sits on the seafront on Calle 1a Este in Casco Viejo, looking across the bay to the modern skyline.

The interior is sometimes open for guided tours on weekends — check ahead, security is tight and you'll need ID — but most visitors just admire the facade and the herons in the small front courtyard. The building is best photographed from the seawall behind it at sunset, with the modern city rising behind. Heads of state from around the world (and Pope Francis in 2019) have been received here.
