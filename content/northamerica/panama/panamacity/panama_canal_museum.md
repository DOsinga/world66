The Museo del Canal Interoceánico, on Plaza de la Independencia in Casco Viejo, is the best place in the country to understand the long, strange story of trying to dig a ditch across Panama. The museum is housed in a 19th-century building that originally served as the headquarters of the French canal company under Ferdinand de Lesseps; the same building was later the U.S. canal commission HQ.

Exhibits cover the pre-canal mule-train trade across the isthmus, the catastrophic French attempt of the 1880s (with its 20,000 yellow-fever deaths), the U.S. construction from 1904 to 1914, the segregated society of the Canal Zone, the 1964 Martyrs' Day riots, the 1977 Torrijos-Carter treaty, and the 2016 expansion. Labels are in Spanish; English audio guides are available. Two hours.
