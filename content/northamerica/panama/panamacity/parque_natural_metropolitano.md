A 232-hectare patch of tropical rainforest on a hill at the western edge of the city — close enough to downtown that you can see the skyline through the trees from the higher trails. The park protects one of the last surviving fragments of Pacific dry forest on the isthmus and is a serious wildlife site despite its size: agoutis, white-faced capuchins, three-toed sloths, anteaters, and the occasional ocelot.

The main entrance is on Avenida Juan Pablo II in Curundú. Five marked trails range from 800 metres to 2 km; the **Cienaguita-Mono Tití loop** is the most popular for birding, the **La Cieneguita** trail is the easiest, and the **Cerro Mono Tití** climb has the best skyline view at the top. Go at dawn for wildlife; the trails are exposed and very hot after 9am. Entrance is a few dollars; allow two to three hours.
