The southern tip of Casco Viejo, where the seaward fortifications curve out to form a broad terrace overlooking the bay. Plaza de Francia is a memorial to the French effort to build the canal in the 1880s under Ferdinand de Lesseps — a project that bankrupted thousands of small investors in France, killed perhaps 22,000 workers, and ended in collapse before the Americans finally finished the job in 1914.

The plaza itself is a pleasant open space ringed by the curved colonial bóvedas (vaulted chambers built into the seawall) which now house a restaurant and the French embassy's cultural centre. The view from the seawall is one of the best in the city: the modern skyline arcs around the bay, and Las Bóvedas walkway extends north along the fortifications. Come at sunset.
