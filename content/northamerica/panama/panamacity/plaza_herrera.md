The westernmost of the four main plazas of Casco Viejo, more residential and less restored than Plaza de la Independencia. The square is named for **General Tomás Herrera**, the 19th-century general who briefly led a Panamanian secession from Colombia in 1840; his bronze statue dominates the centre.

Plaza Herrera marks the boundary between the polished tourist zone of Casco Viejo and the rougher edges of Santa Ana and El Chorrillo to the west. The plaza has a few good budget restaurants, a hostel, and several still-crumbling colonial buildings awaiting restoration. The view from the western edge of the square down the working streets toward Avenida Central is a useful reminder that Casco Viejo's gentrification is recent and far from complete.
