A small open-air nature centre on Culebra Island at the end of the Amador Causeway, run by the **Smithsonian Tropical Research Institute**. The centre is built around a fragment of remnant dry forest with sloths in the trees and a touch-pool with sea stars, sea urchins, and small reef fish from both oceans.

Exhibits focus on the differences between the Pacific and Caribbean coasts of Panama — different fish, different tides (the Pacific has a six-metre tide range, the Caribbean has almost none), different geology. There's an outdoor turtle pool, a small aquarium, a frog house, and the chance to see the Pacific entrance of the canal in the background while you walk. Good for kids; a couple of hours.
