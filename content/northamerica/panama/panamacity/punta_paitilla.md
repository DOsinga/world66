The financial district — two adjacent peninsulas crammed with the glass-and-steel skyscrapers that define the modern Panama skyline. Punta Paitilla, developed in the 1970s and 80s, is the older of the two and still home to most of the banks; Punta Pacifica, built on landfill in the 2000s, holds the residential and hotel towers, including the most photographed building in the city — the **Trump Ocean Club**, now the **JW Marriott Panama**, a 70-storey sail-shaped tower.

There isn't much for the casual traveller to do here. The interest is mostly visual: the density of skyscrapers per square kilometre is the highest in Central America. The best view is from the Cinta Costera looking back at the cluster, or from a rooftop bar across the bay in Casco Viejo at sunset.
