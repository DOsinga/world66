A working-class plaza just outside the eastern edge of Casco Viejo, between the old quarter and the modern city. Plaza Santa Ana is anchored by the white **Iglesia de Santa Ana**, founded in 1764, and is surrounded by old single-storey wooden buildings — the kind that have mostly been knocked down elsewhere in the city.

The square is where the **Avenida Central** pedestrian shopping street begins; it runs east for about a kilometre through the **Calidonia** neighbourhood and is the closest thing Panama City has to a high street. Cheap clothes, electronics, fruit vendors, music blaring from speaker stalls. Not pretty, but a real piece of the city. Caution after dark.
