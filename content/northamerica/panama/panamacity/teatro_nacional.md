A small, Italianate opera house off Avenida B in Casco Viejo, built in 1908 in the optimistic flush of Panamanian independence. The theatre is modelled on La Scala in Milan, with red velvet seats, a horseshoe of four tiers of boxes, and an elaborate ceiling fresco. It holds about 850 people — small by the standards of Latin American national theatres but well-proportioned and acoustically excellent.

The theatre still hosts regular performances — opera, ballet, the Panama National Symphony Orchestra. When there is no rehearsal, you can visit during the day for a modest fee, walk the foyer, and step inside the auditorium. Combine with the **Bolívar Museum** across the street and Plaza Bolívar.
