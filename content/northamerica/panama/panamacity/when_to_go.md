---
title: When to Go
type: section
---

Panama City is hot, humid, and 9 degrees north of the equator — there's no off season for heat, only for rain. **December to mid-April** is the dry season and the obvious window: blue skies, manageable humidity, and most of the country's festivals (Carnival in February, the Black Christ pilgrimage in October). The trade-off is that hotel prices peak and Casco Viejo can feel packed on weekends.

**May to November** is the wet season. Rain usually arrives as a heavy afternoon thunderstorm rather than all-day drizzle, the city is much quieter, and prices drop. The downside: it can rain hard for several days in a row in October and November, the wettest months. If you're combining the capital with a beach or jungle trip, plan around it.

The big festivals worth timing for: **Carnival** (the four days before Ash Wednesday) — Panama City empties out as most locals go to Las Tablas, but the parades along the Cinta Costera are still substantial; **Founding Day** on 15 August at the Panamá Viejo ruins; and **Independence Day** on 3 November, with parades all through the centre.
