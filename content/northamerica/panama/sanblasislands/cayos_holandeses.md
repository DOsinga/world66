story: "The Cayos Holandeses (Dutch Cays) were named after 17th-century Dutch pirates who used the uninhabited islands as a base. Today they are considered the most beautiful part of the entire San Blas archipelago — a cluster of 20 or so tiny islands spread across crystal-clear water at the northwestern end of Guna Yala, with almost no settlement and visibility underwater that can exceed 30 metres on a calm day."
---

The Cayos Holandeses lie at the northwest end of the San Blas archipelago, roughly 100 km from Panama City. Most of the 20 or so islands are uninhabited; a few have a Guna family running a small camp. The water between them is clear, the reefs are largely untouched, and the snorkelling and diving are among the best in the Caribbean. Wind conditions here are more exposed than in the eastern islands closer to Cartí, making the area more popular with cruising sailboats than with day-trippers.

Getting here requires either a full day's journey by boat from Cartí or arriving by sailboat from Cartagena. Multi-day trips from Panama City sometimes include the Cayos Holandeses as an extension; ask operators about options. The extra journey time is worth it for the isolation.
