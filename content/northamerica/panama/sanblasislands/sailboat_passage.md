title: Cartagena to Panama Sailboat Passage
type: poi

The sailing passage between Cartagena in Colombia and Panama, stopping through the San Blas, is one of the classic small-boat journeys in the Western Hemisphere. Typically four to five days in total, the trip involves two or three days at sea crossing the Gulf of Darién, followed by two to three days anchored in the San Blas. Most boats carry six to eight passengers in basic berths; the experience is more camping at sea than yacht cruising.

Boats depart Cartagena frequently between November and April when the trade winds make the passage practical; less frequently the rest of the year. The standard price is around $550 to $700 per person including food. Seasickness on the open-water days is common — the passage crosses the trade wind swells head-on for part of the route. But arriving in the San Blas under sail, after two days of open ocean, is an experience that no road trip can replicate.

Book through hostels in Cartagena or Panama City, or via specialist agencies. Read reviews carefully: boat and captain quality vary considerably.
