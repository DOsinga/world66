Taboga is a small island in the Bay of Panama, about 20km off the Pacific entrance to the canal and reachable by ferry from [Panama City](/northamerica/panama/panamacity) in under an hour. Spanish settlers founded the village in 1524, and for a few decades it served as the staging post for Pizarro's conquest of Peru. Today it is a low-key escape from the capital — a single fishing village clinging to the seafront, a network of footpaths over the green hills, and a couple of beaches that fill with day-trippers on weekends and empty out by Monday morning.

The big sights are quickly told. The [Church of San Pedro](/northamerica/panama/taboga/church_of_san_pedro), founded in 1524, is one of the oldest churches in the Americas still in use. The path up to [Cerro de la Cruz](/northamerica/panama/taboga/cerro_de_la_cruz) leads to a wooden cross with a view that takes in the Panama City skyline, the cargo ships queuing for the canal, and the smaller islands offshore. [Playa Restinga](/northamerica/panama/taboga/playa_restinga), a sand spit that connects the island to the islet of El Morro at low tide, is the main swimming beach.

On the back side of the island lies the [Taboga and Urabá Islands Wildlife Refuge](/northamerica/panama/taboga/wildlife_refuge), home to one of the largest brown pelican breeding colonies in the Pacific. Local boatmen will run you around the rookery on request; the nesting season is December to July.
