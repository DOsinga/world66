Luquillo Beach is the one that appears on the postcards: a crescent of palms on the north-east coast, sheltered by a reef so the water stays flat, which makes it the family beach of choice and a relief after the Atlantic surf further west. **Balneario Monserrate** is the public end, with showers, lifeguards and parking, and one of the few genuinely accessible beaches in the Caribbean — Playa Azul has a ramp and amphibious chairs.

The other reason to stop is the **kioskos**: some sixty numbered stalls strung along Route 3 at the town entrance, frying *alcapurrias*, *bacalaítos* and *empanadillas*, selling beer and grilled fish, and running from breakfast to well past midnight at weekends. It is the most reliable eating on this coast and it costs almost nothing.

[El Yunque](/northamerica/puertorico/elyunque) rises directly behind the town, which makes Luquillo the natural base for the rainforest — closer than San Juan, and you can be at a trailhead in twenty minutes.
