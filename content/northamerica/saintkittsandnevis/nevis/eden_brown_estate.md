Eden Brown Estate sits on Nevis's quieter eastern coast, its roofless stone walls and crumbling archways slowly being reclaimed by vegetation. The plantation was established in the 18th century during the sugar boom and is now one of the more atmospheric ruins on the island. The site is freely accessible, though there's nothing to stop you and little signage.

The ghost story attached to the estate is the reason most visitors come. Whether or not there's anything to it, the isolation and the ruined state of the buildings make Eden Brown more evocative than the typical plantation great house. The eastern coast around the estate is also less visited than the tourist beaches on the west, with rougher Atlantic views and a sense of the island's quieter character. Combine a visit with the nearby Botanical Gardens, a few kilometres south.
