Saint Lucia's national rainforest is the green, mountainous interior that keeps the island from becoming just another beach destination. The reserve protects steep ridges, wet valleys, giant ferns, orchids, and the habitat of the Saint Lucia parrot, a bird that came perilously close to disappearing.

This is not a place for casual wandering off on your own. Trails are humid, muddy, and often require a local guide or forestry permit, and conditions can change quickly after rain. The reward is a very different Saint Lucia: bird calls instead of resort music, dripping canopy instead of trade-wind beaches, and sudden views toward the Pitons or the Atlantic side of the island.

The most accessible introductions are the [Millet Bird Sanctuary Trail](/northamerica/saintlucia/saintlucianationalrainforest/millet_bird_sanctuary) and [Barre de L'Isle Trail](/northamerica/saintlucia/saintlucianationalrainforest/barre_de_lisle_trail). Strong hikers with guides can push deeper toward [Mount Gimie](/northamerica/saintlucia/saintlucianationalrainforest/mount_gimie), the island's highest point, or to waterfalls on the forest edge.
