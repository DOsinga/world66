Bequia is the largest of the Grenadine islands belonging to St. Vincent, a small, hilly island of about 7 square miles set between the Caribbean Sea to the west and the Atlantic to the east. With a population of around 5,000, it has retained a quiet, unhurried pace that the bigger resort islands have long since lost.

[Port Elizabeth](/northamerica/saintvincentandthegrenadines/bequia/port_elizabeth), the main settlement, curves around [Admiralty Bay](/northamerica/saintvincentandthegrenadines/bequia/admiralty_bay) — one of the finest natural anchorages in the eastern Caribbean. Sailors have known it for centuries and the bay still fills with yachts in season. The island has a strong tradition of boat building, and Bequians are known for their craftsmanship; [model boats](/northamerica/saintvincentandthegrenadines/bequia/model_boat_workshops) are the main local souvenir. The beaches on the leeward (west) side, particularly [Princess Margaret Beach](/northamerica/saintvincentandthegrenadines/bequia/princess_margaret_beach) and [Lower Bay](/northamerica/saintvincentandthegrenadines/bequia/lower_bay_beach), are calm and beautiful. Snorkelling and diving are excellent in the surrounding waters. In the north of the island, the [Old Hegg Turtle Sanctuary](/northamerica/saintvincentandthegrenadines/bequia/old_hegg_turtle_sanctuary) raises and releases endangered hawksbill hatchlings; on the south-western tip, the eccentric stone houses of [Moonhole](/northamerica/saintvincentandthegrenadines/bequia/moonhole) are well worth the drive.

Bequia is reached by ferry from Kingstown on St. Vincent — the crossing takes about an hour.
