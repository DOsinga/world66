Bequia is the largest of the Grenadine islands belonging to St. Vincent, a small, hilly island of about 7 square miles set between the Caribbean Sea to the west and the Atlantic to the east. With a population of around 5,000, it has retained a quiet, unhurried pace that the bigger resort islands have long since lost.

Port Elizabeth, the main settlement, curves around [Admiralty Bay](/northamerica/saintvincentandthegrenadines/bequia/admiralty_bay) — one of the finest natural anchorages in the eastern Caribbean. Sailors have known it for centuries and the bay still fills with yachts in season. The island has a strong tradition of [boat building](/northamerica/saintvincentandthegrenadines/bequia/bequia_boat_building), and Bequians are known for their craftsmanship; model boats are the main local souvenir. The beaches on the leeward (west) side, particularly [Princess Margaret Beach](/northamerica/saintvincentandthegrenadines/bequia/princess_margaret_beach) and [Lower Bay](/northamerica/saintvincentandthegrenadines/bequia/lower_bay), are calm and beautiful. Snorkelling and diving are excellent in the surrounding waters.

Bequia is reached by ferry from Kingstown on St. Vincent — the crossing takes about an hour.
