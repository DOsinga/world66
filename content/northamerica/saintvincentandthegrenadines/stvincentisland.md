St Vincent is the volcanic main island of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines — steep, green, and noticeably less polished than the smaller islands that draw most of the country's visitors. About 26 kilometres long and rising to the still-active [La Soufrière](/northamerica/saintvincentandthegrenadines/stvincentisland/la_soufriere) volcano in the north, it is one of the wettest and most rugged islands in the eastern Caribbean. The Atlantic windward coast is wild and battered; the Caribbean leeward coast holds the calmer bays, the black-sand beaches, and most of the settlements.

The capital, [Kingstown](/northamerica/saintvincentandthegrenadines/stvincentisland/kingstown), sits in a deep bay on the south coast under a ring of hills. It is a working West Indian port town — small, busy, unmistakably Caribbean — with a covered market, stone-arched arcades along the main streets, and the country's oldest churches within a few blocks of each other. Just north of town is the [Botanical Garden](/northamerica/saintvincentandthegrenadines/stvincentisland/botanical_gardens), founded in 1765 and the oldest in the western hemisphere; it still holds a descendant of the breadfruit tree Captain Bligh brought from Tahiti on his second voyage. Above the town, [Fort Charlotte](/northamerica/saintvincentandthegrenadines/stvincentisland/fort_charlotte) commands one of the best views on the island.

Inland the country turns vertical fast. The Mesopotamia Valley behind Kingstown is dense with bananas, breadfruit and nutmeg trees; the Vermont Nature Trails climb into rainforest where the St Vincent parrot — the national bird, found nowhere else — still calls from the canopy. Up the leeward coast, fishing villages like [Barrouallie](/northamerica/saintvincentandthegrenadines/stvincentisland/barrouallie) and [Chateaubelair](/northamerica/saintvincentandthegrenadines/stvincentisland/chateaubelair) cling to narrow coastal strips between sea and cliff. Beyond them the road winds toward the Soufrière trailhead at [Richmond Beach](/northamerica/saintvincentandthegrenadines/stvincentisland/richmond_beach) and the slow drama of villages still rebuilding from the 2021 eruption, which dropped ash across half the island.

St Vincent is not a beach-resort island in the way Barbados or Antigua are — most of its sand is dark, and what tourism infrastructure exists is concentrated around Villa and Indian Bay near the airport. The pleasure of the place is in the interior: hiking the volcano, swimming in the Dark View and [Trinity Falls](/northamerica/saintvincentandthegrenadines/stvincentisland/trinity_falls), driving the leeward coast road past cliffs and fishing boats, and spending a couple of evenings in Kingstown before crossing to the Grenadines.
