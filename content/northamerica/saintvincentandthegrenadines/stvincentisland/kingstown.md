Kingstown is the capital and the only real town on St Vincent — a tight grid of two- and three-storey buildings wedged between a steep ridge and a deep harbour. The population is barely 20,000, but on a weekday morning the streets are busy with commerce: market vendors, fishermen unloading at Little Tokyo, schoolchildren in colour-coded uniforms, vans loading for the country routes.

Most of what you'd want to see fits in a short walk. The covered [market](/northamerica/saintvincentandthegrenadines/stvincentisland/kingstown_market) sits at the centre, the [Anglican cathedral](/northamerica/saintvincentandthegrenadines/stvincentisland/st_george_cathedral) and the [Catholic cathedral](/northamerica/saintvincentandthegrenadines/stvincentisland/assumption_cathedral) are within two blocks of each other on Grenville Street, and the waterfront has the cruise terminal at one end and the inter-island ferry dock at the other. Hire a taxi for the climb up to [Fort Charlotte](/northamerica/saintvincentandthegrenadines/stvincentisland/fort_charlotte) — at 600 feet above the harbour the view back over town and out to Bequia is the best in St Vincent. The [Botanical Garden](/northamerica/saintvincentandthegrenadines/stvincentisland/botanical_gardens) is a 15-minute walk north of the centre and worth at least an hour.
