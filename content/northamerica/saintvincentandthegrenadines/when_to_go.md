---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

Saint Vincent and the Grenadines stretch south from the volcanic main island into a chain of small, dry, sailing-friendly islets reaching nearly to Grenada. The climate is tropical and the country sits in the hurricane belt, but storms tend to track north — direct hits are infrequent.

**Dry season (January–May)** is the prime time. Light trade winds, dependable sun, calm seas, and the sailing conditions through the Grenadines are at their best. This is the busiest charter window — book yachts and Bequia/Mustique accommodation well in advance.

**Shoulder (June and November)** is a good value window: prices drop, the weather is still mostly fine, and the islands are quieter.

**Wet/hurricane season (July–October)** brings heavier rain and the active hurricane window. Sailing continues but is more weather-dependent; some smaller operators close.

**Vincy Mas (Carnival)** in late June into early July is the year's biggest party, centred on Kingstown. **Bequia Easter Regatta** is the high point for sailors, drawing classic yachts.

The **La Soufrière volcano** on Saint Vincent erupted in 2021; check the National Emergency Management Office advisories before planning trips to the northern half of the island.
