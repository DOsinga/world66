The Caribbean is an arc of islands stretching from the tip of Florida to the coast of Venezuela, where the Atlantic meets the Caribbean Sea. Over seven thousand islands, islets, and cays are scattered across the basin, ranging from tiny uninhabited rocks to substantial islands like Cuba, Hispaniola, and Jamaica.

What holds the region together is the landscape — white-sand beaches, turquoise shallows, coral reefs, and the warm trade winds that have blown sailors across these waters for centuries. What distinguishes each island is its history: British, French, Dutch, Spanish, and Danish colonial empires all left their marks, producing a patchwork of languages, cuisines, legal systems, and cultures that makes island-hopping across the Caribbean one of the most varied travel experiences in the Americas.

The region divides roughly into the Greater Antilles in the north ([Cuba](/northamerica/thecaribbean/cuba), [Jamaica](/northamerica/jamaica), [Hispaniola](/northamerica/thecaribbean/haiti), [Puerto Rico](/northamerica/thecaribbean/puertorico)) and the Lesser Antilles running southeast — the Leeward Islands ([Antigua](/northamerica/thecaribbean/antiguaandbarbuda), [Saint Kitts](/northamerica/thecaribbean/saintkittsandnevis), [Guadeloupe](/northamerica/thecaribbean/guadeloupe)) and the Windward Islands ([Martinique](/northamerica/thecaribbean/martinique), [Saint Lucia](/northamerica/saintlucia), [Barbados](/northamerica/thecaribbean/barbados), [Trinidad and Tobago](/northamerica/trinidadandtobago)). The Dutch ABC islands — Aruba, Bonaire, Curaçao — sit close to the Venezuelan coast.

Hurricane season runs June through November, with peak risk in August and September. Outside those months the weather is reliably warm and sunny.
