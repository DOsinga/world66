Oranjestad (pop. 35,000) is Aruba's compact Dutch Caribbean capital, and the most rewarding part of the island for anyone interested in something beyond beach and resort. The waterfront is the city's heart: [Venezuelan trading schooners](/northamerica/thecaribbean/aruba/oranjestad/oranjestad_waterfront) dock along the quay selling fresh fish, tropical fruit, and spices, while colourful Dutch colonial facades — pastel pinks, yellows, and greens with stepped gables — line the streets behind. It is a vivid and genuine scene, different in character from the hotel strip a few kilometres north.

The city's historic core is small enough to cover on foot in two to three hours. [Fort Zoutman](/northamerica/thecaribbean/aruba/oranjestad/fort_zoutman), completed in 1798, is the oldest building on the island; the adjacent Willem III Tower was added later as a lighthouse. Together they anchor the [Historical Museum of Aruba](/northamerica/thecaribbean/aruba/oranjestad/historical_museum_aruba) and host the [Bon Bini Festival](/northamerica/thecaribbean/aruba/oranjestad/bon_bini_festival) every Tuesday evening — a weekly celebration of local music, dance, and crafts that is well worth attending. A short walk away, the [National Archaeological Museum](/northamerica/thecaribbean/aruba/oranjestad/national_archaeological_museum) covers the island's Arawak past with more depth than you might expect.

[Wilhelmina Park](/northamerica/thecaribbean/aruba/oranjestad/wilhelmina_park) offers a shaded rest along the waterfront boulevard, and the [Numismatic Museum](/northamerica/thecaribbean/aruba/oranjestad/numismatic_museum) nearby is a curiosity worth a brief visit. Shopping along L.G. Smith Boulevard is aimed squarely at cruise passengers, but the harbour market is the real find. For a panoramic view of the island, [Hooiberg](/northamerica/thecaribbean/aruba/oranjestad/hooiberg) — the volcanic haystack hill — is 10 kilometres east and worth the climb on a clear day.
