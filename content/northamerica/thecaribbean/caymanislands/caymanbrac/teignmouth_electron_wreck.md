The Teignmouth Electron is the wreck of a small fibreglass trimaran on a beach near the western tip of Cayman Brac — not signposted, easy to miss, but one of the strangest pieces of maritime history in the Caribbean. The hulls are weathered and patched with graffiti; the cabin and rig are long gone; the wreck sits a few metres above the high-water mark in the dunes.

Finding it requires asking — most taxi drivers and dive operators know where it is, though it lies on private land and the access can be informal. The story behind the boat — Donald Crowhurst's lonely fraud and disappearance on the 1968 Golden Globe race — has been told in books and a documentary; reading something about it before you visit makes the wreck more haunting. It will not be there much longer. The hulls are slowly disintegrating and pieces have been carried off as souvenirs for decades.
