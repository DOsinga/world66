The whitewashed cathedral on the Plaza Independencia is the heart of colonial Baracoa. The current building dates from the 1830s, replacing an earlier 1512 church destroyed by fire and hurricanes. The interior is plain — bare wooden beams, simple pews, an unfussy altar — but the relic it houses gives the building national significance.

The Cruz de la Parra, the only one of the 29 wooden crosses that Columbus is said to have planted around the New World whose authenticity is taken seriously, is kept here in a side chapel. The cross has been worn down over the centuries by pilgrims taking splinters, and is now protected behind glass. The cathedral keeps fairly relaxed opening hours; if it is locked, ask in the square.
