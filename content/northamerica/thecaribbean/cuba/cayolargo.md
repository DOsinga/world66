Cayo Largo is a small, flat island off the southern coast of [Cuba](/northamerica/thecaribbean/cuba), known almost entirely for its beaches. [Playa Sirena](/northamerica/thecaribbean/cuba/cayolargo/playa_sirena) and [Playa Paraíso](/northamerica/thecaribbean/cuba/cayolargo/playa_paraiso) are routinely named among the best in the Caribbean — long stretches of white sand with warm, shallow, impossibly clear water and almost nobody on them.

The island is a resort destination with no permanent local population. Everything here exists for tourism: a handful of all-inclusive hotels along [Playa Blanca](/northamerica/thecaribbean/cuba/cayolargo/playa_blanca), a marina, a [dive centre](/northamerica/thecaribbean/cuba/cayolargo/diving), and a small [turtle farm](/northamerica/thecaribbean/cuba/cayolargo/turtle_farm). That can feel sterile compared to the rest of Cuba, but the trade-off is the beaches, which are genuinely spectacular. The snorkelling and diving along the surrounding reef are excellent, with good coral and plenty of marine life — and a boat trip to [Cayo Iguana](/northamerica/thecaribbean/cuba/cayolargo/cayo_iguana) lets you walk among a colony of native rock iguanas.

Cayo Largo is reached by short flights from Havana or Varadero. There is no ferry for tourists. The island is small enough to explore by bicycle or golf cart, and most visitors spend their time on the sand. The best weather is November through April; summer brings heat, humidity, and the possibility of hurricanes.
