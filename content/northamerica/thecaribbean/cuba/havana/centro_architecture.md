Centro Habana contains some of Havana's finest Art Deco buildings, constructed in the 1920s and 1930s when the city was booming on sugar money and American investment. The López Serrano building (visible from Vedado), the Bacardí Building at the border with Old Havana, and several apartment towers on the main avenues represent a confident high-rise modernism that was genuinely exceptional in the Caribbean at the time.

The buildings are now divided into apartments and offices, often in poor repair but still architecturally impressive. The contrast between the grandeur of the shells and the improvised interior arrangements of their current residents is one of the defining images of Havana.
