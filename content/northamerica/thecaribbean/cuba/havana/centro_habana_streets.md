Centro Habana is the densest and most intensely urban neighbourhood in Havana — grid-patterned streets packed with tenements, paladares in converted living rooms, mechanics working on American cars in doorways, and the constant improvised commerce that characterises Cuban daily life. It is less photogenic than Old Havana and less grand than Vedado, but it is where the city actually lives.

Neptuno, San Rafael, and the streets running north from the Malecón are the main commercial arteries. The neighbourhood is at its most alive in the evenings when residents come out to talk on front steps and the informal economy — food vendors, card games, music — fills the streets. Walking here without a fixed destination, letting the neighbourhood show itself, gives a more honest picture of Havana than the curated tourist circuits.
