El Cerro has a growing number of paladares — private restaurants operating from former family homes — that offer better value than the tourist-oriented restaurants of Old Havana. The neighbourhood's distance from the main tourist circuit means prices are lower and the clientele is more likely to be Cuban middle class than foreign visitors.

Several paladares have opened in the renovated rooms of the old arcaded houses on Calzada del Cerro and the surrounding streets. Quality is variable but the best serve traditional Cuban cooking — ropa vieja, congri rice, fresh fish — at prices significantly below what you would pay for equivalent quality elsewhere in the city.
