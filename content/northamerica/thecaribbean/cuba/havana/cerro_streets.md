The main streets of El Cerro — particularly Calzada del Cerro — are lined with 19th-century townhouses with distinctive arched colonnades (portales) that once sheltered wealthy Havana families. Many are in various states of decay: stripped of their original paint and fittings, occupied by multiple families in subdivided rooms, but still architecturally remarkable in their bone structure. The neighbourhood is a living demonstration of what Havana looked like before the revolution and what it has endured since.

Walking El Cerro away from the tourist circuit gives a better sense of everyday Havana life than the renovated plazas of the historic centre. People sit outside in the evenings, children play in the streets, and the pace is slower and more domestic than the centre.
