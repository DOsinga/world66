The Malecón is the 8-kilometre seawall and promenade that curves along the north shore of Havana from the Castillo de la Punta in Old Havana to the Vedado neighbourhood in the west. It is the defining public space of the city — simultaneously a road, a gathering place, a fishing pier, a lovers' lane, a meeting point, and a stage for the endless informal theatre of Havana life.

In the evening, the Malecón fills with Havanans of every age and background: couples on the wall, young men fishing in the spray, musicians playing for tips, old women watching the sea. The spray from winter swells sometimes crosses the road entirely and soaks passers-by, which locals take as part of the arrangement. The buildings behind the Malecón — a long succession of crumbling neoclassical and Art Deco facades — are among the most atmospheric in the city, their peeling paint and sagging balconies a monument to both old Havana's grandeur and decades of economic difficulty. Walk it at sunset.
