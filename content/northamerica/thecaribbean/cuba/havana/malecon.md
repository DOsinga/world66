The eight-kilometre seafront promenade running from the mouth of Havana Harbour westward to Vedado, lined with crumbling but still-magnificent 20th-century buildings. As a boulevard for walking it's second to none; as a sunset gathering place it's unmissable. By 6pm, fishermen, couples, schoolchildren, old men with guitars, and virtually everyone else in the city has found a section of the low wall to sit on. The north-facing position means the Atlantic runs straight into it during storms — the spray can clear the wall entirely.
