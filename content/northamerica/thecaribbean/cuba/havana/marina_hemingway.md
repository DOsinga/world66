Marina Hemingway, named for the writer who kept his boat Pilar nearby, is the main marina in Havana — a complex of canals and yacht berths at the western end of the city. American boats began arriving here again after the Obama-era opening of diplomatic relations, and the marina has a distinctive atmosphere where Cuban state officials and foreign sailors navigate an unusual coexistence.

The marina has several restaurants and bars that serve fish and seafood at prices slightly lower than the city centre. The access road from the main city is long but the setting — canals lined with anchored sailboats, pelicans on the dockside — is refreshingly different from urban Havana.
