Nuevo Vedado is one of Havana's most ordinary neighbourhoods — a large residential district without major monuments or tourist infrastructure, where the rhythms of Cuban daily life proceed without reference to visitors. The morning markets, the bicycle repair shops, the children walking to school in uniform — these are the sights of a functioning neighbourhood that has been off the tourist map since the city was constructed.

Spending a morning here before returning to the historic centre recalibrates expectations about what Havana actually is. The contrast between the restored plazas of Old Havana and the working-class residential reality of Nuevo Vedado is one of the most instructive things about visiting Cuba.
