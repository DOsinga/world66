Nuevo Vedado is one of Havana's most liveable residential neighbourhoods — quieter than Centro Habana, less grand than Vedado, and more authentically Cuban in character than Miramar. The neighbourhood is built on a grid of tree-lined streets, with a mix of mid-century apartment blocks and older republican-era houses converted into multi-family dwellings.

The farmers' market on Avenida 26 is a good place to see how Havanans shop and eat — seasonal produce, cheese, rice, beans, and the improvised economy that keeps the city fed. The streets around the market are lined with paladares (private restaurants) that serve the neighbourhood rather than tourists, which means simpler food at lower prices and a more relaxed atmosphere. Walking through here on a weekend morning gives a genuine picture of Havana domestic life.
