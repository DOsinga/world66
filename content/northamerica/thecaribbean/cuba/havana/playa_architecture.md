Playa contains some of the finest 1950s modernist architecture in Havana — a period when Cuban architects were experimenting with international style modified for the Caribbean climate. The neighbourhood was developed primarily for upper-middle-class Cubans before the revolution, and many of the houses from this era survive with their original features: brise-soleil screens, cantilevered upper floors, gardens that flow into interiors.

The streets around Kohly neighbourhood contain the greatest concentration. Many buildings are now used as embassies, state guesthouses, or have been divided into apartments, but the architecture remains largely intact. Architectural historians rate this cluster of modernist houses among the significant concentrations of mid-century residential architecture in Latin America.
