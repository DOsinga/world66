The beaches along the western coast of Havana in the Playa municipality are not particularly good for swimming — the water quality is uneven and the beaches are narrow — but they serve as important social spaces for Habaneros without access to cars or taxis to reach the eastern beaches. Cubans swim, play music, and socialise here on hot evenings.

The rocky coastline between Miramar and Santa Fe is more dramatic than beachy, and there are points where you can sit on the rocks above the sea and watch the waves. The malecón-style waterfront in this part of the city is less famous than the one in central Havana but equally atmospheric.
