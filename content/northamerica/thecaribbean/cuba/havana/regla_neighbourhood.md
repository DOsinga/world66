Regla is the smallest municipality of Havana and one of the most cohesive — a compact working-class town separated from the capital by the harbour and connected only by ferry. Its character is shaped by the sea, by the strong Afro-Cuban spiritual traditions centred on the Virgen de Regla, and by the industrial heritage of the waterfront where sugar and petroleum moved through for generations.

Walking from the ferry dock through the main streets to the church and then into the residential grid behind it takes about an hour and covers most of what the town offers. The streets are quiet, the architecture modest, and the pace is entirely different from tourist Havana. The Yoruba and Abakuá traditions visible in the shrines, the blue-clothed devotees of the Virgen, and the general atmosphere of a place largely untouched by tourism give Regla a significance beyond its size.
