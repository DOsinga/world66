The streets of Regla away from the church and waterfront are a dense working-class neighbourhood that receives almost no tourists. The houses are modest, the streets are narrow, and the atmosphere is that of a small provincial Cuban town that happens to sit across the harbour from a major city. People wave from doorways, children play in the street, and the pace is distinctly slower than Havana proper.

A walk of an hour through Regla's grid of streets, ending at the church and then back to the ferry, gives a genuinely different perspective on Cuban life than anything available in the historic centre. The neighbourhood has a warmth and openness to strangers that the more touristed parts of Havana sometimes lack.
