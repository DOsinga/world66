The southern edge of San Miguel del Padrón municipality borders the small settlement of Diezmero and the agricultural fringes of greater Havana. This is where the city gives way to tobacco farms and smallholdings — an abrupt transition that reminds visitors how thin the margin between the Cuban capital and the countryside actually is.

Diezmero itself is a village of a few hundred people, largely self-sufficient and connected to Havana by infrequent bus services. The landscape here — gentle hills, small farms, wooden houses in Cuban vernacular style — is similar to what exists throughout the island. For those who have the time to explore beyond the organised tourist circuit, a day cycling or travelling by local bus from San Miguel del Padrón out towards Diezmero shows a side of Cuban life that is rarely included in travel itineraries.
