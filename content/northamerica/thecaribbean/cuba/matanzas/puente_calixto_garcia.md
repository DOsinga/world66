  Matanzas translates roughly as "slaughters" in Spanish, and the city takes its name from an incident here in the 1510s when a group of Spanish soldiers was killed while trying to cross the river. The bridge that spans the Río San Juan today was built in the colonial period and rebuilt several times since, most recently named for the independence war general Calixto García.

A historic iron and stone bridge crossing the Río San Juan in the lower part of the city, a few blocks from the Plaza de la Vigía. The current structure dates from the late 19th century and is one of several bridges that cross Matanzas's three rivers — the San Juan, the Yumurí, and the Camarioca. The bridge is still in daily use and offers a good view of the lower city and the bay beyond.

The surrounding area near the bridge and the riverside is one of the older neighbourhoods of Matanzas, with colonial-era houses in various states of repair. Worth walking across as part of a route between the Plaza de la Vigía and the waterfront.
