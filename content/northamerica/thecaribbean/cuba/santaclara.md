Santa Clara is the capital of Villa Clara province, sitting in the centre of Cuba roughly equidistant from Havana and Santiago de Cuba. It's a university city with a lively, younger character than the more touristed colonial towns — the streets around [Parque Vidal](/northamerica/thecaribbean/cuba/santaclara/parque_vidal) fill up most evenings with students, musicians, and families doing what Cubans do everywhere: talking, arguing, flirting, and listening to music with the windows open.

The city is inextricably linked to Che Guevara. The battle of Santa Clara in December 1958 was the decisive engagement of the Cuban Revolution — Guevara's column captured the city by derailing a Batista armoured train loaded with troops and weapons, and the armoured cars still sit where they were toppled, now a monument called the [Tren Blindado](/northamerica/thecaribbean/cuba/santaclara/tren_blindado). The [Che Guevara Mausoleum](/northamerica/thecaribbean/cuba/santaclara/che_guevara_mausoleum) contains his remains, brought back from Bolivia in 1997. Pilgrims from around the world come here; it's a serious, moving place whether you share the politics or not.

Beyond the Che sites, Santa Clara has a genuinely interesting cultural life. [Teatro La Caridad](/northamerica/thecaribbean/cuba/santaclara/teatro_la_caridad) is one of the finest surviving 19th-century theatres in Cuba. [El Mejunje](/northamerica/thecaribbean/cuba/santaclara/el_mejunje) — a cultural centre that has been an openly gay-friendly space since the 1980s, long before tolerance became official Cuban policy — hosts live music, drag shows, and theatre in a crumbling colonial courtyard. The city is an honest slice of Cuban urban life, with none of the theatrical staging of Havana's tourist districts.
