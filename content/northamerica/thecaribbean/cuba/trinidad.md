Trinidad is Cuba's best-preserved colonial town and a UNESCO World Heritage Site — the kind of place where the 18th century feels close enough to touch. Cobblestoned streets climb the hill past pastel-painted houses with red tile roofs, wrought-iron grilles, and heavy wooden doors that open onto cool interior courtyards. At the top sits the [Plaza Mayor](/northamerica/thecaribbean/cuba/trinidad/plaza_mayor), flanked by former sugar baron mansions now turned into museums.

The town gets touristy in the afternoon when day-trip buses arrive from [Varadero](/northamerica/thecaribbean/cuba/varadero) and the beach resorts. Come early in the morning or, better yet, stay the night in a casa particular — the private houses where families rent rooms are one of the best ways to experience Cuban hospitality. After dark, Trinidad comes alive: the [Casa de la Música](/northamerica/thecaribbean/cuba/trinidad/casa_de_la_musica) on the outdoor staircase off the Plaza Mayor is one of Cuba's great live music venues, with salsa bands playing under the stars.

Just outside town, the [Valle de los Ingenios](/northamerica/thecaribbean/cuba/trinidad/valle_de_los_ingenios) (Valley of the Sugar Mills) is another UNESCO site, and the beach at [Playa Ancón](/northamerica/thecaribbean/cuba/trinidad/playa_ancon) is the best stretch of sand on Cuba's southern coast, a twenty-minute drive away.
