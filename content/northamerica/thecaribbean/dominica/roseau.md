With so much wilderness to explore on [Dominica](/northamerica/thecaribbean/dominica), visitors sometimes rush past the capital. Roseau is compact, genuinely lived-in, and worth a half-day. The [Dominica Museum](/northamerica/thecaribbean/dominica/roseau/dominica_museum) in the old colonial post office on the waterfront gives a good grounding in the island's history — colonial, volcanic, and Kalinago. Admission is modest.

The old town centre has cobblestone streets — the stones were brought over as ballast in trading ships — lined with small shops and wooden colonial houses. The [Cathedral of Our Lady of Fair Haven](/northamerica/thecaribbean/dominica/roseau/roseau_cathedral) is worth seeing, as is the seaward terrace of [Fort Young](/northamerica/thecaribbean/dominica/roseau/fort_young) on the headland at the south end of the centre. For shopping, head to Cork Street or to the [New Market](/northamerica/thecaribbean/dominica/roseau/new_market) by the river mouth on Saturday morning.

The [Botanic Gardens](/northamerica/thecaribbean/dominica/roseau/dominica_botanical_gardens) contain examples of most of the trees, shrubs, and flowers found on the island and serve as the headquarters of the Forestry, Wildlife and Parks Division — a useful stop for maps and guides before heading into the interior. Climb up through them to [Morne Bruce](/northamerica/thecaribbean/dominica/roseau/morne_bruce) for the best view over the town and the bay.
