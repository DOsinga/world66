Petite Martinique is the smallest inhabited island in Grenada, a steep volcanic dot east of Carriacou with about 900 residents and a strong boatbuilding and fishing tradition. It is close enough to Carriacou for a day trip, but it feels more remote: fewer vehicles, fewer services, and a social life tied closely to the waterfront.

The island is compact, only about 2.3 square kilometres, and rises sharply to [the Piton](/northamerica/thecaribbean/grenada/petitmartinique/the_piton), the little summit that gives walkers views across the Grenadines. Most visitors arrive by boat, wander the village, swim at [Mang Beach](/northamerica/thecaribbean/grenada/petitmartinique/mang_beach) or [Sanchez Beach](/northamerica/thecaribbean/grenada/petitmartinique/sanchez_beach), and return to Carriacou before evening.

Petite Martinique is not a place for a packed itinerary. Its pleasures are modest and very local: wooden boats pulled up for work, rum shops, fishermen repairing gear, and the occasional burst of island energy around the Whitsuntide regatta. Come with patience, cash, water, and the understanding that schedules bend when the sea says so.
