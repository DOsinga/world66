Grande-Terre is the eastern half of Guadeloupe, separated from Basse-Terre by the narrow Rivière Salée. Where Basse-Terre is volcanic, mountainous, and lush, Grande-Terre is flat, dry, and lined with beaches — the two halves of the butterfly-shaped island could hardly be more different.

The south coast from Sainte-Anne to Saint-François has the best beaches: long stretches of white sand backed by sea-grape trees. [Plage de la Caravelle](/northamerica/thecaribbean/guadeloupe/grandeterre/plage_de_la_caravelle) and Plage du Bourg are popular and well-served; the [Pointe des Châteaux](/northamerica/thecaribbean/guadeloupe/grandeterre/pointe_des_chateaux) headland at the eastern tip offers dramatic cliffs and a wild Atlantic coastline.

Pointe-à-Pitre, Guadeloupe's economic capital, sits where the two islands meet. The city is noisy and chaotic but has a lively market and the [Mémorial ACTe](/northamerica/thecaribbean/guadeloupe/grandeterre/memorial_acte), a striking museum dedicated to the history of slavery in the Caribbean. Outside the city, [Fort Fleur d'Épée](/northamerica/thecaribbean/guadeloupe/grandeterre/fort_fleur_depee) at Le Gosier commands the coast with views that explain why the French and British fought over it for half a century.
