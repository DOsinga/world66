  The name — sugarcane — is a reminder of what made Guadeloupe economically significant to France for two centuries. The island produced some of the most valuable sugar in the Caribbean, grown and processed by enslaved workers. The fact that the restaurant sits near the Mémorial ACTe, which tells that story directly, gives the name a layer of resonance the menu alone would not.

La Canne à Sucre is a well-regarded Creole restaurant in Pointe-à-Pitre, near the waterfront. The cooking is solidly traditional: accras, boudin, grilled fish caught that morning, colombo de poulet, and the slow-braised meat dishes that are central to Guadeloupe's table. The ti' punch — rum, lime, cane syrup — arrives before anything else, as it should.

The restaurant occupies a colonial-era building and the room is pleasant without being fussy. Lunch is the main event: Guadeloupean restaurants peak at midday, and this one is no exception. Portions are generous and prices are fair by island standards. Reservations are worth making for Friday and Saturday lunch.
