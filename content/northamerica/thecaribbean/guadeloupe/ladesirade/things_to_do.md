title: Things to Do
type: section
---

La Désirade rewards visitors who want to slow down rather than tick off sights. The appeal is negative space: empty beaches, unhurried villages, and the quiet satisfaction of arriving somewhere that most people pass over. This is not a resort island — there are no organised excursions, no beach clubs, no nightlife to speak of. What there is: the chance to see one of the Caribbean's most endangered iguanas at close range, good snorkelling off uncrowded reefs, and long stretches of sand with nobody on them.

The island is small enough to walk or cycle in a day. The single road along the southern coast connects all the settlements and gives access to the main beaches. The dramatic northern cliffs are a short walk over the ridge from the road and worth the effort for the Atlantic views. Beyond that, the pleasure is mostly in being here — eating fresh fish, drinking ti-punch in the shade, and watching the fishing boats come in.
