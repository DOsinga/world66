Pointe-à-Pitre is Guadeloupe's commercial pulse: loud, practical, Creole-French, and far more urban than the beach towns along Grande-Terre's south coast. It sits near the narrow join between Grande-Terre and Basse-Terre, which makes it the natural ferry hub for the outer islands and the place where travellers come to shop, transfer, eat, and get their bearings.

The city is not pretty in an easy postcard way. Streets can feel hectic, traffic is dense, and the waterfront has working-port edges as well as restored promenades. But the centre rewards walking: [Place de la Victoire](/northamerica/thecaribbean/guadeloupe/pointeapitre/place_de_la_victoire) gives the city a formal old heart, the [spice market](/northamerica/thecaribbean/guadeloupe/pointeapitre/spice_market) fills the surrounding streets with colour and scent, and [Saint-Pierre-et-Saint-Paul](/northamerica/thecaribbean/guadeloupe/pointeapitre/saint_pierre_et_saint_paul) is one of the island's strongest pieces of iron-and-stone church architecture.

Pointe-à-Pitre also has Guadeloupe's most serious museums. [Mémorial ACTe](/northamerica/thecaribbean/guadeloupe/pointeapitre/memorial_acte) is essential for understanding slavery and the Atlantic world in the French Caribbean, while the [Saint-John Perse Museum](/northamerica/thecaribbean/guadeloupe/pointeapitre/musee_saint_john_perse) and [Musée Schœlcher](/northamerica/thecaribbean/guadeloupe/pointeapitre/musee_schoelcher) preserve older layers of colonial Pointe-à-Pitre.

Come in the morning, when the markets are alive and the heat has not flattened the streets. Use the city as a ferry base, a cultural stop, or a half-day walk before heading out to the beaches; expecting a resort town is the quickest way to miss what Pointe-à-Pitre actually does well.
