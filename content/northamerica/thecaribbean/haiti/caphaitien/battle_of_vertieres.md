Three kilometres southwest of Cap-Haïtien, a simple monument marks the site of the Battle of Vertières on 18 November 1803 — the final battle of the Haitian Revolution and the engagement that secured Haiti's independence. Haitian forces under Jean-Jacques Dessalines defeated the French army of General Rochambeau, forcing the French surrender of Saint-Domingue and clearing the way for Haiti's declaration of independence on 1 January 1804.

The battle site is unpretentious — a roadside monument in a working-class neighbourhood — but the historical significance is immense. Haiti was the first free Black republic in the world, and this engagement was the decisive moment. Haitian schoolchildren visit in school groups; it's rarely on the tourist circuit of international visitors, which makes it feel more genuine. A short moto-taxi ride from the city centre.
