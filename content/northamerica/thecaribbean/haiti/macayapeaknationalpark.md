Macaya Peak National Park protects one of Haiti's most precious landscapes: the cloud forest and high ridges of the Massif de la Hotte on the western end of the southern peninsula. This is not a casual picnic park. It is remote, mountainous, biologically important, and difficult to reach even when Haiti is stable.

The park centres on [Pic Macaya](/northamerica/thecaribbean/haiti/macayapeaknationalpark/pic_macaya), Haiti's second-highest mountain, and nearby [Pic de Formon](/northamerica/thecaribbean/haiti/macayapeaknationalpark/pic_de_formon). Between them are some of the country's last stands of high-elevation cloud forest, habitat for endemic plants, amphibians, and the endangered black-capped petrel.

As of June 2026, foreign governments advise against all travel to Haiti because of crime, kidnapping, unrest, and the collapse of normal security conditions in large parts of the country. Treat this page as a guide to a place worth understanding and protecting, not as encouragement to attempt an independent trip now.

When conditions allow, Macaya is for experienced hikers, researchers, and travellers working with reputable local guides. Access has traditionally been through communities on the southern peninsula, with rough roads, long approaches, and weather that can change quickly in the high forest.
