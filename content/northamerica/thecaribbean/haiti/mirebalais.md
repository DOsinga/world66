Mirebalais is a Central Plateau town on National Road 3, roughly north-east of Port-au-Prince and historically important as a market and administrative centre for the surrounding agricultural country. It is not a classic sightseeing town, but it sits at a strategic crossroads between the capital, Hinche, the Artibonite valley, and the pilgrimage country around Saut-d'Eau.

The town's modern landmark is [Hôpital Universitaire de Mirebalais](/northamerica/thecaribbean/haiti/mirebalais/hopital_universitaire_de_mirebalais), built after the 2010 earthquake and known for its solar power and national medical role. The older rhythm is still the [market](/northamerica/thecaribbean/haiti/mirebalais/mirebalais_market), where produce from the Plateau Central moves through town in dense, hot, practical commerce.

Nearby, [Saut-d'Eau](/northamerica/thecaribbean/haiti/mirebalais/saut_deau_waterfall) is one of Haiti's most important pilgrimage sites, and [Péligre Dam](/northamerica/thecaribbean/haiti/mirebalais/peligre_dam) marks the Artibonite River's great hydroelectric intervention. Both are better understood as part of the wider Mirebalais region than as simple day-trip attractions.

As of June 2026, Haiti is under do-not-travel advisories from the United States, United Kingdom, and other governments. Mirebalais itself has been affected by the wider security crisis. This page is written for context and future use; anyone with a current reason to go needs up-to-date local security advice and serious logistical support.
