The Cathedral of Saint-Louis towers over downtown Fort-de-France in a neo-Byzantine style built largely from iron — a practical choice for a building that has been destroyed six times before the current structure was completed in 1895. The architect Henri Pick used metal frame construction throughout, giving the cathedral a lightness unusual for a building of its size and making it more resistant to the earthquakes and hurricanes that had levelled every previous church on this site.

The interior is spacious and cool — welcome relief on a hot day — with stained glass windows along the nave filtering Caribbean light into the iron structure. The building is still the seat of the Archdiocese of Fort-de-France, and masses are well-attended. The cathedral's size and position a short walk from La Savane make it a natural stop on any walk through the downtown.
