La Trinité is a small town on the Atlantic coast of [Martinique](/northamerica/thecaribbean/martinique), about 20 kilometres northeast of Fort-de-France. It sits on a wide bay facing the Caravelle Peninsula, one of the island's most striking natural areas. The town itself is a working fishing port with a relaxed, unhurried feel — far from the cruise-ship crowds of the capital.

The main reason to visit is the [Presqu'île de la Caravelle](/northamerica/thecaribbean/martinique/latrinite/presquile_de_la_caravelle), a rugged peninsula jutting into the Atlantic. The nature reserve at its tip has excellent hiking trails through dry forest and along dramatic coastal cliffs, with views across to Dominica on a clear day. The ruins of [Château Dubuc](/northamerica/thecaribbean/martinique/latrinite/chateau_dubuc), a former sugar plantation, sit at the eastern end of the trail and are worth exploring for the history and the setting.

La Trinité's waterfront has a handful of restaurants serving fresh seafood — grilled lobster and accras de morue are the local staples. The bay is popular with windsurfers and kiteboarders at [Anse de l'Etang](/northamerica/thecaribbean/martinique/latrinite/anse_de_letang), particularly from December to April when the trade winds blow steadily. If you want to see a more authentic, less polished side of Martinique, La Trinité and its peninsula are an excellent choice.
