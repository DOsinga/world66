Château Dubuc sits at the far eastern tip of the Caravelle Peninsula, a crumbling plantation house and sugar works from the early eighteenth century. The Dubuc family built it in the 1720s and ran a prosperous sugar operation here until the Revolution abolished slavery in 1794 and the estate fell into decline. What remains are the stone walls of the main house, the mill tower, several outbuildings, and a small site museum with displays on plantation life and Martinique's colonial history.

The setting is dramatic — cliffs drop to the Atlantic on three sides, and the ruined walls frame views of nothing but open ocean. The site also includes the remains of a signalling tower used to watch for approaching ships, and several sea caves that local folklore associates with piracy and slave smuggling. Allow an hour for the ruins themselves; they are reached at the end of the Caravelle Peninsula trail.
