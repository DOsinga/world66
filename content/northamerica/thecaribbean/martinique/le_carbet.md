Le Carbet is a small coastal town on the Caribbean side of [Martinique](/northamerica/thecaribbean/martinique), tucked between the sea and the green slopes of Mont Pelée. A [marker on the pier](/northamerica/thecaribbean/martinique/le_carbet/columbus_landing_marker) commemorates Columbus's landing here in 1502 — though the Carib people who were already there might have had a different view of the occasion.

The town has real character: narrow streets, the [Église Saint-Jacques](/northamerica/thecaribbean/martinique/le_carbet/eglise_saint_jacques) dating to 1645, and a string of local restaurants along the dark-sand beach where fishermen still pull in their boats. Just north of town, the [Botanical Gardens at Anse Latouche](/northamerica/thecaribbean/martinique/le_carbet/botanical_gardens_anse_latouche) occupy the ruins of a 17th-century sugar plantation, with an impressive collection of tropical plants set against the jungle-covered hillside. The painter Paul Gauguin lived briefly in Le Carbet in 1887, and the [Musée Gauguin](/northamerica/thecaribbean/martinique/le_carbet/musee_gauguin) marks his stay. Above the town, the [Carbet Falls](/northamerica/thecaribbean/martinique/le_carbet/carbet_falls) — three cascades dropping through rainforest from the Carbet Mountains — are a worthwhile half-day excursion.
