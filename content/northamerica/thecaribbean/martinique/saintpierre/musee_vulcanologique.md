The Musée Vulcanologique Frank Perret is the essential first stop in Saint-Pierre. Its collection of objects recovered from the ruins tells the story of the 8 May 1902 eruption more powerfully than any written account: clocks stopped at 8:02am, glass bottles fused and deformed by the heat, a church bell that melted and slumped out of shape, everyday kitchen items twisted beyond recognition. These are not reconstructions — they are the actual objects from the households of people who died that morning.

The museum is housed in a building that survived the eruption, which gives the displays an additional layer of unease. Photographs from before and after the disaster show a prosperous colonial city in full swing, then rubble. The explanatory panels cover the geology of the eruption, the failure to evacuate despite weeks of warning signs, and the handful of survivors — including Auguste Cyparis, who survived in a dungeon cell and later toured with Barnum & Bailey's circus as "the man who lived through doomsday." The collection is modest but well-curated. Allow an hour.
