Montserrat is a small British Overseas Territory in the eastern Caribbean, and one of the most unusual islands in the region. It was colonised largely by Irish settlers in the 17th century — an origin that has left a distinctive cultural mark, including shamrocks on the national seal and a [St Patrick's Festival](/northamerica/thecaribbean/monserrat/st_patricks_festival) unique in the Caribbean.

The island is volcanic, and the Soufrière Hills volcano has shaped modern Montserrat profoundly. In 1995–1997 a series of major eruptions buried [Plymouth](/northamerica/thecaribbean/monserrat/plymouth_buried_city), the former capital, under ash and pyroclastic flows. The southern half of the island is now an exclusion zone, and Plymouth remains a ghost town visible from the hills above — an eerie and sobering sight best seen from the viewing platform at the [Montserrat Volcano Observatory](/northamerica/thecaribbean/monserrat/montserrat_volcano_observatory). The northern part of the island, centred on [Little Bay](/northamerica/thecaribbean/monserrat/little_bay) and Brades, continues to function normally.

Montserrat is a genuinely beautiful island with forested peaks, rivers, and waterfalls. The protected rainforest of the [Centre Hills](/northamerica/thecaribbean/monserrat/centre_hills) is home to the critically endangered Montserrat oriole, found nowhere else on earth; the dark sand of [Woodlands Beach](/northamerica/thecaribbean/monserrat/woodlands_beach) and the white sand of remote [Rendezvous Beach](/northamerica/thecaribbean/monserrat/rendezvous_beach) are essentially uncrowded; and music fans can hike to the overgrown ruins of George Martin's [AIR Studios](/northamerica/thecaribbean/monserrat/air_studio_ruins), where Dire Straits, the Police, and the Stones recorded in the 1980s.

Access is by small plane from Antigua, or by ferry. The island's character — quiet, green, slightly melancholy in places — is unlike anywhere else in the region.
