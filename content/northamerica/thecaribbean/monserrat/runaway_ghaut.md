A ghaut (pronounced "gut") in Montserrat dialect is a narrow ravine cut by a seasonal stream. Runaway Ghaut is the most famous of them — a small wooded gully crossed by the main north-island road just south of Woodlands, where a year-round spring trickles down to a stone collection basin at the roadside.

The site is genuinely simple — a pull-in for two or three cars, a sign explaining the local legend, a basin of cool spring water, and the steep ravine itself disappearing into bush above and below the road. It is the kind of stop that takes ten minutes and is best treated as a wave-hello to a piece of Montserrat folklore.

The water is reputedly safe and locals drink from it; visitors who want to test the legend can do the same. The ghaut is free to visit and open at all hours.
