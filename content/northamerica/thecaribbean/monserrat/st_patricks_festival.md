Montserrat's St Patrick's Festival is a ten-day national celebration in mid-March, anchored on St Patrick's Day (17 March) and held mostly at Little Bay and across the safe northern villages. The festival mixes Irish-heritage traditions (a shamrock parade, ceilidh-style dances, masquerades), the Afro-Caribbean street carnival (steel pan, soca, calypso, the masquerade troupes), and a series of solemn events marking the failed 1768 enslaved persons' rebellion that was planned to coincide with the date.

It is by some distance the busiest week of the Montserrat year. The island fills with returning diaspora — emerald-passport-holding Montserratians from the UK, Canada, and the eastern US who time their visits to the festival — and visitor numbers spike. Hotels and guesthouses fill several months in advance.

Daytime events are scattered across the island; the main concerts run at Little Bay through the evening. The Iyanola Indigenous Day celebrations on the second weekend honour the island's pre-Columbian Kalinago history.
