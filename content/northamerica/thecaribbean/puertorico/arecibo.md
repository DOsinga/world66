Arecibo is the largest municipality by area in Puerto Rico, a coastal city on the island's north shore known for two very different attractions: the [Cueva del Indio](/northamerica/thecaribbean/puertorico/arecibo/cueva_del_indio), a sea cave with pre-Columbian Taíno petroglyphs carved into its walls, and the former [Arecibo Observatory](/northamerica/thecaribbean/puertorico/arecibo/arecibo_observator), once the world's largest single-aperture radio telescope — now a museum after its dish collapsed in 2020.

The town itself has a long history — it was the third Spanish settlement on the island, founded in the early 17th century. The old centre around the [Cathedral of St. Philip](/northamerica/thecaribbean/puertorico/arecibo/catedral_san_felipe) retains some colonial character, and the [Paseo Víctor Rojas](/northamerica/thecaribbean/puertorico/arecibo/paseo_victor_rojas) promenade along the rivermouth gives views over the rocky north coast. The [Arecibo Lighthouse](/northamerica/thecaribbean/puertorico/arecibo/arecibo_lighthouse) park is a popular family destination on the nearby headland.

The [Río Camuy Cave Park](/northamerica/thecaribbean/puertorico/arecibo/rio_camuy_caves), one of the largest cave systems in the Western Hemisphere, lies about 25 kilometres southwest — a half-day trip from the city through the impressive limestone karst interior.
