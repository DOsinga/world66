Bayamon is Puerto Rico's second largest city, sprawling across the metropolitan area just southwest of San Juan. It functions primarily as a residential and commercial suburb, but it has its own identity and a handful of attractions worth seeing if you are based in the San Juan area. The [Parque de las Ciencias Luis A. Ferré](/northamerica/thecaribbean/puertorico/bayamon/parque_de_las_ciencias) is a large science park with a small zoo, a planetarium, and open-air exhibits spread across pleasant grounds -- it is particularly good for families with children.

The historic center around the [Plaza de Recreo](/northamerica/thecaribbean/puertorico/bayamon/plaza_de_recreo) has been spruced up and has a few colonial-era buildings, including the [Casa Alcaldía](/northamerica/thecaribbean/puertorico/bayamon/casa_alcaldia) (with its distinctive overhead bridge spanning the street) and the [Invención de la Santa Cruz parish church](/northamerica/thecaribbean/puertorico/bayamon/iglesia_santa_cruz). Two small but worthwhile museums sit within a few blocks: the [Casa Museo José Celso Barbosa](/northamerica/thecaribbean/puertorico/bayamon/jose_celso_barbosa_house) honours the pioneering Afro-Puerto Rican physician and politician, while the [Dr. Agustín Stahl House](/northamerica/thecaribbean/puertorico/bayamon/agustin_stahl_house) covers the 19th-century naturalist of the same name.

The Tren Urbano connects Bayamon to San Juan, making it easy to visit without a car. The city is more suburban sprawl than charming old town, but the people are friendly, prices are lower than in tourist-heavy San Juan, and the local food scene is solid -- look for lechoneras and roadside kiosks serving comida criolla. Bayamón is also famous for [chicharrón](/northamerica/thecaribbean/puertorico/bayamon/chicharron_strip), fried pork rind sold from roadside stalls especially along the Carretera La Cuchilla.
