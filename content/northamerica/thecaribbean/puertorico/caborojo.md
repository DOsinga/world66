Cabo Rojo sits at the southwestern tip of Puerto Rico, where the island tapers into a jagged coastline of salt flats, mangroves, and wind-scoured cliffs. It is one of the less-touristed corners of the island — quieter than the east coast resorts, and all the better for it.

The old lighthouse at the very tip of the peninsula, the [Faro Los Morrillos](/northamerica/thecaribbean/puertorico/caborojo/faro_los_morrillos), is the main draw: a sturdy 19th-century structure perched above dramatic sea cliffs with sweeping views over the Caribbean. Below it, [Playa Sucia](/northamerica/thecaribbean/puertorico/caborojo/playa_sucia) is one of Puerto Rico's most beautiful and least-developed beaches. The surrounding [Cabo Rojo National Wildlife Refuge](/northamerica/thecaribbean/puertorico/caborojo/cabo_rojo_salt_flats) protects the historic salt flats — worked since the Taíno era — whose hypersaline ponds turn extraordinary shades of pink and attract serious birders. The yellow-shouldered blackbird, endangered and found almost nowhere else, nests here.

On the bay side, [Boquerón](/northamerica/thecaribbean/puertorico/caborojo/boqueron_beach) is a relaxed fishing village with a long calm beach and excellent seafood. The [San Miguel Arcángel Church](/northamerica/thecaribbean/puertorico/caborojo/san_miguel_arcangel_church) on the main plaza dates to 1783 and the town is the birthplace of Ramón Emeterio Betances, the 19th-century physician who became the foremost advocate for Puerto Rican independence.

Cabo Rojo is about 30 km south of [Mayaguez](/northamerica/thecaribbean/puertorico/mayaguez).
