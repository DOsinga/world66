Pikayo is one of the restaurants that established Puerto Rico as a serious culinary destination rather than just a place for hotel buffets. Chef Wilo Benet has been working here for decades, refining a cuisine that takes local ingredients — plantains, lechón, sofrito, chayote, fresh local fish — and applies modern technique without losing the essential flavors. The results are recognizably Puerto Rican but precisely executed, which is harder to achieve than it sounds.

The tasting menu is the right way to approach Pikayo. It runs through the seasons and the pantry in a logical progression and gives Benet's cooking the context it needs. À la carte works too but can feel incomplete. The room is quiet and well-designed, which makes it a good choice for a dinner when you want to actually have a conversation. Reservations are advisable; this is consistently one of the better-regarded restaurants on the island and fills up on weekends.
