Culebra is a small, arid island off the east coast of [Puerto Rico](/northamerica/thecaribbean/puertorico), reachable by ferry from Ceiba or by a short flight from [San Juan](/northamerica/thecaribbean/puertorico/sanjuan) — the aerial views of the cays and reefs on approach are worth the fare alone.

The island is best known for [Flamenco Beach](/northamerica/thecaribbean/puertorico/culebra/flamenco_beach), consistently ranked among the finest beaches in the Caribbean: a long crescent of white sand backed by low hills, with excellent snorkelling on the reef at either end. Beyond the beach, Culebra is a place of coral reefs, dry scrubland hills, the protected cays of the [Culebra National Wildlife Refuge](/northamerica/thecaribbean/puertorico/culebra/culebra_wildlife_refuge), and the tiny settlement of [Dewey](/northamerica/thecaribbean/puertorico/culebra/dewey), which barely qualifies as a town. A bicycle is the best way to get around — the ride from Dewey to Flamenco takes about 20 minutes.

Beyond Flamenco, the eastern beach of [Playa Zoni](/northamerica/thecaribbean/puertorico/culebra/playa_zoni) and the snorkelling at [Playa Tamarindo](/northamerica/thecaribbean/puertorico/culebra/playa_tamarindo) are well worth the trip. Water taxis from Dewey reach the uninhabited cays of [Culebrita](/northamerica/thecaribbean/puertorico/culebra/culebrita) and [Cayo Luis Peña](/northamerica/thecaribbean/puertorico/culebra/cayo_luis_pena) — for a few hours of solitude and the best snorkelling in the archipelago.
