---
title: "When To Go"
type: section
---

Puerto Rico's climate is consistently warm year-round — Gurabo, in the interior valley, is slightly cooler than the coast but still comfortably tropical. There is no bad season in the conventional sense.

Weekend afternoons, especially Sunday, are the best time to visit if you want the plaza and local restaurants at their liveliest. Local families come out after church, the restaurants do their best trade, and the town has a social energy it lacks on quiet weekdays. If you're primarily interested in hiking in the Sierra de Cayey, weekday mornings are cooler and the trails are empty.

The rainy season runs roughly from August to November. Afternoon showers are common, sometimes heavy, but mornings are usually clear. The rain passes quickly in Puerto Rico's interior. More seriously, September and October sit at the peak of hurricane season — while most visits go without incident, it's worth considering for travel planning purposes, especially if your trip would be disrupted by a weather event.

The coolest months are December to February, when the interior can feel genuinely comfortable rather than simply warm. This is a good time to visit if you find tropical heat tiring.
