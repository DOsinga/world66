The Parque de Bombas is the most photographed building in Puerto Rico and the unofficial symbol of Ponce. The red-and-black striped firehouse, built in 1882 for the city's agricultural and industrial exposition, is a riot of Victorian colour in the middle of Plaza las Delicias: Moorish arches, ornamental fretwork, two octagonal towers, and a paint scheme that makes it impossible to miss. It served as the city's main fire station until 1990 and is now a small free museum dedicated to Ponce's firefighting history.

The museum inside is modest — old equipment, photographs, uniforms — but the exterior is the real reason to visit. It sits opposite the cathedral, creating one of the more visually surprising juxtapositions in Caribbean architecture. The building is best seen in the early morning, when the plaza is quiet and the light falls on the facade without distraction, or at night when it is illuminated.
