Rincón is Puerto Rico's surf town, sitting at the island's westernmost point where the Atlantic meets the Caribbean. It earned its international reputation in 1968 when it hosted the World Surfing Championships, and surfers have been showing up ever since. The winter swells (November to April) bring consistent waves at spots like [Domes Beach](/northamerica/thecaribbean/puertorico/rincon/domes_beach) and [Tres Palmas](/northamerica/thecaribbean/puertorico/rincon/tres_palmas_marine_reserve) — the last a protected marine reserve with a reef break that ranks among the best in the Caribbean.

The town itself is spread out and low-key, without the gloss of the major Puerto Rico resort areas. A small grid of streets around the central plaza, a few dive bars, some excellent restaurants, and the old [Punta Higüero Lighthouse](/northamerica/thecaribbean/puertorico/rincon/punta_higuero_lighthouse) are the landmarks. The pace is relaxed to the point of horizontal; this is deliberately not a place for organised tours and structured sightseeing.

Beyond surfing, the area offers good diving and snorkelling on the reefs south of the lighthouse, [whale watching](/northamerica/thecaribbean/puertorico/rincon/whale_watching) from December to March when humpbacks pass through the Mona Passage on their migration south, and some of the most spectacular sunsets on Puerto Rico from the north-facing beaches and hilltops as the sun drops over the Mona Passage.

One genuinely odd attraction: the ruined dome of the BONUS nuclear reactor on the hillside north of town, decommissioned in the 1960s and never fully dismantled, which has given [Domes Beach](/northamerica/thecaribbean/puertorico/rincon/domes_beach) its name and provided surfers with a uniquely post-industrial backdrop.
