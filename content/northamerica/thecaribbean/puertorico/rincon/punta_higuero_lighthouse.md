  The lighthouse was built by the Spanish in 1892 to mark the westernmost point of Puerto Rico — a navigational necessity given the shallow reefs extending offshore. After the Spanish-American War, the US Lighthouse Service took over; the light is still operational today. The original keeper's house was demolished, but the tower survives in its original condition. Humpback whales are often visible from the promontory between December and March.

The Punta Higüero Lighthouse stands on the headland at Rincón's northwestern tip, where the Atlantic Ocean and the Caribbean Sea officially meet. The 1892 Spanish colonial tower is well-preserved and sits within a small park with good views in both directions along the coast and over the Mona Passage toward the Dominican Republic.

The lighthouse ground floor is occasionally open; the tower itself is not accessible to visitors, but the surrounding park is free to enter and makes a good sunset spot. The parking area fills up on weekends. The famous surf beaches — María's and Domes — are just south of the lighthouse point.
