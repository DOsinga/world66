The 1976 novel that broke open Puerto Rican literature — set in a single afternoon in San Juan during a colossal traffic jam, narrated through the interior monologues of half a dozen residents stuck in their cars while a salsa hit (the fictional "macho's beat" of the title) plays repeatedly on the radio. The prose is rhythmic, full of street slang, and unlike anything in the Spanish-language canon up to that point.

Reading it as a guidebook is the wrong approach — it is a difficult and stylised novel — but it captures the noise and texture of mid-century San Juan more vividly than any conventional history.
