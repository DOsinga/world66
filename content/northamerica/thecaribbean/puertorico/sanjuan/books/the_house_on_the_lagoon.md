Rosario Ferré's 1995 family saga, written in English, spans four generations of a Puerto Rican family in the twentieth century and is set partly in Condado and partly in a fictional version of San Juan high society. Class, race, politics, and the slow shift from Spanish-speaking colony to American territory are all picked apart through the rivalry between the husband who is writing the family history and the wife who is rewriting it from a different angle.

If you stay in Condado, this is the book to read on the beach. Ferré was the daughter of a Puerto Rican governor and her grasp of the island's elite is unmatched.
