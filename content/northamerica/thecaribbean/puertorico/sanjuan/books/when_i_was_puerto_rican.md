Esmeralda Santiago's 1993 memoir of growing up in rural Puerto Rico and then emigrating with her mother and siblings to Brooklyn as a teenager. The early chapters — life in the country outside San Juan in the 1950s — are some of the best writing on mid-century Puerto Rican rural life. The later chapters, on the disorientation of arriving in cold-war New York speaking no English, are the classic Puerto Rican migrant story.

A useful counterweight to the postcard San Juan, and an explanation for why so much of the island's identity is shaped by the diaspora.
