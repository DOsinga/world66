Saint Kitts and Nevis is a two-island federation in the Leeward Islands, part of the Lesser Antilles. It is one of the smallest countries in the Western Hemisphere, with a combined area of just 261 square kilometres and a population of around 42,000. The two islands are separated by a narrow channel called The Narrows, and despite their size they have distinct personalities — Saint Kitts is the larger and more developed, with the capital Basseterre and most of the tourist infrastructure; Nevis is quieter, more rural, and prized by visitors who want to get away from the busier parts of the Caribbean.

Saint Kitts was the first British and French colony in the Caribbean, colonized in the early 17th century. Sugar dominated the economy for three centuries — the landscape is still shaped by abandoned windmill towers and plantation great houses converted into small hotels. Brimstone Hill Fortress, a UNESCO World Heritage Site on the northwestern side of the island, is one of the best-preserved examples of British colonial military architecture in the Americas and worth a morning visit.

Access is by air via Robert Llewellyn Bradshaw International Airport (SKB) near Basseterre, with connections through Antigua and other Caribbean hubs, or by sea — Basseterre's deep-water port handles cruise ships regularly. Nevis has a small airfield at Newcastle (NEV). The crossing between the two islands takes about 45 minutes by ferry.
