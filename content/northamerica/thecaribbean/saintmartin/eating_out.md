---
title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

Saint-Martin has an outsized reputation for food given its size, and it is largely deserved. The French influence on the northern half is real: you will find proper bistros, serious wine lists, and precise cooking alongside Caribbean and Creole options. The island calls itself the "Culinary Capital of the Caribbean" — a claim that is slightly self-promoting but not entirely wrong.

**Grand Case** is the centre of the island's food scene: a single road running along a small beach on the French side's north coast, lined with restaurants on both sides. Prices at the sit-down restaurants are high by Caribbean standards. At the northern end of the strip, the **lolos** — open-air barbecue shacks — offer fresh fish, chicken, ribs, and rice at a fraction of the price. Both ends of the street are worth exploring.

Local Creole cooking draws on African and Caribbean heritage. Look for grilled fish, oxtail stew, rice and peas, johnny cakes (fried dough), and crab and rice — more available at lolos and local spots than at tourist-facing restaurants. Marigot has a morning waterfront market where local produce, spices, and street food are sold. Get there before 10am. A good bottle of French wine is easier to find here than almost anywhere else in the Caribbean, and more reasonably priced than you might expect.

On the Dutch side, Philipsburg and the Maho area have a large number of restaurants aimed at the cruise ship and resort market. If food matters to you, eat on the French side.
