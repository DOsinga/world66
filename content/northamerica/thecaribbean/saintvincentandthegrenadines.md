Saint Vincent and the Grenadines is a small nation in the southern Caribbean, made up of the main island of Saint Vincent and a chain of smaller islands stretching south toward Grenada. The Grenadines — Bequia, Mustique, Canouan, Mayreau, Union Island, and the uninhabited Tobago Cays among them — are among the most beautiful sailing waters in the Caribbean. The Tobago Cays, a cluster of small uninhabited islands ringed by reef, are often cited as the most idyllic anchorage in the entire region.

Saint Vincent itself is a volcanic island, high and rugged, with La Soufrière active volcano dominating the north. The island is lush, agricultural, and relatively undeveloped for tourism. Kingstown, the capital, has a compact colonial center and a busy waterfront market. The Vincentian economy has long depended on banana exports; tourism, particularly sailing, has grown to replace some of that income.

The Grenadines attract a mix of serious sailors, wealthy visitors to Mustique, and independent travellers on a budget who hop between islands on small ferries. Bequia is the most accessible of the outer islands — a short ferry from Kingstown — and has a good anchorage, local boatyards, and an easy-going character. Union Island and Mayreau are quieter still.

Getting here means flying into ET Joshua Airport outside Kingstown, with connections through Barbados, St. Lucia, or Trinidad. LIAT operates most inter-island routes; American Eagle flies direct connections from some North American cities. Between the islands, small ferries and charter boats are the standard transport.
