Charlotte Amalie is the capital of the United States Virgin Islands and the largest city in the territory, built around a deep natural harbour on the south coast of St. Thomas. It was a Danish colonial capital for 250 years before the United States bought the territory in 1917, and the Danish legacy is still legible everywhere — in the red brick and stucco buildings climbing the hillsides, in the street names (Dronningens Gade, Kongens Gade), and in [Fort Christian](/northamerica/thecaribbean/virginislands/charlotteamalie/fort_christian), the 17th-century fortress that anchors the waterfront.

The city has two faces depending on when you arrive. When cruise ships are in port — which is often — the main shopping streets fill with thousands of passengers and the duty-free shops along Main Street become a relentless commercial gauntlet. After 4pm, when the ships leave, a quieter city emerges: shaded alleyways, old warehouses with colonnaded arcades, and the kind of unhurried Caribbean pace that the crowds make impossible to find earlier. The [Charlotte Amalie Historic District](/northamerica/thecaribbean/virginislands/charlotteamalie/historic_district) is the main draw, a compact area of 18th and 19th century buildings that is one of the best-preserved Danish colonial streetscapes in the Caribbean.

Among the unusual sights: [Blackbeard's Castle](/northamerica/thecaribbean/virginislands/charlotteamalie/blackbeards_castle), a Danish watchtower built in 1679 that was given its pirate name by 20th-century tourism promoters; the [St. Thomas Synagogue](/northamerica/thecaribbean/virginislands/charlotteamalie/st_thomas_synagogue), one of the oldest in the western hemisphere, with a sand floor and an unbroken history going back to the Sephardic Jews who came from Brazil via Curaçao in the 17th century; and the [99 Steps](/northamerica/thecaribbean/virginislands/charlotteamalie/ninetynine_steps), a stairway cut from ship's ballast brick that climbs from the shopping district to the hilltop residential streets above.
