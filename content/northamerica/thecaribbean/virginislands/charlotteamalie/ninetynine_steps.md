The 99 Steps is a stairway cut from ballast brick that links the lower waterfront shopping area of Charlotte Amalie with the hillside residential streets above. It is one of several such stairways in the city — the Danes and later settlers built them throughout the 18th and 19th centuries to navigate the steep terrain — but the 99 Steps is the most famous and the most photographed.

The climb is genuinely steep, particularly in the midday heat. But it connects two quite different parts of the city: below, the tourist infrastructure of the historic district; above, a quieter residential neighbourhood of painted wooden houses with sea views. At the top, the [Blackbeard's Castle](/northamerica/thecaribbean/virginislands/charlotteamalie/blackbeards_castle) tower is a few minutes' walk. The [Government House](/northamerica/thecaribbean/virginislands/charlotteamalie/government_house) is nearby, a yellow and white classical building that has served as the official residence since Danish colonial times.
