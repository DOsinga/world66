---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

St. Thomas sits squarely in the hurricane belt. The official Atlantic hurricane season runs June through November, with the peak risk falling in August, September, and October. Storms are not certain — most years pass without a direct hit — but the possibility is real enough that travel insurance is strongly advisable if you visit in those months. Rainfall is also heavier in summer, though showers tend to be short and sharp.

The high season runs December through April, when temperatures are warm but not punishing (mid-to-upper 20s Celsius), trade winds keep the humidity in check, and the likelihood of rain is low. This is when prices peak and the cruise ship traffic is heaviest. Book accommodation well in advance.

May and November are the sweet spots for the independent traveller: shoulder-season rates, smaller crowds, and conditions that are usually still perfectly fine. June is generally manageable but marks the start of the period when you should watch the weather forecasts.
