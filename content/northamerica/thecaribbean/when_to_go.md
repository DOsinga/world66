---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

The Caribbean as a region runs on two seasons — dry and wet — but the timing, intensity, and hurricane risk vary considerably depending on where you are in the arc.

**Dry season (mid-December–April)** is peak season across almost all of the Caribbean. Lower humidity, dependable sun, calm seas, and the lowest hurricane risk. It is also the most expensive and crowded time, especially around Christmas/New Year, Presidents' Day weekend (US), and Easter.

**Shoulder (May–June and November)** is the local sweet spot — good weather, much lower prices, and the hurricane risk is still modest at the edges of the window.

**Wet/hurricane season (June–November)** peaks August–October. Direct hits on any given island in any given year remain unlikely, but the disruption can be severe when they happen. The southern Caribbean (Aruba, Bonaire, Curaçao, Trinidad) sits largely outside the hurricane belt and is the safer wet-season bet.

**Carnival** in the larger islands runs late February into March; **Trinidad's Carnival** is the regional flagship and the biggest party in the Caribbean.

For specifics — humpback whale season in the Dominican Republic, sailing weeks in Antigua and the BVI, turtle nesting in Tobago and Saint Kitts — consult the individual country pages.
