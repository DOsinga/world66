Trinidad and Tobago is the southernmost nation in the Caribbean, sitting just off the coast of Venezuela. The two islands have very different characters: Trinidad is the larger and more industrial, home to the oil refineries and natural gas facilities that have made it one of the wealthiest countries in the Caribbean. [Port of Spain](/northamerica/trinidadandtobago/port_of_spain) is the capital, best known internationally for staging one of the world's great carnivals each February, with costumes, calypso, soca, and steelpan bands that trace back to the emancipation of enslaved Africans.

[Tobago](/northamerica/trinidadandtobago/tobago) is smaller, quieter, and more reliant on tourism. Coral reefs, rainforest, and deserted beaches make it a world away from Trinidad's urban energy. The Nylon Pool — a shallow sandbar in clear water — is one of the more pleasant spots in the southern Caribbean.

Trinidad is also notable for its biodiversity: over 400 bird species, the Caroni Swamp and its scarlet ibis, and the Asa Wright Nature Centre in the Northern Range rain forest.
