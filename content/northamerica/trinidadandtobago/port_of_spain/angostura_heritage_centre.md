  Angostura Aromatic Bitters were invented in Angostura (now Ciudad Bolívar) in Venezuela in 1824 by Dr Johann Siegert, a German surgeon in Simón Bolívar's army who was experimenting with a stomach remedy for his troops. The formula was brought to Trinidad by his son Carlos in 1875. The bitters are used in tiny quantities as a flavouring agent in cocktails worldwide — including the Pink Gin, the Manhattan, and the Old Fashioned — but the label on every bottle is famously too large for the bottle, a design "mistake" made in 1906 that has been kept ever since as a brand identifier.

The Angostura Heritage Centre at the distillery in Laventille, east of Port of Spain, offers guided tours of the rum production and bottling operations and an exhibit on the history of Angostura bitters and rum. The tour ends with a tasting — worth the trip for rum enthusiasts, who will find the full range of Angostura expressions including the premium 1824 and 1787 aged rums.

Angostura has been producing rum in Trinidad since 1947 and bitters since the 1870s. The distillery makes several rum lines including the well-known white, dark, and coconut rums. Tours are available on weekdays; booking in advance is advisable as the schedule changes. The location is about 15 minutes east of downtown.
