**V.S. Naipaul, *A House for Mr Biswas* (1961)** — Naipaul's masterpiece, set in Trinidad and following the life of a Hindu Trinidadian man trying to establish an independent existence in the mid-20th century. The novel is dense with the texture of Trinidadian life, language, and social structure. Essential reading before or during any serious visit.

**V.S. Naipaul, *The Mystic Masseur* (1957)** — An earlier, lighter novel, satirising Caribbean political ambition with considerable wit. A more accessible introduction to Naipaul's Trinidad than the longer works.

**Earl Lovelace, *The Dragon Can't Dance* (1979)** — A novel of Carnival, the yards of East Dry River, steelband culture, and the gap between independence's promise and its social reality. Lovelace writes from inside the Trinidadian working-class experience in a way Naipaul rarely does.

**Derek Walcott, *Omeros* (1990)** — The Caribbean epic by the St Lucian Nobel laureate whose working life was based in Trinidad. Set across the Caribbean and using the framework of Homer, it is the most ambitious literary treatment of the post-colonial Caribbean. Long, but deeply rewarding.

**Michael Anthony, *The Year in San Fernando* (1965)** — A quieter, more domestic novel following a boy from a rural village who comes to work for a family in San Fernando. Clear-eyed and warm without sentimentality, it captures a Trinidad that has changed significantly since the 1940s.
