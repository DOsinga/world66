  The Botanic Gardens were founded in 1818, making them among the oldest in the western hemisphere. The first superintendent, David Lockhart, was a Scottish botanist who assembled a collection that was used to introduce new crops to the Caribbean region. Economic botany was the original purpose: the gardens tested whether plants from other parts of the tropics could be grown profitably in Trinidad. The Royal Botanic Gardens of Trinidad occupy about 25 hectares immediately north of the Queen's Park Savannah, making them a natural extension of an afternoon walk around the park. Founded in 1818, they are one of the oldest botanic gardens in the Americas and have a fine collection of tropical trees, palms, and flowering plants from around the world.

The gardens are well maintained and shaded, providing a cool retreat from the city heat. A Presidential House (the official residence of the President of Trinidad and Tobago) adjoins the gardens but is not open to the public. The Emperor Valley Zoo is at the garden's eastern boundary. Entry to the gardens is free; they open daily from early morning.
