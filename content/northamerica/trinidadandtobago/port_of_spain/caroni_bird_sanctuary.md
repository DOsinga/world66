  The scarlet ibis (Eudocimus ruber) is the national bird of Trinidad and Tobago and one of the most visually striking birds on earth. The colony at Caroni is thought to number around 10,000 birds. At dusk, when the ibises return from their feeding grounds in Venezuela and land in the mangroves, their brilliant red colour against the green trees is genuinely arresting — one of those natural spectacles that exceeds any photograph.

The Caroni Swamp, about 15 kilometres south of Port of Spain, is a protected wetland and mangrove forest famous for its colony of scarlet ibis. An evening boat tour through the swamp channels to watch the ibis return to roost is one of the most popular excursions from Port of Spain and one of the best wildlife experiences in the Caribbean.

Boat tours typically depart in the late afternoon (around 4pm) and return at dusk. Local guides navigate the narrow mangrove channels, pointing out caimans, herons, anacondas (if you're lucky), and other wildlife before anchoring to watch the ibis arrive in waves as the sun goes down. The birds turn from distant dots to a vivid red cloud as they land in the trees. Several operators run the tours from the sanctuary entrance; booking ahead is advisable, especially on weekends.
