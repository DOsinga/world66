The Roman Catholic Cathedral of the Immaculate Conception stands on Independence Square (the former Marine Square) on the southern edge of the downtown grid. Built in the 1830s and 1840s in Gothic Revival style, it replaced an earlier wooden church on the same site. The facade, with its twin towers and pointed arches, is the most prominent religious building visible from the waterfront.

The interior is large and cool — a relief on a hot afternoon — with good stained glass, a painted ceiling, and carved wooden pews. The cathedral is the seat of the Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Port of Spain and holds regular masses. Entry is free; visitors are welcome outside of service times.
