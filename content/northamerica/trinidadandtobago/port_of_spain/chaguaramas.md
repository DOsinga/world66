story: >
  Chaguaramas was a US military base from 1941 to 1977 — one of 99-year leases granted to the Americans by Britain in exchange for destroyers during World War II. The base became a flashpoint for the independence movement: Eric Williams demanded its return from 1956 onwards, mobilising public opinion with speeches that connected the leased territory to the broader argument for sovereignty. The Americans finally handed it back as part of a broader reorganisation of their Caribbean bases, and the area has been developed for tourism and marina facilities since.

Chaguaramas, the peninsula at the northwestern tip of Trinidad, is about 20 kilometres from Port of Spain and makes a popular half-day excursion. The area was a US military base for most of the 20th century and retains some of the infrastructure from that era; it has been developed since the 1970s as Trinidad's main sailing and yachting hub, with several marinas, boat yards, and waterfront restaurants.

The Chaguaramas Military History and Aerospace Museum in the old base camp covers the history of the US presence, the wartime Caribbean theatre, and the independence movement. The Zip-It adventure park in the hills offers zip-lines and tree-top activities. The beach at Williams Bay and the various coves along the peninsula offer swimming close to the city. Gasparee Island, just offshore, has sea caves and calm water accessible by boat from the marina.
