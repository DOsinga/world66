  Brian Lara, born in Santa Cruz in Trinidad in 1969, played his home Tests at Queen's Park Oval. In April 1994, at the Oval, he scored 375 against England to break the world record for the highest individual Test innings — a record that had stood for 36 years. He later broke his own record at the same ground, scoring 400 not out against England in 2004. The Oval has an intimate scale that brings the crowd close to the action, and watching a Test here is considered one of the best cricket-watching experiences in the world.

The Queen's Park Oval in Port of Spain is one of the great cricket grounds of the Caribbean, and a Test match here — against England or Australia — is an experience that combines serious cricket with the festive atmosphere that characterises West Indian crowds at their best. The ground is compact, with the Northern Range visible beyond the boundary, and the atmosphere in the stands is unlike any cricket ground in England or Australia.

The Oval hosts international matches when the West Indies board schedules them; the schedule is irregular and matches sell out. Day tickets are often available closer to the start of play. Even without a Test match, the ground is worth seeing from the outside — it sits in the Woodbrook/Newtown area and the facade is a handsome example of 20th-century sports architecture. The cricket-watching culture of Trinidad extends beyond the Oval: street cricket, beach cricket, and informal games in any available space are part of the city's daily life.
