Fort San Andres is the last surviving Spanish fortification in Trinidad, a small polygonal fort that once guarded the southern approach to the harbour. It dates from the late 18th century, when Trinidad was a Spanish colony, and it survived the British capture of the island in 1797 and several subsequent centuries of neglect in reasonable condition.

The fort is small — it was never intended to be a major fortification — and the surrounding area has been engulfed by the modern city. It now sits somewhat incongruously between commercial buildings near the waterfront. Interior access has varied over the years; check current status locally. The exterior is always visible from the street, and the contrast between the old Spanish stonework and the surrounding city makes for an interesting few minutes.
