---
title: Getting Around
type: section
---

Port of Spain is navigable on foot in the central areas — downtown, Woodbrook, and the Savannah are all within walking distance of each other. The city's traffic is perpetual and severe by small-island standards; driving is not recommended for visitors unfamiliar with Trinidad's road patterns.

**Taxis** are the main mode of transport for visitors. Most are unmarked private cars — Trinidadians call them "PH taxis" (private hire). Agree on the price before you get in; metered taxis are not universal. App-based services (InDrive operates in Trinidad) are increasingly common and useful for pricing transparency. The main taxi stands are in downtown, near Independence Square.

**Route taxis** (shared taxis following fixed routes) are cheap and extensive but confusing for first-time visitors. The destinations are shouted or indicated with hand signals. The main route taxi hubs are at City Gate (near South Quay) for routes south and east, and at various points on the edge of downtown for routes west and north.

**Maxi taxis** (minibuses) serve longer routes across the island. The Chaguanas and San Fernando maxis leave from City Gate. For most visitor purposes, taxis or rented cars are more practical.
