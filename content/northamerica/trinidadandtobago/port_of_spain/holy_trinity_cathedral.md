  The mahogany ceiling of Holy Trinity Cathedral is one of the finest examples of 19th-century ecclesiastical woodwork in the Caribbean. It was carved by local craftsmen and installed during the cathedral's renovation in the 1820s. The gothic hammerbeam construction is based on Westminster Hall in London, though on a smaller scale — the craftsmen worked from engraved illustrations since none of them had been to England.

Holy Trinity Cathedral is the Anglican cathedral of Trinidad, facing Woodford Square on the east side. The original building dates from 1816, making it one of the oldest churches in Port of Spain; the current structure incorporates significant Victorian-era additions. The interior is notable for its mahogany ceiling, stained glass, and a brass eagle lectern that was a gift from a wealthy Trinidadian family.

The cathedral sits in a shaded garden with several interesting memorial tablets on the exterior walls. Entry is free; opening hours vary, but the church is usually open during the day on weekdays. The exterior alone is worth a few minutes' inspection — it is one of the better-maintained historic buildings in the centre of Port of Spain.
