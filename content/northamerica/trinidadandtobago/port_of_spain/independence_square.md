  Independence Square was the site of the first formal reading of Trinidad's Act of Independence on August 31, 1962. Before independence it was called Marine Square, and before that it was the waterfront — the sea was reclaimed in the 19th century to create the downtown area south of the square. The two towers of the Eric Williams Financial Complex (the "Twin Towers") now dominate the view south, named for Trinidad's first Prime Minister and independence architect.

Independence Square is the main east-west boulevard through downtown Port of Spain — a wide, tree-lined boulevard rather than a proper square, running from the Cathedral of the Immaculate Conception in the east to the waterfront in the west. It was renamed from Marine Square at independence in 1962 and remains the symbolic centre of the city's commercial life.

The boulevard is lined with shops, fast-food restaurants, street vendors, and the kind of controlled chaos typical of a Caribbean capital's main drag. Brian Lara Promenade, the pedestrianised section named after Trinidad's legendary cricketer, runs through the middle. The area is busy during the day and quieter in the evenings. Street food vendors are particularly good here during the lunch rush.
