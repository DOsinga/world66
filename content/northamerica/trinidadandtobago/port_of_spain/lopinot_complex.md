The Lopinot Historical Complex, about 25 kilometres east of Port of Spain in the Arouca Valley, preserves a restored early 19th-century cocoa estate that was once the centre of a prosperous plantation and is now a living museum. Charles Joseph Compte de Lopinot, a French planter who fled Haiti after the revolution, established the estate in 1806 and it became one of the most productive in Trinidad during the early period of cocoa cultivation.

The estate house has been restored and now functions as a museum of the cocoa industry and plantation life. The surrounding gardens still have old cocoa trees, and the area is popular with Trinidadians for picnics on weekends. A resident family of the Parang tradition — Venezuelan-influenced Christmas music that has been practised in this valley for generations — occasionally performs; check local listings if you're visiting between September and January.
