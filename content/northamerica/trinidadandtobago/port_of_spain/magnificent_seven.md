The Magnificent Seven is the collective name for a row of seven extravagant Victorian-era mansions along the western side of Queen's Park Savannah. They were built between roughly 1902 and 1910 by Trinidad's wealthiest families, each apparently determined to outdo the others in scale and architectural ambition. The result is one of the most eccentric streetscapes in the Caribbean: a French Second Empire chateau next to a Scottish Baronial castle next to a Moorish fantasy next to a Baroque palace.

The buildings are: Stollmeyer's Castle (Scottish Baronial), Queen's Royal College (Renaissance), Hayes Court (English country house), Mille Fleurs (French inspired), Ambard's House (French Second Empire), Archbishop's House (Romanesque), and Roomor (not open to the public). Several are now government buildings or institutional premises; others have private residents. All can be seen and photographed from the Savannah road, and walking the stretch takes about 20 minutes.
