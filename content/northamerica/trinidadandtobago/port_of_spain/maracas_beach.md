Maracas Beach, on Trinidad's north coast about 45 minutes from Port of Spain over the Northern Range, is the capital's escape beach — the place Trinidadians go on weekends to swim, eat, and recover from the week. The drive over the mountains is itself part of the experience: the road climbs steeply through rainforest before descending to a long arc of pale sand backed by coconut palms.

The beach is beautiful in the standard way of Caribbean beaches, with warm clear water and regular surf. The real draw, beyond the swimming, is the **bake and shark** — shark meat fried and served in fried bread with an elaborate selection of chutneys and sauces — sold from the row of stalls at the beach's edge. Maracas shark-and-bake is a national institution, and Trinidadians who haven't visited in a while will often mention it in the same wistful tone as a missed family member.

The beach gets very crowded on weekend mornings; arrive early or visit on a weekday.
