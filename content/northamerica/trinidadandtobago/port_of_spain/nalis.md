The National Library of Trinidad and Tobago (NALIS) occupies a modern building on Hart Street near the waterfront, opened in 2003. It is a functional public library and research resource, but the building itself is worth noting: it was designed to be climate-controlled and hurricane-resistant, and the architecture reflects the challenges of maintaining a serious research collection in a tropical environment.

The library holds a significant collection of materials on Caribbean history, literature, and culture, and the special collections department is useful for anyone researching Trinidad's history. Day access is available to visitors; a temporary membership can be arranged for extended research visits. The air conditioning is a selling point in the city's heat.
