The National Museum and Art Gallery of Trinidad and Tobago occupies a handsome two-storey colonial building on Frederick Street, near the Memorial Park. Admission is free, and it is one of the more worthwhile free hours you can spend in Port of Spain.

The natural history section covers Trinidad's remarkable biodiversity — the island lies close to the South American mainland and has species from both Caribbean and South American ecosystems. The geology section documents the petroleum history that has shaped the country's economy since the early 20th century. The art gallery holds a respectable collection of Trinidadian and Caribbean painting, with particular strength in the mid-20th century. The highlight for most visitors is the Carnival section: a walk through the history of the mas tradition, steel pan, calypso, and soca, illustrated with costumes, instruments, photographs, and recordings.
