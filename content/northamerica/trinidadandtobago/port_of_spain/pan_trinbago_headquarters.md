Pan Trinbago is the governing body of steelband — the uniquely Trinidadian instrument created in Port of Spain in the 1930s and 1940s, the only new acoustic instrument invented in the 20th century. The headquarters houses a small museum tracing the history of the steel pan, from the improvised percussion instruments made from old oil drums by Afro-Trinidadian musicians in the yards of East Dry River, to the precision-tuned concert instruments used in today's Panorama competitions.

The steel pan was born in the poor districts of Port of Spain during the pre-Carnival celebrations of the 1930s and 1940s, when bamboo percussion instruments (tamboo bamboo) were banned by authorities nervous about disorder. Musicians switched to metal: first biscuit tins and old oil drums, then carefully crafted instruments with specific notes tuned by skilled craftsmen. Today the pan is the national instrument of Trinidad and Tobago, and Panorama — the national steelband competition held during Carnival — attracts massive crowds and years of preparation from the competing bands.
