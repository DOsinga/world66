story: >
  Walter Raleigh visited the Pitch Lake in 1595 and used the asphalt to caulk his ships. He noted in his journal that the lake "had no botom at the least that we could find" and described the pitch as "a most excellent good pitch... most excellent for the use of Cables and cables." The lake is thought to contain 10 million tonnes of asphalt and has been commercially exploited since the mid-19th century; it is approximately 100 metres deep in the centre and continues to replenish itself from underground petroleum deposits.
---

The Pitch Lake at La Brea in southwest Trinidad is one of the natural wonders of the Caribbean: a 40-hectare lake of natural asphalt, the largest natural occurrence of asphalt in the world. The lake surface is solid enough to walk on in most places, though there are patches of soft asphalt and the whole thing slowly heaves and shifts over months and years, occasionally revealing preserved objects from the past.

Guided tours are available and informative; a guide is essential to safely explore the more unusual parts of the lake and to understand what you're seeing. The lake's surface has a strange, moon-like quality in parts, and the smell of petroleum is constant. Tropical birds and plants grow along the edges. The lake is about two hours from Port of Spain, making it a worthwhile full-day excursion, usually combined with a visit to San Fernando.
