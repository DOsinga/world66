The rum shop is a Trinidadian institution: a small, unpretentious neighbourhood bar that typically sells rum (Angostura, the national brand), beer, and basic snacks to a local clientele. They range from tiny establishments with three stools and a single shelf of bottles to larger, more social spaces with tables outside and a sound system. Most are open from mid-morning through late evening.

Angostura rum, made in Port of Spain since 1824, is the national drink. The distillery also produces the bitters used in cocktails worldwide — Angostura Aromatic Bitters appear in nearly every bar on earth, and the plant on the east side of Port of Spain is the largest producer of cocktail bitters anywhere. A visit to the Angostura Heritage Centre (call ahead to confirm tours) gives a sense of how deeply the rum and bitters tradition is woven into Trinidad's economy and identity.
