St James is the working-class neighbourhood west of Woodbrook, known for its late-night food culture, high-energy street life, and the Western Main Road strip that never quite shuts down. It is the most authentically local of Port of Spain's neighbourhoods from a visitor's perspective — less polished than Woodbrook or St Clair, more representative of how most Trinidadians live and eat.

The Western Main Road through St James is where you come for late-night roti and doubles after the bars close, for cook shops (working-class restaurants serving daily plates of rice, peas, and meat) at lunchtime, and for the general energy of a neighbourhood that has been Port of Spain's eating and socialising hub for generations. The annual Hosay festival (a Shia Muslim commemoration adapted into a broader cultural celebration) is centred in St James and is one of the more extraordinary public events in the Caribbean — a procession of elaborately decorated "tadjahs" (replicas of Hussein's tomb) through the streets, watched by Trinidadians of all backgrounds.
