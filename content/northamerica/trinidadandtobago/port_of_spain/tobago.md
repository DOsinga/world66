Tobago, the smaller and quieter twin island of the republic, is a straightforward day trip from Port of Spain by ferry or a 20-minute flight. The contrast with Trinidad is striking: Tobago is calm, largely tourism-oriented, and heavily reliant on its spectacular beaches and reefs. Pigeon Point and Store Bay on the southwest tip are the most accessible beaches; Englishman's Bay on the north coast is wilder and less visited.

The ferry from Port of Spain takes about 2.5 hours to Scarborough and runs regularly; a fast ferry operates but schedules change — check the Water Taxi Service website before planning a trip. Caribbean Airlines flies the route in 20 minutes. A day is enough for a beach visit and a meal; two or three days gives time to explore the coral reefs (Speyside on the east coast has excellent diving), the Tobago Forest Reserve (the oldest protected forest in the western hemisphere, protected since 1776), and the historic forts around Scarborough.
