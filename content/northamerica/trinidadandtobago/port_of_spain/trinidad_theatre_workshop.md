The Trinidad Theatre Workshop was founded by the poet and playwright Derek Walcott in 1959, and it is from here that Walcott developed the work that would eventually earn him the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1992. Walcott used the Workshop to develop a distinctly Caribbean theatrical language — drawing on the oral traditions of the Eastern Caribbean, the French Creole patois of St Lucia (his home island), and the European theatrical tradition — and many of his plays, including "Ti-Jean and his Brothers" and "The Joker of Seville," were developed and first performed here.

The Workshop continues to operate and stage productions. It is not a museum in the conventional sense but rather a living artistic institution; checking their performance schedule during your visit is worthwhile. Port of Spain has a more active live arts scene than most visitors expect, and the Workshop is its most historically significant venue.
