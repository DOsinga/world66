Trinidadian food is one of the most complex and varied culinary traditions in the Caribbean, reflecting the island's multi-ethnic population. African, Indian, Chinese, Spanish, and French influences have all contributed to a cuisine that ranges from street food to elaborate Sunday lunches.

The Sunday lunch tradition in Trinidad is worth knowing about: families cook large quantities of food — rice and peas, callaloo, stewed chicken or beef, macaroni pie, fried plantain, and various vegetables — and share it with extended family and friends. If you have any Trinidadian contacts, an invitation to Sunday lunch is the single best culinary experience available. For restaurants, the best local cooking is not always in the most polished establishments: look for cook shops and informal places serving daily plates, where the food is cooked in large quantities from fresh ingredients every morning.

Specific things to eat: pelau (a one-pot rice dish with pigeon peas, meat, and coconut), buljol (salted cod with tomatoes and onions, served at breakfast), pholourie (fried split pea dumplings with mango chutney), and corn soup — a thick, spiced soup of split peas, corn, and various vegetables served from street carts, excellent after midnight.
