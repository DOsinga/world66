Woodford Square is the central public square of downtown Port of Spain, fronted by the Red House to the west and the Holy Trinity Cathedral to the east. Named after Sir Ralph Woodford, the governor who redesigned much of Port of Spain in the early 19th century, the square has been a focus of public life — and public protest — throughout the city's history.

The square is a pleasant urban park with mature trees, benches, and a small fountain, and it is busy at lunchtime with office workers from the surrounding government buildings. The political speeches and debates that have echoed here since the colonial period still occasionally materialize; it remains one of those rare urban spaces where anyone who wants to make an argument can find listeners.
