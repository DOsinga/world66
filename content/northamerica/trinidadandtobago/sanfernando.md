San Fernando is Trinidad's second city, perched on a hill in the island's industrial south. The city grew on the back of the oil and petrochemical industries that dominate this part of Trinidad, and the working rigs visible from the highway on the approach give you a clear sense of what drives the local economy. Yet San Fernando has a vitality and community pride that the oil-town label does not fully capture.

The Carnival celebrations in San Fernando rival those in Port of Spain for energy if not scale, and many Trinidadians argue that the "Sando" Carnival is more authentic and less commercialised. [San Fernando Hill](/northamerica/trinidadandtobago/sanfernando/san_fernando_hill) offers sweeping views across the southern plains to the Gulf of Paria, and the surrounding streets have a walkable, lived-in character with good roti shops and doubles vendors. The [Naparima Heritage Park](/northamerica/trinidadandtobago/sanfernando/naparima_heritage_park) and the local market are worth exploring for a sense of everyday Trinidadian life.

San Fernando also serves as a base for visiting [Pitch Lake](/northamerica/trinidadandtobago/sanfernando/pitch_lake) at La Brea, the largest natural asphalt deposit in the world — Sir Walter Raleigh caulked his ships here in 1595, and the same material later paved streets in New York and Washington D.C. The Cedros peninsula to the southwest offers quiet beaches and fishing villages where the pace of life slows dramatically.
