Pigeon Point is the most famous beach in Tobago — a long, palm-fringed stretch of white sand on the southwestern tip of the island, about 3 km from Crown Point. The iconic thatched gazebo at the end of a wooden pier has become the defining image of Tobago tourism. The beach is privately managed, with a small entrance fee that covers lifeguards, changing facilities, and beach chairs for hire.

The water here is calm and shallow, sheltered by the Buccoo Reef system to the west, making it ideal for swimming and snorkelling. The reef is visible from the shore as a dark line beneath the water. At low tide you can wade through the Nylon Pool — a shallow sandbar in the middle of the lagoon with barely knee-deep warm water, traditionally said to restore youth to anyone who bathes there. Glass-bottom boat tours to Buccoo Reef and the Nylon Pool depart regularly from the beach.
