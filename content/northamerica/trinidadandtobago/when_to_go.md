---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

Trinidad and Tobago sit at the southern edge of the Caribbean, just off Venezuela. The climate is tropical and the islands are largely below the hurricane belt — direct hits are rare, which is one of the country's quiet advantages.

**Dry season (January–May)** is the peak time. Sunny, breezy, low humidity, and aligned with the country's biggest event: **Carnival**, in the week leading up to Ash Wednesday. Port of Spain in carnival week is one of the great street parties of the world; rooms book out a year in advance and prices triple.

**Wet season (June–December)** brings short heavy afternoon showers, especially September–November. Mornings are usually clear. The wet greenery is at its best, and prices are much lower outside the holidays.

**Tobago's leatherback turtle nesting** runs March through August on the north and east beaches — Grande Riviere and Turtle Beach are prime spots, with regulated night tours.

**Diving** off Tobago is excellent year-round, but visibility is best in the dry months.

If you cannot do Carnival, target **Tobago Heritage Festival** in late July or **Divali** (October/November) for a quieter cultural window.
