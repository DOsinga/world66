The Turks and Caicos Islands are a British Overseas Territory at the southeastern end of the Bahamian archipelago. The islands are flat, low-lying, and surrounded by some of the clearest water in the Atlantic — the Caicos Bank is a vast shallow platform of vivid turquoise. Grace Bay on Providenciales is consistently rated among the world's best beaches: a long arc of fine white sand and gentle surf with good snorkelling over the reef.

Most visitors come specifically for the beach and diving: the wall diving off Providenciales and Grand Turk drops steeply into the deep Atlantic. Grand Turk, the capital, is quieter and has more local character; it was the first island Columbus may have reached in 1492, though historians still debate the claim. Salt Cay, a tiny island nearby, has well-preserved salt ponds and colonial-era windmills from when the salt trade was the territory's economy.
