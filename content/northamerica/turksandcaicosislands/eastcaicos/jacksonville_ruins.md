Jacksonville was established in the 1870s as a sisal plantation settlement. At its peak it supported several hundred workers and their families, a school, a church, and industrial buildings for processing the fibre. The plantation closed in the early 20th century when sisal prices collapsed, and the community was gradually abandoned.

Exploring the ruins today means picking through roofless stone buildings being swallowed by vegetation. The scale of what was here — and the completeness of the abandonment — makes it quietly haunting. There are no signs or interpretive boards; it's just ruins in the bush.
