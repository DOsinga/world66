Providenciales — universally called "Provo" — is the main tourist hub of the Turks and Caicos Islands, a British Overseas Territory in the northern Caribbean southeast of the Bahamas. It's best known for [Grace Bay Beach](/northamerica/turksandcaicosislands/providenciales/grace_bay_beach), a 3-km arc of white sand and luminously clear turquoise water that consistently ranks among the best beaches in the world. There's no argument: it's genuinely spectacular.

Beyond the beach, the main activities are diving and snorkelling. The [Bight Reef (Coral Gardens)](/northamerica/turksandcaicosislands/providenciales/bight_reef) starts within easy swimming distance of Grace Bay and has the densest concentration of turtles and reef fish on the island. [Smith's Reef](/northamerica/turksandcaicosislands/providenciales/smiths_reef) near Turtle Cove is larger and more varied. For serious divers, the [Caicos Wall](/northamerica/turksandcaicosislands/providenciales/caicos_wall_diving) offers vertical drop-offs with exceptional coral cover and, between January and March, humpback whales in the deep channel.

The west of the island is largely undeveloped and worth exploring. [Chalk Sound National Park](/northamerica/turksandcaicosislands/providenciales/chalk_sound) is a tidal lagoon of vivid turquoise dotted with rocky cays — best by kayak. [Sapodilla Hill](/northamerica/turksandcaicosislands/providenciales/sapodilla_hill) has centuries of sailors' carvings in the limestone. [Cheshire Hall Plantation](/northamerica/turksandcaicosislands/providenciales/cheshire_hall), managed by the national trust, is the island's most significant historical site — a reminder that Provo was once an agricultural colony, not a resort.

Providenciales is not cheap. Accommodation ranges from expensive to very expensive, and eating out is pricier than most Caribbean destinations. It's served by its own international airport with direct flights from the US, Canada, and UK.
