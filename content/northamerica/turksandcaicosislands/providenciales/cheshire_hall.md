Cheshire Hall Plantation is the most significant historical site on Providenciales, a remnant of the short-lived agricultural colony established by American Loyalists after 1776. The estate covered 5,000 acres of the island's interior and was worked by several hundred enslaved people growing cotton and sisal for export. What remains today is a substantial ruins complex — the main house walls, outbuildings, and the stone walls of the old field enclosures — set among dry forest regrown since abandonment.

The Turks and Caicos National Trust manages the site and has installed interpretive panels explaining the plantation's history, the Loyalist migration, and the lives of the enslaved workers. The ruins are an easy half-hour walk from the entrance. It is one of the few places on Provo where you can step off the beach resort circuit entirely and encounter the island's complex colonial history.
