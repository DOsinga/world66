The Fourth Avenue Historic District is one block from the Birmingham Civil Rights Institute and easy to overlook, but it rewards attention. During the Jim Crow era, Fourth Avenue North was the commercial spine of Birmingham's Black community — a self-contained economic district built because Black entrepreneurs and customers were excluded from the rest of the city. The buildings that remain, mostly from the 1910s through 1930s, include the A.G. Gaston Motel where Dr. King coordinated the 1963 campaign.

The Taste of Fourth Avenue Jazz Festival animates the district every spring. Walking the block between 16th and 18th streets, alongside the Civil Rights Institute visit, gives a fuller picture of the segregated city than the institute's exhibits alone can provide.
