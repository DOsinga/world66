The Dauphin Island Audubon Bird Sanctuary occupies 164 acres of maritime pine forest, freshwater lake, and oak woodland on the eastern end of the island. For birders, it is one of the most significant spring migration sites in North America. Songbirds flying north from the Yucatan Peninsula cross up to 600 miles of open Gulf before reaching Dauphin Island — their first landfall — and the sanctuary's trees are where they rest and refuel.

More than 345 species have been recorded on the island, including warblers, tanagers, orioles, thrushes, hummingbirds, and birds of prey. The Shell Mound area, with its cathedral-like live oak canopy, is the prime spot. Mornings in late April and early May, during active migration, can produce extraordinary numbers in a small area. The sanctuary has trails and an observation boardwalk around the freshwater lake. Admission is free; arrive at dawn for the best activity.
