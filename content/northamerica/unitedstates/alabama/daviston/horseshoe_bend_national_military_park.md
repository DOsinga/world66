The 2,040-acre park sits about 12 miles north of Dadeville along State Route 49, centered on the horseshoe-shaped bend in the Tallapoosa River where the Red Sticks made their last stand. Start at the visitor center, which has a small but well-curated museum with weapons, maps, and a fiber-optic battle map that walks you through the engagement step by step. A 20-minute film provides context on the Creek War and the broader conflict between the U.S. and indigenous nations during the War of 1812 era.

The park's 2.8-mile paved tour road loops through the battlefield with interpretive stops at the barricade site, the Gun Hill artillery position, and the river overlook where Cherokee warriors swam across to attack the Red Sticks from behind. Pull off at each stop -- the wayside exhibits are genuinely informative, not the usual generic panels. The view from the river bluff makes the Red Sticks' defensive logic immediately clear: the river wraps around three sides, leaving only a narrow neck to defend.

There is no admission fee. The park is open daily from sunrise to sunset, with the visitor center typically open 9 AM to 4:30 PM. Spring and fall are the best times to visit -- summer heat in central Alabama is brutal, and the battlefield has limited shade.
