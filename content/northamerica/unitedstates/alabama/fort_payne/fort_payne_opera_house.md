The Fort Payne Opera House was built in 1889 during the town's brief industrial boom, when speculators poured money into northeastern Alabama in hopes of founding a new industrial city. The building that resulted is far more elaborate than a town this size would normally produce — a Romanesque Revival structure with ornate stonework and a corner tower that still stands out on Gault Avenue North.

The opera house has survived various uses over the decades and is a National Historic Landmark. It's best appreciated from the outside as part of a walk through the Boom Town Historic District downtown, where several other well-built commercial buildings from the same period have also survived.
