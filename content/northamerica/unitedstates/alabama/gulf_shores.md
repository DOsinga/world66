Gulf Shores is Alabama's best-known beach town, sitting where Highway 59 runs into the Gulf of Mexico. It has the expected run of condos, seafood places, souvenir shops, and family amusements, but the reason it clears the travel bar is not the strip. It is the mix of white sand, protected dunes, pine woods, and bird-rich coastal wetlands that sits around it.

The easiest day starts on the sand at [Gulf Shores Public Beach](/northamerica/unitedstates/alabama/gulf_shores/gulf_shores_public_beach), then moves east into [Gulf State Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/alabama/gulf_shores/gulf_state_park), where paved trails and boardwalks cut through nine coastal ecosystems. Go west instead and the coast gets quieter around [Bon Secour National Wildlife Refuge](/northamerica/unitedstates/alabama/gulf_shores/bon_secour_national_wildlife_refuge), one of the best places on this part of the Gulf to see dunes, maritime forest, turtles, and migratory birds.

Gulf Shores is not a subtle place in high summer. Traffic stacks up, restaurants fill, and the central beach can feel busy from breakfast until sunset. Come outside peak weeks, rent a bike for the backcountry trails, or spend a morning on the Fort Morgan peninsula and the town makes a better case for itself than its skyline suggests.
