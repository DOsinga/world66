Montgomery is Alabama's capital and one of the most historically significant cities in the American South. It was the first capital of the Confederacy, and a century later it became a cradle of the civil rights movement. Rosa Parks was arrested here in 1955 for refusing to give up her bus seat, sparking the Montgomery Bus Boycott led by a young Martin Luther King Jr. from the [Dexter Avenue Baptist Church](/northamerica/unitedstates/alabama/montgomery/dexter_avenue_baptist_church).

The city's civil rights landmarks are its main draw. The [Rosa Parks Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/alabama/montgomery/rosa_parks_museum), the [Dexter Parsonage Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/alabama/montgomery/dexter_parsonage_museum) (King's home during the boycott), and the [National Memorial for Peace and Justice](/northamerica/unitedstates/alabama/montgomery/national_memorial_for_peace_and_justice) -- the country's first memorial dedicated to victims of lynching -- are all essential visits. The [Alabama Shakespeare Festival](/northamerica/unitedstates/alabama/montgomery/alabama_shakespeare_festival), located in the Blount Cultural Park, is one of the largest Shakespeare festivals in the world and runs year-round.

Montgomery is also where Hank Williams is buried, and the [Hank Williams Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/alabama/montgomery/hank_williams_museum) downtown houses a large collection of memorabilia, including the powder-blue Cadillac he died in. Interstate 65 through the city is officially named the Hank Williams Lost Highway.
