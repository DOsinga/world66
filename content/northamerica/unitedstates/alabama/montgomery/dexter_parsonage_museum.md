The modest white-frame house where Martin Luther King Jr. lived with his family from 1954 to 1960 has been preserved as it was during his pastorate. Most of the furniture and books in the study, kitchen, and bedrooms belonged to the Kings.

Hour-long guided tours (mornings and early afternoons, Tuesday through Saturday) cover the parsonage and the small Interpretive Center next door, which sketches the bus boycott and the broader civil rights movement using artefacts and photographs from local families. Combine with the Dexter Avenue Baptist Church a few blocks away.
