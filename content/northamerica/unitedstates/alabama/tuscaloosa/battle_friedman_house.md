  The Battle-Friedman House was built in 1835 by a wealthy planter and served as headquarters for Union forces during their occupation of Tuscaloosa in 1865. The irony was not lost on later generations: a house built on the profits of enslaved labour, occupied by the army that ended slavery. The house was later donated to the city and has been a museum since 1965.
---

The Battle-Friedman House is a Greek Revival mansion built in 1835 and one of the best-preserved antebellum homes in Alabama. It is furnished with period pieces and presents a picture of prosperous Tuscaloosa life in the decades before the Civil War. The house is open for tours several days a week.

The building is part of the city's Historic Preservation Society and sits in a neighbourhood of other 19th-century properties that give some sense of what the city looked like before its industrial and academic expansion. The grounds include a formal garden.
