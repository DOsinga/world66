Tuscumbia is a small town in the Shoals area of northwest Alabama with a historic downtown full of family-owned shops and buildings that have stood for over a century. It is best known as the birthplace of Helen Keller.

[Ivy Green](/northamerica/unitedstates/alabama/tuscumbia/ivy_green), the Keller family home built in 1820, is open to visitors year-round. The original pump — where Anne Sullivan's famous breakthrough with the young Helen Keller took place in 1887 — still stands in the garden. The annual Helen Keller Festival in late June brings performances of "The Miracle Worker" to the grounds.

The [Alabama Music Hall of Fame](/northamerica/unitedstates/alabama/tuscumbia/alabama_music_hall_of_fame) is also in Tuscumbia, honouring over 500 musicians from the state. Combined with nearby [Florence](/northamerica/unitedstates/alabama/florence), the Shoals area offers a surprising concentration of cultural attractions — FAME Studios and the Muscle Shoals Sound Studio, where Aretha Franklin and the Rolling Stones recorded, are just a few miles away across the river.
