  The Shoals area produced a disproportionate share of American pop and soul music from the 1960s through the 1980s. Artists as varied as Aretha Franklin, the Rolling Stones, Paul Simon, and Percy Sledge recorded in the small studios of Muscle Shoals. The relaxed, funky groove those sessions produced became known as the "Muscle Shoals sound" — a distinctive blend of Southern soul and gospel that influenced popular music worldwide.
---

The Alabama Music Hall of Fame honours over 500 musicians from the state across rock, rhythm and blues, gospel, country, opera, and contemporary music. The 12,500-square-foot facility opened in 1990 and is the most comprehensive survey of Alabama's extraordinary contribution to American music.

The exhibits trace the careers of Alabama-born inductees from the early blues era to the present day, with instruments, memorabilia, recording equipment, and audio-visual displays. The Hall is particularly strong on the Shoals music scene of the 1960s and 1970s, when the area became a recording destination for some of the biggest names in soul and rock. The building's design incorporates elements referencing the music industry, and the exhibits are well-organised for both the casual visitor and the music enthusiast.
