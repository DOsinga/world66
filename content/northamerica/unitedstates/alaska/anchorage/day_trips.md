The real Alaska is outside the city, and Anchorage is well placed for day trips in three directions.

**South along the Seward Highway** is the most rewarding drive in the area. The road runs along Turnagain Arm with regular pullouts for beluga whales and Dall sheep on the cliffs above. **Beluga Point**, **Bird Point**, and the **Turnagain Arm Bore Tide** are all worth stopping for. **Girdwood**, an hour out, has the Alyeska tram (year-round) and good food at the bottom of the mountain. **Portage Glacier**, a little further, has been retreating for decades but the Begich-Boggs visitor centre and a boat trip across Portage Lake bring you close. Push another two hours and you reach [Seward](/northamerica/unitedstates/alaska/seward) and Kenai Fjords National Park; the boat trips out of Seward harbour to see calving glaciers, orcas, and sea otters are among the best wildlife experiences in Alaska.

**North on the Glenn Highway** takes you to **Eklutna**, with its historic Russian church and the famous painted spirit houses; then on to **Wasilla**, **Palmer**, and the **Matanuska Glacier** where you can walk on the ice with a guide.

**North on the Parks Highway** leads to **Talkeetna**, two and a half hours out, the historic climbing town for Denali and a charming place for an afternoon — and the gateway to Denali National Park itself if you have a longer day.

The **Alaska Railroad** makes some of these trips doable without a car. The summer day trip to Seward is particularly good — train both ways, six hours on the boat in the middle.
