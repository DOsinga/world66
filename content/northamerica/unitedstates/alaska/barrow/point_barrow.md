  The aviator Wiley Post and the humorist Will Rogers died in 1935 when their float plane crashed into a lagoon a few miles short of Point Barrow, attempting to fly to Russia via the Arctic. The town's airport carries both their names, and a memorial near the crash site is one of the few formal markers anywhere on the slope.

Point Barrow itself, the northernmost point of the United States, sits about twelve miles up the spit of land north of town. Reaching it almost always means joining one of the local tundra tours that run from spring into autumn — the road is rough, the polar bears are real, and self-drive is strongly discouraged. The point itself is unmarked: a low spit of gravel where the Chukchi and Beaufort seas meet.

The drive crosses tundra, gas wells, and an Air Force radar installation. Polar bears are routine sightings here in late summer when they come close to shore to scavenge whale carcasses from the spring hunt. A licensed guide is the only sensible way to make the trip.
