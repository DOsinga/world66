---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

The decisive question is light, not warmth. Between mid-May and early August the sun never sets — 82 continuous days of midnight sun. Between mid-November and late January it never rises — 65 days of polar night, lit by a few hours of twilight. The aurora borealis is visible from August to April when the sky is dark enough.

**Summer (June–August)** is the easiest visit: temperatures hover between 35 and 50°F (2–10°C), the sun is up around the clock, and the bowhead whaling festival of Nalukataq is held in late June. The Arctic Ocean is largely frozen until mid-July. **Winter (November–March)** is when you go for the polar night and the strangeness — temperatures drop to -30°F (-34°C), windchill far lower, and the locals run skidoos over the frozen sea. **Spring (April–May)** is the bowhead hunting season, when crews are out on the ice and the village turns inward; it is also a great time for the aurora.
