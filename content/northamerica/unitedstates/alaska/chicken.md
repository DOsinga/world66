Chicken is barely a village, but it has the kind of Alaskan identity that makes a detour stick in your head. It sits on the remote Taylor Highway, close to the Canadian border and the Fortymile gold country, with a seasonal cluster of businesses, a dirt airstrip, and a name that began, according to local legend, because miners could not agree how to spell ptarmigan.

The main reason to stop is the gold-rush landscape. The [Pedro Gold Dredge](/northamerica/unitedstates/alaska/chicken/pedro_gold_dredge) is one of Alaska's most complete surviving dredges, and local claims still let visitors try recreational gold panning. The old schoolhouse story, the [Chicken Creek Cafe and saloon](/northamerica/unitedstates/alaska/chicken/chicken_creek_cafe), and the annual Chickenstock festival round out the place's oddball charm.

Chicken is seasonal and weather-dependent. The Taylor Highway is not maintained through winter, and services are limited even in summer. Treat it as a characterful stop on an Alaska road trip, not a town that will solve your logistics.
