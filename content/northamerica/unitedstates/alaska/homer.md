Homer sits at the end of Sterling Highway on the south shore of the Kenai Peninsula, where the land runs out into Kachemak Bay. Locals call it "the cosmic hamlet by the sea" and also "a drinking town with a fishing problem" — both are accurate. The town has a bohemian streak unusual for Alaska, a serious arts scene, and more espresso cafes per capita than you might expect this far north.

The [Homer Spit](/northamerica/unitedstates/alaska/homer/homer_spit), a 7-kilometre sandbar jutting into the bay, is the town's social centre: ferry terminal, marina, campground, [seafood restaurants](/northamerica/unitedstates/alaska/homer/seafood_restaurants), galleries, and charter fishing boats. [Halibut fishing](/northamerica/unitedstates/alaska/homer/halibut_fishing) is the main draw for visitors — the bay is productive and the catch can be enormous. Bald eagles are a constant presence along the waterfront, more interested in fish scraps than in spectators. At the end of the Spit, the legendary [Salty Dawg Saloon](/northamerica/unitedstates/alaska/homer/salty_dawg_saloon) — a shack wallpapered in dollar bills — is where every fishing story gets told.

The [Pratt Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/alaska/homer/pratt_museum) on Pioneer Avenue is the town's cultural anchor, with exhibits on local ecology, indigenous cultures, and Kenai Peninsula natural history, plus a botanical garden. Water taxis run from the Spit across the bay to [Kachemak Bay State Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/alaska/homer/kachemak_bay_state_park), which has glaciers, tide pools, sea otters, and hiking trails accessible only by boat.
