---
title: Day Trips
type: section
---

Homer's position at the end of the road and the edge of the bay makes it a natural base for exploring further. The two most important day-trip destinations require a boat. Both are worth doing if the weather cooperates.

**Kachemak Bay State Park** is the obvious first trip — a water taxi from the Spit marina drops you at a trailhead in 20–45 minutes and picks you up later the same day. The Grewingk Glacier Trail is the most popular; the Saddle Trail offers harder terrain and bigger views. Tide pool beaches at low water are excellent for wildlife.

**Halibut Cove** is an art community of about 20 residents on an island across the bay, accessible only by the Danny J ferry, which makes a lunch run and an evening run from the Spit in summer. The town has no cars, boardwalks instead of roads, a good restaurant, and several galleries. The evening trip includes dinner; reserve ahead.

**Seldovia** is a small fishing town further around the bay, quieter and more lived-in than Homer. The Alaska Marine Highway ferry calls here, or water taxis make the run in season. Seldovia has a lovely boardwalk along the slough, a couple of good seafood restaurants, and very few visitors.

For those with a rental car, the **Kenai Peninsula** is one long potential day trip: Soldotna and Kenai (salmon fishing towns), Seward (Kenai Fjords National Park and Exit Glacier), and Homer's own backcountry above the bluff are all within a few hours. The Sterling Highway back toward Anchorage passes through excellent moose habitat — you are almost certain to see moose if you drive it slowly.
