The Totem Heritage Center houses thirty-three original nineteenth-century totem poles retrieved from abandoned Tlingit and Haida villages in the 1970s. These are not replicas and not reconstructions — they are the actual poles, weathered and cracking, displayed under a roof to slow their decay. The oldest are thought to date to the 1820s, making them among the earliest surviving examples of Northwest Coast monumental art anywhere.

The scale and condition of the poles is startling. Preservation was deliberate: the programme chose to stabilise and display rather than fully restore, so the colours are faded, the wood worn, and the carvings have the patina of real age rather than the brightness of new work. The interpretive material is good. Allow at least an hour. The centre also runs carving demonstrations in summer, and hosts the Biennial Celebration of Northwest Coast Native Art.
