Kodiak is the main town on Kodiak Island, a green, rain-washed chunk of Alaska in the Gulf of Alaska. It is a working fishing port first and a visitor destination second, which is part of its appeal: boats, canneries, floatplanes, Russian Orthodox history, Alutiiq culture, and bear-country wilderness all press close together.

The town itself has enough for a strong day. [Alutiiq Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/alaska/kodiak/alutiiq_museum) and [Kodiak History Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/alaska/kodiak/kodiak_history_museum) tell the deeper story of the island, while [Holy Resurrection Cathedral](/northamerica/unitedstates/alaska/kodiak/holy_resurrection_cathedral) keeps the Russian Orthodox thread visible in the centre of town. A few miles out, [Fort Abercrombie State Historical Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/alaska/kodiak/fort_abercrombie_state_historical_park) mixes Second World War ruins with spruce forest and cliffs over the ocean.

Kodiak National Wildlife Refuge covers much of the island beyond town, and its famous brown bears are the reason many travellers know the name. Seeing them properly takes planning, weather luck, and often a floatplane, but you do not need a bear-viewing trip for Kodiak to be worthwhile. The place has its own strong identity before you ever leave the harbour.
