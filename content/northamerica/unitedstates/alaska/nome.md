Nome sits on the Bering Sea at the edge of northwest Alaska, far beyond the connected road system. You fly in, then find a small city with a big frontier past: gold on the beaches, the 1925 serum run, the finish of the Iditarod, and a strong Alaska Native presence.

The town is remote but not empty. The [Carrie M. McLain Memorial Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/alaska/nome/carrie_mclain_museum) and [Katirvik Cultural Center](/northamerica/unitedstates/alaska/nome/katirvik_cultural_center) give useful context, while the [Bering Land Bridge Heritage Center](/northamerica/unitedstates/alaska/nome/bering_land_bridge_heritage_center) explains the vast preserve north of town. In March, the Iditarod finish turns Front Street into the end point of Alaska's most famous race.

Nome is also a base for independent drives on gravel roads that simply run out into tundra, coast, and old mining country. It is expensive and weather-dependent, but it has a stronger sense of place than many larger Alaska towns.
