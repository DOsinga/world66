Petersburg is the "Little Norway" of Alaska, a small fishing town on Mitkof Island in the Inside Passage. It was founded by a Norwegian immigrant and still celebrates its heritage with Norwegian Independence Day parades on May 17th and dances at the [Sons of Norway Hall](/northamerica/unitedstates/alaska/petersburg/sons_of_norway_hall).

The town is surrounded by the Tongass National Forest, with glaciers, whales, and bears within reach by charter boat. [LeConte Glacier](/northamerica/unitedstates/alaska/petersburg/leconte_glacier), the southernmost tidewater glacier in North America, calves icebergs into Frederick Sound just to the north. In summer, humpback whales feed in the waters nearby. The commercial fishing fleet — salmon, halibut, shrimp — still dominates the harbour and the local economy.

The [Hammer Slough](/northamerica/unitedstates/alaska/petersburg/hammer_slough) district — cannery buildings and fish houses on pilings reflected in the tidal channel — is the most photographed corner of town, and deservedly so. The [Clausen Memorial Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/alaska/petersburg/clausen_memorial_museum) covers the town's Tlingit and Norwegian history in compact form. [Eagle Roost Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/alaska/petersburg/eagle_roost_park) at the north end of town is one of the easiest places in Southeast Alaska to watch bald eagles at close range.

Petersburg sees about 40,000 visitors a year, far fewer than Juneau or Ketchikan. It is a working town rather than a cruise port, and that is part of its appeal. The population hovers around 3,000, swelling in summer when the canneries hire seasonal workers.
