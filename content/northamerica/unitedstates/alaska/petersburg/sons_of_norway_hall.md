The Sons of Norway Hall, known officially as Fedrelandet Lodge No. 23, is the social and cultural heart of Petersburg's Norwegian heritage community. The two-storey building on Indian Street dates to 1912 and features traditional Norwegian rosemaling decorations on its facade — the distinctive folk-art floral painting that sets it apart from every other building in the Inside Passage.

The hall hosts the annual Little Norway Festival on the weekend nearest May 17th, Norwegian Constitution Day. The festival fills the streets with Viking pageants, folk dancing, seafood dinners, and the kind of exuberant community spirit that only a small, tightly knit town can produce. If you happen to be in Petersburg on a regular weekend, ask locally whether a dance or community event is scheduled — the hall is worth seeing in use rather than just from outside.

story: "The Fedrelandet Lodge was established in 1910 by Norwegian immigrants who had followed their fishing skills and their tradition of cooperative labor to the Inside Passage. Today it remains an active fraternal organisation, making it one of the oldest continuously operating Sons of Norway lodges in Alaska."
