---
title: Things to Do
type: section
---

Petersburg rewards visitors who want something other than a cruise-ship port experience. The town is accessible enough to be comfortable but remote enough to feel genuinely Alaskan. The commercial fishing harbour dominates the waterfront — you can watch the fleet come and go, see boats being worked on, and get a sense of the industry that has sustained this community for over a century.

The [Hammer Slough](/northamerica/unitedstates/alaska/petersburg/hammer_slough) area along the tidal channel is the most photogenic corner of town, with weathered buildings on pilings reflected in the water at high tide. The [Sons of Norway Hall](/northamerica/unitedstates/alaska/petersburg/sons_of_norway_hall) is the cultural anchor of the Norwegian community, worth visiting especially during the Little Norway Festival in May. The [Clausen Memorial Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/alaska/petersburg/clausen_memorial_museum) covers Tlingit heritage, the Norwegian founding, and the cannery era in compact and well-curated form.

Outside of town, the main draws are accessible by charter boat: [LeConte Glacier](/northamerica/unitedstates/alaska/petersburg/leconte_glacier) calves icebergs into Frederick Sound to the north, and humpback whales feed in the waters between the islands throughout summer. [Eagle Roost Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/alaska/petersburg/eagle_roost_park) at the north end of town gives close-up views of bald eagles without leaving the pavement. The [Raven Trail](/northamerica/unitedstates/alaska/petersburg/raven_trail) through the Tongass National Forest offers ridge views and typical Southeast Alaska forest on foot.

The Crystal Lake Salmon Hatchery, a few kilometres out the road, is open to visitors during salmon season and gives a good introduction to how Alaska manages its fisheries.
