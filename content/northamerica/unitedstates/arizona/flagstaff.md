Flagstaff sits at 2,100 metres in the ponderosa pines of northern Arizona, a college town with a mountain-west character that feels nothing like the desert cities to the south. It is the gateway to the [Grand Canyon](/northamerica/unitedstates/arizona/grandcanyon) — the South Rim is about 80 miles north — and a good base for exploring the red rocks of [Sedona](/northamerica/unitedstates/arizona/sedona), the Navajo Nation, and the volcanic landscapes around Sunset Crater.

Northern Arizona University gives the town a youthful energy, and the [historic downtown](/northamerica/unitedstates/arizona/flagstaff/downtown_flagstaff) along Route 66 has good restaurants, brewpubs, and independent shops. In winter, the [Arizona Snowbowl](/northamerica/unitedstates/arizona/flagstaff/arizona_snowbowl) ski area on the San Francisco Peaks draws skiers from across the state. [Lowell Observatory](/northamerica/unitedstates/arizona/flagstaff/lowell_observatory), where Clyde Tombaugh discovered Pluto in 1930, sits on a hill above town and is open for public stargazing. [Walnut Canyon National Monument](/northamerica/unitedstates/arizona/flagstaff/walnut_canyon) and [Wupatki National Monument](/northamerica/unitedstates/arizona/flagstaff/wupatki_national_monument) are both within an hour's drive and contain excellent ancient Sinagua and Ancestral Puebloan sites.

The climate is a welcome relief from the Arizona heat — summer highs hover around 27°C, and winters bring real snow. If you are driving between Phoenix and the Grand Canyon, Flagstaff is the natural place to stop.
