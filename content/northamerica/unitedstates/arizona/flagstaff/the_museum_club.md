story: >
  The Museum Club started life in 1931 as a taxidermy shop and natural history curiosity cabinet run by Dean Eldredge, who filled the massive log building with mounted animals, Native American artifacts, and oddities. After Eldredge died in 1936, the building became a bar and dance hall. The mounted animals stayed. They are still there.
---

The Museum Club, known locally as "The Zoo," is one of the most iconic roadhouses in the American Southwest. The building is a giant log cabin constructed from five intact ponderosa pine trees, their trunks incorporated as pillars, and it sits right on Route 66 on the east side of downtown. Country music, cold beer, and a mounted elk head the size of a small car above the bar.

The Club has hosted legends of country music through the decades and still books live acts most weekends. It is a genuine historic roadhouse, not a themed recreation — the worn floorboards, the animal mounts, and the Route 66 neon are the real article. If you are passing through Flagstaff and want one place to stop for a drink with character, this is it.
