story: "The South Kaibab Trail was built by the National Park Service in 1924–1925, partly to break the monopoly that Ralph Cameron held over the Bright Angel Trail. Cameron had homesteaded the trailhead in 1903 and charged his own toll for years before the park wrestled control from him — a battle that went to the US Supreme Court. The new trail was deliberately routed along a ridge rather than a drainage, giving unobstructed views, but this also means no shade and no water sources anywhere on the route."

Steeper and more exposed than Bright Angel, but arguably the most rewarding trail in the park for the views. The South Kaibab follows a ridge rather than a drainage, which means unobstructed panoramas in every direction. The descent to **Ooh Aah Point** (1.8 km, 183 m descent) is one of the best short hikes in the canyon. **Cedar Ridge** (2.4 km) is a good turnaround for day hikers, with an outhouse and extraordinary views. Beyond Cedar Ridge, the trail drops steeply to **Skeleton Point** (6.4 km), where you can see the river.

There is no water on this trail and very little shade. Bring at least a litre per hour in summer. The trailhead is at Yaki Point, accessible only by shuttle bus from the South Rim Visitor Center (the access road is closed to private vehicles). No mules descend South Kaibab; only ascend.
