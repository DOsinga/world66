The most popular below-rim trail at the Grand Canyon, dropping from the South Rim trailhead near Bright Angel Lodge to the Colorado River over 9.5 miles. The descent passes two rest stations with water (available May to September) at Mile-and-a-Half and Three-Mile, and the shaded oasis of Indian Garden at 4.6 miles, where a creek runs year-round and cottonwood trees provide genuine shade. Day hikers typically turn around at Indian Garden or at Plateau Point (6.1 miles), which offers a direct view down to the river. Do not attempt to hike to the river and back in a single day — the heat and elevation gain on the return are more demanding than most people expect, and the park service is serious about this warning.

story: >
  The trail follows a natural fault line that Havasupai people used for centuries to access the canyon floor. When Fred Harvey's company began operating tourist facilities on the South Rim in the early 1900s, they paved and formalized the path. The two rest houses were built in the 1930s by the Civilian Conservation Corps.
