---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

Spring (March to May) and autumn (September to November) are the best seasons. Temperatures are comfortable on the rim, trail conditions are good, and the light — especially at dawn and dusk — is spectacular. Autumn is slightly less crowded than spring.

Summer (June to August) is the most popular and most brutal time. The South Rim sits at 7,000 feet and stays relatively cool, but temperatures in the canyon interior regularly exceed 110°F (43°C). Most heat-related rescues happen between May and September. If you visit in summer, hike in the early morning, carry far more water than you think you need, and never attempt to hike to the river and back in a single day.

Winter (November to February) brings cold temperatures and occasional snow to the rim, which is dramatically beautiful and much less crowded. The South Rim remains open year-round; the North Rim closes in mid-November and reopens in mid-May. Winter hiking below the rim is easier on the body — the inner canyon temperatures are mild — though icy conditions on upper trails require caution.

Sunrise and sunset are worth planning around at any time of year. The canyon's layered geology changes colour dramatically with the angle of light, and the first hour after dawn or before dusk produces views that midday flat light simply cannot match.
