---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

**Spring (March–May)** is the best time to visit. Days are warm and clear, the trails are passable, wildflowers bloom on the rim, and the crowds — while building — have not yet reached summer levels. Temperatures below the rim are comfortable for hiking in April and early May before the heat sets in.

**Summer (June–August)** brings the peak crowds and dangerous heat. The South Rim sits at 2,100 metres (6,900 ft), so rim temperatures are pleasant — 20–27°C — but the inner canyon regularly exceeds 40°C at river level. Hiking below the rim in summer is dangerous without very early starts and large quantities of water. The North Rim is open from mid-May through mid-October and is cooler and much less crowded. Afternoon thunderstorms are common in July and August.

**Autumn (September–October)** rivals spring as the best time to visit. The summer crowds have thinned, temperatures are cooling, and the canyon light in October is exceptional. Below-rim hiking becomes safe again in September as temperatures moderate.

**Winter (November–February)** brings snow to the rim and a dramatic drop in visitors. The South Rim stays open year-round, though some facilities close. The canyon walls dusted with snow are beautiful. The North Rim closes to vehicles from mid-October and is inaccessible in winter. Ranger programmes continue year-round.
