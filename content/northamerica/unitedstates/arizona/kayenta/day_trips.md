---
title: "Day Trips"
type: section
---

Kayenta's position in the Navajo Nation puts several remarkable destinations within reach.

**Navajo National Monument** is 30 miles southwest on US-160, then north on AZ-564. The monument protects Betatakin and Keet Seel, two of the largest and best-preserved Ancestral Puebloan cliff dwellings in the Southwest. Betatakin can be seen from the rim on a ranger-led tour; Keet Seel requires an 8-mile hike and an advance permit, but rewards the effort with an atmosphere of extraordinary solitude.

**Monument Valley's northern sector** across the Utah border can be explored by continuing north on US-163 beyond the park entrance. The view of the Mittens from the roadside pullout just north of the Arizona-Utah line is one of the most famous in America — and it's free.

**Four Corners Monument** is about 75 miles east on US-160 and US-491. The only point in the United States where four states meet (Arizona, Utah, Colorado, New Mexico) is administered by the Navajo Nation. It's a bit of a tourist cliché, but the drive through the Lukachukai Mountains and the Carrizo plateau is superb.

**Canyon de Chelly National Monument**, roughly 90 miles southeast near Chinle, contains ancient ruins tucked into soaring red sandstone canyons. You can walk to the White House Ruin overlook independently; all other access is with a Navajo guide.
