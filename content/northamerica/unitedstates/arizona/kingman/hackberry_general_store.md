  The store at Hackberry sat shuttered for years after the interstate killed traffic on this stretch of Route 66 in the 1970s. In 1992 the artist Bob Waldmire — son of the Springfield, Illinois inventor of the Cozy Dog corn dog — bought the derelict building and turned it into an unofficial Route 66 visitor centre, hand-painting signs and filling the yard with vintage cars. His descendants now run it as a working store and unofficial museum.
---

About 35 kilometres northeast of Kingman, the Hackberry General Store is the most photographed building on the western stretch of Route 66. The wooden porch sags under a thousand bumper stickers, a 1957 red Corvette is permanently parked out front, and the back yard is a graveyard of pickups, gas pumps, and faded enamel signs.

You can buy cold drinks, ice cream, and Route 66 souvenirs inside. It is just touristy enough to be fun and just authentic enough to be charming. Combine it with a drive along the Hackberry-Peach Springs stretch of old Route 66 for the trip's best windscreen scenery.
