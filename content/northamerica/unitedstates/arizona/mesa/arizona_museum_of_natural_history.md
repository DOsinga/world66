The Arizona Museum of Natural History anchors Mesa's downtown arts district at 53 N MacDonald and is the best reason to spend a half-day in the city centre. Its Dinosaur Mountain gallery is the headline: a full three-storey walk through a recreated canyon where life-size animatronic dinosaurs inhabit Arizona's prehistoric landscape. It is dramatic enough to hold adults as well as children.

Beyond the dinosaurs, the museum covers the deep human history of the region more seriously than most. The exhibit on the Hohokam — the ancient people who built an extraordinary irrigation canal system across the Sonoran Desert long before European contact — is particularly strong, providing context for the Mesa Grande site that sits nearby. There are also rooms dedicated to the Spanish colonial period and Arizona's territorial era.

The museum is open Tuesday through Sunday. Check the website for current hours and any temporary exhibitions. Admission is modest; parking in the adjacent garage is validated.
