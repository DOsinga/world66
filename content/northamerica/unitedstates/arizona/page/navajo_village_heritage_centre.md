  Page sits entirely within the Navajo Nation — the largest Native American reservation in the United States, covering parts of Arizona, Utah, and New Mexico. The town itself was built on land leased from the Navajo by the federal government for the dam's construction. The arrangement has always been complicated, and the complex relationship between the town, the dam, and Navajo sovereignty continues to shape local politics.
---

The Navajo Village Heritage Centre offers an introduction to Navajo culture and history for visitors who want more context than a slot canyon tour provides. The centre includes demonstrations of traditional crafts, cultural presentations, and a reconstructed Navajo village setting that gives some sense of the pre-reservation way of life.

The Navajo Nation has a rich material culture — weaving, silversmithing, and sand painting are the best known — and Page's shops and galleries include both genuine Navajo-made work and tourist imitations. The Heritage Centre can help you tell the difference. Check the centre's opening hours before visiting, as they can vary seasonally.
