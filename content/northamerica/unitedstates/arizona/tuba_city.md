Tuba City — Tó Naneesdizí, "tangled water," in Navajo — sits in the western half of the Navajo Nation, on a high windy plateau between the Grand Canyon and the Painted Desert. With around 9,000 residents it is one of the largest communities on the Reservation, and a useful base for seeing this corner of northern Arizona without staying in the manicured park gateways at Tusayan or Page.

The town's history is unusually layered. The Dominguez–Escalante expedition out of Santa Fe is said to have passed through in 1776; in the 1870s the Mormon missionary Jacob Hamblin established a small colony here, persuading a group of Hopis to settle alongside as the village of Moenkopi for company and protection. The Hopi headman was named Tuuvi, and the settlers anglicised it. The Navajo eventually sued to remove the Mormons from what was clearly Navajo land, and the result is the unusual modern Tuba City — a Navajo administrative town with a Hopi village (still called [Moenkopi](moenkopi)) joined to its southeast edge.

The town itself is dusty, windswept, and practical. Two motels, a small shopping centre with a Bashas' Diné Markets, a couple of fast-food chains, a pizza place and a Chinese restaurant cover most needs. The historic [Tuba Trading Post](tuba_trading_post) is the obvious stop for crafts; the old boarding school and Jacob Hamblin's stone knitting mill stand as reminders of the rougher chapters.

Within an easy radius are some of the more remarkable corners of the Colorado Plateau: the Moenave dinosaur trackway just west of town, the moonscape of Coal Mine Canyon, the painted cliffs and Little Colorado gorge down at Cameron, and the Grand Canyon's eastern entrance an hour's drive away. If you are driving from the Hopi mesas to the Grand Canyon, Tuba City is the obvious place to stop.
