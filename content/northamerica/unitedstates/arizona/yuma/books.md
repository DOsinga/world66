Edward Abbey's *The Monkey Wrench Gang* (1975) is set largely in the desert Southwest and captures the spirit of the Colorado River country that Yuma sits within. Abbey's desert anarchists haunt a landscape of canyon lands, dam infrastructure, and red rock — the same world you see from the Territorial Prison bluff.

Sam Quinones's *Dreamland* (2015) traces the opioid epidemic through border communities including the Arizona-Mexico borderlands. Yuma sits right at that crossing, and the book gives context to the complex social realities of the region in a way no guidebook can.

Francisco Cantú's *The Line Becomes a River* (2018) is a memoir by a former US Border Patrol agent who worked the desert borderlands of Arizona. It's a thoughtful, honest account of life on the line — particularly relevant for anyone curious about the border that runs just south of Yuma.
