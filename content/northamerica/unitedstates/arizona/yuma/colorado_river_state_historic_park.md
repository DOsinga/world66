The Colorado River State Historic Park preserves the Yuma Quartermaster Depot, the US Army supply depot that kept frontier forts stocked from 1865 to 1883. Five original adobe buildings survive from the depot's operational period, making this one of the best-preserved frontier-era military sites in the Southwest. The quartermaster's office, warehouse, and steam-powered machinery are all on display.

The park sits on the riverfront at the point where Yuma's history converges — the spot where the ferry crossing operated, where the steamboats docked, and where the railroad bridge eventually ended the river-trade era. The Colorado here is calmer than it once was, controlled by upstream dams, but the site still conveys the sense of a critical crossing point. Walking the grounds alongside the river on a winter afternoon, with the Californian desert hills on the far bank, is genuinely pleasant.

**Getting there:** 201 N 4th Ave, downtown Yuma. Combined tickets available with Yuma Territorial Prison. Open daily.
