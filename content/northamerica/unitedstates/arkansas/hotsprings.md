Hot Springs is built around the thermal waters that give it its name — 47 springs flowing from the western slope of Hot Springs Mountain at a constant 62°C. The city grew up as a resort town in the 19th century, and [Bathhouse Row](/northamerica/unitedstates/arkansas/hotsprings/bathhouse_row), a line of ornate spa buildings along Central Avenue, is now a National Historic Landmark within [Hot Springs National Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/arkansas/hotsprings/hot_springs_national_park).

The town has a split personality. Central Avenue is touristy — wax museums, alligator farms, and the [Gangster Museum of America](/northamerica/unitedstates/arkansas/hotsprings/gangster_museum), all leaning into the city's pre-war reputation as a wide-open gambling town — but the surrounding Ouachita Mountains are genuinely beautiful, with pine-covered ridges and good hiking trails up to the [Hot Springs Mountain Tower](/northamerica/unitedstates/arkansas/hotsprings/hot_springs_mountain_tower). [Oaklawn Racing Casino Resort](/northamerica/unitedstates/arkansas/hotsprings/oaklawn) draws crowds for Thoroughbred racing season through the spring, and the lakes south of town — [Lake Hamilton](/northamerica/unitedstates/arkansas/hotsprings/lake_hamilton) and quieter [Lake Catherine](/northamerica/unitedstates/arkansas/hotsprings/lake_catherine) — are where the city heads on summer weekends.

The hot springs themselves remain the main event: you can still take a traditional thermal bath at [Buckstaff](/northamerica/unitedstates/arkansas/hotsprings/buckstaff_bathhouse) or, in a more modern communal setting, at [Quapaw Baths](/northamerica/unitedstates/arkansas/hotsprings/quapaw_baths). South of town, [Garvan Woodland Gardens](/northamerica/unitedstates/arkansas/hotsprings/garvan_woodland_gardens) drapes 210 acres of azaleas and pine over a peninsula on Lake Hamilton — the third must-stop after the bathhouses and the national park trails.
