story: >
  Hot Springs was protected as a federal reservation in 1832 — forty years before Yellowstone — making it
  the oldest area in the National Park System. President Andrew Jackson signed the law that set the
  springs aside, though the park status itself only arrived in 1921.
---

The smallest national park in the country and one of the strangest: most of the protected land is the
mountain ridge that feeds the springs, but the headline attraction is **Bathhouse Row**, a strip of
1900s spa architecture sitting in the middle of downtown. The Fordyce Bathhouse serves as the visitor
centre — go inside to see the stained-glass ceilings, marble pools, and gym equipment that looks like
medical-torture furniture.

Behind the row, paved promenades follow the hot creek upslope past the still-flowing spring vents.
Touch the water if you can stand it (it leaves the rock at 62°C). The park's 26 miles of trails climb
into pine and oak forest above town, with Hot Springs Mountain and West Mountain offering quiet
walks just minutes from the bustle of Central Avenue.
