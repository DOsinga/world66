The Arkansas State Capitol is a scaled replica of the U.S. Capitol, its shallow copper dome rising above the tree-lined west end of downtown Little Rock. Built between 1899 and 1915, it serves as the active seat of state government and as an architectural statement that has worn reasonably well for over a century.

Tours of the interior are free and self-guided during business hours. The building is worth entering for the rotunda alone — a double ring of columns under a painted dome, with portraits of Arkansas governors circling the walls. The grounds hold an uncomfortable collection of monuments including a Ten Commandments monolith installed in 2017 (immediately knocked down by a truck driver the same day, then reinstalled), Confederate memorials, and a statue of the suffragist Daisy Bates. The monument to the nine Black students who integrated Central High School is more moving than any of them.
