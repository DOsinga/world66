Little Rock Central High School is still an active school — students attend class here — and also a National Historic Site that commemorates the 1957 school integration crisis, one of the defining confrontations of the Civil Rights Movement. The juxtaposition is intentional: the building remains a working school partly to signal that what happened here was resolved, and integration succeeded.

The visitor centre across the street from the school has exhibits on the crisis and the lives of the Little Rock Nine, the nine Black teenagers who integrated the school at enormous personal cost. Guided tours of the visitor centre are excellent; you can view the exterior of the school itself and the surrounding grounds. The school's architecture — a vast 1927 Gothic Revival building with 101 classrooms — is impressive in its own right, often cited as one of the most beautiful high school buildings in the country. About two miles south of downtown; best reached by car.
