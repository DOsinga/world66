Little Rock Union Station, built in 1921 in a restrained Beaux-Arts style, served as the city's main railway terminal until Amtrak consolidated services in the 1970s. The building is now used for events and offices, but its restored exterior — a long limestone facade with arched windows and a central clock tower — is worth noting as you pass along Markham Street.

The station marks the western edge of the River Market area and sits near the original landing where the city's name-giving "little rock" — a small stone outcropping on the south bank of the Arkansas River — was used as a navigation marker by early settlers. The actual rock, now marked by a small monument, is visible from Riverfront Park a few blocks east.
