The Old State House is the oldest surviving state capitol west of the Mississippi, completed in 1836 when Arkansas was barely a state. Its Greek Revival facade — a colonnaded portico in white stone — looks almost too grand for the modest building behind it, which is exactly the kind of optimistic overstatement that characterised antebellum civic architecture in the South.

The museum inside traces Arkansas political history from statehood through the 20th century. The duelling scars and bullet holes in the House Chamber floor recall an era when legislative disputes sometimes resolved themselves violently — there were two killings in the building during its years as the active capitol. Admission is free. It sits on Markham Street at the edge of the River Market District, within easy walking distance of the Clinton Library.
