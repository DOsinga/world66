Van Buren sits just across the Arkansas River from [Fort Smith](/northamerica/unitedstates/arkansas/fortsmith), a small town of about 24,000 with a well-preserved historic downtown. The [Main Street Historic District](/northamerica/unitedstates/arkansas/van_buren/main_street_historic_district) is lined with antique shops, collectibles stores, and specialty shops housed in handsome nineteenth-century brick buildings — it is one of the better antique-hunting strips in western Arkansas. The [King Opera House](/northamerica/unitedstates/arkansas/van_buren/king_opera_house) at the end of the district is the architectural highlight.

The town is the home station of the [Arkansas & Missouri Railroad](/northamerica/unitedstates/arkansas/van_buren/arkansas_missouri_railroad) excursion train, which runs scenic trips through the Boston Mountains to Winslow and back. The [Drennen-Scott House Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/arkansas/van_buren/drennen_scott_house) preserves an antebellum Greek Revival home connected to the history of Indian Territory administration. Van Buren is also the birthplace of Bob Burns — "The Arkansas Traveler" — whose [childhood home](/northamerica/unitedstates/arkansas/van_buren/bob_burns_house) on Jefferson Street is historically marked; Burns is the unlikely man who both invented the bazooka instrument and gave a World War II weapon its name.
