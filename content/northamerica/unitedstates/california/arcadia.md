Arcadia sits about 20 miles northeast of downtown Los Angeles in the San Gabriel Valley, backed by the San Gabriel Mountains. It's a quiet, well-kept suburb that most visitors pass through on the way to somewhere else — but it has two attractions that make a deliberate stop worthwhile.

The most famous is [Santa Anita Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/arcadia/santa_anita_park), one of the great horse-racing venues in the United States, with a backdrop of mountains that has made it a favorite for photographers and film crews since the 1930s. The other is the [Los Angeles County Arboretum and Botanic Garden](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/arcadia/la_arboretum), a 127-acre property on the site of a historic ranch, with peacocks roaming freely among the trees and a [Queen Anne Cottage](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/arcadia/queen_anne_cottage) that has doubled as a movie location countless times. Behind both, the [Santa Anita Canyon](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/arcadia/santa_anita_canyon) climbs into the San Gabriel mountains for a half-day hike.

Beyond those two draws, Arcadia offers good eating — particularly along [Baldwin Avenue](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/arcadia/baldwin_avenue), where the city's large Chinese-American population has produced a dense stretch of restaurants serving Cantonese, Shanghainese, and Sichuan food. It's an easy half-day trip from Pasadena or central LA.
