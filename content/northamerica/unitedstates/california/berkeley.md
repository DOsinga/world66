Berkeley sits on the eastern shore of San Francisco Bay, dominated by the University of California campus and by a political culture that has been left-of-centre for so long it has become a self-parody — but an endearing one. The university brings intellectual life, good bookshops, and excellent food. The hills behind the city offer some of the best bay views in the region.

[Telegraph Avenue](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/berkeley/telegraph_avenue), running south from the campus, is the traditional heart of student life — vintage clothing stores, cafes, street vendors, and the ghost of 1960s counterculture. The [Gourmet Ghetto](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/berkeley/gourmet_ghetto), a stretch of Shattuck Avenue north of campus, has been a destination for serious eating since Alice Waters opened [Chez Panisse](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/berkeley/chez_panisse) in 1971. The [Campanile](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/berkeley/campanile) — the 307-foot Sather Tower at the heart of campus — is one of the great views in the Bay Area, and the carillon bells ring across the city three times a day.

The [UC Botanical Garden](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/berkeley/uc_botanical_garden) and the [Lawrence Hall of Science](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/berkeley/lawrence_hall_of_science) (a hands-on science museum with panoramic bay views) are both worth an afternoon. The Berkeley Hills are laced with trails; [Tilden Regional Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/berkeley/tilden_regional_park) above the campus is the obvious starting point. For architecture, Bernard Maybeck's [First Church of Christ, Scientist](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/berkeley/first_church_of_christ_scientist) on Dwight Way is a National Historic Landmark that most visitors walk past without noticing. The [BAMPFA](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/berkeley/bampfa) combines a serious art museum with one of the best film archive programmes in the country.
