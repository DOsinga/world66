Carlsbad sits on the Southern California coast between Los Angeles and San Diego, and it punches well above its weight for a mid-size beach city. It is best known as the home of [LEGOLAND California](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/carlsbad/legoland_california) and the [Carlsbad Flower Fields](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/carlsbad/carlsbad_flower_fields) — 50-odd acres of giant ranunculus that bloom in a blaze of red, pink, orange, and white each spring — but there is more to the place than its two signature attractions.

The old downtown village runs along the coast near the original mineral spring that gave the city its name (the waters supposedly matched those of the famous Bohemian spa town of Karlsbad). It is a genuinely pleasant stretch of small shops, cafes, and restaurants with a relaxed local character, far less crowded than the beach towns further south. The coastline here is good: long sandy beaches, clean surf breaks at [Carlsbad State Beach](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/carlsbad/carlsbad_state_beach), and a series of lagoons — especially [Batiquitos Lagoon](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/carlsbad/batiquitos_lagoon) — that attract birders and kayakers.

Carlsbad is also golf country — courses like La Costa Resort host major tournaments, and the climate makes year-round play practical. The Palomar Airport at the city's eastern edge serves private and charter traffic, and the Coaster commuter rail makes San Diego easy to reach without a car. East of town, [Leo Carrillo Ranch Historic Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/carlsbad/leo_carrillo_ranch) preserves a hacienda estate with surprising Hollywood history, and the [Museum of Making Music](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/carlsbad/museum_of_making_music) near LEGOLAND is a rewarding stop for anyone with an interest in how American popular music evolved through its instruments.
