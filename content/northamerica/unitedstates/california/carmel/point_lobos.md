The headland a mile south of Carmel village is one of the most spectacular stretches of Pacific coastline in the United States. Point Lobos State Natural Reserve protects a jagged, wildflower-covered peninsula above kelp forests and sea lion rocks. Sea otters float in the kelp beds along the shore; harbor seals haul out on the beaches; Brandt's cormorants nest on the offshore stacks. In winter, California gray whales are visible from the headlands on their migration south.

The reserve has about twelve miles of trails, from easy coastal walks above the rocks to longer paths through cypress groves and meadows. The Whaler's Cove area has a small museum in a historic Chinese whaling cabin from the 1850s — one of the earliest structures built by Chinese immigrants in California. The Sea Lion Point trail, just half a mile, is the most dramatic short walk: the lions are loud and close. Parking is limited and fills early on weekends and throughout summer; arrive before 9am or pay the day-use fee at the booth and expect to wait. The reserve is worth it.
