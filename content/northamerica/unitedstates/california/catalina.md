Catalina Island sits 26 miles off the coast of Los Angeles, and it feels like a different country. The crossing from San Pedro or Long Beach takes about an hour by ferry, but the moment you step onto the pier in [Avalon](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/catalina/avalon), the freeways and smog are a world away. The water in the harbour is an almost absurd shade of blue.

[Avalon](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/catalina/avalon) is the island's only real town — a compact, walkable strip of restaurants, shops, and art deco architecture dominated by the circular [Casino Building](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/catalina/casino_building) on the point. The [Catalina Island Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/catalina/catalina_island_museum) covers seven thousand years of island history. The [Catalina Island Conservancy](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/catalina/catalina_island_conservancy) manages 88 percent of the island as a protected reserve, home to bison, endemic island foxes, and surprisingly rugged terrain.

You can hike into the interior, [kayak](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/catalina/kayaking) and [snorkel](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/catalina/snorkeling_and_diving) in clear kelp forests, or take a [glass-bottom boat tour](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/catalina/glass_bottom_boat) over the underwater gardens. The remote settlement of [Two Harbors](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/catalina/two_harbors) on the far side of the island is a completely different experience — fewer than a hundred permanent residents, one bar, and excellent sailing anchorage.

The island has been a getaway for Angelenos since the 1920s, and it still draws a mix of day-trippers and weekenders. Come midweek if you want Avalon to yourself.
