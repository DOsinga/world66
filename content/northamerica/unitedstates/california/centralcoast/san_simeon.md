San Simeon is barely a village, but it holds one of the strongest day-trip clusters on the California coast. [Hearst Castle](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/centralcoast/san_simeon/hearst_castle) sits in the hills above Highway 1, the Piedras Blancas shore has one of the easiest elephant-seal viewing spots in the world, and the coast north toward Big Sur is all cliffs, wind, and restless Pacific water.

The place itself is simple: motels, a beach, a pier, and a road that everyone is either driving north to [Big Sur](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/centralcoast/bigsur) or south to Cambria and [San Luis Obispo](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/centralcoast/sanluisobispo). That is not a weakness. San Simeon works because the anchors are close, vivid, and different from one another.

Give Hearst Castle a reserved tour time, then leave room for the coast. The elephant seals are free to see from the boardwalks, and what you get depends on the season: breeding, birthing, moulting, sparring, or just enormous animals sleeping in heaps on the sand.
