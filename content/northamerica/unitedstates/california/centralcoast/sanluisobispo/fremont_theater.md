Built in 1942, the Fremont is one of the finest surviving examples of Art Moderne cinema architecture on the California coast. The exterior is the thing: a sweeping curved facade in cream-colored plaster with a neon sign in deep red and gold that lights up Monterey Street after dark. The sign is large enough to read from two blocks away and remains original. It looks exactly like what Hollywood thought the future would look like in 1942.

The interior matches — streamlined, asymmetric, with the period details intact. The Fremont has been through several lives (mainstream cinema, concert venue, special events) but has not been divided or gutted, so the 1,200-seat auditorium survives as a single room. If a live show or film is playing during your visit, it's worth catching something here just for the space. Even if nothing is on, the exterior alone is worth the detour from Higuera Street, four blocks away.

It's currently operated as a live music and event venue, with occasional film screenings. Check the schedule before you go.
