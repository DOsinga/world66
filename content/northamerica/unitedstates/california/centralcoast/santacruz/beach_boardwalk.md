story: >
  The Giant Dipper, the Boardwalk's wooden roller coaster, was built in 1924 by Arthur Looff and has been running continuously ever since. It was one of the first California landmarks to receive National Historic Landmark designation — not as architecture or history, but as an amusement ride. The coaster's survival owes something to its location: unlike inland amusement parks, the Boardwalk sits on the beach, and the ocean frontage gave it a revenue base that carried it through the decades when wooden coasters elsewhere were being torn down.

The last surviving beachfront amusement park on the West Coast, open since 1907 and still running the same two landmark rides — the 1924 Giant Dipper wooden roller coaster and the 1911 Looff Carousel — that earned it National Historic Landmark status. The Boardwalk stretches along Main Beach, with rides, games, and food stalls occupying a mile of oceanfront.

The Giant Dipper is genuinely thrilling for a century-old wooden coaster: the drops are steep, the turns sharp, and the views of the bay from the top are exceptional. The Looff Carousel has its original hand-carved horses and a band organ that plays automatically. Both are worth the ticket price for the mechanical artistry alone. The park is open year-round on weekends and daily in summer; parking in the adjacent lots is expensive but there is bus access from downtown.
