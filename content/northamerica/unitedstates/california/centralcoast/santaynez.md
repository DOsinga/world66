Santa Ynez is a small town of about 5,000 people in the Santa Ynez Valley, a long flat basin running east to west through Santa Barbara County, about 45 minutes north of Santa Barbara and three hours from Los Angeles. The valley is better known to most visitors than the town itself — it is one of California's premier wine regions, home to more than forty wineries, and was made internationally famous by the 2004 film *Sideways*.

The [wine country](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/centralcoast/santaynez/wine_country) here is genuine and varied. The western end of the valley — nearest Santa Ynez — suits Pinot Noir and Chardonnay; the warmer Ballard Canyon, just south of town, is strong Syrah country. The village of Los Olivos, eight miles east, has the highest concentration of tasting rooms. Five miles west is [Solvang](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/centralcoast/solvang), the Danish-heritage town founded by immigrants in 1911. It is undeniably touristy, but the half-timbered buildings are real enough, the pastries are good, and [Mission Santa Inés](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/centralcoast/solvang/mission_santa_ines) — founded in 1804, still an active parish — is one of the best-preserved California missions and worth the detour.

Santa Ynez itself is quieter. The [Santa Ynez Valley Historical Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/centralcoast/santaynez/santa_ynez_valley_historical_museum) covers the valley's history from Chumash settlement through the rancho and stagecoach eras; the adjacent carriage house holds an impressive collection of horse-drawn vehicles. At the east edge of town, the [Chumash Casino Resort](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/centralcoast/santaynez/chumash_casino_resort) is the region's major entertainment draw, operated by the Santa Ynez Band of Chumash Mission Indians on tribal land.

Highway 154 (San Marcos Pass Road) is the scenic route in from Santa Barbara, climbing over the Santa Ynez Mountains past Lake Cachuma before dropping into the valley. US-101 via Buellton is faster. Either way, the valley is an easy day trip from the coast — though with enough wineries and enough reasons to stay, a night or two is easy to justify.
