The Santa Ynez Valley is one of California's most distinctive wine regions, shaped by a geography that channels cool Pacific air through the east-west Santa Ynez Mountains — one of the few mountain ranges in North America that run sideways to the coast. That marine influence keeps temperatures moderate and lets Pinot Noir and Chardonnay ripen slowly on the valley floor.

The region was made internationally famous by the 2004 film *Sideways*, which was shot here and credited with devastating Merlot sales nationally while sending Pinot Noir demand soaring. The effect was real enough that it gets its own name among economists: the Sideways effect.

Within the valley, different sub-regions have distinct characters. The higher terrain to the north-west — the Los Olivos District AVA — suits Pinot Noir and Chardonnay. Ballard Canyon, a narrow canyon east of Solvang, is warmer and better suited to Syrah and other Rhône varieties; Rusack, Beckmen, and Stolpman are among the better-regarded producers here. The Santa Ynez Valley AVA also supports Sauvignon Blanc and Bordeaux varieties in its eastern and warmer reaches.

Most wineries in the area are open for tasting, typically for a fee of $15–25. The nearest concentration of tasting rooms is in the village of Los Olivos, ten minutes east of Santa Ynez by car, where a dozen rooms line a single block of Grand Avenue. [Solvang](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/centralcoast/solvang) also has tasting rooms within walking distance of each other. A Saturday in the valley — drive a loop, stop at three or four estates, eat lunch in Los Olivos — is one of the better days out on the Central Coast.
