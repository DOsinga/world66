Solvang is a small Danish-heritage town in the Santa Ynez Valley, about a half-hour drive from Santa Barbara. Founded in 1911 by Danish immigrants, it still feels distinctly Scandinavian — half-timbered buildings, a [working windmill](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/centralcoast/solvang/solvang_windmill), and bakeries selling [aebleskiver](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/centralcoast/solvang/aebleskiver) on every corner. It is touristy, yes, but genuinely charming.

Beyond the Danish kitsch, the [Santa Ynez Valley is excellent wine country](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/centralcoast/solvang/santa_ynez_wine_tasting). Dozens of tasting rooms line the streets of Solvang and nearby Los Olivos. [Mission Santa Inés](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/centralcoast/solvang/mission_santa_ines), dating to 1804, is one of the best-preserved California missions and worth a visit. The [Elverhøj Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/centralcoast/solvang/elverhoj_museum) tells the story of the town's Danish-American founders. For an odd diversion, the [Hans Christian Andersen Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/centralcoast/solvang/hans_christian_andersen_museum) sits above a good bookshop, and the [Vintage Motorcycle Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/centralcoast/solvang/vintage_motorcycle_museum) has an unexpectedly serious collection of rare machines. On the highway between Solvang and Buellton, [OstrichLand USA](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/centralcoast/solvang/ostrichland) offers an amusing encounter with the world's largest birds.
