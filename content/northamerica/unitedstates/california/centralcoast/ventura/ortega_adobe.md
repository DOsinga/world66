The Ortega Adobe at 215 West Main Street is a small, restored adobe building that looks unremarkable from the outside but carries a specific piece of American culinary history: this is where Emilio Ortega started the chile-processing operation in the 1890s that grew into the Ortega brand, now one of the most recognised names in Mexican-American food products. The adobe itself dates from the mid-19th century and is one of the older surviving structures in Ventura — an example of the Californio-era construction that preceded American statehood.

The building is managed by the City of Ventura as a small historic site. It is not a full museum — there is no permanent collection or extensive interpretation — but it is well maintained and open at certain hours. It sits on West Main Street within easy walking distance of the Mission and the main downtown strip, making it a natural addition to any walk through the historic core of the city.
