---
title: "Getting There"
type: section
---

**By air**

Bakersfield is served by **Meadows Field Airport (BFL)**, a small regional airport about 6km northwest of downtown. It handles limited domestic service — American Eagle operates connections to Los Angeles and Phoenix, and United Express flies to San Francisco and Denver. For more options, many travelers use **Los Angeles (LAX)**, roughly 180km south via CA-99 and I-5 (about 2 hours in light traffic, considerably more during peak hours).

**By train**

Bakersfield is a stop on Amtrak's **San Joaquin** service, which runs daily between Oakland/Sacramento and Bakersfield. The **Bakersfield Amtrak station** on Truxton Avenue is a compact but functional facility. The San Joaquin does not continue south — at Bakersfield, passengers connect to Amtrak Thruway bus services for onward travel to Los Angeles (roughly 3 hours by bus) and southern California. The train-bus combination is slower than driving but considerably cheaper than flying, and the Valley scenery through the Central Valley agricultural heartland is worth seeing once.

**By road**

Bakersfield sits at the intersection of two major California highways. **CA-99** is the main Central Valley artery running north to Fresno, Sacramento, and the Bay Area; travel times to Los Angeles via CA-99 and I-5 are around 1.5–2 hours depending on the Tejon Pass (known to locals as "the Grapevine"), which can close in winter during snowstorms. **I-5** passes just west of the city and provides the fastest direct route to Los Angeles when CA-99 is congested. From **San Francisco**, count on roughly 4–5 hours via I-5 or CA-99.
