Fresno sits in the heart of California's San Joaquin Valley, surrounded by some of the most productive farmland on earth. It's not a glamorous city, but it has real character — a working agricultural hub that also happens to be the gateway to the Sierra Nevada and [Yosemite National Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/centralvalley/fresno/yosemite_day_trip), just 60 miles to the north on Highway 41.

Stop by a roadside fruit stand and try the figs, sweet grapes, and strawberries. Summers are brutally hot — temperatures regularly climb past 100°F — but evenings cool down, and winters are mild and rainy, rarely dropping below freezing.

The city has a decent collection of museums and gardens, including the [Forestiere Underground Gardens](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/centralvalley/fresno/forestiere_underground_gardens), a subterranean complex hand-dug over 40 years as an escape from the valley heat. Downtown holds two historic anchors: the 1889 [Meux Home](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/centralvalley/fresno/meux_home) and the 1894 [Old Fresno Water Tower](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/centralvalley/fresno/old_fresno_water_tower), both giving a sense of the city's agricultural-boom origins. The [Warnors Theatre](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/centralvalley/fresno/warnors_theatre), a 1928 movie palace, is one of the finest surviving examples of its era in California.

In late winter and early spring, the [Fresno Blossom Trail](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/centralvalley/fresno/blossom_trail) is worth a detour — a 62-mile driving route through orchards ablaze with pink, white, and crimson blossoms. Skiing is available in the Sierras to the east; Kings Canyon and Sequoia National Parks are 60 miles away via Highway 180.
