Hanford is a small Central Valley city that punches well above its weight in historic interest. The main draw is [China Alley](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/centralvalley/hanford/china_alley), a remarkably preserved enclave from the late 19th century when Chinese railroad workers settled here after the transcontinental lines were complete. The 1893 [Taoist Temple](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/centralvalley/hanford/taoist_temple) on China Alley still stands, and you can visit it by reservation — it is one of the oldest Chinese temples in the American West.

Downtown Hanford has aged gracefully. [Courthouse Square](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/centralvalley/hanford/courthouse_square) is a genuine town square anchored by an ornate 1897 courthouse, and an old-fashioned carousel still runs there on weekends. The [Fox Theater](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/centralvalley/hanford/fox_theater), a 1929 movie palace, has been restored and hosts live performances. Across the square the mission-revival [Civic Auditorium](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/centralvalley/hanford/civic_auditorium) and the [Carnegie Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/centralvalley/hanford/carnegie_museum) fill out a surprising afternoon of sightseeing.

Eating well in Hanford is not hard either. [La Bastille](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/centralvalley/hanford/la_bastille) serves steaks in the old county jail, and [Superior Dairy](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/centralvalley/hanford/superior_dairy) has been scooping its own ice cream since 1929. For a small city, there's a surprising amount worth seeing on foot.
