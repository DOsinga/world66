Reedley is a small farming town in the heart of the San Joaquin Valley, about 25 miles southeast of Fresno. Known locally as the "World's Fruit Basket," it sits at the edge of some of the most productive agricultural land in California — orchards and vineyards covering the surrounding flatlands produce peaches, nectarines, plums, and table grapes that end up in supermarkets across the country.

The town's character is shaped by two forces: agriculture and faith. Mennonite immigrants arrived here in the late 19th and early 20th centuries and built lasting institutions — schools, churches, and a quilting tradition that survives today. Their presence gives downtown Reedley a quietly industrious feel that sets it apart from other Central Valley towns of similar size.

G Street is the compact commercial core, where you can find the restored Opera House, the local museum, and the Mennonite Quilting Center within a few blocks of each other. On the western edge of town, the Kings River offers a stretch of cold, clear water that draws swimmers and tubers on hot summer days. Reedley is not a destination in itself, but as a stop on a Central Valley drive — or as a base for Kings Canyon day trips — it earns its keep.
