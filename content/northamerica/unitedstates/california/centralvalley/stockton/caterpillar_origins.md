Stockton is the birthplace of the Caterpillar tractor, one of the most consequential machines in modern agriculture and engineering. Benjamin Holt, a farm machinery manufacturer, developed the first practical continuous-track tractor here in 1904 to solve the problem of cultivating the soft peat soils of the Sacramento Delta. The Holt Manufacturing Company eventually merged with C.L. Best Tractor Company to form Caterpillar Inc.

The Benjamin Holt House, a National Historic Landmark at 1606 N. El Dorado Street, is the surviving residence of the inventor, a Victorian home now in private use. The Stockton area has several other Holt-era industrial buildings. For the broader history of the delta agriculture that drove the invention, the Haggin Museum covers this in its history galleries. The connection between Holt's design and British WWI tank technology is one of the more unexpected threads in Stockton's industrial history.
