Chula Vista is San Diego County's second-largest city, sitting just north of the Mexican border and spreading from the flat coastal plain up into the dry hills above Otay Lakes. It has a strongly Latino character — Chula Vista is Spanish for "beautiful view" — and a relaxed suburban pace that feels quite different from downtown San Diego.

The waterfront has been redeveloped around the [Chula Vista Bayfront](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/chulavista/chula_vista_bayfront), with nature preserves and parks running along San Diego Bay, including the [Sweetwater Marsh National Wildlife Refuge](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/chulavista/sweetwater_marsh). Inland, the city is largely residential, but it draws visitors for one genuinely unusual attraction: the [US Olympic and Paralympic Training Center](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/chulavista/us_olympic_training_center), one of only three official year-round training facilities in the country, sits on a windy plateau above Otay Lakes and is open for public tours. The [Living Coast Discovery Center](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/chulavista/living_coast_discovery_center), a zoo and aquarium inside the wildlife refuge, is one of the most distinctive nature attractions in San Diego County.
