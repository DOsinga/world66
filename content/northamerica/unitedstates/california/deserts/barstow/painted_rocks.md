Painted Rocks is a cluster of desert boulders on the edge of Fort Irwin Military Reservation, north of Barstow, covered over decades in military unit insignia, division patches, and hand-painted tributes from soldiers who trained at the National Training Center. The rocks stand in open desert with no explanation, no facilities, and no other visitors — just layers of military iconography accumulating on granite.

The site sits in a remote stretch of Mojave that requires a drive on unpaved roads, and the boulders themselves are unremarkable in geology. What makes Painted Rocks strange is the sheer density of accumulated painting — this is folk art of a very specific American military subculture, created not for tourists but as a form of unit pride and remembrance. Check road conditions before going and bring water; this is genuinely remote desert.
