  The sculptures are the work of Ricardo Breceda, a Mexican-born artist who was a boot maker until he taught himself welding to build a dinosaur for his daughter. Dennis Avery — heir to the Avery label fortune — bought the first piece in 2007, then commissioned over a hundred more and installed them on his land around Borrego Springs as an open gift to the public. The largest, a 350-foot serpent that appears to weave in and out of the desert floor, took Breceda nearly a year to build.
---

Scattered across the open desert north of town are more than 130 large metal sculptures: dinosaurs, sabretooth cats, mammoths, scorpions the size of pickup trucks, a flock of running horses, and a serpent that snakes for more than a hundred metres across the flats. They sit on private land owned by the late Dennis Avery, who left them open to the public, free of charge.

A map of the sculptures is available at the visitor centre and most local shops. You can drive between them on the public roads — Borrego Springs Road, Big Horn Road, Henderson Canyon Road — and walk in for closer looks. Late afternoon, when the metal catches the low sun, is the photogenic hour.
