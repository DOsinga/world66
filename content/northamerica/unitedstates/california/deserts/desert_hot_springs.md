Desert Hot Springs sits north of Palm Springs, on the quieter side of the Coachella Valley, where the San Bernardino Mountains rise above a grid of low desert streets. Its identity is simple: hot mineral water without the sulfur smell, pulled from aquifers that made the town a spa colony.

The resort side can feel thin if you are not here to soak, and World66 does not do hotel listings. What saves the town is the combination of a strong place story, [Cabot's Pueblo Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/deserts/desert_hot_springs/cabots_pueblo_museum), and nearby desert preserves where water, fault lines, palms, and cottonwoods break up the dry country.

Use Desert Hot Springs as a low-key counterpoint to Palm Springs rather than a replacement for it. Spend the morning at Cabot's, walk Mission Creek or Big Morongo when the heat allows, and save the mineral pools for the end of the day.
