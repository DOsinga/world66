Barker Dam is a short, rewarding stop in Joshua Tree, with desert rock formations, seasonal water, and a bit of ranching history in one loop. The dam was built in the early 1900s to store water for cattle and mining use, and the reservoir still draws birds and other wildlife after rain.

The walk is easy by park standards, but the mood changes with the season. In wet periods it feels like a small oasis; in dry weather it is more about the boulders, the open desert, and the traces of human use in a harsh landscape.
