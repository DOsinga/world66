  The Safari Park began in 1969 as a breeding facility for species too large for the San Diego Zoo — the intention was simply to give elephants room to move. Within a decade it had become one of the most successful wildlife conservation programs in the world, helping to bring the California condor back from the brink of extinction.

The San Diego Zoo Safari Park is the main reason most visitors come to Escondido. Spread across 1,800 acres of chaparral-covered hills in the San Pasqual Valley, the park keeps African and Asian animals in enclosures large enough that you need a tram to cross them. Giraffes, rhinos, lions, elephants, and cheetahs roam territory that genuinely resembles their home landscapes, which makes for far better viewing than a conventional zoo.

The Africa Tram Safari rolls through the large African habitat and is included in general admission. For a closer encounter, various premium experiences — Cheetah Safari, Tiger Trekking, Rhino Rescue — can be booked in advance and are well worth the extra cost. The park opens early and the animals are most active in the morning before the heat builds.
