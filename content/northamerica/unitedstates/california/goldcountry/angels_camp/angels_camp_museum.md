The Angels Camp Museum on Main Street covers Gold Rush history with more care and depth than most small-town museums of this type. The collection includes an impressive array of mining equipment — stamp mills, arrastra wheels, gold-panning tools — along with a carriage collection and a recreation of a 19th-century town scene. There is also material on the Chinese miners who made up a substantial part of the Gold Country workforce, a history that is usually undercovered.

The museum is set across a large outdoor area as well as interior galleries, with pieces of heavy industrial equipment that cannot be displayed indoors. It tells the story of the gold rush not just as a tale of fortune-seekers but as an industrial operation that reshaped the California landscape. Entry is inexpensive and the museum is well run.
