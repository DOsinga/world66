Coloma is where the California Gold Rush began. On 24 January 1848, James W. Marshall spotted gold flakes in the tailrace of a sawmill he was building for John Sutter on the American River. Within a year, tens of thousands of prospectors had flooded into California.

Today the town is a quiet stop in the Sierra Nevada foothills, anchored by the [Marshall Gold Discovery State Historic Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/goldcountry/coloma/marshall_gold_discovery_park). The park preserves a replica of [Sutter's Mill](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/goldcountry/coloma/sutters_mill), a museum with Gold Rush-era exhibits, and the hillside [monument](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/goldcountry/coloma/marshall_monument) marking Marshall's grave. The surrounding stretch of the [South Fork American River](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/goldcountry/coloma/south_fork_rafting) is the busiest whitewater run in California, with rapids ranging from gentle Class II to more serious Class III.
