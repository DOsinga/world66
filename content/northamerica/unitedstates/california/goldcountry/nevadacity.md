Nevada City is one of the best-preserved Gold Rush towns in California — a tight cluster of Victorian storefronts and brick buildings stacked up a hill above Deer Creek, 60 miles northeast of Sacramento. It doesn't feel like a museum piece. The downtown is genuinely alive with restaurants, galleries, and independent shops, and the surrounding hills draw hikers, cyclists, and wine tourists year-round.

The town grew explosively after gold was discovered in the region in 1849, peaking at around 10,000 residents by 1856. What survives today is a compact but remarkably intact historic core. [Broad Street](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/goldcountry/nevadacity/broad_street), the main drag, looks much as it did in the 1860s. The [Nevada Theatre](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/goldcountry/nevadacity/nevada_theatre) at 401 Broad Street — opened in 1865 and still running — is California's oldest continuously operating theater building; Mark Twain and Jack London both performed here.

North of town, about 26 miles on Tyler Foote Road, [Malakoff Diggins State Historic Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/goldcountry/nevadacity/malakoff_diggins) preserves the largest hydraulic gold mine in California. Monitors blasted up to 100,000 tons of gravel a day at its peak in the 1870s, carving a vast rust-and-ochre canyon into the hills. The environmental damage eventually led to the country's first federal injunction against a private mining company, issued in 1884. The canyon is genuinely dramatic and worth the drive. The [South Yuba River State Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/goldcountry/nevadacity/south_yuba_river) at Bridgeport, with its 1862 covered bridge and swimming holes, is a closer and easier half-day excursion.
