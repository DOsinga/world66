  The plaza was originally called City Plaza and served as the social centre of Sacramento from the city's earliest days. It was renamed for César Chávez, founder of the United Farm Workers, in 1999 — a fitting choice given that the Central Valley farmworkers Chávez organised were the labour force behind Sacramento's claim to be the Farm-to-Fork Capital of America.
---

The main public square of downtown Sacramento, a green block bounded by J, K, 9th, and 10th streets, directly across from City Hall. The William Coleman Memorial Fountain, a bronze sculpture by Ralph Stackpole, anchors the centre. The plaza fills with food trucks at lunchtime on weekdays and hosts the Downtown Sacramento Farmers Market on Sundays.

It is a pleasant place to sit and watch Sacramento go about its business — government workers, lobbyists, protesters, skateboarders, and farmers market shoppers coexist in a democratic informality. The surrounding blocks include the mid-century City Hall and several handsome 19th-century commercial buildings.
