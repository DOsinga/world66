  The Central Valley produces about a quarter of US food supply, and Sacramento sits at its northern end. The farmers who supply the most famous restaurants in San Francisco and San Jose sell the same produce at Sacramento's Saturday and Sunday markets, sometimes at significantly lower prices. The variety of stone fruit alone — white peaches, apriums, pluots, nectarines — reflects the extraordinary diversity of Central Valley horticulture.
---

Sacramento runs several farmers markets through the week, with the Saturday market on Capitol Mall being the largest. The markets draw directly from Central Valley farms — many of the vendors are growing within 100 kilometres of the city — and the range of produce reflects the Valley's extraordinary diversity: almonds, citrus, stone fruit, tomatoes, rice, wine grapes, peppers, and winter vegetables that most American cities see as imports from California.

The Sunday market in Cesar Chavez Plaza is smaller and more artisan-focused, with prepared foods and coffee alongside produce. The Tuesday market at Cesar Chavez Plaza caters mainly to the downtown lunch crowd. For the most impressive selection, the Saturday Capitol Mall market is the one to visit.
