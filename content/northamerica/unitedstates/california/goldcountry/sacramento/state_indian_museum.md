  Ishi, the last surviving member of the Yahi people, emerged from hiding in the Sierra Nevada foothills in 1911 after living in isolation for years following the massacre of his tribe. He spent the last five years of his life at UC Berkeley, working with anthropologist Alfred Kroeber. Artefacts related to Ishi's time in the Sierra are among the most moving objects in the museum.

Adjacent to Sutter's Fort, this small but carefully assembled museum presents California's Native peoples on their own terms — not as victims of colonisation (though that history is documented) but as the richest diversity of indigenous cultures in North America, with over 100 distinct groups living in California before European contact.

The permanent collection includes baskets, regalia, tools, and ceremonial objects from across California. The basketry collection is exceptional — California tribes produced some of the most refined basketwork in the world, and examples here show the extraordinary skill involved. A garden outside plants indigenous food plants with explanatory labels.
