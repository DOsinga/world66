story: >
  John Sutter built his fort on this site in 1839 as the hub of his 48,000-acre ranch, New Helvetia. He employed James Marshall to build a sawmill at Coloma, 50 kilometres east, and it was Marshall who discovered gold in the millrace on 24 January 1848 — a discovery Sutter tried desperately to keep secret. He failed. The Gold Rush stripped him of his workers and his land; he died in poverty, his empire dissolved by the very rush his property had triggered.
---

The reconstruction of John Sutter's 1839 fort sits in the middle of modern Sacramento, a thick-walled adobe compound that was the first major Euro-American settlement in the Central Valley. It was the destination for wagon trains crossing the Sierra Nevada, the hospital for the survivors of the Donner Party, and the launching pad for the California Republic — the short-lived Bear Flag Revolt of 1846 was organised in part from here.

The state park runs living-history programmes on weekends, with costumed interpreters demonstrating blacksmithing, weaving, and muzzle-loading. Even without them, the audio tour is worthwhile — the fort is surprisingly large and the restoration faithful to the original, right down to the cannon ports in the inner walls.
