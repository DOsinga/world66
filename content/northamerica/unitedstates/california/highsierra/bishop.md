Bishop is the largest town in the Owens Valley, a narrow strip of high desert between the Sierra Nevada and the White Mountains in eastern [California](/northamerica/unitedstates/california). At 1,200 metres elevation, it serves as the main supply stop for hikers, climbers, and skiers heading into the backcountry.

The town itself is unassuming — a single main street with motels, gear shops, and a few good restaurants — but the surroundings are spectacular. The [Buttermilk Boulders](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/highsierra/bishop/buttermilk_boulders) west of town draw rock climbers from around the world. The [Ancient Bristlecone Pine Forest](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/highsierra/bishop/ancient_bristlecone_pine_forest) in the White Mountains above Bishop is home to the oldest known living trees on earth, some more than 4,700 years old.

The [Laws Railroad Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/highsierra/bishop/laws_railroad_museum), a few kilometres north, preserves a narrow-gauge rail depot from the mining era. The [Paiute-Shoshone Cultural Center](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/highsierra/bishop/paiute_shoshone_cultural_center) documents the history of the region's indigenous people. Before heading out, stop at [Erick Schat's Bakkery](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/highsierra/bishop/erick_schats_bakkery) for sheepherder's bread — it has been keeping Owens Valley travellers fuelled since 1938.
