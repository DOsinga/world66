South Lake Tahoe sits at the southern end of Lake Tahoe, straddling the California-Nevada state line. The lake itself — deep blue, surrounded by snow-capped Sierra Nevada peaks — is the reason people come, and it is genuinely spectacular. The town around it is a different matter: a sprawl of motels, casinos, ski resorts, and fast-food strips that lacks the charm of the lake it serves. Come for the landscape, not the town.

The outdoor options are the main draw year-round. In winter, Heavenly Mountain Resort offers skiing on both sides of the state line with extraordinary lake views from the upper runs. In summer, the lake draws swimmers, kayakers, and paddleboarders to beaches like Pope Beach and Baldwin Beach, while Emerald Bay — a 3-mile glacially carved inlet with a small island and a 1929 Viking-style castle called Vikingsholm — is the most photogenic spot on the lake. The New York gambling strip across the Nevada line operates 24 hours, offers cheaper lodging than California, and has enough live music and nightlife to keep non-skiers busy.

Getting here from Sacramento or San Francisco takes about 3.5 hours by car over the Sierra Nevada. The road over Echo Summit offers the first views of the lake as you descend.
