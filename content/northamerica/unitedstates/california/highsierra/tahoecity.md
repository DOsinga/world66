Tahoe City sits on the northwest shore of [Lake Tahoe](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/highsierra/laketahoe), where the Truckee River begins its run toward Nevada. It is the quieter, more laid-back alternative to South Lake Tahoe — a small mountain town with a handful of good restaurants, a pebble beach, and easy access to some of the Sierra's best skiing and hiking.

[Fanny Bridge](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/highsierra/tahoecity/fanny_bridge_and_lake_tahoe_dam), where the Truckee River flows out of the lake, draws crowds who lean over the railing to watch enormous trout in the clear water below. The adjacent [Gatekeeper's Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/highsierra/tahoecity/gatekeepers_museum) covers the history of the north shore and holds a superb Native American basket collection. In summer the river is popular for [rafting](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/highsierra/tahoecity/truckee_river_rafting), and the [Tahoe Rim Trail](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/highsierra/tahoecity/tahoe_rim_trail) passes through town for hikers heading into the backcountry.

Winter is when Tahoe City earns its reputation. [Palisades Tahoe](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/highsierra/tahoecity/ski_resorts) (host of the 1960 Winter Olympics) is 15 minutes south; Alpine Meadows, Northstar, and Sugar Bowl are all within easy reach. The combination of reliable Sierra snowfall and proximity to San Francisco makes this one of the most visited ski areas in the United States.
