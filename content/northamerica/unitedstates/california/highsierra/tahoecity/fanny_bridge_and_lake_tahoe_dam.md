story: >
  The Lake Tahoe Dam was built between 1909 and 1913 to control the level of the
  lake for downstream irrigation in Nevada — raising the natural lake level by up to
  ten feet. The dam is 18 feet high and 109 feet long, with 17 bays that can release
  up to 2,100 cubic feet per second into the Truckee River. It was listed on the
  National Register of Historic Places in 1981. The adjacent Fanny Bridge gets its
  name from the posture of tourists who lean over the railing to see the fish below.

At the very point where Lake Tahoe ends and the Truckee River begins, the historic Lake Tahoe Dam controls the flow of one of the largest alpine lakes in the world into its only outlet. The structure is modest in scale but significant in consequence: it regulates the top six feet of the lake, which represents an enormous volume of water and critically affects downstream Nevada's agricultural water supply.

Fanny Bridge, immediately adjacent, is where visitors lean over the rail to stare at the enormous rainbow and brown trout visible in the crystal-clear water below. The fish congregate here because the outflow concentrates them. In late summer and early autumn, the trout can reach impressive sizes.

The surrounding area is a pleasant spot to start a walk along the Truckee River's first miles, passing through Tahoe City's small downtown. The river is popular for summer rafting — several outfitters operate from just downstream.
