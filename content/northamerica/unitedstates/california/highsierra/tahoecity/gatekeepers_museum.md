The Gatekeeper's Museum occupies the reconstructed log cabin of the former dam gatekeeper — the official responsible for operating the Lake Tahoe Dam. The original cabin, which dated to the early 20th century, burned in 1978 and was rebuilt using historical photographs and period techniques. The result is an authentic reproduction that works well as a museum space.

The North Lake Tahoe Historical Society operates the site. Inside, the exhibits cover Tahoe City and the north shore's history from Native American occupation through the early tourism era, the Tahoe Tavern resort period, and into the present. Vintage photographs, boats, and period artifacts give a good sense of how the lake evolved from wilderness to resort destination over the 20th century.

The adjacent Marion Steinbach Basket Museum contains one of the finest collections of Native American baskets in California — over 400 baskets representing tribes from across the western United States. It is an unexpected cultural depth for a small lakeside town, and a worthwhile hour.

The museum is seasonal — open summer months only. Admission is modest.
