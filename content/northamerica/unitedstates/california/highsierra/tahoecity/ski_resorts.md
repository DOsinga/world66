Tahoe City's position on the north shore puts it within close reach of some of the best skiing in the United States. Palisades Tahoe (formerly Squaw Valley, host of the 1960 Winter Olympics) is about 15 minutes south of town. Alpine Meadows, one of the most respected intermediate and expert resorts in the region, is directly adjacent. Northstar California is 20 minutes east. Sugar Bowl and Granlibakken — Tahoe's oldest ski resort, dating to the 1930s — are also within easy range.

Annual snowfall in the Lake Tahoe basin averages over 400 inches in good years. The season typically runs from late November to April, with peak conditions in January and February. Palisades Tahoe and Alpine Meadows are now connected by a shared lift system, making them effectively one of the largest ski areas in North America.

For non-skiers, Granlibakken near Tahoe City offers sledding and snowshoeing on a gentle scale that works well for families with young children. The Village at Palisades Tahoe has ice skating, shopping, and dining even if you never put on skis.
