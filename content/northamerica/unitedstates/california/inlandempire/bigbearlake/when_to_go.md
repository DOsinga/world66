---
title: When to Go
type: section
---

Big Bear works in two distinct modes. Winter (December through March) is ski season, when the town fills up on weekends with families from Los Angeles and the Inland Empire. Snowfall can be heavy — the lake sits at 6,752 feet and receives 100 inches of snow in a good year — but it's also variable, and drought years have meant lean seasons. Weekends from January through Presidents Day weekend are the most crowded. Book accommodation months in advance.

Summer (June through September) is the second peak. The lake dominates: boating, fishing, and water sports draw crowds, and the temperatures are a welcome 20–25 degrees cooler than the LA basin. Summer weekends can also be congested on the main road. Spring and autumn are the quietest seasons — the ski slopes are closed, the summer crowds are gone, and the prices drop. Autumn brings the best foliage, particularly the aspens and oaks on the north-facing slopes.
