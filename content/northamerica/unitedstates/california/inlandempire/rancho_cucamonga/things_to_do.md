---
title: "Things to Do"
type: section
---

Rancho Cucamonga's reasons to stop are mostly about Route 66, the old Cucamonga wine country, and the city's collection of surviving historic buildings. The restored 1915 **Cucamonga Service Station** on Foothill Boulevard is the most photogenic Route 66 site in the Inland Empire; the 1860 **John Rains House** — an early Anglo-American adobe — and the adjacent **Tapia Adobe** site together tell the story of the transition from Spanish land-grant ranching to Anglo settlement. The **Etiwanda Historic District** preserves a neighbourhood of Victorian-era homes and eucalyptus-lined streets from the 1880s temperance colony.

The **Cucamonga Valley** was one of the largest wine regions in 19th-century California, and a few historic wineries — most notably **Galleano Winery** in nearby Mira Loma — still operate. The **Pal Recording Studio site** on Archibald Avenue is a pilgrimage for fans of Frank Zappa, who briefly owned and worked out of the studio in the 1960s. Most visitors come for **Victoria Gardens**, a large open-air shopping and cultural district with the Lewis Family Playhouse and public library. The San Gabriel Mountains rise directly behind the city; **Cucamonga Canyon** gives quick access to wilderness trails.
