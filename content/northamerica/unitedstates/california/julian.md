Julian is a small gold-rush town in the Cuyamaca Mountains east of [San Diego](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/sandiego), sitting at about 1,250 metres. Gold was discovered here in 1870 by Fred Coleman, a formerly enslaved man who spotted colour in a creek, and the town's Main Street still has the look of a 19th-century mining settlement — wooden storefronts, a one-room schoolhouse, and the [Eagle and High Peak Mine](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/julian/eagle_mine), which offers underground tours.

The [Julian Gold Rush Hotel](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/julian/julian_gold_rush_hotel) on Main Street is the oldest operating hotel in Southern California, built in 1897 by Albert Robinson, a formerly enslaved Tennessean who came west and stayed. The [Julian Pioneer Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/julian/julian_pioneer_museum) fills in the broader history.

Julian is best known for its apple orchards and the pies that come from them. The town is surrounded by apple farms, and the bakeries on Main Street — Mom's Pies chief among them — compete for the title of best [apple pie in the county](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/julian/apple_pie). It makes an easy day trip from San Diego — about an hour's drive — and is especially popular in autumn when the harvest is in full swing.

The mountains around Julian offer good hiking and outdoor space. [Cuyamaca Rancho State Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/julian/cuyamaca_rancho_state_park) stretches south of town through pine and oak forest, with trails to the peak at 1,900 metres. [William Heise County Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/julian/william_heise_county_park) to the southwest is quieter and well suited to families.
