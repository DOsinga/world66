Livermore sits at the eastern edge of the San Francisco Bay Area, in a sun-baked valley that feels a world away from the fog and tech campuses to the west. It is best known for two things: wine and science. The [Livermore Valley](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/livermore/livermore_valley_wine) is one of California's oldest wine regions, with dozens of wineries open for tastings along Tesla Road and Mines Road — anchored by [Wente](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/livermore/wente_vineyards) and [Concannon](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/livermore/concannon_vineyard), both founded in 1883. Less crowded and less expensive than Napa.

The science connection comes from [Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/livermore/lawrence_livermore_lab), a major federal research facility that has been here since 1952 and is home to the [National Ignition Facility](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/livermore/national_ignition_facility), which achieved the first net-positive nuclear fusion reaction in 2022. You cannot tour the lab itself, but the Discovery Center offers a free window into the work done there. Livermore also famously hosts the [Centennial Light](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/livermore/centennial_light) — the world's longest-burning lightbulb, switched on in 1901.

[Downtown Livermore](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/livermore/downtown_livermore) has been revitalised in recent years, with a walkable stretch of First Street lined with restaurants, tasting rooms, and the [Bankhead Theater](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/livermore/bankhead_theater). It is a pleasant base for exploring the valley's wineries or for a day trip from San Francisco, about an hour's drive west.
