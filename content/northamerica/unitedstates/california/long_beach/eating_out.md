---
title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

Long Beach has a genuinely good restaurant scene that reflects its demographics — one of the most ethnically diverse cities in the US. The Cambodian community in particular has made a lasting mark on the food landscape, and the city has some of the best Cambodian food outside of Southeast Asia.

**Phnom Penh Noodle Shack** on Atlantic Avenue is one of the landmarks — a cramped, no-nonsense restaurant that has been feeding the Cambodian community for decades. The num banh chok (Khmer noodles with fish curry broth) and lort cha (stir-fried rice noodles) are the things to order.

**Michael's on Naples** is the upscale choice for Italian food, with a terrace on the Naples canal. It's expensive but the pasta is made in-house and the setting is unique in the city.

The stretch of **2nd Street in Belmont Shore** has the highest concentration of restaurants and works for casual browsing — there's Mexican, Japanese, Thai, and American food within a few blocks of each other. **Rock Bottom Restaurant & Brewery** has a serviceable brewpub menu and is one of the more reliable options on the strip.

For tacos, the strip of trucks and small restaurants along **Anaheim Street** in the Cambodian-Latino neighbourhood near Pacific is the real thing — $2-3 tacos, excellent al pastor, open until 2am on weekends.
