  The Nature Center was created in 1969 from what had been a gravel pit, then a city dump. The site was remediated and planted with native California species, and it now hosts over 150 bird species through the year. The transformation from industrial waste site to functioning wetland habitat took less than twenty years — one of the more successful urban ecological restoration projects in Southern California.

El Dorado Nature Center is a 100-acre nature preserve within the larger El Dorado Regional Park in east Long Beach, and it is one of the more surprising places in the city. The centre has two lakes, several kilometres of walking trails through riparian woodland, and a bird-watching programme that attracts serious birders as well as casual visitors. Entry requires a modest fee, which keeps the crowds manageable.

The trails loop through habitat that includes oak woodland, grassland, and freshwater marsh — unusual in an urban context. The small nature centre building has interpretive displays on the local ecosystems. Weekend guided walks cover bird identification and native plant ecology. Children particularly enjoy the nature centre's hands-on programmes. The adjacent El Dorado Regional Park has sports fields and picnic areas.
