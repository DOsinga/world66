Los Angeles sits within striking distance of some of Southern California's most rewarding destinations — desert, mountains, and ocean all within two hours.

[Catalina Island](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/catalina) sits about 22 miles offshore, reached by ferry from Long Beach or San Pedro in roughly an hour. The main town of Avalon has a compact walkable harbour and the landmark art deco Casino building; the interior is rugged and home to bison descended from a 1920s film shoot. You can snorkel in clear water at Lover's Cove, kayak along the coastline, or hike the Trans-Catalina Trail.

South on Pacific Coast Highway, [Laguna Beach](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/orangecounty/lagunabeach) is an artsy coastal city with rocky headlands, tide pools, and small sandy coves tucked between cliffs. It's about an hour from downtown — take PCH rather than the freeway for a far better drive past Crystal Cove.

[Malibu](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/malibu) stretches 27 miles along the coast north of [Santa Monica](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/santamonica). Surfrider Beach offers one of the most famous surf breaks in the world; Zuma is the go-to for wide open sand. Santa Monica is its own destination: the historic pier, Third Street Promenade, and the beach path south toward Venice are well worth the trip.

Leave early to beat the freeway traffic in either direction.
