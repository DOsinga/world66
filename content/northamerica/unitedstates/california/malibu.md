Malibu stretches for about 21 miles along the Pacific Coast Highway northwest of Los Angeles, where the Santa Monica Mountains meet the sea. It is less a town than a long coastal strip — the mountains crowd close on one side and the ocean on the other, leaving a narrow margin for the Pacific Coast Highway, a few restaurants and shops, and an unbroken run of beaches.

Malibu is famous for its residents — it has been celebrity real estate since the 1920s — but the beaches themselves are what draw most visitors. Under California law every beach in the state is public access; some homeowners have tried to convince people otherwise, but the right of way to the water exists at any point along the coast.

The surfing is the main draw. Surfrider Beach, just north of the Malibu Pier, is one of the most famous surf breaks in the world — a long, slow right-hander that gave early Californian surfing its character. Zuma Beach, further northwest, is a broader family beach with the cleanest water in the LA area. The Malibu Lagoon, where Malibu Creek meets the Pacific, is a nature area and historic site; the Adamson House on its shore is a National Historic Landmark covered in remarkable hand-painted Malibu tile.

From LA, Malibu is reached via the Pacific Coast Highway west from Santa Monica. The drive is reason enough to come.
