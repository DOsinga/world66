The Computer History Museum is one of the great specialist museums in the world. Located on North Shoreline Boulevard near the bay, it occupies a large former Silicon Graphics building and fills it with an extraordinary collection tracing the full arc of computing history: punch cards and mechanical tabulators, early mainframes, the first personal computers, the machines that made Silicon Valley rich, and the software that ran on all of them.

The exhibits are intelligently designed and avoid both nerdish impenetrability and tourist-friendly dumbing-down. The Revolution exhibit is particularly strong, walking through computing history chronologically with well-chosen artefacts and clear explanations. Docents are often retired engineers from companies like HP, Intel, or Fairchild Semiconductor, and their knowledge is remarkable. Allow two to three hours.
