Arcata is a small university town on Humboldt Bay — progressive, a little quirky, and genuinely pleasant to spend time in. It's home to [Cal Poly Humboldt](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/northcoast/arcata/cal_poly_humboldt) (formerly Humboldt State University), which gives the town its energy and its politics: Arcata has been governed by the Green Party and has declared itself a sanctuary city, banned Styrofoam, and built one of the country's first ecological wastewater treatment wetlands. For a place of 18,000 people, it has an outsized sense of itself, and mostly lives up to it.

The [Arcata Plaza](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/northcoast/arcata/arcata_plaza) at the center of town is the best thing about Arcata — a proper public square with a farmers market, good coffee shops, and independent bookstores. The surrounding streets are walkable and human-scaled in a way that most California towns aren't. The [Jacoby Building](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/northcoast/arcata/jacoby_building) on the Plaza's northeast corner is the oldest surviving commercial building in the city, dating to 1857.

On the edge of Humboldt Bay, the [Arcata Marsh](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/northcoast/arcata/arcata_marsh) is a world-class birding site and a landmark in ecological design — the city's wastewater treatment facility, reconceived as a wildlife sanctuary. From the campus, [Redwood Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/northcoast/arcata/redwood_park) begins where the quad ends, with old-growth coast redwoods reachable in minutes. If you're anywhere in the area on Memorial Day weekend, don't miss the [Kinetic Grand Championship](https://kineticgrandchampionship.com/) — a three-day race of elaborately engineered human-powered sculptures from Arcata to [Ferndale](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/northcoast/ferndale), one of the most genuinely fun spectacles in California.
