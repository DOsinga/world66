Fort Bragg is a working coastal town on the Mendocino County coast — not a resort, but a real place where fishing boats still go out every morning and the fog burns off by afternoon. It sits at the northern end of the dramatic stretch of Highway 1 that makes the Mendocino coast one of the most scenic drives in California, and it makes a better base than the prettier but pricier town of [Mendocino](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/northcoast/mendocino) itself, ten miles south.

The town's two most distinctive attractions could hardly be more different. [Glass Beach](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/northcoast/fortbragg/glass_beach) is a former municipal dump whose decades of discarded glass has been polished by the sea into smooth, frosted fragments — strange and beautiful. The [Skunk Train](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/northcoast/fortbragg/skunk_train) is a heritage railroad that threads 40 miles through redwood canyon into the mountains, following a route built in 1885 to haul lumber.

[Noyo Harbor](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/northcoast/fortbragg/noyo_harbor) is the working heart of the town — fishing boats, whale-watching cruises, and a handful of seafood restaurants on the water. The [Mendocino Coast Botanical Gardens](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/northcoast/fortbragg/mendocino_coast_botanical_gardens) occupy 47 acres of coastal bluff two miles south, with views down to sea stacks and harbor seals. The [North Coast Brewing Company](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/northcoast/fortbragg/north_coast_brewing) operates from a converted church and produces some of the best craft beer on the California coast — the Red Seal Ale and Rasputin Imperial Stout are the picks.

Fort Bragg is practical — reasonable prices, good seafood, shorter drive from San Francisco than you'd expect. Come for the Mendocino coast scenery and the gray whales (November through April) and stay here.
