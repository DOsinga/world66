Fortuna has been described as the Jewel of the North Coast. Located about 20 miles SSW of [Eureka](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/northcoast/eureka), its location about 7 miles from the Pacific Ocean gives it a somewhat more temperate climate. Where 55-62 degrees is the summer norm in Eureka, Fortuna will regularly reach 70-75. Winters are mild and, in a word, wet.

Billing itself as **The Friendly City** (with signs to that effect ringing its downtown core), Fortuna has slowly grown in size from 6,000 or so in the 1970s to its current size of just over 12,000 people. Fiercely proud of their city, Fortunans have resisted efforts by the big-box stores to encroach. It retains its small-town ambience with Mom-and-Pop still manning the counter and shutting the joint down for the high school football game on Friday night.

Like many smaller areas, Fortuna is an expensive pain to get to by any method other than by car. It's served by the Arcata/Eureka Airport about 40 miles to the north, with flights on a handful of regional carriers, but the fares tend toward the exorbitant. Greyhound runs through town, conveniently scheduled from the south but less so from the north, and a regional bus links southern Humboldt with the airport.

Fortuna's signature attraction is the [Fortuna Rodeo](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/northcoast/fortuna/fortuna_rodeo) the third week of July — small-town Americana with a carnival, soap box derby, dances, and a weekend rodeo. The rest of the time the appeal is its setting: the broad [Eel River](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/northcoast/fortuna/eel_river) flats, the redwoods nearby at [Rohner Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/northcoast/fortuna/rohner_park), and the easy day-trip access to the [Fernbridge](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/northcoast/fortuna/fernbridge), one of the oldest concrete arch bridges in the world.
