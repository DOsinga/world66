  The Benbow was built in 1926 by the Benbow family as a resort destination in its own right. Eleanor Roosevelt stayed here in 1934. So did Herbert Hoover. The Benbow Inn is a Tudor Revival hotel on the South Fork of the Eel River, two miles south of Garberville. It opened in 1926 and looks the part — half-timbered gables, leaded windows, a verandah that overlooks the river. Even if you are not staying, it is worth stopping to have a drink in the lobby bar or a meal in the dining room.

In summer the adjacent Benbow State Recreation Area runs down to the riverbank, where a seasonal dam creates a swimming hole popular with locals and campers. The combination of the historic hotel and the easy river access makes this a more appealing stop than Garberville proper for an afternoon or overnight stay.
