---
title: "Day Trips from Healdsburg"
type: section
---

Healdsburg's position at the northern end of Sonoma County puts it within easy reach of some excellent destinations by car.

**Guerneville and the Russian River coast (45 min):** Follow the Russian River southwest on River Road or Westside Road through the redwoods to Guerneville — a laid-back river town with good swimming holes, kayak rentals, and a distinctly different atmosphere from Healdsburg's wine-country polish. From Guerneville, the road continues another 30 minutes to Bodega Bay and the Sonoma Coast, with its dramatic headlands and beaches.

**Mendocino (2 hours):** The drive north on US-101 to Cloverdale, then west on Highway 128 through Anderson Valley, is one of the more scenic in Northern California. Anderson Valley itself is worth stopping in — a cool, foggy appellation producing excellent Pinot Noir and sparkling wine. The town of Mendocino sits on a headland above the Pacific; Portuguese-settled in the 19th century, it retains a Victorian downtown unlike any other on the California coast.

**Point Reyes National Seashore (1.5 hours):** Drive south on US-101 to Petaluma, then west through the dairy country of western Marin County to Point Reyes. The Point Reyes peninsula is a geological anomaly — a chunk of Pacific Plate that arrived here from far south — with elk herds, lighthouse visits, oyster farms, and dramatic coastal hiking.

**Napa Valley (1 hour):** Cross the Mayacamas Mountains east on Highway 128 through Calistoga into Napa Valley. The northern end of Napa — Calistoga, St. Helena — is less crowded than the southern end and retains more of the agricultural character that made the valley famous. The drive over the mountains through Knights Valley is attractive.

**Santa Rosa (25 min):** Sonoma County's largest city is worth an hour for the Charles M. Schulz Museum (creator of Peanuts) and the historic Railroad Square district. More useful as a practical stop — larger grocery stores, a real bookshop, the county airport.
