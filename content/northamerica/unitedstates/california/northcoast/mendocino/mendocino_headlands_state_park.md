The headlands that surround the village on three sides are protected as a state park and are entirely free to walk. The trails follow the clifftops for about three miles, passing sea arches, blow holes where surf surges through underground tunnels, and surge channels carved into the rock by centuries of wave action. On clear days the views north and south along the coast are exceptional. In winter and early spring, gray whales pass close enough to watch from the cliffs during their annual migration between Alaska and Baja California.

The main trailhead is beside the Ford House visitor center on Main Street; from there, the path runs west along the bluffs before turning north to loop back through town. Most people complete the full loop in one to two hours, though it is easy to spend longer watching the ocean. Bring a windproof layer even in summer — the headlands are exposed and the onshore breeze can be cold.
