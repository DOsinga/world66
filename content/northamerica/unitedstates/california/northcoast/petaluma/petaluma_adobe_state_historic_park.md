The adobe is what remains of General Mariano Vallejo's enormous Rancho Petaluma, which once sprawled across some 66,000 acres of rolling hills and river bottom north of the bay. Built in the 1830s and 1840s using Native American labor, the two-story L-shaped structure served as the administrative center of one of the largest private ranchos in California. Rooms were devoted to hide-curing, weaving, soap-making, and other industries that fed the hide-and-tallow trade with Boston merchants.

Today the adobe stands in good condition, preserved as a California State Historic Park, with period furnishings, tools, and interpretive exhibits across multiple rooms. Rangers lead weekend tours that explain the complex economics of mission-era and post-mission California. The setting is authentically rural — open grassland and hills visible in every direction — and the site is quiet and rarely crowded. Admission is a few dollars.
