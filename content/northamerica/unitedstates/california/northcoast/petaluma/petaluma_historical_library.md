The old Carnegie library building at 20 Fourth Street is one of Petaluma's most handsome pieces of architecture — a 1904 neoclassical pile in brick and stone with a fine reading room inside. It now operates as the Petaluma Historical Library and Museum, with collections covering the city's history from the Miwok period through the egg and butter empire of the 19th century to the present day.

The permanent exhibition tells the story of how Petaluma became the "World's Egg Basket" in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, when the area's poultry farms produced eggs by the trainload for San Francisco. The incubator and chicken rearing equipment on display at the museum belongs to an industry that has almost entirely vanished from the area but which shaped the landscape and the economy for decades. There are also rotating exhibitions on local architecture, agriculture, and social history. Admission is free or low-cost.
