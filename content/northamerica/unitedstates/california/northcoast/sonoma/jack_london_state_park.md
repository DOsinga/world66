Jack London spent the last years of his life on an 800-acre ranch in the Valley of the Moon, 13 kilometres north of Sonoma in Glen Ellen. The state historic park that now preserves the property includes the ruins of Wolf House, the author's 26-room dream home that burned before he could live in it, the smaller House of Happy Walls (which served as his widow Charmian's home and is now a museum), and miles of hiking trails through the oak woodland.

The ranch itself was London's most ambitious project — he was trying to build a model farm demonstrating sustainable agriculture before the term existed, with concrete pig pens, a eucalyptus plantation for lumber, and a gravity-fed irrigation system. The museum covers his remarkable life: born in poverty in San Francisco, self-educated, three continents before 30, the highest-paid American writer of his era. Trails connect the key sites; the longer loops climb through beautiful oak forest to ridge views of the Valley of the Moon.
