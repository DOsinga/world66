  Julia Morgan, who designed the Chapel of the Chimes, was the first woman licensed as an architect in California and the designer of Hearst Castle. The columbarium was her personal favourite among all her works. She described it as the project where she most successfully integrated indoor and outdoor space — a claim that is easy to believe once you are standing in one of its garden courtyards looking up at the vaulted ceiling.

A columbarium and funeral chapel designed by architect Julia Morgan in the 1920s, expanded through the 1950s. The complex is a labyrinthine Moorish-Gothic building arranged around internal courtyards and garden rooms, with arched ceilings, fountains, pools of light from stained glass, and thousands of niches holding the remains of Oakland's notable and ordinary dead alike.

It functions as a fully operational columbarium — you can visit freely as long as you are respectful. The building's constant addition of chapels and corridors over decades creates a spatial disorientation that is strangely beautiful. It sits on Piedmont Avenue in the hills above Oakland.
