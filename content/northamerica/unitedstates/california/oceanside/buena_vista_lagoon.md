  Buena Vista Lagoon is one of the last remaining freshwater lagoons in coastal Southern California. Unlike most lagoons along this coast, it isn't tidal — it was cut off from the ocean in the early 20th century by railroad and road construction, and has since filled with fresh water fed by groundwater and seasonal runoff. The change in hydrology created an unusual wildlife habitat that now supports nearly 200 bird species.
---

Buena Vista Lagoon sits on the border between Oceanside and Carlsbad, a 202-acre freshwater reserve that serves as a critical stopover on the Pacific Flyway. The lagoon is managed as an ecological reserve and draws serious birders: herons, egrets, coots, grebes, and hundreds of migratory species pass through or overwinter here.

The Audubon Society's Nature Center on the south shore (at 2202 South Coast Highway) offers trail access and educational programs. Walking trails along the lagoon's edges provide good views of the water without disturbing nesting birds. It's a genuine natural area in the middle of developed Southern California — easily overlooked but worth visiting.
