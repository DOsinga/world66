---
title: When to Go
type: section
---

**Winter (December–April)** is the main season. Palisades Tahoe typically opens in mid-November and can run into May when snowfall cooperates. The resort averages around 450 inches of snow a year, with January and February usually delivering the most reliable conditions. Holiday periods — Christmas week, Presidents' Day weekend — are crowded and expensive; mid-January through early February offers the best combination of snow and reasonable prices.

**Spring skiing** in March and April can be excellent: long sunny days, firm morning snow that softens by midday, and far fewer crowds. Lift ticket prices often drop. The risk is rain at lower elevations, but High Camp almost always has good snow through April.

**Summer (June–September)** is quieter but increasingly popular. The tram runs daily from late June, and the trail networks in the Granite Chief Wilderness are accessible from July once the snowpack melts. Temperatures in the valley are mild — warm afternoons, cool evenings — and the alpine wildflowers peak in July and August.

**Autumn** is the shoulder season: the ski resort is closed, summer crowds have left, and the aspens around Lake Tahoe turn gold in October. It is a good time for hiking without the summer rush, though some restaurants and services run reduced hours.

The Palisades Tahoe spring festival (typically late April) draws crowds for outdoor concerts and pond-skimming competitions.
