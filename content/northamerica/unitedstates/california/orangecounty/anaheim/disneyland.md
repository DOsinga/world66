  Opening day, July 17, 1955, was an internal catastrophe Disney called "Black Sunday." Counterfeit tickets flooded the gates, the crowd swelled to twice the expected number, freshly poured asphalt swallowed women's high heels in the summer heat, the Mark Twain riverboat nearly sank from overloading, and several rides broke down on live national television watched by an estimated 70 million people. Within a week, attendance had stabilized and lines stretched for hours. Within a year, Disneyland was the most visited attraction in the United States.

Disneyland is the original — the template that every other theme park in the world has either copied or defined itself against. Walt Disney opened it on 160 acres of former orange grove in Anaheim in 1955, and it has operated every day since (excepting the pandemic). The park is organized into themed lands radiating from the central hub: Main Street USA, Adventureland, Frontierland, Fantasyland, and Tomorrowland are the original five, with New Orleans Square and others added over the decades.

Adjacent Disney's California Adventure occupies a roughly equivalent footprint to the south, built on what was the original parking lot and opened in 2001. California Adventure has developed into a full-day park in its own right, strongest on thrill rides and with the immersive Cars Land and Avengers Campus among its best areas. The two parks share the Downtown Disney promenade — an outdoor mall of restaurants, shops, and entertainment — which is free to enter.

A single day is not enough for both parks. Most visitors buy a two-day ticket and split their time, prioritizing the classic Disneyland rides (Pirates of the Caribbean, Haunted Mansion, Matterhorn, Space Mountain) on the first day and California Adventure on the second. The Genie+ ride reservation system can reduce wait times significantly on busy days — worth purchasing unless you are visiting in the off-season. The parks are least crowded on weekdays in January and February.
