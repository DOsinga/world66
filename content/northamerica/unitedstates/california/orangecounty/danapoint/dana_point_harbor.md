story: "Before the harbour was built in 1966, Dana Point had one of the best surfing waves in California — a long right-hand break called Killer Dana that reformed three or four times across the bay. The harbour breakwater killed it. Local surfers fought the project for years, and a statue of legendary surfer Hobie Alter at Waterman's Plaza now commemorates the lost wave. The trade-off was a marina with capacity for 2,500 boats, the only major harbour between Newport Beach and Oceanside."
---

The hub of Dana Point: a sheltered marina with shops, restaurants, fishing charters, whale-watching boats, kayak and paddleboard rentals, and the Catalina Express ferry terminal. The harbour has been undergoing a slow, decade-long redevelopment that has gradually replaced its 1970s-era buildings with newer mixed-use structures.

Walk the breakwater out to the lighthouse for views back across the marina. Baby Beach, in a calm corner of the harbour, is the safest swimming spot for small children in the area. The Ocean Institute sits on the harbour's outer point. Whale-watching boats run year-round — grey whales in winter, blue whales and dolphins in summer.
