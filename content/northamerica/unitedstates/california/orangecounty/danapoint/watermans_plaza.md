story: "Killer Dana was one of the best longboard waves in California, breaking across the bay where the harbour now sits. The breakwater built in 1966 killed it. Hobie Alter's foam-and-fibreglass surfboard, mass-produced from a Dana Point factory starting in 1958, did more than any single thing to spread surfing from California to the rest of the world."
---

A small open plaza at the harbour with bronze statues of the legendary surfers and watermen associated with Dana Point: Hobie Alter, Phil Edwards, Bruce Brown of *The Endless Summer*, Joyce Hoffman, and others. Plaques explain who each was and what they did. The plaza is more poignant than a roadside monument has any business being, partly because the wave it commemorates was destroyed by the harbour the plaza now sits inside.

Worth ten minutes on a walk around the harbour. Bring a couple of surf-history nerds and you can stretch it into an hour.
