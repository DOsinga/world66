story: >
  The Laguna Coast Wilderness Park exists because of a decade-long fight against developers who planned to build 10,000 homes on the hills behind Laguna Beach. The land was acquired parcel by parcel through the 1980s and 1990s using a combination of public bonds, private donations, and conservation easements. The completed park covers 7,000 acres and protects the largest remaining stand of coastal sage scrub in Southern California — habitat for the California gnatcatcher and the endangered Quino checkerspot butterfly.
---

The Laguna Coast Wilderness Park covers the hills directly behind Laguna Beach, an unexpected expanse of coastal sage scrub, canyon oak woodland, and chaparral accessible from several trailheads within walking distance of the town. The Ridgeline Trail on the park's western edge offers views back over the coast; the canyon trails through Laurel Canyon and James Dilley Preserve are quieter and more varied.

The park is open daily from dawn to dusk. Dogs are not allowed. The Nix Nature Center near the Canyon Acres entrance has trail maps and information on current conditions. The terrain is dry and exposed in summer — bring water, wear sunscreen, and go early. Spring wildflowers (late February through April) are the best time for color. The park is the Laguna Beach experience that most visitors miss completely.
