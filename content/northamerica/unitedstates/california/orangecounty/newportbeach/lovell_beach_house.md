The Lovell Beach House is one of the most important pieces of modernist architecture in the United States. R.M. Schindler designed it in 1925 and 1926 — a strikingly forward-looking concrete-frame structure raised on five large reinforced-concrete piers like an exoskeleton, with the living spaces suspended within the frame.

The house is a private residence and not open to the public, but the exterior — visible from East Oceanfront on the Balboa Peninsula — is well worth a walk past. It has been a National Historic Landmark since 1971. The visual impact, even now, is striking: the bare concrete bones in the middle of a row of conventional beach cottages.
