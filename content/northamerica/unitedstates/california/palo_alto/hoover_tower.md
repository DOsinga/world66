  The tower is named after Herbert Hoover, class of 1895, who was Stanford's most famous early alumnus. The Hoover Institution inside the tower is a conservative policy think tank that Hoover founded in 1919 with a collection of documents he had gathered while organising food relief in post-WWI Europe. It remains one of the most influential policy research centres in the United States.

The tallest structure on campus at 87 metres, Hoover Tower is the unmistakable landmark visible from most of the Stanford campus and from the surrounding roads. An observation deck near the top offers a panoramic view of the Bay Area — San Francisco Bay to the east, the Santa Cruz Mountains to the west, and the full sweep of Silicon Valley below.

The deck is open to the public during daylight hours for a small fee (a few dollars). The tower also contains exhibition spaces for the Hoover Institution's archival collections, which are worth a look if you have a taste for 20th-century political history.
