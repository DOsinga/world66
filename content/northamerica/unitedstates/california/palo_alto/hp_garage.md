story: >
  In 1938, Bill Hewlett and Dave Packard rented a house at 367 Addison Avenue for $45 a month, using the detached garage as their workshop. Their first product — the HP200A audio oscillator, priced at $54.40 — found an early customer in Walt Disney, who bought eight of them to calibrate the sound systems in the theatres showing Fantasia. The garage was designated a California Historical Landmark in 1989 as the "birthplace of Silicon Valley."

The HP Garage is a small, unassuming wooden shed on a quiet residential street in the Professorville neighbourhood, and that is exactly the point. There is nothing to go inside — the garage is private property and not open to visitors — but it is worth standing in front of for a moment. A small plaque on the wall and a California historical marker explain the story.

The surrounding neighbourhood, with its craftsman bungalows and wide sidewalks, gives a sense of what Palo Alto looked like before it became famous. The garage is a ten-minute walk south of University Avenue.
