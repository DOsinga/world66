story: >
  Gaspar de Portolá's expedition camped under this redwood in November 1769 while exploring the
  unknown peninsula north of Monterey. The tree was already several hundred years old then. The
  name "El Palo Alto" (the tall tree) eventually transferred from the redwood itself to the
  surrounding town and university.
---

A single, lopsided coast redwood standing on the banks of San Francisquito Creek behind the Palo
Alto Caltrain station. It's the oldest landmark in town by an enormous margin — roughly 1,067 years
at last ring count — and the silhouette has appeared on the city seal since 1894.

The tree had a twin until a storm broke it in half in the 1880s; the surviving trunk leans
markedly to one side. A short trail leads down from El Camino Real to the base. The Stanford
band's mascot is a costumed version of the same tree, complete with a face — a piece of Bay Area
absurdity worth knowing about.
