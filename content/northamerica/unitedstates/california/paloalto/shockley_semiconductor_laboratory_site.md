The Shockley Semiconductor Laboratory site is a niche but meaningful stop for anyone interested in Silicon Valley history. William Shockley founded the lab after sharing the Nobel Prize for the transistor, and although the company itself faltered, the engineers who left it helped launch Fairchild Semiconductor and the wider start-up culture that followed.

There is not much to see physically, so this is a history stop rather than a conventional attraction. Pair it with the HP Garage, Stanford, and the Computer History Museum in nearby Mountain View if you are tracing the valley's origins. Its importance lies in the chain reaction that began here, not in the building fabric.
