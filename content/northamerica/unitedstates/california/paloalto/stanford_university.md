  Stanford was founded in 1885 by railroad baron Leland Stanford and his wife Jane in memory of their
  only child, who died of typhoid at fifteen. The endowment was the largest charitable gift in
  American history at that point; the campus opened in 1891 with David Starr Jordan as president.

The Stanford campus is the most beautiful in America by some distance — a Frederick Law Olmsted plan
of long arcades, sandstone walls, palm-lined approaches, and red-tile roofs spread over a former
horse farm at the foot of the Santa Cruz Mountains. The 1891 Main Quad and **Memorial Church** are
the obvious starting point, with the church's gold and Venetian mosaic facade glowing in late
afternoon light.

The **Cantor Arts Center** is free and excellent; the adjacent **Rodin Sculpture Garden** holds the
largest collection of Rodin bronzes outside Paris, including a powerful cast of the Gates of Hell.
Climb **Hoover Tower** (free, 285 feet up) for the long view across to the Bay. Don't miss the
**Papua New Guinea Sculpture Garden** tucked away in a redwood grove — a thicket of carved totems
that few visitors find. The campus is comfortably walkable in a half-day, longer if you stop to
read every Rodin plaque.
