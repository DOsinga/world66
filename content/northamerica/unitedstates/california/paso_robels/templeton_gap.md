The Templeton Gap refers to the natural break in the Santa Lucia Range that channels cool marine air from Morro Bay into the Paso Robles wine region each afternoon. This daily temperature swing — often 20–25°C between the day's high and the overnight low — is what gives the wines their structure and allows grapes to ripen slowly without losing acidity.

The gap itself is not a single place to visit but a district of wineries strung along the Westside and Templeton area. Wineries like Turley Wine Cellars, Castoro Cellars, and Sculpterra are clustered here. It is worth understanding the geography if you are choosing between the Westside and Eastside — the Westside, with its limestone soils and afternoon winds, generally produces wines with more tension; the Eastside, sheltered and warmer, tends toward riper, rounder styles.
