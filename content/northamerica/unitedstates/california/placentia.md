Placentia is a small suburban city in Orange County, California, southeast of Los Angeles, with a population of around 50,000. The name comes from the Latin for "a pleasant place," bestowed in 1879.

The city has an interesting agricultural past — it was here that the first commercial Valencia orange grove in Orange County was planted in 1880, contributing to the region's name and identity. The [George Key Ranch](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/placentia/george_key_ranch), built in 1898, is the oldest standing house in Placentia and a local historic landmark. The [Bradford House](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/placentia/bradford_house) and the site of the 19th-century Ontiveros Adobe on Crowther Avenue are the other main historic points of interest.

As a travel destination, Placentia is primarily of interest as a quiet residential base for visiting [Disneyland](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/placentia/disneyland_day_trip) (a few miles west in Anaheim) and other Orange County attractions. [Old Town Placentia](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/placentia/old_town_placentia) is the closest thing to a walkable centre, and the nearby [Fullerton Arboretum](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/placentia/fullerton_arboretum) is the best outdoor stop. The city is effectively built out and has the character of a mature bedroom community rather than a visitor destination in its own right.
