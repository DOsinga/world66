Salinas is best known as the hometown of John Steinbeck, and the agricultural valley that surrounds it is the landscape he wrote about in East of Eden, Of Mice and Men, and The Grapes of Wrath. The [National Steinbeck Center](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/salinas/national_steinbeck_center) downtown is the main draw for literary travellers, housing manuscripts, personal effects, and the camper truck from Travels with Charley. A few blocks away, the [John Steinbeck House](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/salinas/steinbeck_house) — the Victorian where he was born in 1902 — is open for tours and runs a lunch restaurant. The [John Steinbeck Library](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/salinas/john_steinbeck_library) holds the most complete research archive in California.

The city sits at the northern end of the Salinas Valley, sometimes called the "Salad Bowl of the World" for its staggering agricultural output. Salinas itself is a working agricultural city — unpretentious and largely Latino — and it does not have the polished feel of its coastal neighbours. In July, the [California Rodeo Salinas](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/salinas/california_rodeo) is one of the largest professional rodeos on the West Coast and worth timing a visit around.

What Salinas does have is location. [Monterey](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/monterey) and the coast are just 20 minutes west over the hills, Big Sur is an hour south, and Pinnacles National Park is an hour to the southeast. The former army base of [Fort Ord](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/salinas/fort_ord), now a national monument, is a short drive west with 86 miles of trails through recovering coastal scrubland. If you are exploring this stretch of California and want affordable lodging away from the tourist markup, Salinas is a practical base.
