  Fort Ord was one of the largest US Army bases on the West Coast, and at its
  peak in the 1950s housed 50,000 soldiers. The base was decommissioned in 1994
  and left largely intact — a ghost city of barracks, warehouses, and parade
  grounds slowly being reclaimed by Monterey pine and chaparral. Parts of the
  former base are now a national monument with 86 miles of hiking and mountain
  biking trails.

Fort Ord National Monument covers more than 7,000 acres of the former US Army base, now managed by the Bureau of Land Management as a recreational area and conservation zone. The combination of military history and recovered coastal scrubland habitat makes it one of the more unusual destinations on the Monterey Peninsula.

The trail network is extensive and well-marked, ranging from easy flat walks to more challenging routes through the rolling terrain. The landscape of Monterey pine, coast live oak, and chaparral supports several rare species, including the threatened sand garter snake. Remnants of the former base — concrete slabs, overgrown roads, a few intact structures — appear throughout the trails. The main trailhead at Parker Flats has a good map kiosk. The monument is open for day use; no fee. Mountain bikers and hikers share the trail system.
