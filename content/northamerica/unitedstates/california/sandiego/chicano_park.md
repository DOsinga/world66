Chicano Park sits in the heart of Barrio Logan, wedged under the sweeping concrete structure of the San Diego–Coronado Bridge. The park's eighty-plus murals cover the bridge's support columns and abutments — the largest collection of Chicano outdoor murals in the United States. The paintings span fifty years of work, from the founding muralists of the 1970s to recent additions, covering history, mythology, labour, immigration, and the Aztec past.

The murals are not a gallery — they are floor-to-ceiling on concrete columns meant to hold up a freeway, painted in the middle of a residential neighbourhood that fights for relevance against surrounding industrial uses. The contrast between the brutalist infrastructure and the vivid imagery is part of what makes the place extraordinary. Walking the columns takes about an hour if you stop and read the work.

The neighbourhood around the park — Barrio Logan — has become one of San Diego's more interesting destinations for food and art, with taqueries and galleries along National Avenue within a few blocks.
