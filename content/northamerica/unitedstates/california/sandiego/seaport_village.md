Seaport Village is a 14-acre waterfront shopping and dining complex on the harbour between the Convention Center and the Maritime Museum, built in 1980 on what was previously a water treatment facility. The architecture is a deliberate pastiche of historic California — a New England lighthouse here, a Mexican village plaza there — which dates it as a 1980s commercial concept but has also given it a pleasantly relaxed character compared to the more corporate waterfront developments elsewhere.

The reason to come is the location rather than the shops — the bay views here are unobstructed, stretching south to the Coronado Bridge and north toward the aircraft carriers at the naval station. The harbour seal colony that hauls out on the rocks just south of the complex is visible from the path. The carousel near the entrance (a 1890s hand-carved machine) is a genuine antique.

The restaurants are adequate rather than distinguished. Better to walk through on the way between the Maritime Museum to the north and Embarcadero Park to the south, pausing for the views rather than treating it as a destination.
