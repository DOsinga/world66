San Mateo sits at the geographic center of the San Francisco Peninsula, halfway between San Francisco and San Jose, and it shows: the city has the workaday confidence of a place that doesn't need to prove anything. It's a real town with a real downtown — tree-lined streets, independent restaurants, a walkable grid — set against the backdrop of the Santa Cruz Mountains to the west and the bay to the east.

The city is best known to outsiders for [Coyote Point](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/sanmateo/coyote_point), a bayside park with a natural history museum and a marina, and for the network of trails that run into the hills above town. [Downtown](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/sanmateo/downtown) has a lively restaurant scene anchored by a mix of long-standing Japanese places and newer spots reflecting the Bay Area's broader food culture. [Third and Fourth Avenues](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/sanmateo/downtown_third_fourth) are where most of the action is, and the annual Obon festival in August reflects the neighbourhood's strong Japanese American heritage. The [Japanese garden](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/sanmateo/japanese_garden) in Central Park, designed in 1966, is a genuinely peaceful place amid the Peninsula's suburban density. For serious food, [Sushi Yoshizumi](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/sanmateo/sushi_yoshizumi) on East 4th Avenue runs a Michelin-starred omakase that punches well above its suburban address.

San Mateo County — which San Mateo the city sits in, not to be confused with — stretches from the bay to the coast and includes some genuinely beautiful country: the Filoli estate in Woodside, the tidepools at Fitzgerald Marine Reserve, and the old-growth redwoods of Huddart Park. San Mateo itself makes a practical and pleasant base for exploring the mid-Peninsula.
