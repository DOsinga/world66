The Lobero is the oldest continuously operating theatre in California, opened in 1873 by the Italian-born musician José Lobero and rebuilt in its current Spanish Colonial Revival form in 1924. It is small (about 600 seats) and intimate — most performers complain about, or rave about, how close the front row is to the stage.

The programme leans toward jazz, chamber music, folk, and dance, with occasional theatre. Many touring acts who play larger venues in Los Angeles book the Lobero when they come up the coast, so the calendar punches above the city's weight. Check what's on when planning the visit; an evening here is one of the more pleasant uses of a Santa Barbara night.
