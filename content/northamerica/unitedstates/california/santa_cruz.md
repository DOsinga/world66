Santa Cruz is a laid-back beach city on the northern curve of Monterey Bay, about 75 miles south of San Francisco. The [Boardwalk](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/santa_cruz/santa_cruz_beach_boardwalk) — one of the oldest amusement parks on the West Coast — runs along the main beach, with a 1924 wooden roller coaster and a 1911 carousel that are both National Historic Landmarks.

The town has a strong counterculture streak, thanks in part to the University of California campus in the redwood-covered hills above. [Pacific Avenue](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/santa_cruz/pacific_avenue) downtown is the main drag, with independent bookshops, cafes, surf shops and restaurants — rebuilt after the 1989 Loma Prieta earthquake in a form more pedestrian-friendly than what it replaced. The surf breaks at [Steamer Lane](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/santa_cruz/steamer_lane), right below the lighthouse, draw serious surfers year-round and are good to watch even if you do not ride. The [Santa Cruz Wharf](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/santa_cruz/santa_cruz_wharf) extends 2,745 feet into the bay, with sea lions barking from beneath the decking.

The surrounding area is spectacular. [Henry Cowell Redwoods State Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/santa_cruz/henry_cowell_redwoods) is a short drive into the mountains and has old-growth groves — some trees over 1,500 years old — you can walk through in an afternoon. Ano Nuevo State Park to the north is home to a massive elephant seal colony. [The Mystery Spot](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/santa_cruz/the_mystery_spot), a kitschy roadside attraction in the woods, has been confusing tourists since 1939. [Mission Santa Cruz](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/santa_cruz/mission_santa_cruz) in the old town preserves a 1931 replica of the 1791 California mission.
