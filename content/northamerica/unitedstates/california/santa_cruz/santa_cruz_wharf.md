  The wharf was built in 1914 and has been extended and rebuilt several times since — including after storms in 1983 that destroyed most of the outer section. The replacement planking was made from tropical hardwood, a decision that has aged poorly from an environmental standpoint. More interestingly, the pilings beneath the wharf have been colonised by California sea lions, which haul out on the cross-braces below deck level and can be heard barking from the surface — a sound the restaurants do their best to muffle.

The Santa Cruz Wharf extends 2,745 feet into Monterey Bay, making it one of the longest wooden piers on the West Coast. It is a working pier — fishing boats dock here, and the outer end has a small fish market — but it has long been colonised by restaurants, gift shops, and the sea lions that live beneath the decking and make themselves heard throughout.

Walk the full length for the views back to the Boardwalk and the mountains above the city. Harbour seals and sea otters are regularly spotted from the rail. The clam chowder in a sourdough bread bowl is the standard lunch, available at several stalls on the pier.
