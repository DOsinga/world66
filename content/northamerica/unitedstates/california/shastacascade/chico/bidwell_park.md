  Bidwell Park was donated to the City of Chico in 1905 by Annie Bidwell, the widow of General John Bidwell, who had made the first overland emigrant crossing to California in 1841. At 3,670 acres it is one of the largest municipal parks in the United States — larger than Central Park and Golden Gate Park combined. Parts of the 1938 film The Adventures of Robin Hood, starring Errol Flynn, were filmed in the lower park's towering valley oaks, which were deemed convincing enough to stand in for Sherwood Forest.

Bidwell Park is Chico's defining feature — a ribbon of green that stretches nine miles from downtown up into the oak-studded foothills, following Chico Creek through several distinct environments. The lower park, easily reached on foot from the university, has lawns, picnic areas, a golf course, and a children's playground. The creek widens into swimmable pools in the middle section, most famously at One-Mile and Five-Mile Recreation Areas, where locals come to cool off all summer. The upper park is wilder: undeveloped chaparral and canyon terrain good for hiking, mountain biking, and spotting deer and wild turkeys.

The valley oaks in the lower park are enormous — some are thought to be several hundred years old — and the canopy they form over the main road is a Chico institution. The park is free and open year-round. Summer weekends bring crowds to the swimming holes; early mornings in spring the park is almost entirely yours.
