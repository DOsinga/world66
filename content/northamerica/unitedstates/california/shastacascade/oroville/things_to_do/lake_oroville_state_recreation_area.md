Lake Oroville is California's second-largest reservoir, formed by Oroville Dam and stretching 167 miles of shoreline into the Sierra Nevada foothills. The State Recreation Area surrounding it is the main reason most people visit Oroville — a sprawling aquatic playground that draws boaters, anglers, water-skiers, and houseboaters from across Northern California.

Summer is the peak season, when the blue-green water warms enough for swimming and houseboat rentals fill up weeks in advance. Bidwell Marina and Lime Saddle Marina both offer boat launches and rentals ranging from fishing skiffs to full-sized houseboats that sleep a dozen people. The lake also has several campgrounds, from developed sites with hookups to boat-in primitive camps accessible only by water — the latter being some of the most peaceful camping in the foothills.

Trails wind along parts of the shoreline and up into the surrounding hills. The Lime Saddle area on the western arm of the lake has pleasant hiking to viewpoints above the water. In spring, when the reservoir is high from snowmelt, the lake can be spectacularly beautiful, with green hills reflected in clear water — though water levels fluctuate significantly depending on drought conditions.
