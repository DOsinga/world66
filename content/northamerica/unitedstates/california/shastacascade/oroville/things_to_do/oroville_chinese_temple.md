---
title: "Oroville Chinese Temple"
type: poi
tags: [museum, culture, history]
---

Built in 1863 by Chinese immigrants during the height of California's Gold Rush and railroad construction era, the Oroville Chinese Temple is one of the oldest and best-preserved Chinese religious sites in North America. What makes it exceptional is that it survived intact — the altarpieces, ceremonial robes, lanterns, tapestries, and hand-carved wooden puppets are largely original, giving it an authenticity that most historic sites simply cannot match.

The complex is actually three interconnected temples serving different spiritual traditions: a Buddhist temple, a Taoist temple, and a Confucian temple (or "Moon Temple") that served as a community gathering place. Together they served what was once one of California's largest Chinatowns — Oroville's Chinese population numbered in the thousands during the Gold Rush era, doing mining, farming, and the brutal work of railroad construction through the Sierra Nevada. As the Gold Rush faded and anti-Chinese sentiment and exclusion laws took hold, the community contracted, but the temples survived.

The site is now a California State Historic Landmark, operated by the City of Oroville. A small museum in the connected buildings provides context about the Chinese immigrant experience in the foothills. Visiting the temple is one of the most distinctive things you can do in this part of Northern California — it tells a story about the Gold Rush that the standard pioneer narratives often leave out.
