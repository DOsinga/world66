Redding sits at the northern end of the Sacramento Valley, where Interstate 5 begins its long climb toward the Oregon border. It is not a glamorous destination, but it is the natural base for exploring some of the state's most dramatic landscape: Mount Shasta to the north, Lassen Volcanic National Park to the east, [Shasta Dam](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/shastacascade/redding/shasta_dam) and [Whiskeytown Lake](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/shastacascade/redding/whiskeytown_lake) nearby, and the wild Trinity Alps to the west. The city claims more miles of trail per capita than almost anywhere in the United States.

The [Sundial Bridge](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/shastacascade/redding/sundial_bridge) — a glass-decked cantilever crossing designed by Santiago Calatrava that opened in 2004 and actually functions as the world's largest working sundial — is the city's landmark and the centrepiece of [Turtle Bay Exploration Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/shastacascade/redding/turtle_bay_exploration_park), which combines botanical gardens, a museum and riverside trails. The [Sacramento River Trail](/northamerica/unitedstates/california/shastacascade/redding/sacramento_river_trail) winds through riparian woodland along both banks.

Summers are searingly hot — Redding regularly tops 40°C, with 43°C days not unheard of — so spring and autumn are the best times to visit. If you are driving I-5 between San Francisco and Portland, it is worth stopping for a night rather than pushing through.
