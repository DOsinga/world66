story: >
  Torrance was laid out in 1912 as the first planned industrial city in California, designed by Frederick Law Olmsted Jr. (son of the man who designed Central Park) and the architect Irving Gill. The grid of wide streets, the small civic square, and the surviving Pacific Electric depot all date from that original plan. Most of Los Angeles grew without a plan; Torrance had one, and a surprising amount of it survives.
---

The blocks around El Prado and Cabrillo Avenues make up Old Torrance, the city's original civic and commercial centre. It is a small, low-rise downtown with a handful of restored 1920s storefronts, the Pacific Electric Railway depot (now restored as a museum), and the old Torrance High School building — one of the most-filmed school buildings in the world thanks to its use as the school in Beverly Hills 90210, Buffy the Vampire Slayer, She's All That, and dozens of other productions.

Worth a half-hour walk if you are passing through, especially around the small farmers' market held on Tuesdays. The neighbourhood is also slowly developing a cluster of independent coffee shops and craft breweries that give it a different character to the rest of suburban Torrance.
