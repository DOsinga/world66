title: Books
type: section
---

**The Sunset Strip** by Michael Walker (2013) is the definitive history of the mile-long stretch from Crescent Heights to Doheny — the clubs, the managers, the bands, the riots, the billboards. Walker interviewed dozens of musicians and promoters and the result is the best account of how that particular corner of Los Angeles became the centre of American rock music.

**Wonderland Avenue** by Danny Sugerman (1989) is a memoir by the Doors' manager, written about growing up on the streets of Hollywood and West Hollywood in the 1970s. Sugerman was a teenager running around the Strip during its peak years, and the book is an unusually honest account of the scene's excesses — drugs, rock and roll, Iggy Pop, and the aftermath.

**City of Quartz** by Mike Davis (1990) is a political and social history of Los Angeles that remains essential reading for understanding how the city works and who it excludes. West Hollywood's 1984 incorporation as a separate city — driven partly by gay activists and partly by rent-control advocates fighting landlord conversions — is one of the more interesting episodes Davis doesn't quite cover, but the wider context he provides is indispensable.

**Ask the Dust** by John Fante (1939) is set in Bunker Hill and Pershing Square rather than WeHo, but it's the great Los Angeles novel of the pre-war era and captures something about the city's relationship to failure and ambition that no later book has quite matched. Read it before arriving, not after.
