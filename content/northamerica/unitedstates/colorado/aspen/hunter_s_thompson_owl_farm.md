The gonzo journalist Hunter S. Thompson lived at Owl Farm, his "fortified compound" in Woody Creek 16 km north of Aspen, from 1967 until his suicide there in 2005. He ran for sheriff of Pitkin County from the property in 1970 on the Freak Power ticket (and very nearly won), wrote most of Fear and Loathing on the Campaign Trail '72 there, and made the place a hub for visiting writers, musicians, and political figures from George McGovern to Bill Murray.

The ranch is private — owned and lived in by his widow, Anita Thompson — and not open to the public. The closest you can get is the Woody Creek Tavern, a roadhouse half a mile down the road that was Thompson's hangout for decades and where the booths he favoured are still marked. The walls are covered in Thompson photos, gonzo memorabilia, and what is claimed to be the world's largest collection of celebrity Polaroids — it is the obligatory stop for any Thompson reader passing through.
