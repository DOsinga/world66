The J-Bar in the Hotel Jerome opened with the hotel in 1889 and has operated, with one Prohibition-era pause when it became an ice-cream parlour, ever since. The room is small and dark, with the original pressed-tin ceiling, a heavy wooden back-bar, and a long mirror behind the bottles. Hunter S. Thompson drank here for decades; the booth at the back left was his usual.

The drinks list is conservative — old-fashioned, gimlet, Aspen Crud (the bar's signature, a brandy milkshake invented during Prohibition when it counted as ice cream) — and the pricing is hotel-bar steep. The crowd is a mix of hotel guests and well-heeled locals. The terrace out front, opened in good weather, is the place to be in the evening. It is the most atmospheric place to drink in town.
