---
title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

Aurora is, by some distance, the best place to eat in metropolitan Denver — not for fine dining (Denver proper has the steakhouses and tasting menus) but for the kind of unpretentious, fiercely specific immigrant cooking that emerges when a city builds a refugee resettlement infrastructure and the cooks who arrive start opening places to feed each other.

The geography is simple. Two corridors hold most of the action. **Havana Street**, especially the stretch between Iliff and Montview, is Korean at the north end and increasingly Vietnamese, Mexican, and Indian as you move south — a half-dozen pho counters, several Korean barbecues, a row of Mexican mariscos places, and a scattering of Pakistani and Indian. **East Colfax Avenue**, from Yosemite eastward toward Tower Road, is more Ethiopian, Eritrean, Burmese and Latin American — including Mango House, the refugee-chef food hall that is probably the single most representative meal in the city.

The Stanley Marketplace handles a more polished, sit-down crowd with a strong lineup of independent restaurants. Plenty of taquerias and ramen shops scattered through every strip mall in between. Drive, park, walk in.
