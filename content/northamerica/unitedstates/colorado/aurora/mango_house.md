Mango House is the unofficial heart of Aurora's refugee community — a converted retail building on Colfax that houses a medical clinic, immigration services, youth programs, and the most interesting eating experience in the city: a food hall where refugee chefs cook the food they cooked at home before they left.

The lineup changes as families come and go, but you'll usually find stalls serving Burmese, Syrian, Bhutanese-Nepali, Ethiopian and Eritrean dishes alongside more familiar Mexican and Vietnamese options. Most plates run $8–$12. The room is plain — strip-lit, plastic tables, a children's play corner — but the food is unforced and excellent, and the cross-cultural lunch crowd is the closest thing Aurora has to a town square.
