  Built in 1878 at the peak of the gold boom and reopened in 1932 after a long
  derelict period, the Central City Opera House runs one of the oldest summer
  opera festivals in the United States. The interior is original — the same
  walnut benches, the same painted ceiling — and the acoustics are unusually
  good for a building that small.
---

The Opera House is the cultural reason Central City exists as anything more than a casino strip, and during the summer festival (typically July and early August) the town fills with opera-goers from Denver and beyond. The repertoire mixes warhorses with American premieres, and the resident young-artist programme has launched a long list of professional careers.

The building was hand-built in stone by Cornish miners, and the lobby retains the original frescoes painted by John C. Massman. Tours run in the off-season; even without a performance, it is worth a quick look from the outside. Festival tickets often sell out well in advance.
