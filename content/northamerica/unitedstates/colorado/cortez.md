Cortez sits in the southwest corner of Colorado, surrounded by high desert and the ancient landscape of the Four Corners region. Its main draw is as the gateway to Mesa Verde National Park, where the Ancestral Puebloans built their cliff dwellings into the canyon walls around 1,400 years ago — Mesa Verde is one of the most significant pre-Columbian archaeological sites in North America and is worth at least a full day.

The town reflects its heritage honestly, if a little commercially. The [Cortez Cultural Center](/northamerica/unitedstates/colorado/cortez/cortez_cultural_center) is a free museum with solid Puebloan exhibits and summer evening Native American dance performances. The [Crow Canyon Archaeological Center](/northamerica/unitedstates/colorado/cortez/crow_canyon) lets visitors join actual excavations at Ancestral Puebloan sites. The Ute Mountain Ute tribal park offers guided tours of a less-visited set of cliff dwellings. Cortez has decent restaurants, a few good shops selling authentic Southwestern art and pottery, and a relaxed attitude toward its visitors. It makes a better base for the region than the more tourist-saturated Durango.
