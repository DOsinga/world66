  Crow Canyon is not a museum — it is an active research facility that has produced major
  findings about Ancestral Puebloan society. Its excavations at the nearby Sand Canyon Pueblo
  site, one of the largest Puebloan villages ever discovered, helped establish the timeline
  of population collapse and migration from the Four Corners around AD 1280. Researchers
  here helped show that the abandonment was not a single event but a decades-long process.

Crow Canyon Archaeological Center, a few kilometres west of Cortez, is a research and education facility where archaeologists conduct active excavations alongside enrolled participants. Programmes range from day visits to multi-day residential courses — you can join a dig, work in the laboratory analysing artefacts, or tour the excavation sites with a researcher who explains what they have found and what it means.

The one-day archaeology programme is the most accessible option for visitors passing through. It includes excavation at an actual site, hands-on work with artefacts, and enough context to make the sprawling landscape of the Four Corners start to make sense. Book in advance — places fill up. The residential courses, lasting several days, are aimed at people with a serious interest in archaeology; they are open to adults with no prior experience. The centre's work focuses on the question of why the Ancestral Puebloans left the Colorado Plateau, and its findings continue to reshape understanding of pre-Columbian North America.
