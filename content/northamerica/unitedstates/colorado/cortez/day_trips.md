---
title: "Day Trips"
type: section
---

The Four Corners region has so many significant archaeological sites that a single day trip from Cortez is never enough. Plan for at least three days if you want to see the highlights.

**Mesa Verde National Park** is the main event — one of the most impressive archaeological sites in North America, with cliff dwellings built into the canyon walls by the Ancestral Puebloans around AD 600–1300. The most famous site, Cliff Palace, can only be visited on ranger-led tours that fill up fast in summer; book online well in advance. The park is 10 miles east of Cortez on US 160.

**Hovenweep National Monument**, about 40 miles west on the Colorado-Utah border, preserves six groups of ancient towers and pueblo buildings spread across the canyon rims. It is far less visited than Mesa Verde and has a solitary grandeur that the busier park cannot match. The Square Tower Group, near the visitor centre, is the most accessible and most dramatic.

**Anasazi Heritage Center** in nearby Dolores, 10 miles north on US 491, is the best museum in the region for understanding Ancestral Puebloan culture. The collection spans 1,500 years of the Southwest's prehistoric past, with outstanding pottery, textiles, and tools from excavations across the region. The ruins of Dominguez and Escalante Pueblos are a short walk from the museum entrance.

**Ute Mountain Tribal Park**, south of Cortez on the Ute Mountain Ute reservation, contains cliff dwellings and rock art comparable to Mesa Verde but accessible only on guided tours led by Ute tribal members. The experience is more personal and less crowded than Mesa Verde — and your guide's connection to the land is genuine. Tours run from May to October; book through the Ute Mountain tribal visitor centre.
