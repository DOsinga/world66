Hawkins Preserve is a 122-acre nature preserve within Cortez city limits — an unusual thing for a small Colorado town to have. The preserve protects a stretch of McElmo Creek and the riparian habitat along its banks: cottonwoods, willows, and the bird life that depends on them in the semi-arid plateau country.

Walking trails wind through the preserve past the creek and wetland areas. It is the best place in and around Cortez to see birds without driving anywhere — warblers, herons, and kingfishers are common. The preserve is also used for cross-country skiing in winter, when snow arrives. It is easy to miss since it sits at the southern edge of town; look for the trailhead off US 160 heading west.
