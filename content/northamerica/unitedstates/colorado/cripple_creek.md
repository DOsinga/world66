Cripple Creek is a former gold mining town at 9,494 feet in the Colorado Rockies, about an hour's scenic drive west from [Colorado Springs](/northamerica/unitedstates/colorado/coloradosprings). The drive takes you around the back of Pikes Peak and up near the tree line — bring layers, because snow and sunshine can happen on the same summer day.

The town boomed in the 1890s gold rush and the handsome [brick main street](/northamerica/unitedstates/colorado/cripple_creek/cripple_creek_historic_district) dates from that era. Today nearly every storefront is a casino — Colorado legalised limited-stakes gambling here in 1991 — but enough of the old character survives to make it worth a visit beyond the slot machines. The Butte Opera House, perched above the firehouse, stages melodramas and musicals year-round. The [Mollie Kathleen Gold Mine](/northamerica/unitedstates/colorado/cripple_creek/mollie_kathleen_gold_mine) offers underground tours of a real thousand-foot mine shaft, and the [Cripple Creek & Victor Narrow Gauge Railroad](/northamerica/unitedstates/colorado/cripple_creek/cripple_creek_victor_narrow_gauge_railroad) runs a scenic loop through the old mining district.

The [Mt. Pisgah cemetery](/northamerica/unitedstates/colorado/cripple_creek/mt_pisgah_cemetery) above town has panoramic views and some colourful headstones from the mining days. Ghost tours run on summer evenings. The companion town of [Victor](/northamerica/unitedstates/colorado/cripple_creek/victor_colorado), four miles south, avoided the casino makeover entirely and preserves its 1890s streetscape largely intact — it's quieter and more authentic, and easy to combine with a visit to Cripple Creek.
