story: >
  The brick buildings that line Bennett Avenue were almost entirely built after 1896, the year two catastrophic fires swept through the town within four days of each other. The first fire started in a hotel kitchen; the second, days later, burned what remained. The town rebuilt immediately in brick and stone, which is why the main street looks so uniformly Victorian — it was effectively rebuilt from scratch in a single construction season.
---

Bennett Avenue, the main street, is lined with handsome brick commercial buildings from the late 1890s reconstruction — saloons, banks, and hotels that survived because the town insisted on masonry after the fires. The whole district is listed on the National Register of Historic Places. Most of the ground floors are now casinos, but the facades and upper stories are intact, and the scale of the street — wide, well-proportioned, built for a city of real ambition — gives a sense of what the gold rush towns aspired to be.

Walking the district takes under an hour; the real reward is looking above the casino signage at the ornate cornices and brick detailing of the upper floors. The Butte Opera House, set above a firehouse, stages melodramas and other performances throughout the year and is worth checking in on.
