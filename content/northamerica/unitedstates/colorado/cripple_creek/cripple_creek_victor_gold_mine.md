The Cripple Creek & Victor Gold Mine — still known locally as the Cresson Mine after its historic predecessor — is one of the largest active gold mining operations in the United States. The open-pit mine on the southern edge of the district is visible from the narrow-gauge railroad and from various points on the roads between Cripple Creek and Victor; the scale is striking, with working trucks that appear toy-sized against the pit walls.

The mine is an active industrial operation and is not open to public tours. What visitors see from public roads is essentially the same terrain where some of the richest individual gold finds in American mining history were made in the early twentieth century. The contrast with the romantic image of individual prospectors is instructive — the district's real wealth was always extracted by industrial capital, not by the lone miner with a pan.
