  At its peak in the early 1900s, the Cripple Creek mining district was served by no fewer than four competing full-scale railroads — the Colorado Midland, the Florence & Cripple Creek, the Midland Terminal, and the Colorado Springs & Cripple Creek District Railway. All four are gone. The present two-foot narrow-gauge line is a 1960s-era tourist railway built on the right-of-way of the old Midland Terminal and has no historical connection to those original lines.
---

The Cripple Creek & Victor Narrow Gauge Railroad runs a four-mile loop south from the depot near downtown, skirting the edge of the old mining district through terrain dotted with mine dumps and abandoned headframes. The trip takes about 45 minutes each way and gives a good sense of the scale of the district — from the train you can see the open-pit workings of the still-active Cripple Creek & Victor Gold Mine and the ruins of dozens of smaller operations.

The steam locomotive hauls vintage passenger cars with open observation platforms. The depot has a small display on the history of the district's original railroads. The railroad operates May through October; check current schedules as the route and timings shift between peak and shoulder season.
