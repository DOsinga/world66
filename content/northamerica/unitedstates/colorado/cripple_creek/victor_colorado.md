Victor sits four miles south of Cripple Creek and is in many ways the more interesting town. It was never legalised for gambling in 1991 — only Cripple Creek, Black Hawk, and Central City were — and as a result it has remained quiet and largely unimproved, its downtown a collection of brick commercial buildings from the 1890s and early 1900s that survive simply because no one had reason to replace them.

The Victor Downtown Historic District is on the National Register of Historic Places. The Victor Hotel, a four-storey brick building with an original cage elevator, is one of the best-preserved boom-era hotels in Colorado. The Western Federation of Miners Union Hall, though in poor condition, recalls the district's labour history — the WFM fought some of its most bitter battles here during the strikes of 1894 and 1903–04. Victor makes a good hour's stop on any Cripple Creek visit.
