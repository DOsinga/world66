A 1.3-mile pedestrian-and-transit promenade running from Union Station to Civic Center, the 16th Street Mall was designed by I. M. Pei and Henry N. Cobb in 1982 and paved with a distinctive grey-pink granite pattern said to suggest a diamondback rattlesnake. A free electric shuttle (the MallRide) runs end to end, so you don't really need to walk the whole thing.

The Mall isn't, in itself, a destination — it's full of chain shops and the occasional street performer — but it threads together most of downtown's worth-seeing places, including the Daniels & Fisher Tower, Tabor Center, Skyline Park, and the Wells Fargo "Cash Register" building. A major redesign was finishing in the mid-2020s and the granite pattern was being relaid.
