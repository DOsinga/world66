The Byers-Evans House sits awkwardly between the Denver Art Museum and the Public Library — a Victorian survivor in a sea of newer civic architecture. It was built in 1883 by William Byers, founder of the Rocky Mountain News, and later owned by the Evans family, who shaped much of 19th-century Denver politics. The house is preserved almost as the Evanses left it in the 1980s, down to the magazines on the bedside tables.

It's a small, hourlong stop, and currently doubles as a center for women's history exhibitions. Tickets are cheap, lines are nonexistent, and it gives a sense of how the Civic Center neighborhood used to look before everything around it was rebuilt in concrete and steel.
