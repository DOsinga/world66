The state history museum, opened in 2012, is unusually playful for the genre. The signature exhibit is a "time machine" — a large floor map of Colorado that visitors push wheeled discs across, triggering audio and video stories of specific places at specific moments. There's also a serious permanent gallery on the Sand Creek Massacre, jointly developed with the Cheyenne and Arapaho descendants, which is one of the most honest treatments of frontier violence anywhere in the American West.

Allow a couple of hours. Combined with the State Capitol across the street and the Denver Art Museum next door, you can fill a long morning in Civic Center without crossing a busy road.
