---
title: When to Go
type: section
---

Denver's reputation for sunshine — 300 days a year — is real, and the weather is more often pleasant than not. The trade-off is altitude: at 5,280 feet, sun is fierce, dehydration is faster than you expect, and the air at night chills quickly even in midsummer.

**Late spring (May–June)** is arguably the best time: long warm days, wildflowers in the foothills, baseball season in full swing, and the high country opening up week by week as the snow melts off.

**Summer (June–August)** brings reliable highs in the 80s F and the city's outdoor calendar — Civic Center Eats, free movies in Skyline Park, jazz at City Park, baseball at Coors Field. Afternoon thunderstorms over the mountains are nearly daily; plan hikes for the morning.

**Autumn (September–October)** is the second great window. The aspens turn gold in late September in the high country; daytime temperatures stay mild, and the crowds thin out. Football season fills Empower Field.

**Winter (November–March)** is for the mountains. Denver itself gets less snow than people imagine, and downtown remains walkable most days. The big draw is the proximity to the ski resorts — Loveland and A-Basin both within 90 minutes, Vail and Breckenridge within two hours.
