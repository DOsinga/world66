Arapahoe Basin, universally known as A-Basin, sits just six kilometres east of Keystone on US Highway 6, near the top of Loveland Pass. It is small by comparison — around 1,400 acres — but punches well above its weight for challenging terrain and high-altitude conditions.

The ski area tops out at 13,050 feet on the East Wall, where you are above treeline and skiing on steep, wind-scoured faces with views across Summit County. The Pallavicini run is a Colorado classic: long, exposed, consistently steep, and with a mogul field at the bottom that separates the skiers from the tourists. The more recently opened Beavers and Montezuma Bowl sections added beginner and intermediate terrain and made A-Basin a viable destination for mixed-ability groups.

A-Basin is independently owned, which has long shaped its character: less groomed, less polished, and less expensive than the Vail Resorts properties down the road. The parking lot tailgating culture — known locally as the Beach — is a genuine tradition. People set up grills and camp chairs against their car bumpers, sometimes in April sunshine in shirtsleeves, and spend as much time in the lot as on the mountain.

Epic Pass holders can ski A-Basin on a limited basis; a dedicated A-Basin pass or lift ticket also works. The resort has no ski-in/ski-out lodging — day-trip logistics from Keystone are straightforward.
