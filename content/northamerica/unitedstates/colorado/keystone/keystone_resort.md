Keystone Resort spreads across three interconnected mountains — Dercum Mountain, North Peak, and the Outpost — for a combined 3,148 acres of skiable terrain. With 135 trails and a vertical drop of 2,900 feet, it ranks among the largest ski areas in Colorado, and its position in the Vail Resorts portfolio means it is included on the Epic Pass alongside Vail, Beaver Creek, and Breckenridge.

**Dercum Mountain** is the front face and the first thing you see from the base villages. It holds the bulk of the beginner and intermediate terrain — long, groomed runs that are fast and forgiving. The Happy Trails and Flying Dutchman runs are classic Keystone cruisers: wide, well-groomed, and great for building speed. The summit sits at 11,640 feet.

**North Peak** connects to Dercum via a gondola and serves up more intermediate and advanced terrain on its north-facing slopes, including the Outpost Bowl descent. Conditions here often stay cooler and the snow holds better later in the season than on the sunnier front face.

**The Outpost** is the most remote of the three mountains and rewards the effort to get there. The terrain is more demanding — short steep pitches, glades, and the expansive Outpost Bowl. Expert skiers head here to escape the crowds that cluster around Dercum. The Outpost Gondola from North Peak is the only way up.

The resort opens in mid-November most years and runs through mid-April, one of the longer seasons in Summit County. Snowmaking covers a substantial portion of the mountain, which helps early-season conditions considerably.
