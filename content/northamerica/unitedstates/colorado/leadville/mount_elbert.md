Mount Elbert, at 14,439 feet, is the highest peak in Colorado and the second highest in the lower 48 states. The trailhead is about 13 miles south of Leadville, and the standard route via the south ridge is a long but non-technical hike — no ropes or special equipment required, just fitness, acclimatisation, and good weather judgment. Most hikers take five to eight hours for the round trip.

The summit views are exceptional: on a clear day you can see dozens of other Colorado fourteeners in every direction. Start early — summer thunderstorms typically build over the peaks by early afternoon and the summit is no place to be when lightning arrives. The trailhead is accessible by car with no special clearance required, making Elbert the most convenient fourteener to tackle from Leadville.
