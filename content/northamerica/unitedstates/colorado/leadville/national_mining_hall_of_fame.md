The National Mining Hall of Fame and Museum occupies a Victorian schoolhouse on the north end of Harrison Avenue, Leadville's main street. The museum covers the history of mining in the United States with particular depth on the Colorado silver rush, but also tackles coal, copper, gold, and uranium. The recreated hard-rock mine tunnel is the most visceral exhibit — a dark, narrow passage furnished with period equipment that conveys something of what actual mining felt and sounded like.

The Hall of Fame section honours miners, engineers, and mining pioneers with biographical exhibits. It is not as dry as it sounds — the stories of people like Molly Brown (who got her fortune from a Leadville mine before her Titanic survival made her famous) and Baby Doe Tabor are genuinely dramatic. Budget ninety minutes to two hours for a thorough visit.
