Mesa Verde National Park protects one of the most astonishing cultural landscapes in North America: mesa tops, canyons, and alcoves where Ancestral Pueblo communities built villages, farms, roads, reservoirs, and cliff dwellings long before Europeans arrived in the region. The famous houses tucked under sandstone ledges are only part of the story; the wider park shows a society adapting with skill and imagination to a dry, demanding place.

The big names are [Cliff Palace](/northamerica/unitedstates/colorado/mesaverde/cliff_palace), [Balcony House](/northamerica/unitedstates/colorado/mesaverde/balcony_house), [Spruce Tree House](/northamerica/unitedstates/colorado/mesaverde/spruce_tree_house), and [Long House](/northamerica/unitedstates/colorado/mesaverde/long_house). Some can only be entered on ranger-led tours, and tour tickets can disappear quickly in high season. Even without a tour, the overlooks and mesa-top sites make the park worth visiting.

Do not treat Mesa Verde as a quick roadside stop. The entrance road climbs for miles before the main archaeological areas, and the park is bigger and slower than it looks on a map. Give it at least a full day from [Durango](/northamerica/unitedstates/colorado/durango) or Cortez; two days are better if you want both Chapin Mesa and Wetherill Mesa.
