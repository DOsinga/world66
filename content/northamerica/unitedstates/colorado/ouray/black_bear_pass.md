Black Bear Pass is a 3,886-metre saddle between Ouray and Telluride and the most notorious 4WD road in Colorado. The route climbs out of Ouray on Highway 550, branches west onto a narrow shelf cut into the cliff face, crosses the alpine basin around Ingram Lake, then drops 600 metres into Telluride via a series of one-way switchbacks so tight that long-wheelbase vehicles literally cannot complete them.

The road is one-way (Ouray to Telluride only) below the summit; uphill traffic is forbidden because there is nowhere to pass. A short-wheelbase 4WD with low range, good ground clearance and undamaged tyres is the minimum. Most rental SUVs will not make it. Several Telluride outfitters run guided Jeep tours that include the descent — much the safer way to see it if you are not used to high-exposure mountain driving. The pass usually opens in late July (depending on snowmelt) and closes again with the first heavy snow in October.

The views from the summit basin — back over Ingram Falls and ahead down the Telluride amphitheatre — are arguably the best you can drive to anywhere in the San Juans. Even if you skip the switchback descent, the climb up from the Million Dollar Highway as far as the summit is spectacular and turns around easily.
