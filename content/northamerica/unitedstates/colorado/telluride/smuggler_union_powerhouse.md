The Smuggler-Union Hydroelectric Power Plant sits on a cliff ledge above Bridal Veil Falls, an improbable red-roofed building perched where no building has any right to be. Built in 1907 to power the mines on the plateau above town, it is the oldest operating alternating-current power plant in the US, and it still generates electricity for the local grid today.

Reaching it requires the steep hike up from town past the falls — about 45 minutes from the trailhead. The building itself is privately owned and not open to casual visitors, but the external view from the trail is remarkable: a Victorian industrial building balanced above a hundred-metre drop, with nothing but steep rock and sky behind it. In winter, the frozen waterfall becomes one of the more celebrated ice climbing routes in Colorado, and the powerhouse road is a classic backcountry ski descent for those with the skills for it.
