The Betty Ford Alpine Gardens, on South Frontage Road East in the heart of Vail Village, are an unexpected and genuinely lovely detour from the ski-resort surroundings. At 2,500 metres above sea level they rank among the highest public botanical gardens in the world, and the collection is impressive given the harsh growing conditions: some 3,000 species of alpine, mountain, and sub-alpine plants from Colorado and around the world.

The gardens are divided into several sections: a mountain perennial garden, an alpine rock garden, an international peace garden, and a children's discovery garden. Summer is the obvious time to visit, when the flower beds are at their best — generally late June through September. Admission is free, and the setting against the Gore Range mountains adds considerably to the experience.

A stroll through the gardens takes an easy half-hour; the mountain perennial beds around Ford Park are colourful and well-labelled, giving you a useful introduction to what's growing up and down the surrounding valleys.
