The Colorado Ski and Snowboard Museum, located in Vail Village on South Frontage Road, traces the history of skiing in Colorado from its Norwegian immigrant origins in the mining camps of the 1880s to the present-day resort industry. The collection is surprisingly engaging — vintage skis, boots, and bindings chart the rapid evolution of equipment, and photographs and film footage capture early racing and the development of the major resorts.

A section on the 10th Mountain Division, the elite US Army ski troops trained in Colorado during World War II who went on to found many of Colorado's major ski areas, is particularly worth your time. Many veterans of the 10th Mountain Division — including Pete Seibert, who founded Vail — turned their military skiing experience into civilian resort-building after the war. The museum is free and takes about an hour. It is open year-round and makes an obvious activity on storm days when the visibility on the mountain is poor.
