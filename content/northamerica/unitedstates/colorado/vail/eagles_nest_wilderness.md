  The Eagles Nest Wilderness, designated in 1976, takes its name from a prominent peak visible from Vail. The area is home to elk, black bear, mule deer, and the occasional mountain lion — you are genuinely in wild country once you leave the main valley trails behind.
---

The Eagles Nest Wilderness area rises immediately behind Vail, covering nearly 60,000 hectares of the Gore Range and the Williams Fork Mountains. Access points are scattered throughout the valley, including trailheads at the east end of Vail Village and at various points along Interstate 70. The terrain is steep and dramatic: granite ridges, high alpine lakes, and dense conifer forest below the timberline.

The most accessible day hike from Vail follows the Gore Creek Trail into the wilderness from the Upper East Vail trailhead. The trail climbs steadily through forest and opens into a narrow glacial valley — on a clear day in late summer, the meadows are thick with wildflowers and the peaks above are striking. Reaching the upper basin and its lakes typically takes around four hours return.

Backpackers willing to carry camping gear can access more remote corners of the wilderness, including the cross-country routes along the Gore Range that connect to Summit County to the east. Permit requirements and camping rules change periodically; check with the White River National Forest before heading in.
