Vail is one of the largest ski mountains in North America, with over 2,000 hectares of skiable terrain spread across several distinct zones. The front side offers groomed blue and black runs through the trees, with wide-open cruisers that are well-suited to intermediates. Above the tree line, the terrain opens up dramatically.

The Back Bowls are what set Vail apart. Seven enormous open bowls — Sun Down, Sun Up, Tea Cup, China, Siberia, Inner Mongolia, and Outer Mongolia — fan out behind the mountain's main ridge, each offering vast ungroomed expanses of powder skiing. On a good day after a snowfall, the bowls feel like the surface of the moon, but white. Blue Sky Basin, accessed through the Back Bowls, adds densely gladed tree skiing with a quieter atmosphere.

In summer the mountain reinvents itself: the gondola runs for hikers and mountain bikers, there is a via ferrata route, and the Epic Discovery park offers zip-lines and a ropes course for families. The summit reaches 3,527 metres — on clear days you can see all the way to Pikes Peak.
