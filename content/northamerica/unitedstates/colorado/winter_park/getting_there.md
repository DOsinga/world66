---
title: "Getting There"
type: section
---

Winter Park is about 110 kilometres northwest of Denver, making it one of the most accessible ski resorts from a major US city.

**By car:** Highway 40 over Berthoud Pass is the direct route — about 1.5 to 2 hours from central Denver depending on traffic and road conditions. The pass can be closed or require chains in heavy snow; check conditions before you go. US-6 through Loveland Pass is an alternative but also at high altitude. The Eisenhower Tunnel on I-70 (the route to other Front Range ski towns) does not serve Winter Park directly.

**By train:** The Winter Park Express ski train runs from Denver's Union Station on weekends and selected holidays in winter (January–March), taking about 2 hours and arriving directly at the resort base. This is genuinely the most enjoyable way to get there and eliminates the parking problem. Book well in advance.

**By bus:** Ski bus services run from Denver's Union Station and various Front Range locations during the ski season — cheaper than the train and more flexible in terms of timing, though less scenic. The town itself has a free local shuttle connecting lodging, the resort, and the main strip during winter.
