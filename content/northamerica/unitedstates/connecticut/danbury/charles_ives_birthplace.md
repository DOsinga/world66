The modest wooden house on Mountainville Avenue where Charles Ives was born in 1874 is now a historic landmark. Ives — who won the Pulitzer Prize in 1947 and is considered the first major American composer — grew up immersed in Danbury's rich community music culture: town bands, church choirs, and amateur orchestras. He later incorporated this cacophony of American vernacular music into avant-garde compositions that anticipated musical modernism by decades.

The house itself is not a museum with regular opening hours but is marked with a historic plaque. Danbury's connection to Ives is celebrated more broadly in the city — the performing arts center is named for him, and his music occasionally features in local concerts. For anyone interested in American music history, the house is worth a short detour.
