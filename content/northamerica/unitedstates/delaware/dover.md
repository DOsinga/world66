Dover is the capital of Delaware and sits squarely in the middle of the state, an easy drive from both Philadelphia and the Atlantic beaches. It is a small city — quietly proud of its history — and one that most travelers drive past on the way to somewhere else. That's a mistake worth correcting.

The city was laid out in 1717 on land William Penn had granted decades earlier, and the historic core around [The Green](/northamerica/unitedstates/delaware/dover/dover_green) still looks much as it did in the 18th century. The [Old State House](/northamerica/unitedstates/delaware/dover/delaware_state_house) on South State Street is among the oldest legislative buildings in continuous use in the country. [Christ Church](/northamerica/unitedstates/delaware/dover/christ_church_dover) and its colonial-era burial ground are a short walk away, as is the [Governor's Mansion at Woodburn](/northamerica/unitedstates/delaware/dover/delaware_governors_mansion) and the [Johnson Victrola Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/delaware/dover/johnson_victrola_museum), which honors the Delaware native who invented the Victrola phonograph. The [Golden Fleece Tavern site](/northamerica/unitedstates/delaware/dover/golden_fleece_tavern) marks where Delaware became the first state to ratify the Constitution in 1787.

Dover Air Force Base, on the city's eastern edge, houses the [Air Mobility Command Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/delaware/dover/air_mobility_command_museum), one of the better military aviation collections in the Mid-Atlantic. On the opposite end of the spectrum, [Dover Motor Speedway](/northamerica/unitedstates/delaware/dover/dover_international_speedway) — home to the 46-foot concrete giant Miles the Monster — hosts two major NASCAR race weekends each year. Delaware State University, a historically Black university, adds student energy to an otherwise unhurried town. [Silver Lake Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/delaware/dover/silver_lake) offers pleasant green space and birding on the city's northern edge.
