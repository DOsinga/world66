  position as a grain shipping point resembled his home city on the Black
  Sea. The name stuck, though the American Odessa's prosperity proved
  short-lived — the Chesapeake and Delaware Canal bypassed the town in

  accidental preservation is what makes it so valuable today.

Historic Odessa, about 10 km east of Middletown, is one of the best-preserved 18th-century villages in Delaware and one of the most rewarding small historic sites on the Delmarva Peninsula. Four historic houses and a Quaker meeting house are maintained by Winterthur Museum and are open to visitors, presenting an unusually complete picture of life in a prosperous colonial port community.

The standout is the Corbit-Sharp House, a Georgian mansion built in 1774 that has survived virtually unaltered — original woodwork, original flooring, original hardware, and an outstanding collection of Delaware-made furniture. The Wilson-Warner House next door gives a contrasting view of a more modest merchant's life in the same period. The Federal period houses along Main Street round out the picture. The village has a human scale that makes it very easy to absorb: you can see everything significant in two or three hours, which is exactly the right amount.
