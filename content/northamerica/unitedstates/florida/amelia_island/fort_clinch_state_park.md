  Fort Clinch has the unusual distinction of having been occupied by three different armies — Confederate forces seized it in 1861, Union troops took it back in 1862, and it was briefly reactivated during the Spanish-American War in 1898. Despite all this military attention, the fort never fired a shot in anger. It was still incomplete when the Civil War ended.

Fort Clinch sits at the northern tip of Amelia Island, guarding the Cumberland Sound that separates Florida from Georgia. Construction began in 1847 but was never finished — a pattern that haunted many mid-century American coastal forts as masonry technology fell behind developments in artillery. What remains is one of the best-preserved examples of a mid-nineteenth-century brick fortification in the country, its walls weathered but structurally sound, its parade ground shaded by palms and oaks draped with Spanish moss.

Rangers in period uniform give living history demonstrations on certain weekends, which transforms the fort from an interesting ruin into a genuinely evocative experience. Even without the demonstrations, the scale of the place impresses — the walls are several feet thick, the gun emplacements look directly out to sea, and the views from the ramparts take in the marsh, the sound, and the Georgia shore. The state park surrounding the fort also has excellent nature trails through coastal hammock forest and beach access for fishing and swimming.
