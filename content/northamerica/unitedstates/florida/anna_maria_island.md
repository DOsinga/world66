Anna Maria Island is a seven-mile barrier island off the coast of Manatee County, just south of the mouth of Tampa Bay. It has managed to resist the high-rise development that dominates much of the Florida Gulf coast, and the result is a low-key, old-Florida atmosphere of beach cottages, small restaurants, and quiet streets.

The island has three small communities: Anna Maria at the north end (the quietest, with [Bean Point](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/anna_maria_island/bean_point) — the wild, car-free northern tip — as its best-kept secret), Holmes Beach in the middle (the most commercial), and Bradenton Beach at the south end, where the [Historic City Pier](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/anna_maria_island/bradenton_beach_pier) marks the spot where the original 1921 bridge first connected the island to the mainland.

The beaches are wide, white-sand, and uncrowded by Florida standards. The water is calm and warm. [Kayaking through the mangrove channels](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/anna_maria_island/manatee_kayaking) on the bay side is excellent for spotting manatees, dolphins, and wading birds. The island is small enough to explore entirely by bicycle. For local history, the [Anna Maria Island Historical Society](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/anna_maria_island/anna_maria_historical_society) tells the story of the island's eccentric origins through a cottage that literally fell into Tampa Bay and was rescued.
