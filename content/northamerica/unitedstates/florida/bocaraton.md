Boca Raton is the manicured side of South Florida: red-tile roofs, royal palms, guarded beach parks, and shopping plazas that look as if they were designed by a resort architect with a very strict ruler. It sits between [Delray Beach](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/delray_beach) and [Fort Lauderdale](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/fortlauderdale), and it works best as a quieter base for Palm Beach County rather than as a wild nightlife stop.

The city owes much of its look to architect Addison Mizner, whose Mediterranean Revival fantasies helped define wealthy Florida in the 1920s. [Mizner Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/bocaraton/mizner_park) is the easiest place to see that legacy in modern form: a walkable downtown strip with restaurants, galleries, concerts, and the [Boca Raton Museum of Art](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/bocaraton/boca_raton_museum_of_art). Nearby, the [Boca Raton Historical Society and Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/bocaraton/boca_raton_historical_society_and_museum) tells the story without turning it into a brochure.

The beaches are the real reason to linger. [Red Reef Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/bocaraton/red_reef_park) has a reef close enough to snorkel from shore, while [Spanish River Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/bocaraton/spanish_river_park) gives you a quieter stretch of dune and sea grape shade. Just inland, [Gumbo Limbo Nature Center](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/bocaraton/gumbo_limbo_nature_center) is a small but excellent introduction to sea turtles, mangroves, and the barrier-island ecology that most beach towns pave over.

Boca is not cheap, and it is not especially spontaneous. Parking is regulated, restaurants book up in season, and the city likes order. Accept that, and you get a clean, easy, good-looking coastal stop with enough museums, parks, and shopping to fill a relaxed two or three days.
