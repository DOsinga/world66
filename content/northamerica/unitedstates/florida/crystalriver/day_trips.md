---
title: Day Trips
type: section
---

Crystal River is a good base for exploring the Nature Coast, and several worthwhile destinations are within an easy drive.

**Homosassa Springs Wildlife State Park** is 12 miles south on US-19. The park's main feature is a natural spring where manatees gather year-round — unlike Crystal River, where manatee presence is seasonal, Homosassa has a permanent resident population cared for at the refuge. There's also a full complement of Florida wildlife including black bears, Florida panthers, and a large population of wading birds. The park's "Fish Bowl" underwater observatory lets you watch fish and manatees from below the waterline. About 30 minutes' drive.

**Weeki Wachee Springs State Park** is 40 miles south. Famous since 1947 for its "mermaid shows" performed by swimmers in the spring vent, Weeki Wachee also has a river boat ride through cypress swamp and a water park. The spring itself, one of the largest in Florida, is worth the visit on its own terms. About 45 minutes' drive.

**Cedar Key**, 50 miles north, is a small island town at the end of a causeway — one of the most atmospheric fishing villages left on the Gulf Coast. Famous for locally farmed clams, a small but good history museum, and a collection of weathered waterfront seafood restaurants. The Cedar Keys National Wildlife Refuge protects a string of islands just offshore; kayak rentals are available. About one hour north on US-19 then west on SR-24.

**Gainesville** is 60 miles east. Home to the University of Florida and the Florida Museum of Natural History, which houses the largest collection of natural history specimens in the Southeast, including a complete fossil record of Florida's prehistoric fauna. The Butterfly Rainforest, an enclosed walk-through garden with hundreds of live tropical butterflies, is a standout. About 75 minutes' drive via SR-44 and I-75.
