In Riverfront Park along Beach Street, a small marble headstone marks the grave of Brownie, a mongrel who lived in downtown Daytona for fifteen years and was technically owned by no one. Beach Street merchants fed him, the taxi drivers built him a kennel, and a savings account at the local bank covered his medical care.

The grave is easy to miss — a low, foot-high stone in the shade beside the river walk — but it is one of the most genuinely odd memorials in Florida and a more characteristic glimpse of pre-NASCAR Daytona than anything else you can find downtown. Combine it with a walk through the small but pleasant park along the Halifax River.
