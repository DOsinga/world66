Destin sits on the Florida Panhandle between Choctawhatchee Bay and the Gulf of Mexico. It has the white quartz sand and green water that made the Emerald Coast famous, but the place is more than another strip of condominiums: its old identity is fishing, and the harbor still gives the town a working edge.

The best day here mixes beach time with the water. [Henderson Beach State Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/destin/henderson_beach_state_park) is the cleanest way to see the dunes and Gulf without feeling swallowed by the resort strip. Around the harbor, charter boats, seafood places, dolphin trips, and evening crowds make [Destin Harbor](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/destin/destin_harbor) the busiest part of town.

Destin can be loud and commercial in peak summer. Come outside school holidays if you can, or build your day around early beach hours, a museum stop, and a late walk by the harbor. The [Destin History and Fishing Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/destin/destin_history_and_fishing_museum) is small, but it explains why the town calls itself the "World's Luckiest Fishing Village."
