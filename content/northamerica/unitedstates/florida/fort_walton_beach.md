Fort Walton Beach sits on the Emerald Coast between Pensacola and Destin, with Choctawhatchee Bay on one side and the white sand of Okaloosa Island on the other. It is less polished than Destin and less spring-break loud than parts of Panama City Beach, which is part of the appeal: the city still feels like a year-round Panhandle town with a beach attached.

The Gulf is the obvious draw. Okaloosa Island has clear green water, a long fishing pier, and a public boardwalk beside the sand. Families tend to gravitate to the [Gulfarium](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/fort_walton_beach/gulfarium_marine_adventure_park), while anyone who wants a quieter day can walk the dunes and beach parks west toward Santa Rosa Island.

Fort Walton Beach is also more interesting inland than many resort strips. The [Fort Walton Temple Mound](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/fort_walton_beach/heritage_park_and_cultural_center) is a real pre-Columbian ceremonial mound in the middle of downtown, paired with a small museum and early settlement buildings. North of town, the [Air Force Armament Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/fort_walton_beach/air_force_armament_museum) brings the Eglin Air Force Base story into view with bombers, fighters, missiles, and the heavy machinery of modern air power.

This is a good one- or two-day stop if you are already moving along the Florida Panhandle. Come for the beach, but leave time for the mound and the aviation museum; they give Fort Walton Beach more identity than a simple Gulf Coast resort.
