Bonnet House is one of the few places on the Fort Lauderdale beachfront where you can still see what the coast looked like before the high-rises. The 35-acre estate was built in 1920 by Chicago artist Frederic Clay Bartlett on land between the ocean and the Intracoastal Waterway, and the family managed to keep it largely intact until Evelyn Bartlett donated it to the Florida Trust for Historic Preservation in 1983. Squirrel monkeys — descendants of pets the family kept — still roam the grounds, and a resident flock of swans occupies the pond near the entrance.

Tours of the house reveal the Bartletts' eclectic collecting instincts: shell collections, tropical murals, hand-painted furniture, and a Venetian-style playhouse next to the freshwater lagoon. The gardens are worth the visit on their own — a mix of tropical plantings, live oaks draped in Spanish moss, and open lawns leading to the beach dune. The estate is open Wednesday through Sunday; arrive early to get a tour before the heat peaks.
