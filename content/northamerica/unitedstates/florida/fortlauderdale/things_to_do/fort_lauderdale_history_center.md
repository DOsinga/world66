The Fort Lauderdale History Center is a small museum complex on the north bank of the New River, a short walk from Stranahan House. The main building holds rotating exhibitions on Broward County history, from the Seminole wars and early settlement through the 1920s land boom and the transformation of the beachfront. The collection includes photographs, maps, and artifacts that give context to the older buildings you see on the river.

The complex also includes the New River Inn (1905), the King-Cromartie House, and the Philemon Bryan House — all clustered together and representing the earliest phases of the city's development. The scale is modest; you can see everything in an hour. But the curators know their material well, and the position on the river, with the current skyline visible just downstream, makes the historical contrast vivid in a way that a downtown museum often cannot manage.
