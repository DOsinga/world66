Hugh Taylor Birch State Park is a long, narrow strip of coastal hammock squeezed between the Intracoastal Waterway and A1A, just north of the Las Olas beach access. It is 180 acres of what south Florida's barrier islands looked like before air conditioning made them habitable: a dense tangle of live oaks, gumbo limbo trees, strangler figs, and sabal palms, all draped in bromeliads and supporting a surprisingly complete set of wildlife. Raccoons are the most visible residents, but the park also hosts gopher tortoises, wading birds, and dozens of butterfly species.

The park has a 1.9-mile paved loop road for cycling, a small freshwater lagoon where you can rent canoes and kayaks, and easy walking trails through the hammock. A pedestrian tunnel under A1A connects the park to the beach — a useful detail if you want a swim after your walk. The park is open daily from 8am to sunset and charges a small entrance fee per vehicle. Go early on weekends, when parking fills up by mid-morning.
