The Historic Haile Homestead is a Civil War-era plantation house in Newberry, about 15 miles west of Gainesville, that is remarkable for a single unusual feature: its walls are covered in writing. The Haile family, who owned the property from the 1850s through the 1920s, used the walls, doors, and any available surface as scratch paper — leaving behind personal diary entries, grocery lists, poetry, and family observations written directly in pencil and pen on the plaster.

The house is open for guided tours on weekend mornings and the tour is genuinely engaging. The writing is legible and the guides can decode and interpret the inscriptions, giving a vivid picture of daily life in north-central Florida from the antebellum period through the early 20th century. It is one of the most genuinely unusual historic sites in Florida.
