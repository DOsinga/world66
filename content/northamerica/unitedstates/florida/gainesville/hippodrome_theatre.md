  The Hippodrome started life in 1974 in an abandoned 1911 Federal Building — a grand Beaux-Arts post office and courthouse that had been sitting empty for years. A group of theatre artists occupied the building and staged their first show before they had legal permission to be there. The city eventually gave the building to the company, and the renovation of the historic post office into a working theatre became a landmark project in Gainesville's downtown revitalisation.

The Hippodrome State Theatre is the regional professional theatre in downtown Gainesville, operating out of a restored 1911 Federal Building — a handsome Beaux-Arts structure on SE 2nd Place that was converted into a theatre in the 1970s. The Hippodrome is a genuine company theatre that produces its own work and hosts touring productions, with a season running from autumn through spring.

The building also houses a cinema and a bar that is open independently of performances. For anyone spending a few days in Gainesville and interested in the arts, it is worth checking the current schedule. The downtown location makes it walkable from the University of Florida campus and most of the central accommodation.
