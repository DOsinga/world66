Hollywood Beach is a barrier island community between Miami and Fort Lauderdale on Florida's southeastern coast. It is best known for the [Broadwalk](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/hollywood_beach/broadwalk) — a wide, 2.5-mile oceanfront promenade lined with restaurants, cafes, and vendors that is genuinely lively without being overwhelming. The beach itself is wide and well-kept, and the calm waters make it popular with families.

Hollywood Beach has a more casual feel than South Beach to the south, with more accessible prices and a mixed crowd of locals, retirees, and visitors. The Broadwalk attracts cyclists, rollerbladers, and walkers throughout the day. The [Hollywood Boulevard Historic District](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/hollywood_beach/hollywood_boulevard) a few blocks inland has more interesting dining and an arts scene centred on the [Art and Culture Center](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/hollywood_beach/art_culture_center). The city was founded in the 1920s by developer Joseph Young as a planned city — his [historic house](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/hollywood_beach/joseph_young_house) survives as a reminder of the boom-era ambition. Nearby [Gulfstream Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/hollywood_beach/gulfstream_park) in Hallandale Beach offers thoroughbred racing from January through June.
