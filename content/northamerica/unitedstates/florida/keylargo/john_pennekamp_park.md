John Pennekamp Coral Reef State Park covers around 70 square nautical miles of Atlantic Ocean waters off Key Largo, extending roughly 25 miles along the coast and 3 miles offshore. Established in 1963, it was the first underwater state park in the United States and remains one of the most visited Florida state parks, receiving over a million visitors a year.

The park protects sections of the only living coral barrier reef in the continental United States. Divers find elkhorn coral, brain coral, sea fans, and a rich population of reef fish: parrotfish, angelfish, barracuda, and nurse sharks. Snorkellers can access the shallower reef formations without certification. Glass-bottom boat tours run daily from the park marina for those who prefer to stay dry. Above the waterline, the park includes mangrove channels, seagrass beds, and marked kayak trails through the backcountry. Canoe and kayak rental is available at the marina.
