  Frank Lloyd Wright designed twelve buildings for Florida Southern College between 1938 and 1954, calling the campus "Child of the Sun" in reference to his belief that architecture should grow organically from its natural environment. He was 71 when the first building opened, and many of the structures were built using student labour — college students mixed concrete and laid blocks to reduce costs during the cash-strapped Depression and war years. Wright visited the campus only twice, but corresponded extensively with college president Ludd Spivey, who had essentially promised Wright a blank creative canvas.
---

Florida Southern College possesses the largest single-site collection of Frank Lloyd Wright architecture in the world — twelve buildings ranging from the Annie Pfeiffer Chapel to the Danforth Chapel, spread across a lakeside campus of palms and manicured lawns. Wright called the ensemble "Child of the Sun," and the nickname captures something real: the low, horizontal buildings with their cantilevered roofs and textured concrete blocks are designed to interact with the Florida light, shadow, and heat.

The campus is freely accessible to visitors during daylight hours — you can walk among the buildings, peer into courtyards, and explore the connected covered walkways (esplanades) that Wright designed to link the buildings and provide shade. The Usonian House museum on campus runs guided tours that explain the buildings' history and Wright's organic architecture philosophy. Allow two to three hours for a thorough visit.
