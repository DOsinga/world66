The joint museum complex preserving the adjacent winter homes of Thomas Edison and Henry Ford is the most visited historic site in Florida. Edison wintered here from 1886 until shortly before his death in 1931 — the house, laboratory, and gardens on his property are remarkably intact, giving a vivid sense of how he worked and lived away from New Jersey. The laboratory where he pursued his late-career rubber research is preserved with original equipment. Ford acquired the neighbouring property in 1916, and the two men became close friends; Ford's smaller house is simpler, more domestic, and reflects his personality accordingly.

The 21-acre botanical garden between the two properties contains exotic trees from around the world that Edison collected on his travels, including the celebrated banyan whose canopy now spreads over an acre. The Caloosahatchee riverfront behind the houses makes a pleasant walk. Guided tours of both estates run throughout the day; the combined ticket covers both properties, the museum building, and the laboratory. Plan two to three hours. Arrive early in the morning or on weekdays — this is genuinely popular, and midday in season sees crowds that diminish the experience.
