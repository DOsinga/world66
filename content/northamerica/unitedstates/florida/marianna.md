Marianna is a quiet county seat in Florida's Panhandle, sitting in the rolling red-clay hill country west of Tallahassee. It lacks the beach crowds of the Gulf Coast resorts and is all the better for it — this is old Florida, with Spanish-moss-draped oaks, antebellum courthouse squares, and a pace of life that hasn't changed much in decades.

The main reason travelers stop here is [Florida Caverns State Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/marianna/florida_caverns), just north of town. Unlike the rest of flat, limestone Florida, the caverns offer something genuinely unusual: a guided tour through dry cave chambers filled with stalactites, stalagmites, and flowstone formations. Above ground, the park has swimming in the Blue Hole spring and hiking along the [Chipola River](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/marianna/chipola_river) — good enough to warrant an overnight stay rather than just a quick stop.

Marianna itself is a small town of about 7,000 people, with a historic downtown that rewards a short walk. The [Jackson County Courthouse](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/marianna/jackson_county_courthouse) and the surrounding streets retain some of the character of a 19th-century Southern market town. On the south side of town, the former [Arthur G. Dozier School for Boys](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/marianna/dozier_school) is a darker chapter of Florida's history — a reform school where systematic abuse and unexplained deaths went unacknowledged for over a century.
