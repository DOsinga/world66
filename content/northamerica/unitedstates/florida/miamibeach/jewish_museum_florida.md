The Jewish Museum of Florida occupies two Art Deco synagogues in South Beach that have been beautifully restored and joined as a single museum. The collection tells the story of Jewish life in Florida from 1763 to the present — a history that is unexpectedly rich and runs from Sephardic merchants in the colonial period through the massive immigration from Eastern Europe and the Holocaust to the present-day community.

The South Beach sections of the museum deal with the neighbourhood's decades as a predominantly Jewish retirement community — the years from roughly 1940 to 1980 when it was full of elderly immigrants, Yiddish-speaking shops, and the particular culture of people who had remade themselves in America but kept something of the old world. That community largely dispersed during the South Beach revival; the museum preserves it with affection and scholarly rigour. The buildings themselves are worth seeing for the stained glass and the restoration quality.
