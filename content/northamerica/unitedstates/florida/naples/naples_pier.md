  The Naples Pier has been rebuilt three times — after a 1910 hurricane, a 1926 hurricane, and Hurricane Irma in 2017, which snapped it in two. Each time the city rebuilt it in roughly the same spot, on the same wooden pilings, in the same configuration. It is one of the few structures on the Gulf Coast that is genuinely irreplaceable by sentiment.
---

The Naples Pier is the emotional centre of the city — a 300-metre wooden walkway jutting into the Gulf of Mexico that has drawn crowds since 1889. In the morning, fishermen line the rails with rods and bait buckets; by evening, the crowd shifts to sunset-watchers, who arrive an hour early to stake out the best position on the west-facing end. On a good evening, the pelicans on the pilings, the burning orange sky, and the warm Gulf air combine into something that feels genuinely special rather than merely picturesque.

The pier itself is free and open sunrise to midnight. There are restrooms, a bait shop, and a small concession stand. The beach on either side is public, clean, and the most convenient in the city for anyone staying downtown. Parking is limited and fills early on weekends — arrive before 9am or take a rideshare.
