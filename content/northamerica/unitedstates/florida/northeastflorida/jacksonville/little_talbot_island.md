Little Talbot Island State Park protects one of the best undeveloped beaches in northeast Florida — a 7.5-kilometre stretch of Atlantic coast backed by coastal scrub, maritime hammock, and salt marsh. The beach is wide, the waves are genuine, and on a weekday outside summer you may have long stretches entirely to yourself.

The park has good facilities — picnic areas, a campground, canoe and kayak rentals on the Myrtle Creek side — and excellent birding year-round. The marsh side of the island is one of the best places in the region to see wading birds, raptors, and shorebirds. The beach side has good surf fishing. The campground books up quickly in spring and fall; reserve ahead through the Florida state parks system.
