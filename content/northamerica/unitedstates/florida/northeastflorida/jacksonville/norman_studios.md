The Norman Studios Silent Film Museum preserves the last surviving film studio from Jacksonville's brief but significant early cinema era. In the early 1900s Jacksonville was a major centre of the American film industry, with over thirty studios operating at the city's peak — more than Hollywood, which was still a small town in southern California. The warm weather, varied locations, and cheap real estate made northeast Florida an ideal base for the nascent industry, which relocated to California only when the climate and geography there proved even more suitable.

Richard Norman's studio, established around 1920, is notable specifically for its production of "race films" — independently made films with Black casts intended for Black audiences, at a time when Hollywood was producing almost nothing that represented Black Americans as full human beings. The museum tells this history through exhibits, film screenings, and the physical space of the surviving studio building, which retains much of its original character. Free admission; check opening hours before visiting.
