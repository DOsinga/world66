The Columbia County Historical Museum, housed in the historic Vinzant Building on Marion Street in downtown Lake City, documents the county's history from indigenous Timucua settlements through European contact, the Seminole Wars, the Civil War, and the farming era that shaped the town. The collection includes period artifacts, photographs, and material relating to the Battle of Olustee — fought just 15 miles east — and the broader role north Florida played in the conflict.

Lake City was known as Alligator before 1859, named for a Seminole chief. The museum covers that early period when this crossroads was a frontier outpost at the edge of what was then considered the deep interior of Florida.
