The Battle of Olustee, fought on February 20, 1864, was the largest Civil War engagement in Florida. Union forces pushed inland from Jacksonville hoping to cut Confederate supply lines and recruit freed Black soldiers; the Confederate army met them here and drove them back in a fight that left nearly 3,000 men killed, wounded, or captured. The Union advance into Florida never recovered.

The state park preserves the battlefield about 15 miles east of Lake City on US-90. An interpretive trail with markers winds through the pine flatwoods where the fighting took place. A small museum at the entrance tells the story with artifacts and displays, including the role of United States Colored Troops — both Union and Confederate forces included Black soldiers here. The annual reenactment in February is the largest in the Southeast.
