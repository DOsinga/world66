Osceola National Forest covers 157,000 acres of pine flatwoods, cypress swamps, and wetlands immediately east of Lake City. It's a working forest with a wild core — the Big Gum Swamp Wilderness is one of the most remote and least-visited places in Florida.

The Florida National Scenic Trail passes through Osceola, and the forest section is among the more challenging stretches — wet, flat, and dense. Day hikers use the Ocean Pond area, which offers camping, a swimming beach, and an easy loop trail around the lake. Cyclists use the forest roads. The forest supports breeding populations of red-cockaded woodpeckers, a rare and declining species that prefers old-growth longleaf pine — one of the reasons birders make the trip.
