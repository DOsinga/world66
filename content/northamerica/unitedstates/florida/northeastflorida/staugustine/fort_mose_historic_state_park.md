Fort Mose Historic State Park protects one of St. Augustine's most important historical sites: the location of Gracia Real de Santa Teresa de Mose, a free Black community established in Spanish Florida in the eighteenth century. People who escaped enslavement in the British colonies could gain freedom here by reaching Spanish territory, accepting Catholic baptism, and serving in the militia.

The original fort no longer stands, so the visit is about landscape, interpretation, and context rather than walls and cannons. The museum and boardwalk explain how Fort Mose fits into the story of colonial St. Augustine, Black freedom, Spanish strategy, and the contested borderlands of early America.
