Ocala is the inland Florida that people often miss: rolling horse farms, live oaks, limestone springs, and a small downtown that still feels like the seat of a rural county. It calls itself the Horse Capital of the World, and this is one tourism slogan with a real landscape behind it. Thoroughbred farms spread west of town, fences run along the roads, and equestrian events are part of everyday life rather than a special performance.

The strongest reason to come is [Silver Springs State Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/ocala/silver_springs_state_park), just east of town. Its clear spring basin, glass-bottom boats, paddling routes, and mossy river forest are classic old Florida. [Fort King](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/ocala/fort_king_national_historic_landmark) adds harder history: this was a key U.S. Army post during the Second Seminole War, now interpreted with a reconstructed fort and visitor centre.

Ocala also has more culture than its size suggests. The [Appleton Museum of Art](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/ocala/appleton_museum_of_art) has a serious collection with a natural emphasis on equine art, while the [World Equestrian Center](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/ocala/world_equestrian_center) is the showpiece of the modern horse economy. If you are driving between Gainesville, Orlando, or the Gulf Coast springs, Ocala is worth more than a fuel stop.
