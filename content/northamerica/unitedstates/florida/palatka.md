Palatka is a quiet river town on the St. Johns River in north-central Florida, well placed for anyone wanting a base away from the theme-park corridor. It sits about 50 miles south of [Jacksonville](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/jacksonville), 30 miles east of Gainesville, and 30 miles southwest of [St. Augustine](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/staugustine).

The town has a small, walkable downtown with antique shops and a fishing pier on the [St. Johns River waterfront](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/palatka/st_johns_riverfront). The river is wide and slow here — one of the few in North America that flows north — good for boating and exceptional bass fishing. [Ravine Gardens State Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/palatka/ravine_gardens) has a dramatic landscape of 120-foot ravines blanketed in azaleas that bloom spectacularly from late December to March. Palatka hosts the annual Florida Azalea Festival on the first weekend of March — the longest-running festival in Florida, held every year since 1938. The [Ocala National Forest springs](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/palatka/ocala_springs) — Alexander, Silver Glen, Salt Springs — are a short drive west, with 68°F crystal-clear water for swimming and snorkelling year-round.

The [Bronson-Mulholland House](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/palatka/bronson_mulholland_house) preserves a fine antebellum home on the riverfront bluff — one of the oldest surviving buildings in northeast Florida and a reminder of when Palatka was a fashionable steamboat resort for northern visitors. Harriet Beecher Stowe wintered near here and wrote enthusiastically about the river scenery.
