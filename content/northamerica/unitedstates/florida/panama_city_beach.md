Panama City Beach sits on Florida's northwest Panhandle, where the Gulf of Mexico meets St. Andrews Bay. The beaches here are genuinely beautiful -- wide stretches of white quartz sand and clear emerald water that look more like the Caribbean than the typical American South. It is a drive-to destination, drawing families from across the southeastern United States and snowbirds from the north.

The town was long known as a spring break hotspot, and traces of that reputation linger, but a building boom in recent years has shifted the character toward families and couples. [St. Andrews State Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/panama_city_beach/st_andrews_state_park), at the eastern tip, is the highlight -- a protected stretch of dunes, pine flatwoods, and quiet beach that feels a world away from the condo towers along the main strip. [Pier Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/panama_city_beach/pier_park), a large open-air shopping and entertainment complex, anchors the commercial end of things. The fishing is good, both from the shore and on charter boats, and the [diving on the artificial reefs offshore](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/panama_city_beach/offshore_diving_and_fishing) is surprisingly decent for the Gulf.
