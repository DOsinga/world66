  The Navy sank a number of ships offshore to create artificial reefs,
  including the USS Accokeek and the USS Strength. The wrecks sit in
  depths ranging from 60 to 100 feet and are encrusted with corals and
  sponges that have accumulated over decades. Visibility on the good days
  runs to 60 feet or more, and the fish life around the structures is denser
  than anything you'll find on the natural bottom.

Panama City Beach has a surprisingly active diving scene for a Gulf resort. The Navy and state have deployed dozens of artificial reefs offshore — decommissioned vessels, concrete structures, and limestone boulders — that have become some of the most productive dive sites in the eastern Gulf. Dive charter operators run multiple trips daily from the marina on Grand Lagoon.

Charter fishing is equally well-developed. The Gulf here holds amberjack, grouper, snapper, and kingfish offshore, and inshore fishing on the bay produces redfish, flounder, and speckled trout. Full-day, half-day, and evening charters are available from numerous operators on the marina docks at Grand Lagoon. You don't need to bring anything — tackle, bait, and ice are included.
