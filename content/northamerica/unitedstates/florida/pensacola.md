Pensacola sits on the western tip of the Florida Panhandle, closer in feel to the Gulf Coast South than to the theme-park Florida further down the peninsula. The city has a long history — it claims to be the oldest European settlement in the United States, founded by the Spanish in 1559 — and the compact historic [Seville Square district](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/pensacola/seville_square_historic_district) and [Palafox Street](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/pensacola/palafox_street), with its tree-lined streets and mix of colonial, Victorian, and Creole architecture, are genuinely charming to walk through.

The main draw for most visitors is [Pensacola Beach](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/pensacola/pensacola_beach), a barrier island with white quartz sand and clear emerald water that ranks among the finest beaches in the country. The Naval Air Station, home of the Blue Angels flight demonstration team, is the other big attraction — the [National Naval Aviation Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/pensacola/national_naval_aviation_museum) on the base is free and worth a visit even if you are not a military enthusiast. The [Gulf Islands National Seashore](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/pensacola/gulf_islands_national_seashore) protects the undeveloped barrier island stretches, and the Civil War forts [Fort Pickens](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/pensacola/fort_pickens) and [Fort Barrancas](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/pensacola/fort_barrancas) are among the Gulf Coast's most significant military sites.

Pensacola has a lively arts scene, with the Great Gulfcoast Arts Festival in the first week of November, regular concerts at the civic centre, and fireworks over the bay on the Fourth of July. For divers, the [USS Oriskany](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/pensacola/uss_oriskany) — a decommissioned aircraft carrier sunk as an artificial reef — is one of the most extraordinary dive sites in the Gulf.
