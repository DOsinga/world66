  Fort Barrancas has defensive fortifications from four different empires and nations stacked on top of each other. The Spanish built the original earthen battery in 1698; the British replaced it in stone in 1763; the Spanish returned and rebuilt again in 1787; the Americans reconstructed the whole thing in brick in 1844. Each successive occupier found the previous construction inadequate and started over. The American version, which stands today, is a marvel of 19th-century military engineering with a water battery and dry moat that give it a distinctly European castle character.
---

Fort Barrancas, on the bluff above Pensacola Bay on the Naval Air Station, is one of three connected forts that once defended the bay entrance. The current brick construction dates from the 1840s and incorporates elements of its Spanish predecessors. The design — a classic 19th-century American coastal defense fort with a water battery below the main wall — is elegant in its engineering logic.

The Advanced Redoubt nearby is an additional earthwork fortification from the 1870s, connected to the main fort by an underground tunnel. Both are managed by the National Park Service and open for free self-guided tours. The bluff position gives excellent views over Pensacola Bay. Getting here requires access through the Naval Air Station — bring ID.
