  Palafox Street was named for the Duke of Palafox, a Spanish military hero of the Peninsular War. It has been Pensacola's main commercial street since the Spanish period and has seen more political transitions than almost any other American main street — it was part of Spanish West Florida, British West Florida, Spanish West Florida again, the Floridas territory, and finally the United States in quick succession before 1820. The ornate Saenger Theatre of 1925, now restored, is the architectural anchor of the southern end.

Palafox Street is Pensacola's historic main street, running north from the waterfront through the heart of downtown. The Palafox Historic District contains some of the city's most significant commercial architecture from the late 19th and early 20th centuries, alongside Spanish-era street patterns and foundations.

The street is active with bars, restaurants, and shops, particularly busy on Thursday evening's "Gallery Night" events and on weekends. The Saenger Theatre (1925), a Spanish Baroque-Revival cinema restored to active use, is worth a look even if you're not seeing a show. The Pensacola Blue Wahoos baseball stadium at the southern end of Palafox, on the waterfront, is a pleasant venue for an evening game. Walking north from downtown reveals the transition to the North Hill residential district, one of the finest Victorian-era neighbourhoods in Florida.
