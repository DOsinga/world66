  The current Pensacola Lighthouse, lit in 1859, replaced an earlier lighthouse that was discovered to be too short and in the wrong position to be useful. The Confederates darkened it during the Civil War to prevent Union ships from navigating the bay; Union forces relit it after taking control of Fort Pickens. The lighthouse keeper's quarters are reported to be haunted, and paranormal tours are offered at night for those interested in this dimension of the attraction. The Pensacola Lighthouse stands at the entry to the Naval Air Station, built in 1859 from brick and concrete. At 49 metres, it is one of Florida's tallest lighthouses and gives panoramic views of Pensacola Bay, the Gulf Islands, and the blue horizon of the Gulf of Mexico from the top gallery. On practice days, the Blue Angels fly past at close range — an extraordinary coincidence of historical and modern military presences.

The lighthouse is managed as a museum by a historical organization and is open for tours most days. Climbing the 177 steps to the top is the main attraction; the museum in the keeper's quarters covers the lighthouse's history and the maritime heritage of Pensacola Bay. Located inside the NAS Pensacola perimeter; ID required for access.
