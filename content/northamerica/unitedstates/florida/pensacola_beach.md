Pensacola Beach is a tiny unincorporated strip of barrier island near [Pensacola](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/pensacola), on the Gulf Islands National Seashore. Originally owned by the Army, the land was handed to the state of Florida in 1947 on condition that none of it be sold, so all occupants hold long-term leases. A large section is now protected as one of the ten National Seashores in the United States.

The white quartz sand and clear Gulf water are the main draw. The beach runs for about eight miles, and the eastern end — within the [Gulf Islands National Seashore](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/pensacola_beach/gulf_islands_national_seashore) — is quiet and undeveloped, a sharp contrast to the hotel strip and [boardwalk](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/pensacola_beach/pensacola_beach_boardwalk) closer to town. The sand is particularly fine — ground quartz from the Appalachians — and stays cool even in the summer sun, which is one of the reasons locals rate this beach above most others on the Gulf Coast.
