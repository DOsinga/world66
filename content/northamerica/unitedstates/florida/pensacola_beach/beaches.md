---
title: "Beaches"
type: section
---

Pensacola Beach's eight miles of white quartz sand are the whole point of the place. The sand is among the finest on the Gulf Coast — mineral geologists note that the beach is effectively ground quartz from the Appalachians, which is why it is so white and stays cool in the midday sun. The water is clear and the sea floor is sandy and gentle, making it excellent for swimming, particularly for children.

The western end of the beach, near the boardwalk, is the liveliest part — beach chairs and umbrellas for rent, water sports vendors, and easy access to restaurants. The further east you go toward the national seashore boundary, the quieter and more natural it becomes. In the protected area, the dunes are intact and sea turtles nest in season (late spring through summer); if you see marked nests, give them a wide berth.

The beach faces due south, which means you get good sun all day rather than watching it disappear behind a building by mid-afternoon — an advantage over east-facing Florida beaches. Water temperatures peak in late summer and early autumn; the Gulf stays warm enough for comfortable swimming through October most years.
