About 90 minutes south of Sarasota, Corkscrew is one of the most extraordinary natural areas in Florida — a 2.5-mile boardwalk through the largest old-growth bald cypress forest in North America. The trees are up to 700 years old and tower above a swamp ecosystem that seems genuinely prehistoric. Wood storks nest here in winter in the largest colony in the Southeast; in summer the lush understory fills with wildflowers, alligators, and wading birds.

The boardwalk is flat and accessible. Go in winter for the wood stork nesting season and cooler temperatures, or in summer for maximum greenery and fewer crowds. Take insect repellent. Managed by the Audubon Society, which keeps it in immaculate condition. Worth the drive from Sarasota as a half-day trip.
