Canaveral National Seashore protects one of the longest undeveloped stretches of Atlantic coast in Florida, a barrier island caught between ocean, lagoon, and space-age infrastructure. It sits on the northern edge of the Kennedy Space Center landscape, so the view can swing from sea oats and turtle tracks to rocket gantries on the horizon.

The seashore has two main public approaches. The southern entrance near Titusville leads to [Playalinda Beach](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/spacecoastarea/canaveralnationalseashore/playalinda_beach), the easiest choice from the [Space Coast Area](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/spacecoastarea). The northern entrance from New Smyrna Beach gives access to Apollo Beach, [Turtle Mound](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/spacecoastarea/canaveralnationalseashore/turtle_mound), and the historic Eldora area along [Mosquito Lagoon](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/spacecoastarea/canaveralnationalseashore/mosquito_lagoon).

Come for quiet beach time, birding, paddling, surf fishing, and the strange thrill of watching a launch from a wild shore. Facilities are deliberately limited: boardwalks, vault toilets, parking lots, and not much else. Bring water, shade, insect repellent, and a plan for where you entered, because the north and south districts do not connect by public road through the middle.
