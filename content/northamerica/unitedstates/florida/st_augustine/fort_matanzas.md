Fort Matanzas National Monument sits on a small island in the Matanzas River about 14 miles south of St. Augustine, accessible only by a free ferry operated by the National Park Service. The fort itself is a compact 1742 Spanish watchtower built of coquina — smaller and more utilitarian than the Castillo, but in a far more dramatic setting, surrounded by marsh and open water.

The crossing takes a few minutes, and once on the island you can walk to the fort and climb the narrow staircase to the gun deck for views across the estuary. The surrounding salt marsh is excellent for birdwatching: roseate spoonbills, anhingas, and wading herons are reliably present. The ferry runs on a first-come, first-served basis and can fill up on busy weekends; arrive at the visitor center by mid-morning to secure a spot. The National Park Service also maintains a short trail through the coastal scrub on the main shoreline.
