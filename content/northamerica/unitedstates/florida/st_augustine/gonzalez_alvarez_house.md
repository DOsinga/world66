The González-Álvarez House, known locally as the Oldest House, is the oldest surviving European-built structure in the United States, with construction dating to around 1723 on the site of an even earlier building. It stands just south of the plaza on St. Francis Street, a short walk from the main tourist circuit but often bypassed in favor of more prominent sights — which makes it one of the more pleasantly uncrowded experiences in the city.

The St. Augustine Historical Society runs it as a museum and has been careful to preserve the evidence of the house's evolution through different colonial periods. The ground floor reflects Spanish construction — thick coquina walls, low ceilings — while the upper story is a later British addition with larger windows and a different floor plan. The adjacent museum building provides archaeological and historical context for the site, and the walled garden behind the house is a peaceful place to sit. Allow an hour.
