Memorial Presbyterian Church on Sevilla Street is one of the finest examples of Venetian Renaissance architecture in the United States, built by Henry Flagler in 1890 as a personal memorial. The exterior of pale buff coquina and terracotta is imposing but not overwhelming; the interior is breathtaking — a great coffered dome over the nave, marble floors, gilded woodwork, and a series of stained-glass windows that fill the space with colored light.

The church is still an active congregation and open to visitors during the week. The adjacent mausoleum, where Flagler himself is interred, can be visited as part of a self-guided tour. This is one of the most underappreciated buildings in St. Augustine — it gets far less attention than the Ponce de Leon Hotel across town, but it is arguably the more beautiful of the two.
