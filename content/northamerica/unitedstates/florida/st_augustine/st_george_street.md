St. George Street is the pedestrianised spine of St. Augustine's historic core, running about a third of a mile from the old city gate in the north to the Plaza de la Constitución in the south. The street itself dates from the First Spanish Period, and several of the buildings along it are reconstructions of colonial-era structures standing on their original foundations.

The southern section of the street is commercial — souvenir shops, ice cream parlors, and tourist restaurants — but the northern end near the city gate is quieter and holds the most historically significant buildings: the Peña-Peck House, the Ximenez-Fatio House, and the entrance to the Colonial Quarter. The city gate at the northern end, built in 1808, is a photogenic point of entry. Walk the street in the morning before the tour groups arrive or in the evening when the shops have closed.
