The Ximenez-Fatio House on Aviles Street is one of the best-preserved examples of Spanish colonial domestic architecture in the United States, dating from 1798. It operated as one of St. Augustine's most fashionable boarding houses during the early American period, and its story is now interpreted through the lens of the remarkable women who ran it and the visitors they received.

The Colonial Dames of America have restored the house and run it as a house museum. The ground floor trading room, the upstairs guest rooms, and the kitchen garden are all open, and the guided tours are among the better house museum experiences in the city. The house is unusual in that it still occupies its original footprint on a narrow street in the old town rather than having been moved or heavily modified.
