Tarpon Springs is a small Florida Gulf Coast town with a character unlike anything else in the state. In the early twentieth century, Greek immigrants arrived to harvest sea sponges from the Gulf waters, and their presence shaped the town so thoroughly that it still feels like a transplanted Greek village — complete with [Greek Orthodox churches](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/tarpon_springs/saint_nicholas_greek_orthodox_cathedral), waterfront tavernas, and the smell of fresh-baked baklava drifting off Dodecanese Boulevard.

The [Sponge Docks](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/tarpon_springs/sponge_docks) are the heart of it. Fishing boats still come in loaded with natural sponges, which are sold in the dockside shops alongside Greek ceramics, icons, and tourist kitsch. It can get crowded on weekends, but wander a block or two inland toward [Spring Bayou](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/tarpon_springs/spring_bayou) and you'll find a quieter, genuinely lived-in town. The Greek community here is not a theme park — it's real.

For a Florida day trip or a short stopover, Tarpon Springs punches well above its size. The food alone is worth the drive: fresh seafood, spanakopita, and slow-cooked lamb at prices that haven't caught up with the tourist traffic yet. The [Anclote River](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/tarpon_springs/anclote_river) estuary beyond the docks offers some of the best kayaking on the Gulf Coast, with manatees in winter and easy access to the island preserve at Anclote Key.
