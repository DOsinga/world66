---
title: When to Go
type: section
---

Winter is the best time to visit Tequesta — roughly November through April. Temperatures are warm but not oppressive, typically in the low 70s Fahrenheit (low 20s Celsius) during the day and comfortable at night. The humidity drops to tolerable levels, rain is infrequent, and the hurricane season is over. This is peak season in South Florida, and popular spots fill up, but Tequesta is quiet enough that it never feels overwhelmed.

Summer (June through September) is hot, very humid, and wet. Afternoon thunderstorms are nearly daily, often violent, and temperatures regularly exceed 90°F (32°C) with humidity to match. The wildlife is still active — sea turtles nest on the beaches through summer — but outdoor activity is best confined to early morning before the heat builds. August and September sit in the heart of hurricane season; storms are infrequent but the risk is real.

Spring and autumn are transitional and often pleasant. April and May bring warming temperatures before the full summer heat arrives. October and November are the best months of autumn — the rains ease, temperatures fall to manageable levels, and the snowbirds haven't arrived yet in full force.

The dry season alignment with peak tourist season means that if you are coming primarily to kayak the Loxahatchee or explore Jonathan Dickinson State Park, December through February offers the best combination of weather and wildlife activity. Manatees congregate in the warm spring-fed waters of the river in winter, making cold snaps the best time for sightings.
