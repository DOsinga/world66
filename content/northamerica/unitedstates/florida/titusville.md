Titusville sits across the Indian River Lagoon from Kennedy Space Center, with a clear line of sight to launch pads 39A and 39B. For sixty years that view has been the town's defining feature. When the Saturn V lifted Apollo astronauts toward the moon, half a million spectators jammed Titusville's riverfront; today SpaceX's regular Falcon and Starship missions still draw crowds to the same waterfront parks. If you want to watch a launch from the closest publicly accessible point, this is where you come.

Beyond the rockets, Titusville is a working small city of about 50,000 people. The compact downtown along South Washington Avenue holds a handful of restored 19th-century buildings — the [Pritchard House](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/titusville/pritchard_house) is the showpiece — and the [American Space Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/titusville/american_space_museum), a small private collection of mission artefacts that complements the official Kennedy Space Center Visitor Complex 20 miles to the south. The [Titusville Commercial District](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/titusville/titusville_commercial_district) covers most of the historic core.

The bigger attractions are out of town. [Merritt Island National Wildlife Refuge](/northamerica/unitedstates/florida/merritt_island_national_wildlife_refuge), reached over the A. Max Brewer Bridge, wraps around the entire space center and is one of the best wildlife refuges in Florida — over 350 species of birds, alligators, manatees, and the world's largest population of nesting bald eagles outside Alaska. Canaveral National Seashore runs north from the refuge along 24 miles of undeveloped Atlantic coast.

Titusville is honest about what it is — a launch town. The motels and seafood restaurants fill up around big missions and empty out in between. It is not a destination in the polished tourism sense. But for a few days organised around a launch, a refuge dawn, and a Kennedy Space Center day trip, it more than holds its own.
