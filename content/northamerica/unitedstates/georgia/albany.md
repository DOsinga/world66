Albany is the largest city in southwest Georgia and the seat of Dougherty County. It sits on the Flint River about 170 miles south of Atlanta. Not a common tourist destination, but it has a surprisingly strong arts and cultural scene for a mid-size Southern city, and it carries a weight of history that most places its size don't.

The [Albany Civil Rights Institute](/northamerica/unitedstates/georgia/albany/albany_civil_rights_institute) on Whitney Avenue tells the story of the Albany Movement, one of the early mass campaigns of the Civil Rights era — the city was a significant battleground in 1961 and 1962, when local activists and SNCC organizers launched months of marches, sit-ins, and jail protests that drew Martin Luther King Jr. and ultimately shaped the strategy of later campaigns in Birmingham. The [Albany Museum of Art](/northamerica/unitedstates/georgia/albany/albany_museum_of_art) holds one of the best collections of traditional African art in the Southeast. The [Flint RiverQuarium](/northamerica/unitedstates/georgia/albany/flint_riverquarium), a freshwater aquarium dedicated to the Flint River ecosystem, sits on the riverfront near [Ray Charles Plaza](/northamerica/unitedstates/georgia/albany/ray_charles_plaza) — a striking bronze memorial to Albany's most famous native son, born here in 1930. The [Thronateeska Heritage Center](/northamerica/unitedstates/georgia/albany/thronateeska_heritage_center) covers local history and science, and has a well-regarded full-dome planetarium. North of town, [Chehaw Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/georgia/albany/chehaw_park) holds 800 acres of greenspace with a wild animal park designed by naturalist Jim Fowler of Wild Kingdom.
