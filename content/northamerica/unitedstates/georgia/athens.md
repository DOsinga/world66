Athens is a university town about 65 miles northeast of Atlanta, home to the University of Georgia and a music scene that produced R.E.M. and the B-52s in the late 1970s and '80s. It's an appealing place to spend a day or two — good food, live music venues, and a walkable downtown centered on Broad Street.

The [University of Georgia](/northamerica/unitedstates/georgia/athens/university_of_georgia) campus is worth wandering, particularly the older buildings around North Campus. The [State Botanical Garden](/northamerica/unitedstates/georgia/athens/state_botanical_garden) on the south end of campus is free and surprisingly extensive — 300 acres of themed gardens and river forest. Downtown Athens concentrates most of the restaurants, bars, and live music clubs; it's lively on weekends during the academic year.

The [40 Watt Club](/northamerica/unitedstates/georgia/athens/forty_watt_club) on West Washington Street is the shrine of the Athens music scene, still booking live bands most nights. The [Morton Theatre](/northamerica/unitedstates/georgia/athens/morton_theatre) a block away is one of the first theatres in the South built and operated by an African American businessman, now restored and active again. Outside City Hall, the [Double-Barreled Cannon](/northamerica/unitedstates/georgia/athens/double_barreled_cannon) is the city's most delightful historic artifact — a Civil War experiment that went spectacularly wrong on its first and only test firing.
