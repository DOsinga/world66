The Morton Theatre on West Washington Street is one of the most historically significant buildings in Athens. Built in 1910 by Monroe Morton, a Black businessman and barber who accumulated enough capital to build his own commercial block, it was one of the first theatres in the United States designed, built, owned, and operated by an African American.

The theatre hosted some of the biggest names in vaudeville and early jazz during its prime years — performers like Bessie Smith and Cab Calloway appeared here. After decades of decline and closure, the building was restored and is now a functioning performing arts venue and National Historic Landmark. Concerts, community events, and theatre productions still fill the calendar. The building is a reminder that Athens has had a rich African American cultural life running parallel to its better-known music scene.
