The Old Medical College of Georgia is one of Augusta's best pieces of Greek Revival architecture, a columned 1830s building from the period when the city was becoming a serious medical and educational centre. Its clean facade and raised portico make it feel more like a courthouse or temple than a school for anatomy and surgery.

It is usually appreciated from the outside or as part of a downtown history walk, rather than as a conventional museum stop. Pair it with the Augusta Museum of History, the riverfront and the old government buildings if you want a compact picture of the city's antebellum civic core.
