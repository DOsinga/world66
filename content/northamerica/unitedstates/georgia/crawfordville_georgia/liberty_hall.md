Liberty Hall is the antebellum home of Alexander H. Stephens, now preserved inside A.H. Stephens State Park. The house is a relatively modest two-storey structure, expanded several times from a simple cottage that Stephens first purchased in 1834. Tours take you through the ground-floor rooms, which include a parlour, dining room, and Stephens' personal library.

Stephens was one of the stranger political figures of the Civil War era: a small, sickly man known as "Little Aleck," who weighed perhaps 90 pounds, argued against secession until the moment Georgia voted for it, and then became the Confederacy's second-ranking official. The tension between his stated principles and his actions runs through the house's interpretive materials. A separate Confederate Museum on the park grounds expands on his career and includes period artefacts.
