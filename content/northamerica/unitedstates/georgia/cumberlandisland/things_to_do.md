title: Things to Do
type: section
---

Cumberland Island is best approached as a wilderness experience with a remarkable layer of Gilded Age history on top. The [wild beach](/northamerica/unitedstates/georgia/cumberlandisland/east_beach) along the entire eastern shore is 18 miles of undeveloped Atlantic coast — one of the longest stretches of wild beach on the East Coast of the United States.

The southern end of the island has the most history. The [Dungeness ruins](/northamerica/unitedstates/georgia/cumberlandisland/dungeness_ruins) are the remains of a Carnegie mansion burned in 1959; the tabby foundations, the brick chimneys, and the formal garden gone wild in the subtropical heat are genuinely atmospheric. The [First African Baptist Church](/northamerica/unitedstates/georgia/cumberlandisland/first_african_baptist_church) at the Settlement — a tiny 19th-century structure of boards and light — is where John F. Kennedy Jr. married Carolyn Bessette in 1996, in a ceremony so secret that no press was present.

In the middle of the island, [Plum Orchard](/northamerica/unitedstates/georgia/cumberlandisland/plum_orchard) is a grand 1898 Carnegie mansion occasionally open for National Park Service tours. [The wild horses](/northamerica/unitedstates/georgia/cumberlandisland/wild_horses) are everywhere — feral descendants of horses brought by settlers, now accustomed enough to humans that you can approach quite close.

Wildlife beyond the horses includes alligators in the freshwater ponds, white-tailed deer, wild turkeys, and armadillos. Bird life is excellent, particularly in the maritime forest and along the marsh edges.
