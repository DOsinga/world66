---
title: When to Go
type: section
---

Cumberland Island is accessible year-round, but the experience varies considerably by season.

**Spring (March to May)** is the best all-round time. Temperatures are pleasant (70–80°F), the vegetation is green, wildflowers are blooming, and the island is less crowded than summer. Migratory birds pass through in good numbers.

**Summer (June to August)** is hot and humid — temperatures regularly exceed 90°F and the mosquitoes and biting flies (particularly the notorious "no-see-ums") can be ferocious. Loggerhead turtle nesting activity peaks in June and July. The island is at its busiest in summer; the daily visitor limit means the ferry books out quickly.

**Autumn (September to November)** is excellent. Temperatures moderate, insects become less oppressive, and the forest takes on subtle colour. Loggerhead hatchlings emerge in September and October. Crowds thin noticeably after Labor Day.

**Winter (December to February)** is the quietest period. Temperatures are mild (50–65°F) by most standards, the island is essentially to yourself, and wildlife is active — particularly shore and wading birds. An ideal time for anyone who wants the place in near-solitude.

**Practical notes:** The ferry from St. Marys runs daily except Tuesdays and Wednesdays. The daily visitor limit is 300 people. Reservations are essential in summer and advisable year-round — book via recreation.gov.
