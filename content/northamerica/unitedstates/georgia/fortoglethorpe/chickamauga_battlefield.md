Established in 1890, Chickamauga and Chattanooga National Military Park was the first and is the largest national military park in the United States. The Chickamauga section, straddling the Georgia–Tennessee border near Fort Oglethorpe, preserves the ground where Union and Confederate forces fought one of the most brutal engagements of the Civil War over two days in September 1863.

The park contains miles of driving and walking routes past preserved earthworks, artillery positions, and more than 700 monuments erected by states and veterans' groups beginning in the 1890s. The Fuller Collection of American Military Arms at the Visitor Center is one of the finest assemblies of antique military weapons in the country. A seven-mile driving tour takes about two hours; walking the full network of trails adds considerably more time. Entry is free, and rangers lead guided walks on weekends.
