---
title: Getting Around
type: section
---

A car is essential in Valdosta. The city is built around the I-75 corridor and sprawls outward in the way of mid-size American highway towns: shopping centers, restaurant strips, and motel clusters strung along wide arterial roads that are uncomfortable or impossible to navigate on foot. Without a car, your options are severely limited.

The one exception is the historic downtown, which is a compact, genuinely walkable grid of about ten square blocks. If you're staying downtown, you can walk to the courthouse square, a few cafes, and some restaurants without needing to get back in the car. But downtown is an island: getting from the interstate corridor to the downtown, or from either to Wild Adventures on the south side of town, requires driving.

Ride-share services (Uber and Lyft) operate in Valdosta and are reliable enough for occasional trips, though surge pricing can apply during busy periods around university events. The local public bus system (Valdosta Transit) runs limited routes on a limited schedule and is not practical for visitors.

Valdosta State University's campus is walkable internally, but it sits in its own pocket of the city and isn't within walking distance of the downtown or the I-75 strip.
