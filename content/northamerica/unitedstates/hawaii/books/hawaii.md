Michener's epic novel opens before the islands existed — following lava erupting from the ocean floor — and works through Polynesian voyagers, American missionaries, Chinese labourers, and Japanese immigrants up to the mid-twentieth century. It is long, novelistic in the old-fashioned sense, and unapologetically sweeping. Its portrait of Hawaii as a place shaped by every wave of arrival remains the most comprehensive fictional account of the islands. Read it before you go and you will understand why certain tensions on the islands run as deep as they do.
