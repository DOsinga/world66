Jin, a Korean picture bride, arrives in Honolulu in 1914 knowing almost nothing about the man she is to marry. Brennert follows her life across five decades — through a bad marriage, through work in the laundry and gambling dens, through Honolulu's Chinatown and the sugar plantation world — building a vivid portrait of the immigrant experience that shaped modern Hawaii. The historical research is meticulous, and Brennert has a gift for making the past feel inhabited rather than reconstructed.
