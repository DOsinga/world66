Four granddaughters return to Hawaii to unravel their shared history through Puno, their part-Polynesian, part-New England grandmother. Davenport writes in a dense, sensory style that draws on traditional Hawaiian storytelling, weaving together the mythological and the brutally historical — the overthrow of the monarchy, the plantation era, World War II. It is a book about land and blood and the cost of paradise. Davenport grew up in Hawaii and it shows on every page.
