A sweeping multigenerational novel following a Hawaiian family from the 1840s to the present, told through the matriarch Pono and her four granddaughters. Davenport, who is of Hawaiian and New England descent, writes with fierce lyricism about the land, the sea, and the pain of cultural dispossession. The novel covers the overthrow of the Hawaiian kingdom, the plantation years, World War II, and the struggle to hold onto Hawaiian identity in the face of Americanisation. More raw and politically alive than Michener, and told from the inside.
