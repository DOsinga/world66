A Hilo family's youngest son falls overboard and is carried back to the boat by sharks — from this extraordinary opening, Washburn builds a novel about what it means to be Hawaiian in the modern world, when the land has been taken and the old powers no longer feel certain. The novel moves between the Big Island, Portland, and San Diego, each sibling narrating in a different voice. Magical realism and raw social realism coexist without strain. It is the most powerful contemporary Hawaiian novel, and essential reading for anyone who wants to understand the islands beneath the tourism.
