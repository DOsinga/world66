---
title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

Hawaiian food is its own thing — not Hawaiian in the luau sense, but a street-food and plate-lunch culture shaped by the islands' plantation history. Japanese, Filipino, Chinese, Korean, Portuguese, and Polynesian traditions all fed into the local cuisine, producing dishes you will not find anywhere else: plate lunches with two scoops of rice and macaroni salad, saimin noodle soup, loco moco, musubi. Shave ice is the signature sweet — finely shaved (not crushed) ice with tropical syrups, served from roadside stands across the islands. The farm-to-table movement has taken hold strongly across all the islands, with Maui in particular producing exceptional produce, coffee, and cattle.
