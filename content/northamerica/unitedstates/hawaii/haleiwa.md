Haleiwa is the gateway to Oahu's North Shore and the unofficial capital of Hawaiian surf culture. The small [town](/northamerica/unitedstates/hawaii/haleiwa/haleiwa_town) sits at the mouth of the Anahulu River, where a photogenic rainbow bridge marks the start of a two-lane strip of surf shops, shave ice stands, food trucks, and art galleries. It feels a world apart from the high-rises of [Honolulu](/northamerica/unitedstates/hawaii/honolulu) and Waikiki, an hour's drive to the south. The [Lili'uokalani Protestant Church](/northamerica/unitedstates/hawaii/haleiwa/liliuokalani_protestant_church) on the river's edge has its own piece of Hawaiian royal history.

In winter, the North Shore transforms. The beaches between Haleiwa and Sunset Beach — including [the Banzai Pipeline](/northamerica/unitedstates/hawaii/haleiwa/banzai_pipeline), the most famous wave in surfing — produce swells that draw the world's best surfers and crowds of spectators who line the sand to watch. [Waimea Bay](/northamerica/unitedstates/hawaii/haleiwa/waimea_bay) is the big-wave arena where "The Eddie" Aikau Invitational runs in waves over 30 feet — one of the sport's rarest and most celebrated events. Even if you do not surf, watching from the beach on a big day is unforgettable.

In summer the ocean calms and the same beaches become gentle enough for swimming and snorkelling. Behind Waimea Bay, [Waimea Valley](/northamerica/unitedstates/hawaii/haleiwa/waimea_valley) offers a walk through botanical gardens and Hawaiian archaeological sites to a waterfall with a swimming hole. The [Pu'u o Mahuka Heiau](/northamerica/unitedstates/hawaii/haleiwa/puuo_mahuka_heiau) — the largest heiau on Oahu — overlooks the bay from a hilltop. Back in town, [Matsumoto's shave ice](/northamerica/unitedstates/hawaii/haleiwa/matsumoto_shave_ice) has been serving its rainbow-coloured cones since 1951 and always has a queue.
