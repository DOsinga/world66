The Lili'uokalani Protestant Church stands at the edge of Haleiwa town, a simple white New England-style church that has served the Haleiwa community since 1832. Its association with Hawaii's last queen gives it a significance beyond its modest size. The building that survives today dates from 1961, a replacement of the original structure, but the congregation's continuity stretches back to the earliest years of Protestant mission activity on Oahu.

The church is active and conducts services in both English and Hawaiian. The clock presented by Queen Lili'uokalani — one of the few surviving objects with a direct personal connection to the queen in a church setting — is viewable during visiting hours. The grounds along the Anahulu River are pleasant, and the view across the river from the bridge at the entrance to Haleiwa town is one of the most photographed spots on the North Shore.
