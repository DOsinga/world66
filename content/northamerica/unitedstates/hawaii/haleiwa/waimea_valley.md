  Before European contact, Waimea Valley was one of the most densely populated valleys on Oahu. Hawaiian communities built terraced taro fields up the valley walls, maintained fishponds at the valley mouth, and constructed more than 80 heiau (religious sites) throughout the area. The population collapsed after European contact — first through disease, then through economic disruption — but the physical remains of the agricultural terraces and some heiau foundations are still visible beneath the vegetation.

Waimea Valley is a 1,875-acre nature park and cultural preserve behind Waimea Bay, managed by the Office of Hawaiian Affairs. The walk along the valley floor follows a stream through botanical gardens planted with native and Polynesian species, past archaeological sites that document the valley's long human history, to a waterfall at the back of the valley with a swimming hole below it. The whole walk is about 1.5 miles each way and takes two to three hours at a comfortable pace.

The botanical collection is extensive — the valley has been used for horticultural research since the late nineteenth century and holds hundreds of species. The cultural programming includes demonstrations of traditional Hawaiian skills and, at certain times of year, hula performances. The admission fee goes toward maintaining the valley and supporting Hawaiian cultural programs, which makes it worth paying. Bring swim gear for the waterfall pool.
