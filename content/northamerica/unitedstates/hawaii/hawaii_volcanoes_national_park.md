One of the most extraordinary national parks in the United States, covering 1,300 square kilometres of active volcanic landscape on the Big Island. Two active volcanoes — Kilauea and Mauna Loa — sit within the park. Kilauea's summit caldera at Halemaʻumaʻu is the spiritual home of Pele, the Hawaiian goddess of fire and volcanoes.

The Crater Rim Drive loops around the summit caldera with multiple overlooks, steam vents, and lava tube walks. The Thurston Lava Tube (Nahuku) is a 500-year-old lava tube lined with ferns that you can walk through. The Chain of Craters Road descends 1,100 metres to the coast, passing cinder cones and ancient petroglyphs, and ending where lava flowed over the road in 2003. The Jaggar Museum overlook has the best views of the summit crater — go at night when the glow is most dramatic. Entry fee is $30 per vehicle, valid for seven days.
