Shangri La is Doris Duke's extraordinary estate on the Kahala peninsula, a clifftop compound built in 1937 and filled over six decades with more than 4,500 objects of Islamic art — tiles, textiles, metalwork, ceramics, architectural elements, and paintings from Morocco to Iran to India. The setting alone is remarkable: a Moghul-inspired pleasure palace perched above the Pacific.

Access is by guided tour only, booked through the Honolulu Museum of Art, which administers the property. Tours run Tuesday through Saturday and transport departs from the museum. The tour lasts about two hours. Demand is high and tickets sell weeks in advance, especially in winter. Worth the planning — there is nothing else like it in Hawaii, or possibly anywhere in the United States.
