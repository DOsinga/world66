Huleia National Wildlife Refuge protects a broad wetland and river valley near Lihue, where the Huleia River winds through taro fields, ponds, and lowland habitat. Its main importance is ecological: the refuge shelters endangered Hawaiian waterbirds such as the ae'o, 'alae ke'oke'o, and koloa maoli in a part of Kauai that otherwise sees heavy development pressure.

Public access is limited because the refuge is managed primarily for wildlife, but the landscape can be appreciated from nearby overlooks, paddling routes, and guided outings on the Huleia River. Treat it as a quiet nature stop rather than a trail park, and bring binoculars if birdlife is the reason for going.
