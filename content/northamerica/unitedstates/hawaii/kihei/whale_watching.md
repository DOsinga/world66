story: >
  The Hawaiian Islands Humpback Whale National Marine Sanctuary — which covers the waters around Kihei — was established in 1992 specifically to protect the North Pacific humpback population during their winter breeding season. When the sanctuary was created, the North Pacific humpback population was estimated at around 1,000. Today it exceeds 10,000, making it one of the more successful marine mammal recovery stories on record.
---

Between December and April, humpback whales arrive in the warm shallow waters around Maui to breed and give birth. Kihei's west-facing beaches offer some of the best shore-based whale watching in Hawaii — on a good day you can see breaches, tail slaps, and spouts without leaving the beach. The peak month is typically February.

Boat trips offer closer views and the chance to hear whale song underwater (operators typically drop a hydrophone). Several operators run trips from Kihei Boat Ramp and Maalaea Harbour; Pacific Whale Foundation is one of the more reputable choices. If you prefer to stay on shore, simply park at any of the Kamaole beach parks and scan the horizon with binoculars. The Pacific Whale Foundation also runs a shore-based interpretation station at the beach park during peak season.
