Poipu is the sunny, polished face of south Kauai. The coast here is drier than the north shore, the beaches are easy to reach, and the resort strip never quite erases the lava shelves, monk seals, and red dirt that make Kauai feel like Kauai.

The centre of gravity is [Poipu Beach Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/hawaii/poipu/poipu_beach_park), a pair of sandy coves split by a tombolo where children paddle, snorkellers watch for fish, and sea turtles sometimes haul out at dusk. To the east, [Shipwreck Beach](/northamerica/unitedstates/hawaii/poipu/shipwreck_beach) and the [Mahaulepu Heritage Trail](/northamerica/unitedstates/hawaii/poipu/mahaulepu_heritage_trail) give the area its wilder edge: limestone cliffs, wind, and views along a coast that still feels open.

Poipu also works because it is close to things that are genuinely worth leaving the beach for. [Spouting Horn](/northamerica/unitedstates/hawaii/poipu/spouting_horn) is touristy but still fun when the swell is right, and the National Tropical Botanical Garden's [McBryde Garden](/northamerica/unitedstates/hawaii/poipu/mcbryde_garden) and nearby Allerton Garden are among the best cultivated landscapes in Hawaii. Use [Koloa](/northamerica/unitedstates/hawaii/koloa) for old-town errands and plate lunches, then come back to Poipu for sunset.
