Measured from its base on the ocean floor, Mauna Kea is the tallest mountain on earth — 33,500 feet from base to summit, though only 13,796 of those feet are above sea level. The summit is one of the world's premier sites for astronomical observation, and the cluster of international telescope domes there is visible long before you reach the top. The drive up is dramatic: you pass through cloud forest, alpine desert, and cinder cone landscape. The visitor information station at 9,200 feet is where you should acclimatise before going higher; altitude sickness at the summit is common. Sunset and stargazing from the summit are extraordinary.
