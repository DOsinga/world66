The USS Arizona Memorial sits directly above the sunken hull of the battleship where 1,177 sailors and Marines died on the morning of 7 December 1941. The memorial itself — a white structure spanning the midship section of the wreck — was designed by architect Alfred Preis, an Austrian-American who was himself interned as an enemy alien after Pearl Harbor. You can still see oil rising slowly from the wreck below. The site also includes the USS Missouri, the battleship on whose deck Japan signed the surrender in September 1945, and the USS Bowfin submarine museum. Reserve tickets well in advance — entry to the Arizona Memorial is free but timed tickets run out quickly.

story: >
  Oil still seeps from the Arizona's fuel tanks at the rate of a few quarts per day — a phenomenon locals call "the tears of the Arizona." The ship still holds the remains of 900 of the sailors who died there. The site is an active burial ground, and the National Park Service treats it as such.
