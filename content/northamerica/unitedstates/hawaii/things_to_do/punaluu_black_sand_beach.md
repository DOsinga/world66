One of the Big Island's most distinctive beaches — black volcanic sand, green sea turtles resting on the shore, and a backdrop of palm trees against the stark lava coast. The turtles (honu) are protected and you are required to stay at least six feet away, but they tend to be entirely unbothered by visitors and will often sleep through a crowd gathering nearby. The beach itself is too rough for swimming but is one of the most photographed spots on the island. It sits along the southern coast between Hawaii Volcanoes National Park and the Ka Lae point — the southernmost tip of the United States.
