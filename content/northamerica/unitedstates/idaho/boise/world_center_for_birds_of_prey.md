The World Center for Birds of Prey sits on the sagebrush edge of Boise, where the city gives way to open high desert. It is home to the Peregrine Fund's conservation work and gives visitors a close look at falcons, eagles, owls, and vultures without turning the place into a zoo-style spectacle.

The exhibits are strongest when they connect individual birds to larger recovery stories, especially the peregrine falcon's comeback. Allow time for the outdoor views as well; the setting explains why southern Idaho is such important raptor country.
