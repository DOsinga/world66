Coeur d'Alene sits on the north shore of Lake Coeur d'Alene, close enough to [Spokane](/northamerica/unitedstates/washington/spokane) to be easy, but with a clearer holiday mood than most cities its size. The lake is the point: a long blue sheet of water edged by forested hills, beaches, marinas, and trails.

The best thing about the city is how quickly downtown opens onto the outdoors. [Tubbs Hill](/northamerica/unitedstates/idaho/coeur_dalene/tubbs_hill) rises beside the centre with piney trails and lake views, while [City Park and Independence Point](/northamerica/unitedstates/idaho/coeur_dalene/city_park_and_independence_point) put a public beach, lawns, boat docks, and sunset walks within a few minutes of the shops and restaurants.

Coeur d'Alene is polished, and in summer it can feel busy, but it is not just a resort frontage. The [Museum of North Idaho](/northamerica/unitedstates/idaho/coeur_dalene/museum_of_north_idaho) gives useful context on the Coeur d'Alene people, steamboats, Fort Sherman, railroads, and the mining country east toward [Kellogg](/northamerica/unitedstates/idaho/kellogg). A spare day here is best spent moving between lake, trail, and downtown rather than treating any one sight as the whole story.
