The Victor Railroad Depot is a two-storey frame building in the simplified Craftsman style, built in 1914 when the Oregon Short Line extended its tracks into Teton Valley. It was added to the National Register of Historic Places in 1995. The building has been restored and sits near the centre of Victor as a reminder that this valley was once connected to the outside world by rail rather than highway.

The depot itself is not always open, but the exterior is freely accessible and worth a look. The setting says something about Victor's history: this was once an agricultural town that shipped grain and dairy products by rail to the markets of Salt Lake City. Today the traffic goes the other way — people driving over Teton Pass to ski, hike, and look at the Tetons.
