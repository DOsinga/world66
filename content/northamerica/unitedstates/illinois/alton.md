Alton is a small city perched on limestone bluffs above the Mississippi River, about 15 miles north of downtown St. Louis. The setting is dramatic — the river bends sharply here, and the town climbs up the bluffs in tiers of 19th-century brick buildings. It has the feel of a river town that has seen better days economically but retains genuine character, with a growing arts scene and a lively restaurant strip along Broadway.

The town has deep historical roots. The last of the famous Lincoln-Douglas debates took place at the [Lincoln-Douglas debate site](/northamerica/unitedstates/illinois/alton/lincoln_douglas_site) in 1858, and a life-size [statue of Robert Wadlow](/northamerica/unitedstates/illinois/alton/robert_wadlow_statue) — the tallest person in recorded history, who grew up in Alton — stands near the southern end of town. The [Alton Museum of History and Art](/northamerica/unitedstates/illinois/alton/alton_museum) covers the city's role in the Civil War and the Underground Railroad. Elijah Lovejoy, an abolitionist newspaper editor, was murdered here by a pro-slavery mob in 1837; the [Lovejoy Monument](/northamerica/unitedstates/illinois/alton/lovejoy_monument) in the cemetery honours him.

Broadway and the surrounding blocks are lined with antique shops, galleries, and restaurants. The [Meeting of the Great Rivers Scenic Byway](/northamerica/unitedstates/illinois/alton/great_river_road) runs along the river bluffs south to where the Illinois and Missouri rivers join the Mississippi, offering striking views particularly in autumn when the trees turn. The [Piasa Bird mural](/northamerica/unitedstates/illinois/alton/piasa_bird), a recreation of a pre-contact Native American pictograph on the bluff face north of town, is worth a stop. The [McPike Mansion](/northamerica/unitedstates/illinois/alton/mcpike_mansion), a decaying Victorian Italianate house on the bluff, has a reputation as one of the most haunted buildings in Illinois.
