The Elijah Lovejoy Monument stands in Alton Cemetery, a tall column topped by a figure of Victory, erected in 1897 to commemorate the abolitionist newspaper editor who was murdered in Alton in 1837. Lovejoy, a Presbyterian minister and editor of the Alton Observer, was killed by a pro-slavery mob while defending his printing press — the fourth one destroyed in his attempt to publish anti-slavery journalism in a border state.

The monument is in a quiet part of the cemetery, and the surrounding graves include other figures from Alton's 19th-century history. It is a genuinely moving place for anyone interested in the history of free speech and the abolition movement. The cemetery is open during daylight hours; the monument is near the main entrance.
