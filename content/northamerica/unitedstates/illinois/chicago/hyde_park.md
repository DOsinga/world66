Chicago's university neighbourhood on the South Side, home to the **University of Chicago** — one of the great research universities in the world, whose economics department alone has produced nearly 100 Nobel laureates. The university dominates the neighbourhood physically and intellectually: Gothic quadrangles, the Regenstein Library, the Smart Museum of Art, and the Oriental Institute all sit within a few blocks of each other.

Hyde Park is also the neighbourhood where Barack Obama lived and taught constitutional law before entering politics. The **Museum of Science and Industry** on the lakefront is the largest science museum in the Western Hemisphere, housed in the 1893 World's Columbian Exposition's Fine Arts Building. The neighbourhood has a distinct, slightly insular character compared to the rest of Chicago — quieter, more racially mixed, and focused inward on the university campus.
