A manmade peninsula extending into Lake Michigan at 55th Street, Promontory Point is one of the best places in Chicago to experience the lake at close range. The grassy point is ringed by limestone slabs that serve as informal sunbathing and gathering spots in summer; the views of the skyline to the north are among the finest available from any point accessible on foot.

Built in the 1930s as part of the lakefront park expansion, the Point has no commercial development and has been designated a Chicago landmark in its original form — no new lighting, concessions, or alteration permitted. The contrast with the commercial park infrastructure elsewhere on the lakefront makes it worth seeking out. Walking distance from the Museum of Science and Industry.
