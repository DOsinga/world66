The Loop is the most concentrated assemblage of commercially significant 20th-century architecture in the world, and most of it is visible from the street. The key sequence runs along Dearborn Street from Wacker Drive south to Congress Parkway: the Inland Steel Building (1958), the Marquette Building (1895), the Rookery (1888, interior by Frank Lloyd Wright), the Chicago Board of Trade (1930), and a dozen other buildings that define the history of American commercial architecture.

The Chicago Architecture Center, on Wacker Drive, operates guided walking tours and a riverboat architecture cruise that is the best single introduction to the buildings. The self-guided free map available from the Architecture Center covers approximately 50 buildings on a two-hour walk. The L elevated railway provides a second perspective on the Loop's cornice lines from above street level.
