The West Loop has undergone the most dramatic transformation of any Chicago neighbourhood in the past three decades. What was a working meatpacking and cold-storage district west of the river — gridded with industrial warehouses and elevated truck docks — is now the densest concentration of nationally recognised restaurants in the Midwest, with **Google's Chicago headquarters** anchoring the tech presence that followed the food.

The change came street by street. **Restaurant Row** on **West Randolph Street** between Halsted and Ogden was the early catalyst: Girl and the Goat, Avec, Monteverde, and Au Cheval (whose cheeseburger generated years of national coverage) all opened within a few blocks of one another. The pace accelerated when **Fulton Market** — the actual street where meat was loaded and shipped — became the address for a second wave of restaurants and hotels. The former meatpacking infrastructure has been largely retained: the cold-storage facades, elevated loading bays, and brick warehouses now house restaurants at street level and offices above.

The **Morgan** and **Ashland/Lake** Green and Pink Line stops connect the neighbourhood to the Loop in under ten minutes. On weekend evenings the blocks around Randolph and Fulton are pedestrian-dense enough that the street-level energy approaches that of a proper restaurant district in a European city — which remains unusual for Chicago.

The neighbourhood sits immediately west of the Loop and south of the river. The **United Center** — home of the Bulls and Blackhawks — is about a mile further west, and the walk from Restaurant Row passes through the quieter western residential blocks.
