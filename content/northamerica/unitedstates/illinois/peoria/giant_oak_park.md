Giant Oak Park takes its name from a massive bur oak that has been growing on the site for approximately five centuries — old enough to have been a mature tree when French missionaries first arrived in the Illinois valley in the seventeenth century. Bur oaks are among the longest-lived of the midwestern tree species, adapted to survive the prairie fires that once shaped this landscape, and specimens of this age are increasingly rare as development has taken the open land where they once grew.

The tree itself is the main attraction: its trunk circumference, spread of crown, and overall scale are remarkable, and standing under it gives a concrete sense of what five hundred years of continuous growth looks like. The park surrounding it is a pleasant green space, and the tree provides welcome shade in summer. It is a short detour from the more conventional Peoria sights but worth making.

Giant Oak Park is a small neighbourhood park on the east side of Peoria that centres on a massive bur oak tree estimated to be around 500 years old — meaning it was already a substantial tree when European explorers first reached the Illinois River valley. The tree is approximately 18 feet in circumference and has the broad, gnarled crown typical of old-growth bur oaks that have had space to spread. It is one of the largest trees in the city and one of the few survivors of the original oak savanna that once covered much of central Illinois.

The park around the tree is quiet and largely undiscovered by casual visitors, which makes it a good destination for those who want a moment away from the riverfront bustle. The Illinois prairie oak savanna was one of the most distinctive ecosystems in North America before European settlement replaced it almost entirely with farmland; this single tree is a reminder of what the landscape once looked like.
