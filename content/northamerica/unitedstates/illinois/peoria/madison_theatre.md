The Madison Theatre opened in 1920 as a vaudeville and movie palace, one of the elaborate entertainment venues that prosperous American cities built in the early twentieth century to demonstrate their cultural aspirations. The building's facade and auditorium reflect the ornate commercial architecture of the period, when theater design was meant to transport audiences into a world more glamorous than their daily lives. It was designed to seat over a thousand people and served as Peoria's premier entertainment venue for decades.

The theater has had an eventful subsequent history — periods of neglect, closure, and partial renovation — but it remains standing and has been used for live performances and events. The exterior is one of the more distinctive pieces of early twentieth-century commercial architecture in downtown Peoria, and restoration efforts have kept it in use rather than allowing it to be demolished, as happened to many theaters of its generation.
