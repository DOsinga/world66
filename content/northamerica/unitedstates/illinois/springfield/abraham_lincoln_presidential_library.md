The Abraham Lincoln Presidential Library and Museum is one of the most visited attractions in Illinois, and with good reason. The museum portion offers a comprehensive and emotionally engaging account of Lincoln's life and presidency, from his Kentucky childhood in a log cabin through the Civil War and assassination. The theatrical presentations in the Journeys galleries bring major episodes to life with a combination of life-size environments, artefacts, and audiovisual effects.

The adjacent presidential library is the research heart of the institution, holding millions of documents, photographs, and artefacts related to Lincoln and Illinois history. The library is open to scholars and the public for research; a portion of the collections is on display in the museum. Plan to spend at least three hours; the museum alone takes two to three hours to do properly. Admission is by timed entry; book online in advance during peak season.
