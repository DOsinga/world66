story: "Clyde Tombaugh was born on a farm just outside Streator in 1906 and grew up grinding his own telescope mirrors on the kitchen table. He was working as a young observer at Lowell Observatory in Arizona when, on 18 February 1930, he spotted a faint moving dot on a pair of photographic plates — Pluto. After Pluto was demoted to dwarf-planet status in 2006, Streator's marker stayed up, defiantly listing him as the discoverer of the ninth planet."
---

A small bronze marker downtown commemorates Clyde Tombaugh, the Streator-born astronomer who discovered Pluto in 1930 at the age of 24. The plaque is easy to miss — it sits on a brick plinth near City Park — but it is the closest thing the town has to a tourist pilgrimage site for anyone with an interest in the history of astronomy.

The Streator Public Library keeps a small display of Tombaugh memorabilia and books, and the local astronomy club occasionally runs star parties timed to coincide with the anniversary of the discovery in February.
