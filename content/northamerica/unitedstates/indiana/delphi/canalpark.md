A small living-history park on the northern edge of town, built around a restored section of the Wabash and Erie Canal — once the longest canal in the United States. The park includes a working replica of a wooden canal boat (the **Delphi**) that offers short towpath-drawn trips at weekends in summer, a reconstructed canal lock, an interpretive centre with exhibits on the canal's brief economic boom and rapid collapse, and a network of walking paths along the original towpath.

The park is at its best on a sunny weekend in spring or early autumn, when the boat is running and the volunteers in period dress give talks. Admission is by donation. Walking the paths is free at any time, year-round. A pleasant low-key half-day, particularly with children.
