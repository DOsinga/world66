Evansville is the third-largest city in Indiana, sitting on the Ohio River in the south-western corner of the state near the borders with Kentucky and Illinois. The river gave the city its early importance as a shipping and manufacturing hub, and the [Ohio River Waterfront](/northamerica/unitedstates/indiana/evansville/ohio_river_waterfront) remains one of its more attractive features, with the restored [McCurdy Hotel](/northamerica/unitedstates/indiana/evansville/mccurdy_hotel) anchoring the riverfront historic district.

The [Evansville Museum of Arts, History and Science](/northamerica/unitedstates/indiana/evansville/evansville_museum) is the city's main cultural institution, with a broad collection ranging from fine art to a planetarium. The [Angel Mounds State Historic Site](/northamerica/unitedstates/indiana/evansville/angel_mounds), on the eastern edge of the city, preserves the remains of a large pre-Columbian Mississippian settlement and is one of the best-preserved Native American sites in the Midwest.

The city has a compact, walkable downtown with some good Victorian architecture, including the Art Deco [Hulman Building](/northamerica/unitedstates/indiana/evansville/hulman_building), the opulent [Reitz Home Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/indiana/evansville/reitz_home_museum), and the 1938 [Greyhound Bus Terminal](/northamerica/unitedstates/indiana/evansville/greyhound_bus_terminal) — a Streamline Moderne gem. The [Haynie's Corner Arts District](/northamerica/unitedstates/indiana/evansville/haynies_corner) is the best area for independent restaurants and bars. Outside the downtown, [Wesselman Woods](/northamerica/unitedstates/indiana/evansville/wesselman_woods) is one of the last old-growth urban forests in the country, and [Bosse Field](/northamerica/unitedstates/indiana/evansville/bosse_field) — the 1915 baseball stadium where "A League of Their Own" was filmed — is worth an evening visit when the Otters are playing.
