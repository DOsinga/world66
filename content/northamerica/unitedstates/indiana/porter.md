Porter is a small town on the southern shore of Lake Michigan, about 50 miles southeast of Chicago. Its main claim to interest is its location inside [Indiana Dunes National Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/indiana/porter/indiana_dunes_national_park), which stretches for 25 miles along the lakeshore.

The dunes are the draw. Miles of sandy beach, forested dunes up to 60 metres high, bogs, wetlands, and oak savanna create a surprising diversity of landscape within sight of the Chicago skyline on a clear day. The park ranks eighth among US national parks for plant diversity — over 1,400 species have been recorded, including some that are threatened or endangered in Indiana.

The Dunes Succession Trail near Porter gives a good introduction to the ecology that made this area famous: Dr. Henry Cowles conducted his pioneering studies on ecological succession here in the early 1900s, essentially inventing the field. Beyond the beach, the [Bailly Homestead and Chellberg Farm](/northamerica/unitedstates/indiana/porter/bailly_homestead_and_chellberg_farm) preserve the area's pre-industrial history, and the strange collection of [Century of Progress Homes](/northamerica/unitedstates/indiana/porter/century_of_progress_homes) — experimental houses from the 1933 Chicago World's Fair — sit just along the shore. For the most dramatic dune experience, [Mount Baldy](/northamerica/unitedstates/indiana/porter/mount_baldy) requires a ranger-led tour but is worth it.

Porter is easily reached by train from Chicago on the South Shore Line, making the dunes a practical day trip from the city.
