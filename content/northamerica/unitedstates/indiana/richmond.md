Richmond is a small city in eastern Indiana, the county seat of Wayne County, founded by Quakers from North Carolina who settled along the [Whitewater Gorge](/northamerica/unitedstates/indiana/richmond/whitewater_gorge) in the early nineteenth century. It sits on the old National Road (US 40), the first federally funded highway, which brought a steady flow of pioneers heading west.

The town has two genuinely notable claims to fame. The [Levi Coffin House](/northamerica/unitedstates/indiana/richmond/levi_coffin_house) in nearby Fountain City was the most important station on the Underground Railroad — Coffin and his wife are estimated to have helped over 2,000 escaped slaves reach freedom. And Gennett Records, a studio in the Whitewater Gorge, was where some of the earliest jazz recordings were made in the 1920s — Louis Armstrong, Bix Beiderbecke, and Hoagy Carmichael all recorded here. The [Gennett Walk of Fame](/northamerica/unitedstates/indiana/richmond/gennett_walk_of_fame) marks the site.

Richmond also has a surprisingly good pair of museums. The [Wayne County Historical Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/indiana/richmond/wayne_county_historical_museum) holds pioneer artefacts, an Egyptian mummy, and locally manufactured automobiles. The [Richmond Art Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/indiana/richmond/richmond_art_museum) has a strong collection of American Impressionists and the Hoosier Group of Indiana painters. The Whitewater Gorge Trail and Cardinal Greenway offer pleasant walking and cycling. [Earlham College](/northamerica/unitedstates/indiana/richmond/earlham_college), the Quaker liberal arts college on the west side of the city, is worth a walk through the campus.
