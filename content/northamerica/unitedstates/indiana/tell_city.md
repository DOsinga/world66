Tell City sits on the Ohio River in southern [Indiana](/northamerica/unitedstates/indiana), the largest town in Perry County. It was founded in 1857 by a Swiss colonisation society and named after the legendary William Tell — the [Swiss heritage](/northamerica/unitedstates/indiana/tell_city/swiss_heritage_district) is still visible in the town's layout and architecture, and the [Tell City Pretzel Co.](/northamerica/unitedstates/indiana/tell_city/tell_city_pretzels) has been baking the same hard, hand-twisted pretzels by the same Swiss family recipe since 1858.

The river setting is the main attraction. The Ohio bends dramatically here, and the forested hills of the [Hoosier National Forest](/northamerica/unitedstates/indiana/tell_city/hoosier_national_forest) rise just to the north. It is a quiet place, good for a stop on a drive along the river or as a base for exploring the surrounding hill country. Ten minutes downriver in Cannelton, the cathedral-like [Cannelton Cotton Mill](/northamerica/unitedstates/indiana/tell_city/cannelton_cotton_mill) is the largest surviving pre-Civil War industrial building in the American Midwest.
