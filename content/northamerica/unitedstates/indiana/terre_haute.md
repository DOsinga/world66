Terre Haute is a west-central Indiana city on the Wabash River, about an hour southwest of [Indianapolis](/northamerica/unitedstates/indiana/indianapolis). Its name means "high land" in French, a reminder of early river geography and the old settlement routes that crossed this part of Indiana.

The city has a worn Midwestern texture: rail lines, brick downtown blocks, college campuses, and pieces of industry that have outlived their original use. The reason to stop is not one grand landmark but a cluster of useful sights. The [Eugene V. Debs House](/northamerica/unitedstates/indiana/terre_haute/eugene_v_debs_house) is the strongest, preserving the home of one of America's most important labor leaders. Downtown also has the [Swope Art Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/indiana/terre_haute/swope_art_museum), while Saint Mary-of-the-Woods adds pilgrimage history and quiet grounds northwest of the city.

Terre Haute is best for travelers already crossing Indiana on I-70 or US 40, not as a long detour. Give it a day if you like labor history, small American museums, college towns, and cities that show their scars rather than smoothing them over.
