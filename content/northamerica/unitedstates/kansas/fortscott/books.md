**A Choice of Weapons** by Gordon Parks (1966). Parks' memoir opens with his boyhood in Fort Scott, recounting the poverty, segregation, and violence he witnessed growing up in 1920s Kansas. The book is a classic of American autobiography, and the Fort Scott chapters give the town a literary dimension that most small Kansas cities lack. Indispensable reading before or after a visit.

**The Learning Tree** by Gordon Parks (1963). A semi-autobiographical novel set in Cherokee Flats, Kansas (clearly based on Fort Scott), following a young Black boy coming of age in a racially divided small town. Parks later adapted it into a film, which became the first Hollywood studio film directed by a Black director.

**Bleeding Kansas** by Nicole Etcheson (2004). The best scholarly account of the violent struggle over whether Kansas would enter the Union as a free or slave state. Fort Scott and its surrounding county were at the epicentre of these conflicts, and Etcheson's research makes sense of a chaotic period that is easy to misread.
