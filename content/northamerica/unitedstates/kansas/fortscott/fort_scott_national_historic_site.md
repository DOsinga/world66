story: >
  Fort Scott was established in 1842 as one of a chain of frontier posts along the "Permanent Indian Frontier" — a line the US government had guaranteed would never be settled by white Americans. Within a decade that promise had collapsed, and the fort became a staging point for the very westward expansion it was supposed to limit. During Bleeding Kansas in the 1856–1859 period, the fort changed hands multiple times between pro-slavery and Free State factions, each claiming it as a base of operations.
---

Fort Scott National Historic Site preserves the original 1840s military post, which served successively as a frontier garrison, a base during the Bleeding Kansas crisis, and a Union supply depot during the Civil War. The 20 restored historic structures — barracks, officers' quarters, hospital, dragoon stables — are arranged around a parade ground and furnished to their 1840s appearance.

The site tells the story of a post that witnessed several of the major conflicts that shaped 19th-century America. The first Black infantry regiment, the 1st Kansas Colored Infantry, was mustered here in 1862 — among the first Black soldiers officially enlisted in the Union Army. Their service began from this parade ground and eventually included action at the Battle of Honey Springs. The interpretation is honest about this history, and the combination of reconstructed frontier buildings and genuine historical weight makes Fort Scott one of the more substantial small-city historic sites in the Midwest.
