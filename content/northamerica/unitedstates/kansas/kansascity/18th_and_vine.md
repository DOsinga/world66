The 18th and Vine neighbourhood is the birthplace and spiritual home of Kansas City jazz. In the 1920s and 30s it was one of the most concentrated African American cultural districts in America — over 100 jazz clubs within a few blocks, anchored by the musicians' union hall and the clubs where Charlie Parker, Count Basie, and Lester Young all cut their teeth.

Today the neighbourhood is quieter, but the American Jazz Museum, the Negro Leagues Baseball Museum, and the Mutual Musicians Foundation give it genuine historical substance. The Blue Room jazz club hosts live music on weekends. The 18th and Vine intersection itself is marked with bronze plaques — you can literally stand at the corner and read the history.
