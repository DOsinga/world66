Tallgrass Prairie National Preserve is where the Flint Hills still look like the old middle of North America: rolling grass, limestone ridges, big skies, and wind that never seems to clock out. Less than one percent of the continent's original tallgrass prairie survives, and this preserve protects one of the best remaining pieces.

Start at the [visitor center](/northamerica/unitedstates/kansas/tallgrassprairie/visitor_center), then walk up to the [Spring Hill Ranch](/northamerica/unitedstates/kansas/tallgrassprairie/spring_hill_ranch) and its great limestone barn. The [Southwind Nature Trail](/northamerica/unitedstates/kansas/tallgrassprairie/southwind_nature_trail) is the easiest way to feel the prairie underfoot, while the [bison pasture](/northamerica/unitedstates/kansas/tallgrassprairie/bison_pasture) gives the place a living sense of what was almost lost.

This is not a theme-park landscape. It is quiet, exposed, and weather-driven. Spring can bring wildflowers and prescribed burns; summer is green and hot; fall is when the grasses stand highest and glow bronze in the late light. Bring water, check trail closures before you set out, and give bison far more room than you think they need.
