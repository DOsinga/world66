The Evel Knievel Museum at Topeka's Harley-Davidson dealership is more interesting than it sounds. The collection includes several of the original Harley-Davidson XR-750 motorcycles used in Knievel's most famous jumps, the Skycycle X-2 rocket that he rode in his failed attempt to jump the Snake River Canyon in 1974, leathers and capes from various performances, and extensive documentation of his career and cultural footprint.

The exhibits contextualise Knievel not just as a stuntman but as a media phenomenon — someone who understood television spectacle before the age of reality TV and built a persona that transcended sport. For anyone who grew up watching him on ABC's Wide World of Sports, or simply finds the audacity of his career interesting, this is a better-than-expected two hours. Admission is paid; the dealership is worth a look even if the museum doesn't appeal.
