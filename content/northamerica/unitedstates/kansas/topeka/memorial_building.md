story: The Memorial Building was constructed in 1914 as Kansas's memorial to the soldiers of the Civil War and later conflicts. It houses the Kansas Historical Society's collections alongside a 2,700-seat auditorium. The building's rotunda contains a mosaic floor, murals, and bronze tablets listing Kansas soldiers who died in the Civil War — one of the more sombre and dignified Civil War memorials in a state that, as a Free State, lost proportionally large numbers of men to the conflict.

The Memorial Building at 120 SW Tenth Avenue sits diagonally from the State Capitol and serves as both a memorial and a public event space. The architecture is Beaux-Arts, with a prominent dome and formal facade that make it one of the better-looking civic buildings in central Topeka. The interior is worth a visit for the rotunda and the historical displays maintained by the Kansas Historical Society.

The building's auditorium has hosted political conventions, concerts, and public events since its opening. The combination of memorial function and practical civic use gives it a less solemn atmosphere than a dedicated monument — it remains in active use and feels lived-in. The adjacent grounds contain additional memorials and are part of the civic campus around the Capitol.
