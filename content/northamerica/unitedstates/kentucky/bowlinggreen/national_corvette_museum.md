America's most dedicated shrine to a single car model. The National Corvette Museum traces the Corvette's history from its 1953 debut through every generation, with over 80 vehicles on display at any time, including concept cars, race prototypes, and personal cars of famous owners. The rotating collection is genuinely interesting even for non-gearheads; the design evolution across seven decades tells a story about American taste and engineering ambition.

The museum sits directly across the highway from the General Motors Corvette Assembly Plant, the only place where Corvettes are made. Factory tours run on weekday mornings and must be booked in advance — they fill up quickly, especially in summer. Plan to spend a full morning: tour the factory first, then cross to the museum for the afternoon.
