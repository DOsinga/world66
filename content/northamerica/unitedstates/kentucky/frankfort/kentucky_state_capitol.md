  The capitol's First Lady Doll Collection is stranger than it sounds: 36 hand-dressed mannequins representing each First Lady of the Commonwealth, arranged in a parlour-like display on the first floor. It was started in 1965 by the wife of Governor Edward Breathitt and has been added to ever since, becoming one of the stranger exhibits in any American capitol building.

Kentucky's current state capitol, completed in 1910, is one of the finest Beaux-Arts public buildings in the American South. Designed by Frank Mills Andrews, it sits on a rise south of the Kentucky River with broad limestone steps and a prominent dome visible from most of the city. The interior is equally grand — the staircases replicate those of the Paris Opéra Garnier, and the rotunda is lined with marble and bronze statues of Kentucky's most celebrated citizens, including Abraham Lincoln, Henry Clay, and Alben Barkley.

Note: as of August 2025, the building has been closed for major renovations estimated to last until 2029. The exterior and grounds remain accessible.
