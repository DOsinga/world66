Maysville is a small Kentucky river town with more texture than its size suggests. It sits on the Ohio River northeast of Lexington, facing Aberdeen, Ohio, and its brick downtown still reads as a 19th-century port rather than a generic highway stop.

The appeal is history at walking speed. The riverfront, the [Simon Kenton Memorial Bridge](/northamerica/unitedstates/kentucky/maysville/simon_kenton_memorial_bridge), the restored [Russell Theatre](/northamerica/unitedstates/kentucky/maysville/russell_theatre), and the local museums give Maysville a compact sense of place. The [National Underground Railroad Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/kentucky/maysville/national_underground_railroad_museum) is the serious stop; it connects the town's Ohio River setting to the lives of people escaping slavery.

Old Washington, now part of Maysville, adds an earlier frontier layer a few miles inland. Bourbon is part of the story too: [Old Pogue Distillery](/northamerica/unitedstates/kentucky/maysville/old_pogue_distillery) keeps the local whiskey tradition alive on a hill above town. Maysville is not a big destination, but if you are travelling through northern Kentucky or following the Ohio River, it is worth the detour.
