Paducah sits at the meeting of the Ohio and Tennessee rivers in far western Kentucky. It is a working river town with a surprisingly strong arts identity, recognised by UNESCO as a Creative City of Crafts and Folk Art.

The headline stop is the [National Quilt Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/kentucky/paducah/national_quilt_museum), which treats quilts as serious contemporary art rather than domestic craft. A few blocks away, the [Paducah Wall to Wall Floodwall Murals](/northamerica/unitedstates/kentucky/paducah/paducah_wall_to_wall_floodwall_murals) turn the river barrier into a long painted local history, and [Lower Town](/northamerica/unitedstates/kentucky/paducah/lowertown_arts_district) has galleries and old houses that show how deeply the arts push has shaped the city.

Paducah is compact enough to see on foot if you stay around downtown and the riverfront. It is not Louisville or Nashville, and it does not need to be. The pleasure is in finding a small city that has picked a cultural lane and committed to it.
