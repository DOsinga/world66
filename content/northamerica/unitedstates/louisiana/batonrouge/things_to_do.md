---
title: "Things to Do"
type: section
---

Baton Rouge's main sights cluster around the capitol area and the riverfront. The twin capitol buildings — one Art Deco, one Gothic Revival — are the city's most distinctive landmarks and both are open to visitors.

The **Capitol Park Museum** at the foot of the New State Capitol covers Louisiana history from Native American cultures to the offshore oil era, with a full-size flatboat replica and vintage Mardi Gras floats. The **USS Kidd**, a World War II destroyer moored on the riverfront, is one of the few such ships in the country open for full deck-to-engine-room exploration.

The **Old Louisiana Governor's Mansion** and the **Pentagon Barracks** — both a short walk from the New State Capitol — round out the civic history of the bluff above the river. The **Shaw Center for the Arts** is the main performing arts venue and houses the LSU Museum of Art.

On the LSU campus, the **Rural Life Museum** and the grounds of **Magnolia Mound Plantation** are worth a half-day for anyone interested in Louisiana's plantation-era history and Creole architectural tradition. The **LSU Campus Mounds**, two 11,000-year-old earthworks tucked between the campus lakes, are easily missed but remarkable. **Tiger Stadium** — Death Valley — is open for tours on non-game days; attending a game in autumn is the definitive Baton Rouge experience.
