Augusta has been Maine's capital since 1827, seven years after statehood — and it has the feel of a small New England city that takes its civic role seriously without making a fuss about it. The population hovers around 18,000, and the pace is unhurried. Government is the main business here, and the granite [Capitol dome](/northamerica/unitedstates/maine/augusta/maine_state_capitol) designed by Charles Bulfinch anchors a compact downtown that can be walked in an afternoon.

The Kennebec River is the city's backbone. [Fort Western](/northamerica/unitedstates/maine/augusta/fort_western), built in 1754 to protect English settlers during the French and Indian War, stands on the east bank and is the oldest surviving wooden fort in New England — a genuinely interesting place to spend an hour, especially if you catch one of the living-history demonstrations. The riverfront has been cleaned up in recent decades, and the [Kennebec River Rail Trail](/northamerica/unitedstates/maine/augusta/kennebec_river_rail_trail) runs south from Augusta through [Hallowell](/northamerica/unitedstates/maine/hallowell) and Gardiner, following the water with good views along the way. Bring a bike.

The cluster of state buildings along State Street repays a slow walk. [Blaine House](/northamerica/unitedstates/maine/augusta/blaine_house), the governor's mansion since 1919, sits a short distance from the Capitol and is open for weekday morning tours. The [Maine State Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/maine/augusta/maine_state_museum), directly across the parking lot from the Capitol, is better than you might expect — its exhibits on 19th-century industry and Maine's native peoples include a working three-level mill model and well-crafted wildlife dioramas. Admission is modest.

West of downtown, the [Pine Tree State Arboretum](/northamerica/unitedstates/maine/augusta/pine_tree_state_arboretum) covers 224 acres of meadow and woodland with free year-round access. Fall color here is excellent. [Belgrade Lakes](/northamerica/unitedstates/maine/belgrade_lakes), a string of lakes popular with canoeists and anglers, is a short drive northwest and makes a good half-day trip.
