Bangor was the 19th-century lumber capital of the world, and the town hasn't forgotten it — a 31-foot [Paul Bunyan statue](/northamerica/unitedstates/maine/bangor/paul_bunyan_statue) stands on Main Street, staking Maine's claim as the lumberjack's true birthplace (sorry, Minnesota). Today Bangor is the commercial center of northern Maine and the last sizable city before the wilderness begins.

[Stephen King](/northamerica/unitedstates/maine/bangor/stephen_kings_house) lives here, and fans will recognize the town's Victorian architecture from his novels — the real Bangor is the basis for the fictional town of Derry. The [Thomas Hill Standpipe](/northamerica/unitedstates/maine/bangor/thomas_hill_standpipe) appears in *It*, [Mount Hope Cemetery](/northamerica/unitedstates/maine/bangor/mount_hope_cemetery) featured in the *Pet Sematary* film, and walking tours connect the dots between King's fiction and Bangor's streets.

The [waterfront](/northamerica/unitedstates/maine/bangor/bangor_waterfront) along the Penobscot River has been redeveloped with a walking trail and summer concert venue. The [Zillman Art Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/maine/bangor/zillman_art_museum) in the city center holds a serious collection including works by Warhol, Hockney, Hopper, and the Maine modernist Marsden Hartley. For those with children, the [Maine Discovery Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/maine/bangor/maine_discovery_museum) is one of the better children's museums in New England.

Just outside the city, the [Orono Bog Boardwalk](/northamerica/unitedstates/maine/bangor/orono_bog_boardwalk) offers an easy walk through a remarkable peatland ecosystem, and the [Bangor City Forest](/northamerica/unitedstates/maine/bangor/city_forest) has 680 acres of trails within the city limits. [Acadia National Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/maine/bangor/acadia_national_park_day_trip) is 90 minutes east — close enough for a long day trip.
