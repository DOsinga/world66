The Rufus Porter Museum is the best surprise in Bridgton. Porter (1792–1884) was a western Maine native who crammed several remarkable careers into one very long life: itinerant muralist, inventor, journalist, and founder of *Scientific American*. The museum occupies a pair of historic buildings in the center of town and celebrates this chronically underestimated figure with rotating exhibits on both his art and his inventions.

The murals are the highlight. Between 1825 and 1845 Porter painted approximately 160 houses and inns across New England, covering walls with landscapes, harbor scenes, and idyllic townscapes in his distinctive freehand style. He used stencils for foliage and a cork stopper to stamp leaves — efficient techniques that let him complete a room in a day. Several surviving murals have been documented, and the museum holds strong materials on his technique and the buildings where his work can still be found.

The museum is open seasonally; check ahead before visiting in shoulder months.
