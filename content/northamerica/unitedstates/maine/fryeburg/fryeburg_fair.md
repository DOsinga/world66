story: >
  The Fryeburg Fair has been running every October since 1851, and it has not lost its footing as the biggest agricultural fair in New England. It draws upward of 300,000 visitors in a single week -- for livestock competitions, harness racing, ox pulls, and carnival food that arrives once a year and is better for it.
---

The fairgrounds sit just north of the village center and transform completely each fall. The week-long event fills with working farmers showing cattle, sheep, and draft horses alongside a midway, grandstand acts, and more fried dough than any single region should be able to sustain. The ox pull is a particular highlight -- teams of oxen hauling increasingly impossible loads while the crowd gets louder and louder.

Timing matters here. The fair runs the first full week of October, which typically aligns with peak fall foliage in western Maine. The combination of leaf color on the surrounding hills and the smell of wood smoke and frying fat from the fairgrounds is genuinely hard to beat. Come on a weekday if you can; weekends are extremely crowded.
