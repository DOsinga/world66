---
title: "Things to Do in Kennebunkport"
type: section
---

Kennebunkport's appeal is coastal New England at its most accessible: good beaches, a compact harbour district, and a landscape of white clapboard houses and boat-filled inlets. [Dock Square](/northamerica/unitedstates/maine/kennebunkport/dock_square) is the commercial hub — a cluster of shops, galleries, and restaurants around a tidal cove. It's pleasant to walk through, particularly on a weekday morning before the summer crowds arrive.

The beaches are the main attraction in season. [Goose Rocks Beach](/northamerica/unitedstates/maine/kennebunkport/goose_rocks_beach), on the north shore of the cape, is the most beautiful and the least crowded. [Kennebunk Beach](/northamerica/unitedstates/maine/kennebunkport/kennebunk_beach), on the western shore, is more accessible and has good surf. [Cape Porpoise](/northamerica/unitedstates/maine/kennebunkport/cape_porpoise), a tiny fishing village a few kilometres east, is worth the detour — fewer tourists, a working harbour, and excellent lobster.

[Walker's Point](/northamerica/unitedstates/maine/kennebunkport/walkers_point) — the Bush family compound visible from Ocean Avenue — is an unavoidable curiosity; boats cruise past it in summer. The [Seashore Trolley Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/maine/kennebunkport/seashore_trolley_museum) outside town is the world's largest collection of streetcars, an unexpectedly engaging place for a few hours. The offshore [Goat Island Light](/northamerica/unitedstates/maine/kennebunkport/goat_island_light) is reachable by kayak and visible from Cape Porpoise harbour.
