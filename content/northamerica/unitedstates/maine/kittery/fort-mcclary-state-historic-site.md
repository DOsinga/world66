Fort McClary sits on a narrow point of land at Kittery Point, jutting into the mouth of the Piscataqua River. The hexagonal log blockhouse is the most photographed structure in Kittery — and rightly so. Built in 1844 on the site of much older earthworks, it is the last remaining fortification from a system of defenses that protected this stretch of coast from the colonial period through the Civil War. The grounds also contain the remains of earthworks and the ruins of earlier structures, making it a layered site rather than a single-period exhibit.

The state park grounds are open year-round, though the blockhouse itself is only accessible in season. Even off-season the walk around the point is worthwhile — the views across to New Castle Island and back toward the shipyard are excellent, and the area is quiet enough that you can actually think. A small parking area sits just off Route 103. This is Kittery outside the outlet corridor, and it is a different town entirely.
