story: "Fort McClary has been in almost continuous military use from 1715, when Massachusetts colonists first built a blockhouse here to defend against the French and their Native American allies, through to the Second World War, when it served as an observation post. It was named after Major Andrew McClary, the highest-ranking American officer killed at the Battle of Bunker Hill in 1775. The hexagonal blockhouse that survives today dates from 1844 and is one of the best-preserved examples of its kind in New England."
---
Fort McClary sits on a rocky promontory at Kittery Point, commanding views across the Piscataqua River estuary towards Portsmouth Harbor and the Atlantic beyond. The fort's most prominent feature is its hexagonal wooden blockhouse, dating to 1844 and still standing in excellent condition within a small state historic site.

The grounds are pleasant for a walk, with picnic tables and views that explain immediately why this spot was fortified for over two centuries. A small museum building explains the fort's history. It is a short drive from the outlet strip along the winding road out to Kittery Point, and a good reason to venture beyond the highway.
