The Kittery Historical and Naval Museum is a small, volunteer-run institution tucked just off Route 1, easy to miss if you are only here for the outlets. The collection focuses on the town's long relationship with the sea and the naval yard: ship models, navigational instruments, historical photographs of the shipyard during its wartime production peak, and documents tracing Kittery's role as Maine's oldest incorporated town (1647). It is not a large collection, but the curation is careful and the staff know the material well.

Worth visiting if you have any interest in the area's history beyond the shopping corridor. The museum covers the full span from colonial-era fishing and shipbuilding through the Treaty of Portsmouth in 1905 — which was negotiated at the naval yard and ended the Russo-Japanese War — and into the twentieth century submarine era. Open seasonally; check hours before going, as it runs on limited staffing.
