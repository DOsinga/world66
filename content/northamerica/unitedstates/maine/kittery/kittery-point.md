Kittery Point is the narrow peninsula that juts southeast from the town center toward the open ocean. Route 103 follows its spine, passing through some of the oldest continuously inhabited landscape in Maine. The houses along the road are a mix of colonial-era sea captains' homes and later summer estates, many of them sitting close to the road with granite outcroppings and tidal coves on either side. This is what the rest of Kittery looked like before Route 1 arrived.

The outer tip of the peninsula looks across to the Isles of Shoals, a cluster of small islands about ten miles offshore. Smuttynose Island, visible on clear days, was the site of an 1873 double murder that became one of the most written-about crimes of the nineteenth century — Celia Thaxter's account and, later, Anita Shreve's novel The Weight of Water both drew on it. From shore you cannot tell the islands apart at that distance, but knowing the history gives the view a different weight. The road ends near Fort McClary; the whole drive out and back takes less than thirty minutes and is worth every one of them.
