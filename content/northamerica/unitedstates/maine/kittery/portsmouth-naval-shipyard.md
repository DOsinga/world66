Established in 1800, Portsmouth Naval Shipyard is the oldest continuously operating naval shipyard in the United States, though the site's military use goes back even further — the Continental Navy built ships here during the Revolution. During World War II the yard became one of the most productive submarine-building facilities in the country, launching over 70 submarines between 1941 and 1945. Workers came from across New England, and Kittery's population roughly doubled during the war years.

The shipyard remains an active military installation today, focused on submarine maintenance and overhaul. You cannot tour the facility, but you can see it clearly from the waterfront at Kittery Point or from the Memorial Bridge connecting Kittery to Portsmouth. The massive dry docks and the submarine berths along the river are visible from multiple vantage points, a reminder that this is a working yard, not a museum piece.
