Portsmouth Naval Shipyard is the oldest continuously operating naval shipyard in the United States, established in 1800 on an island in the Piscataqua River. Despite its name, it lies entirely within Kittery, Maine — a geographical quirk that has puzzled visitors for over two centuries. The shipyard is an active military installation and not open to general visitors, but it is visible from the Kittery shore and from the Memorial Bridge.

The shipyard's history spans the full range of American naval warfare, from wooden frigates through the age of submarines — it has specialised in submarine construction and repair since the First World War. The Treaty of Portsmouth, which ended the Russo-Japanese War in 1905 and earned Theodore Roosevelt the Nobel Peace Prize, was signed in the shipyard's Building 86.
