The William Whipple House is one of Kittery's more significant historic structures, associated with a signer of the Declaration of Independence who was born here in 1730. Whipple went on to become a successful merchant, a brigadier general in the Continental Army, and one of three men who signed the Declaration on behalf of New Hampshire. The house reflects the prosperous maritime character of colonial Kittery, when the town's shipyards and merchants were among the most active in the region.

The structure is a private property and not regularly open for interior tours, but the exterior is accessible on the street and forms part of any walking tour of Kittery's older residential areas. Kittery Point Road has several other colonial-era houses in the vicinity, making this a worthwhile stretch for anyone interested in New England's built history rather than just the outlet shopping.
