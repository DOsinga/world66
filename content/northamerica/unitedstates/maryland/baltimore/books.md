Baltimore has inspired some distinctly local literature — the city's gritty, idiosyncratic character has proven more productive for writers than its reputation might suggest.

**Baltimore Blues** — Laura Lippman's debut novel introduced private detective Tess Monaghan and launched a series set entirely in Baltimore's neighbourhoods, bars, and newsrooms. Lippman grew up in Baltimore and worked at the Sun alongside David Simon; her fiction captures the city's geography, its class tensions, and its specific flavour of mid-Atlantic urban life with the accuracy of someone who walks those streets. Any of the Monaghan novels work as Baltimore companions.

**Shock Value: A Tasteful Book About Bad Taste** — John Waters's memoir covers his early life making underground films in Baltimore in the 1960s and 1970s, and is essentially a love letter to the city's strangeness. Waters grew up in Baltimore's suburbs, chose to stay when he could easily have left, and has spent decades documenting and celebrating the city's peculiar working-class aesthetic. Reading Waters explains Hampden, explains the city's relationship with kitsch, and explains why Baltimore has never been absorbed into the generic American urban character.

**Narrative of the Life of Frederick Douglass, an American Slave** — Douglass's 1845 autobiography is one of the most important American books of the 19th century, and a substantial part of it is set in Baltimore. His descriptions of working in the Fells Point shipyards, of teaching himself to read, and of the city's complex position as a slave-holding urban centre with a large free Black population give Baltimore a literary presence it is rarely given credit for. Essential before visiting the city.

**The Corner: A Year in the Life of an Inner-City Neighbourhood** — David Simon's follow-up to his earlier journalism about the Baltimore police department is a full-year immersion in a single corner of the West Baltimore drug trade. Simon and Burns lived in and reported on the neighbourhood for a year, and the result is one of the most unsparing accounts of American urban poverty and the failure of the war on drugs ever written. It later became the basis for the TV series *The Corner* and informed *The Wire*. Reading it before or after visiting Baltimore's west side is transformative.
