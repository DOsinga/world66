  The Women's Industrial Exchange was founded in 1880 as a charity to provide income
  for "genteel women in reduced circumstances" — widows and single women of the
  middle class who had fallen on hard times but whose social standing prohibited
  them from taking ordinary employment. They made handicrafts and food for sale in
  the Exchange, which operated as a tearoom and shop on Charles Street. The tearoom
  survived into the 21st century as the oldest continuously operating restaurant in
  Baltimore before closing; it has since reopened under new management.

The Women's Industrial Exchange on Charles Street in Mount Vernon is a historic institution that operated as a tearoom for over a century, serving the kind of simple, comforting food — cream of crab soup, chicken salad, tomato aspic — that once defined Baltimore's middle-class lunch culture. It is now reopened under new management and maintains a connection to its historic menu.

Whether you find it charmingly anachronistic or genuinely wonderful depends on your relationship with cream soups and silver service. The space itself is lovely — a Victorian commercial interior that has survived largely intact. Worth visiting for the atmosphere as much as the food.
