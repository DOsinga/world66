story: >
  In its 1840s heyday the Institute was one of the most fashionable girls' schools in the South — the daughters of plantation owners came here to study Latin, music, and natural philosophy in a Greek Revival temple high above the mill town. After the Civil War destroyed its market, the building drifted through stints as a hotel, a hospital for Civil War veterans, a nursing home, and finally a roofless ruin. The county stabilised the granite walls in the 1990s rather than rebuilding, leaving the columns standing under the open sky.

Above the historic district, on a windy ridge that overlooks the gorge, the gutted shell of the Patapsco Female Institute stands surrounded by lawn. The site is now a county park: you can walk through the open colonnade, climb the steps where the central hall used to be, and look down on the river and the railroad station from where the parlours used to face. There is no museum and almost no signage — the ruin is left to speak for itself.

The grounds are open daily; the interior is sometimes used for ghost tours and outdoor theatre in summer. Bring sun protection — the hill is exposed.
