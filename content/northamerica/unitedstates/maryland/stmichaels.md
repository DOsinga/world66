St. Michaels is a small, well-preserved harbour town on [Maryland](/northamerica/unitedstates/maryland)'s Eastern Shore, sitting between the Miles River and Broad Creek on the Chesapeake Bay. It dates from the colonial era and still has the feel of a working waterfront town, with crabbers and oystermen going out in the morning and tourists filling the restaurants by evening.

The [Chesapeake Bay Maritime Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/maryland/stmichaels/chesapeake_bay_maritime_museum), spread across 18 acres on the harbour, is the main attraction — a collection of historic boats, a working boatyard, and exhibits on the bay's ecology and waterman culture. The [St. Michaels Historic District](/northamerica/unitedstates/maryland/stmichaels/historic_district) is a pleasant walk: handsome colonial, Federal, and Victorian houses line the streets, including the [Cannonball House](/northamerica/unitedstates/maryland/stmichaels/cannonball_house), a landmark from the War of 1812. Shops run to antiques, art galleries, and specialty food stores.

The seafood, fresh from the bay, is the main reason to eat here — crab cakes, oysters, and rockfish are the staples. The [Crab Claw Restaurant](/northamerica/unitedstates/maryland/stmichaels/crab_claw_restaurant) right on the harbour is the most famous spot, though lines in summer can be long. St. Michaels is about 75 miles from [Washington, D.C.](/northamerica/unitedstates/washingtondc) and [Baltimore](/northamerica/unitedstates/maryland/baltimore), making it a popular weekend escape. Nearby [Tilghman Island](/northamerica/unitedstates/maryland/tilghman_island) and Oxford are worth a half-day side trip.
