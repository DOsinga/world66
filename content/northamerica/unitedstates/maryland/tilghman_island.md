Tilghman Island is a small working watermen's village on Maryland's Eastern Shore, separated from the mainland by Knapps Narrows and reached by drawbridge. It's genuinely unspoiled — no resort sprawl, no chain restaurants, just crab boats, skipjacks, and the wide Chesapeake sky.

The island is home to the last commercial sailing fleet in North America. The skipjacks, traditional Chesapeake oyster dredgers, can be seen at [Dogwood Harbor](/northamerica/unitedstates/maryland/tilghman_island/dogwood_harbor) and some still work the bay in season. The oldest, the [*Rebecca T. Ruark*](/northamerica/unitedstates/maryland/tilghman_island/rebecca_t_ruark), built in 1886, offers public sailing trips on the bay. It's a rare place where the old way of life has survived intact, not been staged for tourists.

At the island's southern tip, [Black Walnut Point](/northamerica/unitedstates/maryland/tilghman_island/black_walnut_point) is a quiet nature reserve with osprey nests and views across the open water. Tilghman sits a short drive from St. Michaels, Oxford, and Easton, making it a natural stopping point on a broader Eastern Shore loop. If you're cruising the Chesapeake Bay, it's a worthy port of call. Come for the fresh seafood, the quiet, and the feeling that the rest of the world hasn't quite caught up yet.
