story: >
  Each spring the spotted salamanders of North Amherst migrate from the upland forest to the vernal pools across Henry Street to breed. Before 1987 most were flattened by cars. After a sustained local campaign, the town installed two concrete tunnels under the road — among the very first amphibian tunnels in North America. Volunteers still patrol the crossing on rainy March nights.
---

A pair of low concrete amphibian tunnels under Henry Street in North Amherst, marked by small signs. On any given day they are not much to look at. On a rainy night in late March or early April they are the scene of one of the strangest sights in Massachusetts: dozens of yellow-spotted black salamanders crawling across the road and dropping into the tunnel mouths.

The annual Big Night, when the migration peaks, is organised by the **Hitchcock Center for the Environment** with volunteer crossing guards. Bring a torch and warm waterproofs. Outside spring, the site is more of a curiosity than a destination.
