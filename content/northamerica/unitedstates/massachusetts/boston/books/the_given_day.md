Lehane's most ambitious novel covers a single year — 1919 — in Boston, when the city was hit by the Spanish flu, a molasses flood, a race riot, and the famous police strike that ended the careers of the entire force. Two parallel lives run through the book: Danny Coughlin, an Irish cop wrestling with whether to join the strike, and Luther Laurence, a Black domestic worker fleeing trouble in Tulsa who finds his own kind of trouble in Boston.

Babe Ruth appears throughout in extended interludes, in his last year with the Red Sox before the sale to the Yankees. It is a sweeping historical novel of a city that was visibly remaking itself, and Lehane gets the period right without ever lapsing into pastiche.
