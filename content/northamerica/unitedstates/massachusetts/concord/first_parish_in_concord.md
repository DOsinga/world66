First Parish stands on the town green at the centre of Concord's civic and literary history. The congregation dates back to the 1630s, and its meetinghouse tradition is wrapped into the same local story as the Old North Bridge, Emerson, Thoreau, and the town meetings that shaped colonial Massachusetts.

The present building is a working Unitarian Universalist church, so it is not a museum you wander through at will. It is still worth pausing outside when you are walking between Monument Square, the Concord Museum, and the Emerson sites: the white clapboard facade gives a good sense of how closely religion, politics, and village life sat together in old New England.
