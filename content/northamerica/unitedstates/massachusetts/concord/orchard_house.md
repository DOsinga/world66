Orchard House on Lexington Road is the home where Louisa May Alcott wrote *Little Women* and lived with her family from 1858 to 1877. The house is preserved essentially as it was in the Alcotts' time — the family's furniture and possessions are still in place, the rooms unchanged, and the guided tours that wind through the house give a genuine sense of the overcrowded, intellectually fervent household that the novel describes.

Bronson Alcott was one of the Concord Transcendentalists, a friend of Emerson and Thoreau, and the household was at the centre of the movement's intellectual life — conversations, visits, and debates were constant. Louisa paid the bills. Tours are the only way to see the interior and are offered regularly throughout the day; they are conducted by guides who know the family's history in detail and bring the characters of the novel to life in a way that the rooms alone cannot. The property is a National Historic Landmark managed by the Louisa May Alcott Memorial Association.
