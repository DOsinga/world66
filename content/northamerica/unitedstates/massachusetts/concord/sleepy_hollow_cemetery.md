Sleepy Hollow Cemetery in Concord contains Author's Ridge, a small hillside section that holds the graves of four of the most significant American writers of the 19th century: Ralph Waldo Emerson, Henry David Thoreau, Nathaniel Hawthorne, and Louisa May Alcott. The concentration of literary significance in this quiet, tree-shaded corner of a New England cemetery is extraordinary — there is no comparable gathering of major writers' graves anywhere in America.

The graves are simple, especially compared to the elaborate Victorian monuments elsewhere in the cemetery. Thoreau's marker is the smallest — a plain stone reading simply "HENRY" — in keeping with his philosophy of simplicity. Emerson's grave is marked by a large pink quartz boulder. Alcott's grave always carries fresh flowers and small tokens left by readers of *Little Women* from around the world. The walk through the cemetery from the main entrance to Author's Ridge is short and pleasant; the cemetery itself is an early example of the landscape cemetery style, with winding paths through natural terrain rather than formal grid layouts.
