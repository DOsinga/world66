Great Barrington sits in the southwestern corner of Massachusetts, tucked between the Taconic and Berkshire hills where Connecticut and New York press in from two sides. It's the largest town in the southern Berkshires and functions as the commercial and cultural hub for the region — a compact Main Street lined with good restaurants, galleries, and independent shops, surrounded by serious countryside.

The town is well-positioned for outdoor pursuits. [The Appalachian Trail](/northamerica/unitedstates/massachusetts/great_barrington/appalachian_trail) crosses within easy reach, viewpoints like Prospect Rock and East Rock on Mount Bryant reward those willing to climb, and the Housatonic River runs through the valley below. In early July, the Berkshires Arts Festival takes over the Butternut ski area grounds (green grass, no snow), with dozens of craft and fine art vendors spread across the base of the mountain — worth timing a visit around if you're in the area.

History is embedded in the town more deeply than it first appears. A [stone marker](/northamerica/unitedstates/massachusetts/great_barrington/shays_rebellion_site) on a street corner commemorates the final battle of Shays' Rebellion — the 1787 farmers' revolt that helped accelerate the drafting of the US Constitution. A [1895 newsboy statue](/northamerica/unitedstates/massachusetts/great_barrington/newsboy_statue) on Main Street is believed to be the world's first monument dedicated to a working child. And the [W.E.B. Du Bois boyhood homesite](/northamerica/unitedstates/massachusetts/great_barrington/w_e_b_du_bois_memorial) memorialises Great Barrington's most famous son — the civil rights leader and intellectual who grew up here before leaving to confront a very different America.

Getting here is easy: under two and a half hours by car from both New York City and Boston, which makes Great Barrington a popular weekend escape for both cities. Plan accordingly on summer and fall weekends.
