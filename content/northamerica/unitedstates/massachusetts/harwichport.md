Harwich Port is a village in the town of Harwich on Cape Cod, Massachusetts, sitting on Nantucket Sound on the Cape's south shore. It is one of the more attractive and low-key of the Cape's many small centres — a modest harbour with fishing and pleasure boats, a pleasant main street, and beaches along the sound that are calmer and generally warmer than the ocean-facing beaches on the north shore.

The area attracts a mix of families on summer holiday and people who have been coming to the same houses for decades. [Bank Street Beach](/northamerica/unitedstates/massachusetts/harwichport/bank_street_beach) is right at the edge of the village, while the larger [Red River Beach](/northamerica/unitedstates/massachusetts/harwichport/red_river_beach) in East Harwich has full facilities for families. [Wychmere Harbor](/northamerica/unitedstates/massachusetts/harwichport/wychmere_harbor) — created by cutting a channel through the barrier beach in 1888 — is one of the more photogenic small working harbours on the Cape. The [Cape Cod Rail Trail](/northamerica/unitedstates/massachusetts/harwichport/cape_cod_rail_trail) passes through town, offering 26 miles of flat cycling through pine woods and past cranberry bogs.

Harwich Port has a handful of decent restaurants and a summer-season atmosphere without the traffic and commercial intensity of Hyannis to the west. It is a good base for exploring the mid-Cape.
