The Hancock-Clarke House, built in 1698, was the home of the Reverend Jonas Clarke and his wife Lucy Bowes, who was John Hancock's cousin. Hancock and Samuel Adams were staying here as guests on the night of April 18–19, 1775, when Paul Revere rode up to warn them that the British were coming.

The house is now a museum with period furniture and exhibits on the events of that night, including the drum captured from the British during the battle. Guided tours run May through October. The house is a short walk from the Battle Green, along the original road that Revere rode down.
