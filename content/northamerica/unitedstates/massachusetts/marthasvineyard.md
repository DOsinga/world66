Martha's Vineyard is an island roughly 11 kilometres off the southern coast of [Cape Cod](/northamerica/unitedstates/massachusetts/capecod), accessible by ferry from Woods Hole. About 25 kilometres long and 16 kilometres wide, it has been one of New England's most popular summer destinations for over a century, drawing both old-money families and more recent celebrity residents — Barack Obama, the Clintons, and various Hollywood figures have all spent summers here.

The island divides into distinct parts. The Up-Island western end is rural and scenic, with the fishing town of [Menemsha](/northamerica/unitedstates/massachusetts/marthasvineyard/menemsha), wild beaches at [Aquinnah](/northamerica/unitedstates/massachusetts/marthasvineyard/aquinnah_cliffs) (formerly Gay Head) with its famous coloured clay cliffs, and quiet back roads through stone walls and meadows. The Down-Island eastern end holds the three main towns: [Vineyard Haven](/northamerica/unitedstates/massachusetts/marthasvineyard/vineyard_haven), the ferry terminus and year-round hub; [Oak Bluffs](/northamerica/unitedstates/massachusetts/marthasvineyard/oak_bluffs), with its Victorian [gingerbread cottages](/northamerica/unitedstates/massachusetts/marthasvineyard/gingerbread_cottages), a [carousel from 1876](/northamerica/unitedstates/massachusetts/marthasvineyard/flying_horses_carousel), and a livelier, younger crowd; and [Edgartown](/northamerica/unitedstates/massachusetts/marthasvineyard/edgartown), the most refined, with Federal-period whaling captains' houses and a smart yacht harbour.

The island is most alive from Memorial Day through Labour Day; outside summer it is much quieter, with many businesses closed. Getting around without a car is possible using the seasonal bus system, but a bicycle or rental car expands your options considerably.
