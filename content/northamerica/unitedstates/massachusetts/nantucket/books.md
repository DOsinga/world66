**In the Heart of the Sea** by Nathaniel Philbrick (2000) — The definitive account of the 1820 sinking of the whaleship *Essex*, stove by a sperm whale in the Pacific and the basis for Melville's *Moby-Dick*. Philbrick is a Nantucket resident and writes about the island's whaling history with real authority. Essential reading before any visit to the Whaling Museum.

**Moby-Dick** by Herman Melville (1851) — Melville's novel opens in New Bedford and Nantucket, and the Nantucket sections — the Spouter-Inn, the chapel, the departure from the harbor — repay attention knowing you're standing in the actual places. The whole novel benefits from knowing what Nantucket actually smelled and sounded like in the 1840s.

**Away Off Shore** by Nathaniel Philbrick (1994) — Philbrick's history of Nantucket from its settlement to the end of the whaling era. Less dramatic than *In the Heart of the Sea* but essential context for understanding why the island looks the way it does and why it still carries so much of its 19th-century character.

**The Nantucket Diet Murders** by Virginia Rich (1985) — A cozy mystery set on the island in winter, when the summer people have left and the year-round community closes ranks. Accurate in its portrayal of how the island changes when it isn't being performed for visitors.
