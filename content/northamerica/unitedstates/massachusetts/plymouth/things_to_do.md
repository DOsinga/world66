Plymouth wears its 1620 history without apology, and most of what draws visitors here is directly tied to it. The living history at [Plimoth Patuxent](/northamerica/unitedstates/massachusetts/plymouth/plimoth_patuxent) is genuinely excellent — better than the famous (and surprisingly anticlimactic) [Plymouth Rock](/northamerica/unitedstates/massachusetts/plymouth/plymouth_rock). The [Mayflower II](/northamerica/unitedstates/massachusetts/plymouth/mayflower_ii) replica at State Pier gives you a visceral sense of how small and impractical a vessel the Pilgrims crossed on. [Burial Hill](/northamerica/unitedstates/massachusetts/plymouth/burial_hill) rewards the short climb with harbor views and the oldest Pilgrim gravestones.

Beyond the Colonial-era sites, the town has a pleasant waterfront along Water Street with seafood restaurants, and the surrounding area is classic Massachusetts cranberry country — bog tours run through October.
