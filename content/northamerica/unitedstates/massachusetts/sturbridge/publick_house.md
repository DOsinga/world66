The Publick House has been operating on the Sturbridge Common since 1771, which makes it one of the older continuously operating inns in New England. The dining room serves traditional New England food — Indian pudding (a molasses-sweetened cornmeal porridge baked for hours), Yankee pot roast, chicken pot pie, clam chowder, and roasted meats. The menu doesn't chase trends, which is either a virtue or a limitation depending on what you're after. The portions are large and the execution is consistent.

The setting reinforces the food's character: exposed beams, wide-plank floors, and fireplaces burning in cold weather. It's the most obvious choice for dinner after a day at Old Sturbridge Village, and many visitors make the pairing intentional. The service is attentive without being fussy. Reservations are worth making on summer and fall weekends when the village draws large crowds. The attached tavern, Colonel Ebenezer Crafts Inn, operates separately and is good for a drink without committing to a full meal.
