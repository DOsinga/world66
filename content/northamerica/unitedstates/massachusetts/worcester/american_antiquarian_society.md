The American Antiquarian Society is a national research library specialising in printed material from the period before 1877 — books, newspapers, broadsides, almanacs, sheet music, and graphic arts. Founded in 1812 by the Worcester printer Isaiah Thomas, who had himself printed the first American edition of *Mother Goose* and reported the battles of Lexington and Concord, it is one of the country's most important rare-book libraries.

The reading room is reserved for researchers, but the public hall hosts exhibits drawn from the collection. Free guided tours are offered most Wednesday afternoons; for anyone interested in early American printing, journalism, or popular culture, this is a quietly fascinating stop.
