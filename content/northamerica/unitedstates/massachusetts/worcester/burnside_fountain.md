story: Sculptor Charles Harvey was supposed to depict a boy riding the turtle to symbolise Worcester's role in providing fresh water to its citizens. He died before completing the work; his student Sherry Edmundson Fry finished it from sketches, and the result — a naked boy seemingly grappling with a sea turtle — has confused Worcesterites since it was installed in 1912.

Officially called the Burnside Fountain, this small bronze statue on the edge of the Worcester Common has been universally known as "Turtle Boy" for over a century. A naked child crouches astride a sea turtle in a pose that locals have spent decades trying to interpret charitably. The intended meaning was civic — a symbol of the city's investment in clean water — but it has become Worcester's most beloved and most-photographed oddity.

The statue sits at the corner of Front and Salem streets, easy to combine with a visit to the Common. Locals are fiercely protective of Turtle Boy; the statue has been temporarily relocated for restoration on several occasions to public outcry.
