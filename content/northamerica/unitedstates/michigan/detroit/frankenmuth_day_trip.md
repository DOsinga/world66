Frankenmuth, about 90 minutes north of Detroit on I-75, is a small Michigan city that has leaned hard into its German Lutheran immigrant heritage and built one of the state's biggest tourist draws around it. The buildings along Main Street are reliably half-timbered, the shops sell Christmas ornaments, cuckoo clocks, and bratwurst, and the whole effect is more Bavarian theme park than historic town — but visitors come in enormous numbers and the place delivers what it promises.

The twin anchors are Zehnder's and the Bavarian Inn, two rival German restaurants that have faced off across Covered Bridge Park for decades. Both serve family-style all-you-can-eat chicken dinners that are the meal people drive from Detroit to eat. Neither is especially adventurous cooking, but both are generously portioned and reliably satisfying. Bronner's Christmas Wonderland, open year-round, occupies a retail complex the size of a small stadium and is a legitimate destination for anyone who takes Christmas decorating seriously.
