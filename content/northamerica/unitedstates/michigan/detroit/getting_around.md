---
title: 'Getting Around'
type: section
---

Detroit is a driving city — the layout, the distances, and the post-war planning all assume a car. Renting one is the easiest way to see Dearborn (Henry Ford Museum), Bloomfield Hills (Cranbrook), Belle Isle, and the various neighbourhoods. Parking downtown is plentiful and not expensive by big-city standards.

For downtown and Midtown alone, mass transit works. The **QLine** streetcar runs the length of Woodward Avenue between downtown and New Center, stopping at most of the major attractions. The **People Mover** is a small elevated loop around the downtown core — useful and pleasingly weird. The **DDOT** and **SMART** bus systems cover the city and inner suburbs respectively.

Uber and Lyft both run. Cycling has improved with new lanes along the RiverWalk and the Dequindre Cut, and MoGo bike-share has stations across downtown, Midtown, and a few outlying neighbourhoods.
