Flint is a working-class city in eastern Michigan that was once the birthplace of General Motors and the heart of American automobile manufacturing. The boom years built grand Art Deco buildings downtown and a cultural infrastructure — museums, theatres, a university — that still anchors the city today.

The [Sloan Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/michigan/flint/sloan_museum) chronicles the region's automotive and industrial history, while the [Flint Institute of Arts](/northamerica/unitedstates/michigan/flint/flint_institute_of_arts) houses a surprisingly strong collection for a city this size. The University of Michigan-Flint campus adds some energy to the downtown core, and the restored [Capitol Theatre](/northamerica/unitedstates/michigan/flint/capitol_theatre) hosts concerts in one of the most atmospheric movie palaces left in the Midwest. Flint's story is one of industrial rise and difficult decline, and it wears that history openly — a visit here offers an honest look at a side of America that most tourist itineraries skip entirely.

To trace the city's industrial origins, walk the two blocks of West Water Street where the [Durant-Dort Carriage Company Office](/northamerica/unitedstates/michigan/flint/durant_dort_carriage_office) and [Factory One](/northamerica/unitedstates/michigan/flint/factory_one) sit on opposite sides of the road — General Motors was effectively born in these buildings. The [Sit-Downers Memorial](/northamerica/unitedstates/michigan/flint/sitdowners_memorial) in Chevy Commons marks the 1936-37 strike that built the United Auto Workers and changed American labour. For a lighter visit, the year-round [Flint Farmers' Market](/northamerica/unitedstates/michigan/flint/flint_farmers_market) is the city's de facto town square.
