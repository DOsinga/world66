Frankenmuth is a small city about 20 miles north of Flint, Michigan, that has leaned hard into its German heritage. Founded in 1845 by Bavarian immigrants, it has built a thriving tourist trade around its Bavarian architecture, German food, and year-round Christmas spirit. It's unusual enough to be worth a visit if you're in the area.

The [Bavarian Inn](/northamerica/unitedstates/michigan/frankenmuth/bavarian_inn) on Main Street is the main restaurant draw — a sprawling place serving traditional German-American food, in competition with the equally enormous [Zehnder's](/northamerica/unitedstates/michigan/frankenmuth/zehnders) a few doors down. Down the road, [Willi's Sausage Shop](/northamerica/unitedstates/michigan/frankenmuth/willis_sausage) makes and sells the smoked meats on the premises, and is worth a stop. Carriage rides pick up passengers near the [covered bridge](/northamerica/unitedstates/michigan/frankenmuth/wooden_covered_bridge). [Bronner's Christmas Wonderland](/northamerica/unitedstates/michigan/frankenmuth/bronners_christmas_wonderland), open year-round, is billed as the world's largest Christmas store and draws visitors from across the Midwest — allow a couple of hours if you go.
