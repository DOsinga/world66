Grand Rapids is Michigan's second-largest city, sitting on the Grand River in the western part of the state. It earned the nickname "Furniture City" in the late 19th century when it was the center of American furniture manufacturing, and that heritage is visible in its well-preserved Victorian architecture and the ArtPrize competition it hosts each autumn — one of the largest art events in the world by attendance and prize money.

The city has a strong craft beer culture: there are more than 80 breweries in Kent County, earning the region recognition as a serious destination for beer tourists. Downtown has a compact walkable core with museums, restaurants, and the Grand River running through it.

Grand Rapids sits in an excellent position for day trips. To the northwest, Lake Michigan is about 45 minutes away. To the north, Sleeping Bear Dunes National Lakeshore is three hours up the coast — massive sand dunes rising 450 feet above Lake Michigan, with hiking, swimming, and some of the finest scenery in the Midwest. Traverse City, Holland (known for its tulip festival and Dutch heritage), Muskegon, and Frankenmuth are all within comfortable driving distance.
