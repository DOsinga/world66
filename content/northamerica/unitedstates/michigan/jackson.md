Jackson sits at the geographic centre of Michigan's Lower Peninsula, a mid-sized city with a handful of claims to fame. The Republican Party held its first major convention here in 1854, [under the oaks](/northamerica/unitedstates/michigan/jackson/under_the_oaks_park) on what is now a small park. The city later served as a Civil War training facility and became a railroad hub — the switching and repair headquarters for the Michigan Central Railroad, with the original [Amtrak depot](/northamerica/unitedstates/michigan/jackson/jackson_amtrak_station) still in daily use.

The main attraction is [Cascade Falls Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/michigan/jackson/cascade_falls), home to one of the largest man-made waterfalls anywhere — a concrete cascade illuminated with coloured lights on summer evenings. The park also has hiking trails, paddle boats in summer, and an ice rink in winter.

The [Ella Sharp Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/michigan/jackson/ella_sharp_museum) preserves Jackson's farming heritage in a complex that includes a log cabin, one-room schoolhouse, country store, and the Merriman Sharp Farmhouse. The [All Star Dairy](/northamerica/unitedstates/michigan/jackson/all_star_dairy_parlour) (known locally as The Parlour) is an ice cream institution — brave visitors attempt their 21-scoop Dare-To-Be-Great sundae.

Racing fans know Jackson as the home of the [Michigan International Speedway](/northamerica/unitedstates/michigan/jackson/michigan_international_speedway), one of the largest and fastest oval tracks in the United States, hosting NASCAR races each summer. On the third weekend in July, the [Hot Air Jubilee](/northamerica/unitedstates/michigan/jackson/hot_air_jubilee) fills the sky above Jackson County Airport with dozens of balloons. The state's first penitentiary still stands at the north edge of town, with a wing now run as the [Cell Block 7 prison museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/michigan/jackson/cell_block_7).
