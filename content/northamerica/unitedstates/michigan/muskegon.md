Muskegon is a waterfront city on the eastern shore of Lake Michigan, where the Muskegon River flows through Muskegon Lake before emptying into Lake Michigan through a wide channel. It was once called the "Lumber Queen of the World" — in 1887 alone, 47 sawmills surrounding Muskegon Lake cut 665 million board feet of timber — and the grand Victorian homes built during that era are still visible throughout the city.

The waterfront is the city's main attraction. The [USS Silversides](/northamerica/unitedstates/michigan/muskegon/uss_silversides) submarine — one of the most decorated American subs of the Pacific War — is permanently moored and open for tours, and you can even spend the night in the torpedo bunks. The adjacent [LST 393](/northamerica/unitedstates/michigan/muskegon/lst_393), a WWII landing ship that hit the beaches at Normandy, is one of only two surviving examples of its class. Inland, the [Hackley and Hume Historic Site](/northamerica/unitedstates/michigan/muskegon/hackley_hume) preserves two of Michigan's most ornate Victorian mansions, and the [Muskegon Museum of Art](/northamerica/unitedstates/michigan/muskegon/muskegon_museum_of_art) has a solid collection of American and European painting.

[Muskegon State Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/michigan/muskegon/muskegon_state_park) offers Lake Michigan beaches in summer and a luge track in winter. [Michigan's Adventure](/northamerica/unitedstates/michigan/muskegon/michigans_adventure), one of the largest amusement parks in the state, is just north of the city. The [Lake Express ferry](/northamerica/unitedstates/michigan/muskegon/lake_express_ferry) connects Muskegon to Milwaukee across the lake in 2.5 hours.
