  Leland's Fishtown is one of the last surviving commercial fishing villages on the Great Lakes, with a cluster of weather-beaten shanties, net sheds, and docks that date from the late 19th century. Leland, about 25 miles northwest of Traverse City on the Leelanau Peninsula, is worth visiting for Fishtown alone. The historic district sits where the Leland River meets Lake Michigan, and the collection of grey-weathered shacks strung along the docks is the most atmospheric spot in the region. The Carlson's Fisheries smokehouse has been selling smoked whitefish and trout from the same dock for generations; buy some for the drive back.

The Manitou Island ferry departs from Leland's dock, connecting to North and South Manitou Islands in Sleeping Bear Dunes National Lakeshore. A day trip to South Manitou, with its old lighthouse and wild dunes, is one of the most remote and rewarding excursions in the region. The ferry schedule is seasonal — check times in advance.
