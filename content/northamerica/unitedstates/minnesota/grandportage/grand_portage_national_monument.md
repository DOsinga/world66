The Monument preserves the location of the Great Hall, kitchen, canoe warehouse, and palisaded depot of the North West Company, the most powerful fur-trading operation in late 18th-century North America. The buildings standing today are full-scale reconstructions on the original archaeological sites; inside, costumed interpreters demonstrate clerking, cooking on the hearth, and birch-bark canoe repair.

The Heritage Center, built into a hillside above the depot, holds exhibits on Ojibwe culture and the fur trade — short films, original trade goods, and the only known surviving Montreal canoe. Admission to the Heritage Center is free; the depot has a small fee in summer. The monument is co-managed with the Grand Portage Band of Lake Superior Chippewa, and Ojibwe perspectives anchor the interpretation throughout.
