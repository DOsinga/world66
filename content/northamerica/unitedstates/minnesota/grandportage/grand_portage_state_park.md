The High Falls of the Pigeon River, at 120 feet, are the tallest waterfall in Minnesota and the reason for the original portage that gave the area its name — voyageurs could not paddle the lower Pigeon and instead carried their canoes and goods overland for 8.5 miles to bypass the falls and the rapids above them.

A half-mile paved trail leads from the visitor centre to two viewing platforms directly over the falls; a longer trail continues 3.5 miles round-trip to the Middle Falls, which see far fewer visitors. The state park sits on land of the Grand Portage Indian Reservation and is operated cooperatively with the tribe. Free admission for a state park — unusual in Minnesota.
