story: >
  The lake was renamed Lake Calhoun in 1817 after John C. Calhoun, the South Carolina senator who as Secretary of War authorized the construction of Fort Snelling. In 2018, following a protracted legal and political battle, the state of Minnesota restored the lake's original Dakota name: Bde Maka Ska, meaning "White Earth Lake." It was the first major geographical feature in the United States to have a Native name officially restored after a colonial renaming.

Bde Maka Ska is the largest of the Chain of Lakes that runs through the western neighbourhoods of Minneapolis — a 400-acre lake surrounded by 5.7 miles of parkway trails used year-round by runners, cyclists, skaters, and in winter, cross-country skiers. The lake connects by channel to Lake of the Isles to the north and to Lake Harriet to the south, creating one of the best urban trail systems in America.

Beaches on the east and west shores are open for swimming from June through August, with lifeguards on duty. Canoe, kayak, and paddleboard rental is available at the northeast corner in summer. In winter, the lake freezes solid enough for ice skating and occasionally ice fishing, and the Park Board maintains a skating loop with a warming house. The surrounding residential streets — Victorian and Craftsman houses in the Kenwood neighbourhood — are worth exploring.
