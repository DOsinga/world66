  The Guthrie was founded in 1963 by Tyrone Guthrie, the Irish director who wanted to create a regional repertory theatre far from Broadway's commercial pressures. He chose Minneapolis because the city promised sustained civic and financial support — and delivered on it. The original building, designed by Ralph Rapson, was demolished in 2006 when the theatre moved to its current Jean Nouvel building on the riverbank. Guthrie died in 1971, long before the move, but the theatre's reputation as one of America's two or three finest regional companies has continued to grow.
---

The Guthrie Theater is housed in a striking Jean Nouvel building on the Mississippi riverbank — a blue-clad structure with a dramatic cantilevered balcony called the "Endless Bridge" that projects 178 feet over the river, offering one of the best views in Minneapolis. The building alone justifies a visit even if you are not attending a performance; take the elevator to the top floor and walk out onto the bridge.

The theatre itself has three stages and a season running from September through June, with a repertoire that mixes Shakespeare and classical drama with contemporary American and international work. Tickets are generally much cheaper than Broadway equivalents for comparable production quality. Even mid-week performances tend to be well-attended — Minneapolis has a serious theatre audience.
