The Washburn Park Water Tower is an Art Deco water tower in south Minneapolis, built in 1932 and notable for its ornamental decoration: eight allegorical "Guardians of Health" carved in terracotta stand around the base of the tank, representing various threats to the city's water supply. The tower is a distinctive landmark on the southwest Minneapolis skyline and a good example of the Art Deco civic aesthetic applied to functional infrastructure.

The tower is not normally open to the public but is visible from the street in Washburn Park. It is part of a walking or cycling route through the south Minneapolis neighbourhoods that includes Lake Harriet, the Lynhurst neighbourhood, and the Minnehaha Creek Corridor.
