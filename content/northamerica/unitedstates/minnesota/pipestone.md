Pipestone is a small prairie town in far southwestern Minnesota, but it has more weight than its size suggests. Its name comes from the red pipestone, or catlinite, quarried just north of town for ceremonial pipes by Indigenous peoples from across North America.

The essential stop is [Pipestone National Monument](/northamerica/unitedstates/minnesota/pipestone/pipestone_national_monument), where active quarry pits, tallgrass prairie, quartzite ledges, and Winnewissa Falls sit inside a compact and deeply meaningful landscape. In town, the [Pipestone County Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/minnesota/pipestone/pipestone_county_museum) and [historic downtown](/northamerica/unitedstates/minnesota/pipestone/pipestone_historic_downtown) help explain the other local stone story: settlers built much of the town from pink and red Sioux quartzite.

Pipestone is not a place for a long urban itinerary. It is very good for half a day or a quiet overnight on the prairie, especially if you slow down enough to walk the monument trail, look closely at the downtown masonry, and understand why the quarries remain sacred.
