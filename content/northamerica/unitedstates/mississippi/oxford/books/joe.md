Larry Brown was a Lafayette County firefighter who taught himself to write in his off-hours and produced some of the rawest fiction to come out of Mississippi in the second half of the 20th century. *Joe* (1991) is his strongest novel — the story of a hard-drinking forest worker and a poor white teenager who falls into his orbit, set in the country immediately north of Oxford.

The book is short, brutal, and full of the kind of working-class north Mississippi voices that almost never appear in Faulkner. Brown died in 2004 and is buried in nearby Tula. *Big Bad Love*, his linked short story collection, is a good companion.
