Starkville is a small Mississippi city with one large personality: Mississippi State University. On football weekends the town swells, cowbells ring everywhere, and the line between campus, downtown, and the Cotton District gets pleasantly blurry. That college-town energy is the reason to come, but not the only one.

The surprise is how much history and nature sit within easy reach. The [Ulysses S. Grant Presidential Library](ulysses_s_grant_presidential_library) gives Starkville a serious Civil War and Reconstruction anchor unusual for a town this size. Just south of town, [Sam D. Hamilton Noxubee National Wildlife Refuge](sam_d_hamilton_noxubee_national_wildlife_refuge) protects cypress, lakes, boardwalks, and bird habitat that feel very far from the SEC tailgate lots.

The [Cotton District](cotton_district) is Starkville's best place to wander without an agenda. It began as a mill-worker area and was remade into a compact, walkable neighbourhood of student houses, porches, bars, and restaurants. Starkville is not a major destination in the way Oxford or Natchez are, but if you are already crossing north Mississippi, it can fill a good day.
