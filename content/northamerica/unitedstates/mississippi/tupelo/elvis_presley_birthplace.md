The house where Elvis Aaron Presley was born on 8 January 1935 is a tiny two-room shotgun house on Old Saltillo Road, now preserved inside a small memorial park. The structure has been carefully maintained — whitewashed boards, a modest front porch, rooms barely big enough for a family of three — and the effect is genuinely moving. Whatever you think of Elvis, the contrast between this and the later excess of Graceland says something real about American life in the 20th century.

The adjacent Elvis Presley Museum holds a solid collection of memorabilia: childhood photographs, personal effects, stage costumes, and exhibits tracing his rise from this impoverished corner of Mississippi to global fame. The museum does not overwhelm; it presents the story with some restraint and lets the artifacts speak. A small chapel built by fans stands on the grounds, and a bronze statue of the young Elvis gazes toward the birthplace. The site is operated by the City of Tupelo and is well maintained.
