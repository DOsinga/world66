The Natchez Trace Parkway is a 716-km scenic road managed by the National Park Service, running from Natchez in southwestern Mississippi all the way to Nashville, Tennessee. The section near Tupelo is among the most accessible entry points, and even a short drive north or south from the city reveals why this road has a devoted following: two lanes of pavement winding through old-growth hardwood forest, no commercial traffic, no billboards, very little development.

Several historic sites and natural areas lie close to Tupelo along the Trace, including the Chickasaw Village Site and the Tupelo Visitor Center at milepost 266, which has exhibits on the road's history and the cultures it connected. The drive is especially beautiful in early spring when the dogwood blooms, and in autumn when the hardwoods turn. Cyclists use the Trace extensively — it is one of the few long-distance road cycling routes in the South with minimal traffic. Allow at least two hours for a meaningful stretch of driving; a full day to reach Nashville.
