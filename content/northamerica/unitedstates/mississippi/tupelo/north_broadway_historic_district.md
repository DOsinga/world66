The North Broadway Historic District is a four-block stretch of late 19th- and early 20th-century commercial and residential architecture listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1985. The district showcases the prosperity Tupelo enjoyed as a rail and cotton centre in the late 1800s, with brick commercial buildings, Victorian-era homes, and several churches dating from the same period.

Walking the district takes about half an hour and pairs naturally with downtown Tupelo's modest restaurant and café scene. It is not a major tourist attraction in its own right, but it gives the Elvis pilgrimage a bit of historical context and shows Tupelo as something more than a one-attraction town. The First United Methodist Church at 412 West Main Street, a handsome brick building on the edge of the district, is also listed on the National Register.
