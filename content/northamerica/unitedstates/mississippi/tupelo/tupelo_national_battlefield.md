Tupelo National Battlefield is a compact National Park Service site on West Main Street, preserving the memory of the July 1864 Battle of Tupelo. The battle was not a clean theatrical victory, but it mattered strategically: Union forces kept Confederate cavalry away from the rail lines supporting Sherman's Atlanta campaign.

Do not expect a sprawling battlefield park. The preserved site is small, with a monument, cannon, flag, and interpretive material, and the modern city presses close around it. That contrast is part of the experience. It works best as a short, thoughtful history stop, especially when paired with other Civil War and civil-rights context in northern Mississippi.
