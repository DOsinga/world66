The Tupelo National Battlefield is one of the smallest units in the National Park Service — just one acre marking the site of the two-day Battle of Tupelo (also known as the Battle of Harrisburg), fought 14–15 July 1864. The battle was strategically important: Union forces were specifically tasked with keeping the Confederate cavalry commander Nathan Bedford Forrest from disrupting Sherman's supply lines to the south.

A simple granite monument and interpretive markers explain the engagement. The site is compact and can be visited in 20–30 minutes, but it is well maintained and the historical context is clearly explained. The nearby Natchez Trace Parkway visitor facilities provide additional regional context. For Civil War enthusiasts, pairing this site with the Brices Cross Roads National Battlefield Site (about 27 km north) gives a fuller picture of the 1864 Mississippi campaign.
