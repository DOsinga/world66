story: >
  When Vicksburg fell on 4 July 1863, President Lincoln declared that "the Father of Waters again goes unvexed to the sea." The city's residents were so humiliated by surrendering on Independence Day that they refused to celebrate the Fourth of July for more than eighty years — some say the tradition only resumed after World War Two, when local soldiers fought alongside Northerners and gradually made their peace.
---

The Vicksburg National Military Park preserves the 47-day siege of 1863 in extraordinary detail across 1,800 acres of battlefield. A 16-mile tour road winds past more than 1,300 monuments, markers, and cannon positions — the densest collection of Civil War memorials anywhere in the country. Most states that sent soldiers to the campaign raised monuments here, from simple granite shafts to elaborate architectural structures.

Drive the loop at your own pace or join a ranger-led tour. The visitor centre at the north entrance has good exhibits on the siege tactics and civilian experience — the population of Vicksburg spent the final weeks living in caves dug into the city's bluffs. Allow at least three hours for the park; half a day if you want to walk any of the original earthworks. Admission covers the USS Cairo Museum.
