Hannibal is a town that has fed more novels than it has produced — and there is one obvious reading list.

**The Adventures of Tom Sawyer** (Mark Twain, 1876). Twain's most popular novel and the one most directly built from his Hannibal childhood. The fence, the cave, the island, and the river are all here.

**The Adventures of Huckleberry Finn** (Mark Twain, 1884). The darker and greater of the Twain river novels, beginning in a Hannibal-shaped town called St Petersburg before drifting south down the Mississippi.

**Life on the Mississippi** (Mark Twain, 1883). Memoir of Twain's years as a steamboat pilot, with a long section revisiting Hannibal in the 1880s. The best of his non-fiction.

**The Bandit of Hell's Bend** (Edgar Rice Burroughs, 1925). Not set in Hannibal, but written in the rooms Burroughs rented at the Mark Twain Hotel — a footnote, but a charming one for visitors.
