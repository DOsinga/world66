The historic heart of Kansas City's African American community and the neighborhood that gave the world Kansas City jazz. In the 1920s and 1930s, the intersection of 18th Street and Vine was surrounded by jazz clubs, restaurants, and theaters catering to the Black community at a time when segregation barred them from white establishments downtown. The music that emerged — loose, swinging, blues-drenched — produced Charlie Parker, Count Basie, and Big Joe Turner.

The district fell into serious decline after desegregation and decades of disinvestment. Its revival came with the opening of the American Jazz Museum and the Negro Leagues Baseball Museum in 1997, both now anchors of a slowly regenerating neighborhood. The Blue Room jazz club, attached to the Jazz Museum, keeps live music alive in the space where it was born. The Gem Theater, a restored 1912 vaudeville house, hosts concerts and events. Walking these blocks on a weekend night, with music spilling out of open doors, connects you to something real about American cultural history.
