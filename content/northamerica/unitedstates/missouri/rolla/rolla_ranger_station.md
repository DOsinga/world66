  The Rolla Ranger Station is one of the finest examples of Rustic Revival architecture associated with the US Forest Service in the Midwest. The Rolla Ranger Station Historic District preserves a collection of CCC-era buildings that served as the administrative headquarters for the Mark Twain National Forest. The structures, built of local stone and timber in the 1930s, are handsome examples of the Rustic Revival style favoured by the Forest Service during the New Deal era.

The site is located at the edge of Rolla, just west of the city centre, and is still used by Forest Service staff. It offers a good introduction to the history of Mark Twain National Forest, which covers 1.5 million acres of the Ozarks and is the primary reason outdoor enthusiasts come to this part of Missouri.
