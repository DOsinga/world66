Nathanael Greene/Close Memorial Park is Springfield's largest city park, covering more than 100 acres on the southwest side of the city. It contains sports facilities, picnic grounds, a botanical center, and several distinct garden spaces. The crown jewel is the Japanese Stroll Garden, a formal garden designed by landscape architect Koichi Kawana and completed in the 1980s as a gift from Springfield's Japanese sister city, Isesaki.

The stroll garden features a teahouse, stone lanterns, koi ponds, a zigzag bridge, and plantings that shift dramatically with the seasons — cherry blossoms in spring, dense green in summer, vivid foliage in fall. The design follows traditional Japanese principles in which the garden reveals itself gradually as visitors move along the winding path. The park also contains the Mizumoto Japanese Stroll Garden, the Springfield Botanical Gardens, and the Wildflower Garden, making it a full half-day destination for anyone interested in horticulture or simply a peaceful place to walk. Admission to the gardens is free, though some special events carry a charge.
