St. Joseph sits on the Missouri River north of [Kansas City](/northamerica/unitedstates/missouri/kansascity), and its best stories belong to the restless nineteenth-century West. This was where the Pony Express began in 1860, where emigrants outfitted themselves before pushing onto the plains, and where Jesse James was shot in 1882 after years as America's most famous outlaw.

The city is not polished in the way larger Missouri destinations can be, but that roughness suits it. The main reason to come is the cluster of serious small museums around the old Pony Express story: [Patee House Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/missouri/st_joseph/patee_house_museum), the [Jesse James Home](/northamerica/unitedstates/missouri/st_joseph/jesse_james_home), and the [Pony Express National Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/missouri/st_joseph/pony_express_national_museum) are close enough to make a compact history day.

St. Joseph also has a more unexpected museum streak. The [Glore Psychiatric Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/missouri/st_joseph/glore_psychiatric_museum) is strange, sobering, and memorable, while the [Albrecht-Kemper Museum of Art](/northamerica/unitedstates/missouri/st_joseph/albrecht_kemper_museum_of_art) gives the city a quieter cultural anchor beyond frontier lore.
