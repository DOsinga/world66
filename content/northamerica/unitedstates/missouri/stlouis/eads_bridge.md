The Eads Bridge, designed by James Buchanan Eads and opened in 1874, was a stunning piece of engineering for its day — the first bridge to span the Mississippi below the confluence with the Missouri, the first major use of true steel rather than wrought iron in a structure, and the first significant use of pneumatic caissons in North America.

The bridge still carries MetroLink trains on its lower deck and road traffic above. Walking across it, by foot or bike, is one of the simple pleasures of downtown St Louis — the river below, the Arch immediately south, the East St Louis skyline (such as it is) ahead. The east-end approach has good vantage points for photographs of the bridge and the Arch together.
