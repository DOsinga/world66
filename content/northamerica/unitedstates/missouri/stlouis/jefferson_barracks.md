Jefferson Barracks, on the Mississippi about 15 kilometres south of downtown, was the largest US Army post west of the Mississippi for most of the 19th century. Founded in 1826, it served as the western headquarters of the army during the Indian Wars and a training base through both World Wars. Almost every famous American general of the 1800s — Grant, Sherman, Sheridan, Lee — passed through here at some point.

The complex is now divided: most of the former post is a county park with running trails, an old powder magazine museum, and reconstructed officer's quarters. The southern half is the active Jefferson Barracks National Cemetery, one of the largest in the country, with over 220,000 burials including a substantial Civil War section. A free National Park-affiliated museum interprets the post's military history.
