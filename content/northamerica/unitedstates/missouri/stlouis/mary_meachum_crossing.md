Mary Meachum Freedom Crossing marks a stretch of Mississippi riverbank where, on the night of 21 May 1855, a free Black woman named Mary Meachum tried to lead nine enslaved people across the river to freedom in Illinois. The attempt was intercepted by armed federal marshals on the Illinois side; several of the escapees were caught and re-enslaved.

It was designated Missouri's first National Underground Railroad Network to Freedom site in 2001. The riverbank itself is modest — a path, some interpretive signs — but the site stands as a reminder that the underground railroad ran right through St Louis, a city that sat on the dividing line between slave and free territory. The Greenway path along the river connects the site to downtown.
