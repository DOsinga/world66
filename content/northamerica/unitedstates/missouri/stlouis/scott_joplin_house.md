The Scott Joplin House is the only surviving St Louis residence of the composer who, more than any other figure, defined ragtime music. Joplin and his wife lived in this second-floor flat from 1900 to 1903 — the years he wrote "The Entertainer" and several of his most famous compositions.

The house is now a Missouri State Historic Site. A small museum on the ground floor covers Joplin's life, his St Louis years, and the music's revival after the 1973 film "The Sting" used "The Entertainer" as its theme. The flat itself is preserved approximately as Joplin would have known it, with a period piano in the parlour. Modest entry fee.
