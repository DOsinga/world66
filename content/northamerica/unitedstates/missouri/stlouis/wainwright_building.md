The Wainwright Building, designed by Louis Sullivan and completed in 1891, is one of the most important buildings in the history of architecture — among the first true skyscrapers and the first to express its steel frame in its facade. Sullivan's famous dictum "form follows function" was conceived in part for this building.

The terracotta ornamentation on the upper floors is exuberant rather than restrained, and the vertical piers between the windows make the ten-storey building feel taller than it is — a deliberate effect that influenced almost every tall building that followed. It is still in use as state offices and the lobby can be entered during business hours. From outside, the building is visible from Chestnut Street between 7th and 8th.
