Bozeman sits in the Gallatin Valley at the foot of the Bridger Mountains, a college town that has become one of Montana's most visited cities. Home to [Montana State University](/northamerica/unitedstates/montana/bozeman/montana_state_university), it has a lively, outdoorsy character — the kind of place where hikers and skiers share coffee shops with researchers and ranchers.

The city is two hours from Yellowstone's north entrance, which makes it a natural base for visiting the park. [Bridger Bowl](/northamerica/unitedstates/montana/bozeman/bridger_bowl) ski area is just north of town, and [Hyalite Canyon](/northamerica/unitedstates/montana/bozeman/hyalite_canyon) offers year-round hiking and climbing a short drive south. [Main Street](/northamerica/unitedstates/montana/bozeman/main_street) has independent restaurants, bookshops, and bars that give it a sense of genuine life rather than tourist infrastructure.

The [Museum of the Rockies](/northamerica/unitedstates/montana/bozeman/museum_of_the_rockies) is the standout cultural attraction, with one of the best dinosaur fossil collections in North America — the paleontologist Jack Horner ran his field operations out of here for decades. The [American Computer Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/montana/bozeman/american_computer_museum) is a more unlikely draw, tracing the history of computing from Babylonian clay tablets to early microprocessors, and is better than it sounds.
