A small upstairs restaurant on the second floor of a survivor building on Main Street — climb a steep staircase past faded photographs of the original 1911 dining room. The menu has barely changed in a century: chow mein, chop suey, noodles, fried rice, sweet and sour pork, served in private red-curtained booths.

Open evenings only. Cash and cards. The chow mein is the dish to order; the historical experience is the meal.
