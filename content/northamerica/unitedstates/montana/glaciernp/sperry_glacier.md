Sperry Glacier is one of Glacier National Park's named icefields, tucked high in rugged country above the Lake McDonald side of the park. It is not a roadside sight; reaching the glacier area means a demanding backcountry hike, usually involving serious elevation gain and changeable mountain weather.

That difficulty is part of why it matters. The glacier is a vivid, sobering destination for hikers who want to understand the park beyond scenic drives, and it also makes climate change visible in a place whose very name is tied to ice. Go prepared, check trail conditions, and treat it as a mountain objective rather than a casual viewpoint.
