The bronze statue of Abraham Lincoln on the south grounds of the state capitol is a work by Daniel Chester French, the sculptor also responsible for the Lincoln Memorial in Washington. This earlier piece, cast in 1912, shows a standing Lincoln at Gettysburg — a more intimate scale than the Washington colossus, and more affecting for it.

The statue is on the capitol grounds and easy to visit in combination with a tour of the building. Lincoln the city was named after Abraham Lincoln (over the strong objection of Nebraska Democrats who found the name politically obnoxious), which gives the statue a peculiar civic significance — a monument to the man whose name the city carries, placed in front of the building that his party's policies helped make possible.
