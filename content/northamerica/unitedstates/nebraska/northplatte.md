North Platte is a city in western Nebraska at the confluence of the North and South Platte rivers, serving as a regional centre for the surrounding ranch country. It sits on the old Oregon, California, and Mormon pioneer trails, and the landscape here — wide, flat, and open — gives a strong sense of what those travellers crossed.

The city is associated with Buffalo Bill Cody, who lived here and ran his famous Wild West show from nearby [Scout's Rest Ranch](/northamerica/unitedstates/nebraska/northplatte/buffalo_bill_ranch), now a state historical park with preserved buildings and exhibits about the frontier showman's life.

North Platte is also home to **[Bailey Yard](/northamerica/unitedstates/nebraska/northplatte/golden_spike_tower)**, the largest railroad classification yard in the world. The [Golden Spike Tower](/northamerica/unitedstates/nebraska/northplatte/golden_spike_tower) visitor centre sits above the yard and offers views over miles of tracks and hundreds of freight cars being sorted at any given time — an oddly compelling sight for anyone interested in how goods move across a continent.

During the Second World War, the city earned a quieter form of fame: the [North Platte Canteen](/northamerica/unitedstates/nebraska/northplatte/north_platte_canteen) operated out of the Union Pacific station from 1941 to 1946, serving free food and hospitality to an estimated six million soldiers passing through on troop trains. The [Lincoln County Historical Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/nebraska/northplatte/lincoln_county_historical_museum) has the best exhibit on both the canteen and the pioneer trail history of the region.
