  Bailey Yard covers roughly 4,900 acres and sorts around 10,000 freight cars
  every day across 315 kilometres of track. The hump — a gentle engineered rise
  that gravity-routes cars to the correct track — is the mechanical heart of the
  operation. Watching a cut of cars roll silently away from the hump crest and
  slot themselves into place without any locomotive involvement is a strangely
  satisfying piece of industrial choreography.

The Bailey Yard is the largest railroad classification yard in the world, owned and operated by Union Pacific, and it sits right on the edge of North Platte. The Golden Spike Tower is a purpose-built eight-storey observation structure that gives the public a panoramic view directly over the yard.

From the top deck you look out over a sea of parallel tracks stretching to the horizon, with freight trains being broken apart and reassembled around the clock. Interpretive panels explain how a classification yard works — how the hump and computer-controlled switches route individual cars to the right outbound train — and there is a ground-level museum floor with Union Pacific history exhibits. The tower is open daily during summer; hours are shorter in winter. It is the most compelling industrial sight between Chicago and Denver.
