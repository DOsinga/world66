Omaha sits on the western bank of the Missouri River and has been a transit and trade hub since the mid-19th century, when it served as the eastern terminus of the first transcontinental railroad. Today it's Nebraska's largest city, home to several Fortune 500 companies (including Berkshire Hathaway) and a quietly confident place that rewards visitors who spend more than a day.

The [Old Market](/northamerica/unitedstates/nebraska/omaha/old_market), a converted warehouse district in the northeast of downtown, is the liveliest part of the city on foot. The [Henry Doorly Zoo](/northamerica/unitedstates/nebraska/omaha/henrydoorlyzoo) is genuinely world-class and worth the trip on its own. [Boys Town](/northamerica/unitedstates/nebraska/omaha/boys_town) — the residential campus made famous by the 1938 film — is still an active community a few miles west of the centre, and is open to visitors.

Omaha has a strong Czech, Polish, and Scandinavian immigrant heritage, visible in the food and architecture of the South 13th Street corridor. The [Joslyn Art Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/nebraska/omaha/joslyn_art_museum) is worth an afternoon, especially for the Karl Bodmer watercolors of the pre-contact American West. The [Durham Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/nebraska/omaha/durham_western_her) in the old Union Station covers the city's railroad history well. The city's summers are hot and humid; winters are cold with reliable snow.
