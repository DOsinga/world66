Henderson is Nevada's second-largest city, sitting just southeast of Las Vegas with a very different personality. While the Strip glitters a few miles away, Henderson is quieter and more suburban — a city of parks, trails, and neighborhoods where people actually live.

The city traces its origins to World War II, when the federal government built a magnesium processing plant here to supply the war effort. After the war, the plant closed and Henderson incorporated as a city in 1953. That industrial past is long gone; today it's one of the fastest-growing cities in the country.

Henderson's own attractions are modest but genuine: the [Henderson Bird Viewing Preserve](/northamerica/unitedstates/nevada/henderson/henderson_bird_viewing_preserve) is a superb wetland birding spot set on reclaimed water treatment ponds, and the [Clark County Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/nevada/henderson/clark_county_museum) has an excellent outdoor Heritage Street with restored historic buildings. The [Water Street District](/northamerica/unitedstates/nevada/henderson/water_street_district) gives the city a small but genuine downtown with cafes and local restaurants. [Lake Las Vegas](/northamerica/unitedstates/nevada/henderson/lake_las_vegas), an artificial lake on the eastern edge of the city, is an improbable but pleasant resort area. [Sloan Canyon](/northamerica/unitedstates/nevada/henderson/sloan_canyon), 15 miles south, has over 1,700 Native American petroglyphs. But the real appeal is its position as a launch pad. [Valley of Fire State Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/nevada/redrockcanyon), Hoover Dam, and Lake Mead are all within an hour's drive, making Henderson a quieter and often cheaper base than Las Vegas for exploring the Mojave.
