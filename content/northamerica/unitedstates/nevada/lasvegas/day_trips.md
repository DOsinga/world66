Nevada's dramatic landscape is just a short drive from the Strip. [Red Rock Canyon](/northamerica/unitedstates/nevada/redrockcanyon), 20 miles west, has a 13-mile scenic loop drive past banded sandstone cliffs and a network of hiking trails — the closest serious nature to the city. Southeast, [Hoover Dam and Lake Mead](/northamerica/unitedstates/nevada/hoover_dam_lake_mead) draw millions each year to one of the great engineering feats of the 20th century and the largest reservoir in the country; pair the dam with the quiet, gambling-free streets of [Boulder City](/northamerica/unitedstates/nevada/bouldercity) just up the hill. An hour northeast, [Valley of Fire State Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/nevada/valley_of_fire) is named for its vivid red sandstone formations and holds petroglyphs thousands of years old.

When the desert basin is unbearable, [Mount Charleston](/northamerica/unitedstates/nevada/mt_charleston), about 45 minutes northwest, sits high enough to see snow in winter and offers a 30-degree temperature drop in summer. Farther afield, [Death Valley National Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/nevada/death_valley) is a two-hour drive west and worth the long day if you want to stand at the lowest and hottest point in North America, while [Grand Canyon West](/northamerica/unitedstates/nevada/grand_canyon_west), 2.5 hours southeast on Hualapai tribal land, puts you on the glass-floored Skywalk over a 4,000-foot drop.

Closer in, the near-ghost town of [Nelson](/northamerica/unitedstates/nevada/nelson) in Eldorado Canyon offers an old gold mine, rusted-out cars, and a stunning Colorado River overlook 45 minutes southeast. And for the truly curious, [Rachel](/northamerica/unitedstates/nevada/rachel) on the Extraterrestrial Highway — gateway to the Area 51 lore — makes for a weird and wonderful day out about 90 miles north. The petroglyphs at [Grapevine Canyon](/northamerica/unitedstates/nevada/grapevine_canyon_petroglyphs) are another worthwhile stop on the way toward the Colorado River.
