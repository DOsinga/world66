Nevada's dramatic landscape is just a short drive from the Strip. Red Rock Canyon, 20 miles west, has a 13-mile scenic loop drive past banded sandstone cliffs and a network of hiking trails. Hoover Dam is 30 miles southeast and more than 10 million visitors come each year to see this engineering marvel. Lake Mead, the 16th-largest man-made lake in the country, offers jet skiing, kayaking, and camping in the Mojave Desert. Valley of Fire State Park, 60 miles northeast, is named for its vivid red sandstone formations and contains petroglyphs thousands of years old.

Mount Charleston, about 45 minutes northwest, sits high enough to see snow in winter and gives a 30-degree temperature drop in summer. Death Valley National Park is a two-hour drive west and worth the long day if you want to see the lowest and hottest point in North America. For the truly curious, Area 51 and the tiny town of Rachel on the Extraterrestrial Highway make for a weird and wonderful day out, about 90 miles north.
