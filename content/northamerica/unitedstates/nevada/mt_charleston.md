When the Vegas summer hits 110°F, Mount Charleston offers a 30-degree drop in temperature an hour's drive northwest of the city. The 11,916-foot peak — Nevada's eighth-highest mountain — rises out of the Mojave Desert and is cloaked in ponderosa pine, white fir, and aspen, a different planet from the desert basin below. Geologically it is a "sky island," its high forests cut off from any other range, and it shelters plants and animals found nowhere else in southern Nevada.

Several hiking trails radiate from the Kyle Canyon and Lee Canyon trailheads, ranging from short family strolls like the Mary Jane Falls trail to the long, strenuous climb to Charleston Peak itself. Spring brings wildflowers and rushing snowmelt; autumn turns the aspen gold. Even a casual visitor can drive the canyon roads, picnic under the pines, and feel the temperature fall with every switchback.

In winter the small Lee Canyon ski area opens for snowboarding, beginner-friendly skiing, and snowshoeing, with lifts running roughly December through March depending on snowfall — a genuine novelty within an hour of the casinos. The Mount Charleston Lodge has burned down more than once over the decades, but the current rebuild offers a restaurant and a comfortable bar with a wood fire, a pleasant place to spend a few hours when the desert basin is unbearable.

Bring layers whatever the season; it can be shorts-weather in the city and snowing at the trailheads on the same afternoon. There is no entrance fee, but the popular trailheads fill early on summer weekends.
