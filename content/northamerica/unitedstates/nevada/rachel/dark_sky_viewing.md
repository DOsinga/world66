The skies above Rachel are among the darkest in the contiguous United States. No major town within 100 miles, no commercial air routes overhead, and virtually no artificial light of any kind means that on a clear moonless night the Milky Way is visible as a vivid band across the entire sky. The nearest Bortle Class 1 (the darkest possible) zones are within driving distance, but Rachel itself is dark enough that the difference is academic — what you see here will astonish anyone who has only ever stargazed near a city.

The best approach is simply to drive a short distance out of town, pull off the highway, cut your headlights, and wait fifteen minutes for your eyes to adapt. The dry desert air gives exceptional clarity, and at elevations above 4,000 feet the atmosphere is thinner than at sea level. A pair of binoculars dramatically increases what you can see — star clusters, nebulae, and the dark lanes within the Milky Way become distinct. The sonic booms from military aircraft are occasional but should not be mistaken for UFOs; they are louder than expected but entirely predictable in their cause.
