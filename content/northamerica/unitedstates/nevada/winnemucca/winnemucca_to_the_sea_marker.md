  The Winnemucca to the Sea Highway was promoted in the 1960s as a marketing route — a 470-mile drive linking landlocked Winnemucca to Crescent City on the Pacific coast of California, partly along US-95 and Oregon state highways. The driftwood slab marker at the Winnemucca end was floated downriver from the Pacific to dramatise the connection. The route never quite caught on with travellers.

A large slab of weathered driftwood mounted on a stone base on Winnemucca Boulevard marks the eastern terminus of the Winnemucca to the Sea Highway, a 1960s tourist-route designation linking the Nevada desert town to the Pacific coast in California. The driftwood was reputedly floated down rivers from the ocean to make the point.

Easy to miss on the strip but worth a two-minute pause — a small monument to the kind of mid-century American highway-promotion campaign that has otherwise vanished entirely.
