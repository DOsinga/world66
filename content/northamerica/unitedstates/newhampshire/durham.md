Durham is a compact college town on the Oyster River in southeastern New Hampshire, built almost entirely around the University of New Hampshire. The campus anchors the centre of town and sets the rhythm of daily life: the population nearly doubles during the academic year, when students fill the coffee shops and bars along Main Street, then drops sharply in summer when things turn quiet and unhurried.

For a place this small, Durham carries some historical weight. The Oyster River settlement, one of the oldest in New Hampshire, was nearly destroyed in a 1694 raid by Abenaki warriors — an event that echoes through local place names and the Durham Historic District along the waterfront. The John Sullivan House, home to a Revolutionary War general and three-time governor of New Hampshire, still stands on Newmarket Road.

Today, though, Durham is most interesting as a university town done right. UNH's campus blends red-brick Georgian buildings with open quads, and the Museum of Art holds a serious collection that punches well above the town's size. The Amtrak Downeaster stops here on its run between Boston and Portland, making Durham a practical base for exploring the Seacoast region — Portsmouth is fifteen minutes by car, and the Atlantic beaches at Hampton are not much further.
