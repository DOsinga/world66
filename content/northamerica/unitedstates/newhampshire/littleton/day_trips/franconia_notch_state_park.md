The crown jewel of the western White Mountains, Franconia Notch State Park sits about 12 miles south of Littleton along I-93. The park packs a remarkable amount into one valley: the Flume Gorge, a narrow granite chasm with waterfalls and wooden walkways threading through it; Echo Lake, a clear mountain lake at the base of Cannon Mountain with a beach and canoe rental; and the Cannon Mountain Aerial Tramway, which carries visitors 2,180 feet to the summit with views stretching into Vermont, Maine, and Quebec on clear days.

The Old Man of the Mountain — the craggy rock profile that appeared on the New Hampshire state quarter — collapsed in 2003, but a series of profile markers along Profile Lake still let you line up where it once hung. In winter, Cannon Mountain is a serious ski area, one of the oldest in New England, with challenging terrain and relatively modest lift prices compared to Vermont resorts.

story: >
  The Old Man of the Mountain was a natural rock formation on a cliff face that, when viewed from exactly the right angle along Profile Lake, resolved into a stern human profile — forehead, nose, lips, and chin formed by five separate granite ledges. It appeared on the state quarter, licence plates, and road signs, and was so beloved that when it collapsed overnight on 3 May 2003, the governor of New Hampshire wept publicly. Nathaniel Hawthorne wrote a story about it in 1850. Nothing was left but the iron rods that had been bolted in decades earlier in a failed attempt to hold it together.
