The Millyard Museum sits inside one of the original Amoskeag factory buildings and tells the full story of the manufacturing complex that was, for a few decades in the late 19th century, the largest textile operation in the world. The permanent exhibition — "Woven in Time: 11,000 Years at Amoskeag Falls" — begins with Indigenous use of the river's fishing grounds, moves through the founding of the mill city in the 1830s, and follows the Amoskeag Manufacturing Company through its spectacular rise and its collapse in the Great Depression.

The exhibit handles the human dimensions honestly: the French-Canadian and Greek immigrant communities who worked the looms, the 1922 strike that began the company's unraveling, and the decades of post-collapse decay before the millyard was reimagined as offices and research space. The textile machinery on display is beautifully maintained, and the building itself — high ceilings, original timber frames, thick brick walls — is part of the exhibit. Plan two hours; there is more here than the compact floor plan suggests.

The museum is run by the Manchester Historic Association and serves as the best entry point to the broader millyard, which now houses universities, tech companies, and restaurants in the same Victorian mill buildings. After the museum, walk north along the river to see the full scale of the complex.
