The Portsmouth African Burying Ground is a small memorial park on Chestnut Street marking the site where an 18th-century burial ground for enslaved and free African Americans was discovered in 2003. A memorial marker was placed in 2015 after a decade of community engagement about how to appropriately acknowledge the site. The remains of three individuals were reinterred there with ceremony.

The memorial is not large — it occupies a small portion of a city block — but it carries real weight. Portsmouth's colonial prosperity was built substantially on participation in the slave trade and on enslaved labour, a history that is inadequately represented in the city's more prominent historical narratives. The plaques and the nearby African Heritage Trail markers help fill in this gap. The trail connects sites across the city associated with Portsmouth's Black history.
