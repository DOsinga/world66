Sugar Hill sits on a long ridge about 10 miles southwest of Littleton, a village of barely 500 people whose setting — overlooking the Franconia Range to the north and Vermont's Green Mountains to the west — punches well above its size. On a clear day the panorama from Route 117 is one of the finest valley-and-mountain views in New England.

The town is best known for its June lupine bloom. For two weeks in mid-June, the roadsides, old fields, and meadows fill with tall spires of purple, pink, and white blooms, producing one of the most vivid floral displays in the region. The annual Lupine Festival, timed to the peak bloom, includes farm tours, artist open studios, and guided wildflower walks. If you can visit only once, make it June.

Harman's Cheese and Country Store on Route 117 has been operating continuously since 1955 and ships its aged sharp cheddar nationwide, but buying a wedge in person — still wrapped in the same minimal fashion — is the thing to do. The cheese is aged at least two years and is genuinely exceptional. Polly's Pancake Parlor, a Sugar Hill institution since 1938, serves breakfast made from stone-ground flours milled on site; the buckwheat and cornmeal pancakes are the ones to order.

The village itself is quiet and uncommercialised by design — there is no resort infrastructure here, just the store, the restaurant, a few inns, and the views. It makes a pleasant half-day excursion from Littleton, best combined with a drive along the Franconia Notch corridor on the way back.
