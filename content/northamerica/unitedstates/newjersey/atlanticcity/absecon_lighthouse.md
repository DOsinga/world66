The tallest lighthouse in New Jersey and one of the most historic in the country stands at the northern end of Atlantic City, a reminder of what this barrier island looked like before the boardwalk and the casinos arrived. Built in 1857, the brick tower rises 56 metres and has 228 steps to the top. When the light was in operation it could be seen 19 nautical miles out to sea.

The lighthouse is open to visitors who can climb the spiral stair to the lantern room for a panoramic view of the city, the barrier island, and the coast stretching north and south. The surrounding keeper's quarters have been restored and include a small museum covering the lighthouse's history and the evolution of navigation on this stretch of coast. It sits in a quiet residential neighbourhood at the north end of the island, which makes for an interesting contrast with the boardwalk a few blocks away.
