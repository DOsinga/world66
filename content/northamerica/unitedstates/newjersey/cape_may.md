Cape May sits at the very southern tip of New Jersey, where the Delaware Bay meets the Atlantic. It is one of the oldest seaside resorts in the United States — presidents from Lincoln to Harrison holidayed here — and the entire town is a National Historic Landmark district, packed with elaborate Victorian houses painted in bright colours.

The [beach](/northamerica/unitedstates/newjersey/cape_may/cape_may_beach) is wide and sandy, and the town has a genteel, unhurried quality that feels more New England than New Jersey. Walking the streets past the gingerbread-trimmed cottages and grand old hotels — like the restored [Congress Hall](/northamerica/unitedstates/newjersey/cape_may/congress_hall) and the [Emlen Physick Estate](/northamerica/unitedstates/newjersey/cape_may/emlen_physick_estate) — is the main activity. The [Washington Street Mall](/northamerica/unitedstates/newjersey/cape_may/washington_street_mall), a pedestrian shopping street, runs through the centre. The 1859 [Cape May Lighthouse](/northamerica/unitedstates/newjersey/cape_may/cape_may_lighthouse) at the point is climbable, and the concrete [WWII bunker on the beach](/northamerica/unitedstates/newjersey/cape_may/wwii_bunker) and the wreck of the [SS Atlantus at Sunset Beach](/northamerica/unitedstates/newjersey/cape_may/sunset_beach) are atmospheric reminders of the bay's strategic past.

Cape May is also one of the best birdwatching spots on the East Coast, sitting on a major migratory flyway. In autumn, hawks, warblers, and shorebirds pass through in enormous numbers. The [Cape May Bird Observatory](/northamerica/unitedstates/newjersey/cape_may/cape_may_bird_observatory) draws serious birders from across the country.

The off-season (October through April) is worth considering: the Victorian charm is still there, the crowds are not, and prices drop considerably.
