Lambertville sits on the New Jersey side of the Delaware River, directly opposite New Hope, Pennsylvania, connected by a pedestrian bridge. The two towns together form one of the most visited antiques and arts destinations in the Mid-Atlantic — Lambertville is the quieter of the pair, with Victorian and Federal townhouses lining streets that have changed little since the 19th century.

The town calls itself the "Antique Capital of New Jersey," and it makes a reasonable case: the number of dealers, galleries, and antique shops concentrated in a few blocks is impressive, ranging from 18th-century American furniture to mid-century modern. The [People's Store Antiques Center](/northamerica/unitedstates/newjersey/lambertville/peoples_store) is the largest single venue, with over 50 dealers spread across a four-storey building dating to 1839. The art scene is genuine too — local galleries show work by established regional artists rather than the tourist-market prints that fill similar towns.

The [Delaware and Raritan Canal towpath](/northamerica/unitedstates/newjersey/lambertville/delaware_raritan_canal) runs through town, offering a flat, car-free path good for walking, jogging, and cycling — one of the better linear parks in New Jersey. The Delaware River is nearby for canoe rentals and tubing in summer. The [New Hope-Lambertville Bridge](/northamerica/unitedstates/newjersey/lambertville/new_hope_lambertville_bridge) is worth crossing on foot for the views of the river and the easy access to New Hope's restaurants and nightlife when Lambertville's slower pace is not enough.
