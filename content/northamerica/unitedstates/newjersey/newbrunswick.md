New Brunswick is a small city on the Raritan River, defined above all by [Rutgers University](/northamerica/unitedstates/newjersey/newbrunswick/rutgers_university) — the State University of New Jersey, whose campus sprawls across much of the town. The result is a college-town atmosphere with a good mix of cheap food, live music, and a surprisingly active theatre scene centred on the [State Theatre](/northamerica/unitedstates/newjersey/newbrunswick/state_theatre) and the [George Street Playhouse](/northamerica/unitedstates/newjersey/newbrunswick/george_street_playhouse) on Livingston Avenue.

The Dutch founded the town in the 17th century, and a handful of colonial-era buildings survive in the old centre around Neilson Street — [Christ Church](/northamerica/unitedstates/newjersey/newbrunswick/christ_church), the [First Reformed Church](/northamerica/unitedstates/newjersey/newbrunswick/first_reformed_church), and a clutch of brick and brownstone houses. The free [Zimmerli Art Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/newjersey/newbrunswick/zimmerli_art_museum) on the Rutgers campus is the best museum for an hour or two, and the [Rutgers Geology Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/newjersey/newbrunswick/rutgers_geology_museum) in Geology Hall is a delightful Victorian-era one-room cabinet of natural history.

Trains run every half hour to New York City from the [station](/northamerica/unitedstates/newjersey/newbrunswick/new_brunswick_station) on Route 27, making New Brunswick an easy day trip or stopover.
