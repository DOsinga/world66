Eugenides spent two decades teaching at Princeton's Lewis Center for the Arts, and *The Marriage Plot* — his 2011 campus novel — is steeped in the look and feel of an east-coast Ivy League education in the early 1980s. The novel follows three Brown University seniors through their last weeks of college and the year after, but the cadences are unmistakably the cadences of Princeton's eating clubs, semiotics seminars, and graduate-student gossip.

A useful book for anyone trying to understand why so much of the American novel is set on small college campuses: the action is small, the stakes are smaller, and what Eugenides catches better than almost anyone is how a few inches of academic infatuation can derail the rest of a life.
