Trenton is more historically dense than most visitors expect. The Revolutionary War sites alone justify a half-day: Washington's crossing and the battle that followed happened here, and three of the key sites — the Old Barracks, the State House, and the battlefield monument — are within easy walking distance of each other. The [New Jersey State Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/newjersey/trenton/new_jersey_state_museum) is free and broader than its name suggests, covering natural history and fine art alongside the expected state history.

The industrial legacy is equally compelling. [John Roebling's wire works](/northamerica/unitedstates/newjersey/trenton/roebling_wire_works), where the cables for the Brooklyn Bridge were made, still stands on the south side of town. The famous [Trenton Makes bridge sign](/northamerica/unitedstates/newjersey/trenton/trenton_makes_bridge) — lit in red neon — spans the Delaware and has become the city's most recognisable image.

For neighbourhood character, [Mill Hill](/northamerica/unitedstates/newjersey/trenton/mill_hill) has the best-preserved 18th and 19th century streetscapes, while [Chambersburg](/northamerica/unitedstates/newjersey/trenton/chambersburg) to the south retains its Italian-American identity with bakeries and old-school trattorias.
