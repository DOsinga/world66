Clovis is a town of about 32,000 near the Texas border, surrounded by cattle ranches and open plains. It is not a prime tourist destination, but it works as a stopover between New Mexico's northern mountains and its southern deserts. Clovis is also home to Cannon Air Force Base, and the town that gave the Clovis archaeological culture its name — some of the earliest evidence of human habitation in North America was found at [Blackwater Draw](/northamerica/unitedstates/newmexico/clovis/blackwater_draw), twenty miles to the south.

The downtown has a tight cluster of New Deal–era buildings worth a slow walk: the Art Deco [Curry County Courthouse](/northamerica/unitedstates/newmexico/clovis/curry_county_courthouse), the restored [State Theater](/northamerica/unitedstates/newmexico/clovis/state_theater), the towering [Hotel Clovis](/northamerica/unitedstates/newmexico/clovis/hotel_clovis), and the old 1920 [Lyceum Theater](/northamerica/unitedstates/newmexico/clovis/lyceum_theater). The town's most consequential building, however, is [Norman Petty Recording Studios](/northamerica/unitedstates/newmexico/clovis/norman_petty_studios), the small concrete-block recording shed where Buddy Holly cut "That'll Be the Day" and most of his catalogue. A green-chile cheeseburger at the 1952 drive-in [Foxy](/northamerica/unitedstates/newmexico/clovis/foxys_drive_in) closes the day.
