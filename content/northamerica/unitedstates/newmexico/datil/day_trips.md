---
title: 'Day Trips'
type: section
---

**Pie Town** (22 miles west on US-60) is a hamlet almost as small as Datil but with an outsized reputation, mostly built on the Pie-O-Neer café and its rotating selection of New Mexico–style pies. The drive west from Datil climbs through the Datil Mountains on a beautiful stretch of highway. Pie Town was famously documented by photographer Russell Lee for the Farm Security Administration in 1940, and the images of Depression-era homesteaders are displayed in the café.

**Gila Wilderness** — the first designated wilderness area in the United States, designated in 1924 at the instigation of Aldo Leopold — begins about 50 miles south of Datil and covers more than 500,000 acres of the Mogollon Mountains. The Gila Cliff Dwellings National Monument (around 90 miles by road from Datil via NM-12 and NM-35) protects cliff dwellings built by the Mogollon people around 1280 CE, reached by a short trail through a canyon. Allow a full day; the roads are good but slow.

**Magdalena** (40 miles east on US-60) is the nearest proper town, with a gas station, grocery, and the quiet charm of a 19th-century cattle-shipping hub that hasn't been over-restored. The Magdalena Mountains rise immediately above town, and the Langmuir Research Site atop South Baldy Peak (operated by New Mexico Tech) is visible from town. Socorro, another 25 miles east, is the regional hub with full services, restaurants, a university campus, and the Bosque del Apache National Wildlife Refuge a short drive south — one of the best places in the USA to see sandhill crane and snow goose migrations in autumn and winter.
