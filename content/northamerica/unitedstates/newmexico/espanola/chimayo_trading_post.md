The Chimayo Trading Post and E.D. Trujillo House is a historic commercial building that has been listed on the National Register of Historic Places. The structure dates to the early 20th century and represents the kind of small-scale trading post architecture that once characterised the rural commercial landscape of northern New Mexico, where Native American, Hispanic, and Anglo cultures met in the marketplace.

The building served as a point of exchange for the distinctive weaving traditions of the Española Valley, particularly the Rio Grande blankets and Chimayo weavings produced by local Hispanic artisans. Chimayo weaving is one of the most distinctive craft traditions in the Southwest, using treadle looms brought over by the Spanish colonists and adapted with local designs and natural dyes over three centuries. The trading post is part of a broader cultural landscape in the valley where colonial-era craft traditions are still practiced by families who can trace their weaving heritage back generations.
