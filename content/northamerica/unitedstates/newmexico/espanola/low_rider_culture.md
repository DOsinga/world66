Española is widely recognised as the Low Rider Capital of the World, a title the town takes seriously. The low rider tradition here is not a subculture at the margins but a central expression of the region's Chicano and Hispanic identity, with roots in the 1940s and 1950s when young men began customising their cars as a form of artistic and cultural pride.

The best time to see the cars is on a warm Friday or Saturday evening along Riverside Drive, where the cruising culture has persisted for decades despite periodic attempts by local authorities to restrict it. The cars on display range from immaculately preserved classics from the 1950s and 1960s to recent builds featuring elaborate airbrushed murals, velvet interiors, and hydraulic systems that can make the car hop on command. The lowrider culture here directly inspired the famous hydraulic-hopping cars scene that later spread to California, and enthusiasts from around the country visit Española specifically to experience the genuine article. Several local shops along Paseo de Oñate do restoration and custom work, and the owners are generally happy to talk about their craft.
