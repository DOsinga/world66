The Chief Theater on West Coal Avenue is one of the finest surviving examples of Pueblo Deco architecture in New Mexico — a style that emerged in the 1920s and 30s, blending Art Deco geometry with the forms and motifs of Southwestern Pueblo architecture. Built in 1920 and listed on the National Register of Historic Places, it represents the moment when Gallup was a genuine boomtown, flush with railroad money and trading post wealth.

The building has changed hands and uses several times over the decades, but the exterior remains intact and the distinctive Pueblo Deco detailing is clearly visible. The old commercial district along Coal Avenue and Route 66 has a number of buildings from this era — the Gallup Commercial Historic District was listed on the National Register in 2016, recognising 65 contributing historic structures — and the Chief Theater is the standout among them.
