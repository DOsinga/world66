The Basilica of San Albino stands on the north side of the Mesilla Plaza, its twin bell towers visible across the flat valley landscape for a considerable distance. The current building dates from 1906, but the site has held a church since the 1850s when the original mission was established for the farming community of Mesilla. The basilica was elevated to minor basilica status in 1983, one of only four in New Mexico.

The interior is relatively modest by basilica standards — this is a working parish church in a small town, not a major pilgrimage site — but the whitewashed walls, wooden pews, and painted wooden retablos create an atmosphere of genuine devotion that tourist-oriented churches often lack. Services are held in Spanish, which reflects the predominant culture of the surrounding community.

The plaza setting amplifies the basilica's presence. Sitting on the plaza benches facing the church, with the mountains behind you and the old adobe buildings to each side, is one of the more evocative experiences the Southwest has to offer.
