The Organ Mountains rise abruptly from the desert floor east of Las Cruces, with the highest point (Organ Needle) reaching 2,727 metres. The national monument, established in 2014, protects the mountains and the surrounding desert landscape — canyons, arroyos, lava flows, and grasslands that stretch south toward the Mexican border. The range is technically part of the southern Rocky Mountains, though it feels isolated enough from the main range to be its own distinct world.

Hiking here ranges from easy desert walks to serious technical climbing on the granite spires. The Dripping Springs trail (easy, about 5 km round trip) leads to a historic sanatorium in a canyon mouth with good views and spring wildflowers. The Pine Tree Trail climbs higher into the mountain with juniper and piñon forests. For serious climbers, the Organ Needles offer multi-pitch routes on solid granite.

The monument is also excellent for wildlife. Mule deer, javelinas, coyotes, and roadrunners are common; golden eagles hunt the ridgelines; and the desert tortoise inhabits the lower slopes. Spring (March–May) is the best time for wildflowers and cooler hiking temperatures. Summer is hot but manageable in the early morning.
