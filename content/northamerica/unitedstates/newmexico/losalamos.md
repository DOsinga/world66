Los Alamos is best known as the birthplace of the atomic bomb, and the [Los Alamos National Laboratory](https://www.lanl.gov/) still dominates the town. But the real draw for visitors is [Bandelier National Monument](/northamerica/unitedstates/newmexico/losalamos/bandelier_national_monument), located between [Santa Fe](/northamerica/unitedstates/newmexico/santafe) and Los Alamos. Bandelier preserves Ancestral Pueblo dwellings in sheer-walled canyons and across forested mesas — carved directly into the volcanic tuff by people who lived here from around 1150 to 1550 CE.

The town itself was built from scratch in 1943 by the Manhattan Project, when the U.S. Army took over the site of the Los Alamos Ranch School and erected a secret city on the Pajarito Plateau. The [Bradbury Science Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/newmexico/losalamos/bradbury_science_museum) covers the Project's history in depth and is free to visit. The [Los Alamos Historical Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/newmexico/losalamos/los_alamos_historical_museum) adds the civilian side — what it was like to live in a classified community that didn't officially exist.

Beyond the history, Los Alamos sits in exceptional landscape. The [Valles Caldera](/northamerica/unitedstates/newmexico/losalamos/valles_caldera) — a 22-kilometre volcanic crater turned summer elk pasture — is 15 miles west. The Jemez Mountains offer hiking and, in winter, skiing at [Pajarito Mountain](/northamerica/unitedstates/newmexico/losalamos/pajarito_mountain). The altitude is around 2,200 metres, which keeps the summers cool and the light clear.
