---
title: Day Trips
type: section
---

Ramah's remote location makes it a good base for exploring a stretch of western New Mexico that most visitors skip entirely. The area within an hour's drive is unusually rich.

**El Morro National Monument** (30 miles southwest) is the standout stop — a sandstone bluff covered in inscriptions from Ancestral Puebloan petroglyphs to Spanish colonial signatures to 19th-century pioneer names. Worth at least half a day.

**Zuni Pueblo** (25 miles west) is one of the largest and most culturally intact pueblos in New Mexico, with a permanent population of around 10,000. The historic A:shiwi mission church, rebuilt after its destruction in the Pueblo Revolt of 1680, is one of the most striking adobe buildings in the Southwest. The pueblo welcomes respectful visitors but has restrictions on photography; call ahead.

**El Malpais National Monument** (40 miles northeast near Grants) protects a landscape of lava flows, sandstone bluffs, and ice caves. The contrast between the black basalt and the red sandstone cliffs is extraordinary.
