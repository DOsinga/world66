Santa Fe is the oldest state capital in the United States, founded in 1610, and it wears those four centuries with effortless style. The entire downtown is built in adobe -- by law -- giving the city a warm, earth-toned character unlike any other American city. Add world-class art galleries on [Canyon Road](/northamerica/unitedstates/newmexico/santafe/canyon_road), the celebrated [Santa Fe Opera](/northamerica/unitedstates/newmexico/santafe/santa_fe_opera), and some of the best restaurants in the Southwest, and you have a place that punches far above its weight.

The [Plaza](/northamerica/unitedstates/newmexico/santafe/santa_fe_plaza) is the historic heart of town, where Native American artisans from over twenty tribes sell handmade jewelry and crafts daily under the portal of the [Palace of the Governors](/northamerica/unitedstates/newmexico/santafe/palace_of_the_governors). From here, narrow streets wind past galleries, churches like the [Cathedral Basilica](/northamerica/unitedstates/newmexico/santafe/cathedral_basilica) and the [Loretto Chapel](/northamerica/unitedstates/newmexico/santafe/loretto_chapel), and boutiques. The city has an almost European feel -- walkable, beautiful, and very easy to spend money in.

Santa Fe sits at 7,000 feet, so the air is thin and the light is extraordinary -- which explains why artists have been drawn here for over a century. Summers are warm and dry with afternoon thunderstorms, while winters bring snow and good skiing at Ski Santa Fe, just a short drive up the mountain.

Beyond the art and architecture, Santa Fe is a gateway to the pueblos of northern New Mexico, the hot springs at [Ojo Caliente](/northamerica/unitedstates/newmexico/ojo_caliente), and the high desert landscapes that stretch in every direction. It is not a cheap destination, but it is a deeply rewarding one.
