**Death Comes for the Archbishop** by Willa Cather. A 1927 novel built around the historical Archbishop Lamy, the French cleric who tried — with little success — to bring Roman Catholic order to the Hispanic and Pueblo Catholicism of nineteenth-century New Mexico. The slow, observed prose and the spare descriptions of the New Mexican landscape are foundational. Read it before walking into the Cathedral Basilica.

**The Milagro Beanfield War** by John Nichols. A 1974 comic novel of land, water, and Hispanic-Anglo conflict in a fictional northern New Mexico village. Funny, angry, and the best fictional portrait of how Hispano New Mexicans see their own history.

**Bless Me, Ultima** by Rudolfo Anaya. A coming-of-age novel set in 1940s eastern New Mexico, anchored in curanderismo (folk medicine) and the Catholic-pagan layered religion of the rural Hispano communities. A staple of New Mexican high schools.

**Anything by Tony Hillerman.** The Navajo detective novels (*A Thief of Time*, *Dance Hall of the Dead*) are set across the Navajo and Hopi reservations west of here, but they capture the high-desert New Mexico atmosphere better than any travel book.
