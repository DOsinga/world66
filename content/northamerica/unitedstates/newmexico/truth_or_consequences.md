In local parlance, it's called "T or C", and lies about two hours south of Albuquerque on Interstate 25. The former city of Hot Springs, Truth or Consequences is an interesting little town. Named after the television show of the 1950s, it's one of the more unusual places in the American West. The old bathhouses and hot springs still carry on in the historic downtown district, and the town has developed a genuine art scene alongside the spa trade. Many old buildings have been lovingly restored and now serve as restaurants, delis, bed and breakfast lodges, and galleries.

The hot springs are the main draw. Downtown T or C sits atop a large geothermal aquifer that pushes mineral-rich water to the surface at around 107°F. About a dozen bathhouses and spas have grown up around this resource over the decades, ranging from the historic indoor tubs of [Blackstone Hotsprings](/northamerica/unitedstates/newmexico/truth_or_consequences/blackstone_hotsprings) to the open-air riverside pools of [Riverbend Hot Springs](/northamerica/unitedstates/newmexico/truth_or_consequences/riverbend_hot_springs), which lets you soak with the Rio Grande flowing past below.

For boaters and water recreation enthusiasts, the city is important due to its closeness to [Elephant Butte Lake](/northamerica/unitedstates/newmexico/truth_or_consequences/elephant_butte_lake), the biggest reservoir in New Mexico. The nearby Gila National Forest is a draw for outdoor enthusiasts, and several of New Mexico's better ghost towns are close by. The [Geronimo Springs Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/newmexico/truth_or_consequences/geronimo_springs_museum) downtown tells the story of Apache history, local geology, and the improbable radio-show bet that gave the city its name.

For the curious, [Spaceport America](/northamerica/unitedstates/newmexico/truth_or_consequences/spaceport_america) — the world's first purpose-built commercial spaceport — sits about 45 miles south, accessible on a guided tour. The drive out through the Jornada del Muerto desert is striking in its own right.
