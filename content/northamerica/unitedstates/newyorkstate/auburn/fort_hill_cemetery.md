Fort Hill Cemetery occupies a prominent hilltop in Auburn on the site of a former Cayuga Indian fortification. Established in 1851, it was designed in the Romantic landscape tradition then popular in American cemetery design — rolling terrain, winding paths, mature trees, and views across the city — and contains the graves of many of Auburn's most notable historical figures.

Harriet Tubman is buried here, in a section near the entrance, and her grave has become a site of pilgrimage. William H. Seward's tomb is also in the cemetery, as are the graves of other figures from Auburn's 19th-century civic and industrial history. A walk through the cemetery is effectively a walk through the city's past.

The hilltop location gives views over Auburn and the surrounding Finger Lakes countryside. The cemetery is open during daylight hours and admission is free. A map identifying the locations of notable graves is available at the entrance.
