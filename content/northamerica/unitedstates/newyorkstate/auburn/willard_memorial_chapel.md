The Willard Memorial Chapel is one of the most extraordinary interiors in upstate New York and one of the most significant surviving works of Louis Comfort Tiffany. Built in 1894, the chapel was designed entirely by Tiffany's studio — windows, mosaics, lamps, woodwork, and furnishings — creating a unified decorative scheme that no other surviving Tiffany space can match.

The opalescent glass windows are the centrepiece: ten windows depicting biblical scenes, rendered in Tiffany's characteristic style of layered glass that glows from within when backlit by sunlight. The mosaic work on the walls and floor is equally refined. The building is small and intimate; the density of craftsmanship concentrated into its compact space is remarkable.

The chapel operates as a museum and is open for guided tours. Admission is modest; check opening hours, as they are limited. The chapel adjoins the Welch Memorial Hall, which is architecturally connected but less ornate. For anyone even slightly interested in American decorative arts, the Willard Chapel is not to be missed.
