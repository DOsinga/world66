The William H. Seward House Museum is the preserved home of William Henry Seward, one of the most consequential American statesmen of the 19th century. Seward was Lincoln's Secretary of State, a leading opponent of slavery who had himself been a serious contender for the Republican presidential nomination in 1860, and the man who negotiated the Alaska Purchase from Russia in 1867 — an acquisition so controversial it was nicknamed "Seward's Folly."

The house, a Federal-style mansion on South Street, has been preserved as it was during Seward's lifetime, with original furnishings, correspondence, and memorabilia from his long career. Guided tours trace both Seward's political life and his role in Auburn's community, including his early support for Harriet Tubman when she settled in the city.

The museum holds significant archival material on the abolitionist movement, the Civil War, and Reconstruction-era politics. For visitors interested in American history, the combination of this house and the Harriet Tubman National Historical Park makes Auburn a remarkably rich stop.
