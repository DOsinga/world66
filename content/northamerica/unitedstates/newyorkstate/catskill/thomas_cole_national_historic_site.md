Thomas Cole's home and studio, known as Cedar Grove, is the birthplace of the Hudson River School — America's first major landscape painting movement. Cole arrived here in 1836 and spent the rest of his life painting the scenery of the Catskills and Hudson Valley from this spot. The site preserves two studios, the Federal-style house, and grounds that feel much as they did when Cole walked them.

The main house is filled with Cole's furniture, letters, and personal effects, while the Old Studio holds some of his small-scale study paintings. The New Studio, built after Cole's death, was used by his students and followers. Guided tours run regularly and do a good job of explaining not just Cole's life but the broader significance of landscape painting in 19th-century American culture — at a time when nature was seen as evidence of God's presence, these paintings were understood as something close to religious works.

The house sits at the edge of the village on Spring Street, within easy walking distance of the main street.

**Address:** 218 Spring Street, Catskill, NY  
**Hours:** May–November, Wednesday–Sunday; check the website for exact hours  
**Admission:** Adults $15, students and seniors $12
