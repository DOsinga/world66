Hudson has one of the more colourful backstories of any small city in New York State. Founded in 1785 by a group of Nantucket whalers looking for a river port safe from British warships, it boomed through the early 19th century as a whaling and trading centre. After the whaling industry collapsed it reinvented itself — rather notoriously — as a semi-official centre for gambling and prostitution that operated openly until a state police raid in 1950. Since then it has transformed once more, this time into one of the most talked-about small cities in the Hudson River Valley.

Just 2.5 square miles in area, the city is a walkable catalogue of American architectural styles — early Federal and Greek Revival houses from the founding decade sit alongside Italianate mansions and Victorian commercial buildings, most of them handsomely restored. The entire downtown is a National Register Historic District. Warren Street, almost exactly a mile long, is the main draw: it became known in the 1980s for its dense cluster of antiques shops when rents were low and buildings were empty, and has since diversified into some of the best restaurants in the region, galleries, boutiques, and independent bookshops.

The cultural scene punches well above the town's weight. Hudson Hall occupies the beautifully restored 1855 Opera House on Warren Street and runs a year-round programme of music, theatre, and literary events. Basilica Hudson — a former ironworks near the train station — hosts larger contemporary arts events and one of the valley's best markets. And just south of town, Olana, the Persian-inspired hilltop estate of Hudson River School painter Frederic Church, offers some of the finest views of the Catskill Mountains and the river below.

The town is genuinely diverse — African-American, Bangladeshi, Latino, and LGBTQ+ communities all have a visible presence — and that social mix gives Hudson an energy that purely gentrified towns lack. It is two hours from New York Penn Station by Amtrak, making it an unusually rail-accessible weekend destination; the station drops you at the foot of Warren Street.
