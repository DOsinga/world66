The Robert H. Jackson Center occupies the old Cathouse mansion on North Main Street, a turreted Victorian pile that has been converted into a museum and conference centre dedicated to Jackson's legacy. Exhibits cover his journey from a small-town Jamestown lawyer to the Supreme Court, his role at Nuremberg, and his major opinions on press freedom, executive power, and civil rights.

It is a small, serious museum — at its best when international scholars and lawyers visit for events, but the permanent displays make a strong case on a quiet morning. Free to enter, donations welcome; check the schedule for lectures and screenings.
