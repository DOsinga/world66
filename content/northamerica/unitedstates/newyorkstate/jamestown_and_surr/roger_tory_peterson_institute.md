The Roger Tory Peterson Institute of Natural History sits on a wooded site at the northern edge of Jamestown, on the road towards Chautauqua Lake. The building, designed by Robert A. M. Stern, houses Peterson's personal archive of paintings, photographs and field notes, plus changing exhibitions on natural history illustration.

The grounds — about 27 acres of trails through second-growth forest — are open to walkers and are a quiet place to spot the birds Peterson grew up watching. A small shop sells signed prints and the field guides he is famous for. Free admission to the grounds; modest charge for the museum.
