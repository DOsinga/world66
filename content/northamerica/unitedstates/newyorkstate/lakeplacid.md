Lake Placid is a small Adirondack village that twice hosted the Winter Olympics — in 1932 and 1980 — and it has never quite let go of that identity. The Olympic venues are still in use: you can ride the bobsled run at [Mount Van Hoevenberg](/northamerica/unitedstates/newyorkstate/lakeplacid/mt_van_hoevenberg), watch ski jumpers train at the [90-metre towers](/northamerica/unitedstates/newyorkstate/lakeplacid/ski_jumping_complex), and skate on the same oval where Eric Heiden won five gold medals. The [Olympic Center](/northamerica/unitedstates/newyorkstate/lakeplacid/herb_brooks_arena) on Main Street houses the rink where the US hockey team beat the Soviets in the Miracle on Ice; the [Olympic Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/newyorkstate/lakeplacid/olympic_museum) next door tells the full story.

Beyond the Olympic legacy, Lake Placid is simply a beautiful mountain town. [Mirror Lake](/northamerica/unitedstates/newyorkstate/lakeplacid/mirror_lake), right in the village centre, is perfect for swimming in summer and skating in winter. [Whiteface Mountain](/northamerica/unitedstates/newyorkstate/lakeplacid/whiteface_mountain), the tallest ski area in the eastern US, is twenty minutes away. The surrounding [High Peaks Wilderness](/northamerica/unitedstates/newyorkstate/lakeplacid/high_peaks) has some of the best hiking in the northeast — the Adirondack 46ers, the peaks above 4,000 feet, are a classic challenge.

South of town, [John Brown Farm](/northamerica/unitedstates/newyorkstate/lakeplacid/john_brown_farm) is the home and burial place of the abolitionist who led the Harper's Ferry raid in 1859 — a visit that adds historical depth to the Olympic landscape. The village itself is walkable, with a Main Street full of outdoors shops, restaurants, and the kind of cosy bars you want after a cold day on the mountain.
