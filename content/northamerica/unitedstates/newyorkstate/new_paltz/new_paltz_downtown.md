The downtown core of New Paltz is compact enough to walk completely in an afternoon, but dense enough with bookshops, cafes, galleries, and restaurants to occupy a full day. Main Street is the spine, running between the old train station and the village green, lined with a mix of 19th-century commercial buildings and newer storefronts. The character is emphatically college-town: informal, politically progressive, and welcoming to anyone.

The area immediately around the intersection of Main Street and Huguenot Street is the social center of the village, where foot traffic concentrates on weekend afternoons. A few blocks off the main drag in any direction, the streets get quieter and more residential, with Victorian-era houses and the occasional antique store. The village holds a farmers market on Sunday mornings in summer on the village green — a good way to pick up Hudson Valley produce, local cheese, and the general social atmosphere of a place that takes its local food seriously.
