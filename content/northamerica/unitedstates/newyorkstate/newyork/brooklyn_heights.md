Brooklyn Heights is the most architecturally coherent of Brooklyn's old neighbourhoods, a compact grid of Federal, Greek Revival and Italianate rowhouses that dates from the 1820s and 1830s, when Brooklyn was a separate city and its heights overlooking New York Harbour made it a desirable address for Manhattan merchants. The neighbourhood became the first in New York City to receive historic district designation in 1965, and the main residential streets — Pierrepont, Montague, Remsen, Joralemon — have barely changed since. Walking them gives a more authentic sense of 19th-century bourgeois New York than almost anywhere else in the five boroughs.

The **Brooklyn Heights Promenade** is one of the great urban vantage points on the East Coast: a cantilevered esplanade running for three blocks along the cliff above the Brooklyn-Queens Expressway, looking directly across the East River at the Lower Manhattan skyline and the Brooklyn Bridge. The view is best at dusk, when the buildings light up against a fading sky. Below the promenade, **Brooklyn Bridge Park** has converted former commercial piers into lawns, playgrounds, kayak launches and a restored carousel. **Montague Street** is the neighbourhood's commercial strip, modest in scale, with a bookshop, a few restaurants and the kind of dry cleaner and hardware store that have been driven out of most of the borough by rising rents. The neighbourhood adjoins **DUMBO** to the north and **Cobble Hill** to the south.
