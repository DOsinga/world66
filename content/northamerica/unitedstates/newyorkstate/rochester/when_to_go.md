---
title: When to Go
type: section
---

Rochester has four genuine seasons, though "genuine" in this context includes winters that can be severe. The city sits in the snow belt south of Lake Ontario, and lake-effect snowstorms from November through March can dump a foot of snow in a day. Locals are stoic and well-equipped; visitors should be aware that winter travel can be disrupted, though the city remains fully functional.

**May** is the finest month to visit. The **Lilac Festival** in Highland Park runs for about ten days in mid-May, when over 1,200 lilac varieties bloom simultaneously and the park fills with music, food vendors, and a genuinely festive atmosphere. The festival is free and draws up to half a million visitors — book accommodation early if you're timing a trip around it.

**June through September** is pleasant: warm but rarely oppressive, with outdoor farmers' markets in full swing and the Genesee trails accessible for hiking and cycling. **September** brings the Clothesline Festival at the Memorial Art Gallery, one of the region's largest outdoor art fairs.

**October** is beautiful for fall foliage — the gorge trail below High Falls is spectacular under autumn colour — though temperatures drop quickly toward the end of the month. **November through March** is winter, with the understanding that winter here means business. If you come in February, dress accordingly and lean into the fact that indoor Rochester — the George Eastman Museum, the Strong, the market — is genuinely excellent.
