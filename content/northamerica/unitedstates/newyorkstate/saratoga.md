Saratoga Springs is a small city in upstate New York best known for its thoroughbred horse racing — the [Saratoga Race Course](/northamerica/unitedstates/newyorkstate/saratoga/saratoga_race_course), open each August, is one of the oldest and most prestigious tracks in the country. But the city has far more going on than horse racing, and it rewards a visit year-round.

The town grew up around its natural mineral springs, which still bubble up in several public springs in [Congress Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/newyorkstate/saratoga/congress_park) and the [Saratoga Spa State Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/newyorkstate/saratoga/saratoga_spa_state_park). The park also houses the [Saratoga Performing Arts Center](/northamerica/unitedstates/newyorkstate/saratoga/spac) (SPAC), a major outdoor venue for the New York City Ballet and Philadelphia Orchestra in summer. [Yaddo](/northamerica/unitedstates/newyorkstate/saratoga/yaddo), the famous artists' retreat, occupies a grand estate on the edge of town.

[Downtown Broadway](/northamerica/unitedstates/newyorkstate/saratoga/broadway) is walkable and lively, with Victorian architecture, independent shops, restaurants, and cafes. The folk music club [Caffè Lena](/northamerica/unitedstates/newyorkstate/saratoga/caffe_lena) has been operating since 1960. The [Saratoga National Historical Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/newyorkstate/saratoga/saratoga_national_historical_park), 12 miles south, preserves the site of the 1777 Battle of Saratoga — the turning point of the American Revolution. The Adirondack Park begins just to the north, and the city sits at exits 13–15 of Interstate 87 (Adirondack Northway).
