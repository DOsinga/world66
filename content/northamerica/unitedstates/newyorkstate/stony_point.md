Stony Point is a northern Rockland County town on the west bank of the Hudson, far enough from New York City to feel more river-and-woods than suburb. It is not a full weekend destination, but it clears the day-trip bar because its best sight is genuinely important: the point where American troops stormed a British position in 1779.

The [Stony Point Battlefield](/northamerica/unitedstates/newyorkstate/stony_point/stony_point_battlefield) is the reason to come. The headland has Revolutionary War earthworks, Hudson views, and the [Stony Point Light](/northamerica/unitedstates/newyorkstate/stony_point/stony_point_light), the oldest lighthouse on the river. Inland, the western edge of town touches the woods and lakes of Harriman State Park.

Treat Stony Point as a focused Hudson Valley stop rather than a town to browse all day. Pair the battlefield with a walk in the parkland, a performance at Penguin Rep if one is on, or a wider trip through Haverstraw, Bear Mountain, and the lower Hudson.
