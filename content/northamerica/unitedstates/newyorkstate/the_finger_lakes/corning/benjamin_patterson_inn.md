The Benjamin Patterson Inn Museum Complex preserves a group of historic structures from the late 18th and early 19th centuries, including a restored 1796 inn, a log cabin, a barn, a schoolhouse, and a one-room schoolhouse. The inn was a stopping point on the route between the Chemung Valley and the western frontier during the early settlement period of the Southern Tier.

The buildings have been relocated and restored on a single site to provide an accessible overview of domestic life and travel in upstate New York around the turn of the 19th century. Guided tours are available and include demonstrations of period crafts and household activities. The complex is run as a living history museum and is especially popular with school groups.

The site provides an interesting counterpoint to Corning's glass museums — a glimpse of the region before the industrial era that the glass industry represented. It is a short drive north of downtown.

**Address:** 59 W Pulteney St, Corning, NY 14830. **Phone:** 607-937-5281.
