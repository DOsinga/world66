The Corning Museum of Glass is one of the finest specialty museums in the United States, holding the world's largest collection of glass art and artefacts — more than 50,000 objects covering 35 centuries of glassmaking history. The museum occupies a sprawling complex north of downtown Corning, with separate galleries for historical glassware, contemporary studio glass, and the science and technology of glass manufacturing.

The Hot Glass Show is the most popular element: several times daily, skilled gaffers shape molten glass at over 2,000°F in front of a live audience, demonstrating techniques that have changed relatively little since the Roman period. The Sculpture Studio and Innovation Center give visitors the chance to make their own glass objects — from simple paperweights to more ambitious projects — under expert guidance. The contemporary glass galleries feature major works by Dale Chihuly, Lino Tagliapietra, and other leading studio glass artists. The historical collections include Roman, Islamic, Venetian, and Bohemian glass that charts the full development of the craft.

Allow a full half-day minimum; many visitors spend the entire day. The museum café is good. The complex includes a glass shop that sells everything from $10 ornaments to significant artworks.

**Address:** One Museum Way, Corning, NY 14830. **Hours:** 9am–5pm daily (until 8pm in summer). **Phone:** 1-800-732-6845. **Web:** [cmog.org](https://www.cmog.org)
