The wooden horses were carved at North Tonawanda and shipped on the railroad in pieces; some still ride at Coney Island, Hershey Park, and several hundred lesser parks across North America."
---

The factory where Allan Herschell built carousel horses for half a century is now a working museum: you can ride a fully restored 1916 #1 Special carousel, watch the carving shop in action, and walk through workshops where the chisels, paint kits, and original templates still hang on the walls. A separate gallery covers the mechanical music history of North Tonawanda — the city was also home to Wurlitzer jukeboxes and theatre organs.

A second floor contains a hands-on kids' gallery and a miniature North Tonawanda made of toy buildings and a running model carousel. The museum is open from April through December and is the single best thing to do in town.
