The NASCAR Hall of Fame in Uptown Charlotte is the definitive museum for anyone interested in American motorsport, and worth visiting even for those who think they are not. The building itself is striking — a swooping glass structure next to the convention center — and inside, the exhibits cover the full sweep of stock car racing from its moonshining roots in the Carolina hills to its present status as one of the most popular spectator sports in the United States.

Interactive simulators let you feel what it is like to take a turn at racing speed, and the main gallery displays a rotating collection of legendary cars. The hall of fame induction ceremony, held each year in Charlotte, brings the racing world's elite to town. Charlotte is NASCAR's home city — dozens of team headquarters and race shops are based in and around the city, so the context here is real rather than staged.

story: "NASCAR's origins in the Carolina hill country are genuine: during Prohibition, drivers built fast, heavily modified cars to outrun federal agents while running illegal moonshine. The skills developed — quick cornering, mechanical improvisation, running in close quarters at speed — translated directly into the racing circuit when it was formally organized in 1948 by Bill France Sr."
