story: Washington Duke returned from the Civil War to his farm north of Durham with fifty cents in his pocket and a stock of brightleaf tobacco. He and his sons hand-rolled the first cigarettes in the small log smokehouse here, sold them to passing soldiers, and within thirty years had built American Tobacco — the largest tobacco company on earth, and the basis of the family wealth that would later fund Duke University.

Duke Homestead, four miles north of downtown, is the preserved farm and tobacco workshop where the Duke family launched the business that would dominate American tobacco for half a century. The site includes the original frame farmhouse, two early tobacco factories, and the Tobacco Museum — a thorough and surprisingly unflinching history of the industry, its economic impact, and its public-health legacy.

The site is a State Historic Site, free to enter, and a half-day visit. Guided tours are offered Tuesday to Saturday. Combine with a visit to the Bennett Place historic site fifteen minutes west for a fuller picture of post-Civil-War Durham.
