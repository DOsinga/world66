The most important place in Greensboro by some distance. The museum occupies the original F. W. Woolworth's Five and Dime building on South Elm Street, and a portion of the original lunch counter has been preserved exactly where the four A&T students sat down on 1 February 1960. The rest of the building has been turned into a substantial museum tracing the African American struggle for civil rights through the 19th and 20th centuries, with extensive material on segregation, the Greensboro sit-in, and what followed.

Visits are by guided tour only and last about 75 minutes. Book ahead in summer — the tours fill up. Allow time afterwards to walk around downtown; the city's main civic monuments are within a few blocks.
