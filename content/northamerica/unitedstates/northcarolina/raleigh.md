Raleigh is the state capital and one of the most livable cities in the American South. The unofficial motto is "Trees, Tees, and PhDs" — a nod to its 150-plus parks, excellent golf courses, and the concentration of universities in the Research Triangle.

You can spend a morning exploring three free state museums within walking distance of each other: the [North Carolina Museum of Art](/northamerica/unitedstates/northcarolina/raleigh/nc_museum_of_art), the [Museum of History](/northamerica/unitedstates/northcarolina/raleigh/nc_museum_of_history), and the [Museum of Natural Sciences](/northamerica/unitedstates/northcarolina/raleigh/nc_museum_of_natural_sciences). All are genuinely good, not just good-for-free.

After the museums, stroll through [Historic Oakwood](/northamerica/unitedstates/northcarolina/raleigh/historic_oakwood), a Victorian-era neighbourhood listed on the National Register of Historic Places. The tree-lined streets, ornate houses, and old graveyard full of prominent Raleigh figures make for a pleasant walk. The [North Carolina State Capitol](/northamerica/unitedstates/northcarolina/raleigh/nc_state_capitol) and the [North Carolina Freedom Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/northcarolina/raleigh/nc_freedom_park) complete the downtown civic circuit.

The [State Farmers Market](/northamerica/unitedstates/northcarolina/raleigh/state_farmers_market) is one of the largest in the Southeast and a great place to buy seasonal produce, baked goods, and local honey. The restaurants inside serve Southern comfort food that draws locals year-round.

With more than 150 parks, gardens, lakes, and greenways, Raleigh offers plenty of outdoor recreation. The [Neuse River Greenway](/northamerica/unitedstates/northcarolina/raleigh/neuse_river_greenway) runs for nearly 30 miles and is popular with runners, cyclists, and walkers.
