Salisbury National Cemetery sits on the site of the Salisbury Confederate Prison, the Confederacy's largest prison for Union soldiers. Between 1861 and 1865, more than 15,000 men were held in a compound built for 2,500; over 11,000 died of disease, exposure, and starvation, and are buried here in eighteen long trenches.

The trenches are still visible — long, grass-covered mounds that run in parallel lines across the cemetery — and a federal monument lists every regiment whose men lie below. It is a quiet, sobering place, well kept and almost always empty, set just south of the historic downtown. The 1908 ornamental gates and the small visitor information board explain the prison's grim history.
