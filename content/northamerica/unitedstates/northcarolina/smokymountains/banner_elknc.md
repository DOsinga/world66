Banner Elk is a small mountain town in the North Carolina high country, sitting at nearly 3,700 feet elevation in Avery County. It is best known as the gateway to [Sugar Mountain](/northamerica/unitedstates/northcarolina/smokymountains/banner_elknc/sugar_mountain_ski_resort) and Beech Mountain ski resorts — the largest in the Southeast — which makes it a busy winter destination for skiers and snowboarders from across the region. In summer, the high elevation keeps temperatures cool while the surrounding peaks and forests draw hikers, cyclists, and fly fishermen.

[Lees-McRae College](/northamerica/unitedstates/northcarolina/smokymountains/banner_elknc/lees_mcrae_college) has been at the heart of the town since 1900, founded by a Presbyterian missionary who came to serve the isolated Appalachian communities. Its stone campus and wildlife rehabilitation center give Banner Elk an intellectual character that sits alongside its ski-resort identity. [Shawneehaw Creek](/northamerica/unitedstates/northcarolina/smokymountains/banner_elknc/shawneehaw_creek), whose Cherokee name refers to the serviceberry tree, runs through town and provides accessible trout fishing.

The strangest thing within a short drive is the [Land of Oz](/northamerica/unitedstates/northcarolina/smokymountains/banner_elknc/land_of_oz), a derelict 1970s theme park on Beech Mountain that opens once a year for its Autumn at Oz festival — a genuinely peculiar and entertaining afternoon on the mountaintop. Every October the [Woolly Worm Festival](/northamerica/unitedstates/northcarolina/smokymountains/banner_elknc/woolly_worm_festival) brings tens of thousands of visitors to watch caterpillar races and debate the severity of the coming winter, coinciding neatly with peak fall foliage season.
