  In the 1993 film "The Fugitive," Harrison Ford's character leaps from the top of this dam to escape Tommy Lee Jones. The 225-foot drop into the gorge below was achieved with a combination of stunt work and location filming at the real dam. The production crew reportedly paid the Tapoco Hydro Project for access, and the scene remains one of the most memorable in 1990s action cinema. Visitors still arrive specifically to stand where Ford supposedly jumped.

The Cheoah Dam is a 225-foot concrete arch dam on the Little Tennessee River, built in 1919 by the Alcoa Power Company to serve aluminium smelting operations further downstream. It is a handsome piece of early industrial engineering, its curved face rising from the narrow gorge in a single graceful arc. The dam forms Cheoah Lake above and releases water into the dramatic gorge below.

For kayakers and whitewater enthusiasts, the stretch below Cheoah is legendary — but only when the dam is releasing water, which happens on a limited schedule. Check release dates through the American Whitewater Association before planning a paddle. The overlook above the dam is accessible by car from the road along the lake's eastern shore, and the views down into the gorge are dramatic in any season.
