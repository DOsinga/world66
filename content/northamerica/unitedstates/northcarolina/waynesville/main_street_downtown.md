  Waynesville's downtown survived the collapse of the textile and tanning industries that devastated many Southern Appalachian towns in the 20th century, largely because artists and craftspeople moved in when rents fell. The gallery scene that grew from that transition is now one of the town's most distinctive features.
---

Waynesville's Main Street is one of the more intact small-town downtowns in western North Carolina — a walkable stretch of independent galleries, bookshops, outfitters, and restaurants that hasn't been replaced by chains. The architecture is mostly early 20th century commercial brick, well-maintained and enlivened by the gallery windows and café tables spilling onto the pavement.

Allow a couple of hours to walk the length of it and poke into the shops. The concentration of working studios and galleries makes it particularly interesting for anyone interested in Appalachian craft traditions — pottery, weaving, woodworking — alongside contemporary art.
