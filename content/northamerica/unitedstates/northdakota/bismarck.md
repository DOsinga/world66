Bismarck is the capital of North Dakota and sits on the east bank of the Missouri River, a position that shaped both its founding and its character. It's not on most travellers' radar, but it punches above its weight as a state capital: the modernist skyscraper of the [state capitol](/northamerica/unitedstates/northdakota/bismarck/north_dakota_state_capitol), nicknamed the "Skyscraper of the Prairie," is the tallest building in the state, and the former Governor's Mansion nearby is a handsome Victorian-era residence open for tours.

The [North Dakota Heritage Center](/northamerica/unitedstates/northdakota/bismarck/north_dakota_heritage_center), attached to the State Capitol complex, is the main reason to stop — a substantial museum covering Native American history, the Ice Age, pioneer settlement and the state's natural history, all done well. The [Keelboat Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/northdakota/bismarck/keelboat_park) along the Missouri River has a full-scale replica of the Lewis and Clark expedition's keelboat alongside sculptures of the Corps of Discovery.

The Missouri River is the town's best outdoor asset. A short drive south leads to [Fort Abraham Lincoln State Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/northdakota/bismarck/fort_abraham_lincoln_state_park), where General Custer was stationed before his fatal expedition to the Little Bighorn, and where reconstructed Mandan earth lodges show how the original inhabitants lived. The [Double Ditch Indian Village Historic Site](/northamerica/unitedstates/northdakota/bismarck/double_ditch_indian_village), north of town, preserves the earthworks of a large Mandan settlement still visible on the river bluffs.
