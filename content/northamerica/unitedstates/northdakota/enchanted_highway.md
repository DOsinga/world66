The Enchanted Highway is not Regent, and that is the point. The village at the southern end is tiny; the travel value is the 32-mile rural road from Interstate 94 near Gladstone down to Regent, where huge scrap-metal sculptures rise out of wheat fields and prairie sky.

Local artist Gary Greff began the project in 1990 as a way to draw travellers off the interstate. It worked, at least in the strange and memorable way roadside art can work. [Geese in Flight](/northamerica/unitedstates/northdakota/enchanted_highway/geese_in_flight) is visible from I-94 and holds a Guinness record as a giant scrap-metal sculpture; farther south, [Giant Grasshoppers](/northamerica/unitedstates/northdakota/enchanted_highway/giant_grasshoppers), [Fisherman's Dream](/northamerica/unitedstates/northdakota/enchanted_highway/fishermans_dream), and [The Tin Family](/northamerica/unitedstates/northdakota/enchanted_highway/the_tin_family) turn the drive into a slow, odd scavenger hunt.

This is worth doing if you are already crossing western North Dakota and want the day to feel less like a long haul. Bring fuel, water, and a loose sense of humour; there is a long empty gap between some sculptures, and that emptiness is part of the experience.
