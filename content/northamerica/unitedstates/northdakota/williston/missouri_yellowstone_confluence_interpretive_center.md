The Missouri-Yellowstone Confluence Interpretive Center explains one of the most
important geographic meetings in the northern plains: the point where the Yellowstone
River joins the Missouri. Exhibits connect the rivers to Native history, the fur trade,
military posts, steamboats, and Lewis and Clark-era exploration.

It pairs naturally with nearby Fort Buford and Fort Union, turning a remote-feeling
landscape into a coherent historical day. The site is strongest when you step outside
after the exhibits and look at the rivers themselves; the geography is the main
artifact.
