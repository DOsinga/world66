---
title: "Fort Union Trading Post National Historic Site"
type: poi
---

At the confluence of the Missouri and Yellowstone rivers, about 40 km northwest of Williston, Fort Union was the most important fur-trading post on the upper Missouri for the better part of five decades. Built in 1828 by the American Fur Company, it was the hub through which beaver pelts, bison robes, and pemmican moved east and trade goods — blankets, guns, kettles, glass beads — moved west to the Assiniboine, Crow, Blackfeet, and other nations who made the place their own commercial capital as much as the company's. At its height, Fort Union was doing business on a scale that made it one of the most significant commercial sites between St Louis and the Pacific.

The National Park Service has reconstructed the Bourgeois House and the main trade house to their 1850s appearance, with period furnishings and interpreters in summer who explain the mechanics of the fur trade in concrete, unsentimental terms. The archaeology programme has been particularly thorough here, and the on-site exhibits show what the excavations turned up — the material culture of the trade is more interesting than many visitors expect. Outside the stockade, the setting is genuinely spectacular: the wide confluence of two great rivers, the cottonwood bottomland, and the open sky above it all.

Allow two to three hours. The site is open daily from May through September; winter hours are limited. There is no fee.
