---
title: "James Memorial Arts Center"
type: poi
---

The James Memorial Arts Center is Williston's main cultural venue, housed in a handsome brick building in the older part of downtown. It serves as a combined art gallery, performance space, and community arts hub — the kind of institution that punches well above its weight for a city of this size, partly because the oil-boom years brought both money and a transient population that apparently wanted somewhere to go that wasn't a bar.

The gallery programme mixes exhibitions of work by regional artists with occasional shows of national scope. The permanent collection leans toward plains landscapes and Native American art, with a particular strength in works depicting the Missouri River country and the fur-trade era. The building itself is worth a look — it was constructed in 1927 as a memorial to local residents who died in the First World War, and the interior retains much of its original woodwork.

Check the current schedule before visiting, as opening hours and programmes vary by season. Admission to the galleries is free. The Arts Center is on First Avenue West in downtown Williston, within easy walking distance of the main restaurant strip.
