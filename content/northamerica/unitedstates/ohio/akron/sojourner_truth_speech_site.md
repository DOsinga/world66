In May 1851 the Akron Women's Rights Convention gathered in the Old Stone Church on the corner of High and East Market streets, and Sojourner Truth — born enslaved in New York, by then a travelling preacher and abolitionist — rose to speak. The address that has come down to history as "Ain't I a Woman?" was given here, in the heart of what is now downtown Akron.

The original church was demolished in 1881; the site today is part of the United Way of Summit County campus. A bronze marker mounted in the small plaza beside the building names Truth and quotes a fragment of the speech. It is a quiet, unremarkable spot — easy to walk past — but it is one of the most significant sites in the history of American oratory.

The marker is on a public plaza and accessible any time.
