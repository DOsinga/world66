story: >
  The name "Cuyahoga" comes from a Mohawk word meaning "crooked river," and the falls that give the city its name were the longest portage route on the entire Ohio-Erie canal system. Native Americans had been detouring around these rapids for centuries before European settlers ever arrived. The river's total drop through the gorge — roughly 200 feet — actually exceeds that of Niagara Falls, though spread over two miles rather than a single plunge.
---

The Cuyahoga River drops through a series of rapids and cascades for about two miles as it passes through the city, creating the defining feature of the landscape. The gorge is accessible from several points along Gorge Metro Park, which runs along the eastern bank. Walking trails give you views of the whitewater from above, while more adventurous visitors can get close to the water level at several access points.

The rapids are most dramatic in spring and after heavy rain, when the river runs fast and brown. In summer the water level drops and the rock formations become more visible — layered shale and sandstone carved into channels and pools over millennia. Sunset from the footbridge near Front Street is one of the better views in the area, with the gorge opening up and the city's old industrial buildings in the background.
