Delaware is a small city about half an hour north of Columbus, Ohio. It has a [handsome downtown](/northamerica/unitedstates/ohio/delaware/downtown_delaware) of nineteenth-century buildings in Greek Revival and Victorian styles, several streets of stately homes on the National Register of Historic Places, and the kind of quiet charm that rewards a slow walk.

The town is the birthplace of Rutherford B. Hayes, the 19th President of the United States, though the actual house is long gone — only a [plaque outside a gas station](/northamerica/unitedstates/ohio/delaware/hayes_birthplace) marks the spot. [Ohio Wesleyan University](/northamerica/unitedstates/ohio/delaware/ohio_wesleyan_university) occupies a leafy campus in the centre of town; its oldest building, [Elliott Hall](/northamerica/unitedstates/ohio/delaware/elliott_hall) (1833), is a fine example of Greek Revival architecture.

Harness racing fans know Delaware as the home of the [Little Brown Jug](/northamerica/unitedstates/ohio/delaware/little_brown_jug), one of the premier events in the sport. The race has been held here every year since 1946 as part of the Delaware County Fair, drawing large crowds each September.
