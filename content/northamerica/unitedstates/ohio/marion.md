Marion is a small city in central Ohio, about 75 km north of Columbus. It is best known as the birthplace and home of Warren G. Harding, the 29th President of the United States. The [Harding Home](/northamerica/unitedstates/ohio/marion/harding_home), a Queen Anne-style house where Harding conducted his famous 1920 front-porch presidential campaign, is preserved as a museum and open for tours. The [Harding Memorial](/northamerica/unitedstates/ohio/marion/harding_memorial), an impressive circular colonnade of white Georgia marble designed by Cass Gilbert, marks the graves of Harding and his wife Florence on the south side of town.

Beyond the presidential connection, Marion has a handful of genuine curiosities. The [Octagon House](/northamerica/unitedstates/ohio/marion/octagon_house) is a well-preserved specimen of the 1850s architectural craze for eight-sided homes. The [Merchant Ball](/northamerica/unitedstates/ohio/marion/merchant_ball) in Marion Cemetery is a polished granite sphere that cemetery records show has been slowly rolling since the 1890s. The [Palace Theatre](/northamerica/unitedstates/ohio/marion/palace_theatre), a 1928 movie palace with a Mediterranean atmospheric interior, still hosts live performances. The Marion County Popcorn Festival held each September around the [Marion County Courthouse](/northamerica/unitedstates/ohio/marion/marion_county_courthouse) claims to be one of the largest in the country.
