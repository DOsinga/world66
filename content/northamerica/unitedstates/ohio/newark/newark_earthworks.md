The Newark Earthworks are the largest surviving set of geometric earthen enclosures in the world, built by the Hopewell culture between roughly 100 BC and 500 AD. Three sections survive: the Great Circle (nearly 1,200 feet in diameter, with an interior moat), the Octagon Earthworks (a precise eight-sided enclosure connected to a perfect circle, aligned with the 18.6-year lunar cycle), and the smaller Wright Earthworks nearby. In 2023 the site was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site as part of the Hopewell Ceremonial Earthworks group.

The Great Circle is managed as a state park and is freely accessible; walk around and through the earth walls, which in places rise nearly eight feet. The Octagon Earthworks are leased to a golf club — a peculiar arrangement — but are open to the public without charge on certain days each month. The on-site museum at the Great Circle explains the Hopewell culture and the engineering that produced the earthworks.
