Erie Sand Barrens protects a landscape that once covered far more of the Lake Erie shore: sandy ridges, prairie openings, and scattered oaks left behind by old beach lines. It is not a blockbuster sight like Cedar Point, but it gives Sandusky a different texture from roller coasters and island ferries. In late spring and summer the preserve is best for wildflowers, butterflies, and a quiet walk through habitat that has mostly disappeared elsewhere in northern Ohio.

The trails are short and simple, so this is a selective stop rather than a half-day expedition. Come if you have a car, want a break from the waterfront crowds, or are interested in natural history. Bring insect repellent in warm weather and do not expect facilities beyond the basics; the reward is the rare landscape itself.
