Toledo is an industrial city of about 270,000 people on the Maumee River in northwest Ohio, at the western end of Lake Erie. It has an honest rust-belt character that doesn't pretend to be something it isn't — and it has one genuinely excellent reason to visit. The [Toledo Museum of Art](/northamerica/unitedstates/ohio/toledo/toledo_museum_of_art) holds one of the most comprehensive art collections in the United States, particularly strong in glass art (Toledo has a long history of glass manufacturing), ancient Greek and Roman works, and European painting. The museum's Glass Pavilion, a stunning contemporary building designed by SANAA, is worth the trip on its own.

The Toledo Mud Hens, the minor league baseball team whose name became famous through M*A*S*H, play here at [Fifth Third Field](/northamerica/unitedstates/ohio/toledo/fifth_third_field), a pleasant downtown ballpark. The [Toledo Zoo](/northamerica/unitedstates/ohio/toledo/toledo_zoo) is well regarded, particularly for its hippopotamus exhibit (the "hippo experience" is apparently a big draw). The city's position on Lake Erie means there's decent fishing, and the Erie islands — including Put-in-Bay — are accessible by ferry not far to the east.

For a city this size, Toledo punches well above its weight on quirky one-room museums: the [Blair Museum of Lithophanes](/northamerica/unitedstates/ohio/toledo/blair_lithophanes) is the most obsessive, the [National Museum of the Great Lakes](/northamerica/unitedstates/ohio/toledo/national_museum_great_lakes) the most useful. The city's signature meal is a Hungarian hot dog at [Tony Packo's](/northamerica/unitedstates/ohio/toledo/tony_packos), the East Toledo institution whose celebrity-signed buns came to national notice through M*A*S*H.

Toledo sits at the corner of Ohio, Michigan, and Indiana, and is a practical stop if you're driving between Cleveland or Columbus and Detroit or Chicago.
