The Toledo Museum of Art is the single best reason to be in the city, and one of the half-dozen most important regional art museums in the United States. The collection holds works by Rembrandt, El Greco, Goya, Cézanne, Picasso and Monet alongside an outstanding ancient Greek and Roman gallery and pre-Columbian, Asian and Islamic wings. The European paintings hang in the neoclassical 1912 main building.

The Glass Pavilion, across Monroe Street, holds the glass collection — Tiffany, Steuben, Czech and Venetian, contemporary American studio glass — in a building that is itself a glass artwork, with walls of curved transparent panels that bend the space around the exhibits. Live glass-blowing demonstrations run several times a day in the working hotshop attached to the pavilion. Admission to the whole museum is free; closed Mondays.
