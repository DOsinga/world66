The Muskingum River Improvements, completed in 1841, were a significant piece of 19th-century civil engineering — a series of locks and dams that made the Muskingum River navigable for more than 160 km. Several of the original locks survive and remain operational, making them some of the oldest functioning navigation locks in the United States.

Lock 10, near Zanesville, is among the most accessible and retains its original hand-operated mechanism. Pleasure boats can still use the locks in season; watching the old wooden lock gates being operated by hand is a vivid reminder of the era when river transport was the lifeblood of Ohio's interior. The riverbanks here are also pleasant for walking.
