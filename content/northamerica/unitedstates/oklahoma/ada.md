Ada is a city of around 17,000 people and the county seat of Pontotoc County, sitting in the rolling Cross Timbers country of south-central Oklahoma, roughly equidistant between Oklahoma City and Dallas. The Cross Timbers landscape — post oaks and blackjacks giving way to tallgrass meadows — gives the area a character distinct from the Great Plains flatlands to the west.

The city's most significant identity comes from its role as the seat of the Chickasaw Nation, one of the five largest Native American nations in the United States. The tribal complex here houses the nation's government, and Chickasaw influence is woven through the local economy and culture. The Chickasaw Cultural Center, a short drive south in Sulphur, is the best place to understand the nation's history and traditions — it is among the finest cultural facilities in Oklahoma and well worth the detour.

Downtown Ada clusters around the Pontotoc County Courthouse and retains a handful of architecturally interesting buildings from the 1920s and 1930s. The McSwain Theatre, a restored historic cinema now used as a live performance venue, anchors the cultural life of the city centre. Wintersmith Park, on the south side of town, was built by the Works Progress Administration in the 1930s and offers walking trails, a small lake, and a relaxed green escape from the main road strip.

Ada is a practical overnight stop on the drive between Dallas and Oklahoma City, with all the chain motels and fast-food restaurants that implies — but it rewards a few hours of exploration if you give it the chance.
