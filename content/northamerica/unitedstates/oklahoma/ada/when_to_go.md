---
title: When to Go
type: section
---

Ada sits in the humid subtropical zone of south-central Oklahoma, which means hot, humid summers and mild winters with occasional ice storms. Spring (March through May) is the most pleasant season — temperatures are comfortable, wildflowers appear in the Cross Timbers, and the landscape is at its greenest. Fall offers similar conditions and cleaner light.

Summer is brutal: July and August regularly push above 38°C (100°F) with high humidity. If you're travelling through in summer, plan outdoor activities for early morning and retreat indoors by midday.

Winters are unpredictable. The region sits at the boundary of warm Gulf air and cold Arctic fronts, so ice storms can arrive with little warning between November and February, making roads genuinely dangerous. Snowfall is light and doesn't stay long.

The **Chickasaw Nation Annual Meeting**, held in Ada each autumn, draws tribal members from across the country and is worth attending if you're interested in Native American governance and culture — it's one of the larger such events in the southern plains.
