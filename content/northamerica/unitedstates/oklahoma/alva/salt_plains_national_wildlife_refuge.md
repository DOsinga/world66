About 40 miles southeast of Alva, the Salt Plains National Wildlife Refuge protects a 32,000-acre stretch of mudflat, marsh, and reservoir on the Salt Fork of the Arkansas River. The salt crust is a remnant of an inland sea; in summer the surface is blindingly white and you can see for miles.

The refuge is a major stop on the Central Flyway and one of the few inland breeding sites for the snowy plover. Whooping cranes, sandhill cranes, and white pelicans pass through on migration. The selenite crystal digging area is open from April through mid-October — bring a shovel, water, and a sunhat.
