Bartlesville in northeast Oklahoma is a small city with a disproportionate cultural legacy. Oil wealth from Phillips Petroleum — founded here in 1917 — funded two of the state's most remarkable attractions: [Price Tower](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma/bartlesville/price_tower), Frank Lloyd Wright's only completed skyscraper, and [Woolaroc](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma/bartlesville/woolaroc), Frank Phillips's wildlife ranch and western art museum 14 miles southwest, which holds one of the finest collections of Native American art and Western Americana in the country.

The [Nellie Johnstone No. 1](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma/bartlesville/nellie_johnstone_no1), a replica derrick on the Caney River, marks the site of Oklahoma's first commercial oil well (1897), the discovery that triggered the boom that made all of this possible. In June, the [OK Mozart International Festival](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma/bartlesville/ok_mozart_festival) draws classical musicians and audiences from across the country.

A day trip to nearby Dewey includes the Tom Mix Museum covering the silent film cowboy star, and Prairie Song, a recreated 1800s pioneer village. Bartlesville is 47 miles north of Tulsa; a car is essential.
