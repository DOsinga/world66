Designated a National Historic Landmark in 1975, the Boley Historic District preserves the original commercial heart of one of Oklahoma's most significant all-Black towns. Boley was founded in 1903 on land allotted under the Dawes Act, and by 1910 it was thriving — a deliberate proof of concept that Black communities could build and sustain their own institutions in the face of Jim Crow.

The district roughly bounded by Seward Avenue, Walnut and Cedar Streets contains brick and stone commercial buildings dating from the first decade of the 20th century. Walking the streets gives a vivid sense of the town's ambition: these are not modest structures. They were built to last, and to demonstrate that permanence and prosperity were possible. The contrast between what once was and what remains today is itself part of the story — a lesson in what systematic economic exclusion did to communities that had built themselves up against the odds.
