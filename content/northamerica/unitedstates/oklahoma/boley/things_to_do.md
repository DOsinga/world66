---

Boley is a small town, and you won't need more than a few hours to take it in — but those hours are some of the most historically resonant you can spend in Oklahoma. This was one of the most successful all-Black towns in the United States at its early-20th-century peak, and what remains carries the weight of that story.

The [Boley Historic District](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma/boley/boley_historic_district), listed as a National Historic Landmark, preserves the original business streets from the town's prosperous era. Walk along Seward Avenue and you'll pass buildings that once housed a bank, a hotel, a newspaper, a cotton gin, a telephone exchange, and a waterworks — all founded and run by Black Oklahomans at a time when such institutions were routinely denied to them elsewhere.

The [Farmers and Merchants Bank](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma/boley/farmers_merchants_bank) building, site of the 1932 Pretty Boy Floyd robbery attempt, still stands. The [Masonic Lodge](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma/boley/masonic_lodge), a three-story 1912 structure, was the tallest building between Okmulgee and Oklahoma City when it was built.

The biggest annual event is the [Boley Rodeo](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma/boley/boley_rodeo), held over Memorial Day weekend — one of the oldest and most celebrated Black rodeos in the United States, drawing competitors and visitors from across the country.
