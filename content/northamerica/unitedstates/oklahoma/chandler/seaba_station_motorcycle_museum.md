Seaba Station is a restored 1921 filling station about five miles east of Chandler on the original Route 66 alignment, now housing an eccentric collection of vintage motorcycles gathered over decades. The building itself is a fine example of early roadside commercial architecture — a white stucco structure with a distinctive hipped roof and gas pump islands still in place out front.

Inside, the collection runs from early 20th-century machines through to mid-century American and European bikes, including Indian, Henderson, Harley-Davidson, BSA, and Triumph. The curation is informal — this is a labour of love rather than a professional museum — and the atmosphere is accordingly relaxed. Bikers stopping along the Route 66 corridor will appreciate it most, but the building's history and the roadside setting make it worthwhile for any traveller interested in the highway's commercial heritage.
