Elk City is a town of about 12,000 people in western [Oklahoma](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma), sitting on Historic [Route 66](/northamerica/unitedstates/gettingaround/route_66__the_m) at the intersection of I-40 and US-283. For Route 66 travelers, it is one of the better stops along the Oklahoma stretch, thanks mainly to the [National Route 66 Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma/elk_city/national_route_66_museum) complex. The museum covers not just the Mother Road but also the broader history of the region, with separate buildings dedicated to Old Town life, farm and ranch culture, blacksmithing, and transportation. A giant kachina figure out front marks the spot.

The town grew up around cattle ranching and oil, and both industries are still visible in the surrounding landscape of pump jacks and feedlots. [Downtown Elk City](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma/elk_city/elk_city_downtown) has a few blocks of brick-front buildings from the oil boom era, some restored, and a handful of local restaurants. The [Anadarko Basin Museum of Natural History](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma/elk_city/anadarko_basin_museum) focuses on the geology and paleontology of one of North America's deepest sedimentary basins. The [historic Route 66 alignment](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma/elk_city/route_66_historic_driving) through and around town is driveable and atmospheric, particularly the sections west toward Sayre.

Elk City makes a practical overnight stop on the long I-40 drive across Oklahoma and the Texas Panhandle. It has the usual chain motels and restaurants, but the Route 66 Museum alone makes it worth more than a gas-and-go stop.
