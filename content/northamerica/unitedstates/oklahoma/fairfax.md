Fairfax is a small town of about 1,500 people in Osage County, in the heart of the Osage Nation. It gained worldwide attention through David Grann's book *Killers of the Flower Moon*, which documents the systematic murders of Osage people during the 1920s oil boom — a dark chapter in American history that unfolded right here. The empty lot where the Smith family home was dynamited in March 1923 is on Main Street; the [Osage Bank of Fairfax](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma/fairfax/osage_bank_of_fairfax), where the chief conspirator William Hale served as cashier and trustee, still stands a block away.

The town is also the hometown of [Maria Tallchief](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma/fairfax/tallchief_birthplace), one of the most celebrated ballerinas of the 20th century and the first major Native American prima ballerina; her sister Marjorie, also a ballerina of international standing, was born here too. Four miles down the road, the traditional Osage village of [Gray Horse](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma/fairfax/gray_horse) still holds its annual June **Inlonshka** dances. Fairfax is a quiet place, but its history runs deep. For the wider Osage story, drive 25 miles east to [Pawhuska](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma/pawhuska), the seat of the Osage Nation, and the **Tallgrass Prairie Preserve** beyond it.
