A small, traditional Osage Indian village about four miles south-east of Fairfax, set in a wooded valley well off the highway. Most of the year it is quiet — just a few houses, a dance arbour and the village cemetery — but for one weekend each June it hosts the **Inlonshka dances**, a ceremonial drum dance held in turn by each of the three Osage villages over a four-week period.

Visitors are welcome to the public part of the dances if respectful and properly dressed (skirts for women, long trousers and a shirt for men, no photography), but the rest of the year there is little to do beyond drive through and read the historical markers. Combine with a visit to the Osage Nation Museum in [Pawhuska](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma/pawhuska) for context.
