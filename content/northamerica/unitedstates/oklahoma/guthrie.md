Guthrie is a small city of about 10,000 people in central [Oklahoma](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma), roughly 30 miles north of [Oklahoma City](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma/oklahomacity). It was Oklahoma's first state capital, a status it lost in 1910 when the government was moved to Oklahoma City in a controversial midnight decision. What this loss preserved, however, was the downtown: because Guthrie never had the growth pressure to tear down its Victorian-era buildings, the town center remains remarkably intact. The 1,400-acre historic district is one of the largest contiguous urban areas on the National Register of Historic Places.

Walking the downtown streets feels like stepping into a frontier-era movie set, except everything is real -- ornate brick facades, pressed-tin ceilings, and buildings that date to the 1889 Land Run. The [Oklahoma Territorial Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma/guthrie/oklahoma_territorial_museum) tells the story of the run and the territory's early years. The [Scottish Rite Masonic Temple](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma/guthrie/scottish_rite_temple), an enormous neoclassical structure, is worth a look even from the outside — guided tours reveal a 2,500-seat theatre and extraordinary ornamental craftsmanship inside. The [Pollard Theatre](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma/guthrie/pollard_theatre) is Oklahoma's oldest professional theatre company and stages shows year-round. Guthrie also hosts the annual Oklahoma International Bluegrass Festival each October.

The [Lazy E Arena](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma/guthrie/lazy_e_arena), just south of town, is one of the premier indoor rodeo and equestrian venues in the country and hosts major events year-round. Guthrie makes an easy and rewarding day trip from Oklahoma City, or a pleasant stop if you are driving north toward Kansas.
