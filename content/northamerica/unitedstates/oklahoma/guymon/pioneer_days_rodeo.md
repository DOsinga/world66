  Guymon's rodeo dates back to 1932, when it was started as a way of drawing trade into a Panhandle town fast losing population to the Dust Bowl. Pioneer Days has run almost every year since — through the Depression, the war, and the gradual emptying-out of the plains around it — making it one of the longest continuously held rodeos in the United States.
---

The Pioneer Days Rodeo is Guymon's marquee event and the reason most outside visitors come. Held on the first weekend of May at the rodeo arena south of town, it draws competitors from across the professional rodeo circuit — bull riders, ropers, barrel racers — and the prize purse is large enough to attract serious talent. The three days of competition are bracketed by a parade, a carnival, and a long, dusty round of street parties along Main Street.

If you have never been to a working professional rodeo, this is a good place to start. The crowd is genuinely local, the food is barbecue and funnel cake, and the riding is the real thing. Tickets are available at the gate; the cheaper grandstand seats put you closer to the action than the box seats and are the better deal.
