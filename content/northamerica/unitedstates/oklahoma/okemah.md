Okemah is a small town of about 3,300 people in Okfuskee County, [Oklahoma](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma). Its main claim to fame is as the birthplace of Woody Guthrie, the folk singer and songwriter who wrote "This Land Is Your Land." A sign on the edge of town once read "a singing town" — though the relationship between Okemah and its most famous son has not always been comfortable. A [bronze statue of Guthrie](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma/okemah/woody_guthrie_statue), unveiled in 2018, now stands on the courthouse square.

Each July the town hosts the [Woody Guthrie Folk Festival](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma/okemah/woody_guthrie_folk_festival), a free four-day gathering of folk and roots musicians from across the country. It is a low-key, community-run affair held at the [Crystal Theatre](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma/okemah/crystal_theatre) downtown, with concerts, jam sessions, and songwriting workshops. The festival draws a loyal crowd and is the main reason most visitors come to Okemah.

Beyond Guthrie, the [Highland Cemetery](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma/okemah/highland_cemetery) on the east edge of town holds the graves of his mother Nora Belle and a memorial to the Muscogee Creek leader Chitto Harjo — small reminders that this stretch of Oklahoma has a complicated history beneath its quiet main street.
