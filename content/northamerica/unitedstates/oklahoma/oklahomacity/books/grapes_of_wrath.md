The Pulitzer-winning 1939 novel that defined the world's image of Dust Bowl Oklahoma — and arguably Oklahoma itself — for two generations. Steinbeck's Joad family, evicted from their Oklahoma farm in the great drought, drive west on Route 66 looking for work in California; what they find there is just as harsh as what they left.

Steinbeck was a Californian who covered the migrants for newspapers in the late 1930s, and Oklahomans have a complicated relationship with the book: there is gratitude for the empathy and irritation at the simplifications. Read it before driving Route 66 — Oklahoma City and the long, flat stretch west toward the Texas line is exactly the landscape the Joads cross in chapter twelve.
