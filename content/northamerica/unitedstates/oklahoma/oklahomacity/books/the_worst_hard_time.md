The definitive history of the Dust Bowl, narrated through the lives of a half-dozen families who stayed in the Oklahoma Panhandle and the surrounding plains when most others fled to California. Egan, a New York Times reporter, drew on hundreds of hours of interviews with the dwindling number of survivors and built the book around a single sustained idea — that the Dust Bowl was a man-made ecological disaster caused by the plough-up of native grassland during the wheat boom of the 1910s and 20s.

A useful counter-narrative to the Steinbeck-Okie-California version of the period. Anyone visiting Oklahoma History Center will understand the Dust Bowl gallery there much better for having read it first.
