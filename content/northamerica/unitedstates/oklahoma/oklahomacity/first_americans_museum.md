A spectacular new museum on the south bank of the Oklahoma River, opened in 2021 after a thirty-year planning process. The architecture is the first thing you notice: a 33-metre-high earthwork mound (echoing the mounds of pre-contact Mississippian peoples), a great hall oriented to the equinox sunrise, and a giant ring-shaped Hall of the People around a circular floor.

The exhibits cover the histories and cultures of all thirty-nine tribes now headquartered in Oklahoma — Cherokee, Choctaw, Chickasaw, Creek, Seminole, and many smaller nations — through contemporary art, video, immersive installations, and traditional artefacts. The restaurant on site, Thirty Nine, serves modern Indigenous cuisine. Plan three or four hours; the museum easily justifies that.
