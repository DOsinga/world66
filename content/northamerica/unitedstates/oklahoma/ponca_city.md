Ponca City is a city of around 24,000 in Kay County in north-central Oklahoma, about 100 miles north of Oklahoma City. It was built on oil — E.W. Marland, who founded what became Conoco Oil, made his fortune here in the 1910s and 20s and left behind a remarkable legacy in the form of the [Marland Mansion](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma/ponca_city/marland_mansion), a 55-room Italian Renaissance estate modelled on the Villa Farnese, now a museum open to the public. His earlier and more modest home, the [Marland Grand Home](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma/ponca_city/marland_grand_home), is also open as a museum.

The city also has the [Pioneer Woman Statue](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma/ponca_city/pioneer_woman_statue) on Grand Avenue, a 17-foot bronze by sculptor Bryant Baker that was dedicated in 1930 as a tribute to the women who helped settle the Oklahoma Territory. Three smaller versions were exhibited across the US before Ponca City was chosen for the full-size monument. The adjacent Pioneer Woman Museum tells the story of women in Oklahoma history.

Other landmarks worth seeking out: the restored 1927 [Poncan Theatre](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma/ponca_city/poncan_theatre); the free [Conoco Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma/ponca_city/conoco_museum) on the south side of downtown; the bronze [Standing Bear Memorial](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma/ponca_city/standing_bear_park) honouring the Ponca chief whose 1879 court case established Native Americans as legal persons; and the ruined headquarters of the [101 Ranch](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma/ponca_city/101_ranch_site) fifteen miles south, the famous Wild West show ranch where Marland's first oil well was drilled.

It's a modest stop rather than a destination in itself, but the Marland Mansion alone is genuinely worth the detour for anyone interested in the wild extravagance of early 20th-century oil wealth.
