Poteau (pronounced "puh-TOE") is the county seat of LeFlore County in southeastern Oklahoma, set in a valley nearly encircled by mountains: Sugar Loaf Mountain to the east, the Winding Stair range to the south, and [Cavanal Hill](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma/poteau/cavanal_hill) to the west — which lays claim to being the world's highest hill at just under 548 metres. The distinction is dubious, but the setting is genuine.

The area has a deep pre-Columbian history. Between 500 and 1300 AD, the Spiro people built ceremonial mounds across the valley, and the [Spiro Mounds Archaeological Center](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma/poteau/spiro_mounds) east of town is one of the most significant Native American archaeological sites in the country. Nearby, the [Heavener Runestone](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma/poteau/heavener_runestone) — a carved stone bearing what may be Norse runes — has generated a century of debate about pre-Columbian European contact; it now sits in a state park.

The [Kerr Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma/poteau/kerr_museum) holds a wide-ranging collection covering the full history of the Poteau River Valley, from Spiro Mound artifacts to an improbably large collection of barbed wire (over 300 types). **Carl Albert State College**, founded in 1933, was the first two-year community college in Oklahoma and the first public school in the state to fully integrate. Downtown, the 1926 [LeFlore County Courthouse](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma/poteau/leflore_county_courthouse) and the old [Hotel Lowrey](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma/poteau/hotel_lowrey) anchor a modest historic centre.

The [Talimena National Scenic Byway](/northamerica/unitedstates/oklahoma/poteau/talimena_scenic_byway) begins just south of town, climbing through the Ouachita Mountains with long views across the forested ridgelines — best in mid-October when the hardwoods turn.
