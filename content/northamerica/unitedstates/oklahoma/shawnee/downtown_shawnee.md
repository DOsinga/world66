  Shawnee was founded in 1895 on land opened by the Land Run of 1891, and a surprising number of the commercial buildings erected in the following twenty years are still standing along Main Street. The town grew fast during the Oklahoma oil boom and had the civic confidence to build substantial masonry buildings — the legacy of that confidence is a downtown district with considerably more architectural character than most Oklahoma cities of similar size.

Shawnee's historic downtown along Main Street preserves a compact row of early-twentieth-century commercial architecture — two- and three-storey brick buildings with terracotta ornament, pressed-metal cornices, and display windows that reflect the town's ambitions during the territorial and early statehood years. The district is walkable in under an hour and makes a pleasant contrast to the chain-retail sprawl that dominates most of the city's edges.

A few independently owned businesses and cafes have taken root in the historic buildings, though the district is not fully revived by any measure. The former Aldridge Hotel and the Santa Fe Depot are among the more notable survivors. The depot no longer serves trains, but the building has been preserved and gives a sense of Shawnee's importance as a regional rail hub in the early twentieth century.
