A pair of substantial brick buildings from 1910, built when Waynoka was an important division point on the Atchison, Topeka and Santa Fe Railway. The Harvey House — one of the chain of railway restaurants that Fred Harvey ran across the Southwest — fed travellers stepping off long-distance trains. The waitresses, the "Harvey Girls," lived in dormitories upstairs.

Both buildings now house museums. The Waynoka Air Rail Museum tells the story of the brief, strange period in 1929 when Waynoka was a stop on the first transcontinental air-rail service, with passengers transferring from Ford Tri-Motor aircraft to overnight Pullman trains. There's also a railroad museum with rolling stock outside.
