The Seminole Nation Museum on South Wewoka Avenue is the reason most travellers stop here. It traces the Seminole journey from the Florida swamps through forced removal in the 1830s to resettlement in what was then Indian Territory. The exhibits move chronologically — a Florida chickee dwelling, the Trail of Tears, the rebuilding of the Seminole Nation in Oklahoma, allotment, statehood, oil.

The collections are unusually frank. A whole gallery is given to the Seminole Freedmen, descendants of Black people who lived among the Seminole and whose tribal status remains contested today. The museum also runs a small folk-art store with beadwork, baskets and patchwork quilts made by Seminole artisans. Open Tuesday to Saturday; admission is free, donations welcome.
