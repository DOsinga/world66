Florence sits where the Siuslaw River meets the [Oregon](/northamerica/unitedstates/oregon) coast, hemmed in to the south by the largest coastal sand dunes in North America. The Oregon Dunes National Recreation Area begins immediately outside town and stretches 65 kilometres down the coast, with crests that rise over 150 metres and open sand sheets where ATVs carve their tracks between the ridges. Most visitors arrive chasing exactly that — the dunes are the main event.

But Florence rewards lingering. Old Town lines the north bank of the Siuslaw River on Bay Street, a walkable strip of Victorian-era buildings with seafood restaurants, galleries, and a waterfront where fishing boats still tie up alongside pleasure craft. The 1936 Siuslaw River Bridge anchors the scene at the east end — an Art Deco concrete arch designed by Conde McCullough, one of five he built along the Oregon coast in the same decade.

North of town, the coast turns dramatic. Sea Lion Caves, 13 kilometres up Highway 101, holds a wild Steller sea lion colony inside the largest sea cave in the United States. Farther north, Heceta Head Lighthouse stands on a basalt headland above a perfect cove, the most photographed lighthouse on the Oregon coast and still an active aid to navigation since 1894. Jessie M. Honeyman State Park, 5 kilometres south, offers freshwater lake swimming and a base camp for dune hiking without the ATV crowds.

Dungeness crab is the local food, pulled from the bay and served cracked whole or folded into soups and cakes at the Old Town restaurants. In spring, rhododendrons bloom across the parks and the hills in a show that has sustained the town's oldest festival for over a century. Florence is a small town — population under 10,000 — and it moves at the pace of one.
