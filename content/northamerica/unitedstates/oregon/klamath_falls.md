Klamath Falls is often treated as the practical stop before Crater Lake, Lava Beds, or the Oregon-California border. It can do that job, but it also has enough of its own high-desert character to reward a day. The city sits between Upper Klamath Lake and Lake Ewauna, with big skies, birdlife, old rail corridors, and a downtown that still feels tied to timber and ranch country.

The "falls" are not a Niagara moment. They were rapids and cascades on the short Link River, reshaped by irrigation and hydroelectric works, but the [Link River Trail](/northamerica/unitedstates/oregon/klamath_falls/link_river_trail) still gives you a direct walk from town into the city's namesake landscape. [Moore Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/oregon/klamath_falls/moore_park) and the edge of Upper Klamath Lake add easy birding and lake views.

Klamath Falls also has a surprisingly strong museum stop in [The Favell Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/oregon/klamath_falls/favell_museum), with Native American artifacts and western art, and the [OC&E Woods Line State Trail](/northamerica/unitedstates/oregon/klamath_falls/oce_woods_line_state_trail) begins in town before running deep into the basin. It is not polished, but it is real Oregon: volcanic, windy, practical, and better than it first looks from the highway.
