Pendleton is the sort of Eastern Oregon town that explains itself slowly. From the highway it can look like a service stop in dry wheat country, but the old centre has real weight: brick storefronts, saddle shops, a famous woollen mill, and a rodeo tradition that still fills the town every September.

The best visit is built around three strands of local history. The [Pendleton Underground Tours](/northamerica/unitedstates/oregon/pendleton/pendleton_underground_tours) lead beneath downtown into rooms tied to saloons, gambling, Chinese immigrant workers, and the less polished side of a frontier railroad town. The [Pendleton Round-Up and Happy Canyon Hall of Fame](/northamerica/unitedstates/oregon/pendleton/pendleton_round_up_hall_of_fame) explains why the rodeo is not just an event here but part of the town's identity. East of town, the [Tamastslikt Cultural Institute](/northamerica/unitedstates/oregon/pendleton/tamastslikt_cultural_institute) tells the story from the perspective of the Cayuse, Umatilla, and Walla Walla people.

Pendleton is not a polished resort town, and that is part of the point. Come for a day if you are crossing Oregon on I-84, or make it an overnight stop if you want a small, specific western town with more substance than its size suggests.
