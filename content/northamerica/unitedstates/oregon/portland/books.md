---
title: "Books"
type: section
---

Portland has a strong literary culture — for a city its size, the number of independent bookshops is remarkable. Beyond Powell's City of Books (the essential stop), the Powell's on Hawthorne is a full branch in its own right. Title Wave Books (the Friends of the Library sale shop) on N Interstate has excellent used stock at low prices.

For reading about Portland, Tom McCall's autobiography *Maverick: The Story of an Oregon Governor* explains the political decisions — including the Willamette River clean-up and the removal of Harbor Drive — that shaped the modern city. Ursula K. Le Guin, who grew up in Portland and returned later in life, wrote extensively about the Pacific Northwest; *The Language of the Night* collects her essays. Brian Doyle's *Mink River* is a lyrical novel set on the Oregon Coast, and his essays on Portland are collected in *Portland, Oregonian* (various editions). Tom Robbins's early novels, including *Even Cowgirls Get the Blues*, are set in the Oregon cultural landscape of the 1970s.
