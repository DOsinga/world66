  Hoyt Arboretum was established in 1928 as a living collection of tree species from around the world. It holds one of the largest collections of conifers in the United States, including several champion trees — the largest known specimens of their species in American cultivation. The Vietnam Veterans Living Memorial sits at the arboretum's edge, where the Wildwood Trail begins its run north into Forest Park.
---

Hoyt Arboretum covers 190 acres on the slopes of Washington Park, holding over 6,000 specimens from more than 2,000 species. Twelve miles of trails wind through themed sections: the Holly Collection, the Bamboo Grove, the Magnolia Collection, and a long stretch of conifers including massive coast redwoods, dawn redwoods, and dawn cypress. It's a quiet, unhurried alternative to the more crowded Japanese Garden nearby.

The arboretum is free and open year-round, from dawn to dusk. The visitor centre at the top of the park has maps and botanical information. Autumn brings spectacular colour to the deciduous sections. The arboretum connects directly to Forest Park at its north end and to the Washington Park MAX station (a steep walk or shuttle ride south).
