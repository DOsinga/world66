  Huber's is the oldest restaurant in Portland, opened in 1879 as a free lunch counter by a man named Frank Huber who wanted to draw customers to his saloon. The restaurant closed briefly during Prohibition, then reopened and has been in continuous operation since 1945. The tableside preparation of Spanish Coffee — rum, triple sec, coffee, and flambéed cream — has been performed at Huber's tables since 1975 and is the primary reason most people visit.

Huber's Cafe in the Oregon Pioneer Building, a short walk from Pioneer Courthouse Square, is Portland's oldest operating restaurant. The interior dates largely from 1910, when the bar and back room were installed: mahogany panelling, stained glass, and a vaulted skylight ceiling. The dining room is a functioning piece of downtown Portland history rather than a themed recreation.

The menu centres on turkey — this was always a turkey-focused house, and it still does a remarkable amount of turkey business — but the main draw is the Spanish Coffee service, in which a waiter pours 151-proof rum into a glass, lights it, spins the glass to caramelise the sugar, and adds coffee and cream in a tableside performance that takes about two minutes. Order one. The lunch is a straightforward American diner menu that is good value in an expensive neighbourhood.
