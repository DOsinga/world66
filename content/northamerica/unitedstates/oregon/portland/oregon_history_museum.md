  The Oregon Historical Society building on the South Park Blocks is hard to miss: the south and east facades are covered by two enormous trompe-l'œil murals by artist Richard Haas, installed in 1989. The south wall depicts the Lewis and Clark Expedition's arrival at the Pacific; the east wall shows Oregon pioneers on the Oregon Trail. Each mural is painted with such accuracy that from a distance the painted architectural elements appear to be real additions to the building.

The Oregon Historical Society Museum, on the South Park Blocks adjacent to the Portland Art Museum and Portland State University, covers 10,000 years of Oregon history from pre-contact Indigenous nations through Spanish and British exploration, the Oregon Trail era, statehood, and the 20th century. The exhibits include the largest collection of Oregon photographic archives in existence and a substantial collection of Indigenous artefacts.

The museum underwent significant renovation and expansion in recent years and now includes a substantial wing focused on Indigenous Pacific Northwest history created with extensive tribal input. Admission is required; Oregon Historical Society members enter free. The South Park Blocks location puts it within easy walking distance of both the Portland Art Museum and Powell's Books.
