  Oregon produces more timber than any other US state, and Portland grew wealthy from the lumber industry in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The World Forestry Center in Washington Park opened in 1971 to tell the story of forest ecosystems, wood products, and the relationship between forests and human communities globally. The building itself — designed by Pietro Belluschi and Wolff Zimmer Gunsul Frasca — is a significant example of Pacific Northwest modernism.
---

The World Forestry Center sits in Washington Park, directly adjacent to the Oregon Zoo and Hoyt Arboretum. The Discovery Museum explores forest ecosystems in Oregon and around the world — old-growth forests, tropical rainforests, and managed timber plantations — through interactive exhibits aimed primarily at families and school groups. The 70-foot-tall "talking tree" in the lobby has been greeting visitors since the building opened.

The centre also holds the Magness Memorial Tree Farm, a working demonstration tree farm on the property. The attached Discovery Museum Annex covers the history of forestry and timber in Oregon specifically. Admission required; combination tickets available with the Oregon Zoo next door. The Washington Park MAX station is a 15-minute walk south.
