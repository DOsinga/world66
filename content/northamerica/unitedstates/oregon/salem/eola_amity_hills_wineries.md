The Eola-Amity Hills AVA sits in the hills west of the Willamette River, a fifteen-minute drive from downtown Salem, and it produces some of Oregon's most serious Pinot Noir. The sub-appellation runs about fifteen miles north to south between the towns of Amity and Eola, with vineyards set at elevations between 250 and 700 feet. The key here is the Van Duzer Corridor — a gap in the Coast Range that funnels cool Pacific air into the valley each afternoon, slowing ripening and preserving the acidity that makes these wines age well.

**Cristom Vineyards** (6905 Spring Valley Rd NW) is a benchmark estate for the area, farming biodynamically and producing single-vineyard Pinots named after women in the family — Marjorie, Louise, Jessie, Eileen. The tasting room opens Thursday through Sunday and no reservation is required for walk-in tastings.

**Bethel Heights Vineyard** (6060 Bethel Heights Rd NW) is one of the pioneer estates of the Eola-Amity Hills, with vines planted since 1977. Their block-designated Pinots are consistently among the valley's best, and the hilltop tasting room has a view across the valley to the Cascades on clear days.

Most tasting rooms in the area are open Thursday through Sunday, May through November, and charge a modest fee ($20–30) waived with purchase. The Oregon Wine Board website lists current hours and reservation requirements.
