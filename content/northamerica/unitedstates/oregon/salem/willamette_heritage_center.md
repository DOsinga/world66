The Willamette Heritage Center occupies a five-acre site on the old mill race — the waterway that once powered Salem's flour and woolen mills — and it's a genuinely impressive piece of Pacific Northwest history. Fourteen historic buildings survive on the grounds, including the Thomas Kay Woolen Mill, which ran from 1889 until 1962 and is now the heart of the museum complex.

Inside the mill, the original machinery is preserved in place: jacquard looms, spinning frames, a 150-horsepower steam engine. The textile and industrial displays are well-explained without being dry, and the research library holds an unusually deep archive for local genealogical and historical research.

The Jason Lee House (1841) is the oldest remaining frame house in the Pacific Northwest, and the adjacent Parsonage and Methodist Mission buildings give a vivid picture of the missionary era. Budget two to three hours if you want to see it properly.
