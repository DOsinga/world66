---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

Sisters sits at 975 metres on the eastern slope of the Cascades and gets four pronounced seasons. **Summer** (June through September) is the peak — warm days in the high 20s°C, cool nights, very low humidity, and weeks of clear sky. The downside is that this is when everyone visits; expect crowds during the major events (rodeo in early June, quilt show on the second Saturday of July, folk festival in September) and book accommodation well in advance.

**Autumn** (October to early November) is excellent — aspens turn yellow, the high country is briefly walkable before snow, prices drop, and the crowds thin.

**Winter** (December to March) brings reliable snow and turns Sisters into a ski-base for Hoodoo and a Nordic skiing centre. Town stays open year-round, restaurants and shops reduced hours; some of the higher Cascade highways (notably Highway 242 over McKenzie Pass) close from October until late spring.

**Spring** (April to May) is the awkward shoulder season — winter snow is melting, the high trails are still buried, but the town is quiet and prices are at their lowest.
