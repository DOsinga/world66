Lahaska is a tiny crossroads in Buckingham Township, [Bucks County](/northamerica/unitedstates/pennsylvania/bucks_county), about an hour north of [Philadelphia](/northamerica/unitedstates/pennsylvania/philadelphia). There is not much to the village itself, but it punches well above its weight as a day-trip destination thanks to [Peddler's Village](/northamerica/unitedstates/pennsylvania/bucks_county/lahaska/peddlers_village), a sprawling collection of specialty shops and restaurants set among landscaped gardens along Route 202.

Inside Peddler's Village, the [Grand Carousel](/northamerica/unitedstates/pennsylvania/bucks_county/lahaska/grand_carousel) — a 1922 hand-carved Dentzel carousel — is worth a ride even if you are well past childhood. A short walk along Route 202 is the [Buckingham Friends Meeting House](/northamerica/unitedstates/pennsylvania/bucks_county/lahaska/buckingham_friends_meeting), the oldest religious building in Bucks County, built in 1768 by Quaker settlers from England. If you are already exploring the area around [New Hope](/northamerica/unitedstates/pennsylvania/newhope) or [Doylestown](/northamerica/unitedstates/pennsylvania/bucks_county/doylestown), Lahaska is worth a stop for an afternoon of browsing and a meal. The village was once known as Hentown, after a chicken hatchery that occupied a six-acre plot here. The chickens are long gone, replaced by boutiques.
