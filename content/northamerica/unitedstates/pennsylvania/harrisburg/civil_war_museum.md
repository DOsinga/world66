The National Civil War Museum in Reservoir Park is unusual in the crowded field of Civil War institutions: it was explicitly designed to present both the Union and Confederate perspectives, with equal gallery space and equal interpretive weight given to each side. The result is a more nuanced and less triumphal account of the war than you will find in most Northern museums. The collections cover not just military history but the experiences of civilians, enslaved people, and the social context that made the war inevitable.

The museum holds over 24,000 original artifacts — firearms, uniforms, documents, personal effects — supplemented by dioramas and multimedia installations. The galleries are arranged chronologically from the antebellum period through Reconstruction. The building itself sits on a hilltop in Reservoir Park with good views over the city. Harrisburg was the transfer point for hundreds of thousands of Union troops, and the museum connects that local history to the broader national story. Allow two to three hours.
