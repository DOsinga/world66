---
title: When to Go
type: section
---

Johnstown sits in a valley in the Allegheny Mountains and has a fully continental climate with cold winters and warm, humid summers. The mountains can intensify rainfall and snowfall compared to lower Pennsylvania.

**Spring (April–May)** is a pleasant time to visit — the hills go green quickly, the weather is mild, and the rivers run high from snowmelt. May is historically significant: the Great Flood occurred on May 31, 1889, and the Johnstown Flood Museum and National Memorial mark the anniversary each year with commemorative events.

**Summer (June–August)** is the peak season. The surrounding state parks are fully open for swimming, boating, and hiking. Days can be hot and humid, with afternoon thunderstorms common. The inclined plane and outdoor sites are at their most accessible.

**Autumn (September–October)** brings excellent fall foliage to the surrounding mountains. The Allegheny highlands colour up by mid-October and hiking is comfortable in cooler temperatures. The area is less crowded than leaf-peeping hotspots further east.

**Winter (November–March)** is cold and can be snowy. Indoor attractions like the Flood Museum and Heritage Discovery Center are open year-round, but the National Memorial near St. Michael has reduced hours and the state parks have limited services. Road conditions in the mountains can be difficult.
