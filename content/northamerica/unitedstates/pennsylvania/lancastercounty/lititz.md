Lititz is a small town of about 10,000 in the northern hills of [Lancaster County](/northamerica/unitedstates/pennsylvania/lancastercounty). Founded by Moravians in 1756, it has a [main street](/northamerica/unitedstates/pennsylvania/lancastercounty/lititz/main_street) that retains much of its old-world charm — brick facades, wide sidewalks, antique shops, and small cafes that feel genuinely local rather than assembled for tourists. The whole core of the town is a [National Historic District](/northamerica/unitedstates/pennsylvania/lancastercounty/lititz/lititz_moravian_historic_district), anchored by the 1787 [Moravian Church](/northamerica/unitedstates/pennsylvania/lancastercounty/lititz/moravian_church).

Two food landmarks make Lititz worth the detour. The [Julius Sturgis Pretzel Bakery](/northamerica/unitedstates/pennsylvania/lancastercounty/lititz/julius_sturgis_pretzel_bakery), the oldest commercial pretzel bakery in America, offers hands-on tours where you can twist your own pretzel by hand. A few blocks away, the [Wilbur Chocolate](/northamerica/unitedstates/pennsylvania/lancastercounty/lititz/wilbur_chocolate) Company's candy store sells chocolate that has been made in Lititz since 1900. Both are unpretentious and fun.

[Lititz Springs Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/pennsylvania/lancastercounty/lititz/lititz_springs_park) in the center of town hosts one of Pennsylvania's most distinctive Fourth of July celebrations: at dusk, thousands of candles are floated on the spring-fed creek, and the whole town gathers to watch them drift past. The park is pleasant year-round, and the walk along the creek into the surrounding residential streets gives a good sense of how quietly handsome this place is.
