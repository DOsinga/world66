  PNC Park opened in 2001 and is consistently rated one of the best baseball venues in the United States — not because the Pittsburgh Pirates have been particularly successful, but because the architects (HOK Sport, now Populous) aligned the field so that the entire Pittsburgh skyline sits behind the right field wall. When a home run clears the fence into the Allegheny River, the view of the batter, the ball, and the skyscrapers behind it is exactly what baseball stadiums should look like.

PNC Park sits on the north bank of the Allegheny River, directly facing downtown Pittsburgh, and the view from almost every seat in the stadium is remarkable — the skyline, the river, and the three bridges (Roberto Clemente Bridge, Andy Warhol Bridge, and Rachel Carson Bridge) forming a backdrop unlike anything in baseball. Game or no game, the Roberto Clemente Bridge leading from downtown to the park closes to cars on game days, creating a pedestrian promenade that fills with fans for several hours before first pitch.

The Pittsburgh Pirates have had a long period of mediocrity interrupted by occasional excitement, but that has never much affected the stadium's atmosphere on a warm summer evening. Single-game tickets are usually available and not expensive by major league standards. Tours of the facility run on non-game days and include the press boxes, dugouts, and warning track. The Allegheny River Bicycle Trail passes directly below the outfield wall, making a waterfront bike ride easy to combine with a game.
