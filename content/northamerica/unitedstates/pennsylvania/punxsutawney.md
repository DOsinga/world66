Punxsutawney is a small borough in western Pennsylvania that has managed to turn one February morning into a national ritual. The town is home to Punxsutawney Phil, the groundhog whose shadow is supposed to forecast the rest of winter, and the whole place leans into the joke with admirable seriousness.

The main reason to come is [Groundhog Day](/northamerica/unitedstates/pennsylvania/punxsutawney/gobblers_knob), when thousands of people crowd onto Gobbler's Knob before sunrise. Outside the festival, Punxsutawney is quieter but still odd enough to reward a short stop: Phil's downtown burrow, the [Weather Discovery Center](/northamerica/unitedstates/pennsylvania/punxsutawney/weather_discovery_center), and a scattering of painted groundhog statues make it feel like a town with one story and no embarrassment about telling it.

It is not a broad destination, and nobody should cross Pennsylvania for a normal weekday here. But if you are already in the Pennsylvania Wilds or driving between Pittsburgh and State College, Punxsutawney has enough identity for a good half-day and one very American story to take away.
