The Nathanael Greene Homestead, known as Spell Hall, stands at 50 Taft Street in a rural part of eastern Coventry. Built in 1770 for Nathanael Greene — later George Washington's second-in-command and the general most responsible for winning the Southern Campaign — it is a handsome two-and-a-half-storey colonial house in excellent preservation, surrounded by mature trees and farmland that has not changed dramatically since the 18th century.

The house is open for guided tours on weekends from May through October, run by volunteer guides who know their material well. The rooms are furnished with period pieces, some original to the Greene family. Greene lived here for only six years before the war began; he never returned. The story of what happened to him afterward — and the extraordinary competence he displayed despite no military training — is told in the tour and is genuinely gripping for anyone interested in the period.
