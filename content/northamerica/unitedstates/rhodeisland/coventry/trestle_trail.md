The Trestle Trail runs roughly 18 miles from Coventry through the forests of western Rhode Island and into eastern Connecticut, following a former rail corridor that was converted to multiuse trail in the 1980s and 1990s. The surface is packed gravel and crushed stone, making it well-suited for cycling, walking, and cross-country skiing in winter. The western sections are particularly quiet, passing through second-growth forest and wetlands with very little road crossings.

The trail takes its name from the wooden trestle bridges that carry it over stream valleys and boggy lowland — several are still original structures, repaired rather than replaced. The most scenic sections are in the middle stretch, where the trail passes through unbroken woodland far from any road. Mountain bikes handle the surface well; road bikes less so. The eastern trailhead is accessible from Coventry's town centre; parking is available at several points along the route.
