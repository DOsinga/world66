Providence punches well above its weight for a city of 180,000. It's home to Brown University and the Rhode Island School of Design (RISD), which together give the city a creative, restless energy — you'll find galleries, experimental restaurants, and design studios crammed into the same [Federal Hill](/northamerica/unitedstates/rhodeisland/providence/federal_hill) blocks as century-old Italian bakeries and red-sauce joints.

The food scene is the city's most reliable draw. Federal Hill is New England's densest concentration of Italian-American cooking: [Angelo's Civita Farnese](/northamerica/unitedstates/rhodeisland/providence/angelos) on Atwells Avenue has been open since 1924, and the delis on the plaza hold their own against anything in Boston's North End. Downtown, a younger generation of chefs has pushed Providence onto national food media's radar with a wave of inventive, ingredient-focused restaurants.

[WaterFire](/northamerica/unitedstates/rhodeisland/providence/waterfire) is the event Providence is known for beyond Rhode Island. On selected evenings from spring through autumn, nearly a hundred bonfires are lit on iron braziers floating on the three rivers that converge downtown. The effect — fire on dark water, gondoliers, crowds on both banks — is genuinely unlike anything else in the Northeast, and it's free. Check waterfirearts.org for the schedule.

The [RISD Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/rhodeisland/providence/risd_museum) on Benefit Street is one of the strongest small art museums in New England, with particular depth in American decorative arts, textiles, and contemporary work. [Brown University](/northamerica/unitedstates/rhodeisland/providence/brown_university) and the surrounding [College Hill](/northamerica/unitedstates/rhodeisland/providence/college_hill) neighborhood are worth an afternoon: the architecture runs from colonial-era wood-frames to Beaux-Arts, and Thayer Street has the coffee shops and bookstores to match. The [Providence Athenaeum](/northamerica/unitedstates/rhodeisland/providence/providence_athenaeum) on Benefit Street — a haunt of both Edgar Allan Poe and H.P. Lovecraft — is open to the public and worth ten minutes of anyone's time.

Providence is compact and walkable. The train station is a short walk from downtown, and Amtrak's Northeast Regional and Acela stop here, making it an easy day trip from New York (3 hrs) or Boston (45 min). The [Rhode Island State House](/northamerica/unitedstates/rhodeisland/providence/rhode_island_state_house) dome — one of the largest self-supporting marble domes in the world — is visible from much of the city.
