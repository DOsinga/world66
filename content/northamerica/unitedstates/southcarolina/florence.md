Florence sits at the intersection of I-95 and I-20 in the Pee Dee region of South Carolina, roughly halfway between New York and Miami. Most visitors encounter it as a stop on the interstate, but there is enough here to make it more than a fuel break.

The downtown area has a small but pleasant historic district centred on the [Florence County Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/southcarolina/florence/florence_county_museum), which covers local history from the Civil War through the railroad era. Florence's most sobering site is the [Florence Stockade](/northamerica/unitedstates/southcarolina/florence/florence_stockade), a Confederate prisoner-of-war camp that held up to 18,000 Union soldiers in 1864–65; over 2,800 died here. The Florence National Cemetery on the south side of town marks the site. For those interested in Cold War oddities, the [Mars Bluff Crater](/northamerica/unitedstates/southcarolina/florence/mars_bluff_crater) — where the US Air Force accidentally dropped a nuclear bomb on a residential neighbourhood in 1958 — is about 12 km east.

Florence is a practical overnight stop for anyone heading to [Myrtle Beach](/northamerica/unitedstates/southcarolina/myrtlebeach), about 90 minutes east, or [Charleston](/northamerica/unitedstates/southcarolina/charleston), about 2.5 hours south.
