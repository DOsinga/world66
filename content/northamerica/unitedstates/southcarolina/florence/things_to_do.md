Florence's most significant attraction is its Civil War history. The [Florence Stockade](/northamerica/unitedstates/southcarolina/florence/florence_stockade), a Confederate prisoner-of-war camp that held up to 18,000 Union soldiers in brutal conditions in 1864–65, is commemorated at the Florence National Cemetery on the south side of town. More than 2,800 prisoners died here. The War Between the States Museum on West Palmetto Street covers the stockade and the broader conflict in the Pee Dee region.

The [Florence County Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/southcarolina/florence/florence_county_museum) in the downtown historic district covers local history from prehistoric times through the railroad era to the present. The Florence Downtown Historic District itself — with its concentration of late 19th and early 20th-century commercial buildings — is worth a short walk.

For those interested in Cold War oddities, the [Mars Bluff Crater](/northamerica/unitedstates/southcarolina/florence/mars_bluff_crater) marks where a US Air Force B-47 accidentally dropped a nuclear bomb in 1958. The bomb's conventional trigger detonated on impact, destroying a farmhouse and injuring six people. A historical marker explains the incident; the crater is on private land about 12 km east of town.
