Greenville sits at the edge of the Blue Ridge foothills in upstate South Carolina, about 100 miles northwest of [Columbia](/northamerica/unitedstates/southcarolina/columbia). What was once a mill city has become one of the American South's most striking downtown revivals — the sort of place that surprises visitors who weren't expecting much and ends up keeping them longer than planned.

The centrepiece is Falls Park on the Reedy River, a 32-acre park built around a waterfall that was hidden beneath an elevated road for decades. When the highway came down in 2004, the falls were revealed and the Liberty Bridge — a slender, curving pedestrian span — was built to cross them. The park and Main Street together form a walkable core that locals are genuinely proud of: good restaurants, independent shops, and the Peace Center performing arts complex all within easy reach of each other.

The West End neighbourhood, just south of the falls, has the city's best baseball experience at Fluor Field — a minor-league ballpark modelled on Fenway Park — and the small Shoeless Joe Jackson Museum honouring the Greenville-born player whose career ended in the 1919 World Series scandal. To the north, the Greenville County Museum of Art holds a serious collection of American Southern art, while the Upcountry History Museum covers the region's often-overlooked past.

Beyond downtown, Cleveland Park and the Greenville Zoo offer a quieter half-day, and the Blue Ridge foothills begin just thirty minutes north, with Table Rock State Park and its waterfalls a straightforward day trip from the city.
