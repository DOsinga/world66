Hilton Head Island is a twelve-mile barrier island off the South Carolina coast, connected to the mainland by a causeway and known across the American Southeast as one of the most popular resort destinations on the Atlantic seaboard. Around two million people visit each year, drawn by the beaches, the golf courses, and an unusual degree of ecological planning that keeps the island looking green and unhurried despite the crowds.

The island was one of the first ecologically planned resorts in the United States. Light pollution ordinances protect the nesting grounds of loggerhead sea turtles. Live oaks, palmetto trees, and salt marshes are woven through the resort developments rather than bulldozed for them, so even the resort areas feel sheltered and natural. Dolphins surface close to the beach; alligators sun themselves by the lagoons. Wildlife is genuinely part of daily life here.

Golf and tennis are the island's main draws for sports visitors — there are more than two dozen golf courses, including the Harbour Town Links at Sea Pines, which hosts the PGA's Heritage Classic. The beaches are wide, hard-packed, and backed by dunes, and the tidal creeks and marshes behind the island are excellent for kayaking and boat tours. Miles of paved cycling paths run through nature preserves and connect the various resort areas. Sea Pines Plantation, at the southern end of the island, is the largest and most self-contained resort community, with its own marina, lighthouse, and beach access.
