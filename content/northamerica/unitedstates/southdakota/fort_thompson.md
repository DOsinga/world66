Fort Thompson sits along the banks of the Missouri River near [Big Bend Dam](/northamerica/unitedstates/southdakota/fort_thompson/big_bend_dam), in the middle of the Crow Creek Sioux Reservation in central South Dakota. It is one of the poorest counties in the nation, but what the area lacks in wealth it makes up for in stark beauty: wide sky, open hills, and the blue expanse of [Lake Sharpe](/northamerica/unitedstates/southdakota/fort_thompson/lake_sharpe) — the reservoir the dam created — stretching north toward Pierre.

The landscape carries a complicated history. The dam, completed in 1963, flooded much of the original townsite and reservation bottomland, displacing Crow Creek Sioux families who were never adequately compensated. The [Fort Thompson Mounds](/northamerica/unitedstates/southdakota/fort_thompson/fort_thompson_mounds) — ancient burial and ceremonial mounds that survived on higher ground — are the most historically significant site, a reminder of a much deeper continuum of habitation here. There is not much to do in the conventional tourist sense, but the back roads, the reservoir, and the chance to understand this stretch of the Missouri on its own terms make it worth a stop for travelers crossing South Dakota who want more than Mount Rushmore.
