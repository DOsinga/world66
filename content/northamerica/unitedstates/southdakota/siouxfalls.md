Sioux Falls is South Dakota's largest city by a wide margin, a growing plains metropolis built around the waterfalls of the Big Sioux River. [Falls Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/southdakota/siouxfalls/falls_park), where the river drops over exposed quartzite ledges in the middle of town, is the obvious first stop — the pink Sioux quartzite gives the cascades an unexpected warmth, and the ruins of a 19th-century mill add to the scene. The [Arc of Dreams](/northamerica/unitedstates/southdakota/siouxfalls/arc_of_dreams), a soaring stainless steel sculpture spanning the river near the park entrance, has become the city's most recognisable landmark.

Downtown has been revitalised around Phillips Avenue and the [Washington Pavilion](/northamerica/unitedstates/southdakota/siouxfalls/washington_pavilion), a former school building converted into an arts and science centre. The [SculptureWalk](/northamerica/unitedstates/southdakota/siouxfalls/sculpture_walk) along the main streets rotates annually, and the restaurant scene is better than you would expect for a mid-sized plains city — [Lakota cuisine at Watecha Bowl](/northamerica/unitedstates/southdakota/siouxfalls/watecha_bowl), Vietnamese restaurants reflecting recent immigration, and a handful of craft breweries including [Fernson Downtown](/northamerica/unitedstates/southdakota/siouxfalls/fernson_downtown).

The [Old Courthouse Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/southdakota/siouxfalls/old_courthouse_museum) in its magnificent pink quartzite building tells a surprising story: at the turn of the 20th century, Sioux Falls was the divorce capital of America, drawing wealthy Eastern clients who took advantage of South Dakota's permissive laws.

Sioux Falls is not a destination most travelers seek out, but it rewards a night or two and is a practical base for trips to the [Black Hills](/northamerica/unitedstates/southdakota/blackhills) or the Badlands, both about four hours west.
