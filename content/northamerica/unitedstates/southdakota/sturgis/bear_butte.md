Bear Butte is a laccolith — a dome of igneous rock that pushed up through the sedimentary layers and now stands some 1,200 feet above the surrounding prairie, visible for miles in every direction. The mountain has been sacred to Northern Plains peoples, including the Lakota and Cheyenne, for thousands of years, and continues to serve as a site of fasting and vision quests today.

The two-mile summit trail gains about 1,100 feet and rewards you with views across four states on a clear day. The trail passes through ponderosa pine and meadow, and the mountain hosts raptors, pronghorn, and bison in the state park below. The state park itself has a small visitor centre explaining the geological and cultural history, a lake, camping, and horse trails. It is one of the most underrated stops in the Black Hills.
