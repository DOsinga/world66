Fort Meade was an active US Army post from 1878 to 1944, housing cavalry units throughout the Indian Wars, the Spanish-American War, and both World Wars before mechanisation made horse cavalry obsolete. The Cavalry Museum occupies the original buildings around the historic parade ground and traces this full arc — from the frontier missions of the 1880s to the transition to armour in the 1940s.

The exhibits cover Native American history and the context of the Black Hills conflict, daily life in the frontier cavalry, the Buffalo Soldiers (Black regiments stationed here), and the fort's role in both World Wars. Most of the original buildings still stand around the parade ground: barracks, officers' quarters, and the quartermaster stores, giving you a strong sense of what garrison life looked like. The adjacent Fort Meade National Cemetery and the Old Fort Meade Cemetery are both worth a short visit.
