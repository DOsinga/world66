  The building served as Clarksville's post office and federal courthouse before becoming a museum. During the Civil War, Clarksville changed hands multiple times between Union and Confederate forces, and the federal building was seized by both sides. Union forces permanently occupied the town in 1862; the post office reopened and the building has been in continuous federal or public use ever since.

The Customs House Museum and Cultural Center is the cultural centrepiece of downtown Clarksville, housed in a striking Italianate building completed in 1898. It is the second-largest general-interest museum in Tennessee, covering art, history, and science across multiple galleries. The permanent collection includes works by notable Tennessee and American artists, exhibits on the region's history from Native American settlement through the Civil War to the present, and rotating science and natural history exhibitions.

The building itself is worth the visit: the ornate Victorian facade with its stone detailing, soaring interior with cast-iron columns, and the restored period post office section. The museum runs a busy programme of temporary exhibitions and community events. Admission is very reasonably priced and free on certain days.
