A few minutes south of Cleveland, just north of the Tennessee–Georgia line, **Red Clay State Historic Park** preserves the site of the last seat of the Cherokee Nation east of the Mississippi. From 1832 until the **Trail of Tears** in 1838, the General Council of the Cherokee Nation met under brush arbours by a blue limestone spring here, having been forced out of their previous capital at New Echota by Georgia state law.

The park has a small but excellent **interpretive centre** with displays on Cherokee government, the legal struggle against removal, and the forced march itself; outside, a series of reconstructed council houses, a sleeping hut and a farmhouse mark the meeting ground. A short trail leads down to the **Blue Hole spring** that gave the council its water. The site is sober and quietly powerful and almost never crowded.

The state of Georgia operates a paired site at New Echota, an hour to the south.
