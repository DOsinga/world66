The Pancake Pantry has been serving breakfast on the main strip since 1961, which by Gatlinburg standards makes it practically ancient. The menu runs to some two dozen pancake varieties — wild blueberry, sweet potato, Austrian apple, buttermilk — plus eggs, country ham, and the kind of large American breakfast that sets you up for a full day on mountain trails. The coffee is reliable, the service is practiced and efficient, and the food consistently delivers what it promises.

The queue is real. On summer and fall weekends, the line extends out the door and down the sidewalk well before 9am, and the wait can stretch to an hour or more. The calculus is straightforward: either come early (doors open at 7am), come late (after 10:30 when the first wave has cleared), or accept the wait as part of the Gatlinburg experience. Most people who've eaten here once come back, which tells you something. It's not fancy, but it's the kind of breakfast that's hard to do better.
