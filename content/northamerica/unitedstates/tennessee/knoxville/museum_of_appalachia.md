The Museum of Appalachia sits about 16 miles north of downtown Knoxville in the town of Norris, and the drive is worth making. Founded by John Rice Irwin and opened in 1969, it is one of the most remarkable folk museums in America — a 35-acre open-air complex of authentic log cabins, barns, and outbuildings moved from across Appalachia, furnished with thousands of original artefacts.

The museum holds over 35,000 objects collected from families across the region: tools, musical instruments, quilts, furniture, and the accumulated material life of mountain culture over two centuries. The collection grew from Irwin's personal obsession with preserving Appalachian material culture before it disappeared. The resulting place has the feeling of something genuinely preserved rather than reconstructed — messy, dense, and specific in a way that sanitised heritage sites never are. The annual Tennessee Fall Homecoming festival in October brings traditional musicians and craftspeople from across the region and is one of the better folk events in the Southeast.
