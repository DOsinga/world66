Old Gray Cemetery opened in 1852 as Knoxville's main burial ground and remains a quiet, pleasingly Gothic green space north of downtown. The cemetery contains the graves of several significant Knoxville figures, including William Gibbs McAdoo (Treasury Secretary under Woodrow Wilson) and Joseph Mabry, whose family feud ended in a 1882 triple shooting in front of a crowd of witnesses on Gay Street.

The grounds feature elaborate Victorian funerary sculpture — tall obelisks, draped urns, weeping angels — and mature trees that make the place genuinely pleasant to walk through. The Friends of Old Gray publish a self-guided walking tour map that gives context to the notable monuments. It is the kind of place that rewards visitors who like the combination of local history and Gothic atmosphere. Admission is free.
