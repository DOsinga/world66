  The 1982 World's Fair was the last major international exposition held in the United States.
  The theme was energy — a prescient choice in the post-oil-crisis era — and over 11 million
  people attended. When it closed, Knoxville found itself with a park, a performance amphitheatre,
  and the Sunsphere. Not everyone expected the fair to succeed; skeptics called it "a fair
  for a city that nobody visits." They were wrong.

World's Fair Park is the green legacy of the 1982 World's Fair — a 10-acre urban park between downtown and the University of Tennessee campus, centred on a reflecting pool and framed by the Sunsphere tower. The park hosts outdoor concerts at the Tennessee Amphitheater and the Lawson McGhee Library sits on its eastern edge.

It is a pleasant place to walk on any day, with the combination of open lawn, mature trees, and the geometry of the Sunsphere making for good photographs. The Knoxville Museum of Art faces the park on its west side. On summer evenings the park fills for concerts and community events, and the Dogwood Arts Festival each April takes the park as one of its focal points.
