Grisham's 1991 breakthrough thriller is set largely in Memphis, following a young Harvard Law graduate who takes what seems like a dream job at a small Memphis law firm — only to discover that his new employers are deeply connected to the Chicago mob. The Memphis backdrop is specific and convincing: the firm's offices in the mid-rise business district, the restaurants on the Beale Street side of downtown, the residential feel of East Memphis where the lawyers live in large houses with manicured lawns. Grisham was himself a lawyer in nearby Southaven, Mississippi, and knows this milieu from the inside.

The novel captures something real about the Memphis professional class of the late 1980s — its ambitions, its conservatism, its sense of being a large city in a small-city world. It is not literature, but it is good entertainment, and it will put you in the particular atmosphere of the place.
