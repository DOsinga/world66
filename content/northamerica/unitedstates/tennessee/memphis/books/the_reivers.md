Faulkner's final novel, published in 1962, follows an eleven-year-old boy, a car thief, and a horse trader on a picaresque journey from Jefferson, Mississippi, to Memphis — the big city, both thrilling and morally chaotic, that stands just beyond the horizon of the fictional Yoknapatawpha County. Memphis appears in the novel as a place of temptation and consequence, specifically in the person of Miss Reba's brothel in the red-light district. It is Faulkner at his most relaxed and comic, and the portrait of early twentieth-century Memphis — the dirt roads, the automobiles, the vice districts — is drawn from life.

The novel won the Pulitzer Prize posthumously. Faulkner died before he could collect it. For anyone visiting both Memphis and Oxford on the same trip, The Reivers provides a natural literary bridge between the two cities.
