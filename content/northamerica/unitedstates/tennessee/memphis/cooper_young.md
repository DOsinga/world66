Cooper-Young is the neighborhood intersection that gives the district its name — Cooper Street crossing Young Avenue — and the few blocks surrounding it constitute the liveliest independent commercial strip in Memphis. Record shops, vintage clothing stores, an excellent used bookstore, a comic book shop, a fine art gallery, a wine bar, and a rotating cast of independent boutiques occupy storefronts along both streets. The restaurants and bars are reviewed elsewhere on this page, but they form an important part of the neighborhood's character.

The Cooper-Young Community Association hosts an annual festival every September that draws crowds from across the city and beyond. The neighborhood has a self-conscious identity as the progressive, arts-inclined end of Midtown Memphis, which in practice means it is comfortable and well-maintained without being sanitized. The side streets are lined with Craftsman bungalows and small houses that tell the story of Midtown's twentieth-century residential development. It is an easy place to spend an afternoon browsing and eating.
