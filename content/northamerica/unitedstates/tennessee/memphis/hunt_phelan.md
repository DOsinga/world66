The Hunt-Phelan Home is one of the oldest surviving structures in Memphis, built in 1828 by slave trader George Vance and later owned by the Hunt and Phelan families. The house saw the full sweep of Memphis history: it was here when the city was a muddy river trading post, here through the yellow fever epidemics, here through both world wars. Its most dramatic chapter came during the Civil War, when it served successively as Confederate and Union headquarters.

Guided tours of the interior take visitors through the period-furnished rooms and explain the house's complicated history without flinching from the darker elements. The gardens are well-maintained and the house is in better shape than many comparable properties. It is a short walk from the Beale Street entertainment district.
