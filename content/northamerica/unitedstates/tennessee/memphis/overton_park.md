story: >
  In 1966 the Tennessee Department of Transportation announced a plan to drive Interstate 40 through the middle of Overton Park, demolishing 26 acres of old-growth forest. A coalition of citizens sued to stop it. The case — Citizens to Preserve Overton Park v. Volpe — reached the Supreme Court in 1971, which ruled in the citizens' favour. The decision established the principle that federal agencies must take environmental concerns seriously before authorising projects, a ruling that shaped environmental law for decades. The interstate runs to the north of the park, as it has always done.

Overton Park is one of the great urban parks in America — 342 acres of meadow, golf course, and genuinely old-growth forest in the middle of Midtown Memphis. The old-growth section, known as the Old Forest, is extraordinary: an intact hardwood forest of oak, hickory, and beech that has never been logged, right inside a major city. Walking the trails through it, particularly in autumn when the canopy turns, feels nothing like a typical city park.

The park contains the Memphis Zoo, the Memphis Brooks Museum of Art, the Memphis College of Art, a golf course, a shell for outdoor concerts, and miles of walking and cycling trails. It is the social centre of Midtown — on a weekend morning, the parking lots are full with people running, cycling, visiting the zoo, or simply walking in the forest. The park has no admission charge.
