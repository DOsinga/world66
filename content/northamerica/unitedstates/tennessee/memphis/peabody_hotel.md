story: >
  The duck tradition started in the 1930s when the Peabody's general manager, Frank Schutt, returned from a hunting trip in Arkansas and placed his live duck decoys in the hotel's ornate lobby fountain as a prank. Guests found it delightful, and management discovered that people were coming just to see the ducks. A full parade protocol was eventually developed — the ducks descend from the roof in an elevator at 11am, walk down the red carpet to John Philip Sousa's "King Cotton March," spend the day in the fountain, and process out again at 5pm.

The Peabody has been the grandest hotel in Memphis since it opened in its current form in 1925, and it remains the social centre of downtown. The lobby — high ceilings, Italian marble fountain, carved woodwork — is worth seeing on its own, and the twice-daily duck march draws a crowd of tourists and regulars at 11am and 5pm every day. The Duckmaster, an actual job title, leads five North American mallards along a red carpet to Sousa marches.

Beyond the ducks, the Peabody is a genuine piece of Memphis architecture and history. The Skyway ballroom on the top floor has hosted everyone from Tommy Dorsey to Elvis. The Peabody Rooftop Bar is a good spot for drinks above the city. You don't need to be a guest to visit the lobby or the bar; the lobby is essentially a public space.
