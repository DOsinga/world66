  Bradley Academy was founded in 1884 as one of the first schools for African American children in Murfreesboro, built on a site that had been a Union Army encampment during the Civil War. For decades it educated generations of Black students who were excluded from other public schools. The building survived urban renewal that demolished much of Murfreesboro's Black neighbourhood, and its preservation was driven by the community it once served.

Bradley Academy Museum occupies the 1884 schoolhouse that served Murfreesboro's African American community for nearly a century. Now listed on the National Register of Historic Places, it operates as a museum and community centre focusing on the history of Black life in Rutherford County from the antebellum period through the Civil Rights era.

The museum's exhibits document the experience of enslaved people in Middle Tennessee, the formation of freedmen's communities after the Civil War, and the long struggle for educational equality. It is one of the more thoughtful small museums in the region, maintaining a frank and detailed record of a history that is often absent from the Civil War-focused narrative that dominates Murfreesboro's official attractions.
