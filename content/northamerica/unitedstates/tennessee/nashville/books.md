---
title: "Books"
type: section
---

Nashville is built on songs, but it has a strong literary culture too — it is the birthplace of the **Southern Agrarians** (the 1930s Vanderbilt writers' circle), home of **Ann Patchett's Parnassus Books** in Green Hills, and the setting for some of the best country music memoirs ever written.

- **Robert K. Oermann, *A Century of Country* (1999)** — the most readable single-volume history of country music as an industry, much of it set in Nashville.
- **Marshall Chapman, *They Came to Nashville* (2010)** — interviews with songwriters about their first arrival in the city. Funny, vivid, and the best book on the songwriting tradition.
- **Peter Cooper, *Johnny's Cash and Charley's Pride* (2017)** — collected pieces by the *Tennessean*'s long-time music critic, full of dispatches from Music Row.
- **Robert Hicks, *The Widow of the South* (2005)** — historical novel set just south of Nashville at the Battle of Franklin, useful background to the Civil War history of the area.
- **Ann Patchett, *State of Wonder* (2011)** — not set in Nashville, but written there by the bookseller and novelist who has done more than anyone to keep independent literary life alive in the city.
