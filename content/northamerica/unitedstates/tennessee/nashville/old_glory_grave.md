In the quiet **Nashville City Cemetery** on Fourth Avenue South lies the grave of Captain William Driver, the 19th-century sea captain who coined the phrase **"Old Glory"** for the American flag. Driver retired from the sea to Nashville in the 1830s; during the Civil War, with Tennessee in Confederate hands, he hid the original Old Glory flag inside a quilt sewn by his daughters, and brought it out in 1862 when Union troops took the city.

The flag itself is now in the Smithsonian. Driver's grave, with a stone-carved flag draped over the marker, is one of the few US gravesites legally permitted to fly the American flag 24 hours a day. A quiet, slightly sombre destination, and one of the more historically resonant small spots in Nashville.
