The home of the seventh President, Andrew Jackson, sits about twelve miles east of downtown Nashville on a thousand-acre plantation. The Greek Revival mansion, built between 1819 and 1836, is one of the best-preserved presidential homes in the country — the original wallpaper, furniture, and family possessions remain in place. Around the house, the grounds include slave cabins, the smokehouse, the kitchen garden, Jackson's tomb, and the Old Hermitage Church.

The museum is unsparing about Jackson's role as a slaveholder and the architect of the Indian Removal Act of 1830, which forced the Cherokee, Choctaw, and other south-eastern peoples onto the Trail of Tears. Allow three hours; admission includes the house tour, the grounds walk, and the small history museum at the visitor centre.
