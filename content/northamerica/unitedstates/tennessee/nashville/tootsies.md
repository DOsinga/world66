The purple-painted building on Lower Broadway behind the Ryman has been a working-musician bar since 1960 and is the most storied of all the Nashville honky-tonks. The ground floor is a narrow, packed room with a small stage at the back; two more floors of bars and live music stack above. Live country bands play from 10 a.m. to last call, every day, no cover.

The walls are papered with autographed photos of the artists who drank, wrote, and sang here. Despite its fame, Tootsie's remains a real bar — the beer is cold, the band tip jar is the only money the musicians make, and the music is louder and rawer than at the more polished celebrity venues down the strip.
