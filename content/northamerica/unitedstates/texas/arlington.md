Arlington sits midway between [Dallas](/northamerica/unitedstates/texas/dallas) and [Fort Worth](/northamerica/unitedstates/texas/fort_worth), and with over 400,000 residents it is anything but a suburb. The city's identity is built almost entirely around its entertainment district — a remarkable concentration of major venues in a single square mile: AT&T Stadium (the Dallas Cowboys), Globe Life Field (the Texas Rangers), Six Flags Over Texas, and the Texas Live dining complex. On game days, the area fills with tailgating crowds and the scale of the whole enterprise becomes clear.

Beyond the stadium zone, Arlington has more to offer than its entertainment-park reputation suggests. River Legacy Parks provides 1,300 acres of wooded trail along the Trinity River, and the new National Medal of Honor Museum (opened 2024) is a serious and worthwhile attraction. The University of Texas at Arlington anchors a district with more life than the surrounding sprawl would suggest.

The single most important fact about visiting Arlington: it is the largest city in the United States without a public transportation system. You will need a car or rideshare for everything. DFW International Airport is 20 minutes north; Dallas Love Field is about 30 minutes east. Once there, the entertainment district is walkable within itself, but nothing else in the city is accessible on foot.
