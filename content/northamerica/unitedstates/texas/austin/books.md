---
title: "Books"
type: section
---

**Dazed and Confused** (dir. Richard Linklater, 1993) is a film rather than a book, but it captures pre-tech Austin better than almost any written account — the one that made the moonlight tower famous and cemented Linklater's Austin-as-state-of-mind sensibility.

**The Gay Place** by Billy Lee Brammer (1961) is a novel about Texas politics in the late 1950s, set in a thinly fictionalised Austin with a governor clearly modelled on Lyndon Johnson. Brammer was Johnson's aide and the novel was scandalous at publication; it is now considered one of the great Texas novels, and its portrait of Capitol-culture Austin is irreplaceable.

**Lonesome Dove** by Larry McMurtry (1985) is not set in Austin — it follows a cattle drive from South Texas to Montana — but it is the definitive Texas novel and reading it before a visit to Texas changes how you see the landscape. McMurtry grew up in Archer City, northwest of Fort Worth, and his Texas is both specific and universal.

**The Raven** by Marquis James (1929), a biography of Sam Houston, tells the story of one of the most extraordinary American political figures of the 19th century — hero of San Jacinto, president of the Republic of Texas, governor of two states, expelled from the mansion you can visit on Colorado Street for refusing to join the Confederacy.
