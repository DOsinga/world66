  Henry Rosenberg was a Swiss immigrant who came to Galveston in the 1840s and made a fortune in dry goods and banking. When he died in 1893, he left his estate to the city with instructions to build a public library and a series of drinking fountains for "man and beast" — the fountains, designed with separate troughs for dogs and horses, still stand at several locations around the city. Rosenberg never married and left no family; his money went entirely to Galveston.

The Rosenberg Library, opened in 1904, is the oldest continuously operating public library in Texas and one of the finest Carnegie-era library buildings in the American South. The brick Beaux-Arts building on Sealy Avenue houses not just a working public library but the Galveston and Texas History Center, one of the best local history archives in the state.

The archive holds maps, photographs, manuscripts, and records documenting Galveston from its founding through the 20th century, with particular strength in the 1900 hurricane and the city's extraordinary recovery. The newspaper archive is extensive. Researchers can use the collections by appointment; the general public can visit the main library and the gallery-quality exhibitions that rotate through the history center. The building's reading rooms, with their high ceilings and ornamental plasterwork, are worth seeing in their own right.
