Under the Hood Café operated at 17 South College Street in Killeen from 2009 until 2013, functioning as a GI-rights resource centre and informal community space for soldiers from Fort Hood. It offered free coffee, legal advice, and a sympathetic ear to service members struggling with the moral and psychological costs of repeated combat deployments during the peak years of operations in Iraq and Afghanistan.

The café is closed now and the building is unremarkable, but it belongs to a specific tradition with deep American roots: the off-base coffeehouse as a site of soldier dissent. During the Vietnam War, a network of such places provided soldiers with information about conscientious objector status and discharge options. Under the Hood revived that model in the twenty-first century, and its brief existence generated national attention. The corner is worth a moment if you are thinking about the less visible history of what it means to live and serve in a military town.
