Pampa sits in the open ranch country of the Texas Panhandle, about an hour northeast of [Amarillo](/northamerica/unitedstates/texas/amarillo). It is not a polished tourist town, and that is part of the point: the grain elevators, oil-field roads, and wind-scoured streets feel very much like the High Plains.

The town's best reason to stop is its local history. The [White Deer Land Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/texas/pampa/white_deer_land_museum) tells the story of the ranching company that helped shape Gray County, while the [Woody Guthrie Folk Music Center](/northamerica/unitedstates/texas/pampa/woody_guthrie_folk_music_center) remembers the years when Guthrie lived here and began becoming a musician. The smaller [Freedom Museum USA](/northamerica/unitedstates/texas/pampa/freedom_museum_usa) adds a World War II air-base story that is easy to miss from the highway.

Pampa is best as a deliberate Panhandle detour rather than a long stay. Come during the Top o' Texas Rodeo if you can, or pair the town with a slow drive through the Canadian River breaks and the ranching country north of I-40.
