Port Aransas sits on the northern tip of Mustang Island, a barrier island on the Texas Gulf Coast just east of [Corpus Christi](/northamerica/unitedstates/texas/corpuschristi). It is the kind of relaxed beach town that Texans have been driving to for generations: wide sandy beaches, good fishing, and an easy pace that starts to feel obligatory the moment you cross the ferry from the mainland.

The fishing is genuinely excellent — Port Aransas is one of the premier sportfishing destinations on the Gulf Coast. Offshore, the warm Gulf waters hold kingfish, mahi-mahi, and wahoo; in the bay and channels, redfish, flounder, and speckled trout are year-round targets. Charter boats are available at the [marina](/northamerica/unitedstates/texas/port_aransas/port_aransas_marina), ranging from small skiff rentals to fully equipped offshore vessels. The annual fishing rodeos in summer draw competitors from across Texas.

The [beaches on the Gulf side](/northamerica/unitedstates/texas/port_aransas/mustang_island_beach) are broad and gently sloping, good for swimming and shelling. Dolphins are common in the shipping channel, and the [Leonabelle Turnbull Birding Center](/northamerica/unitedstates/texas/port_aransas/leonabelle_turnbull_birding_center) at the north end of town is an excellent stop on the Great Texas Coastal Birding Trail. You reach Port Aransas by free ferry from Aransas Pass — the short crossing is part of the arrival ritual.
