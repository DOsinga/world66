  On January 22, 1927, a passenger train struck a bus at this crossing, killing ten members of the Baylor University basketball team who were heading to Austin for a game. The tragedy prompted Texas to pass its first law requiring vehicles to stop at all railroad crossings, and a bronze plaque on the bridge commemorates the ten young men who died. The bridge was renamed in their honour.

The Immortal Ten Bridge carries Mays Street over the railroad tracks in the heart of old Round Rock. To most visitors it is simply a road bridge in the middle of the historic district, but a plaque on the railing marks it as a memorial to one of the more poignant disasters in Texas sports history — the 1927 train crash that killed ten members of the Baylor University basketball team.

The bridge sits at the southern edge of the old commercial district, just a short walk from the Round Rock Commercial Historic District. It is a quiet spot now, with the railroad below and the old limestone storefronts nearby, and the memorial plaque rewards those who take a moment to read it.
