story: >
  When the Aztec Theatre opened in 1926, it was the most elaborately decorated movie palace in Texas and one of the most spectacular in the United States. The exterior is covered in hand-painted Aztec murals by the San Antonio artist José Arpa. The interior featured a 1,400-pipe organ, an artificial sky ceiling with moving clouds, and plaster sculptures of Mexican deities. Its architect, Robert Kelly, designed it to evoke a Mexican jungle temple — which made it exotic and disturbing in equal measure to San Antonio's Anglo establishment.
---

A 1926 movie palace on Commerce Street downtown, restored to its original Aztec-themed glory after decades of neglect. The exterior is covered in Aztec-inspired painted decorations; inside, the 2,800-seat auditorium has a barrel-vaulted ceiling painted like an open sky and walls covered in carved and painted plaster Mesoamerican motifs. When the lights dim and the ceiling darkens to starlight, it remains one of the most extraordinary theatre experiences in the country.

The Aztec now hosts concerts, comedy, and theatrical events rather than films. Check the schedule and try to see something here — the venue is reason enough regardless of what's on. Tours of the building are sometimes available on non-event days.
