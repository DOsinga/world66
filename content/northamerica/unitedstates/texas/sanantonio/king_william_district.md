story: >
  King William Street was named by German immigrants who settled this neighbourhood in the 1860s and 1870s in honour of Kaiser Wilhelm I of Prussia. It became the most fashionable address in San Antonio during the Gilded Age — cotton merchants, brewery owners, and cattle barons built elaborate Victorian mansions here. The area fell into decline after World War I and was nearly demolished for urban renewal in the 1960s; a community campaign to designate it a historic district saved it in 1967.
---

San Antonio's finest Victorian neighbourhood, twelve blocks of Italianate, Queen Anne, and Greek Revival mansions built by the German merchant class between 1870 and 1900, running south from downtown along the King William Street. The houses are well-maintained and privately owned; the streetscape is one of the most coherent Victorian historic districts in the American South.

The neighbourhood connects to the River Walk at its northern end, making it an easy walk from downtown. The Guenther House at the south end (home of Pioneer Flour Mills, the oldest continuously operating mill in Texas) has been turned into a museum, restaurant, and bakery — a popular brunch spot on weekends. Walk the full length of the street for the architecture, then cut east to South Alamo and Southtown for coffee and food.
