story: >
  San Fernando Cathedral has a contested interior: a marble sarcophagus in the sanctuary is said to hold the ashes of the Alamo defenders, including Bowie, Crockett, and Travis. Historians dispute this — the remains were likely scattered or destroyed — but the tradition persists. During General Santa Anna's siege of the Alamo in 1836, he flew a red flag of no-quarter from the cathedral's tower to signal that no prisoners would be taken.

The oldest standing church in Texas, built between 1738 and 1749 by Canary Island colonists brought to San Antonio to establish a permanent civilian settlement. The original building has been expanded and rebuilt, and what stands today is largely a 19th-century Gothic Revival facade grafted onto an older core, but the sanctuary space preserves the original Baroque altar and a sense of genuine age.

San Fernando sits on Main Plaza (Plaza de Armas), at the literal centre of the city — a historical marker on the floor notes the competing claims of Main Plaza and Military Plaza for the title of San Antonio's original civic centre. Free entry; the cathedral is an active parish and often busy with worshippers even on weekday afternoons.
