  The building is sometimes called the Governor's Palace, but it was never actually the official residence of a Spanish governor — it was the administrative headquarters of the presidio captain and his family. The keystone above the entrance, carved with the arms of the Habsburg dynasty and dated 1749, is the most important Spanish colonial inscription in Texas.

The only complete example of an aristocratic 18th-century Spanish colonial residence remaining in the United States. Built around 1749 as the headquarters of the captain of the Presidio de Béxar, it has ten rooms arranged around a courtyard, with thick adobe walls, carved wooden lintels, and a peaceful garden. The interior is furnished in period style — heavy colonial furniture, iron locks, tiled floors.

The building was falling apart by the early 20th century, used as a tamal shop, a saloon, and a school at various points. It was purchased by the city in 1928 and carefully restored. A small admission fee; open Tuesday–Sunday. One of the most authentic colonial interiors in the American Southwest.
