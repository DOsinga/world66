Sweetwater is a small West Texas city on I-20, halfway between Abilene and the rolling edge of the Panhandle. At first glance it looks like a practical highway stop, but it has a stranger and more specific identity than most towns its size: wartime women pilots, wind farms on the horizon, and one of Texas's most peculiar annual festivals.

The best reason to stop is the National WASP WWII Museum at Avenger Field, where Women Airforce Service Pilots trained during the Second World War. The story is national, but the setting is local and physical: hangars, runways, flat sky, and the sense that this dry stretch of Texas briefly sat at the edge of a very modern war.

Sweetwater also leans into its oddities. Nolan County's wind turbines make the surrounding country feel like an energy landscape, while the Sweetwater Jaycees Rattlesnake Roundup brings snake hunts, cooking, contests, and a fairground atmosphere every March. It is not a town to build a whole trip around, but if you are crossing West Texas with a spare half day, it has more personality than the map suggests.
