Bluff is a tiny town wedged in a red-rock canyon along the San Juan River in southeastern Utah. Founded in 1880 by one of the last Mormon wagon train expeditions — the famous Hole-in-the-Rock pioneers who hauled their wagons down a near-vertical sandstone crack — Bluff has since evolved into something unexpected: a funky artist's colony with a handful of good places to eat and sleep.

Savvy southwest travellers use Bluff as a base. [Cedar Mesa and Grand Gulch](/northamerica/unitedstates/utah/bluff/cedar_mesa) are just to the north, with hundreds of Ancestral Puebloan ruins accessible on foot. Monument Valley is an hour south. The Navajo Nation stretches in every direction. [San Juan River float trips](/northamerica/unitedstates/utah/bluff/san_juan_river) run daily down to Mexican Hat and beyond — one of the quieter and more beautiful river journeys in the Colorado Plateau country.

The [17 Room Ruin](/northamerica/unitedstates/utah/bluff/seventeen_room_ruin) on the canyon walls above town provides an accessible introduction to the Ancestral Puebloan archaeology of the region. [Comb Ridge](/northamerica/unitedstates/utah/bluff/comb_ridge) to the northwest marks the edge of the Navajo Nation and contains its own network of canyon ruins.
