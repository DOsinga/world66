Cedar City sits on I-15 in southern Utah, high enough for cold winters and close enough to the canyon country to be useful without feeling like a pure gateway town. It is a college town, a theatre town, and a practical base for the Markagunt Plateau.

The reason to stay in town is the [Utah Shakespeare Festival](/northamerica/unitedstates/utah/cedar_city/utah_shakespeare_festival), which gives Cedar City a cultural weight unusual for a place this size. Southern Utah University keeps the centre lively, and the [Southern Utah Museum of Art](/northamerica/unitedstates/utah/cedar_city/southern_utah_museum_of_art) adds a strong regional-art stop next to the theatres.

Cedar City's older story is iron, wagons, and Mormon settlement. [Frontier Homestead State Park Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/utah/cedar_city/frontier_homestead_state_park_museum) is the best place to understand that side of town before driving into the forests and lava-dark plateaus east of Main Street.

The outdoors are close and genuinely worth the detour. [Cedar Breaks National Monument](/northamerica/unitedstates/utah/cedar_city/cedar_breaks_national_monument) is the local showpiece, a high rim of bristlecone pine, wildflower meadows, and a red-rock amphitheatre more than 10,000 feet above sea level. In winter, nearby [Brian Head Resort](/northamerica/unitedstates/utah/cedar_city/brian_head_resort) turns the same high country into southern Utah's main ski area.
