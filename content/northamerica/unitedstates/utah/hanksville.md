Tiny Hanksville is surrounded by overwhelmingly dramatic country. Home to around 300 people, this desert crossroads has a colourful past as a polygamist hideout, an outlaw refuge — Butch Cassidy and his Wild Bunch reportedly frequented the area — and a hardscrabble ranching town. Even today it is not easy to get here; long drives across burning, empty desert are pretty much the only way in.

Local landmarks include the [Hollow Mountain](/northamerica/unitedstates/utah/hanksville/hollow_mountain) store and gas station, carved into a sandstone knob, and the restored Wolverton Mill at the BLM office, which was airlifted by helicopter from its original perch on Mount Ellen. Gas, food and lodging are available but limited — fill up here before heading into the backcountry.

Hanksville sits at a junction of routes into some of southern Utah's most spectacular landscape. Highway 24 heads west to [Capitol Reef National Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/utah/capitolreef), Highway 95 runs south to Hite Marina and Blanding, and a dusty dirt road wanders into the [Henry Mountains](/northamerica/unitedstates/utah/hanksville/henry_mountains) toward Lonesome Beaver Campground on Mount Ellen. Just northwest of town, the [Mars Desert Research Station](/northamerica/unitedstates/utah/hanksville/mars_desert_research_station) operates a simulated Mars habitat in a landscape that NASA researchers consider one of the most Mars-like environments on Earth.
