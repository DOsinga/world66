Grand Staircase-Escalante National Monument, designated in 1996, is the largest national monument in the lower 48 states and one of the most remote. The monument covers nearly 2,900 square kilometres of canyon country, desert plateaus, and badlands, most of it accessible only on dirt roads or on foot. Kanab serves as the western gateway, with the Kanab field office of the BLM providing maps and permits.

The monument's highlights include the Coyote Buttes (home of The Wave, one of the most photogenic sandstone formations in the Southwest), the Escalante River canyons, the Cottonwood Canyon Road, and a continuous series of viewpoints along Scenic Byway 12 to the north. Permits for The Wave are allocated by lottery months in advance. The rest of the monument is less regulated but requires a high-clearance vehicle for most of the good stuff. Spring (April–May) and autumn (September–October) are the best seasons.
