Manti is a small town in the Sanpete Valley of central Utah, founded in 1849 by Mormon pioneers. It sits beneath the striking white limestone [Manti Utah Temple](/northamerica/unitedstates/utah/manti/manti_utah_temple), perched on a hill above town and visible for miles — one of the oldest and most architecturally impressive temples in the state.

The town's main claim to fame is the annual [Manti Pageant](/northamerica/unitedstates/utah/manti/manti_pageant), a large outdoor theatrical production held on the temple grounds each June that draws tens of thousands of visitors. Outside pageant season, Manti is a quiet agricultural town with a well-preserved Main Street — the [Sanpete County Courthouse](/northamerica/unitedstates/utah/manti/sanpete_county_courthouse) and [Manti Tabernacle](/northamerica/unitedstates/utah/manti/manti_tabernacle) are both worth a look — and access to the [Manti-La Sal National Forest](/northamerica/unitedstates/utah/manti/manti_la_sal_forest) for hiking, fishing and autumn foliage.
