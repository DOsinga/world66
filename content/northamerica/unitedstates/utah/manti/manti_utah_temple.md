The Manti Utah Temple sits on a limestone promontory above the town, visible from miles in every direction across the flat Sanpete Valley. Built from locally quarried oolite limestone between 1877 and 1888, it is the fifth temple constructed by the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints and one of the most architecturally striking. The twin-towered castellated exterior, with its octagonal spires and textured stone walls, has a distinctly medieval quality unusual in Mormon temple design.

The grounds are open to the public and offer good views across the valley to the mountains beyond. The interior is restricted to members of the church with current temple recommends. Even for non-Mormon visitors, the hilltop setting and the quality of the 19th-century stonework make it a worthwhile stop. The walk up the sloped grounds from town takes about ten minutes.
