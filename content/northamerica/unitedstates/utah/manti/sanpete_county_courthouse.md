The Sanpete County Courthouse stands on North Main Street and is one of the best-preserved examples of Richardsonian Romanesque architecture in Utah outside the major cities. Built in 1894, the red sandstone building has a heavy arched entrance, a prominent clock tower, and the solid, civic confidence of a prosperous frontier county asserting itself. It is listed on the National Register of Historic Places.

The building is still in active use as the county courthouse, so access to the interior is limited to business hours on weekdays. The exterior is worth a look simply for the craftsmanship — the stonework, the detailing on the tower, and the contrast with the white limestone of the temple on the hill above make for an interesting visual pairing.
