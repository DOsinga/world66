Arches National Park contains more than 2,000 natural sandstone arches within a compact area of desert canyon, making it one of the most concentrated arch formations on earth. The park is five miles north of Moab on US-191 — close enough that it can feel like an extension of the town rather than a separate wilderness.

[Delicate Arch][delicate_arch] is the most famous: a 52-foot freestanding arch that appears on Utah's licence plates and draws more than a million visitors a year. The 3-mile round-trip hike to reach it is steep but manageable, crossing open slickrock with no shade. Do it at sunrise or late afternoon — midday in summer is brutal. Landscape Arch, in the Devils Garden area at the north end of the park, is longer and more fragile-looking — a thin ribbon of stone spanning 290 feet. The Windows section and Balanced Rock are accessible by short walks from the main road.

The park now requires timed-entry reservations from April to October — book well in advance at recreation.gov. Campground reservations at Devils Garden fill months ahead for spring weekends.
