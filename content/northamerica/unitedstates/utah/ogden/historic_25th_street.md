  In the late 1800s and early 1900s, 25th Street was one of the roughest blocks in the American West — a strip of saloons, brothels, and gambling dens that thrived on the railroad trade. Historic 25th Street runs from Union Station east into the heart of downtown Ogden, and is the most atmospheric block in the city. The original brick buildings — mostly dating from the 1890s to 1920s — now house independent restaurants, microbreweries, coffee shops, antique stores, and a scattering of live music venues. On warm evenings the street fills with people, and it has real energy for a city this size.

The architecture tells the story. Wide facades, pressed-tin ceilings, cast-iron details — these are the bones of a railroad-boom commercial district. A heritage walking tour map is available at the visitor centre near Union Station, and it is worth picking up to understand what each building originally was. A few of the basement establishments still feel like they haven't entirely shaken off their earlier history, which adds to the charm.
