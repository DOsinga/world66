Panguitch is a small ranching town along the Sevier River in south-central Utah, straddling US-89 about 25 miles from the entrance to [Bryce Canyon National Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/utah/panguitch/bryce_canyon). Most visitors use it as a base for exploring Bryce and the surrounding red-rock country — it has classic roadside motels, a handful of diners, and none of the resort-town polish of nearby Springdale or Moab.

The town itself has a quiet charm. The [historic downtown](/northamerica/unitedstates/utah/panguitch/downtown_historic_district) is lined with handsome Victorian-era brick storefronts and pioneer homes built by Mormon settlers in the 1880s — the whole district is listed on the National Register of Historic Places. John D. Lee, the only man convicted for the Mountain Meadows Massacre of 1857, is buried in the local cemetery. The surrounding landscape — sagebrush flats giving way to ponderosa forests and hoodoo-studded canyons — is pure American West.

On the road to Bryce, [Red Canyon](/northamerica/unitedstates/utah/panguitch/red_canyon) delivers similar geology for free: the same orange hoodoos and red cliffs, far fewer people, and good short trails. Fifty-five miles to the west, [Cedar Breaks National Monument](/northamerica/unitedstates/utah/panguitch/cedar_breaks) is a quieter alternative to Bryce with an amphitheater three miles wide and views across the Markagunt Plateau. Panguitch is well-placed as a budget base for all of it — accommodation here costs noticeably less than in the gateway towns right at the park entrances.
