Bryce Canyon is not actually a canyon but a series of natural amphitheaters carved into the edge of the Paunsaugunt Plateau. The landscape is defined by hoodoos — tall, thin spires of rock in shades of orange, red, and white — clustered in dense forests that look like nothing else on earth. The park sits at elevations between 8,000 and 9,100 feet, so the light and temperature shift constantly throughout the day.

The rim trail is the easiest way in: a mostly flat path linking overlooks at Sunrise Point, Sunset Point, Inspiration Point, and Bryce Point. Sunrise and sunset are the best times, when the low light turns the hoodoos a deep amber. For a closer look, the Navajo Loop and Queens Garden trails descend into the amphitheater — together they make a 2.9-mile loop that takes most walkers two to three hours. The Peek-a-Boo Loop (5.5 miles) goes deeper and is usually less crowded.

The park entrance is on UT-12, about 25 miles east of Panguitch. A free shuttle runs between viewpoints in summer, which saves the headache of parking. Entry costs $35 per vehicle and covers a week. Expect crowds at the main viewpoints from May through September — the first overlooks fill early. Plan to be on the rim before 8am or after 5pm for elbow room.

Winter is underrated: the hoodoos dusted in snow against a blue sky are spectacular, and the crowds vanish. Snowshoes and micro-spikes are available for rent near the visitor center.
