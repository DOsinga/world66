Utah Olympic Park, about five miles north of Park City, was built for the 2002 Winter Olympics and hosted the ski jumping, bobsled, luge, and skeleton events. Today it functions as a training facility for US Olympic athletes and as a visitor attraction, with a museum, ski jumping demonstrations in summer, and thrill rides on the bobsled track.

The **Alf Engen Ski Museum** inside the park tells the history of skiing in Utah and the western United States, with a particular focus on the development of the Wasatch ski industry. The Olympic museum section covers the 2002 Games with photographs, equipment, and memorabilia.

The summer bobsled experience — riding the track in a modified sled at speeds up to 80mph — is the biggest draw for non-museum visitors and sells out on busy weekends. Book in advance. Ski jumping demonstrations, using a ramp that lands athletes in a water-filled pool, run on summer weekends and are free to watch.
