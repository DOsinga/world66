  Alta opened in 1938 as one of the first ski resorts in the United States.
  Lift tickets cost one dollar. The resort remains deliberately uncommercial
  by modern standards: no terrain parks, no music on the mountain, and —
  famously — snowboarders are not permitted. Alta is one of only three
  ski resorts in the US that bans snowboards.

Alta is the more serene and tradition-minded of the two resorts in Little Cottonwood Canyon, adjacent to Snowbird. It is skiers-only — no snowboards — and the atmosphere is correspondingly serious. The terrain is excellent, especially in the back bowls and the high cirques above the main lifts, and the snow quality is consistently superb.

Alta has a long history of hosting serious skiers who find Snowbird's crowds less appealing, and the resort still feels less commercialised than most big American ski areas. Stays at the Alta Lodge, one of the oldest ski lodges in the West, are a proper mountain experience.
