  The bison herd on Antelope Island was established in 1893 when 12 animals
  were transported to the island. Today the herd numbers around 700 and is
  managed by annual auction; surplus animals are sold at an autumn roundup

  one of the most significant in North America because it has never been

Antelope Island is a 28,000-acre state park in the Great Salt Lake, connected to the mainland by a causeway 22 miles northwest of Salt Lake City. It has a herd of 700 free-ranging bison, pronghorn antelope, bighorn sheep, coyotes, and extraordinary bird life along the lake shore — up to a million migratory shorebirds at peak season.

The island's beaches are on the hypersaline lake; the water is so buoyant you float effortlessly but the brine flies and the smell are part of the experience. Camping is available. The drive out and back, with stops for bison spotting, makes a good half-day from Salt Lake City.
