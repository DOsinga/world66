The Assembly Hall stands on the southwest corner of Temple Square, a Victorian Gothic building completed in 1882 from granite quarried from Little Cottonwood Canyon — the same stone used for the Salt Lake Temple itself. Its spires and arched windows make it one of the most photogenic buildings in the complex.

The hall is used for public worship services and community concerts, particularly the free Friday and Saturday evening concerts performed by local musicians and orchestras throughout the year. The building's interior features original woodwork and a fine pipe organ. It is often overlooked by visitors in favour of the larger Tabernacle, but the architecture alone makes it worth a look.
