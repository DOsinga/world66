Fort Douglas was established in 1862 by Colonel Patrick Connor and his California Volunteers, ostensibly to protect the Overland Mail route but in practice to keep watch on the Mormon population. The fort remained active for over 130 years; during World War II it housed a prisoner of war camp for German soldiers.

The Fort Douglas Military Museum on the University of Utah campus covers the fort's history from its founding through both World Wars. It is a small, well-presented museum with good collections of Civil War–era equipment, WWI and WWII artefacts, and material on the Japanese American soldiers — the 442nd Regimental Combat Team — who trained nearby. Free admission.
