  The Great Salt Lake is a remnant of the ancient Lake Bonneville, which once
  covered most of northwestern Utah to a depth of nearly 1,000 feet. Its
  fluctuating salinity — sometimes eight times that of the ocean — supports
  astronomical numbers of brine shrimp and brine flies, which in turn feed
  millions of migratory birds. The lake has been shrinking for decades, and
  by 2022 was at its lowest recorded level.

The Great Salt Lake is the largest saltwater lake in the Western Hemisphere — on good days — though it has been losing water to agricultural and municipal diversions for decades. At its remaining size it is still vast, strange, and worth seeing: the salt-crusted shoreline, the pink-tinted algae in hypersaline bays, and the absolute flatness of the surface on a calm day create a landscape unlike anywhere else in America.

The easiest access from Salt Lake City is via Antelope Island State Park, a 45-minute drive west. The island has bison, pronghorn, and shore birds, as well as the Fielding Garr Ranch, one of the oldest continuously operating ranches in Utah.
