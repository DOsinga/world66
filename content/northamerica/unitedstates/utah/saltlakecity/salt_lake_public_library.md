The Salt Lake City Main Library is an architectural landmark as much as a library. Designed by Moshe Safdie and opened in 2003, the building's defining feature is a curved glass wall along its south face that creates a six-story atrium visible from the street. Inside, the reading rooms open onto balconies and terraces with good views of the Wasatch Mountains.

The rooftop urban garden is open to the public and offers one of the better free viewpoints in downtown Salt Lake City. The library hosts regular events, exhibitions, and community programmes and is worth visiting even if you have no interest in checking out books.
