The Salt Lake Temple is the centrepiece of Temple Square and the most iconic building in Salt Lake City. Its six granite spires, the tallest topped by a gilded statue of the angel Moroni, rise above the downtown skyline and are visible from most of the city. Construction lasted 40 years, from 1853 to 1893.

The temple itself is open only to members of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-Day Saints, but the exterior and the surrounding Temple Square grounds are open to all. The visitor centres on the square's north and south ends provide exhibits on LDS history and theology for those who want context.
