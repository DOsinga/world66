Vernal is the main town in the Uintah Basin of northeastern Utah, sitting at the edge of some of the state's most spectacular dinosaur country. It is the gateway to Dinosaur National Monument, where a quarry wall exposes over 1,500 dinosaur bones in situ — one of the great fossil sites in North America.

The town itself is a small, practical place, notable for being one of the largest cities in the United States without a railway. The surrounding landscape is dramatic: red rock canyons, the Green River, and the Flaming Gorge National Recreation Area to the north. The [Utah Field House of Natural History State Park Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/utah/vernal/utah_field_house) in town has full-size dinosaur replicas and a good fossil collection. Beside it on Main Street stands [Dinah the Pink Dinosaur](/northamerica/unitedstates/utah/vernal/dinah_the_pink_dinosaur), a 40-foot roadside statue from the 1950s that is now listed on the National Register of Historic Places.

Beyond town, the [McConkie Ranch petroglyphs](/northamerica/unitedstates/utah/vernal/mcconkie_ranch_petroglyphs) preserve some of the finest Fremont rock art in Utah, and [Fantasy Canyon](/northamerica/unitedstates/utah/vernal/fantasy_canyon) — an hour's drive south through oil-field country — offers an eerily sculptural landscape of eroded sandstone formations. The [Vernal Utah Temple](/northamerica/unitedstates/utah/vernal/vernal_utah_temple) on the west side of town is a notable piece of local architecture, converted from a 1907 tabernacle.
