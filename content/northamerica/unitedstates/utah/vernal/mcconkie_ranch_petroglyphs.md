  The petroglyphs here were made by the Fremont people, who inhabited the region from roughly 700 to 1300 AD. The McConkie Ranch petroglyphs are one of the best rock art sites in Utah, and one of the least crowded. Located about 15 miles northwest of Vernal on the dry canyon walls above Dry Fork Creek, the site contains hundreds of petroglyphs made by the Fremont people over several centuries. The figures include large, elaborate anthropomorphs with trapezoidal bodies, shields, and headdresses — the "Barrier Canyon style" at its most vivid.

The walk along the canyon wall is straightforward: a well-worn path follows the base of the sandstone cliffs for about a mile, with the petroglyphs at eye level and above. The private ranch charges a small access fee. Take water, and go early in the morning in summer to avoid the heat and the flat midday light. The petroglyphs photograph best in the early morning or late afternoon. From Vernal, take US-191 north to Dry Fork Canyon Road; the ranch is signposted.
