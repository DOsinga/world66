title: Sun Tunnels

Nancy Holt's Sun Tunnels are four massive concrete cylinders, each 5.5 metres long and 2.7 metres in diameter, arranged in an X pattern in the Great Basin desert about 100 kilometres north of Wendover near the tiny town of Lucin. Created between 1973 and 1976, the work aligns with the sunrise and sunset of the summer and winter solstices, framing the desert light at its most extreme. Holes drilled through the cylinder walls form the patterns of four constellations — Draco, Perseus, Columba, and Capricorn — so that sunlight during the day and moonlight at night project dots of light on the interior walls.

The drive to reach them is entirely on unpaved roads through empty rangeland. There are no facilities. The work has no signage explaining it — you either know what it is or you discover it. The solstice gatherings attract crowds; on an ordinary weekday you may be entirely alone. Holt described the piece as a way of making the vast landscape human-scaled, and stepping inside one of the tunnels to look down its length at a circle of desert sky goes some way toward achieving that.
