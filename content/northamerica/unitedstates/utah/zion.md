Zion National Park is the sandstone cathedral of southern Utah: a canyon system of red and cream cliffs, hanging gardens, river narrows, and improbable trails that climb straight out of the valley floor. It is also crowded, permit-heavy, and intensely managed in peak season, so the best trips balance awe with logistics.

Most first-time visitors focus on Zion Canyon, reached from [Springdale](/northamerica/unitedstates/utah/springdale). This is where you find [Angels Landing](/northamerica/unitedstates/utah/zion/angels_landing), [The Narrows](/northamerica/unitedstates/utah/zion/the_narrows), [Emerald Pools](/northamerica/unitedstates/utah/zion/emerald_pools), and the shuttle road under cliffs that make buses feel toy-sized. The [Zion-Mount Carmel Highway](/northamerica/unitedstates/utah/zion/zion_mount_carmel_highway) cuts east through slickrock, tunnels, and the strange cross-hatched face of [Checkerboard Mesa](/northamerica/unitedstates/utah/zion/checkerboard_mesa).

The quieter side is Kolob Canyons, reached from Interstate 15 well northwest of the main canyon. It has fewer services and less name recognition, but the red finger canyons and [Taylor Creek Trail](/northamerica/unitedstates/utah/zion/taylor_creek_trail) are a relief when the main valley feels overwhelmed.

Zion rewards early starts. In summer, heat and crowds punish late mornings; in spring and autumn, the light is better and the canyon breathes a little. Check shuttle rules, flash-flood forecasts, and Angels Landing permit requirements before you build the day around one famous hike.
