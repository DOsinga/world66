Brattleboro is the largest town in southern Vermont, sitting where the West River meets the Connecticut. It has a reputation for progressive politics, independent-minded residents, and a lively arts scene that punches well above its size — the town has more artists and musicians per capita than almost anywhere in New England.

[Main Street](/northamerica/unitedstates/vermont/brattleboro/downtown_main_street) is lined with independent bookshops, galleries, ethnic restaurants, and food co-ops. The [Brattleboro Museum and Art Center](/northamerica/unitedstates/vermont/brattleboro/brattleboro_museum) rotates quality contemporary exhibitions in the town's old Union Station. The farmers' market draws crowds on Saturdays throughout the growing season. The [Latchis Hotel and Theatre](/northamerica/unitedstates/vermont/brattleboro/latchis_hotel), one of the few Art Deco buildings in Vermont, is both a cinema and one of the better hotels in town.

The town's unusual industrial history lives on in the [Estey Organ Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/vermont/brattleboro/estey_organ_museum) on Birge Street, housed in the factory complex of a company that once employed 700 people making reed organs for the world. In winter, the [Harris Hill Ski Jump](/northamerica/unitedstates/vermont/brattleboro/harris_hill_ski_jump) above town hosts competitions at an Olympic-calibre venue that has been in continuous use since 1922.

The literary connection is worth noting: Rudyard Kipling lived in the hills just north of town from 1892 to 1896, writing The Jungle Book in a house he called [Naulakha](/northamerica/unitedstates/vermont/brattleboro/kipling_naulakha). The [Connecticut River](/northamerica/unitedstates/vermont/brattleboro/connecticut_river) forms the eastern edge of town, with trails along the bank and a well-preserved covered bridge a mile upstream.
