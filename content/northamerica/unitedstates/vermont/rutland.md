Rutland is Vermont's third-largest city, set in a valley in the Green Mountains at the intersection of Routes 4 and 7. It built its prosperity on marble quarrying — the surrounding area contains some of the world's highest-quality marble deposits, used in the Lincoln Memorial and other landmark buildings — and later became a key railroad junction.

Today Rutland is a working city without much tourist polish, but its location makes it a practical base for exploring central Vermont. Killington, one of New England's biggest ski resorts, is just 20 km to the east, making Rutland a cheaper alternative to staying in Killington village. The [Paramount Theatre](/northamerica/unitedstates/vermont/rutland/paramount_theater), a restored 1914 movie palace on Merchants Row, is the city's cultural anchor and hosts concerts and events through the year. The [Chaffee Art Center](/northamerica/unitedstates/vermont/rutland/chaffee_art_center), in a Victorian mansion near the centre, shows local and regional artists.

A short drive out of town, [Wilson Castle](/northamerica/unitedstates/vermont/rutland/wilson_castle) is an improbable Victorian Gothic mansion with 84 rooms and a chaotic history worth hearing about on a guided tour. The [Vermont State Fairgrounds](/northamerica/unitedstates/vermont/rutland/vermont_state_fair) hosts the state fair in late August, one of the oldest in New England.

The Marble Valley Regional Transit District runs bus service within the city. Route 4 east leads quickly into dramatic mountain scenery toward [Woodstock](/northamerica/unitedstates/vermont/woodstock) and Quechee Gorge.
