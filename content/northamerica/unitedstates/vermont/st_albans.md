St. Albans is a small northern Vermont city with a proper downtown green, red-brick blocks, old railway weight, and Lake Champlain a few miles to the west. It is not as polished as Burlington or Stowe, but it has enough story for a relaxed day if you are already in this corner of the state.

The town calls itself the Maple Sugar Capital of the World, and the annual Vermont Maple Festival gives that claim some life. Its other strong identity is historical: the 1864 St. Albans Raid was the northernmost Confederate action of the American Civil War, and the [Saint Albans Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/vermont/st_albans/saint_albans_museum) is the place to make sense of it.

Use St. Albans as both town and lake stop. Walk [Taylor Park and the historic district](/northamerica/unitedstates/vermont/st_albans/taylor_park_and_historic_district), then head to [St. Albans Bay Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/vermont/st_albans/st_albans_bay_park) or the Missisquoi wetlands if you want open water, birds, and a quieter Lake Champlain than you get farther south.
