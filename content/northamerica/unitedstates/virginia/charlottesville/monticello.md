story: "Thomas Jefferson spent 40 years designing and redesigning Monticello, and the result is one of the most idiosyncratic houses in America — part Palladian villa, part working farm experiment, with a vegetable garden that Jefferson maintained as a laboratory for new plant varieties. The house looks smaller than expected from the outside because Jefferson deliberately disguised the upper floor behind a low balustrade. What has changed most since the 20th century is the interpretation: the Monticello foundation has substantially expanded the presentation of the 400-600 enslaved people who lived and worked here, making the estate a genuinely complex historical site rather than a simple founder's shrine."

Thomas Jefferson's hilltop plantation and home, 5 kilometres southeast of downtown Charlottesville. The house, gardens, and grounds are a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Timed entry tickets are required and should be booked in advance for spring and autumn weekends. Allow at least 2 hours. The visitor centre has good exhibits on both Jefferson and the plantation's enslaved workforce; the house tour itself is ticketed separately.
