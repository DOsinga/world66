The Colonial Parkway is the physical spine of Virginia's Historic Triangle — a 23-mile road built by the National Park Service in the 1930s and 1940s to link [Williamsburg](/northamerica/unitedstates/virginia/williamsburg), [Jamestown](/northamerica/unitedstates/virginia/jamestown), and [Yorktown](/northamerica/unitedstates/virginia/yorktown) in a single unbroken journey. Federal law prohibits any commercial development along its length: no billboards, no petrol stations, no modern signage. What remains is a road that feels designed to slow you down — aggregate surfacing in red and grey, gentle curves through mature oak and pine forest, and long stretches where the only view is the tidal water of the James or the York.

Driving the full route without stops takes about 45 minutes. The sensible way to use it is as connective tissue between the three sites: start at one end, visit each settlement in roughly chronological order — 1607 at Jamestown, the colonial capital at Williamsburg, the 1781 surrender at Yorktown — and arrive at the other end having covered 175 years of early American history in an afternoon. Several river overlooks along the way make natural stopping points; bald eagles and ospreys are a regular sight over the water.

The parkway is also open to cyclists, and sections near each town are popular for morning rides. It is worth noting that the road was designed before modern speed expectations: it is narrow, unhurried, and unsuitable for any kind of rush. That is entirely the point. If you have only one afternoon in the Historic Triangle and need to see all three places, the Colonial Parkway is the route that holds the experience together.
