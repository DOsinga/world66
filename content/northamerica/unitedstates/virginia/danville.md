Danville sits on the Dan River just above the North Carolina line, a former tobacco and textile city with more history than its modest size suggests. It was briefly the last capital of the Confederacy, later a major mill town, and now a place trying to turn old warehouses and riverfront land into something visitors can use.

The city is strongest when you read it through its buildings. The [Danville Museum of Fine Arts and History](/northamerica/unitedstates/virginia/danville/danville_museum_of_fine_arts_and_history) occupies the Sutherlin Mansion, where Jefferson Davis stayed during the Confederacy's final days. Nearby, [Millionaires' Row](/northamerica/unitedstates/virginia/danville/millionaires_row) shows what tobacco money bought, while the [Danville Riverwalk Trail](/northamerica/unitedstates/virginia/danville/danville_riverwalk_trail) gives the city a calmer modern spine along the water.

Danville is not polished like Williamsburg or scenic like the Blue Ridge towns to the west. Its appeal is rougher: industrial brick, Civil Rights history, old money houses, and a river that has become useful again. Add the eccentric [AAF Tank Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/virginia/danville/aaf_tank_museum), and it clears the bar for travelers already moving through Southside Virginia.
