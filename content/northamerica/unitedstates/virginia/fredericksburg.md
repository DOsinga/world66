Fredericksburg sits on the south bank of the Rappahannock River, about halfway between Washington DC and Richmond, and its position explains almost everything about its history. Founded in 1728, it was a prosperous colonial port and market town — George Washington grew up across the river at [Ferry Farm](/northamerica/unitedstates/virginia/fredericksburg/ferry_farm), and both his mother and his brother James Monroe lived here. Then the Civil War came through four times. The town changed hands repeatedly between 1862 and 1864, and the fighting that happened in and around it — at [Fredericksburg](/northamerica/unitedstates/virginia/fredericksburg/fredericksburg_spotsylvania_park), Chancellorsville, the Wilderness, and Spotsylvania Court House — killed or wounded more than 100,000 men.

The result is a place with an unusually dense concentration of history in a small space. The colonial-era downtown — centered on Caroline Street and the [Market Square area](/northamerica/unitedstates/virginia/fredericksburg/fredericksburg_town_hall) — has survived largely intact, with Federal and Georgian brick buildings that now house restaurants, galleries, and independent shops. [Kenmore](/northamerica/unitedstates/virginia/fredericksburg/kenmore), the plantation house Washington's sister Betty Lewis lived in, sits within walking distance of the commercial district. So does the [Rising Sun Tavern](/northamerica/unitedstates/virginia/fredericksburg/rising_sun_tavern), which has been giving guided tours in 18th-century costume since the 1970s.

The military history is inescapable but well-presented. The [Fredericksburg and Spotsylvania National Military Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/virginia/fredericksburg/fredericksburg_spotsylvania_park) manages four separate battlefields and the [Chatham Manor](/northamerica/unitedstates/virginia/fredericksburg/chatham_manor) — a Georgian mansion across the river that both Washington and Lincoln used — giving a coherent picture of four years of brutal campaigning in this corridor. The downtown [Fredericksburg Area Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/virginia/fredericksburg/fredericksburg_area_museum) covers the full span from Native American settlement through Reconstruction, with a frank treatment of the slave trade that operated openly in the town center.

Fredericksburg is a genuine small city of about 30,000 rather than a museum piece. The University of Mary Washington gives it a student population and a lively bar and restaurant scene for its size. It is easily reached by Amtrak or MARC commuter rail from Washington — about 90 minutes — and makes a rewarding day trip or overnight stop on a drive between the two capital cities.
