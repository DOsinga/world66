The Slave Auction Block is a granite curb stone at the corner of William and Charles Streets that served as one of the principal auction sites for enslaved people in Fredericksburg from at least the 1820s through the Civil War. It is a simple object — unadorned stone, flush with the sidewalk — and the historical marker beside it is the only indication of what it signifies.

Fredericksburg was an important node in the domestic slave trade, well-positioned between the Chesapeake slave-breeding regions and the Mississippi Valley plantations that consumed an ever-growing supply of forced labor. Thousands of men, women, and children were auctioned here. The block was designated a National Historic Landmark in 2023 after years of debate about its significance and its preservation. It is worth a visit both for what it represents and because the historical marker provides unusually direct language about the realities of the trade.
