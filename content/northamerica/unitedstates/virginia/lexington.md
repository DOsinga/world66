Lexington sits in the Shenandoah Valley, a small and walkable town that contains more American history per square mile than almost anywhere outside Washington DC. Two campuses dominate the centre — [Washington and Lee University](/northamerica/unitedstates/virginia/lexington/washington_and_lee_university), one of the oldest in America, and the [Virginia Military Institute](/northamerica/unitedstates/virginia/lexington/virginia_military_institute), the country's oldest state military college — and between them they give the town its particular atmosphere: serious, well-kept, and acutely conscious of its past.

The Civil War is everywhere. Robert E. Lee is buried in the [University Chapel](/northamerica/unitedstates/virginia/lexington/university_chapel) on the Washington and Lee campus, along with his horse Traveller. The [Stonewall Jackson House](/northamerica/unitedstates/virginia/lexington/stonewall_jackson_house), where Jackson lived before the war, is open to visitors. And the [George C. Marshall Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/virginia/lexington/george_c_marshall_museum) on the VMI campus covers the VMI alumnus who became Army Chief of Staff, Secretary of State, and Nobel Peace Prize winner.

The surrounding landscape is reward enough in itself. The [Chessie Nature Trail](/northamerica/unitedstates/virginia/lexington/chessie_nature_trail) follows the Maury River east from town along a converted railway line, with Blue Ridge Mountain views and excellent birdwatching. Natural Bridge and the rest of the Shenandoah Valley are within easy reach for day trips.
