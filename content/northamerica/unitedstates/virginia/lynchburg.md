Lynchburg is a mid-sized city of about 80,000 people set on seven hills above the James River in central Virginia. Founded in 1757, it was one of the wealthiest cities in the antebellum South thanks to the tobacco trade, and its hilltop neighborhoods are filled with beautifully preserved 19th-century architecture. The city survived the Civil War largely intact, which means the historic fabric is remarkably complete.

The [James River Bluff Walk](/northamerica/unitedstates/virginia/lynchburg/james_river_bluff_walk) along the hilltop above the James River offers sweeping views of the Blue Ridge Mountains and is a good starting point for exploring the city. [Court House Hill](/northamerica/unitedstates/virginia/lynchburg/court_house_hill) is the most architecturally distinguished neighbourhood, with Federal, Greek Revival, and Victorian houses lining steep streets. [Monument Terrace](/northamerica/unitedstates/virginia/lynchburg/monument_terrace), the ceremonial staircase connecting downtown to the hilltop courthouse, is the symbolic centre of the city. The [Legacy Museum of African American History](/northamerica/unitedstates/virginia/lynchburg/legacy_museum) and the [Old City Cemetery](/northamerica/unitedstates/virginia/lynchburg/old_city_cemetery) both offer thoughtful looks at the city's complex past, including its role in the tobacco economy and the Civil War. [Point of Honor](/northamerica/unitedstates/virginia/lynchburg/point_of_honor), a Federal mansion on the river bluff, is one of the finest house museums in the region.

Lynchburg sits at the foot of the Blue Ridge, making it a natural base for outdoor excursions. The Blue Ridge Parkway is a short drive west, and the James River offers kayaking, tubing, and fishing through the heart of the city. The Appalachian Trail crosses nearby, and Peaks of Otter on the Parkway is one of the most popular day hikes in Virginia. Downtown has been quietly revitalizing, with good restaurants and breweries along Main Street and the riverfront. The city is also home to several colleges, including Randolph College and the University of Lynchburg, which give it more cultural life than you might expect.
