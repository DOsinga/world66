Appomattox Court House National Historical Park preserves the small Virginia village where General Robert E. Lee surrendered the Army of Northern Virginia to Ulysses S. Grant in April 1865. For travellers based in Lynchburg, it is one of the most consequential historic sites within easy reach, and the quietness of the place is part of its force.

The reconstructed McLean House is the focal point, but the park works best when you walk the lanes and see the courthouse, tavern, law offices, and surrounding fields as a village rather than a single room in a history book. Allow enough time for the visitor centre and at least a short walk; the story lands more clearly when you see how ordinary the setting is.
