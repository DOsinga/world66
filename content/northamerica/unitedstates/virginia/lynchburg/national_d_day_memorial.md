The National D-Day Memorial stands in Bedford, a small Virginia town that suffered extraordinary losses on 6 June 1944. Its location gives the memorial a particular force: this is not an abstract military monument, but a national site anchored in a community that paid heavily for the Normandy landings.

The memorial uses plazas, sculpture, water, and a large arch to move visitors through the story of planning, landing, and sacrifice. It is a worthwhile excursion from Lynchburg if you have a car and an interest in Second World War history. Allow time to read, not just photograph; the details are what keep the place from becoming merely grand.
