Thomas Jefferson's Poplar Forest was Jefferson's private retreat, an octagonal house and plantation landscape outside Lynchburg. The architecture is the central draw: a carefully planned, geometric building that shows Jefferson experimenting away from the public stage of Monticello.

The site also interprets the enslaved community and plantation economy that made the retreat possible. It is a serious historic visit rather than a quick photo stop, and it rewards travelers interested in early American architecture, archaeology and public history.
