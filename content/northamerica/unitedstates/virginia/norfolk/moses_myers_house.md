The Moses Myers House is one of downtown Norfolk's most rewarding small historic sites. Built at the end of the 18th century, it preserves the home of a Jewish merchant family whose fortunes were tied to Norfolk's Atlantic trade, with period rooms that feel domestic rather than staged.

Its interest is in detail: furniture, portraits, household objects, and the way a prosperous family lived in a port city still shaping its identity after the American Revolution. It is close enough to the Chrysler Museum and the NEON District to fold into a short downtown walk.
