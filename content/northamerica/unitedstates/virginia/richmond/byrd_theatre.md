  The Byrd was built in 1928 by Rowland Bossieux, a local theater impresario, and designed to resemble a grand European opera house at a fraction of the cost. Its signature feature is the Wurlitzer organ, which was installed in 1928 and is still played at weekend midnight shows. The organist rises from the pit on a hydraulic lift as the audience applauds — a ritual that has been unchanged since the 1920s.

Carytown's beloved art deco movie palace, built in 1928 and one of the finest surviving movie theatres in the American South. The interior is extraordinary: a painted ceiling mimicking a Mediterranean sky, baroque plasterwork, gilded columns, and red velvet everywhere. The Byrd shows a mix of new films at reduced prices and classic film series, with midnight shows on weekends accompanied by the theatre's original 1928 Wurlitzer organ.

The Byrd is independently owned and operated, and its continued existence in a neighbourhood as lively as Carytown is a tribute to Richmond's commitment to its architectural heritage. See anything here once; the building is better than most of what plays in it.
