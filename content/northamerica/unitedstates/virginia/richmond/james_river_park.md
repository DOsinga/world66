  Richmond is one of very few cities in America with Class III and IV whitewater rapids running through its urban core. The Falls of the James — the series of rapids that stopped navigation from the coast — were the reason Richmond was founded here in 1737: this was the farthest inland you could bring an oceangoing vessel, and the falls powered the mills that made the city prosperous.

One of the most unusual urban parks in America: miles of trails, wooded islands, swimming holes, and serious whitewater rapids running through the middle of Richmond. The James River drops about 40 feet over the granite falls as it passes through the city, creating rapids that attract kayakers and surfers from across the region. The North Bank Trail runs along the river for seven miles and connects the major access points.

The best spots for swimming are the quarry pond at Pony Pasture and the pools below the rapids on Belle Isle. The river is swimmable from spring through fall; in summer, Richmond residents treat it as a public beach. The whitewater is serious — don't wade into the main channel without experience — but the calmer sections above and below are family-friendly.
