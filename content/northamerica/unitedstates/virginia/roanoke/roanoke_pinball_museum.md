The Roanoke Pinball Museum is on the lower level of Center in the Square, with around fifty playable pinball machines spanning the 1930s woodrail era through to modern Stern releases. Admission gets you unlimited play on every machine in the museum.

The collection runs the full history of the format: early percussive games where steel balls hit metal pegs, mid-century flipper games with bells and pop-up targets, brightly lit 1970s solid-state machines, and 1990s and 2000s machines built around licensed franchises. Several machines have explanatory plaques covering the manufacturer, the year, and what made the model significant in pinball history.
