Rapidan Camp was Herbert Hoover's presidential retreat in the Blue Ridge, built in 1929 as a summer escape from Washington. The restored Brown House shows the camp as it looked during Hoover's presidency, when the mountain setting offered both privacy and a place for quiet political meetings.

It is one of Shenandoah National Park's more interesting historic stops because it shows the park before it was fully a park. In high season, ranger-guided tours give the visit much more context than simply walking past the buildings.
