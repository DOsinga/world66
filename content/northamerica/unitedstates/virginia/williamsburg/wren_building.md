The Wren Building stands at the western end of the Duke of Gloucester Street and forms the anchor of the College of William & Mary campus. Built between 1695 and 1700, it is considered the oldest academic building in continuous use in the United States. The building is named after Sir Christopher Wren, though evidence that he actually designed it is disputed — the attribution came later.

The brick exterior in the English Renaissance style is one of the most recognisable academic buildings in America, and the Wren Yard in front of it is the social heart of the William & Mary campus. The building houses a restored chapel, the Great Hall, and faculty offices; it is open to visitors and makes a natural connection point between the Colonial Williamsburg historic district and the college campus immediately to its west.
