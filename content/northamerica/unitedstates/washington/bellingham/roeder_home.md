The Roeder Home is a handsome late-19th-century house connected with Henry Roeder, one of the figures behind early Bellingham's lumber and settlement history. Its turrets, porches, and preserved interior details make it a useful stop for visitors interested in the city's older residential architecture rather than just its waterfront and college-town energy.

The house is operated by Whatcom County and is used for public and private events, so the experience depends heavily on opening times. When accessible, it gives a compact look at the social world of Bellingham's early elite and pairs well with the Pickett House and the downtown historic district.
