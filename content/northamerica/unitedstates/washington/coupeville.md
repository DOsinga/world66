Coupeville is a small seaport on Penn Cove, halfway up Whidbey Island, and one of Washington's most rewarding small-town stops. It sits inside Ebey's Landing National Historical Reserve, where old farms, prairie bluffs, military forts, and a Victorian waterfront have been protected as one lived-in landscape rather than a sealed museum.

The town is compact: Front Street, the wharf, a few blocks of old houses, and the cove where mussel rafts sit offshore. The wider reserve is the reason to stay longer. Trails climb above the water at [Ebey's Landing](/northamerica/unitedstates/washington/coupeville/ebeys_landing), while [Fort Casey State Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/washington/coupeville/fort_casey_state_park) guards Admiralty Inlet a short drive south.

Coupeville is a very good spare-day trip from Seattle, Port Townsend, or anywhere on Whidbey. Come by ferry if you can; the crossing from Port Townsend makes the town feel properly maritime before you even arrive.
