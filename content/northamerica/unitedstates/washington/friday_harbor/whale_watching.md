The waters around the San Juan Islands are among the most reliable places in the world to see orcas in the wild. The Southern Resident killer whales — three pods totaling fewer than 75 individuals — use the channels between the islands from late spring through autumn, following the Chinook salmon runs. On a good summer day, sightings are almost guaranteed; even on quieter days, the channels offer Steller sea lions, harbour seals, Dall's porpoise, and bald eagles as compensation.

Several tour operators run whale-watching trips from the Friday Harbor dock, ranging from two to three hours. Smaller boats get closer to the whales (within legal approach distances); larger vessels are more stable in chop. Most operators have naturalists on board who explain what they are seeing and provide context on the animals' social structure and conservation status. The Whale Museum can advise on operators that follow responsible wildlife-watching guidelines.

The best months are June through September, when the salmon runs are strongest and the whales are most reliably present. The same channels can also produce humpback whales, which have been returning to the area in increasing numbers in recent years. Even without whales, the boat ride through the archipelago — islands forested down to the waterline, mountains visible on clear days — is worthwhile on its own.
