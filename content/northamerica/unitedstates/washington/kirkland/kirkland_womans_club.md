The Kirkland Woman's Club building is one of the older surviving civic structures in downtown Kirkland, listed on the National Register of Historic Places. It stands on a corner near the other historic commercial buildings of the Peter Kirk era, part of a small cluster of early 20th-century architecture that gives Kirkland's downtown its modest historic character.

The building still functions as an event and meeting space. From the outside, its simple wooden structure and proportions read clearly as small-town civic architecture of the 1910s — a time capsule embedded in a city block otherwise much altered by commercial development.

For visitors interested in the Peter Kirk era, the Woman's Club is part of a self-guided heritage walk that takes in the Peter Kirk Building, the Masonic Lodge Building, and the Dr. Trueblood House — a compact circuit that can be done in under an hour from Marina Park.
