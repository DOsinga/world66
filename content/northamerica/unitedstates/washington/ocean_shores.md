Ocean Shores is a small resort community on a sandy peninsula between Grays Harbor and the Pacific Ocean, on Washington's central coast. The beaches here are wide and dramatic — miles of flat sand backed by dunes, with the kind of moody Pacific weather that makes the Washington coast distinctive.

The town is a popular weekend escape from Seattle and the Puget Sound cities. The main draws are the [beach itself](/northamerica/unitedstates/washington/ocean_shores/ocean_shores_beach) — good for [kite flying](/northamerica/unitedstates/washington/ocean_shores/kite_flying), [razor clamming](/northamerica/unitedstates/washington/ocean_shores/razor_clamming) in season, and long walks — and easy access to the wilder coastline and rainforests of the Olympic Peninsula to the north. [Damon Point State Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/washington/ocean_shores/damon_point_state_park) at the southern tip of the peninsula is one of the better wildlife-watching spots on the Washington coast, and the [Ocean Shores Interpretive Center](/northamerica/unitedstates/washington/ocean_shores/ocean_shores_interpretive_center) near the northern end has good exhibits on the coastal ecology and migration routes.

Ocean Shores is not glamorous — it is a low-key beach town with motels, a casino, and a handful of restaurants — but the setting is genuine and the beach is spectacular. Just across Grays Harbor in Westport, the [Grays Harbor Lighthouse](/northamerica/unitedstates/washington/ocean_shores/grays_harbor_lighthouse) is the tallest in Washington State and worth combining into a day out.
