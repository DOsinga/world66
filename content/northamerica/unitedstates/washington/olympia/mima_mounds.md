story: >
  The Mima Mounds — thousands of low domed hummocks scattered across an
  open prairie south of Olympia — have defied scientific explanation for
  more than 150 years. Theories include glacial retreat patterns, seismic
  resonance during the 1700 Cascadia earthquake, and an enormous,
  centuries-long colony of pocket gophers. The current consensus is gophers,
  but no one is entirely sure.
---

A 600-acre preserve of glacial outwash prairie 25 km southwest of Olympia, covered by thousands of low domed mounds — typically 2 metres high and 8 metres across — whose origin has never been definitively explained. Theories range from prehistoric earthquake liquefaction to long-term gopher activity.

A short network of paved trails leads from the small visitor centre out across the prairie; a wooden tower lets you see the mound pattern from above. The prairie is at its most spectacular in May, when the wildflower display includes camas, balsamroot and the rare golden paintbrush. Free entry, day-use only.
