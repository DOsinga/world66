The Olympic Peninsula is the green, rain-battered northwest corner of Washington, separated from Seattle by Puget Sound and from Canada by the Strait of Juan de Fuca. It is a region of ferry rides, logging towns, tribal lands, lavender fields, glacial lakes, and some of the wildest public coastline in the lower 48 states.

[Olympic National Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/washington/olympicnationalpark) is the headline, but the peninsula is larger than the park. [Port Townsend](/northamerica/unitedstates/washington/olympicpeninsula/port_townsend) has Victorian seaport character; [Port Angeles](/northamerica/unitedstates/washington/olympicpeninsula/port_angeles) is the practical northern base; [Forks](/northamerica/unitedstates/washington/olympicpeninsula/forks) opens the way to rainforest and coast; and [Cape Flattery](/northamerica/unitedstates/washington/olympicpeninsula/cape_flattery) feels like the continent running out of land.

The peninsula is best treated as a loop rather than a checklist. Take your time on US-101, stop for tide pools, forests, and small-town cafes, and accept that rain is part of the bargain. Even in summer, the west side can feel soaked and elemental while the northeast sits in a partial rain shadow around Sequim.
