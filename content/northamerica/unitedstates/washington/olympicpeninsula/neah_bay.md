Neah Bay is the Makah community near the Olympic Peninsula's far northwest tip, where the Strait of Juan de Fuca meets the Pacific. It feels remote in the best way: the road ends, the weather moves fast, and the surrounding coast is all forest, sea stacks, tide pools, and working tribal land.

The town's identity is Makah first, scenery second. Start with the [Makah Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/washington/olympicpeninsula/neah_bay/makah_museum), whose Ozette artefacts make the long human history of this coast tangible. Then drive out to [Cape Flattery](/northamerica/unitedstates/washington/olympicpeninsula/cape_flattery), the short trail to the northwestern tip, or make the longer hike to [Shi Shi Beach](/northamerica/unitedstates/washington/olympicpeninsula/shi_shi_beach).

Neah Bay is not a casual add-on to Seattle or even Port Angeles. It is a deliberate trip, and visitors should follow Makah rules on permits, access, speed limits, and where photography is welcome. Do that, and a spare day here feels like the edge of the map.
