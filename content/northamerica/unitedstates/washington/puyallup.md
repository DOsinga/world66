**Puyallup** is a city in the shadow of Mount Rainier in Washington state, about 30 minutes south of Seattle and Tacoma. The name comes from the Puyallup people and is pronounced "pew-AL-up."

The city is best known for the [Washington State Fair](/northamerica/unitedstates/washington/puyallup/washington_state_fair) (still called "the Puyallup Fair" by locals), held every September at the Western Washington Fairgrounds — it is one of the largest fairs in the country. The surrounding valley is historically agricultural, known for daffodil farms and berry fields, and the Daffodil Festival parade in April is a long-standing tradition. Mount Rainier dominates the view to the southeast on clear days.

The historic downtown centres on [Pioneer Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/washington/puyallup/pioneer_park) and a walkable stretch of independent shops and cafes. The [Meeker Mansion](/northamerica/unitedstates/washington/puyallup/meeker_mansion) on Spring Street is the most significant historic building — built in 1890 by the Oregon Trail pioneer Ezra Meeker and now a house museum. The [Foothills Trail](/northamerica/unitedstates/washington/puyallup/foothills_trail) is the best outdoor option, running 15 miles through daffodil fields toward Orting with Rainier views on clear days.
