  Ezra Meeker made his fortune growing hops in the Puyallup Valley, then lost it in a blight. In his seventies, broke and convinced that the Oregon Trail was being forgotten, he set out to retrace the route by ox-drawn wagon — in 1906. The Italianate Victorian home built by Oregon Trail pioneer Ezra Meeker in 1890, when the Puyallup Valley hop industry was making fortunes. The 17-room mansion is a handsome example of late Victorian domestic architecture — elaborate woodwork, wraparound porches, period furnishings — and now operates as a house museum managed by the Ezra Meeker Historical Society.

Tours are led by volunteers and cover both the architecture and Meeker's remarkable life: the Oregon Trail crossing in 1852, the hop-farming enterprise, the late-in-life campaign to mark the trail with monuments that he personally funded and placed. Open Wednesday through Sunday; check hours before visiting as they vary seasonally.
