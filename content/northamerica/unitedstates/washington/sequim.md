Sequim is the dry surprise of the [Olympic Peninsula](/northamerica/unitedstates/washington/olympicpeninsula). The Olympic Mountains wring out the weather before it reaches town, leaving a valley with far more sun than the rest of western Washington. That rain shadow is not just trivia; it shapes the farms, the light, and the way locals sell the place.

The famous result is lavender. In early and mid-summer, farms around the Dungeness Valley open their fields for cutting, wandering, and very purple shopping. Outside bloom season, Sequim still has a strong anchor in [Dungeness National Wildlife Refuge](/northamerica/unitedstates/washington/sequim/dungeness_national_wildlife_refuge), where a long sand spit points into the Strait of Juan de Fuca toward a lighthouse.

Sequim works best as a slow day on the north Olympic coast. Walk the spit if you have the legs, stop at a farm if the lavender is blooming, and leave time for the [Dungeness River Nature Center](/northamerica/unitedstates/washington/sequim/dungeness_river_nature_center) or a detour toward Port Angeles. It is not as dramatic as Hurricane Ridge, but it has a clear identity of its own.
