Westport is a small fishing and beach town on the southern Washington coast, sitting at the mouth of Grays Harbor where the bay meets the Pacific Ocean. It is the kind of place that gets described as the end of the road — US 105 terminates here — and it has the straightforward, unpretentious character that comes with that geography.

The town built its identity on fishing, and the fishing fleet is still the heart of it. Charter companies run trips for salmon, halibut, and albacore, and the docks have the smell and noise of a working port. The Westport Light, built in 1898, is the tallest lighthouse on the West Coast and now sits in a state park a short walk from the beach. At the end of Neddie Rose Drive stands the Fisherman's Memorial, a reminder that the sea here is not always generous.

Surfers know Westport as the best break in Washington State. The cold water requires a full wetsuit year-round, but the waves are consistent and the crowds are a fraction of what you'd find further south. Gray whales pass through Grays Harbor during their spring migration from Baja California to the Bering Sea, and the charter fleet offers whale watching trips at reasonable prices.
