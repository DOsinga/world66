The US National Arboretum occupies 446 acres of wooded hills in the northeastern part of Washington DC, about four miles from the Mall. It is operated by the Department of Agriculture as a research and educational facility, and it is free to visit every day of the year.

The azalea collection is spectacular in April and May, when 15,000 azalea plants covering 15 acres bloom across the hillside. The Japanese garden, National Herb Garden, and bonsai museum add variety. The National Capitol Columns in the meadow below the administration building are the most unexpected sight: twenty-two ancient sandstone Corinthian columns in a field, looking like a ruined Greek temple that has been transplanted to the suburbs.
