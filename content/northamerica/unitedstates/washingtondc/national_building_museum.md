The National Building Museum occupies the old Pension Bureau building, a massive red brick Italianate structure completed in 1887 to process Civil War pension claims. The exterior frieze wraps around all four sides of the building showing a continuous procession of soldiers, sailors, and marines — over 1,200 figures in total.

The interior's great hall, with its eight enormous Corinthian columns (the largest interior columns in the world when built), has been used for presidential inaugural balls since Grover Cleveland's second term. The museum's permanent collection covers architecture, engineering, and design; temporary exhibitions focus on individual buildings, cities, and design movements. The building itself is the best exhibit. Admission is charged for permanent galleries; the great hall is free to enter.
