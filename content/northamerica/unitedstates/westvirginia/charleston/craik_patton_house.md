Craik-Patton House is a preserved Greek Revival house that helps tell Charleston's nineteenth-century story. Built before West Virginia became a state, it interprets domestic life, migration, and social status in the Kanawha Valley through period rooms and local history exhibits.

The house is a useful stop because Charleston's historic fabric can be easy to miss between government buildings and modern roads. Visiting gives a more intimate view of the city: not only the capitol and riverfront, but the families, architecture, and settlement patterns that shaped the valley.
