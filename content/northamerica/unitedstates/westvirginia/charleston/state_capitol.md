  Cass Gilbert — the architect of the US Supreme Court and the Woolworth Building — designed the West Virginia capitol after the previous building burned down in 1921. The dome is five feet taller than the US Capitol's and was originally clad in copper that turned green; the current gold-leaf finish was applied during a restoration in 2005.
---

Cass Gilbert's 1932 capitol is the most impressive building in the state — a 293-foot gold dome that outshines the national model it's modelled on. Inside, the rotunda features a two-ton bronze chandelier (designed never to fall, even if the cables broke) and Italian marble brought up the Kanawha by barge. Free guided tours run on the hour from Monday to Saturday and cover the rotunda, Senate, House, and Governor's reception room.

The Capitol Complex grounds are open at all hours and worth a slow walk: several Civil War monuments, the famous Bernie Wiepper sculpture of "Lincoln Walks at Midnight", and the state history museum next door. The black squirrels that live on the grounds, descended from a 1930s introduction, are a local curiosity.
