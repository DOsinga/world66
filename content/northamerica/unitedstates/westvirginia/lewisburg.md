Lewisburg is one of West Virginia's best small-town stops: old enough to have frontier and Civil War layers, lively enough to support theatres and galleries, and close enough to caves and mountain trails to make a day feel full. It sits in the Greenbrier Valley, where the landscape is gentler than the New River Gorge but still unmistakably Appalachian.

The centre is the place to start. Walk the historic district, step into [Carnegie Hall](/northamerica/unitedstates/westvirginia/lewisburg/carnegie_hall), and give the [North House Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/westvirginia/lewisburg/north_house_museum) time if you want the Greenbrier Valley story. [Old Stone Church](/northamerica/unitedstates/westvirginia/lewisburg/old_stone_church) adds an older, quieter note a few blocks away.

Lewisburg also has a strong day-out anchor just beyond town: [Lost World Caverns](/northamerica/unitedstates/westvirginia/lewisburg/lost_world_caverns), a show cave with a big chamber, strange formations, and enough roadside folklore to keep it from feeling too polished. If you are already in southern West Virginia, Lewisburg is easily worth more than a coffee stop.
