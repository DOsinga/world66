Morgantown is West Virginia University's town, and the university shapes everything about it — the energy, the nightlife, the rhythm of the year. Visit during the academic year and you'll find a lively, quasi-bohemian atmosphere that belies the surrounding Appalachian hills. Visit in summer and the place goes quiet. Plan accordingly.

The university runs a unique automated transit system called the [PRT](/northamerica/unitedstates/westvirginia/morgantown/prt) (Personal Rapid Transit), a fleet of driverless electric pods that connect the [main downtown campus](/northamerica/unitedstates/westvirginia/morgantown/wvu_campus) to the upstream Evansdale campus. It's a genuine oddity — personal urban transport from the 1970s that still works — and worth a ride for anyone curious about experimental infrastructure.

Downtown Morgantown sits along the [Monongahela River](/northamerica/unitedstates/westvirginia/morgantown/monongahela_waterfront), with [High Street](/northamerica/unitedstates/westvirginia/morgantown/high_street) at its core. The [Old Stone House](/northamerica/unitedstates/westvirginia/morgantown/old_stone_house) near the river is one of the oldest structures in West Virginia, a solid Georgian building dating to 1795 that survives as a reminder of the town's pre-university past.

Outside town, [Coopers Rock State Forest](/northamerica/unitedstates/westvirginia/morgantown/coopers_rock) lies a short drive east on I-68. The overlook above Cheat Canyon is genuinely dramatic — a long view across forested ridges and the river winding below. Cheat Lake, formed by the Cheat River dam, has become a popular spot for boating and recreation.

On fall Saturdays, [WVU football](/northamerica/unitedstates/westvirginia/morgantown/wvu_football) takes over the city. Milan Puskar Stadium on the Evansdale campus seats over 60,000 — nearly twice the city's population — and game days are a full civic event from dawn.

Morgantown sits at the junction of Interstates 79 and 68, about 75 miles south of Pittsburgh.
