---
title: When to Go
type: section
---

**Fall** (September–November) is the peak season, driven entirely by WVU football. Home game Saturdays transform the city — if you want to experience Morgantown at its most alive, come on a game weekend. The Appalachian foliage in October is also excellent, and Coopers Rock is at its most spectacular.

**Spring** (March–May) is the second-best time. The campus is still full of students, the weather is mild, and the hillsides green up quickly after winter. Ramps — wild onions foraged from the Appalachian hollows — appear on menus in April, a brief local season worth catching.

**Summer** is quiet. With students gone, Morgantown loses most of its energy. Coopers Rock and Cheat Lake get local use, but the city itself feels subdued. Accommodation is easy to find and cheap.

**Winter** is cold and often grey. The hills get snow, and I-68 over the mountains can be treacherous in bad conditions. The PRT keeps running. If you're here for skiing, Snowshoe Mountain Resort is about two hours south.
