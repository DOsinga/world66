Wheeling sits in the narrow Northern Panhandle of West Virginia, squeezed between Ohio and Pennsylvania along the Ohio River. In the early 1800s it was the western terminus of the National Road and the site of the first bridge across the Ohio — which made it one of the most important gateways to the American West before the railroads arrived.

That history is written into the city's fabric. The compact downtown and [Wheeling Island](/northamerica/unitedstates/westvirginia/wheeling/downtown_and_wheeling_island) preserve some remarkably intact Victorian streetscapes — grand brick storefronts and ornate row houses built when Wheeling was a booming industrial city making nails, glass, and cigars. The [Centre Market](/northamerica/unitedstates/westvirginia/wheeling/centre_market), in continuous use since 1853, anchors the downtown. The [West Virginia Independence Hall](/northamerica/unitedstates/westvirginia/wheeling/west_virginia_independence_hall) tells the story of how the state split from Virginia during the Civil War.

Just outside the city, [Oglebay Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/westvirginia/wheeling/oglebay_park) is the standout attraction — a former private estate turned public park with formal gardens, a mansion museum, and good hiking. The restored [Capitol Theatre](/northamerica/unitedstates/westvirginia/wheeling/capitol_theatre) — West Virginia's largest, seating 2,500 — hosts concerts and touring shows through the year. Wheeling is an honest, unsentimental place, and that's part of its appeal.
