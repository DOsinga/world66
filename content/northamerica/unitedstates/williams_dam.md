Williams Dam is a quiet village on the East Fork of the White River in southern Indiana, population around 400. Once a more substantial community — with a gas station, stores, a twelve-grade school, a hotel and restaurant, a dance hall, and a water-powered generating plant that served the surrounding area — it is now a shadow of its WWII-era peak.

Today the village survives on tourism. The fishing and scenery are the main draws, and Williams is home to one of Indiana's few remaining intact covered bridges, the [Williams Bridge](/northamerica/unitedstates/williams_dam/williams_bridge) of 1884. A short drive east are the [Bluespring Caverns](/northamerica/unitedstates/williams_dam/bluespring_caverns), home to North America's longest navigable underground river. There is a small restaurant in the village and a bait-and-tackle shop that doubles as a local-history museum.
