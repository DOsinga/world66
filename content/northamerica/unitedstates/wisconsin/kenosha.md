Kenosha sits on Lake Michigan between Milwaukee and Chicago, close enough to both that it is often treated as a commuter edge rather than a destination. That sells it short. The old harbour has been turned into a compact museum district, downtown still has red-brick storefronts and vintage streetcars, and the lakefront gives the city a better front door than many larger places on the Great Lakes.

The best reason to stop is the group of museums by the water. The [Kenosha Public Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/wisconsin/kenosha/kenosha_public_museum) has natural-history displays and a fine-art collection, while the [Civil War Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/wisconsin/kenosha/civil_war_museum) tells the war from an Upper Midwest angle. A few blocks inland, the [Dinosaur Discovery Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/wisconsin/kenosha/dinosaur_discovery_museum) turns a former Beaux-Arts post office into a small but serious dinosaur stop.

Kenosha also has a useful sense of place. Nash, Rambler, and AMC cars were built here for much of the twentieth century, and the loss of that industry still explains the city's rougher edges. Ride the [Kenosha Streetcar](/northamerica/unitedstates/wisconsin/kenosha/kenosha_streetcar) around the harbour loop, walk the lakefront, and you get a city that is modest but not bland.
