  Gideon Hixon arrived in La Crosse in the 1850s and made his fortune in the Wisconsin lumber trade. His 1859 Italianate mansion, built when La Crosse was a booming river town, reflects the optimism of that era. Hixon refused to sell the house during the timber bust that followed — it stayed in the family until 1971 and was then donated to the La Crosse County Historical Society with most of its original furnishings intact, which is why it is so unusually well preserved.

The Gideon C. Hixon House is a National Historic Landmark and one of the best-preserved Victorian homes in Wisconsin. Built in 1859 in the Italianate style, it stands on a residential street close to downtown and is open for tours during the warmer months. Unlike many historic houses that have been stripped and redecorated over generations, the Hixon House retains a remarkable amount of its original furniture, wallpaper, and decorative objects.

The house tells the story of La Crosse's lumber boom years, when the city was a major processing point for the white pine forests of northern Wisconsin. Hixon's parlors, dining rooms, and upstairs bedrooms give a vivid picture of upper-middle-class life in a river town in the 1860s and 1870s. The garden and carriage house are also worth a look. Tours are guided and run for about an hour; check the La Crosse County Historical Society's website for current hours.
