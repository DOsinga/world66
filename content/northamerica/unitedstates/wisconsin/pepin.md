Pepin is a small village on the eastern shore of [Lake Pepin](/northamerica/unitedstates/wisconsin/pepin/lake_pepin), a 28-mile-long natural widening of the Mississippi River in western Wisconsin. It is best known as the birthplace of Laura Ingalls Wilder — the [cabin site](/northamerica/unitedstates/wisconsin/pepin/laura_ingalls_wilder_cabin) is seven miles north of town, and September's Laura Ingalls Wilder Days draw fans of the books from across the country.

The village was settled in 1846, named after two French trappers, and grew as a steamboat stop on the Mississippi. Mark Twain knew it; wealthy Chicagoans summered on the lake. Today it is a quiet place with a handful of restaurants, shops, and a marina with transient docking for visitors arriving by water.

[Lake Pepin](/northamerica/unitedstates/wisconsin/pepin/lake_pepin) itself, formed by the delta of the Chippewa River, is the real draw — sailing, fishing, kayaking in summer, ice boating and cross-country skiing in winter. The lake is also where Ralph Samuelson invented water skiing in 1922. The [Harbor View Cafe](/northamerica/unitedstates/wisconsin/pepin/harbor_view_cafe) has built a culinary reputation that reaches well beyond Wisconsin — a seasonal, cash-only operation that fills on weekends with visitors from the Twin Cities. Pepin is about 70 miles southeast of Minneapolis-St. Paul on the [Great River Road](/northamerica/unitedstates/wisconsin/pepin/great_river_road) and makes a pleasant weekend escape.
