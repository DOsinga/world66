Sheboygan sits on the shore of Lake Michigan in eastern Wisconsin and calls itself the Bratwurst Capital of the World — a title earned by the German immigrants who settled here in the nineteenth century. A proper Sheboygan brat is a double bratwurst in a hard roll with mustard and onions, and every August the city hosts Brat Days, a festival dedicated to the cause.

Beyond sausage, Sheboygan has a surprisingly good art scene centred on the [John Michael Kohler Arts Center](/northamerica/unitedstates/wisconsin/sheboygan/john_michael_kohler_arts_center), which houses an eccentric and excellent collection of outsider and folk art — including the [James Tellen Woodland Sculpture Garden](/northamerica/unitedstates/wisconsin/sheboygan/tellen_sculpture_garden), a remarkable environment created by a self-taught folk artist. The lakefront offers sandy beaches, a harbour district with restaurants, and some of the best [freshwater surfing](/northamerica/unitedstates/wisconsin/sheboygan/lake_michigan_surfing) in the Great Lakes — Sheboygan has hosted national surfing competitions on waves that form during autumn storms. [Kohler-Andrae State Park](/northamerica/unitedstates/wisconsin/sheboygan/kohler_andrae_state_park), a few miles to the south, protects a rare stretch of natural Lake Michigan shoreline with sand dunes and excellent swimming.
