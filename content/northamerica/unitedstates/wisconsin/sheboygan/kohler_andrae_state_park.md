  The dunes at Kohler-Andrae were nearly lost to a proposed development in the 1920s before Marie Christine Andrae donated her family's lakeside property to the state of Wisconsin in 1927. The Kohler family added an adjoining tract in 1966. The result is 1,000 acres of protected Lake Michigan shoreline with dunes reaching 50 feet — a rare remnant of the natural lakefront landscape that once extended the full length of Wisconsin.
---

Kohler-Andrae State Park sits about five miles south of Sheboygan on the Lake Michigan shoreline, with two miles of sandy beach backed by sand dunes, coastal forest, and a boardwalk trail through the dune ecosystem. The park protects one of the few remaining significant stretches of natural Lake Michigan shoreline in Wisconsin, and the dune landscape — grasses, cottonwoods, and the hummocked sand formations shaped by the lake wind — feels genuinely wild in a way the surrounding flatlands do not.

The beach is broad and usable for swimming in summer; in autumn, when the crowds have gone, it's better still. The Woodland Dunes Nature Trail takes you through successional forest behind the dunes where deer are common. The park also has a decent campground if you want to stay. Day-use entry requires a Wisconsin state park sticker.
