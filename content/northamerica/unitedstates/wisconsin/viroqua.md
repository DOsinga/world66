Viroqua is a small town of about 5,000 people in the heart of Wisconsin's Driftless Region — the southwest corner of the state that was never flattened by glaciers. The result is a landscape of winding coulees, sandstone bluffs, spring-fed streams, and valleys that look like nowhere else in the Midwest.

The town sits along US Route 14, about ninety miles west of Madison. It's an unlikely mix: old-school farming community and countercultural enclave, where conventional and organic farmers share the same county roads, and where you'll find art galleries, a farmers' market, live music venues, and good coffee within a few blocks of each other. The restored [historic Main Street](/northamerica/unitedstates/wisconsin/viroqua/main_street) and the member-owned [Viroqua Food Co-op](/northamerica/unitedstates/wisconsin/viroqua/viroqua_food_coop) are the visible centre of it. The surrounding Vernon County has one of the highest concentrations of organic farms in the country.

Viroqua is small enough to walk across in twenty minutes, but it punches above its weight culturally. It makes a good base for exploring the broader Driftless Area — the hiking, the trout streams, and the quiet back roads that loop through the hills.
