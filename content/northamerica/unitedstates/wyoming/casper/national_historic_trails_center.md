  in 1847 Brigham Young's Mormon pioneer company ran a ferry on the site to earn cash from passing

A federally run museum perched on a bluff above the North Platte, dedicated to the great westward
migrations of the 19th century. The exhibits are unusually well-mounted for a regional museum:
mock wagon crossings of the Platte (you sit in a covered wagon while the floor rocks), Pony
Express recreations, Native American perspectives on the migrations, and a thoughtful section on
disease and death on the trail.

Allow ninety minutes to two hours. Free; the bluff itself gives a fine view across the river
valley toward town. Closed Sunday and Monday in winter.
