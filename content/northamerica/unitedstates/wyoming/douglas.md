Douglas is a small Wyoming ranching town on the North Platte River, known for two things: the [Wyoming State Fair](/northamerica/unitedstates/wyoming/douglas/wyoming_state_fair), held here every August since 1905, and the [Jackalope](/northamerica/unitedstates/wyoming/douglas/jackalope_square). The mythical antlered jackrabbit was invented here in 1934 by local taxidermist Douglas Herrick, who grafted a set of deer antlers onto a jackrabbit carcass and sold the result to the Hotel LaBonte for $10. Douglas has embraced the joke ever since — the giant [Jackalope Square](/northamerica/unitedstates/wyoming/douglas/jackalope_square) statue stands in the centre of town, and the Chamber of Commerce issues official hunting licenses good for a single fictional date each year.

The history runs deeper than the kitsch. [Fort Fetterman State Historic Site](/northamerica/unitedstates/wyoming/douglas/fort_fetterman_state_historic_site), eleven miles northwest on a bluff above the North Platte, was the Army's main staging post for the campaigns that ended the Indian Wars on the northern plains in 1876. The [Wyoming Pioneer Memorial Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/wyoming/douglas/wyoming_pioneer_memorial_museum) on the fairgrounds covers this history in detail, and includes Tom Horn's saddle in its collection. More surprisingly, the western side of town holds the site of a massive World War II prisoner of war camp — the [Camp Douglas POW Site](/northamerica/unitedstates/wyoming/douglas/camp_douglas_pow_site) once held 3,000 German prisoners, and Italian inmates painted murals on the Officers' Club walls that are now on the National Register of Historic Places.

Downtown Douglas has a coherent grid of late Victorian and Edwardian brick buildings, including the [Hotel LaBonte](/northamerica/unitedstates/wyoming/douglas/hotel_labonte) (1913), the [College Inn Bar](/northamerica/unitedstates/wyoming/douglas/college_inn_bar) (1906, listed on the National Register), the 1886 railroad depot that is now the [Douglas Railroad Interpretive Center](/northamerica/unitedstates/wyoming/douglas/douglas_railroad_interpretive_center), and the Gothic Revival [Christ Episcopal Church](/northamerica/unitedstates/wyoming/douglas/christ_episcopal_church) from 1898. The North Platte offers blue-ribbon trout fishing almost from the town's doorstep. To the south, [Laramie Peak](/northamerica/unitedstates/wyoming/douglas/laramie_peak_hiking) (10,275 feet) in the Medicine Bow National Forest gives serious hikers a full-day objective with big views.
