Grand Teton is the sharp-edged counterpoint to [Yellowstone](/northamerica/unitedstates/wyoming/yellowstone). Where Yellowstone sprawls around geysers, forests, and volcanic plateaus, Grand Teton rises abruptly from the valley floor in one of the cleanest mountain skylines in North America. The range looks almost theatrical: no foothills, just water, sagebrush, and granite.

The park is compact enough to understand quickly but rich enough to reward several days. [Jenny Lake](/northamerica/unitedstates/wyoming/grandteton/jenny_lake) is the classic first stop, [Hidden Falls](/northamerica/unitedstates/wyoming/grandteton/hidden_falls) and [Cascade Canyon](/northamerica/unitedstates/wyoming/grandteton/cascade_canyon_trail) pull hikers into the mountains, and [Schwabacher Landing](/northamerica/unitedstates/wyoming/grandteton/schwabacher_landing) gives the postcard reflection of the Tetons in the Snake River.

Grand Teton is also easier to pair with ordinary travel than many national parks. Jackson is just south of the park, Yellowstone is directly north, and the valley roads make sunrise viewpoints, lake walks, and wildlife drives straightforward. The trick is not access; it is resisting the temptation to treat the park as a scenic drive-through.
