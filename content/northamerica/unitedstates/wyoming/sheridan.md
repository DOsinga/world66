Sheridan sits in the Powder River basin at the foot of the Bighorn Mountains in northern Wyoming, a ranching town that has kept more of its character than most places its size. The historic downtown along Main Street has well-preserved brick buildings, good restaurants, and the [Mint Bar](/northamerica/unitedstates/wyoming/sheridan/mint_bar) — a classic Western saloon with saddles for bar stools, open since 1907. [King's Saddlery and Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/wyoming/sheridan/kings_saddlery) and the [Historic Sheridan Inn](/northamerica/unitedstates/wyoming/sheridan/sheridan_inn) (a National Historic Landmark where Buffalo Bill auditioned Wild West performers) complete the essential Main Street circuit.

The real draw is the landscape. The [Bighorn National Forest](/northamerica/unitedstates/wyoming/sheridan/bighorn_national_forest) rises steeply to the west, with fishing streams, alpine meadows, and the spectacular switchbacks of US Route 14 climbing over the range. The [Tongue River Canyon](/northamerica/unitedstates/wyoming/sheridan/tongue_river_canyon) trail — sheer limestone walls following the river through the foothills — is a highlight accessible as a day trip. Cloud Peak Wilderness offers serious backcountry hiking, and in winter the area gets deep snow for cross-country skiing and snowmobiling.

Sheridan is also the gateway to the region's ranching heritage. The [Brinton Museum](/northamerica/unitedstates/wyoming/sheridan/brinton_museum) in Big Horn, 12 miles south, is an outstanding collection of Western and Native American art in a working ranch setting — one of the best small museums in the Rocky Mountain West. The [Trail End State Historic Site](/northamerica/unitedstates/wyoming/sheridan/trail_end), a Flemish Revival mansion on Clarendon Avenue, tells the story of the cattle barons who built this town. The Sheridan WYO Rodeo in July is one of the largest in the state.
