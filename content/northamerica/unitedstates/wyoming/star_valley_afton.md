Star Valley is a long, fertile valley in western Wyoming, flanked by the Salt River Range to the east and the Wyoming Range to the west. A string of small towns runs down its length — Thayne, Auburn, Grover, Afton, Smoot — with Afton serving as the valley's commercial hub and largest town. The valley floor sits at around 1,900 metres and is ringed by peaks that hold snow into June, keeping the air clean and the summers short.

The valley's most unusual attraction is the Periodic Spring near Afton — one of only three known cold-water intermittent springs in the world. Fed by an underground siphon in the limestone of the Salt River Range, it pulses water every 18 minutes or so during late summer and early autumn, gushing forcefully for several minutes before stopping dead. The effect is genuinely strange, and the short hike up Swift Creek Canyon to reach it passes through scenery that would justify the trip on its own.

Afton itself is a small, tidy Mormon town anchored by a prominent LDS temple and famous for the archway spanning Washington Street, built from thousands of interlocking elk antlers. The arch has been a local landmark since 1958 and appears on virtually every postcard of the valley. The town's most celebrated son is Rulon Gardner, who grew up on a farm here and went on to shock the sports world by defeating the previously unbeaten Russian wrestler Alexander Karelin at the 2000 Sydney Olympics.

Visitors come to Star Valley for the outdoor life: fishing on the Salt River, elk and deer hunting in autumn, snowmobiling and cross-country skiing in winter, and hiking into the Wyoming and Salt River ranges in summer. The valley has genuine Western character without the crowds or the prices of Jackson Hole, 90 km to the north.
