title: "Books"
type: section
---

**In Patagonia** by Bruce Chatwin (1977). The classic English-language travel book about the region, a rambling and brilliantly written sequence of fragments stitched together as Chatwin chased rumours of bandits, Welsh settlers, and a piece of mylodon skin across the south. Half of it isn't even true. Still the best single book on the spirit of Patagonia.

**The Old Patagonian Express** by Paul Theroux (1979). Theroux travelled overland from Boston to Esquel — south of Bariloche — by train and bus, ending his journey on the narrow-gauge **La Trochita** that still runs out of Esquel today. Gives a sense of how isolated the region was within living memory.

**Idle Days in Patagonia** by W. H. Hudson (1893). The Anglo-Argentine naturalist's account of an unplanned year on the Río Negro after a shooting accident left him stranded. Patient and observant, with descriptions of the steppe birds and people that still hold up.

**El Pasajero del Tren del Fin del Mundo** by Adolfo Bioy Casares — a slim Argentine novel about a doomed train journey, set in the wider Patagonian imagination that Bariloche helped create. Available in English as **The Russian Doll**.
