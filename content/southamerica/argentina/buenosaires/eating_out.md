---
title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

Eating in Buenos Aires means steak, at least some of the time. The Argentine *parrilla* (grill) is its own institution: a wood or charcoal fire, a slow cook, cuts you won't find elsewhere (*tira de asado*, *entraña*, *mollejas*), and quantities that justify skipping lunch. The best parrillas are not in the tourist areas — they are in working-class neighbourhoods like Palermo and Villa Crespo, filled with families eating Sunday lunch at 2pm in a way that continues until 6pm.

Beyond the parrilla, Buenos Aires has a serious Italian-inflected food culture — pasta, pizza in a thick Buenos Aires style, and the Italian café tradition transplanted wholesale. Palermo Hollywood has the best restaurant variety, with Peruvian, Japanese, and contemporary Argentine cooking alongside traditional *asados*. San Telmo is good for cheap lunch. Prices vary dramatically depending on whether you pay in pesos at the "blue rate" or in dollars — ask locally.
