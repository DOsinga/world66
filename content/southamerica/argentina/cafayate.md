Cafayate is a small, sun-baked town in the Calchaquí Valley of northwestern Argentina, surrounded by vineyards that produce some of the country's best white wines. At 1,660 metres elevation and with more than 300 days of sunshine a year, this is one of the highest wine regions in the world, and the Torrontés grape — floral, dry, and distinctly Argentine — reaches its peak here.

The town itself is quiet and walkable, centred on a leafy plaza dominated by the [Cathedral of Our Lady of the Rosary](/southamerica/argentina/cafayate/our_lady_cathedral). The [Museo de la Vid y el Vino](/southamerica/argentina/cafayate/museo_de_la_vid) tells the valley's wine story well. The main square has good empanada shops and [ice cream parlours serving wine-flavoured helado](/southamerica/argentina/cafayate/wine_ice_cream) — the Torrontés flavour is worth trying, and the Malbec version is even more surprising.

The drive to Cafayate from [Salta](/southamerica/argentina/salta) through the [Quebrada de Cafayate](/southamerica/argentina/cafayate/quebrada_de_cafayate) is one of the most spectacular road trips in Argentina. The Ruta 68 winds through a canyon of red sandstone formations — the Garganta del Diablo, the Anfiteatro, the Castillos — carved by wind and water into improbable shapes. Most visitors make the drive a loop, going south through the Quebrada and returning via [Cachi and the upper Calchaquí Valley](/southamerica/argentina/cafayate/cachi_and_calchaqui_valley).
