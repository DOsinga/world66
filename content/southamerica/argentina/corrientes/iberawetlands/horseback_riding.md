  The Corrientes gaucho tradition runs deep in the esteros. Local guides who lead horseback tours through the wetland edges often come from families that have worked cattle in the region for generations — families that understand the landscape with an intimacy no visiting naturalist can match. They know which embalsados (floating islands) will bear the weight of a horse, where the marsh deer graze at dawn, and how to read the moods of animals that tourists see as spectacles and they know as neighbours.
---

Horseback riding is an outstanding way to explore the transitional zones between the open lagoon and the higher ground — habitats that boats cannot reach. Tours operate from Colonia Carlos Pellegrini through local estancias and guide services, lasting anywhere from two hours to a full day.

On horseback, you move quietly through the wetland margins, flushing birds from the reed beds and approaching giant anteaters that might scatter from the sound of an engine. Marsh deer, one of South America's most elegant large mammals, are often seen in the flooded grasslands best accessed on horse. The landscape changes character entirely from this angle — the horizon opens up, the sense of scale becomes apparent, and the relationship between water and land that defines the Iberá becomes tangible in a way it cannot from a boat.
