Uspallata is a green oasis village at around 1,750 metres on Ruta 7, the road over the Andes from [Mendoza](/southamerica/argentina/cuyo/mendoza) to Chile. The valley around it is a wide stretch of bare, many-coloured mountains, and it looked enough like the Himalaya to stand in for Tibet in the 1997 film *Seven Years in Tibet*, which was filmed here.

It is the last proper town before the border, which makes it the base for the high-mountain road beyond: Puente del Inca, the natural stone bridge crusted in orange mineral deposits; the viewpoint on Aconcagua at the Horcones valley; and Penitentes, a small ski area. Staying in Uspallata rather than making the long round trip from Mendoza in a day gives you the mountains in the morning, before the tour buses.

In the village itself, Las Bóvedas are a group of domed adobe furnaces from the eighteenth century, used for smelting metal from the nearby mines, and now a small museum. The rest of the appeal is the landscape and the quiet: riding, walking, and the drive west towards the border tunnel, with a summer-only dirt road branching up to the Cristo Redentor statue at 3,800 metres.

Ruta 7 closes in winter storms, sometimes for days; check the pass status before relying on it to cross into Chile.
