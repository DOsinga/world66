Puerto Iguazú is a low-rise frontier town of around 80,000 people, eighteen kilometres downstream from the falls and the base from which most travellers visit them. It has none of the chaos of Foz do Iguaçu across the river — it is small, walkable and unhurried, with a single grid of tree-lined streets stepping down toward the river.

The centre is Avenida Victoria Aguirre, where most of the restaurants, ATMs and tour offices cluster. The Feria de Artesanos (craft market) on the corner of Bompland and Aguirre sells reasonable Guaraní crafts. Avenida Tres Fronteras runs west from the centre to the Triple Frontier viewpoint and is the most pleasant evening walk in town.
