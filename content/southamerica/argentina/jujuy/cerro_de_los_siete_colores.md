The seven-coloured hill rises directly above the village of Purmamarca and is among the most photographed natural features in Argentina. The bands of colour are the product of marine and lacustrine sediments laid down over hundreds of millions of years and then thrust upward and tilted as the Andes rose: red from iron oxide, yellow from limonite, green from copper, white from limestone, mauve from lead.

The view is best from the village plaza early in the morning, when the sun catches the eastern face at a low angle. A signed circular walk (Paseo de los Colorados, about 3 km) loops behind the hill, offering different perspectives and quieter angles less seen by day-trippers.
