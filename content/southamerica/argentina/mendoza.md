Mendoza is the wine capital of Argentina, a sun-drenched city at the foot of the Andes that draws visitors as much for its outdoor adventure as for its world-class Malbec. Set at 760 metres above sea level, it sits roughly 200 km from [Santiago](/southamerica/chile/santiago) across the mountains, and the proximity to the Andes shapes almost everything about it — the climate, the irrigation channels that water its vineyards, and the easy access to some of South America's finest high-altitude terrain.

The city was founded in 1561 and rebuilt after a catastrophic earthquake in 1861, which explains why historic architecture is sparse but the street grid is wide and deliberate. Tall sycamores line the avenues, and a network of acequias (irrigation ditches) runs alongside the footpaths, giving the city centre a leafy, unhurried character. [Plaza Independencia](/southamerica/argentina/mendoza/plaza_independencia), the central square, is ringed by four smaller plazas — each with its own character — and the [Teatro Independencia](/southamerica/argentina/mendoza/teatro_independencia), a neoclassical opera house from 1925, faces it from the north edge. The [Ruinas de San Francisco](/southamerica/argentina/mendoza/ruinas_de_san_francisco) and [Museo Fundacional](/southamerica/argentina/mendoza/museo_fundacional) tell the story of what was lost in that earthquake. The [Parque San Martín](/southamerica/argentina/mendoza/parque_san_martin), a vast Beaux-Arts park on the western edge of the centre, is the focal point for weekend life, and [Cerro de la Gloria](/southamerica/argentina/mendoza/cerro_de_la_gloria) at its summit gives the city's best views of the Andes.

Around Mendoza stretch the major wine-producing valleys. [Maipú](/southamerica/argentina/mendoza/maipu_wine_tour), closest to the city, is best explored by bicycle between bodegas. [Luján de Cuyo](/southamerica/argentina/mendoza/lujan_de_cuyo), Argentina's first demarcated wine appellation, produces the benchmark high-altitude Malbec. The [Uco Valley](/southamerica/argentina/mendoza/uco_valley), 100 kilometres south in the Andean foothills at 900 to 1,200 metres, is the region's most ambitious frontier — its extreme day-night temperature swings producing wines of distinctive concentration and freshness. The harvest festival, the **Fiesta de la Vendimia**, fills the city each March with parades, concerts, and theatrical spectacle.

Beyond the vines, the Andes offer [white-water rafting on the Mendoza River](/southamerica/argentina/mendoza/rafting_mendoza_river) in the Potrerillos canyon, skiing at Las Leñas in winter, and the perpetual draw of [Aconcagua](/southamerica/argentina/mendoza/aconcagua), the highest peak in the Western Hemisphere at 6,961 metres. The road northwest toward the mountain passes [Puente del Inca](/southamerica/argentina/mendoza/puente_del_inca), a natural mineral-encrusted arch at 2,744 metres that ranks among the most visually striking geological formations in the Andes. Nightlife centres on [Calle Arístides Villanueva](/southamerica/argentina/mendoza/aristides_villanueva), the main bar and restaurant strip.
