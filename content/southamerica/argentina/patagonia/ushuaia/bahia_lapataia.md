Bahía Lapataia is the end of the road — literally. A small bay on the Beagle Channel inside Tierra del Fuego National Park, it marks where Route 3 terminates at the southernmost driveable point in the Americas. The setting is austere and beautiful: a narrow cove flanked by subantarctic forest, the channel stretching south toward the Chilean islands and, beyond them, Antarctica.

A short boardwalk trail loops around the bay and through the surrounding peat bogs, which can look extraordinary in the low evening light — the water black, the sedges gold. Cormorants nest on the rocky outcrops in the channel, and you may see southern sea lions hauled out on the stones nearby. The wind off the channel is almost always present.

Getting here requires walking or cycling the Senda Costera trail from the park entrance (about 8 kilometres each way), taking the train to the park and walking the rest, or driving to the park's paid parking area and walking a short trail to the bay. It is worth the effort.
