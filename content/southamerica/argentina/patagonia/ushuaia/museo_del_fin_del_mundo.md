The End of the World Museum is Ushuaia's main cultural institution and a good place to understand the region's layered history before heading out into it. The collection covers natural history — with an impressive section on Fuegian flora and fauna, including birds of the channel — alongside exhibits on the Yagán and Selk'nam peoples who lived in this region for thousands of years before European contact, and on the European explorers and settlers who arrived from the late 18th century onward.

A separate section covers the history of Ushuaia's infamous prison, the Presidio de Ushuaia, which operated from 1896 to 1947 and housed Argentina's most dangerous criminals. The prison was effectively what built the city — prisoners provided the labour for construction projects and the woodcutting that cleared the surrounding hills. The original prison building is now a separate museum worth visiting for the cell reconstructions and prisoner histories.

The museum is centrally located on Maipú, a few blocks from the harbour. Opening hours vary by season; check locally.
