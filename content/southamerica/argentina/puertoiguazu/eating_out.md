---
title: Eating Out
type: section
---

Puerto Iguazú is a tourist town, and the food reflects that — lots of mid-range Argentine grills, a few river-fish specialists, and a small but growing scene of restaurants doing something more interesting with the produce of the surrounding subtropics. The local cuisine fuses Argentine, Guaraní, and Brazilian influences: try **surubí** (a giant Paraná river catfish), **pacú** (a vegetarian river fish that the locals grill whole), **mandioca** (cassava) in every conceivable form, and *chipá* (cheese-bread rolls from across the border in Paraguay).

Most restaurants cluster on or near **Avenida Victoria Aguirre** and **Avenida Brasil**, the two main streets running away from the bus terminal. Prices have crept up in dollar terms over the last decade but Iguazú remains substantially cheaper than Buenos Aires.
