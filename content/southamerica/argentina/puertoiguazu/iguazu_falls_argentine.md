The Argentine side of the falls is where you spend the most time, get closest to the water, and feel the spray on your face. **Parque Nacional Iguazú** has three main walkways: the **Upper Circuit** (Circuito Superior), a 1.7 km loop along the top of the cascades; the **Lower Circuit** (Circuito Inferior), 1.4 km of paths descending to viewpoints at the bottom of various falls; and the **Devil's Throat Walkway** (Paseo Garganta del Diablo), a 1.1 km elevated metal walkway reached by a small free train from the visitor centre.

Plan for a full day. The park opens at 8am — get there at opening if you can; by mid-morning the place fills with tour groups. Bring cash for the entrance fee (different prices for Argentinians, Mercosur visitors, and other foreigners — and the foreign price has crept up sharply), plenty of water, sunblock, and either a waterproof or quick-drying clothes. The visitor centre at the entrance has lockers, a small museum, restaurants, and a shop. Multi-day entry is discounted on the second day.
