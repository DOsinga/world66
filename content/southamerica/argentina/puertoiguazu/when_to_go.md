---
title: When to Go
type: section
---

The falls are spectacular year-round, but water volume varies considerably. April and May bring the highest flows, after the summer rains fill the Iguazú River basin — the Devil's Throat roars at its loudest and the spray is impenetrable. This is the most dramatic time to visit, though the walkways can be partially closed when water levels are very high.

June through August is the Argentine winter: cooler temperatures (comfortable rather than cold), lower humidity, and excellent conditions for the long circuit walks and wildlife watching. Dawn birding is particularly good. The falls have less volume but remain impressive.

December through February is hot and humid — genuinely intense heat that makes extended walking tiring. The crowds are also at their peak, with Argentine school summer holidays adding to the international visitor flow.

October and November are the most balanced months: good water levels after the dry season, comfortable temperatures, and lighter crowds than peak season. A good time to visit if you have flexibility.

Avoid Argentine school holidays (July for winter break, January for summer) if you want thinner crowds and lower accommodation prices.
