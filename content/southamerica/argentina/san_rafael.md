San Rafael is the second city of Mendoza province, sitting about 230 km south of Mendoza city in a valley of vineyards and fruit orchards at the foot of the Andes. It's a quieter, less polished version of Mendoza — around 175,000 people, a relaxed pace, and wine country on every side. Most travellers use it as a base for the [Atuel Canyon](/southamerica/argentina/san_rafael/atuel_canyon) or as a staging post for the ski resort at [Las Leñas](/southamerica/argentina/san_rafael/las_lenas).

The wine scene here is genuine and affordable. The region has around a hundred wineries, most of them small family operations that have been working the same land since Italian and Spanish immigrants arrived in the late 19th century. [Bianchi](/southamerica/argentina/san_rafael/bodega_bianchi) is the most famous label, founded by an Italian immigrant in 1928; [Suter](/southamerica/argentina/san_rafael/bodega_suter), of Swiss origin, is even older. Others welcome visitors with a handshake rather than a ticketing system. Wine tour lunches in San Rafael cost a fraction of what they charge in the Luján de Cuyo appellation.

The [Atuel Canyon](/southamerica/argentina/san_rafael/atuel_canyon), 60 km south-west, is the landscape that brings people here who aren't just chasing wine. The Atuel River has carved through red and ochre rock to create a canyon of real drama. You can raft the rapids, kayak the calmer stretches, ride a catamaran on [Dique Valle Grande](/southamerica/argentina/san_rafael/valle_grande_reservoir), or hike the trails along the rim. The valley fills with campers in summer. East of town, at the historic [Finca Los Alamos](/southamerica/argentina/san_rafael/finca_los_alamos), the [Borges Memorial Maze](/southamerica/argentina/san_rafael/borges_memorial_maze) is one of the more curious literary tributes in South America.

San Rafael is three to four hours from Mendoza by bus, or a scenic drive through wine country along Route 144.
