Tierra del Fuego is the great island at the southern tip of South America, divided between Argentina and Chile along a north-south line. It is one of the most remote and dramatic landscapes on the continent — dense Nothofagus forests, glacial lakes, peat bogs, and mountains that plunge directly into the Beagle Channel. Wind and rain are constants; the light, when it comes, is extraordinary.

[Ushuaia](/southamerica/argentina/patagonia/ushuaia), on the Argentine side, is the base for most visitors. It bills itself as the southernmost city in the world and has the infrastructure — restaurants, gear shops, national park — to back up that claim. The [Tierra del Fuego National Park](/southamerica/argentina/tierradelfuego/tierradelfuego_national_park), just west of Ushuaia, offers excellent hiking through coastal beech forest to the shores of Lapataia Bay, where the Pan-American Highway system ends in a small wooden sign at the edge of the water.

Boat trips on the [Beagle Channel](/southamerica/argentina/tierradelfuego/beagle_channel) reach sea lion rocks, cormorant colonies, and penguin nesting grounds at Martillo Island. Above town, a trail leads up through lenga beech forest to the retreating [Martial Glacier](/southamerica/argentina/tierradelfuego/martial_glacier) with views across the channel. The island is also the main departure point for Antarctic cruises — agencies in Ushuaia sell last-minute berths at reduced prices in March and April.
