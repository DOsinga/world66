The Beagle Channel is the strait that separates the Argentine and Chilean portions of Tierra del Fuego from a chain of smaller islands to the south, ending at Cape Horn. Named after HMS Beagle — the ship that carried Darwin on his voyage of discovery — the channel is one of the most dramatic seaways in the world, with mountains plunging directly into dark water on both sides.

Boat excursions from Ushuaia take two to four hours and make stops at sea lion colonies, cormorant rocks, and the famous Eclaireurs Lighthouse. More ambitious trips sail out to Martillo Island, where a colony of Magellanic penguins nests from October to April — you can walk among them in small groups. Full-day tours continue to Estancia Harberton, Argentina's oldest estancia, with a museum of marine mammals and seabirds.
