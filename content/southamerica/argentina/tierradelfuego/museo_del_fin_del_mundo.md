The End of the World Museum (Museo del Fin del Mundo) occupies a handsome 1912 building on Ushuaia's main waterfront street. The collection spans the full arc of human history on the island: the Selknam and Yamana indigenous peoples who lived here for thousands of years, the European explorers who encountered them in the nineteenth century, the penal colony era, and the development of the city and port.

Particularly affecting is the section on the Yamana people, whose population was devastated by introduced diseases after European contact. Objects, photographs, and documentary material tell a story that is both specific to this extreme southern edge of the continent and painfully familiar from indigenous histories worldwide.
