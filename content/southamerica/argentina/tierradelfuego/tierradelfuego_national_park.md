Tierra del Fuego National Park protects nearly 69,000 hectares of subantarctic forest, glacial lakes, peat bogs, and Beagle Channel coastline at the foot of the Andes. It is the only Argentine national park that combines marine, forest, and mountain environments in one place. The park begins about 11 kilometres west of Ushuaia, and the entrance is a short drive down National Road 3.

The most popular trail is the Senda Costera, an eight-kilometre coastal path from Ensenada Bay to Lapataia Bay, winding through lenga beech forest above the Beagle Channel. At Lapataia Bay itself — Argentina's only fjord — the famous Route 3 sign marks the terminus of the entire Pan-American Highway network. The forests around Lago Roca are the best place to spot the crimson-headed Magellanic woodpecker, one of the most striking birds in Patagonia.
