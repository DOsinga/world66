Amboró National Park covers more than 4,400 square kilometres of dramatic terrain west of [Santa Cruz](/southamerica/bolivia/santacruz), where the eastern Andes drop into the Amazon basin and three distinct ecosystems — cloud forest, lowland jungle, and Andean montane scrub — converge in one protected area. That triple collision makes it extraordinary: over 820 bird species have been recorded here, more than in all of Europe, and the mammal list includes spectacled bears, jaguars, tapirs, and giant armadillos.

Two gateway towns give access to the park from different directions. [Samaipata](/southamerica/bolivia/samaipata), two to three hours west of Santa Cruz on the old Cochabamba road, is the entrance to the southern cloud-forest section. The Giant Fern Trail from this side passes through tree ferns reaching 10 metres tall, with views across sandstone cliffs and Andean foothills to the lowlands below. Buena Vista, to the north, opens into the park's warmer lowland sector, where multi-day jungle treks are organised.

All visits require a certified guide and a permit — both are easily arranged in Samaipata or Buena Vista, and skipping them is not just inadvisable but prohibited. Trails are unmarked and the terrain can be disorienting. A one-day cloud-forest hike from the Samaipata entrance is the most popular and accessible option; for the lowland section, budget two or three days. The park rewards patience: bigger mammals require time in deeper forest, but birders will start tallying extraordinary species within the first hour.
