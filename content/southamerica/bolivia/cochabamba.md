Cochabamba sits in a broad valley in central Bolivia at around 2,500 metres — low enough that the climate is genuinely pleasant year-round, which is why locals call it the "City of Eternal Spring." It's Bolivia's third largest city and its gastronomic capital: Bolivians from La Paz and Santa Cruz make the trip just to eat here.

The city is sprawling and not conventionally beautiful, but it has real energy. [La Cancha](/southamerica/bolivia/cochabamba/la_cancha) — one of the largest open-air markets in South America — is a full-sensory experience, and the [Plaza 14 de Septiembre](/southamerica/bolivia/cochabamba/plaza_14_de_septiembre) gives the city a human-scale colonial centre worth lingering in. The [Cristo de la Concordia](/southamerica/bolivia/cochabamba/cristo_de_la_concordia) statue looms over everything from the Cerro de San Pedro hill, visible from almost anywhere in the valley.

Cochabamba is also a practical base for getting into the Bolivian countryside. The Chapare region to the north — humid cloud forest giving way to Amazon lowlands — is a half-day bus ride away, Torotoro National Park's dinosaur footprints and canyon landscapes are three hours south, and the colonial town of Sucre is reachable by road or a short flight.
