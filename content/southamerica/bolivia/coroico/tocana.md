A small Afro-Bolivian village about 15 kilometres south of Coroico, deep in the cloudforest of the Nor Yungas. Tocaña and the nearby communities are home to most of Bolivia's small Afro-descendant population — descendants of enslaved Africans brought to the colonial silver mines at Potosí and later moved here to grow coca and coffee. Many residents still speak with a distinctive accent and practise their own music and dance traditions.

The village is reached by a single rough road from Coroico. Saya — Afro-Bolivian drumming and dance — is performed for visitors when groups arrange ahead; just turning up means a quiet day of farms and forest. The community offers coffee and coca tours and basic homestays. Treat the visit with respect: this is a living village, not a heritage attraction.
