The Ethnographic and Folkloric Museum in Oruro's city centre is the best place to understand the Carnaval outside of experiencing it. The museum presents the major dances — Diablada, Morenada, Caporales, Tinku, Suri Sikuri — through full costume displays, masks, musical instruments, and documentation of their origins and social significance.

The Diablada mask collection is particularly impressive. These are not mass-produced costume pieces but hand-crafted works, made from plaster and painted in elaborate detail, representing a distinct artistic tradition that traces back at least to the 18th century. The museum also covers the pre-Columbian and colonial history of the region, with exhibits on the Uru people who inhabited the shores of Lake Poopó and the altiplano before Spanish colonisation. Small admission fee; well-labelled in Spanish.
