The Santuario del Socavón is Oruro's most important building — a Baroque church built into the hillside above a former mine entrance, dedicated to the Virgen del Socavón, the patroness of miners and the spiritual centre of the Carnaval de Oruro. The original chapel dates to 1781, built over the site where the miraculous image of the Virgin was found in a mine shaft; the current sanctuary is a 20th-century reconstruction with a striking painted interior and a tower that dominates the hill above the city centre.

The crypt below the sanctuary descends into the actual mine shaft, now converted into a museum of mining and Carnival history. The collection documents the Diablada and other dances performed at Carnival, with original costumes, masks, and instruments that convey the extraordinary visual density of the festival. The mine tunnels visible from the museum entrance are a reminder that this is not a folkloric museum but an active site of religious devotion — miners still pray here before descending into the altiplano's surviving mines.
