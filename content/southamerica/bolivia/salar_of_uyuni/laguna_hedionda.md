Laguna Hedionda is not named to flatter it: the sulphur in and around the lake gives off a sharp smell, especially when the wind drops. The reward is the setting. Flamingos feed in the shallows, volcanic cones sit on the horizon, and the water shifts from blue to grey depending on the sky.

It is a classic stop on the multi-day Uyuni route, usually somewhere between the salt flat and the deeper south of the Eduardo Avaroa reserve. Do not expect solitude, but do take a few minutes away from the vehicles; the birdlife and the scale of the plateau are easier to appreciate once the engines are off.
