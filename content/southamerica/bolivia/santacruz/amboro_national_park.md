Amboró National Park covers 4,000 square kilometres of dramatic terrain west of Santa Cruz, where the Andes drop into the Amazon basin and three distinct ecosystems — cloud forest, lowland jungle, and montane scrub — converge. It is one of the most biodiverse parks in Bolivia, with spectacled bears, jaguars, more than 800 species of bird, and stands of giant fern trees that look prehistoric.

The most accessible entry points are Buena Vista and Samaipata, both reached by road from Santa Cruz in two to three hours. Buena Vista is the gateway to the lowland section, with multi-day jungle treks; Samaipata gives access to the cloud forest and the spectacular sandstone cliffs of Mataracú and Volcanes. Hire a guide — trails are unmarked and a permit is required.
