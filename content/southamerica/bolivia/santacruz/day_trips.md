title: Day Trips
type: section
---

Santa Cruz works best as a base. The city itself is short on sights, but within a few hours' drive lie some of Bolivia's most spectacular landscapes and cultural sites.

The closest escape is [Lomas de Arena](/southamerica/bolivia/lomas_de_arena), a 14,000-hectare protected park of towering sand dunes and seasonal lagoons just 12 km south of the city — a half-day by 4x4. West of the city, [Amboró National Park](/southamerica/bolivia/amboro_national_park) covers more than 4,400 square kilometres where the Andes meet the Amazon, with over 820 bird species and terrain ranging from cloud forest to lowland jungle. The mountain town of [Samaipata](/southamerica/bolivia/samaipata), a three-hour drive up into the Andean foothills, is the park's most accessible gateway and also the base for visiting El Fuerte, a UNESCO-listed pre-Inca ceremonial rock. Both Amboró and Samaipata work as full-day or one-night trips.

The [Jesuit Missions of Chiquitos](/southamerica/bolivia/jesuit_missions), a UNESCO World Heritage circuit of six restored 18th-century mission churches scattered across the plains east of Santa Cruz, need at least four days to do properly and reward a week. Further afield, multi-day tours run into the Pantanal wetlands toward the Brazilian border and north to the Beni jungle around Trinidad and Rurrenabaque.
