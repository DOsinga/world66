Samaipata is a small town in the foothills of the Andes, a three-hour drive from Santa Cruz on the old road to Cochabamba. It sits at 1,650 metres — cool, green, and a complete change of climate from the lowland heat of Santa Cruz — and has become a long-stay favourite for European travellers, with good cafes, hostels, and a relaxed pace.

The main draw is El Fuerte, a UNESCO-listed pre-Columbian sacred rock 9 km outside town. A single sandstone outcrop, roughly 250 metres long, was carved over centuries with niches, channels, seats, and zoomorphic figures — first by the Chané, then expanded by the Inca as the empire's south-eastern frontier outpost. It is one of the largest carved monoliths in the world, and the site remains spectacularly mysterious; nobody is quite sure what most of the carvings were for.
