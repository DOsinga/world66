Águas de São Pedro is one of Brazil's smallest municipalities, a planned spa town folded into the countryside northwest of [São Paulo](/southamerica/brazil/saopaulo). It exists because of water: three mineral springs, a municipal bath house, and a century-old belief that a weekend here should leave you a little healthier.

The town is compact and deliberately gentle. The two essential stops, the [Spa Thermal Doutor Octavio Moura Andrade](/southamerica/brazil/aguas_de_sao_pedro/spa_thermal_doutor_octavio_moura_andrade) and the [Fontanário Municipal](/southamerica/brazil/aguas_de_sao_pedro/fontanario_municipal), sit close together on Avenida Carlos Mauro. Around them are leafy streets, small cafes, and the sort of tidy resort-town landscaping that tells you Águas was planned for promenading rather than rushing.

It is not a grand destination, and it does not pretend to be. Come if you like old Brazilian spa culture, family water-park days, and quiet green corners such as [Mini Horto](/southamerica/brazil/aguas_de_sao_pedro/mini_horto). If you are already in the Circuito das Águas or Piracicaba region with a spare day, Águas de São Pedro is a pleasant, very specific detour.
