Arraial da Ajuda is a beach village on the coast of southern Bahia, across the river from [Porto Seguro](/southamerica/brazil/portoseguro) — the spot where the Portuguese first landed in Brazil over 500 years ago. Despite its proximity to Porto Seguro's package tourism, Arraial has kept its village atmosphere: dirt streets lined with colourful houses, a hilltop church with ocean views, and a string of beautiful beaches backed by low cliffs.

The beaches are the main draw. [Mucugê](/southamerica/brazil/arraial_da_ajuda/praia_do_mucuge) and [Parracho](/southamerica/brazil/arraial_da_ajuda/praia_do_parracho) are the liveliest, with beach bars and music. [Pitinga](/southamerica/brazil/arraial_da_ajuda/praia_da_pitinga), further south, has dramatic clay cliffs and fewer crowds. For real solitude, walk south to [Taipe](/southamerica/brazil/arraial_da_ajuda/praia_de_taipe) or Rio da Barra. The water is warm year-round, the sun reliable, and the coconut water cold.

Arraial also has serious nightlife for a small village — parties start after midnight and go until morning, especially during Carnival, New Year, and the São João festival in June. The [Rua do Mucugê](/southamerica/brazil/arraial_da_ajuda/rua_do_mucuge), the main street, is lined with restaurants, bars, and shops. From here, you can take day trips south to [Trancoso](/southamerica/brazil/trancoso) (a quieter, more upscale village) and Caraíva (even more remote, and until recently without electric light).

The old [Igreja Nossa Senhora d'Ajuda](/southamerica/brazil/arraial_da_ajuda/igreja_nossa_senhora_dajuda) sits at the top of the village, with a viewpoint over the river and the coast. To get here, fly to Porto Seguro from any major Brazilian city, then take the [ferry across the river](/southamerica/brazil/arraial_da_ajuda/balsa_buranhem) and a short bus or taxi up the hill.
