  The old port warehouses that once processed rubber and Brazil nuts have been converted into an
  open-sided complex of restaurants, bars, and performance spaces right on the Guamá River.
  The iron structures were imported from England in the early twentieth century; the renovation
  preserved the bones of the buildings while opening them to the river breeze. In the evening,
  when the light drops and the river goes orange, it is the most pleasant place in the city to
  have a beer.
---

The complex has a craft brewery, several restaurants serving Amazonian cuisine, and a small theater. There is also a viewing platform where you can watch river traffic — the enormous boats that serve as passenger ferries between Belém and Manaus, a journey of four days.

It can feel slightly touristy during peak hours, but locals come here too, particularly on weekend evenings. The location directly on the waterfront, looking out toward Marajó Island, is genuinely impressive.
