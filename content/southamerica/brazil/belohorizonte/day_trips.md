Belo Horizonte sits in the heart of Brazil's 18th-century mining region, surrounded by colonial towns and natural parks that make excellent day trips.

The gold-mining towns are the most popular excursions. [Ouro Preto](/southamerica/brazil/ouropreto), about two hours southeast, is the grandest — a UNESCO World Heritage city of baroque churches and Aleijadinho sculpture that needs a full day. [Congonhas](/southamerica/brazil/congonhas), 80 km south, has the twelve soapstone prophets that many consider Aleijadinho's masterpiece and works well as a half-day or combined with Ouro Preto. Closest of all is [Sabará](/southamerica/brazil/sabara), only 20 km east and reachable by city bus, with three remarkable colonial churches including the Chinese-influenced Nossa Senhora do Ó.

For something other than baroque, the open-air contemporary art park at [Inhotim](/southamerica/brazil/belohorizonte/inhotim_daytrip), 60 km west near Brumadinho, is the single most rewarding excursion for cultural visitors — allow a whole day. The [Maquiné Cave](/southamerica/brazil/belohorizonte/maquine_cave) to the north is one of Brazil's oldest show caves, with illuminated chambers and a park to round out a day. And [Parque Natural do Caraça](/southamerica/brazil/belohorizonte/parque_do_caraca) to the east offers mountain hiking through high-elevation Atlantic Forest — bring a jacket, as it gets cold after sunset even in summer.
