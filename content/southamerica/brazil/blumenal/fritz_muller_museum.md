  Fritz Müller came to Brazil in 1852, initially as a colonist, and spent the
  rest of his life studying the biology of the Atlantic Forest and the rivers
  of Santa Catarina. His correspondence with Charles Darwin — which began in
  the 1860s and continued for decades — was pivotal in providing Darwin with
  evidence for natural selection from tropical species. Darwin described Müller
  as "one of the greatest naturalists in the world." Müller died in Blumenau
  in 1897.

The Fritz Müller Museum honors the German naturalist who spent four decades studying the ecology of the Atlantic Forest and the rivers around Blumenau, corresponding with Charles Darwin and contributing crucial evidence for the theory of evolution. The museum is small but worthwhile — a reminder that Blumenau's contribution to history goes beyond beer and half-timbered buildings.

The exhibits cover Müller's scientific work, his collections of local flora and fauna, and his relationship with Darwin. For anyone interested in the history of evolutionary biology, this is an unexpected find in a Brazilian beer city.
