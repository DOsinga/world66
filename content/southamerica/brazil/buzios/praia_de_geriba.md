  Geribá has been a surf destination since Brazilian surf culture began developing in the 1970s. The beach hosted professional surf competitions for years, and the wave break at the western end of the beach — known simply as "O Canto" — is still regarded as one of the best point breaks in the state of Rio de Janeiro.
---

Geribá is the big open-ocean beach, facing the Atlantic directly and catching swells that the sheltered eastern beaches never see. It is the longest beach on the peninsula, stretching over two kilometres of open sand with consistent waves that make it a reliable surf destination.

Non-surfers have plenty of reason to come here too. The beach is wide enough that the surf crowd concentrates at the western end while the rest of the strand stays relatively calm. There are kiosks and beach bars spaced along the waterfront, and the setting — open sea, a slight breeze off the water, the peninsula curling away to the east — is genuinely beautiful in the late afternoon light.
