Rua das Pedras — literally "Street of Stones" — is the cobblestone spine of Vila de Búzios, and the closest thing the town has to a main street. It runs for about 400 metres through the old fishing village turned resort, lined on both sides with restaurants, boutiques, jewellers selling aquamarines, and the kind of artisan shops that sell locally made things alongside the things that aren't. During the day it's relaxed enough to wander; after 9 pm in high season it's genuinely lively.

The street's cobblestones are genuine — they predate the resort era — and the scale is human. You can walk the length of it in ten minutes, though stopping to look at things makes that considerably longer. The quality of goods for sale is higher than most Brazilian beach resorts; local designers have shops here alongside the souvenir stands.

Rua das Pedras connects the Orla Bardot waterfront at one end to the hills behind town at the other. Most of the good restaurants and bars cluster along its length or on the short side streets running off it.
