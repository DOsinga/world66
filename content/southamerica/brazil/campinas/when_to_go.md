---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

Campinas has a tropical highland climate — warm and humid in summer, noticeably drier and cooler in winter, but never cold. Its elevation (about 680 metres above sea level) keeps it a few degrees cooler than São Paulo city and considerably more bearable than coastal Brazil in the heat of the year.

**November to March** is the rainy season. Afternoons frequently bring heavy downpours, and December and January can be relentlessly wet. Temperatures reach 30–32°C. This is also the period when the city is at its most lively — Carnival falls in February, and the Festa de Nossa Senhora da Conceição in December draws large crowds.

**April to October** is the dry season and the more comfortable time to visit. May through September brings cool, clear days and evenings that drop to 15–18°C — warm enough for a light jacket in the evenings but pleasant for walking. July is the coolest month; frost is not unknown, though it is rare at this altitude.

**June** is worth timing a trip around if you are interested in the Festa Junina celebrations. Campinas does them well: the forró music, quadrilha dancing, traditional food stalls selling canjica, quentão (warm spiced cachaça), and corn-based sweets create a particular atmosphere that feels genuinely local rather than tourist-facing. Street parties run through the month in different neighbourhoods.

For practical purposes, May to August is the sweet spot: good weather, no flooding, and the city going about its normal business.
