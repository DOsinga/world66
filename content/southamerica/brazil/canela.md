Canela sits just eight kilometres from Gramado in the Serra Gaúcha highlands, and while it is usually mentioned in the same breath as its more famous neighbour, it has a character of its own. Canela is quieter and less polished, and more rooted in the forested landscape that surrounds it. Where Gramado trades on chocolate shops and Bavarian half-timbering, Canela earns its reputation through natural scenery.

The main reason to come is the Cascata do Caracol, a 131-metre single-drop waterfall that falls in one unbroken column from the basalt rim of the plateau into a forested gorge below. The waterfall is inside the Parque Estadual do Caracol, a short drive from town, where you can stand on a wooden viewing platform at the top for the vertiginous view or descend by glass elevator and iron staircase to a lookout near the base. The surrounding park has araucária forest trails and good birdwatching; the umbrella-shaped Paraná pines are an endangered species and this is one of the finer stands remaining in Rio Grande do Sul.

The town centre has a pleasant main square anchored by the Catedral de Pedra, a stone Gothic-revival church built in stages through the 20th century. The stone was quarried locally and gives the cathedral a rougher, more honest look than the German-style architecture across the hill in Gramado. There are several good pousadas and restaurants, serving the Serra Gaúcha mix of fondue, Italian pasta, and locally produced cheese.

From Canela you can drive to the [Parque da Ferradura](/southamerica/brazil/parque_da_ferradura) and its canyon viewpoints, or continue east towards the [Aparados da Serra](/southamerica/brazil/aparados_da_serra) national parks. The town makes a quieter alternative base to Gramado if you want to spend more time in the forests and spend less time in chocolate shops.
