The Centro-Oeste is Brazil's interior: Goiás, Mato Grosso and Mato Grosso do Sul, with the capital, [Brasília](/southamerica/brazil/brasilia), planted in the middle of it in 1960. It is cattle and soya country, flat and enormous, but it holds three of Brazil's finest natural areas.

The [Pantanal](/southamerica/brazil/centro_oeste/pantanal) is the largest tropical wetland in the world, and the best place in the Americas to see wildlife: jaguars on the riverbanks, giant otters, capybaras, caimans by the thousand and hyacinth macaws. The northern Pantanal is reached from [Cuiabá](/southamerica/brazil/centro_oeste/cuiaba) along the Transpantaneira; the southern from [Campo Grande](/southamerica/brazil/centro_oeste/campo_grande) and [Corumbá](/southamerica/brazil/centro_oeste/corumba).

South of it, [Bonito](/southamerica/brazil/centro_oeste/bonito) has rivers so clear that floating down them with a snorkel is like looking into an aquarium; [Nobres](/southamerica/brazil/centro_oeste/nobres) is its quieter northern equivalent. Above Cuiabá, the red cliffs and waterfalls of the [Chapada dos Guimarães](/southamerica/brazil/centro_oeste/chapada_dos_guimaraes) rise from the plains.

In Goiás, the [Chapada dos Veadeiros](/southamerica/brazil/centro_oeste/chapada_dos_veadeiros), reached from [Alto Paraíso de Goiás](/southamerica/brazil/centro_oeste/alto_paraiso) and [Cavalcante](/southamerica/brazil/centro_oeste/cavalcante), is a high plateau of canyons and waterfalls in the cerrado savanna, and the colonial towns of [Pirenópolis](/southamerica/brazil/centro_oeste/pirenopolis) and [Goiás Velho](/southamerica/brazil/centro_oeste/goias) keep the feel of the gold-rush eighteenth century.

The dry season, May to September, is the time for almost everything here — the Pantanal's wildlife gathers around the shrinking water, and the Chapada trails are at their best.
