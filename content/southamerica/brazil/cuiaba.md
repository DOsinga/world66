Cuiabá is the capital of Mato Grosso and the main gateway to the northern Pantanal, the vast wetland that is one of the best places in South America to see wildlife. The city itself is hot — among the hottest in Brazil — and sprawling, but it has a certain rough-edged energy. The riverside area and the old centre around the Praça da República have been partly revitalised, and the [Museu Rondon](/southamerica/brazil/cuiaba/museu_rondon) at the university campus has a good collection of indigenous artefacts.

Most visitors pass through Cuiabá on the way to the Pantanal lodges along the Transpantaneira highway, which begins at Poconé about an hour south. The wildlife viewing along this raised dirt road — capybaras, caimans, jabiru storks, howler monkeys, and with luck a jaguar — is extraordinary. Cuiabá is also the starting point for trips to Chapada dos Guimarães, a sandstone plateau with dramatic cliffs, red rock formations, and waterfalls about 65 km to the northeast.

The city holds an unexpected distinction: a pyramid monument in the Coxipó da Ponte district marks what is officially designated as the [Geodetic Centre of South America](/southamerica/brazil/cuiaba/geodetic_centre) — the mathematical heart of the continent. The [Cathedral Basilica of Bom Jesus](/southamerica/brazil/cuiaba/catedral_bom_jesus) and the 18th-century [Igreja de Nossa Senhora do Rosário](/southamerica/brazil/cuiaba/igreja_nossa_senhora_rosario) give the old centre an architectural character that makes the inevitable transit stop more interesting than you might expect. The [Mãe Bonifácia State Park](/southamerica/brazil/cuiaba/mae_bonifacia_park) provides an accessible slice of cerrado forest within the city limits.
