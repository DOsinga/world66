Curitiba is the capital of Paraná state and one of Brazil's best-planned cities. While it lacks the drama of Rio or the energy of São Paulo, it is a genuinely pleasant place — clean, green, well-organised, and full of parks. The innovative bus rapid transit system, designed in the 1970s, became a model copied by cities worldwide.

The city has a strong European immigrant heritage, particularly Italian, German, Polish, Ukrainian, and Japanese. This shows in the neighbourhoods: [Santa Felicidade](/southamerica/brazil/curitiba/santa_felicidade) is the Italian quarter, with long-table restaurants serving enormous portions of polenta and pasta. The [Bosque Alemão](/southamerica/brazil/curitiba/bosque_alemao) and the [Ukrainian memorial in Parque Tingui](/southamerica/brazil/curitiba/parque_tingui) reflect the city's multicultural roots. The neighbourhood of [Batel](/southamerica/brazil/curitiba/batel) is the most cosmopolitan part of the city, with the best concentration of restaurants and bars.

The [Oscar Niemeyer Museum](/southamerica/brazil/curitiba/oscar_niemeyer_museum), housed in a striking eye-shaped building designed by Niemeyer himself at age 94, is the city's best museum. The [Botanical Garden](/southamerica/brazil/curitiba/botanical_garden), with its Art Nouveau-style greenhouse, is the most-photographed spot. The historic centre around [Largo da Ordem](/southamerica/brazil/curitiba/largo_da_ordem) hosts a lively Sunday market. The [Ópera de Arame](/southamerica/brazil/curitiba/opera_de_arame) and [Teatro Guaíra](/southamerica/brazil/curitiba/teatro_guaira) are the city's main performing arts venues.

Curitiba also serves as a base for one of Brazil's great train journeys — the Serra Verde Express to Paranaguá, which descends through Atlantic Forest and mountainous terrain to the coast. The scenery is spectacular, and [Guaratuba](/southamerica/brazil/guratuba) and Ilha do Mel are easily reached from Paranaguá.
