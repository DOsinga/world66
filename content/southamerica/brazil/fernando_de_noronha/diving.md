Fernando de Noronha Marine National Park is one of the best diving destinations in the South Atlantic. Water temperatures hover around 28°C year-round, visibility regularly reaches 40 metres or more, and the diversity of life is extraordinary: reef sharks, hawksbill turtles, eagle rays, moray eels, and an abundance of reef fish in conditions that mainland Brazil simply cannot match.

Several dive operators work on the island, offering everything from introductory dives for beginners to technical dives at deeper wrecks and caves. The Cabeço dive site, a submerged rock pinnacle, is famous for encounters with white-tipped reef sharks. For non-divers, snorkelling in the shallower bays — particularly at Praia do Cachorro and Baía do Sueste — provides access to turtles and rays without any equipment beyond a mask.
