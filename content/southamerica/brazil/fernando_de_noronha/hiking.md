Fernando de Noronha is small enough that you can hike across most of it in a morning, and the coastal paths are genuinely excellent. The trail along the northern shore — the windward side — passes through dry Atlantic forest and along clifftops with views down to beaches that are otherwise inaccessible. The contrast between the wild, wave-pounded northern coast and the calm, sheltered southern bays is dramatic.

The most popular hike runs from the main village toward Baía do Sancho, passing through the marine park and descending via the cliff crack. Various shorter walks connect the hilltop viewpoints and the archaeological sites left by the island's former prison population. Guides are recommended for anything beyond the main paths, as some trails are unmarked.
