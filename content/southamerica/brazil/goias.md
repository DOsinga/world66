Goiás, often called the City of Goiás, is the former capital of Goiás state and one of central Brazil's best preserved colonial towns. It began as Vila Boa during the eighteenth-century gold rush, when bandeirantes pushed into the interior and built churches, administrative houses, and narrow streets along the Red River.

The historic centre is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site. It is smaller and quieter than [Goiânia](/southamerica/brazil/goiania), the planned capital that replaced it in the 1930s, but that is exactly the appeal. The old town still feels like a place shaped by gold, Catholic processions, red-tiled roofs, and the hot, dry edge of the cerrado.

Most visits are simple: walk the historic centre, stop at the [Bandeiras Museum](/southamerica/brazil/goias/bandeiras_museum), visit the [Cora Coralina House](/southamerica/brazil/goias/cora_coralina_house), and sit out the fiercest afternoon heat somewhere shaded. Goiás works as a long day trip from Goiânia, but an overnight stay gives the town its best hours.
