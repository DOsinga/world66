The neighbouring town of [Canela](/southamerica/brazil/canela), ten minutes' drive away, is effectively part of the Gramado experience and is where the Cascata do Caracol, [Parque da Ferradura](/southamerica/brazil/parque_da_ferradura), and most of the canyon viewpoints sit. The Ferradura is a horseshoe-shaped canyon more than 400 metres deep — the rim viewpoint gives you one of the best natural panoramas in the Serra Gaúcha, and a trail descends inside to a small waterfall and pool. Canela itself has its own character: quieter, more forested, with the fine stone Catedral de Pedra in the main square.

Beyond Canela, the wider Serra Gaúcha is full of vineyards, cheese producers, and dramatic canyons. [Bento Gonçalves](/southamerica/brazil/bento_goncalves) and the Vale dos Vinhedos wine valley lie about 70 kilometres to the south-west, with cellar doors, rural restaurants, and Brazil's first DOC wine appellation. A half-day drive through the vineyard roads, with a proper tasting lunch, makes an excellent counterpoint to Gramado's mountain scenery.

The [Aparados da Serra](/southamerica/brazil/aparados_da_serra) National Park, around three hours east, contains the spectacular Itaimbezinho canyon — a 700-metre-deep gorge that is one of the most impressive in Brazil. It is a long day from Gramado but entirely doable; many visitors prefer to stay overnight in the village of [Cambará do Sul](/southamerica/brazil/cambara_do_sul) to have the parks to themselves in the morning and to combine Itaimbezinho with the Fortaleza canyon in the adjacent Serra Geral National Park.
