Ilha Grande is a 193 km² island of Atlantic Forest, granite peaks, and unreasonably beautiful beaches, set in the great bay of [Angra dos Reis](/southamerica/brazil/angradosreis) on Brazil's Green Coast. There are no roads on the island, no cars, no banks, and almost no street lighting; visitors get around on foot, by boat, or — in the village — by handcart. It is one of those rare destinations that arrived in the twenty-first century as a working ecological reserve rather than as a resort, and the result is a place where the rainforest comes right down to the sand.

Most boats land at [Vila do Abraão](/southamerica/brazil/ilhagrande/vila_do_abraao), a loose grid of pousadas, restaurants, dive shops, and small bars facing a horseshoe bay on the north coast. Abraão is the only real settlement and the obvious base. From here, trails fan out across the steep, forested interior — past the [ruins of the old prison](/southamerica/brazil/ilhagrande/old_prison) and the [aqueduct](/southamerica/brazil/ilhagrande/aqueduct), up to the granite summit of [Pico do Papagaio](/southamerica/brazil/ilhagrande/pico_do_papagaio), or all the way across the island to [Lopes Mendes](/southamerica/brazil/ilhagrande/lopes_mendes), which regularly turns up on lists of the world's best beaches.

The island's strange history is part of what kept it wild. For more than a century it housed a leper colony and then a notorious maximum-security prison (the Cândido Mendes penitentiary, blown up in 1994); it also held a yellow-fever quarantine station. Development was forbidden throughout, and when the prison closed the whole island was designated a state park. The forest is now home to howler monkeys, caimans, sea turtles, and, on the southern beaches, a thriving population of nesting birds.

Practical notes: bring cash. There is one slow ATM in Abraão and it is often broken. Boats run to Abraão from Angra dos Reis, Conceição de Jacareí, and Mangaratiba on the mainland, several times a day. Stay at least two nights — three or four is better. The water is clear, warm, and full of fish; bring a snorkel mask if you have one.
