The state park covers most of the island's interior — about 152 square kilometres of Atlantic Forest, granite peaks, and headwater streams. It is the reason Ilha Grande still looks the way it does. Howler monkeys are the most audible inhabitants, especially at dawn, and the park is also home to capuchin monkeys, agoutis, and a long list of birds you are more likely to hear than see.

There is no central park entrance; trails simply run out of Abraão into the protected zone. The state park office in Abraão has free maps and can advise on trail conditions, which change quickly in the rainy season (November to March, roughly). Some trails require a guide; others are open to all visitors who register at the trailhead.
