Lauro Müller is a small city in the coal-mining heartland of southern Santa Catarina, sitting at the foot of the Serra Geral escarpment. The city exists because of coal — the British started mining here in 1842, making this one of Brazil's earliest industrial sites — and the landscape exists because of geology: the escarpment drops dramatically away from the high plateau of Santa Catarina to the coastal lowlands below.

The [Serra do Rio do Rastro](/southamerica/brazil/lauro_mller/serra_do_rio_do_rastro) is the main reason to come here. The road climbs the escarpment in a series of tight switchbacks cut directly into the rock face, threading through dense Atlantic Forest with waterfalls dropping alongside it — genuinely spectacular, and one of the great mountain drives in South America.

Back in town, the [Castelo de Laje](/southamerica/brazil/lauro_mller/castelo_de_laje) is the most distinctive building — a building modelled on a Swiss castle, constructed between 1917 and 1919 by industrialist Henrique Laje. Near the base of the serra, the [Grotto of the Amaral](/southamerica/brazil/lauro_mller/grotto_amaral) is accessible via the old road toward Orleans.

The city marks several festivals through the year, of which the Festa de Santa Bárbara in December — patron saint of miners, celebrated for over 65 years — is the most distinctive. Restaurants in town tend toward Italian food, reflecting the area's immigrant heritage from the late 19th century.
