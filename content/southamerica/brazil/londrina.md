Londrina is northern Paraná's largest city, a planned agricultural boomtown that grew with extraordinary speed from a patch of Atlantic forest. It was founded in 1929 by a British land company — the name means "Little London" in Portuguese — which sold parcels of the fertile red soil to thousands of European and Japanese immigrants who came to grow coffee. For several decades in the mid-20th century it was one of the fastest-growing cities in South America.

The coffee era is over, replaced by soy and wheat, but the city has retained its prosperity and its cosmopolitan character. Londrina has a large Japanese-Brazilian community — one of the largest outside São Paulo — and the result is a restaurant scene that is genuinely varied, with good sushi and Japanese cooking alongside the inevitable rodízio churrasco and Italian pizza. The city is also a university town, with the [State University of Londrina](/southamerica/brazil/londrina/state_university) giving it a student energy that keeps the bar and live music scene alive.

Sights are modest but worth a half-day: the [Museu Histórico de Londrina](/southamerica/brazil/londrina/museu_historico) in the old train station, the substantial [Cathedral](/southamerica/brazil/londrina/cathedral), and the Lago Igapó parks in the city centre. The [Estádio do Café](/southamerica/brazil/londrina/estadio_do_cafe) — the coffee stadium, naturally — hosts football and music events and is a landmark of the city's self-image.
