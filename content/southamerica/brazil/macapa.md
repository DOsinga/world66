Macapá is the capital of Amapá, on the north bank of the Amazon River near the mouth of the great estuary. It is one of Brazil's more unusual state capitals: cut by the Equator, hard to reach by road from the rest of the country, and turned toward the river as much as toward the Brazilian interior.

The city has enough for a strong day if you are already in the far north. The [Fortaleza de São José](/southamerica/brazil/macapa/fortaleza_de_sao_jose) is the serious sight, a low 18th-century star fort built to guard Portuguese claims at the Amazon mouth. The [Marco Zero](/southamerica/brazil/macapa/marco_zero) is more playful: a monument and stadium complex where the Equator line is the attraction.

Macapá is not polished. The heat is heavy, the distances are awkward, and the Amazon here is a broad brown horizon rather than a jungle fantasy. But that is also the point. It feels like a real frontier capital, with riverfront walks, local museums, and a geography lesson made visible in the street grid.
