Lençóis is the main gateway town for Chapada Diamantina National Park, a well-preserved 19th-century diamond-mining settlement with cobbled streets, pastel-coloured colonial houses, and a comfortable tourist infrastructure that somehow manages not to feel overbuilt. The town sits in a valley at 400 metres, with rivers running through the lower streets and the flat-topped plateau visible in every direction.

The historic centre is attractive and genuinely preserved rather than reconstructed — many of the 19th-century townhouses are still lived in by families whose roots in the town go back to the mining era. A market runs on weekends in the main square, the Mercado Cultural hosts local music, and the restaurants and pousadas (guesthouses) serve well. The rivers running through town — the Lençóis and Ribeirão de Lençóis — have swimming holes a short walk from the centre, and the trailheads for park hikes begin at the edge of town.
