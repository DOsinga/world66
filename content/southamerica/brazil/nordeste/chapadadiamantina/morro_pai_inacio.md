Morro do Pai Inácio is the most iconic viewpoint in Chapada Diamantina and probably the single most photographed view in the park: a flat-topped quartzite mesa that rises above the surrounding plateau, looking out over a vast landscape of tabletop hills, river valleys, and distant escarpments. The climb from the park entrance takes about 45 minutes on a well-marked trail and requires no technical equipment; the summit is accessible to anyone with reasonable fitness.

At the top, the 360-degree view takes in the defining geography of the Chapada — the endless plateau landscape interrupted by the mesa forms that give the region its character, with the Sincorá Range to the south and the great valley of the Paraguaçu River stretching westward. Sunset is the classic time to be on the summit, when the light turns the quartzite walls orange and the valley fills with shadow below. The trail is accessible year-round but can be slippery after rain. No guide is strictly required for Morro do Pai Inácio, though hiring one enriches the visit with context on the geology and local history.
