---
title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

Itacaré punches well above its weight for food. The local specialities are Bahian: **fish moqueca** in a clay pot with coconut milk and dendê palm oil; **bobó de camarão** (shrimp in a manioc and coconut purée); **acarajé** (deep-fried black-eyed pea fritters filled with shrimp and vatapá), sold from street stalls in the early evening.

Several international restaurants — owned by transplants from Rio, São Paulo, France, and Italy — line Rua Pedro Longo and the back streets. Expect to pay R$80-150 per head for a proper sit-down dinner with drinks. For something cheaper, the **per-kilo** lunches a block back from Pedro Longo are excellent value, with grilled fish, beans, salads, and fried plantain on rotation. End the evening with a caipirinha at one of the beachfront kiosks.
