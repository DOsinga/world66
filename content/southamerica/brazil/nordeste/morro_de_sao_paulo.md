Morro de São Paulo is a car-free village on the island of Tinharé, south of [Salvador](/southamerica/brazil/nordeste/salvador), and the most popular beach escape from the city. It is reached by catamaran from Salvador in about two and a half hours — the crossing can be rough — or by a shorter combination of ferry, road and boat via Valença.

There are no cars, so luggage goes up the hill from the jetty in wheelbarrows. The village is a steep tangle of pousadas, bars and shops, and below it the beaches are simply numbered: Primeira Praia is small and built up; Segunda Praia is the party beach, with bars and music late into the night; Terceira and Quarta Praia are quieter, with reefs that form natural pools at low tide, and Quarta runs on for kilometres of empty sand.

The ruins of a seventeenth-century fort guard the point, and a zip line runs from the lighthouse down to the sea. Boat trips go around the island, and to [Boipeba](/southamerica/brazil/nordeste/boipeba), its quieter neighbour to the south.

Morro is busy in summer and at New Year, and very much a party place on Segunda Praia. Out of season, or down at Quarta Praia, it is much gentler.
