The Olinda Regional Museum sits in an eighteenth-century house on Rua do Amparo, one of the streets where the old city still feels lived-in rather than staged. Its rooms preserve furniture, religious pieces, decorative objects, and domestic material connected to Pernambuco’s colonial and imperial layers.

It is a good museum because it does not pull you out of Olinda’s historic fabric. You move from churches and steep lanes into a house that explains how status, religion, and everyday life looked indoors, then step back into the same UNESCO-listed streets with a little more context.
