**The Masters and the Slaves** by Gilberto Freyre — not specifically about São Luís, but an essential work for understanding how Brazilian culture emerged from the intersection of Portuguese, African, and indigenous traditions. Much of what makes Maranhão distinctive — its food, its music, its social structures — traces back to the dynamics Freyre describes.

**The Posthumous Memoirs of Brás Cubas** by Machado de Assis — Brazil's greatest novelist, and this darkly comic masterpiece is the best introduction to 19th-century Brazilian society, which was the world that made São Luís wealthy. The narrator is dead from the first page, and it only gets better from there.

**City of God** by Paulo Lins — not set in São Luís but in the Rio favelas of the 1970s and 1980s. Read before or during a Brazil trip as a corrective to the tourist brochure version of the country. The connections between poverty, crime, and race that Lins describes are as relevant to Maranhão as to Rio.
