The Norte is the Amazon. It covers nearly half of Brazil's territory — Amazonas, Pará, Amapá, Rondônia, Roraima, Acre and Tocantins — and holds under a tenth of its people, most of them in a handful of river cities. Roads are few; the rivers are the highways, and many trips here involve a boat.

[Manaus](/southamerica/brazil/norte/manaus) is where most visitors start: a city of two million in the middle of the forest, with a rubber-boom opera house and the Meeting of the Waters, where the black Rio Negro and the muddy Solimões run side by side without mixing. Upstream, the [Anavilhanas](/southamerica/brazil/norte/anavilhanas) archipelago and the remote [Jaú National Park](/southamerica/brazil/norte/jau_national_park) are the Rio Negro at its wildest; far to the west, [Mamirauá](/southamerica/brazil/norte/mamiraua) is the best place in Brazil to see the Amazon's wildlife in flooded forest. [Presidente Figueiredo](/southamerica/brazil/norte/presidente_figueiredo) is waterfall country two hours north.

Downriver, [Belém](/southamerica/brazil/norte/belem), at the mouth, has the best food market in Brazil and the ferry to [Ilha de Marajó](/southamerica/brazil/norte/marajo), an island of buffalo and mangrove. Halfway between the two cities, [Santarém](/southamerica/brazil/norte/santarem) is the jumping-off point for [Alter do Chão](/southamerica/brazil/norte/alter_do_chao), whose river beaches are the region's great surprise.

Away from the forest, [Jalapão](/southamerica/brazil/norte/jalapao) in Tocantins is a savanna of orange dunes and spring pools, and [Macapá](/southamerica/brazil/norte/macapa) sits on the equator at the mouth of the Amazon.

The rainy season, roughly December to May, raises the rivers by many metres and floods the forest; the dry months uncover the beaches. Neither is the wrong time — they are different trips.
