The Madeira-Mamoré Railway Complex is Porto Velho's defining historic site. The railway was built to bypass dangerous rapids on the Madeira and Mamoré rivers, linking Brazil's new Acre territory and Bolivian trade routes to the Amazon system.

The surviving station area and railway remains can feel neglected, but the story is powerful. It explains why Porto Velho exists at all, and why the city still feels like a place built around movement, cargo, and the river.
