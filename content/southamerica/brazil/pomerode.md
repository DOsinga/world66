Pomerode is a small city in the Itajaí Valley of Santa Catarina state, 32 km north of Blumenau, founded by German immigrants from Pomerania in the 19th century. It has the distinction of being the city with the highest percentage of German speakers in Brazil — around 70% of the population still use the language or its dialects in daily life. Walking around the town you'll see half-timbered (enxaimel) buildings that would not look out of place in rural Germany.

The [Pomerode Zoological Garden](/southamerica/brazil/pomerode/pomerode_zoo), inaugurated in 1932, is one of the oldest zoos in Brazil and sits right in the centre of town, housing over 600 animals across more than 120 species. The [Museu Pomerano](/southamerica/brazil/pomerode/museu_pomerano) covers the history of German immigration and regional culture, and the [Carl Weege Immigrant House Museum](/southamerica/brazil/pomerode/carl_weege_house) preserves the intact home of one of the town's first settlers. Walking the [enxaimel architecture trail](/southamerica/brazil/pomerode/enxaimel_architecture) through the old centre is the most rewarding way to spend a morning here.

The best time to visit is during the Festa Pomerana in January, a popular cultural festival with traditional German brass band music, folk dancing in regional dress, and plenty of local food — including the Brezel (a salted pretzel), Strudel, and poppy seed rolls. The town also has 16 active hunting and shooting clubs in the German tradition, and several choral groups that perform regularly.
