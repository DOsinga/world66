---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

Ponta Grossa sits at about 900 metres above sea level on the Serra Geral plateau, which gives it a cooler and more temperate climate than the Brazilian coast. Summers (December to February) are warm and wet, with afternoon thunderstorms common. Winters (June to August) are mild and dry — occasionally cold enough for frost at night — and the clear skies make for ideal conditions at the natural parks.

The dry season from April to September is the best time to visit for hiking and canyon exploration. Rain can make the trails at Buraco do Padre slippery and the canyon floor at Vila Velha muddy, so plan outdoor excursions for dry-weather windows if possible. The city has no major festival that would affect accommodation prices or availability.
