The Basílica Menor Nossa Senhora das Dores — Our Lady of Sorrows — is the oldest church in Porto Alegre still in its original location, with construction beginning in 1807 and the building reaching essentially its current form by the mid-19th century. It is a handsome colonial structure with a white facade and twin towers, sitting on a slight rise a few streets south of Praça da Matriz. Compared to the grand metropolitan cathedral nearby, it is smaller and more intimate, and its colonial character is better preserved.

The interior is the main reason to visit: it retains original 18th- and 19th-century woodwork, gilded altarpieces, and azulejo tile work that give a much clearer picture of what Porto Alegre's religious architecture looked like before the city grew into a metropolis. Several important 19th-century Porto Alegre figures are buried in the crypt. The church is an active parish and holds regular Masses; visiting between services is generally possible. It sits on a quiet street that has changed relatively little since the 19th century — if you stand in front of the church and look away from the modern buildings, you get a reasonable impression of what this corner of the colonial city looked like.
