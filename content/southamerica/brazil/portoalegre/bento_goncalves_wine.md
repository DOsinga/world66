Bento Gonçalves, about 120 kilometres north of Porto Alegre, is the capital of Brazilian wine — a declaration that would have been laughable thirty years ago and is now defensible with a straight face. The **Vale dos Vinhedos** wine region around the town holds Brazil's first and only DOC appellation, awarded in 2012 for wines made from Merlot, Cabernet Franc, and Chardonnay grapes grown in the hills settled by Italian immigrants in the 19th century. The wines are not yet internationally famous but they have improved enormously and several estates — **Miolo**, **Cave de Amadeu**, **Pizzato** — now produce genuinely good bottles.

Getting there from Porto Alegre is straightforward by bus (about two hours) or much more pleasant by car, which lets you stop at viewpoints and smaller estates along the way. Most wineries offer tastings and cellar tours; some require reservations and some do not. The gastronomic tradition in the Vale dos Vinhedos reflects the Italian heritage: excellent pasta, cured meats, and the local bread called **pão colonial** that you will find at every farm. A tourist steam train called the **Maria Fumaça** runs through the vineyards between Bento Gonçalves and Carlos Barbosa on certain days and is a charming if slow way to see the landscape. Combine with a night in Bento Gonçalves itself — a pleasant if unremarkable town — if you want to visit multiple estates without rushing.
