The Cais Mauá is Porto Alegre's waterfront precinct, a long strip of converted early 20th-century port warehouses along the Guaíba shore north of the Usina do Gasômetro. The revitalisation project has been controversial — some of the historic warehouses were demolished, the commercial mix is uneven, and the whole thing felt underused for years after opening. But the waterfront promenade itself is undeniably good: a wide esplanade facing the Guaíba, with views across the water, a constant breeze, and enough space to feel genuinely open in a city that is otherwise fairly dense.

The best times to visit are weekend afternoons and evenings, when the warehouses host food stalls, craft markets, and occasional concerts, and the promenade fills with locals walking and cycling. Several of the converted warehouses contain bars, restaurants, and event spaces; the quality varies but there are good options. The floating restaurants moored at the wharf are a Porto Alegre tradition. Look for the **Marco Zero** — the symbolic origin point of the city's street grid — at the southern end of the wharf. From the promenade you can book boat trips onto the Guaíba for sunset, which is the best possible way to understand the scale of the water and why the city is built the way it is.
