title: Canela and Parque do Caracol

Canela sits just a few kilometres from Gramado in the Serra Gaúcha and makes an excellent companion destination: slightly less touristic than its famous neighbour but with better natural scenery. The main attraction is the **Parque Estadual do Caracol**, a few kilometres from the town centre, where a 131-metre waterfall drops through Atlantic Forest in one of the most impressive natural sights in southern Brazil. The access involves either a steep staircase descent (400-odd steps) or a cable car, both of which give you different and equally good perspectives of the falls.

The park also has well-maintained hiking trails through the araucária (Paraná pine) forest — the distinctive umbrella-shaped trees that cover the Serra Gaúcha hills and are one of the defining images of Rio Grande do Sul. The araucária are an endangered species; the park protects a good stand of them. Canela town itself has a pretty main square with a large Flamboyant tree and a Gothic-style Catedral de Pedra (Stone Cathedral) that was built in stages through the 20th century and is genuinely handsome. The town has several good pousadas if you want to overnight and explore the surrounding countryside properly. Buses from Porto Alegre run direct to Canela via Gramado.
