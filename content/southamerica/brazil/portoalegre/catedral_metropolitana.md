The Metropolitan Cathedral of Porto Alegre dominates the eastern end of Praça da Matriz with its pair of towers and heavy Romanesque-Byzantine facade. It is a large, serious building — grey stone, substantial proportions, none of the florid decoration of Brazil's baroque churches — and it suits the northern European character of the city rather well. Construction stretched across most of the 20th century, with work proceeding in fits and starts depending on the diocese's finances. The interior is spacious and cool, with good stained glass and an unexpectedly moving quality in the late afternoon when the light comes through the west windows.

The cathedral is an active parish church and open most of the day for visitors. It is less visually spectacular than the baroque gems of Ouro Preto or Salvador, but it has a dignified presence that grows on you. The steps outside are a popular meeting place, and from here you get one of the best views of the Praça da Matriz — the Teatro São Pedro across the square, the Palácio Piratini behind you, and the old city spreading away toward the waterfront. Worth fifteen minutes of your time even if cathedral architecture is not your primary interest.
