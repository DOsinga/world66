The Chalé da Praça XV is a small Victorian iron pavilion in the Praça XV de Novembro — a delicate green-painted structure that looks like it was airlifted from a Parisian park, which is not too far from the truth: the ironwork was manufactured in Europe and assembled here in 1885. For most of its life it has operated as a bar and café, and today it remains one of the most atmospheric places in the city centre to have a cold beer on a warm afternoon.

The setting is lovely: the pavilion sits in a shaded square surrounded by old buildings, away from the main commercial traffic of Rua da Praia, and the combination of the Victorian ironwork, the mature trees, and the outdoor tables gives it a quality that Porto Alegre's newer bars rarely match. The menu is simple — beer, caipirinhas, simple snacks — but nobody really comes for the food. They come for the place: a piece of 19th-century urban life that has survived into the present without being over-restored or turned into something it is not. On Friday afternoons in good weather the Praça XV comes alive with an after-work crowd and the Chalé is at its best.
