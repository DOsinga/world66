Cidade Baixa — the Lower City — is Porto Alegre's most energetic neighbourhood, a slightly scruffy grid of streets that has been the city's bohemian heart for decades. In the 19th century it was a working-class district of small houses and corner bars; it still is, but now those houses hold tattoo parlours, independent record shops, vintage clothing stores, and some of the best cheap restaurants in the city. The streets around Rua da República and Rua João Alfredo become one long outdoor party from Thursday through Saturday, with bar tables spread across the pavements and music drifting out of every door.

The neighbourhood's diversity is one of its strengths. Students from the nearby UFRGS faculties, the city's LGBTQ+ community, artists, and working families all share the same blocks in a way that feels natural rather than manufactured. The **Opinião** venue on Rua José do Patrocínio hosts the city's best live music — MPB legends and upcoming bands alike play here. During the day the neighbourhood is quieter but worth exploring: the **Museu de Porto Alegre Joaquim Felizardo**, a local history museum in a handsome old building, is a good introduction to the city's past. Come back at night and stay late.
