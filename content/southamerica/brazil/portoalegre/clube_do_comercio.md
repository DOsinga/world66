The Clube do Comércio is one of the most ornate buildings in Porto Alegre's Centro Histórico: a mid-19th-century social club building whose eclectic facade — a mixture of neoclassical and baroque elements, heavily decorated with pilasters and mouldings — stands out dramatically among the more restrained commercial buildings around it. The club was founded in 1858 by Porto Alegre's merchant class, who needed a proper venue for their dinners, dances, and social dealings; the building was completed a few years later and has been serving the same function, with modifications, ever since.

The club is private and not generally open to non-members, but the building can be admired from the street and is included in most walking tours of the Centro Histórico. It represents the confidence of Porto Alegre's 19th-century commercial class — the importers, exporters, and merchants who grew wealthy on trade up and down the Guaíba and through the port — and the strong European aesthetic sensibility they brought to everything they built. The facade is best seen in the morning light. The street level has some good commercial buildings from the same period; this block, between the Praça da Alfândega and the Praça da Matriz, contains some of the densest concentration of fine 19th-century architecture in the city.
