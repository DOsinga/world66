The Estádio Beira-Rio sits on the southern shore of the Rio Guaíba and is one of the most beautifully situated football grounds in Brazil. Sport Club Internacional — Internacional, or simply "Inter" — plays here, and the relationship between the club and the city is intense in a way that non-football cities find difficult to understand. Porto Alegre is divided between Inter and their crosstown rival Grêmio; every person in the city has a team, and discussions about which is superior can last hours.

Going to a match is strongly recommended if your timing allows. The stadium holds 50,000 people and a sold-out game — particularly a Grenal, the local derby — is one of the great Brazilian football experiences: the noise, the smoke from flares, the vertical choreography of the crowd. Even without a Grenal, a normal league match has a passionate atmosphere. Tickets are available online through the club's website; getting there is easiest by Uber. The stadium also offers museum visits and stadium tours on non-match days, which give you access to the pitch level and the trophy room. International's trophy cabinet includes a Copa Libertadores and a FIFA Club World Cup, which makes it more interesting than most.
