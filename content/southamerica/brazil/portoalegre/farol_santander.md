The Farol Santander — Santander Lighthouse — is what happens when a major bank buys one of the city's most important historic buildings and actually does something interesting with it. The 1932 Art Deco skyscraper on Rua Sete de Setembro was Porto Alegre's first genuine skyscraper and for a brief period the tallest building in Brazil. Santander acquired it, restored the facades and banking halls to their original splendour, and opened the building as a cultural centre in 2017. The result is one of the best adaptive reuse projects in southern Brazil.

The banking hall on the ground floor — all marble columns, coffered ceilings, and ornate brass fittings — is used for rotating art exhibitions and is spectacular on its own terms. The upper floors host galleries, cinema, a café, and a restaurant with a terrace. But the real reason to come is the **mirante** on the roof: the 360-degree view over Porto Alegre and the Rio Guaíba is the best in the city, especially in the late afternoon when the water turns gold and the Serra Gaúcha hills are visible on clear days. There is an admission charge for the upper floors but it is modest and the view is worth it. The gift shop sells better design objects than almost anywhere else in the city.
