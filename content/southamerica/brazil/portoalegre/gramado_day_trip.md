Gramado is one of the most visited destinations in Brazil, which comes as a surprise to travellers who arrive expecting something more authentically Brazilian. The town — 127 kilometres north of Porto Alegre in the Serra Gaúcha hills — has been playing at being a Swiss-German alpine village for so long that the performance has acquired a kind of sincerity. The streets are lined with chalet-style buildings, the hydrangeas are everywhere from October through April (the town plants them in extraordinary quantities), the chocolate shops are excellent, and the temperature is reliably cool even in Brazilian summer. It is not subtle, but it is genuinely pleasant.

The bus from Porto Alegre's rodoviária takes about two and a half hours and runs frequently. The town itself is easily walkable: the main avenue, Avenida Borges de Medeiros, has the chocolate shops, restaurants, and cafés that most visitors come for. The best reason to come, beyond the chocolate, is the scenery: the surrounding Serra Gaúcha landscape — deep green valleys, waterfalls, small farms — is beautiful, especially from October to January when the hydrangeas are blooming. The **Parque do Caracol** (the 131-metre waterfall is nearby in Canela, technically a separate town but adjacent) is the main natural sight. Gramado's famous **film festival** runs in August; the town's **Christmas** (Natal Luz) celebrations, from October through January, are notorious for their excess.
