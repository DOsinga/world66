The Museu Júlio de Castilhos is Rio Grande do Sul's official state history museum, housed in a handsome late 19th-century mansion a short walk from Praça da Matriz. The collection covers the full sweep of the state's history: indigenous artefacts from before European contact, colonial-era documents and religious objects, weapons and equipment from the Farroupilha revolution of 1835, and an extensive collection of items relating to gaúcho material culture — bombachas (the traditional wide-legged trousers), spurs, saddles, silver mate equipment, and the working tools of the pampas cattle culture.

The museum is not large but it is well-organised and the collections are genuinely interesting if you want to understand what makes Rio Grande do Sul different from the rest of Brazil. The Farroupilha revolution — a decade-long separatist uprising against the Brazilian imperial government — is treated here with the pride of a founding myth: the gaúcho identity of independence and self-reliance that still distinguishes the state has its roots in that conflict. The late 19th-century positivist republic period, when the state was governed according to Auguste Comte's philosophy in ways that were simultaneously progressive and authoritarian, is another fascinating chapter. Admission is free and the building alone — with its original 19th-century furnishings largely intact — is worth seeing.
