The Assembleia Legislativa do Rio Grande do Sul occupies the northern side of Praça da Matriz in a large neoclassical building that was completed in 1921 and has housed the state parliament ever since. Rio Grande do Sul has always been one of Brazil's most politically active states — it has produced more Brazilian presidents than any other state and its political culture is unusually combative — and the Assembly building has seen its share of history, from the Farroupilha period debates to the Positivist republic experiments to the complex political battles of the 20th century.

The building is open to visitors during working hours and occasionally for guided tours that take you into the main chamber and the historic committee rooms. Even without a tour, the exterior is worth seeing as part of the Praça da Matriz ensemble: the four great buildings around the square — cathedral, palace, theatre, and assembly — represent the complete apparatus of civic power in a way that few Brazilian city squares manage. Rio Grande do Sul's state assembly has historically been one of the more interesting in Brazil for the variety of political positions represented: the gaúcho political tradition has always had strong currents of both socialist and conservative thought, and the assembly's debates have occasionally generated national news.
