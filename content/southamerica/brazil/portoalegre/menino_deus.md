Menino Deus — Holy Child — is one of Porto Alegre's oldest residential districts, a quiet, slightly hilly neighbourhood of early 20th-century houses and apartment blocks south of the city centre. It lacks the nightlife energy of Cidade Baixa or the café culture of Moinhos de Vento, but it has something those neighbourhoods sometimes lack: a sense that real people actually live there. The streets are tree-lined, the pace is slow, and the neighbourhood restaurants — the kind that are full of families at lunch on weekends — are excellent.

The **Santa Casa de Misericórdia**, Porto Alegre's oldest hospital, anchors the northern edge of the neighbourhood in an imposing old building. The streets around Rua Cristóvão Colombo and Avenida Getúlio Vargas have the best concentration of restaurants: small traditional places serving generous portions of gaúcho food, some Italian-influenced pasta houses, and a few newer spots with more contemporary menus. Menino Deus is also one of the better areas for traditional craft food shops — **açougues** (butchers) with excellent local beef and **delicatessens** with cured meats and cheeses from the Serra Gaúcha. It is not a neighbourhood you need to make a special trip to, but if you are staying nearby or want to eat where the locals eat, it is worth knowing about.
