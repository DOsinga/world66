The Mercado Público Central is Porto Alegre's most important building after the government palaces — not because of its architecture, though the mid-19th-century neoclassical structure is handsome, but because of its role in the city's daily life. Since 1869 it has been the place where portoalegrenses eat, shop, gossip, and argue. The building occupies a full city block near the waterfront, its four main entrances leading into a labyrinth of more than a hundred permanent stalls selling everything imaginable: fresh produce, meat, seafood, chimarrão equipment, regional food products, leather goods, hardware, and considerably more.

The eating options are what most visitors come for. **Gambrinus**, operating since 1889, is the most famous restaurant: a proper sit-down place with excellent churrasco and a list of local wines. Around it are counters serving pastéis, grilled sausage sandwiches, fresh juice, and the city's best churrasco sandwiches at very reasonable prices. The market is busiest on Saturday mornings when it feels like the entire city has turned up for lunch. On weekdays it is slightly calmer but still lively. Do not leave without buying a **cuia** (the gourd for drinking chimarrão) and a **bombilla** (the metal straw) — the market stalls have better quality and selection than any tourist shop.
