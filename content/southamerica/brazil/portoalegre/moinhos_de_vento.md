Moinhos de Vento — Windmills — takes its name from the Dutch-style windmills that once ground grain on this rise above the city centre. Nothing so picturesque remains, but the neighbourhood is still the most pleasant part of Porto Alegre: wide tree-lined streets, good independent cafés, contemporary restaurants, and the lovely **Parque Moinhos de Vento** at its centre. It is where Porto Alegre's professional and upper-middle-class families have settled, and the quality of the shops and food reflects that without feeling exclusionary.

The streets around Rua Padre Chagas are the main café strip, with places open all day for espresso, brunch, and the kind of slightly elaborate lunch that upper Porto Alegre regards as a birthright. The park itself is a daily meeting place: joggers, dog-walkers, families on weekend afternoons, and the occasional fitness class all coexist in a few hectares of well-kept green. It is much smaller than Redenção but more intimate. **Shopping Moinhos** at the edge of the neighbourhood handles the mall-going crowd. For visitors, Moinhos de Vento makes a good base: quieter than Bom Fim, more interesting than the business hotels of the centre, and well-connected by bus to everywhere else.
