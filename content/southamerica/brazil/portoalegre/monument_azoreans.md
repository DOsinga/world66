title: Monument to the Azoreans

The Monumento ao Expedicionário Açoriano stands in a small square south of the city centre and commemorates the Azorean settlers who founded Porto Alegre in 1772. The monument — a group of bronze figures representing the original colonising families who came from the Portuguese Azores archipelago — is one of the clearer reminders that Porto Alegre's European identity predates the German and Italian immigration waves of the 19th century by a full century. The Azorean founders came with their fishing and farming traditions, their particular Portuguese dialect, and their saints, and they established the settlement that would eventually become a city of four million.

The surrounding square is pleasant and slightly off the main tourist circuit, which makes it quieter than the Praça da Matriz. The monument itself is well-made — the bronze figures have genuine expression and the composition is thoughtful — and the plaques around the base trace the history of the settlement. It is a ten-minute walk from the Praça da Matriz through streets that become progressively less touristy, which is itself an interesting walk. This part of the city has good examples of late 19th- and early 20th-century domestic architecture: modest houses and small apartment buildings that give a better sense of how ordinary Porto Alegre lived than the grand institutional buildings of the centre.
