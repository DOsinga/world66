The Museu de Comunicação Social Hipólito José da Costa is Porto Alegre's most underrated museum, housed in an impressive early 20th-century building that was originally the printing house for the state's largest newspaper. The museum is dedicated to the history of communication — printing, radio, television, and now digital media — and it is particularly strong on the history of Brazilian journalism, which has its own complicated and important story in a country that spent decades under military censorship.

The collection includes original printing presses, early radio equipment, historic newspapers and magazines, and an extensive archive of broadcasting history. The building itself is one of the finest industrial-heritage structures in Porto Alegre: the main pressroom, with its high ceilings and original structural ironwork, has been preserved and adapted as the main exhibition hall. The museum is named for Hipólito José da Costa, the 18th-century journalist born in what is now Rio Grande do Sul who founded the **Correio Braziliense** in London — the first genuinely independent Brazilian newspaper, published in exile because it could not operate safely in Brazil. His story is one of the surprising threads of Brazilian intellectual history, and the museum tells it well.
