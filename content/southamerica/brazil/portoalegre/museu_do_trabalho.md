The Museu do Trabalho — Museum of Work — is housed in a beautifully preserved early 20th-century workers' social club building in the Floresta neighbourhood, north of the city centre. It is one of Porto Alegre's best-kept secrets: a small museum with a collection dedicated to the history of labour in Rio Grande do Sul, from the early industrial period through the political upheavals of the 20th century, presented in a building that is itself a piece of labour history.

The collection documents the work lives of the German and Italian immigrants who came to fill the state's factories and workshops, the development of the union movement, and the particular political culture of a state that produced both conservative gaúcho landowners and some of Brazil's most militant leftist politics. The contradictions are interesting, and the museum addresses them with more nuance than such institutions often manage. The building — a social club built by workers' associations in the early 1900s — is charming: painted in its original colours, with a small auditorium and a garden that is used for community events. Not a major attraction but thoroughly worthwhile for anyone interested in social history.
