title: Paço Municipal (City Hall)

The Paço Municipal — Porto Alegre's city hall — is a dignified early 20th-century building in the Praça Montevidéu, a short walk from the Praça da Alfândega and the Farol Santander. The building was completed in 1901 and houses the offices of the city's executive government; it is not open for general visits, but the exterior is worth pausing at as part of any walking tour of the city centre. The neoclassical facade, with its central clock tower, is one of the more photographed buildings in central Porto Alegre.

The surrounding Praça Montevidéu is a pleasant urban square with mature trees and a good fountain, somewhat quieter than the busier commercial squares nearby and a good place to rest on a walking tour. The city hall's position near the waterfront reflects the 19th-century logic of Porto Alegre's development: the commercial and governmental heart of the city grew up around the port, with the wharves, customs buildings, and municipal offices clustered together near the Guaíba shore. Walking from the Paço Municipal north to the Cais Mauá takes you through this historical waterfront district, past several of the city's best examples of late 19th- and early 20th-century commercial architecture.
