Parque Estadual de Itapuã, about 60 kilometres south of Porto Alegre, is the best natural escape within easy reach of the city. The park occupies a long peninsula of forested headlands, rocky beaches, and lagoon shoreline on the edge of the Lagoa dos Patos — the vast coastal lagoon that stretches south toward Uruguay. The scenery is striking: outcrops of ancient rock covered in Atlantic Forest, clear water (the lagoon is freshwater here), white sandy beaches that feel almost Caribbean on calm summer days, and very little development.

The beaches of Itapuã are the nearest swim-in-the-water option for Porto Alegre residents, and they can be crowded on summer weekends (January and February especially) — arrive early to claim a good spot. But the park is large enough that the hiking trails into the interior, the rock formations, and the quieter northern beaches remain uncrowded even on busy days. Birding is excellent: the park is a significant wetland area with capybaras, caimans, and a wide variety of marsh birds visible from the trails. Boat trips on the Lagoa dos Patos can be arranged at the park entrance. Getting there requires a car or a tour (buses run but the connections are complicated); a car rental from the Porto Alegre airport for a day is the most practical option.
