Rua dos Andradas — Rua da Praia to everyone who lives in Porto Alegre — is the pedestrianised commercial spine of the Centro Histórico, running east-west through the heart of the old city. It has been the main shopping street since the colonial period, and in its current pedestrianised form it is genuinely lively: a constant flow of people, street vendors, shoe-shiners, food carts, and the kind of commercial bustle that makes a city centre feel alive rather than just existing.

The street itself has attractive late 19th- and early 20th-century buildings at the upper levels — look up above the ground-floor shop fronts and you will see ornate facades that the modern shop signage does its best to hide. The best stretch for walking is between the Praça da Alfândega (where the MARGS is) and the area around the Mercado Público. The leather shops here are worth investigating: Rio Grande do Sul has genuine craft traditions in boots, belts, and equestrian equipment, and the prices are far below what you would pay in São Paulo. The street's atmosphere peaks on weekday lunchtimes and Saturday mornings; Sunday afternoons it is quieter and easier to appreciate the architecture.
