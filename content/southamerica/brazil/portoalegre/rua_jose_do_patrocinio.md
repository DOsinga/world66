Rua José do Patrocínio is the axis of Cidade Baixa's nightlife, a street that would be perfectly ordinary by day — terrace houses, small shops, a couple of bakeries — and becomes one of the most animated streets in southern Brazil by Thursday evening. The bars here are mostly small, informal, and spilling onto the pavement: no velvet ropes, no dress code, no attitude. Cold chopp (draft beer), caipirinhas made with local cachaça, and pastel from the street vendors are the consistent through-line.

The range of bars is wide: you can find a traditional boteco (corner bar) with a Fla-Flu game on the television, a craft beer bar with twenty taps and a complicated menu, a gay bar with a dance floor already going at 10pm, and a bar playing live forró all within a hundred metres of each other. The street and its surroundings are at their best on Friday nights when the weekend properly begins and the Cidade Baixa crowd — diverse, unpretentious, genuinely mixed — fills the pavements. Porto Alegre is a late-night city: dinner does not start until 9pm, bars fill up from 10pm, and the serious clubs do not get going until after midnight. Pace yourself accordingly.
