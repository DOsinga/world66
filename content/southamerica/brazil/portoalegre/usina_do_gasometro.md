The Usina do Gasômetro is Porto Alegre's most photographed building and, in the late afternoon, its most popular gathering place. The former thermoelectric power station — a large brick industrial building with an enormous chimney on the southern end of the waterfront — was converted into a cultural centre in 1991 and has housed performance spaces, exhibition galleries, a cinema, and cafés ever since. But what makes it essential is the terrace facing the Rio Guaíba: this is where the whole city comes to watch the sunset.

The sunsets over the Guaíba from the Gasômetro terrace are the best in Porto Alegre, which means the best in Brazil by the city's own somewhat biased reckoning — but they are genuinely extraordinary on clear evenings, the water turning from silver to gold to deep orange as the sun sets behind the distant hills. The terrace bar fills up steadily from about 5pm and is packed by 6pm; come early to get a spot at the railing. Inside, the main exhibition hall is used for large-scale contemporary art shows and the cinema screens art-house and Brazilian independent films. The building's industrial bones — exposed brick, original machinery in some spaces, the vast chimney — are sympathetically preserved and give it a character that purpose-built cultural centres rarely achieve.
