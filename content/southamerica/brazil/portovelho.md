Porto Velho is the capital of Rondônia, set on the Madeira River in the western Brazilian Amazon. It is a working city first: a transport hub, state capital, river port, and service centre for a huge interior region. At first glance it can look plain, but it has enough river life and railway history to reward a spare day.

The city's identity comes from the Madeira-Mamoré Railway, built in the early 20th century to move rubber and other goods around the unnavigable stretches of the Madeira. The old [railway complex](/southamerica/brazil/portovelho/madeira_mamore_railway_complex), the [Three Water Boxes](/southamerica/brazil/portovelho/three_water_boxes), and the central riverfront all belong to that story. Around them, Porto Velho feels more Amazonian than monumental: markets, boats, heat, and muddy water moving past the city.

Do not come expecting a polished tourist town. Come if you are already crossing the western Amazon, travelling onward by river, or curious about a frontier capital whose best sights are tied to engineering, migration, and the Madeira itself.
