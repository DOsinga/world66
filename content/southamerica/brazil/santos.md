Santos is Brazil's largest port and one of the busiest in Latin America, but it is also a beach city with a long seafront and a proud footballing heritage — this is where Pelé played for 18 years. The [Pelé Museum](/southamerica/brazil/santos/pele_museum) at the Vila Belmiro stadium is a pilgrimage for football fans.

The old coffee exchange building, the [Bolsa do Café](/southamerica/brazil/santos/coffee_museum), is the most impressive building in town — an ornate early 20th-century hall with stained glass and painted ceilings that reflects the days when Santos was the coffee capital of the world. The [historic centre](/southamerica/brazil/santos/centro_historico_nbh) has been partly restored, and the Valongo neighbourhood and old [tram line](/southamerica/brazil/santos/tourist_tram) are worth exploring. The [seafront garden](/southamerica/brazil/santos/beachfront_garden) running along the beach is in the Guinness Book as the largest beachfront garden in the world. Up on [Monte Serrat](/southamerica/brazil/santos/monte_serrat), reached by funicular, the view takes in the whole port and city.

Santos is less than an hour from [São Paulo](/southamerica/brazil/saopaulo) by road, making it the city's default beach escape. The [beaches](/southamerica/brazil/santos/santos_beaches) are long and urban, backed by high-rise apartments — many of them famously [leaning](/southamerica/brazil/santos/leaning_buildings) on the soft soil. The food is good — seafood restaurants and traditional Portuguese-Brazilian cooking.
