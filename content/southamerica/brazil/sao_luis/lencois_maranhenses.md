The Lençóis Maranhenses National Park, 250 kilometres east of São Luís, is one of the most otherworldly landscapes in South America: enormous white sand dunes, up to 40 metres high, extending for over 1,500 square kilometres along the coast, their hollows filled from January to September with freshwater lagoons of impossible turquoise and emerald clarity.

Most visitors come with a tour operator from São Luís, which involves a roughly four-hour bus journey to the town of Barreirinhas at the park's entrance. From Barreirinhas, 4WD vehicles take you into the dunes, or you can walk. The most famous lagoons — Lagoa Bonita, Lagoa Azul, Lagoa Bonita — are stunning. If you can, go on a weekday: weekends bring large crowds from São Luís, and the experience of standing alone on a white dune above a turquoise lake is considerably better than standing in a queue. Allow at least two days: one for the dunes, one for the river journey through the park's edge.
