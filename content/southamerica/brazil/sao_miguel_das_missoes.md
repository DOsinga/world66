São Miguel das Missões is a small town in northwestern Rio Grande do Sul, but it has one reason to matter that is more than enough: the red sandstone ruins of São Miguel Arcanjo, Brazil's part of the UNESCO-listed Jesuit Missions of the Guaranis.

The town grew around the old reduction, so the ruins are not a side trip from São Miguel; they are its centre. The broken church facade, the mission square, the museum of carved saints, and the quiet mission fountain give a better sense of the Jesuit-Guarani world than a long list of smaller sights ever could.

Come for the archaeology and give it time. The site is especially good late in the day, when the stone warms in the low sun and the emptiness of the old mission plan becomes easier to read. São Miguel is not a big urban stop, but for travelers crossing the south of Brazil or following the missions route, it is worth the detour.
