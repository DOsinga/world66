---
title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

São Paulo has the best food scene in South America. The city is built on immigration — Italian, Japanese, Lebanese, Portuguese — and every wave brought its kitchen with it. The Japanese diaspora alone runs to a million and a half people, the largest outside Japan, and Liberdade has izakaya and ramen on the level of mid-priced Tokyo. The fine-dining scene reaches international standards at places like D.O.M., Maní, and A Casa do Porco. The cheap end runs from the lanchonete pão-de-queijo counter on every corner to the *padarias* that bake fresh bread morning and afternoon.

The neighbourhoods to focus on: **Jardins** for upmarket dining, **Vila Madalena** and **Pinheiros** for younger and more inventive kitchens, **Liberdade** for Japanese, **Bixiga** for old-school Italian, the **Mercado Municipal** downtown for the legendary mortadella sandwich and pastel de bacalhau. The drink to know about is the **caipirinha** — São Paulo is the working capital of Brazilian cachaça, and almost every bar does a serious version.
