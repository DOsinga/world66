The Jardins — 'gardens' — were laid out in the 1910s by the British planner Barry Parker, the same architect who had laid out Letchworth Garden City in England, as a low-density residential district on the slopes south of Avenida Paulista. The original logic — wide tree-lined streets, deep landscaped front gardens, no high-rises — has largely held. Today this is the most upmarket residential and shopping district in São Paulo and the home of the city's most ambitious restaurants.

The shopping spine is **Rua Oscar Freire** — the international fashion houses, the better Brazilian designers — with **Alameda Lorena** and **Alameda Itu** as the side streets. The food map of the Jardins runs from D.O.M. and Maní (fine dining of international stature) through Fasano (the city's grand Italian institution) to the long-running bistros and bars around Alameda Tietê. Walk from Paulista down through the Jardins toward Ibirapuera Park on a sunny afternoon — it is the most pleasant urban walk in the city.
