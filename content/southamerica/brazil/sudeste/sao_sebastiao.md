São Sebastião is a historic town on São Paulo's north coast, facing [Ilhabela](/southamerica/brazil/sudeste/ilhabela) across a narrow channel, and the starting point of the ferry to the island. Its small colonial centre, with a church on the waterfront square, dates from the seventeenth century.

Most visitors come for the beaches, which run south of town along the Rio–Santos road. Maresias is the best known, a lively surf beach with bars and a party scene; Juquehy and Barra do Sahy are calmer and popular with families; and Camburi and Boiçucanga sit between them. Behind them all the Atlantic forest climbs steeply up the Serra do Mar.

São Sebastião is about three hours from [São Paulo](/southamerica/brazil/saopaulo), and the beaches fill up with paulistanos on summer weekends, when the road gets jammed. Midweek, or outside January and February, the coast is far more peaceful.
