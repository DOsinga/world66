Bom Fim sits between Parque Farroupilha (Redenção) to the west and the middle-class apartment blocks of Moinhos de Vento to the north, and it has a character all its own: intellectual, slightly chaotic, and very Porto Alegre. It developed as a Jewish neighbourhood in the early 20th century, and a significant community still lives here — you will find synagogues, a kosher restaurant or two, and Jewish cultural institutions alongside the student bars and bookshops. The **Livraria Palavras e Afins** and similar independent booksellers give the neighbourhood its bookish reputation.

Avenida Osvaldo Aranha is the main street and a classic Porto Alegre promenade: wide pavement, constant foot traffic, cafés and bars at street level, and a view of the park across the road. On Sunday mornings the Brique da Redenção market spills along this strip and the neighbourhood is at its best. The student population from the nearby UFRGS campus keeps the bar scene young and inexpensive; good pizza, Middle Eastern food (a legacy of Lebanese immigration), and all-day cafés are the eating options. Bom Fim is where Porto Alegre's intellectuals, activists, and creative class have always lived, and the neighbourhood still carries that reputation with some pride.
