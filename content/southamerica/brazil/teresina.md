Teresina is the inland capital of Piauí, sitting between the Parnaíba and Poti rivers in Brazil's northeast. It is not a beach city, and it does not pretend to be one. The first thing most visitors notice is the heat: Teresina is one of Brazil's hottest state capitals, and the city works best if you move early, rest in the middle of the day, and return to the riverfront when the light softens.

The city has a quiet, practical character. It was founded in the 1850s to replace Oeiras as state capital, and its centre still carries the symbols of that decision: the [Palácio de Karnak](/southamerica/brazil/teresina/palacio_de_karnak), the [Museu do Piauí](/southamerica/brazil/teresina/museu_do_piaui), old churches, and the craft shops around Praça Pedro II. Teresina is also a river city. The best short outing is north to the [Encontro dos Rios](/southamerica/brazil/teresina/encontro_dos_rios), where the Poti meets the Parnaíba and boatmen work the muddy water.

This is a selective stop, not a headline Brazilian destination. But if you are crossing Piauí or have a day between buses, Teresina gives you a clear sense of the state's interior culture: hot streets, river breezes, religious processions, hammocks and leatherwork in the craft market, and a city that feels more like a large provincial town than a capital.
