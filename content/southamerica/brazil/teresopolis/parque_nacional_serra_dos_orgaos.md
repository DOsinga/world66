The Parque Nacional da Serra dos Órgãos protects a dramatic stretch of Atlantic Forest in the Serra do Mar mountains, with its main entrance right at the edge of Teresópolis. The park is best known for its extraordinary rock formations — dramatic granite spires that jut above the forest canopy and are visible from most of the town. The Dedo de Deus ("Finger of God") is the most famous, a pointed 1,692-metre pinnacle that appears on the Teresópolis city logo.

The park has an excellent trail network covering all ability levels. The easy walks through dense Atlantic Forest are rewarding for birdwatching — the forest is home to toucans, hummingbirds, and the endangered woolly spider monkey. More serious hikers tackle the two-day Travessia, crossing from Teresópolis to Petrópolis along a ridge trail with stunning views in both directions. Technical climbers find excellent routes on the main spires. Entry fees are reasonable and park infrastructure — trails, maps, visitor centre — is well maintained.
