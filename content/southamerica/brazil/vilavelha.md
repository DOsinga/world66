Vila Velha is a coastal city in Espírito Santo state, sitting directly across the Penha Channel from Vitória, the state capital. The two cities blend into each other at the waterfront, but Vila Velha has a distinct character: more residential, a bit more relaxed, and home to some of the best beaches in the metropolitan area.

[Praia da Costa](/southamerica/brazil/vilavelha/praia_da_costa) is the standout — a long, clean stretch of urban beach that draws locals and visitors in roughly equal measure. [Itaparica](/southamerica/brazil/vilavelha/praia_de_itaparica) to the south is quieter and popular with families. The beaches here are a genuine reason to stay rather than just crossing to Vitória.

The most striking landmark is the [Convento da Penha](/southamerica/brazil/vilavelha/convento_da_penha), a 16th-century Franciscan convent perched on a 154-metre rocky outcrop that rises abruptly from the surrounding flatlands. The views over the bay and Vitória's skyline are worth the climb alone. It's one of the oldest pilgrimage sites in Brazil and still draws enormous crowds during the Festa do Espírito Santo each May.

The [Prainha Historic Site](/southamerica/brazil/vilavelha/prainha_historic_site) preserves the oldest neighbourhood in the city — Portuguese settlers first landed here in 1535. The [Fort of São Francisco Xavier de Piratininga](/southamerica/brazil/vilavelha/fort_sao_francisco_xavier) on the headland and the [Church of Our Lady of the Rosary](/southamerica/brazil/vilavelha/church_our_lady_rosary) are both worth a look. The [Chocolates Garoto factory](/southamerica/brazil/vilavelha/garoto_factory) is a less obvious attraction: one of Brazil's best-loved chocolate brands has been made here since 1929.

Getting to Vitória is easy — buses and the [Terceira Ponte](/southamerica/brazil/vilavelha/terceira_ponte) connect them constantly. Vila Velha works well as a base if you want beach access alongside Vitória's restaurants and nightlife.
