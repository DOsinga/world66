---
title: "Eating Out"
type: section
---

Viña del Mar eats well, drawing on Chile's Pacific coast tradition of excellent seafood — congrio (conger eel), corvina (sea bass), and locos (abalone) appear regularly, along with the usual empanadas and cazuelas. The city has a more upscale restaurant scene than you might expect for a beach resort, catering to Santiago weekenders who arrive with money to spend.

The main strip along Avenida San Martín and the streets around the Casino have the highest concentration of restaurants, from no-frills marisquerías to more refined modern Chilean cooking. Seafood is best eaten fresh and simply prepared — a squeeze of lemon on a plate of raw clams beats any elaborate sauce.
