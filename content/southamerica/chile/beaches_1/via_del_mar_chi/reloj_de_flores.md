  The clock was installed in 1962 on a sloping flower bed beside the coastal avenue, modelled on similar clocks in Geneva and Edinburgh. It keeps accurate time — the hands are mechanical — while the face is replanted several times a year with seasonal flowers. It has appeared on Chilean postcards and tourism materials so consistently for sixty years that it has become genuinely symbolic of the city, which is a rare fate for what is essentially a very large garden ornament.
---

The Reloj de Flores is Viña del Mar's most photographed landmark: a working clock with mechanical hands set into a hillside of bedding plants along Avenida Marina. The flowers are changed several times a year by the city's gardeners, keeping the face colourful through the seasons. It sounds minor and it looks exactly as it sounds, but somehow the combination of scale and setting — the Pacific visible beyond, the esplanade busy with walkers — makes it genuinely charming rather than just kitsch.

It is on the way to or from the Wulff Castle and the beach, so you will almost certainly walk past it regardless. Stop for a minute, take the photograph that everyone takes, and move on with an honest verdict on whether clocks should be made of flowers.
