  The castle was built in 1906 by Gustavo Wulff, a German-Chilean businessman who wanted a residence that looked directly onto the Pacific. He got what he wanted: the building sits on a rocky promontory at the mouth of the Marga Marga estuary, connected to the mainland by a footbridge, with open sea on three sides. It was donated to the city in 1962 and is now used for cultural events and exhibitions.
---

Wulff Castle is the most dramatic building in Viña del Mar, a turreted Germanic manor perched on a rocky outcrop at the edge of the sea. From the esplanade along Avenida Marina you can see it clearly, standing apart from the city on its small peninsula, the waves breaking against the rocks below. It looks like something that has been transplanted from a northern European cliff and left here by accident, which is more or less what happened.

The interior is open to visitors and houses rotating exhibitions, but the real draw is the exterior and the view from the bridge connecting it to the mainland. Come at sunset, when the light turns the white facade orange and the Pacific glitters behind it.
