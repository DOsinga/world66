Chillán is a mid-sized city in south-central Chile, the capital of Ñuble Region, known for two things: it was the birthplace of Bernardo O'Higgins, Chile's independence hero, and it is the gateway to the [Termas de Chillán](/southamerica/chile/chillan/termas_de_chillan), one of Chile's best hot springs and ski resorts.

The city itself was largely rebuilt after a devastating earthquake in 1939 that killed some 24,000 people. It is functional rather than beautiful, but the [Feria de Chillán](/southamerica/chile/chillan/feria_de_chillan) — the large outdoor market — is one of the best in central Chile, selling produce, leather goods, woven textiles, and longaniza chillanejas, the local sausage that is famous across the country.

The [Escuela México](/southamerica/chile/chillan/escuela_mexico), a school built with Mexican aid after the earthquake, contains murals by David Alfaro Siqueiros and Xavier Guerrero — an unexpected piece of Mexican muralism in the Chilean countryside. The [Termas de Chillán](/southamerica/chile/chillan/termas_de_chillan), about 80 kilometres east in the Andes, has excellent skiing in winter (June–September) and volcanic hot pools year-round. The [Volcán Chillán](/southamerica/chile/chillan/volcan_chillan) is an active volcanic complex; hiking around it is spectacular and the terrain is like nothing else in the Chilean lake district.
