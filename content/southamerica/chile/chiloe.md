Chiloé is Chile with the weather turned inward: green, wet, wooden, and full of stories. The Isla Grande lies just beyond [Puerto Montt](/southamerica/chile/puertomontt), but the short ferry crossing over the Chacao Channel feels like a proper threshold. On the other side are shingled houses, fishing coves, foggy pastures, and a culture that has always looked both to the sea and to its own legends.

The island's great visual signatures are in [Castro](/southamerica/chile/chiloe/castro_palafitos), where brightly painted palafitos stand on stilts over the water, and in the UNESCO-listed wooden churches scattered through the archipelago. [Iglesia San Francisco](/southamerica/chile/chiloe/iglesia_san_francisco_de_castro), [Nercón](/southamerica/chile/chiloe/iglesia_de_nercon), [Dalcahue](/southamerica/chile/chiloe/dalcahue_market), [Achao](/southamerica/chile/chiloe/iglesia_de_achao), [Chonchi](/southamerica/chile/chiloe/iglesia_de_chonchi), and [Tenaún](/southamerica/chile/chiloe/iglesia_de_tenaun) each show a different version of the same Chilote carpentry genius.

Nature is never far away. The west coast is wilder, with beaches, peatlands, and the temperate rainforest of [Chiloé National Park](/southamerica/chile/chiloe/chiloe_national_park). North of Ancud, boat trips to [Puñihuil](/southamerica/chile/chiloe/punihuil_penguins) visit one of the few places where Humboldt and Magellanic penguins nest side by side. The south, around Tantauco, feels more remote still.

Come for two or three days if you only want Castro, Ancud, and a church circuit; come for a week if the island starts working on you. Chiloé rewards slow travel, rainproof clothing, and a tolerance for ferry schedules, muddy paths, and meals built around potatoes, seafood, and woodsmoke.
