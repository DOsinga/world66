A small Castro-based operator working out of Pid Pid in the countryside rather than an office in town, and the only one on the island that publishes what it charges.

Their catalogue is the clearest single summary of what Chiloé actually offers a visitor, so it is worth listing: the **patrimonial wooden churches** in three separate routes — Dalcahue and Isla Quinchao, Chonchi and Isla Lemuy, and Colo, Tenaún and San Juan — plus a Castro city tour; **Parque Nacional Chiloé** on the Sendero El Tepual and the walk out to Punta Pirulil and the **Muelle de las Almas**, the "dock of souls" that ends in mid-air over the Pacific; the **Río Yaldad trail in Parque Tantauco**; **kayaking at Chepu**, on the sunken forest left by the 1960 earthquake, combined either with the Puñihuil boat trip or a forest walk; **bird-watching at Bahía Pullao** on Quinchao, which is a Hemispheric Reserve site for Hudsonian godwits; and three **bike routes** — Parque Nacional Chiloé, Isla Quinchao and Isla Lemuy — each sold guided or self-guided at less than half the guided price.

Longer programmes run to five days of "Patagonía Insular" and combinations with Lago Llanquihue.

The pricing structure tells you something about how Chiloé works: a solo traveller pays **CLP 290,000** for the Muelle de la Luz and Chepu day, and the same day in a group of ten costs **CLP 90,000** a head. If you are alone, find someone to go with.
