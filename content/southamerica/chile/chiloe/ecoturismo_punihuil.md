The three islets off Puñihuil beach were declared a Monumento Natural in 1999 for a reason worth stating precisely, because the site states it precisely: they hold a **mixed breeding colony of Humboldt and Magellanic penguins**, the two species nesting on the same rocks. Their own page is blunt that this does not happen anywhere else.

The trips are small wooden boats off an open beach, twenty-odd minutes out and around the islets. What makes them unusual among penguin trips is the regulation: **a municipal ordinance caps the number of sailings per day**, which the operators describe on their own site as the reason the colony is still there. So this is a trip that can sell out, and in January and February it does.

Also on the rocks and in the water on a good run: sea otters, Magellanic cormorants, kelp geese, and — with luck — the blue whales that feed off western Chiloé in late summer.

The beach has a visitor centre, cabins, footpaths through Chilote forest, and a cluster of restaurants serving curanto and the day's shellfish. It is about 27 km west of Ancud on a road that is gravel for the last stretch.
