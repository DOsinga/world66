  Ancud was the last royalist stronghold in South America. Spanish forces held the fort here until 1826, nearly four years after Chilean independence was declared and long after Spain had surrendered everywhere else on the continent. Ancud is the first town you reach when the ferry from Puerto Montt arrives at Chiloé. It sits at the northern tip of the main island, on a harbour protected by a headland, and has the casual feel of a fishing port that knows it doesn't need to try very hard to attract visitors. The [Museo Regional de Ancud](/southamerica/chile/chiloearchipelago/museo_ancud) on the main plaza is an excellent introduction to Chiloé's history and culture.

The town's sea lion colony at Fuerte San Antonio — the ruins of the Spanish fort where the last royalists held out — is a spectacle. A few dozen sea lions haul themselves onto the rocks below the fortress walls and bark continuously. The bay is also good for watching Magellanic penguins, which nest on nearby islands and are occasionally visible from the waterfront. Ancud is a calmer, less touristed base than Castro if you prefer to avoid the crowds.
