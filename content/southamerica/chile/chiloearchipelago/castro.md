Castro is the capital of Chiloé Province and the archipelago's main town, a compact city of wooden houses cascading down steep hills to the fjord below. It is rainy, practical, and visually memorable: fishing boats in the bay, wooden shingles on old houses, and bright palafitos standing on stilts over the tide.

The Plaza de Armas is dominated by the remarkable [Church of San Francisco](/southamerica/chile/chiloearchipelago/castro/church_of_san_francisco) in its improbable yellow-and-lilac paint scheme. Below the church, markets and waterfront stalls sell what you came to Chiloé to find: native potatoes, smoked shellfish, woollen ponchos, and the ingredients for curanto, the island feast of seafood, meat, and potatoes.

The [palafitos](/southamerica/chile/chiloearchipelago/castro/palafitos) are the image most travelers remember. Some are still working homes, others have become guesthouses and restaurants, but together they give Castro a waterfront unlike anywhere else in Chile.

Castro is also a good base for the rest of [Chiloé Archipelago](/southamerica/chile/chiloearchipelago). Buses run to Ancud, Dalcahue, Chonchi, Quellón, and Chiloé National Park, and the town is small enough to explore on foot between rides.
