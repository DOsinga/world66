The Church of Chonchi, dedicated to St Charles Borromeo, stands on the steep hillside of the town of Chonchi, about 25 km south of Castro. Chonchi is one of the best-preserved small towns in Chiloé — it was wealthy in the late 19th century from timber export and the money shows in the wooden buildings around the main plaza. The church crowns it all: a three-tower facade in yellow and cream, the central tower higher than the flanking ones.

The interior has the classic Chiloé features: barrel-vaulted wooden ceiling, locally painted decoration, and the quiet sense of a working church that has served a continuous parish since its foundation. Chonchi's town itself deserves an hour of exploration: the wooden buildings along the main street have been well maintained, the harbour below has fishing boats, and the views across the fjord are excellent. It's on the road south from Castro and makes a good stop on the way to the southern tip of the island.
