Ahu Te Peu is one of Rapa Nui's quieter archaeological landscapes, spread along the northwest coast with old house foundations, stone enclosures, caves, and ahu remains rather than a single restored row of moai. That makes it less immediately dramatic than Tongariki or Rano Raraku, but more revealing of how a coastal settlement actually worked.

Come with a guide if you can, because the low stone forms make much more sense when someone explains hare paenga boat-shaped houses, gardens, and clan areas. The site also works as part of a coastal walk, giving you open sea, archaeology, and a break from the island's more photographed stops.
