story: "Angelmó's name is a corruption of 'Anchor Mole', the English term used for the small mole built here by William Wheelwright when he ran the first steamboat services into Puerto Montt in the 1850s. The fish market kept the misheard pronunciation. Even now, conger eel, king crab, and curanto draw locals out of the centre for Sunday lunch."
---

The fishing port and craft market two kilometres west of the centre is the most photographed and best-loved corner of Puerto Montt. A long shed of stalls sells alpaca knitwear, copper pans, mate gourds, and amber jewellery; the parallel row of cocinerías does the seafood lunches the city is famous for.

Curanto, a one-pot stew of mussels, clams, sausage, smoked pork, chicken, and potato traditionally steamed underground in hot stones, is the dish to try. Pair it with chicha de manzana, a sharp local apple cider. Best at lunchtime; many stalls close by 4 p.m.
