About 7 km north of the city on the road to the airport, this open-air maritime museum is the work of a local enthusiast who decided, almost single-handedly, to reconstruct the most important ships in the exploration of the Strait of Magellan. The result is a small fleet of full-scale replicas you can climb aboard: Magellan's **Nao Victoria**, the **HMS Beagle** that carried Darwin through the strait in 1834, Shackleton's lifeboat **James Caird**, and Ancud, the 1843 schooner that established the Chilean claim to Patagonia.

It sounds like a tourist trap and isn't — the ships are obsessively researched, you can go down into the holds and up into the rigging, and the contrast in scale between Magellan's tiny 85-foot caravel and the modern container ships anchoring offshore is genuinely moving. Allow at least 90 minutes.
