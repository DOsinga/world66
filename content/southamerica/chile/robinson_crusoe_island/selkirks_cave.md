A shallow cave on the north shore of the island, traditionally pointed out as the spot where Alexander Selkirk took his first shelter when he was put ashore in October 1704. Whether or not it is the real place is anyone's guess. What is undeniable is that it is a beautifully quiet spot looking out over the empty Pacific, much as it must have looked to Selkirk.

The walk in is short but rough, with some scrambling over boulders. Local guides at the CONAF office or in the village can show you the way.
