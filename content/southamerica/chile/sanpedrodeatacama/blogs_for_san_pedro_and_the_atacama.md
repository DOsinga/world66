San Pedro is a small town with an enormous hinterland, and the writing about it splits cleanly in two. Half of it is logistics — what a tour costs, whether you can drive yourself, how many days you need before the altitude stops hurting. The other half is the argument about whether the place is worth the effort, because a week of dawn departures and two-hour drives is not everyone's holiday. The blogs below cover both halves honestly, and two of them are in Spanish or written by Chileans, which is not the norm for this part of the world.

The odd one out is Vulcano Expediciones, a local mountain-guide outfit rather than a blog. We list them because their inventory of a hundred things to do in and around San Pedro is genuinely the most complete anyone has made, and it turned up a dozen places worth adding to this guide — the standing ignimbrite columns on the Tara road, the cactus canyon at Guatín, the petroglyph fields north of town. Read it as a checklist rather than as advice, and remember that the ALMA visitor tours it mentions have been suspended for years.
