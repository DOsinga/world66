San Pedro eats late and expensively. Almost everything is on Caracoles, almost everything has adobe walls and a fire, and the prices are the highest in northern Chile — you are paying for the fact that every ingredient came up the road from Calama. The cooking worth seeking out is the cooking that uses what grows here: llama, quinoa, chañar, rica-rica, the potatoes from the villages along the salt flat.

One practical point that matters more than any restaurant: tours to the geysers leave between four and five in the morning, long before any hotel serves breakfast. Know where you are getting food the night before.

These pages draw on the hundred-item list kept by Vulcano Expediciones, a local guiding outfit, which we list in [6 Blogs for San Pedro and the Atacama](/southamerica/chile/sanpedrodeatacama/blogs_for_san_pedro_and_the_atacama).
