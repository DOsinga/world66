A French bakery in the middle of the Atacama, run by a French baker, turning out nine kinds of baguette and a case of croissants under the algarrobo trees on Gustavo Le Paige. It should not work and it entirely does. The laminated pastry is the real thing, which after a week of desert breakfasts is a more emotional experience than it sounds.

The single most useful fact about it: it opens at five in the morning. Every tour to [El Tatio](/southamerica/chile/sanpedrodeatacama/el_tatio_geysers) leaves in the dark and the hotel breakfast does not start until long after you have gone, so this is where you get food into you before three hours in a cold minibus. Buy extra for the road; nothing is open at 4,300 metres.

Later in the day it is a straightforward café — coffee, sandwiches on their own bread, tables outside in the shade. Cash is easiest.
