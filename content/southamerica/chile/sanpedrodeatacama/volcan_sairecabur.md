Sairecabur stands at 5,971 metres on the border with Bolivia, north-east of San Pedro, and it is the mountain guides use to find out whether you are ready for anything bigger. A mining track climbs to around 5,750 metres, which leaves a short scramble over loose volcanic blocks to the summit — perhaps an hour of walking, but an hour at an altitude where an hour is a long time.

The crater is broad and the rim is littered with the remains of a high-altitude astronomical observatory, abandoned after a few years of operation. From the top the view runs north along the whole Bolivian volcanic chain and south over [Licancabur](/southamerica/chile/sanpedrodeatacama/licancabur_volcano) to the Salar de Atacama.

Do not treat the short walk as a reason to skip acclimatising. Going from San Pedro at 2,400 metres to nearly 6,000 in a morning is how people end up with a headache that follows them for three days. Most operators want you to have done [Cerro Toco](/southamerica/chile/sanpedrodeatacama/cerro_toco) first.
