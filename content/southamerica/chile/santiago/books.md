**Pablo Neruda** — Start with *Twenty Love Poems and a Song of Despair* (1924) or the *Canto General* (1950). The poems ground themselves in Chilean landscapes, and reading them before visiting the Sierra Nevada or the Atacama changes how you see those places. After visiting La Chascona, try *Memoirs* (also translated as *I Confess That I Have Lived*), his autobiography — lush, opinionated, and full of the extraordinary life he led.

**Isabel Allende** — *The House of the Spirits* (1982), her debut novel, is the essential introduction to 20th-century Chilean political history as seen through the lives of three generations of women. It fictionalises the Allende/Pinochet era without pretending to documentary precision, which is why it works.

**Roberto Bolaño** — *The Savage Detectives* (1998) is nominally Mexican, but Bolaño was Chilean and Santiago marks the starting point of his obsessions. His novel *Nocturno de Chile* (2000) deals directly with the dictatorship years and the complicity of Chile's intellectual class with the Pinochet regime.

**Ariel Dorfman** — *Death and the Maiden* (1990), a play rather than a novel, is the best dramatic work to come out of the dictatorship: three characters, an isolated house, and the question of whether the man one character believes tortured her is actually who she thinks he is.
