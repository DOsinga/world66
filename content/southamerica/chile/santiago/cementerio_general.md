The Cementerio General is Chile's most important cemetery and one of the largest in South America, covering nearly 85 hectares north of the city centre. It holds over two million burials and reads as a complete history of Chilean society — from the grandest neoclassical mausoleums of the 19th-century oligarchy to the simple graves of ordinary families. Salvador Allende, Pablo Neruda, Violeta Parra, and many other defining figures of Chilean culture and politics are buried here.

The cemetery has been open to visitors since its founding in 1821 and is treated as a public park as well as a burial ground. The main avenues are lined with funeral monuments of extraordinary ambition and variety: Egyptian obelisks, Greek temples, art nouveau angels, modernist concrete slabs. It is both moving and architecturally fascinating. Pick up a map at the entrance to find the graves of notable figures. Open daily.
