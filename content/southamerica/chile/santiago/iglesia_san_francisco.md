  The Iglesia San Francisco was completed in 1618 and survived the major earthquakes of 1647 and 1730 that destroyed most of colonial Santiago — the only colonial building in the city to do so. The Mapuche Virgin inside — a small wooden figure brought by Pedro de Valdivia from Spain in 1540 — is considered the oldest image of the Virgin Mary in Chile.

The Iglesia San Francisco is the oldest surviving building in Santiago, completed in 1618 and one of the very few colonial structures left in a city that earthquakes have repeatedly levelled. The thick adobe walls and the sober Baroque facade look weathered and ancient in the best way — a genuine survivor in a city where most things are relatively new.

The attached museum holds a collection of colonial religious art, including 17th-century paintings from the Cusco School, carved wooden altars, and vestments. The small cloister is particularly beautiful — one of the few genuinely old courtyards in the city. The church is on the Alameda, diagonally opposite from the GAM cultural centre. Open for visits outside of mass times; the museum has separate hours.
