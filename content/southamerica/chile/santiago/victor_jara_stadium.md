story: >
  After the September 11, 1973 coup, the military used the Chile Stadium (now renamed Estadio Víctor Jara) as a detention and torture centre. Over 5,000 prisoners were held here in the weeks following the coup. Víctor Jara — Chile's most beloved folk singer and a supporter of the Allende government — was arrested, tortured, and murdered here in the days following the coup. His hands were broken before he died; guards reportedly forced him to play guitar. He was 40 years old. The stadium was renamed for him in 2003.

The Estadio Víctor Jara is a sports complex near Estación Central in western Santiago, but it is known primarily as one of the sites of the most documented human rights abuses of the Pinochet dictatorship. Visiting the stadium today is a sombre experience — the building is in regular use for sports events, but the historical record of what happened here in 1973 makes it a place of genuine memorial weight.

A plaque and small memorial inside mark the historical events. The stadium is not typically open for general tourism but can be viewed from outside, and the surrounding neighbourhood — with its early 20th-century republican architecture — is worth exploring. The visit is best combined with the nearby Museo de la Memoria, which contextualises the events with detailed documentation.
