Valparaíso is Chile’s great port city — chaotic, colourful, and unlike anywhere else in the country. Built across forty-two hills that tumble toward the Pacific, it is a city of steep alleys, painted houses, and [funicular elevators](/southamerica/chile/valparaiso/ascensores) that have been hauling passengers up to the hillside neighbourhoods since the 1880s. It can be visited as a day trip from [Santiago](/southamerica/chile/santiago), but it rewards a longer stay.

The hills of [Cerro Alegre](/southamerica/chile/valparaiso/cerro_alegre) and [Cerro Concepción](/southamerica/chile/valparaiso/cerro_concepcion) are the most visited — their streets lined with murals, boutique hotels, and terrace restaurants with views over the bay. [La Sebastiana](/southamerica/chile/valparaiso/la_sebastiana), Pablo Neruda’s eccentric hilltop house, is now a museum and one of the most satisfying half-days you can spend in the city. The [Naval Museum](/southamerica/chile/valparaiso/navalmuseum) on Cerro Artillería covers Chile’s maritime history and has a panoramic harbour view from its terrace.

The flat city around [Plaza Sotomayor](/southamerica/chile/valparaiso/plaza_sotomayor) and [La Matriz](/southamerica/chile/valparaiso/lamatriz) is grittier and more authentic — this is the working port, not the tourist postcard. Take a harbour tour from [Muelle Prat](/southamerica/chile/valparaiso/muelle_prat) to see the container terminals and the city’s hills from the water. On New Year’s Eve, Valparaíso hosts one of the most spectacular fireworks displays in South America, visible from the entire hillside.
