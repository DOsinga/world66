Bogota sits at 2,640 metres in the eastern Andes, a sprawling capital of eight million people where colonial churches share blocks with glass towers and street art covers every spare wall. The altitude catches newcomers off guard -- the air is thin, the sun surprisingly strong, and the temperature hovers around 14 degrees year-round. Give yourself a day to adjust before tackling the hills.

The historic heart is **[La Candelaria](/southamerica/colombia/bogota/la_candelaria)**, a tight grid of cobblestoned streets lined with painted colonial houses, universities, and more museums per block than anywhere else in South America. **[Plaza Bolivar](/southamerica/colombia/bogota/plaza_bolivar)** anchors the district, flanked by the [cathedral](/southamerica/colombia/bogota/catedral_primada), the congress, and the [Palace of Justice](/southamerica/colombia/bogota/palacio_de_justicia). From here it is a short walk to the **[Museo del Oro](/southamerica/colombia/bogota/museodeloro)**, home to the world's largest collection of pre-Columbian gold, and the **[Museo Botero](/southamerica/colombia/bogota/museo_botero)**, where Fernando Botero's rotund figures share space with Picassos and Dalís.

The cable car up **[Monserrate](/southamerica/colombia/bogota/monserrate)** is the one thing every visitor does, and for good reason -- the view from 3,150 metres over the city and the savanna beyond is extraordinary. Go on a clear morning. The northern neighbourhoods around the [Zona Rosa](/southamerica/colombia/bogota/zona_rosa) and [Parque de la 93](/southamerica/colombia/bogota/parque_93) are where Bogotanos go out at night: salsa clubs, craft beer bars, upscale restaurants. The contrast with the grittier south is sharp.

On Sundays, the [Ciclovía](/southamerica/colombia/bogota/ciclovia) closes over 120 km of roads to traffic, turning the city into an enormous cycling and walking circuit. The [Parque Simón Bolívar](/southamerica/colombia/bogota/parque_simon_bolivar) and [Usaquén](/southamerica/colombia/bogota/usaquen)'s colonial square with its flea market are both worth a morning. For architecture, Rogelio Salmona's [Biblioteca Virgilio Barco](/southamerica/colombia/bogota/biblioteca_virgilio_barco) at the edge of the park is one of the finest modern buildings in South America.

Bogota is the jumping-off point for day trips to the salt cathedral of [Zipaquira](/southamerica/colombia/zipaquira), the colonial village of Villa de Leyva, and the lakes and páramo of the surrounding highlands. Spend at least three days here -- the city rewards those who slow down and wander.
