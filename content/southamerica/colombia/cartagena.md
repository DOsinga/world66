Cartagena de Indias (pop. 888,000) is Colombia's most visited city and one of the finest colonial towns in the Americas. It divides neatly into two parts: the walled Ciudad Antigua, a UNESCO World Heritage Site of cobbled streets, baroque churches, and colourful balconied houses, and the modern beach resort of Bocagrande to the south, all high-rise hotels and Caribbean sand. A third district worth knowing is [Getsemaní](/southamerica/colombia/cartagena/getsemani), just outside the walls, which has become the city's most interesting neighbourhood for nightlife and street art.

The Spanish built [the walls](/southamerica/colombia/cartagena/city_walls) after Francis Drake sacked the city in 1586 and kept building them for two more centuries. They were never breached. Within the walls, the [Cathedral](/southamerica/colombia/cartagena/cathedral_de_cartagena) and [Palace of the Inquisition](/southamerica/colombia/cartagena/palace_of_the_inqu) face [Plaza de Bolívar](/southamerica/colombia/cartagena/plaza_de_bolivar), and the [Castillo de San Felipe](/southamerica/colombia/cartagena/castillo_de_san_fe) watches over the city from a hilltop. The [Convento de la Popa](/southamerica/colombia/cartagena/convento_de_la_popa) on the highest point offers the best panoramic views.

Beyond the city, the [Islas del Rosario](/southamerica/colombia/cartagena/islas_del_rosario) archipelago offers excellent snorkelling and deserted Caribbean beaches. [Playa Blanca](/southamerica/colombia/cartagena/playa_blanca) on Isla Barú is the finest beach within easy reach. The [Volcán del Totumo](/southamerica/colombia/cartagena/volcan_totumo) mud baths make for an odd but popular half-day trip.

From Cartagena you can travel on to [Cali](/southamerica/colombia/cali), [Medellín](/southamerica/colombia/medellin), [Santa Marta](/southamerica/colombia/santamarta) — from where you can visit the [Ciudad Perdida](/southamerica/colombia/ciudadperdida) of the Tayrona — or to [Bogotá](/southamerica/colombia/bogota).
