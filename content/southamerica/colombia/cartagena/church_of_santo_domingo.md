story: The Church of Santo Domingo is the oldest surviving church in Cartagena, begun in the late 16th century. Its bell tower has developed a significant lean over the centuries — the original tower collapsed and the replacement began to tilt as well. A persistent local legend explains this as the work of the devil, who knocked over the first tower in a rage when he found himself unable to interfere with the church's construction. Outside the entrance, a bronze sculpture of a reclining woman by Fernando Botero sits at the edge of the square.

The Iglesia de Santo Domingo stands on the plaza of the same name, one of the most pleasant squares in the old city. The church's leaning tower is a Cartagena landmark, and the Botero sculpture outside — a characteristically voluptuous bronze figure — is one of the most photographed objects in the city. The sculpture was a gift from Botero himself, Colombia's most famous visual artist, and sits in perfect contrast to the colonial stonework behind it.

The church interior is relatively plain but historically interesting, and the adjacent plaza is one of the better spots in the old city for an evening drink — the bars and restaurants facing the church tend to be a step above the more tourist-focused options near the clock tower.
