The walk to the Ciudad Perdida is four days, forty-odd kilometres, and about twelve hundred stone steps at the end of it. There is no road, no other way in, and no version of the trip you do alone: the law requires an authorised agency, and the trail passes through Kogi, Wiwa and Arhuaco land.

What surprises most people is that **the price is the same everywhere** — 1,860,000 Colombian pesos for the four-day trek at every operator below, each figure read off the company's own booking page. It covers the guide, all food, hammocks or bunks at the camps, and the mule that carries the bags. The ICANH park entry, a flat 75,000 pesos, is separate.

The three-day express, counter-intuitively, costs **more** — 2,200,000 — because the same distance is compressed and the logistics are worse. Take the four days.

So the choice is not about money. It is about who guides you: whether the company is indigenous-owned, how big the groups are, and whether anybody on the walk can explain what the Tayrona built and why they left it.
