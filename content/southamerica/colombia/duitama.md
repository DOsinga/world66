Duitama is a small city in the Boyacá highlands of Colombia, about three hours northeast of Bogotá. It sits at 2,500 metres in a green valley surrounded by mountains, and the climate is cool and pleasant year-round. The city is a practical base for exploring Boyacá — one of Colombia's most historically rich departments.

The main attraction nearby is the [Pueblito Boyacense](/southamerica/colombia/duitama/pueblito_boyacense), a replica village on the outskirts that recreates the architectural styles of different Boyacá towns. More interesting are the genuine colonial towns within easy reach: [Tibasosa](/southamerica/colombia/duitama/tibasosa), just to the south, has colonial balconies and a square that Simón Bolívar visited in 1820. [Villa de Leyva](/southamerica/colombia/villadeleiva), 90 minutes by road, is one of Colombia's finest colonial towns. The adventure town of San Gil — with rafting, paragliding, and caving — is two hours northeast.

Duitama itself is modest but has a lively market and good local food — particularly the Boyacá staples of cuchuco soup and arepas boyacenses. Cycling fans will know the city as the host of the 1995 UCI Road World Championships, where Abraham Olano won the road race and Jeannie Longo took the women's title.
