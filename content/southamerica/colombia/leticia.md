Leticia is Colombia's far southern Amazon city, cut off from the rest of the country by road but tied tightly to the river and to its neighbours: Tabatinga in Brazil and Santa Rosa in Peru. It feels less like an edge than a meeting point, with three countries, several Indigenous cultures, river trade, jungle guides, and motorbikes all sharing the same humid streets.

The town itself is not beautiful in a conventional way, but it has a strong identity. At dusk, [Parque Santander](/southamerica/colombia/leticia/parque_santander) fills with screeching parakeets. The [Ethnographic Museum](/southamerica/colombia/leticia/ethnographic_museum) gives a useful primer before visiting communities upriver. Just outside town, [Mundo Amazonico](/southamerica/colombia/leticia/mundo_amazonico) and the Yahuarcaca lakes offer easier ways into Amazon ecology.

Leticia is also the main Colombian base for longer Amazon trips: boats to Puerto Narino, jungle lodges, dolphin-watching lakes, and river journeys toward Peru or Brazil. Come prepared for heat, rain, mosquitoes, and slow logistics; that is part of the bargain.
