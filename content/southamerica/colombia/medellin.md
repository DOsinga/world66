Medellin is the most striking Colombian city to visit. Once the most dangerous city in the world — Pablo Escobar's Medellin cartel ran it through the late 1980s — it has transformed itself more dramatically than almost any other place on the continent. The cable cars that climb from the metro stations up the valley walls to the once-forgotten barrios are a civic symbol, but the real change is in the street: Paisas, as locals call themselves, have thrown themselves at the project of reinvention.

The city sits in a narrow valley in the Andes at 1,500 metres, which gives it the mild year-round climate that earned it the nickname **La Ciudad de la Eterna Primavera** — the City of Eternal Spring. Average temperatures hover around 22°C, and it rarely gets hot or cold. You will not need either sunscreen or a coat most days.

Downtown is dense and chaotic, anchored by [Plaza Botero](/southamerica/colombia/medellin/plaza_botero) — twenty-three bronze sculptures of deliberately rotund figures donated by the painter Fernando Botero, Medellin's most famous son. The [Museo de Antioquia](/southamerica/colombia/medellin/museo_de_antioquia) on the same square holds the bulk of his painted work, and the rest of [El Centro](/southamerica/colombia/medellin/el_centro) rewards a guided walk past the [Catedral Metropolitana](/southamerica/colombia/medellin/catedral_metropolitana) — one of the largest brick buildings in the world — and the needle-shaped [Edificio Coltejer](/southamerica/colombia/medellin/edificio_coltejer). North of there, in the reinvented barrio of [Comuna 13](/southamerica/colombia/medellin/comuna_13), the graffitied [outdoor escalators](/southamerica/colombia/medellin/escaleras_electricas_comuna13) that carry residents up the hillside are one of the best urban walks in Latin America.

[El Poblado](/southamerica/colombia/medellin/el_poblado) is where most travellers stay and drink — a leafy district full of cafes, good restaurants, and the bar-strip of [Parque Lleras](/southamerica/colombia/medellin/parque_lleras) and the more design-conscious [Provenza](/southamerica/colombia/medellin/provenza) a few blocks north. Longer-stay visitors increasingly choose flatter, more local-feeling [Laureles](/southamerica/colombia/medellin/laureles) instead. For a slower pace, take the [Metrocable](/southamerica/colombia/medellin/metrocable) up to [Santo Domingo Savio](/southamerica/colombia/medellin/santo_domingo_savio) for the views — Mazzanti's [Biblioteca España](/southamerica/colombia/medellin/biblioteca_espana) cubes still loom on the ridge — then on to [Parque Arví](/southamerica/colombia/medellin/parque_arvi), a cloud-forest reserve that starts at the top of the cable car line. The [Museo Casa de la Memoria](/southamerica/colombia/medellin/casa_de_la_memoria) is the place to confront the city's recent history; the [Feria de las Flores](/southamerica/colombia/medellin/feria_de_las_flores) in August is the most exuberant reason to time a visit.
