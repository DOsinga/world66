Salento is the most painted, most photographed, and most visited town in Colombia's coffee country — a small grid of streets on a ridge in the western Andes, painted in bright colours, surrounded by mountains and coffee farms, with the extraordinary Valle de Cocora just a short jeep ride away. It earns its popularity. The combination of handsome architecture, good food, and one of the most dramatic landscapes in Colombia within easy reach makes it hard to leave.

The town itself is compact: a central plaza with a church, several streets of colourful balconied houses, craft shops selling bamboo artesanías, and restaurants serving trout from the mountain streams and the local specialty bandeja paisa. The viewpoint at the end of Calle Real (the main street) gives a panorama across the coffee-growing valleys. But it is the Valle de Cocora — a 30-minute jeep ride from the plaza — that most visitors come for: a valley of improbably tall wax palms (Colombia's national tree, which can grow to 60 metres) rising from rolling green pastures, backed by cloud forest.

[Hiking in the Cocora Valley](/southamerica/colombia/salento/cocora_valley) is the headline activity, a 4–6 hour loop through the palm valley and cloud forest with a hummingbird house and a waterfall on the route. The small towns of the Quindío department — Armenia, Montenegro, Filandia — are within day-trip range, as are several traditional coffee farms that offer tours. Salento is particularly popular during Colombian holiday weekends; visiting mid-week avoids the crowds considerably.
