  Bolívar arrived at the quinta in December 1830, gravely ill and politically broken. He had resigned from the presidency of Gran Colombia eight months earlier after his vision of a unified South America collapsed under regional jealousies and his own increasingly autocratic rule. He died here on December 17, aged 47, reportedly saying: "if my death contributes to the end of partisanship and the consolidation of the union, I shall descend peacefully into my grave." The Quinta de San Pedro Alejandrino is where Simón Bolívar, liberator of five South American nations, spent his final weeks and died on December 17, 1830. The 17th-century hacienda sits in the foothills southeast of the city, surrounded by extensive botanical gardens with tropical trees, palms, and the original sugarcane fields that once made it a working estate.

The visit covers the hacienda house, where Bolívar's small room and simple bed are preserved, and the grand Museo Bolivariano — a purpose-built gallery holding paintings, sculptures, and ceremonial objects donated by the five countries he helped liberate. The room where he died is kept as a kind of secular shrine. The gardens are beautiful and peaceful, worth an hour of wandering in their own right. Open daily 9:30 to 16:30; budget at least two hours for a proper visit.
