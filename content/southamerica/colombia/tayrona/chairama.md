Chairama, known locally as Pueblito, is a pre-Columbian archaeological site within Tayrona National Park, a remnant of the settlements built by the Tayrona civilization that dominated this region before European contact. Stone terraces, paths, and circular house foundations are carved into a hillside in the jungle, accessible by a steep trail that branches off the main beach route.

The site is not excavated or displayed like a formal museum — you walk through the jungle and encounter stone structures emerging from undergrowth, sometimes with howler monkeys in the canopy overhead. Rangers are occasionally present to provide explanation. The site is smaller than the famous Ciudad Perdida higher in the mountains, but it is reachable in a day hike and gives a tangible sense of how densely this coastline was inhabited before the conquest. The trail from Cabo San Juan to Chairama and out to the western gate at Calabazo is a popular two-day route.
