---
title: "Whitewater Rafting on the Río Negro"
type: poi
---

The Río Negro is the reason Tobia is on the map. The river cuts a dramatic canyon through the cordillera on its way toward the Magdalena valley, and the section near the village holds Class III and Class IV rapids — challenging enough to be genuinely exciting, manageable enough that no prior experience is needed. The rapids have names, character and a reliable rhythm: pools of flat water give way to narrow chutes and wave trains, the canyon walls crowd in overhead, and the warm river temperature (warm by Colombian highland standards, at least) makes a capsize feel more like a bonus than a disaster.

Half-day trips are the most common option and typically cover the most technical section of river, lasting around two to three hours on the water. Full-day packages combine a longer run with lunch at one of the camp restaurants and often include a second activity such as zip lines or climbing. All operators provide helmets, life jackets and a safety briefing before launch; guides ride the lead and sweep boats and are generally experienced with the specific quirks of this stretch of river.

The dry season months — roughly December to February and July to August — bring lower, cleaner water and faster current; the rainy season raises the river and can push some rapids toward the upper end of Class IV. Most operators run year-round, adjusting the section of river to match current conditions. Weekends fill up fast, particularly in July and during the Christmas and Easter holiday periods, so booking a day or two in advance is wise.
