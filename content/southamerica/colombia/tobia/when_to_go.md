---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

Tobia sits at about 900 metres above sea level in a river canyon that enjoys a significantly drier, warmer climate than Bogotá. The best time to visit is during Colombia's dry seasons: roughly December to February and June to August. During these months the Río Negro runs at lower, cleaner levels, the rapids are at their most technically consistent, and the days are reliably sunny and warm. July in particular draws large crowds, as Bogotanos take their mid-year holidays and the canyon fills with groups.

The rainy seasons — March to May and September to November — bring heavier rainfall to the cordillera above, which raises river levels and can push some rapids beyond what operators consider safe for general groups. Responsible operators adjust the sections they use or close temporarily during high-water events. Visits are still possible in the rainy season, but it is worth checking current river conditions with your operator before travelling.

The most important practical consideration is that Tobia is a weekend destination. Operators run full schedules on Saturdays and Sundays and during national holiday periods; weekday availability is much thinner and some smaller camps effectively close during the week. If you want a quieter experience with more personalised attention, a weekday visit during a dry-season month is the ideal combination — if you can arrange it.
