The Hunzahúa Well — Pozo de Hunzahúa in Spanish — is a circular sunken pit on the northern edge of central Tunja, surrounded by a small park and named for the legend of the Muisca chief whose sister-lover supposedly created it by spilling chicha as she fled the city. The Muisca treated the well as sacred and it was a pilgrimage site within the confederation.

It is not a spectacular site visually — the well itself is now mostly silted up and the surrounding park is unremarkable — but it carries a real story, and the panels on the Muisca origin myth are interesting. Best combined with a visit to the Cojines del Zaque a few blocks south; together the two sites give some sense of what was here before the Spanish founded their colonial city on top.
