Valledupar is the birthplace and capital of vallenato, the accordion-driven folk music that is to Colombia's Caribbean coast what tango is to Buenos Aires. Every April the city hosts the [Festival de la Leyenda Vallenata](/southamerica/colombia/valledupar/festival_vallenata), a multi-day competition that draws musicians and crowds from across the country. Even outside the festival, vallenato is everywhere — in the plazas, in the bars, on the bus.

The city sits in a valley between the Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta and the Serranía del Perijá, giving it a hot but breezy climate and access to river swimming spots in the foothills. The Río Guatapurí runs through town, and locals gather at the [Balneario Hurtado](/southamerica/colombia/valledupar/balneario_hurtado) to cool off. The [Plaza de los Fundadores](/southamerica/colombia/valledupar/plaza_de_los_fundadores) is where life happens in the evening, and the [Casa Museo del Acordeon](/southamerica/colombia/valledupar/museo_del_acordeon) tells the story of how a German instrument became the heart of a Colombian culture. Valledupar sees far fewer tourists than Cartagena or Santa Marta, which is part of its appeal — this is Colombian life without the tourist infrastructure.
