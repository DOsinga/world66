---
title: "Things to Do"
type: section
---

Zipaquirá's main attraction — and for most visitors the only reason to come — is the [Catedral de Sal](/southamerica/colombia/zipaquira/catedral_de_sal), an underground cathedral carved from a working salt mine, 180 metres below the surface. It is genuinely remarkable and well worth the trip from [Bogotá](/southamerica/colombia/bogota).

The colonial town itself has a pleasant main square and some historic buildings worth an hour of wandering after visiting the cathedral. The [Catedral Diocesana](/southamerica/colombia/zipaquira/catedral_diocesana) on the Plaza de los Comuneros is an attractive 16th-century colonial church; the square itself, surrounded by whitewashed buildings with terracotta roofs, is one of the better preserved colonial plazas in Cundinamarca. The **Casa de los Quevedo-Zornoza** on the plaza houses a small history museum.

The town also has markets selling local products — Zipaquirá is known for its chicharrón and local sausages — and a scattering of restaurants around the main square where you can eat well and cheaply before or after the cathedral visit.

Nearby, the [Tren de la Sabana](/southamerica/colombia/zipaquira/tren_de_la_sabana) tourist train runs from Bogotá on weekends and is a pleasant alternative to the bus if you want the journey to feel like an occasion.
