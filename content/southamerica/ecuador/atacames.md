Atacames is a lively beach town on Ecuador's northern Pacific coast, spread along a wide stretch of fine grey sand. This is where Ecuadorians from the highlands come to let loose — a beachside playground of thatched-roof bars, ceviche stalls, and thumping music rather than a resort in any polished sense. Foreign visitors are the exception rather than the norm.

The [main beach](/southamerica/ecuador/atacames/playa_de_atacames) runs for nearly seven kilometres and the atmosphere shifts through the day: quiet mornings give way to a buzzing afternoon scene as vendors work the sand, and the evenings bring open-air dancing and cocktails served in coconut shells along the [beachfront bar strip](/southamerica/ecuador/atacames/marimba_bars). On weekends and holidays it gets packed and loud; midweek is mellower. The water is warm year-round and the sunsets are reliable. Atacames is not refined, but it is fun, cheap, and authentically Ecuadorian.

For quieter options, the nearby fishing village of [Súa](/southamerica/ecuador/atacames/sua) sits 8 km south in a sheltered bay with birds and fresh fish, and the beach settlement of [Same](/southamerica/ecuador/atacames/same) is another few kilometres beyond. [Tonsupa](/southamerica/ecuador/atacames/playa_de_tonsupa), just north of town, is slightly more residential and calmer. The [Río Atacames estuary](/southamerica/ecuador/atacames/estuario_rio_atacames) at the eastern end of the beach is worth a short boat tour for birdwatchers.
