Bahia de Caraquez sits on a narrow peninsula where the Rio Chone meets the Pacific, on Ecuador's central coast. It bills itself as an "eco-city" and has a cleaner, quieter feel than most Ecuadorian coastal towns — palm-lined streets, a [waterfront malecon](/southamerica/ecuador/bahiadecaraquez/malecon), and a relaxed pace. The town was badly damaged by an earthquake in 1998 and a subsequent El Niño flood, and the rebuilding effort led to the eco-city initiative.

The main reason to visit is the surrounding area. [Chirije](/southamerica/ecuador/bahiadecaraquez/chirije), about 30 kilometres south along the coast, is an archaeological and ecological reserve where pre-Columbian Bahia culture sites sit amid 238 hectares of dry tropical forest and unspoiled beaches. The Bahia culture (500 BC to AD 500) were seafaring merchants who traded shells and ornaments as far as Mexico and Chile. [Isla Corazón](/southamerica/ecuador/bahiadecaraquez/isla_corazon), a mangrove island in the estuary, is home to one of Ecuador's largest frigate bird colonies and can be visited by boat. The waters around Bahia also offer [dolphin watching](/southamerica/ecuador/bahiadecaraquez/dolphin_watching) year-round and humpback whales from June to October.
