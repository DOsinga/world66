Calderón is a small town on the northern outskirts of [Quito](/southamerica/ecuador/quito), famous throughout Ecuador for its [bread dough figurines](/southamerica/ecuador/calderon/bread_dough_figuri) — a craft tradition that goes back centuries and is still very much alive. The figures are made by hand from a dough of flour, salt, and glue, then painted in vivid colours and varnished so they last indefinitely. You will find them everywhere: nativity scenes, miniature musicians, animals, folkloric characters.

The town itself is pleasant and unpretentious, with an [attractive main square](/southamerica/ecuador/calderon/iglesia_de_calderon) and a church worth a look. Most travelers come on a half-day trip from Quito, browse the [workshops](/southamerica/ecuador/calderon/mazapan_workshops) and souvenir shops, and head back — it is easy to do independently on the public buses. If you have time, wandering off the main strip reveals the quieter residential streets where many of the craftspeople work, and the [municipal cemetery](/southamerica/ecuador/calderon/calderon_cemetery) on the edge of town is one of the more striking examples of Andean Day-of-the-Dead tradition near Quito.
