The Andean cock-of-the-rock is one of South America's most spectacular birds: the male a flaming orange-red with a disc-shaped crest that covers his entire head and beak. Around Mindo, several lodges and tour operators offer dawn visits to known lek sites — locations where males gather to display, sometimes in groups of a dozen or more. The Paz Bird Sanctuary operates one of the more accessible, with a hide that allows close observation without disturbing the birds.

Visits require an early start (4–5am) to reach the lek before dawn. The performance runs for about 20–30 minutes as the light comes up — males bob, spread their wings, chase each other, and call raucously while females observe from the canopy. When they disperse into the forest, it is sudden and complete. The combination of the display itself and the misty cloud forest setting at first light makes this one of Ecuador's better wildlife experiences. Most guesthouses in Mindo can arrange transport and guiding; it's worth booking the evening before, as good guides fill up.
