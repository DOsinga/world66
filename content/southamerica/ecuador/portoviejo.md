Portoviejo is the capital of Manabí province on Ecuador's central Pacific coast — one of the oldest cities in the country, founded in 1535. It sits about 30 km inland from the sea, a hot and purposeful commercial city that most travellers pass through on their way to the coast without stopping. That is a mistake, at least for a night.

The city was badly damaged in the 2016 earthquake (magnitude 7.8, over 300 dead across Manabí) and has rebuilt itself with ambition. The centrepiece of that effort is Parque Las Vegas, a 10-hectare linear park along the Portoviejo River that replaced rubble with riverside promenades, cycle paths and shade trees. The city centre has been redesigned to put pedestrians first, and the results are genuinely pleasant to walk around.

What makes Portoviejo worth your time is the food. In 2019 UNESCO named it a Creative City of Gastronomy — the first Ecuadorian city to receive that designation. Manabí cuisine is Ecuador's most distinctive coastal cooking: corviche (fried green plantain balls filled with fish and peanuts), viche (a rich peanut and fish soup), green empanadas, and ceviche made with local shellfish. The ingredients are peanuts, plantains, corn, yucca and fresh fish, and the cooking has deep pre-Columbian roots. The city's restaurants and market stalls take this heritage seriously.

Nearby, the village of Montecristi — 40 minutes west — is where the Panama hat was actually invented, woven by Manabí artisans whose work was mistakenly credited to Panama when the hats passed through that country on their way to Europe. The nearby beaches at Crucita and San Jacinto are quiet and cheap. Portoviejo makes a better base for the region than it gets credit for, and a night here is well rewarded if you eat well and walk the river park.
