Quito is one of the highest capital cities in the world, at 2,850 metres above sea level, and the first city to be declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site (in 1978, jointly with Kraków). Its [Centro Histórico](/southamerica/ecuador/quito/centro_historico) is one of the best-preserved colonial urban centres in the Americas — forty churches, seventeen plazas, and streets that have changed little since the 16th century. The backdrop is extraordinary: the city fills a long, narrow valley with the active Pichincha volcano immediately to the west and a ring of snowcapped peaks visible on clear days.

Quito is not the biggest city in Ecuador (that is [Guayaquil](/southamerica/ecuador/guayaquil)) but it is the main international entry point and transport hub. It is also genuinely one of the best cities in South America to spend a week: the combination of the colonial city, the modern neighbourhoods, the altitude (which gives it a permanently spring-like climate), and the ease of day trips to remarkable nearby landscapes makes it an unusually complete destination.

The [Historic Centre](/southamerica/ecuador/quito/centro_historico) is where most travellers spend most of their time — wandering between the [Plaza Grande](/southamerica/ecuador/quito/plaza_grande), the [Church of La Compañía](/southamerica/ecuador/quito/church_la_compania), the vast [San Francisco](/southamerica/ecuador/quito/basilica_san_francisco) complex, and dozens of smaller churches and museums. The [Basílica del Voto Nacional](/southamerica/ecuador/quito/basilica_del_voto_nacional) tower climb is essential. For views, [El Panecillo](/southamerica/ecuador/quito/el_panecillo) hill and the [Teleférico](/southamerica/ecuador/quito/teleferico) cable car are the two options; the Teleférico is more dramatic. Day trips to [Mindo](/southamerica/ecuador/quito/mindo_day_trip) cloud forest and the [Mitad del Mundo](/southamerica/ecuador/quito/mitad_del_mundo) equatorial monument round out the standard visit.
