**Papillon** by Henri Charrière (1969) — The most famous account of the penal colony, written by a convict who claimed to have escaped multiple times. Compelling reading, though historians have since questioned how much of it is memoir and how much is embellishment. Read it for the atmosphere rather than the facts.

**Devil's Island: Colony of the Damned** by Alexander Miles — A rigorous history of the penal colony from its founding to its closure, drawing on French colonial records and prisoner testimonies. More reliable than Papillon as a guide to what life on the islands was actually like.

**The Dreyfus Affair** by Piers Paul Read (2012) — The full story of Alfred Dreyfus, the Jewish army officer falsely convicted of treason and exiled to Devil's Island, whose case became a defining moment in French political history. Essential background for understanding the most famous prisoner the islands held.

**South America on a Shoestring** (Lonely Planet) or a comparable French Guiana travel guide — For practical context on getting to and around French Guiana more broadly, including Cayenne, Kourou, and the interior.
