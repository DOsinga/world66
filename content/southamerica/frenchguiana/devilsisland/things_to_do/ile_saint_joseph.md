The most atmospheric of the islands, Ile Saint-Joseph is where the solitary confinement cells — the cachots — are found. Prisoners were held here in total silence; even the guards wore rubber-soled shoes. Today the cells are partially flooded and smothered in jungle growth, roots cracking through the stone and ferns spilling through the barred windows. The effect is genuinely eerie.

A small boat runs between Ile Royale and Ile Saint-Joseph; the fare is paid separately. The crossing is short but can be choppy. Plan to spend at least an hour or two exploring the overgrown paths and ruins before returning for the afternoon boat back to Kourou.
