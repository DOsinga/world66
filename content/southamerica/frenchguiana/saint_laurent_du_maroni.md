Saint-Laurent-du-Maroni is French Guiana's second town and its most interesting one. It was built as the administrative capital of the penal colony: convicts sentenced in France were landed here, processed at the Camp de la Transportation, and sent on to the forest camps or to the [Îles du Salut](/southamerica/frenchguiana/devilsisland). The camp still stands in the middle of town, its cell blocks and solitary-confinement wing open on guided tours, with Papillon's supposed cell among the graffiti.

The rest of the old colonial quarter has the wide streets and shuttered administrative buildings of a place designed by the prison service, and it is slowly being restored. Beyond it, the town is a meeting point of peoples: Ndyuka and Aluku Maroons, descendants of Africans who escaped Dutch plantations and built their own societies upriver; Kali'na and other Amerindians; Hmong farmers; and French officials.

The Maroni is the border. Pirogues cross constantly to Albina in Suriname, and going upriver — to the rapids, the Maroon villages and eventually [Maripasoula](/southamerica/frenchguiana/maripasoula) — is the main reason travellers come. It is about three hours from [Cayenne](/southamerica/frenchguiana/cayenne) by road.
