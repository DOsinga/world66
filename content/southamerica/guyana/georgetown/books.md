---
title: "Books"
type: section
---

**The Ventriloquist's Tale** by Pauline Melville — A multi-generational novel set partly in Georgetown and partly in the Rupununi savanna. Melville's prose moves between the colonial city and the indigenous interior, and her sense of Guyana's strangeness — a country at once Caribbean, South American, and Amazonian — is unmatched.

**A House for Mr Biswas** by V.S. Naipaul — Set in Trinidad, but indispensable for understanding the Indo-Caribbean world that shaped large parts of Georgetown's culture. Naipaul's portrait of an Indo-Trinidadian man's struggle for identity and a home of his own resonates strongly with Indo-Guyanese experience.

**Wilson Harris, The Palace of the Peacock** — The first novel of one of the great Guyanese writers, a dense, dreamlike journey up a river into the interior. It is not an easy read, but no novel captures the Guyanese landscape and its colonial inheritance more powerfully.

**The Sly Company of People Who Care** by Rahul Bhattacharya — An Indian writer's first novel about a year spent drifting through contemporary Guyana, from Georgetown to the interior. Sharp, funny, observant, and the best recent foreign account of the country.

**Walter Rodney, A Groundings with My Brothers** — The collected lectures of the Guyanese historian and political activist assassinated in Georgetown in 1980. Essential for understanding the radical politics that shaped — and continues to shape — modern Guyanese society.
