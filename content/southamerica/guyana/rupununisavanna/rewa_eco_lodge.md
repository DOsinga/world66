Built in 2005 by the 361 people of Rewa village — Macushi, Patamona, Wapishana and Waiwai — with Conservation International's help, explicitly to replace hunting income with tourism. It sits where the Rewa meets the Rupununi.

It is world-known among fly fishermen for catch-and-release giant arapaima, a fish that can pass three metres and has to be landed by hand in shallow water; peacock bass too. For everyone else there is wildlife tracking, forest trails and some of the best birding in the Rupununi.

Rates are not published on the site — international outfitters package a week at around USD 4,500 — so ask directly. WhatsApp is the working channel.
