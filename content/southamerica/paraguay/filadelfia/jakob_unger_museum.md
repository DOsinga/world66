They had been promised land in Paraguay's Chaco — but on arrival found a near-waterless thornscrub where the first wells took years to dig and where infant mortality in the first decade ran above one in three."

A pair of small museums on the south side of the town centre that tell the story of the Mennonite colonisation of the Chaco. The Colony House Museum is a wooden settler's house preserved from the early years of the colony — sparse, austere, with a kitchen, a Plattdeutsch family Bible, and the agricultural tools the first arrivals had to invent uses for in this completely unfamiliar landscape.

The Jakob Unger natural-history museum next door, named after the Mennonite naturalist who founded it in 1955, has a substantial collection of mounted Chaco fauna, indigenous artefacts, and exhibits on local plants. Together the two museums take an hour and a half and are by far the best introduction you can get to this odd corner of the world.
