Museo Industrial sits on the Fernheim cooperative's industrial grounds and tells a very Filadelfia story: how Mennonite settlers turned a harsh Chaco environment into a working agricultural economy. The displays focus on machinery, generators, refinery equipment, and peanut oil production rather than polished museum theatrics, which is exactly the point.

It is best visited alongside Filadelfia's other Mennonite history stops, because the machines make more sense when you understand how isolated the early colony was. Expect a modest, locally run museum rather than a big national institution, but for travellers interested in the Paraguayan Chaco it gives useful texture to the town's practical, cooperative identity.
