Paraguay's rivers are brown and its lakes are green. Laguna Blanca is neither: 147 hectares of spring-fed water lying on a bed of white calcified sand, clear enough to see fish at seven metres. People come here to dive, which is not a sentence anyone expects to write about a landlocked country with no mountains.

The lake sits on private land in Santa Bárbara district, San Pedro, about 270 km north of Asunción off Ruta 3 and 28 km east of Santa Rosa del Aguaray. It has been run as **Rancho Laguna Blanca** since 1997 and was formally declared a protected area under private ownership in 2010, covering 804 hectares including the surrounding cerrado.

That cerrado is the second reason to come. This is the southernmost patch of the Brazilian cerrado ecosystem and it holds species that occur nowhere else in Paraguay — 283 bird species have been recorded, and the sand-dune lizards and frogs here have produced a run of new-species descriptions. Herpetologists know this place.

There is camping, rustic lodging and a small restaurant; bring what you need. Weekends in January it is a Paraguayan holiday spot with music and beer; midweek out of season it is empty. The road in is unpaved and rain makes it slow. Bring a mask even if you do not dive.
