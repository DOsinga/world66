The lodge inside Mbaracayú began as the park rangers' houses, which occasionally put up visiting researchers. Demand grew with the reserve's reputation, the foundation decided to open it properly, and the cabins were remodelled into what is now three buildings with twelve rooms, plus a camping area and a kitchen that cooks with what is grown nearby.

From the lodge the reserve opens up: marked trails, canoe trips on the Jejuí headwaters, visits to the waterfalls — Salto Karapá is the one SENATUR photographs, though nobody publishes where it is — and some climbing. This is the base for the 440 bird species and 62 Atlantic-forest endemics the foundation counts, and there is nowhere else to sleep inside the boundary.

It doubles as the training hotel for the Centro Educativo Mbaracayú, the foundation's boarding school for rural girls, whose students do their hospitality placements here under the permanent staff. That is worth knowing before you arrive: the service is part of somebody's education.

Two numbers answer for this place. The foundation's Asunción office, `+595 985 191 000`, is the one with a WhatsApp and the one the foundation itself puts on the reserve page. SENATUR publishes a local mobile, `+595 985 854 152`, for the Mbaracayú end. Try Asunción first.
