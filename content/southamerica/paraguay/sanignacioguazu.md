San Ignacio Guazú was the first of them. Founded in 1609 by Marcial de Lorenzana and Francisco de San Martín, it is where the Jesuit-Guaraní experiment started, and every other mission in Paraguay was founded out of it. There is no ruin: the town simply carried on living in and around the mission, and the plan of the reduction is still legible in the street grid if you know to look.

What there is instead is the **Museo Diocesano de Arte Jesuítico-Guaraní**, housed in the surviving Casa de los Indios on the plaza — one long colonial building with a deep veranda. Inside are several hundred carved and polychromed wooden figures made by Guaraní sculptors in the 17th and 18th centuries, arranged across four rooms. It is the best collection of Hispano-Guaraní baroque in the country, and better than anything on display at Trinidad or Jesús, where the sculpture mostly did not survive. The town calls itself the capital of the style and has a fair claim.

The parish church is a 1919–1932 rebuild, neo-Romanesque, but it keeps the mission-era imagery inside.

San Ignacio is 226 km south of Asunción on Ruta 1, and every Encarnación bus stops. It is the natural first stop on a mission circuit done from the north, and 25 km from [Santa María de Fe](#17-santa-maría-de-fe--a-village-with-a-great-museum-and-howler-monkeys-in-the-square). An hour in the museum, lunch on the plaza, and on.
