Santaní — officially San Estanislao — is a small market town in the San Pedro department, a few hours northeast of [Asunción](/southamerica/paraguay/asuncion) in one of Paraguay's poorest and most rural provinces. If you are looking for monuments and museums, this is not the place. But if you want to see small-town Paraguayan life without any tourist infrastructure or pretense, Santaní delivers exactly that.

The town revolves around its [central plaza](/southamerica/paraguay/santan/plaza_san_estanislao) and the modest [Iglesia San Estanislao](/southamerica/paraguay/santan/iglesia_san_estanislao). Life moves slowly here: motorcycles and bicycles outnumber cars, and the social scene centers on "la playa" — the local swimming and gathering spot along the [Jejuí river](/southamerica/paraguay/santan/rio_jejui_river) — and pickup games of "voli," the Paraguayan hands-free volleyball played with astonishing skill. The [Mercado Central](/southamerica/paraguay/santan/mercado_central) provides the most vivid snapshot of agricultural San Pedro: yerba mate sold in bulk, hand-woven baskets, and vendors with loud opinions. The surrounding countryside is flat agricultural land, mostly cattle ranches and small farms growing yerba mate, cotton, and cassava.

Santaní is best understood as a window into rural Paraguay rather than a destination in itself. The people are welcoming, cold beer and fresh tortillas are cheap and plentiful, and the pace of life is genuinely unhurried. If you are passing through on your way to the Chaco or toward the Brazilian border, it makes a reasonable stop to stretch your legs and eat something home-cooked.
