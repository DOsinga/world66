The Ybytyruzú is a 40 km ridge of forested sandstone hills east of Villarrica, protected since 1990 as a 30,000-hectare managed-resources reserve. In a country that is essentially flat, this counts as mountains, and Paraguayans treat it accordingly.

**Cerro Tres Kandú**, 842 m, is the highest point in Paraguay. The walk up from the Melgarejo side takes two to three hours and is steep rather than technical; local guides run it and are worth taking, because the trail forks repeatedly through private land. The view from the top is most of Guairá.

**Salto Suizo** is the country's highest waterfall at 62 m, dropping into a bamboo-choked gorge about 9 km from Colonia Independencia down a track that is fine in the dry and unpleasant otherwise. Smaller falls and swimming pools are scattered along the Takuara stream.

**Colonia Independencia** is the base most people use: a German-settled village with vineyards, a winery or two, beer and pork on the menus, and the only real tourist infrastructure in the range. The combination — Guaraní place names, German food, subtropical bamboo forest — is one of the odder things in Paraguay and entirely characteristic of it.

Go in the dry season for the tracks; go after rain for the waterfall. You cannot have both.
