  The Aypate complex controlled the Inca road that linked the coast with the highland centres of Tumipampa and Cajamarca. Its construction required moving stone blocks weighing several tonnes to a ridge at nearly 3,000 metres — all without wheeled transport or draft animals larger than a llama.

A complex of 15th-century Inca ruins — a ceremonial and administrative centre — roughly 40 km out of Ayabaca, built from the same grey granite blocks as Machu Picchu. The site is rarely visited: getting there means a bumpy drive followed by a twenty-minute walk up part of the original Inca road. At the trailhead, a local family offers very basic lodging for anyone who wants to stay overnight. Bring water and warm clothes — at 2,900 metres the afternoon weather turns fast.

The ruins sit on a ridge with sweeping views into the valleys below, and the scale of the stonework makes it easy to imagine this as a functioning administrative hub connected to the wider Inca road system. A guide from the [Museo de Ayabaca](/southamerica/peru/ayabaca/museo_de_ayavaca) in town can help arrange transport and knows the site well. Going without local knowledge is possible but you will miss most of the context.
