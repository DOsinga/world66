The sarcophagi of Karajía are eight clay coffins shaped like standing human figures, perched on a narrow limestone ledge on a cliff face above the Utcubamba valley. The Chachapoyas people placed their elite dead in these extraordinary clay structures — some 2.5 metres tall with elongated heads — in a location accessible only by rope, seemingly as much to impress the living as to protect the dead.

The viewing point is reached by a 45-minute walk from the village of Cruzpata, along a path that crosses the valley and climbs through cloud forest. The figures are clearly visible from the viewing platform; binoculars help with detail. The skulls of additional individuals are visible in niches beside the main figures. The site combines well with a visit to the Gocta Falls on the same day trip.
