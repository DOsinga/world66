A 90-minute drive east of Tarapoto on the banks of the Huallaga river, Chazuta is a small, unhurried riverine town that earns its place on the itinerary for one thing above all: its **ceramics**. Local women have been shaping black-fired earthenware — pots, bowls, and figurines decorated with thin geometric designs in natural pigments — for generations. The Wasichay association of women potters runs a workshop in the village where you can watch the full process from raw clay to finished piece, and buy directly at fair prices.

Chazuta also marks a geographic threshold. This is where the upper Huallaga ends and the lower river begins: the water broadens here into the navigable lowland Amazon system that carries on down to Yurimaguas and eventually to the main river. A handful of operators run river trips from the waterfront, and simple guesthouses in town make it easy to stay overnight if you want an early start on the water. Combis run from Tarapoto's terminal three or four times a day, making Chazuta a straightforward half-day or full-day excursion.

The surrounding hills and coffee farms add a quiet backdrop to the town. The area around Chazuta has grown cacao and coffee for decades, and local cooperatives will sometimes allow visits if arranged in advance through a guide in Tarapoto. Come on a weekday morning when the pottery workshops are active and the market near the waterfront is at its liveliest.
