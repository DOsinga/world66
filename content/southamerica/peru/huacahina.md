Huacachina is an oasis in the desert, one of the most visually striking places in Peru. A small lagoon surrounded by palm trees sits entirely encircled by enormous sand dunes that rise up to 100 metres above the water's edge. The village is small — a few streets and guesthouses clustered around the lake — and the whole settlement lies just 4 kilometres from the city of [Ica](/southamerica/peru/ica), which is the practical base for the area.

The dunes are the main attraction. Buggy tours charge around the steep slopes, dropping passengers at the crests for sandboarding down the faces. It is genuinely exhilarating and takes about two hours. Tours run to late afternoon, finishing around sunset when the dunes glow orange over the water — one of the more memorable scenes in South America.

The lagoon itself is pleasant to walk around. A short stroll from the water's edge gives views back over the oasis with its backdrop of dunes. Huacachina makes an easy stop between Lima and Cusco or Arequipa; from Ica a taxi costs only a few soles.
