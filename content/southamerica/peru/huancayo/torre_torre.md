Just over a kilometre east of the city centre, Torre Torre is one of the Mantaro Valley's strangest sights. Wind and rain have carved a hillside of soft volcanic material into clusters of tall earth columns, some reaching 30 metres high. They look more like a Cappadocian landscape than the Peruvian highlands. In the late afternoon the light turns the clay orange and the effect is striking.

The walk from central Huancayo takes about twenty minutes on foot, or a short taxi ride. A rough path winds between the columns and up to a viewpoint with panoramic views over the valley and the city below. There is nothing formal here — no entrance fee, no visitor centre — which makes it feel like a genuine local discovery rather than a managed attraction. Follow the road east past the Estadio Huancayo and look for the signs pointing uphill.
