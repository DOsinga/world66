Huánuco sits in a fertile valley on the eastern slopes of the Andes at around 1,800 metres, giving it a mild, pleasant climate that locals proudly call "the best in the world." The city is the capital of the Huánuco region and serves as a market town for the surrounding agricultural areas, which produce coffee, cacao, and tropical fruit. It is not on most tourist itineraries, which means you get an authentic Peruvian highland city without the crowds.

The main draw for visitors is [Kotosh](/southamerica/peru/huanuco/kotosh), an archaeological site about five kilometres outside the city that contains the Templo de las Manos Cruzadas — a temple with crossed-hands reliefs dating back some 4,000 years, making it one of the oldest known structures in the Americas. The site is modest in scale but historically significant. In town, the [Plaza de Armas](/southamerica/peru/huanuco/plaza_de_armas) is attractive and lively, and the [Cathedral of the Lord of Burgos](/southamerica/peru/huanuco/catedral_del_senor_de_burgos) on the square has a fine colonial interior. Huánuco is also a useful staging point for trips into the [Carpish cloud forest](/southamerica/peru/huanuco/carpish_mountains) to the east, one of the most biodiverse zones in Peru.
