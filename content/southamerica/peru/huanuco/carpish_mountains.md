East of Huánuco, the road to Tingo María climbs through the Cordillera Carpish and into one of Peru's most biodiverse cloud forest zones. The Carpish mountains sit at the transition between the dry Andean highlands and the humid Amazon basin, creating the misty, moss-draped forest environment that birders and botanists travel across the world to see. The area is a hotspot for endemic bird species, orchids, and bromeliads, with many species found nowhere else on earth.

The most accessible introduction is the drive itself — the road winds through extraordinary scenery with frequent stops possible at forest-edge viewpoints. For more serious birding, several community-run birding lodges operate in the villages along the Carpish pass road, offering guided walks at dawn when bird activity is highest. This is not a formal national park and infrastructure is minimal, but the natural richness is outstanding. The area around Acomayo, about 40 kilometres from Huánuco, is particularly well-regarded among birders. The road can be affected by landslides in the wet season; check conditions locally before setting out.
