  The Huayhuash circuit passes through the territory of several rural communities who charge entrance fees at their checkpoints. The trek is one of the few in the Andes where you pay several separate entry fees to different villages along the way.

The Huayhuash Circuit is widely considered one of the ten greatest treks in the world. The 12–14 day route circles the Cordillera Huayhuash, a compact massif south of Huaraz that contains six peaks above 6,000 metres in a relatively small area. The circuit passes through extremely remote country, crossing seven high passes above 4,500 metres, and requires full camping and food self-sufficiency.

Unlike the Santa Cruz trek, the Huayhuash Circuit sees far fewer visitors and demands a higher level of fitness and mountain experience. The payoff is jaw-dropping scenery: the first view of Siula Grande from the Siula pass, the reflection of Jirishanca in Lago Jahuacocha, and the knife-edge ridges at Carhuac pass are all genuinely extraordinary. The circuit can be done in either direction; most trekkers go anticlockwise starting from Chiquian or Llamac, a three- to four-hour bus ride from Huaraz. Hiring an arriero and cook is strongly recommended.
