---
title: "Bars and Cafes"
type: section
---

Lima's bar scene tracks its restaurant scene — a handful of internationally famous places, a lot of competent middle ground, and one classic drink that defines them all: the **pisco sour**, made with the unaged grape brandy that is Peru's spirit. Where to drink it depends on what you want from the evening. For the traditional version, head to the **Gran Hotel Bolívar** on Plaza San Martín or the wood-panelled **Bar Cordano** across from the Government Palace; both have been serving since the early 1900s, both cost about a third of what Miraflores charges. For modern cocktail bartending, the World's 50 Best-ranked **Carnaval** in San Isidro is the technical leader, and **Ayahuasca** in a converted Barranco mansion is the busy hipster favourite.

Barranco is the obvious district for an evening bar crawl: most of the cocktail bars, music venues, and after-hours spots cluster within a few blocks of the **Puente de los Suspiros**. Miraflores leans more toward restaurant bars and rooftop terraces; San Isidro is sleeker and quieter. For wine, the bistros around Calle Sáenz Peña in Barranco have the deepest South American lists.
