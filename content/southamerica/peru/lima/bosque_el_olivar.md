In the middle of the San Isidro business district, **El Bosque El Olivar** is a 23-hectare grove of olive trees descended from Spanish saplings planted in 1560. Once a working estate that produced the olive oil for colonial Lima, it became a public park in 1959 and is now one of the most pleasant open spaces in the city.

Footpaths wind between gnarled, sometimes massive trunks under a high canopy. Joggers and dog walkers use it from dawn; in the evening the park fills with families and couples. The **Iglesia de la Virgen del Pilar** at the southern edge is one of San Isidro's small landmarks. Free, open all day, ringed by some of the most expensive houses in Lima.
