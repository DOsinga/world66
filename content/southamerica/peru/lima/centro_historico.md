The **Centro Histórico** — UNESCO World Heritage since 1991 — is the colonial city of Lima as the Viceroyalty left it, packed into about 30 city blocks around the **Plaza Mayor**. Crumbling Baroque churches, *casonas* (colonial mansions) with carved wooden balconies, narrow streets of *azulejo*-tiled facades, and a working market sit alongside heavy traffic and street vendors.

Highlights are best seen on foot in a morning: the Plaza Mayor, the **Cathedral**, the catacombs of **San Francisco**, the **Casa de Aliaga** (continuously inhabited since 1535), and the elegant pedestrian street **Jirón de la Unión** running south. Pickpockets are a real risk in busy areas; keep valuables out of sight, especially around bus stations and at the Mercado Central.
