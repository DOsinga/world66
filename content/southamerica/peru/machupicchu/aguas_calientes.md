Aguas Calientes (officially Machupicchu Pueblo) is the only place to sleep near Machu Picchu. Situated at the bottom of the Urubamba gorge, hemmed in by mountains on all sides, it is accessible only by train or on foot — there is no road. The village exists almost entirely to serve visitors to the ruins above, and it does so with an impressive range of restaurants, guesthouses, and outfitters along the pedestrianised Avenida Pachacutec.

The hot springs that give the town its name — "hot waters" — are at the upper end of the main street: a series of outdoor thermal pools fed by natural geothermal springs, open daily and popular in the evenings after a long day at the ruins. They are not spectacular by hot spring standards but are exactly what tired legs need. Towels and lockers can be rented on site.

The **Machu Picchu Museum** (Museo de Sitio Manuel Chávez Ballón) is a small but well-presented introduction to the history and archaeology of the site, located near the shuttle bus terminal. It is worth visiting either before or after the ruins. The surrounding town is otherwise functional rather than characterful — the architecture is modern, the streets are crowded with tourist restaurants, and prices are significantly higher than in Cusco. Plan your meals in Aguas Calientes as a necessary part of the visit rather than a highlight.

The train station is the focal point of the village; trains to Ollantaytambo and Cusco depart from here. Shuttle buses to the ruins load outside the main plaza.
