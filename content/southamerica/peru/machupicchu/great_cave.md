The Great Cave, often discussed together with the Temple of the Moon, is one of Machu Picchu's more demanding secondary sites. Tucked below Huayna Picchu, it combines natural rock shelter, finely worked Inca stone, and a setting that feels far removed from the crowded postcard terraces above.

Reaching it requires a steep, tiring circuit and should only be attempted if your ticket, time, and fitness allow. The reward is a much quieter encounter with the sanctuary's ritual landscape, where the Incas used caves, carved stone, and mountain terrain as a single architectural language.
