The Inca Trail is the most famous multi-day trek in South America and one of the most celebrated hiking routes in the world. The classic four-day, 43-kilometre route starts at km 82 on the Urubamba railway, climbs through cloud forest and over three mountain passes — the highest at 4,215 metres — past a string of archaeological sites, and arrives at dawn on the final morning at the Sun Gate above Machu Picchu.

Access is strictly controlled by the Peruvian government: only 500 people per day are allowed on the trail (including guides and porters), and permits sell out up to six months in advance for the peak season between May and September. Permits are issued to licensed agencies, and individual independent trekking is not permitted. Book early or miss out entirely.

The trail itself is well-maintained and the Inca paving is intact for much of the route, though the famous Inca stairs — steep, narrow stone steps that seem to go on forever — are the main physical challenge. The campsites are established and the agencies provide equipment; porters carry the group gear. Altitude sickness is a real risk for those coming from sea level, and a night or two in Cusco to acclimatise before starting is strongly recommended.

Two shorter alternatives do not require the same advance booking: the **Two-Day Trail** starts at km 104 and joins the main trail for the final section, arriving at the Sun Gate with an overnight at Wiñay Wayna. The **One-Day** version is possible but very rushed and not recommended.
