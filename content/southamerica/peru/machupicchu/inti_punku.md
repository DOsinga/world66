Inti Punku — the Sun Gate — stands at 2,720 metres on the original Inca Trail, about 45 minutes' walk from the main Machu Picchu site. It served as a ceremonial entrance to the citadel, and its doorway is oriented to align with the winter solstice sunrise, so that light falls directly through the portal and onto the Intihuatana and other sacred points of the site below.

For Inca Trail trekkers, this is the traditional destination of the final morning — emerging through the Sun Gate at dawn after four days of walking, with Machu Picchu laid out below in the morning mist, is one of the great arrivals in travel. For day visitors, the walk up from the site takes about 45 minutes on a mostly-paved Inca path and rewards you with a completely different perspective: the citadel appears far below, surrounded by the deep green of the canyon and the river loop beneath it.

The walk requires some uphill stamina at altitude (2,430–2,720 metres elevation change of about 290 metres), but the path is well-maintained and not technical. Trekkers arriving on the Inca Trail pass through the Sun Gate before descending to the site entrance; day visitors can do the walk in either direction as a satisfying half-day addition to the main circuit. Check whether the Inti Punku walk is included in your specific circuit ticket.
