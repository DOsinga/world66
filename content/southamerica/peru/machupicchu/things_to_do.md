---
title: "Things to Do"
type: section
---

The obvious thing to do at Machu Picchu is to walk the site itself. A typical visit follows a one-way circuit through the upper terraces and the main ceremonial buildings — the [Temple of the Three Windows](/southamerica/peru/machupicchu/temple_three_windows), the Principal Temple, the [Intihuatana](/southamerica/peru/machupicchu/intihuatana), the Sacred Rock — and down into the lower residential quarter. Beyond that, there are two worthwhile extras, both requiring separately-booked tickets. **[Huayna Picchu](/southamerica/peru/machupicchu/huayna_picchu)**, the sharp peak behind the site, is a steep one-hour climb to a viewpoint and a small set of ruins above the main city. **[Machu Picchu Mountain](/southamerica/peru/machupicchu/machu_picchu_mountain)** — on the opposite side — is higher, less crowded, and gives a wider panorama. The **[Sun Gate (Inti Punku)](/southamerica/peru/machupicchu/inti_punku)** is a 45-minute walk up from the site on the original Inca Trail — the spot where trail trekkers get their first view at dawn.

Getting to Machu Picchu on foot is part of the experience. The classic **[Inca Trail](/southamerica/peru/machupicchu/inca_trail)** is a four-day, 43 km trek from kilometre 82 on the railway past a string of Inca ruins, culminating with a dawn arrival at the Sun Gate; permits are strictly limited and must be booked four to six months ahead. Alternatives include the longer and higher **[Salkantay Trek](/southamerica/peru/machupicchu/salkantay_trek)**, the less-crowded **Lares** and **Choquequirao** routes, and the gentler **Inca Jungle Trek** that combines hiking with cycling and rafting. All of them end at Machu Picchu.
