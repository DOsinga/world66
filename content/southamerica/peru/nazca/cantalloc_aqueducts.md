  The puquios of Nazca baffled researchers for decades because the water they carry comes from a source that seems impossible given the depth of the water table. A network of ancient underground aqueducts built by the Nazca culture to bring groundwater from the Andes foothills to the desert plain. The puquios — as they are known locally — were constructed roughly 1,500 years ago and are still functioning today. They are one of the most impressive feats of engineering in the pre-Columbian Americas, delivering water through a system of tunnels and channels to fields that would otherwise be useless desert.

The aqueducts are visible at the surface through characteristic funnel-shaped spiral openings in the ground. The Cantalloc complex, about 3 km east of Nazca town, is the most accessible, with a series of these spirals visible in the landscape alongside a section of open channel. Many visitors find the aqueducts more interesting than the Nazca Lines in some ways — here you can actually see and touch a working piece of ancient engineering rather than looking at lines from a plane.
