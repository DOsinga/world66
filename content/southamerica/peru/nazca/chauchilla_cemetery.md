  For decades after its discovery in the 1920s, Chauchilla was systematically looted by grave robbers who scattered human remains across the desert in search of ceramics and gold. The Peruvian government finally secured the site in 1997 and researchers spent years collecting bones, reassembling mummies, and returning them to their original burial pits — which is why the tombs you see today are a careful reconstruction of what was here before the plunder.
---

An ancient Nazca burial site about 30 km south of Nazca on a dirt track off the Panamericana. The cemetery dates from roughly 200 to 900 AD and contains mummified remains in open burial pits. The combination of the intensely dry desert climate and the Nazca practice of wrapping the dead in textiles and positioning them seated preserved the bodies extraordinarily well — hair, skin, and clothing are often still intact, and the mummies stare outward from their pits across the same desert in which they were buried over a thousand years ago.

The site is protected and organised into a series of clearly marked open tombs that you walk between on a defined path. Guides explain the burial practices and the gradual reassembly of the cemetery after decades of looting. The experience is arresting in a way that a conventional museum is not — these are not exhibits behind glass but the actual dead in the actual landscape. Tours from Nazca town take around three hours return.
