---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

Nazca is one of the driest places on earth and the climate changes little through the year — dry, sunny, and extremely hot. Daytime temperatures regularly exceed 35°C from November to March, when flying can also be affected by thermal turbulence that makes the overflight more uncomfortable. The cooler months from May to October are more pleasant for daytime activity and generally produce calmer conditions for flights, though the lines can be seen in any season.

Rain is essentially non-existent; the lines have survived largely because of it. The desert blooms briefly when moisture from the Andes occasionally reaches the plain, but this is unpredictable. There is no best or worst time to visit in the conventional sense — just hotter and less hot.
