story: >
  The name "Tambo Colorado" — Red Inn — comes from the vivid red, yellow, and white mineral pigments the Inca applied to the adobe walls. Much of this original paintwork survives, which is extraordinary for a 500-year-old earthen structure in a desert environment. The colours have held partly because the site was never built over and partly because the Pisco valley's extreme dryness acts as a natural preservative.
---

Tambo Colorado is one of the best-preserved Inca administrative sites on the Peruvian coast, located about 45 kilometres east of Pisco in the dry Pisco River valley. Built in the fifteenth century during the reign of the Inca Tupac Yupanqui, it served as a way station and administrative hub on the royal road linking Cusco to the coast. The complex includes a great plaza, storehouses, residential areas, and the remains of the palace where provincial governors and visiting Inca royalty stayed.

The setting is stark and dramatic — low painted adobe structures set against dry hills and a wide river valley. A local guide adds considerably to the visit by explaining the spatial organisation and the surviving colour pigments. Tambo Colorado is reached by collective or taxi from Pisco; most organised tours combine it with a stop at the local market in Humay.
