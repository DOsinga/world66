story: >
  Almirante Miguel Grau, born here in 1834, commanded the Peruvian monitor Huáscar during the War of the Pacific against Chile. His chivalrous conduct — he repeatedly rescued shipwrecked enemy sailors and personally escorted their possessions back to their families — earned him the lasting nickname Caballero de los Mares, the Knight of the Seas. He died on his own bridge at the Battle of Angamos in 1879.
---

The childhood home of Peru's most revered naval hero is now a small but careful museum on the corner of Jirón Tacna. The original 19th-century house has been restored with period furniture, family portraits, and a collection of Grau's personal effects — uniforms, letters, charts, and a scale model of the Huáscar that he commanded.

Entry is free. The custodian is a navy veteran who will walk you through the rooms with practised patience and considerable pride. Allow about thirty minutes.
