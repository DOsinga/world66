Piura is well placed for excursions along the northern coast and into the surrounding countryside. [Paita](/southamerica/peru/paita), 55 kilometres west, is the most practical day trip — a working port city in a sheltered bay where the ceviche is excellent and the story of Manuela Sáenz gives the waterfront an unexpected edge. The beach village of [Catacaos](/southamerica/peru/piura/catacaos), just 12 kilometres south, is the best artisan market in the region: gold filigree, straw hats, and good cheap food that alone justify the short ride.

[Narihualá](/southamerica/peru/piura/narihuala), also near Catacaos, is the principal archaeological site of the Piura valley — an earthen pyramid built by the Tallán people before the Inca conquest, with a small on-site museum and a valley-wide view from the top.

[Máncora](/southamerica/peru/mancora), 180 kilometres north on the Panamericana, is too far for a comfortable day trip but worth one or two nights. Peru's most popular surf town, it has warm water, a long left-hand point break, and a lively beach scene; humpback whales are visible offshore from July to October.
