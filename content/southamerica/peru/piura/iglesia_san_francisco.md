Two blocks northwest of the Plaza de Armas, the Franciscan church of San Francisco is a modest single-nave colonial building from the early seventeenth century, much rebuilt after earthquakes. Its real significance is historical rather than architectural — this is the cradle of Peruvian independence.

A small plaque outside marks the spot, and the city stages re-enactments here every January. Inside, the church retains a handful of colonial paintings and a quietly handsome wooden choir.
