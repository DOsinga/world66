Lago Yarinacocha is the reason most travellers come to Pucallpa. It's a long, sinuous oxbow lake — about 20 kilometres end to end — left behind when the Ucayali River cut a new course. The water is calm, brown, and home to two species of freshwater dolphin: the pink Amazonian *boto* and the smaller grey *tucuxi*, both regularly seen in early morning and late afternoon.

The starting point for boat trips is Puerto Callao, on the lake's southern shore and a 20-minute mototaxi ride from central Pucallpa. The standard half-day trip takes in two or three Shipibo communities, a stretch of flooded forest for dolphin-watching, and a stop for lunch at one of the lakeside restaurants. Negotiate with the boatmen on the malecón; expect to pay 80–150 soles for a private launch for two to four people. Sunset on the lake, with parakeets coming in to roost in the trees, is the single best thing you can do in Pucallpa.
