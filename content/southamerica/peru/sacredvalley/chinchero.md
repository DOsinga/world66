A small village sitting on a plateau at 3,762 metres — higher than Cusco — above the main Sacred Valley. Chinchero is famous for two things: its weaving cooperatives, which produce some of the finest natural-dye textiles in Peru, and a striking colonial church built directly on the foundations of an Inca royal estate.

The Sunday market is smaller than Pisac's and far more locally oriented, with Quechua-speaking villagers from the surrounding hills coming down to trade staples. Several of the weaving cooperatives — Centro de Textiles Tradicionales del Cusco, Awana Kancha — offer free demonstrations of the natural-dye process and the traditional backstrap weaving technique, with high-quality textiles for sale.

Chinchero is about 30 kilometres from Cusco on the road to Urubamba. It works well as a stop on the way down into the valley or as a half-day trip on its own.
