The classic Inca Trail is a four-day, three-night hike from a trailhead at Kilometre 82 (near Ollantaytambo) along the original Inca road to Machu Picchu. The walk passes through stunning montane scenery — cloud forest, high alpine passes, several intact Inca sites — and finishes by arriving at Machu Picchu through the Inti Punku (Sun Gate) at dawn on the fourth day.

The trail is one of the world's great walks, but it is heavily regulated and not easy to organise. Permits must be booked in advance (typically 4–6 months before, longer for peak season) through a licensed agency; independent walking is not allowed. The route is closed during February each year for maintenance.

A shorter two-day version of the trail covers only the final section and ends with the same Sun Gate arrival. For those who cannot get a classic permit, alternatives include the Lares trek, Salkantay trek, or simply taking the train. None match the original trail for combining trekking with archaeology — but each has its own merits.
