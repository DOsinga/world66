A single carved-stone outcrop near the upper end of the Pisac ruins, traditionally interpreted as an Inti Watana — a "hitching post of the sun" used to track the solstices and equinoxes. Several similar stones survive across the Inca world; the most famous is at Machu Picchu.

The walk from the lower terraces of Pisac up to the Inti Watana takes about an hour at altitude and passes through the most spectacular section of the ruins, including the carved stone gateways and the cliff-face cemetery. From the top, the views down the gorge toward the Sacred Valley are some of the best in the area.
