The standard alternative to the Inca Trail, especially for travellers who could not get a permit or who want a less crowded route. The Lares trek is a three- to four-day walk over high passes in the Cordillera Urubamba, north of the Sacred Valley, that finishes either back in the valley or at Ollantaytambo for the train onward to Machu Picchu.

The trek does not directly visit Machu Picchu but is normally sold as a package that ends with the train and a guided visit. It is harder than the Inca Trail in places — the passes are higher (up to 4,800 m) and the days are long — but the route passes through remote Quechua villages that see almost no tourists, and the high-altitude scenery is exceptional.

Numerous Cusco-based agencies run the trek. Expect to pay around USD 400 to 800 per person for the full trip including train and Machu Picchu entry. Choose an operator that pays its porters and arrieros (muleteers) fairly — the cheap end of the market is cheap for a reason.
