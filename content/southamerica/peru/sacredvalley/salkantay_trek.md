A challenging five-day trek around the flanks of Salkantay (6,271 m), one of the most sacred peaks in the Cusco region. The route climbs to a pass at 4,650 m, then descends rapidly through dramatic alpine and cloud-forest terrain to Aguas Calientes and Machu Picchu.

Salkantay is the standard alternative for travellers who could not get an Inca Trail permit. The walking is harder than the Inca Trail — the pass is higher and the days are longer — but there are no permit caps, the scenery is genuinely spectacular, and the route is far less crowded. Numerous Cusco-based agencies run all-inclusive treks for around USD 400 to 700 per person including the Machu Picchu visit.

Recent variations include shorter three- or four-day versions, and treks that finish with a zipline canopy run rather than a final road-and-train transfer. Choose your operator carefully — wages and gear quality vary widely.
