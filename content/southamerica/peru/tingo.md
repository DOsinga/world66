Tingo sits in the deep Utcubamba valley of northern Peru, at 1,950 metres, where the only reason to come is one of the most remarkable ancient sites in South America. High on the forested ridge above hangs Kuélap — a fortress of the Chachapoyas, the "Warriors of the Clouds," built before the Inca ever arrived and containing more stone than the Great Pyramid of Giza. For most travellers, Tingo is a base and nothing more, and that is perfectly fine: the mountain above rewards every hour you give it.

Old Tingo itself is a tiny riverside village that can be walked end to end in five minutes. There is a restaurant, a small shop, and a handful of basic guesthouses. Nuevo Tingo, a few kilometres further up the mountain road, is where the cable car (teleférico) departs and where most practical facilities now concentrate. The two together make a workable base — quiet, surrounded by cloudforest, and mercifully uncrowded compared to Peru's more famous archaeological sites.

The Chachapoyas built Kuélap over several centuries from around the 6th century AD, reaching its mature form between 900 and 1100. Their circular stone towers, decorated with geometric friezes, stand inside perimeter walls that reach 20 metres in places — yet the Chachapoyas left almost no written record. When the Inca finally conquered them in the 1470s after fierce resistance, and the Spanish arrived a century later, this corner of Peru faded so thoroughly from outside knowledge that a provincial judge stumbled upon Kuélap in 1843 and could hardly believe what he had found.

Come with at least two nights. One is not enough to do the site justice and allow for the travel time involved in reaching this corner of Amazonas.
