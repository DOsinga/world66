---
title: When to Go
type: section
---

The dry season in the Amazonas region runs from May to October, and this is the best time to visit Tingo. Skies are clearer, the trail from Old Tingo is less muddy, and Kuélap is more pleasant to explore when you are not sheltering from rain beneath the ancient walls. June and July are the busiest months but still feel quiet by Peruvian standards.

The wet season (November to April) brings heavy rainfall and mist that can dramatically reduce visibility from the ridge. The trail becomes slippery and the higher sections of Kuélap can be lost in cloud. That said, the cloudforest is at its greenest and most atmospheric in these months, and the site sees almost nobody. If you go in the wet season, plan around the mornings: clouds typically build through the afternoon.

Temperatures at Kuélap's elevation of 3,000 metres are cool year-round — bring a jacket even in the dry season, as the exposed ridge can be cold in the wind. The valley floor at Tingo (1,950m) is noticeably warmer.

There are no major festivals in Tingo itself, but Chachapoyas celebrates regional fiestas in August that are worth factoring in if you are basing yourself in the city and making day trips to Tingo.
