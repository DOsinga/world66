Trujillo is Peru's third-largest city, sitting on the northern coastal desert about 560 km north of Lima. It is one of the country's most important archaeological destinations, surrounded by pre-Columbian ruins that span over a thousand years of civilisation.

The headline site is **[Chan Chan](/southamerica/peru/trujillo_1/chan_chan)**, the vast adobe capital of the Chimú Empire and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. At its peak in the 15th century it housed some 60,000 people, making it the largest pre-Columbian city in South America. The [Huaca de la Luna](/southamerica/peru/trujillo_1/huaca_de_la_luna), a massive Moche pyramid about 8 km south of the city, dates from around the 1st to 8th centuries AD and contains remarkably preserved polychrome murals.

The city itself has a handsome colonial centre with brightly painted mansions around the [Plaza de Armas](/southamerica/peru/trujillo_1/plaza_de_armas). The eccentric [Museo del Juguete](/southamerica/peru/trujillo_1/museo_del_juguete) — Peru's only toy museum, housed in a colonial mansion — displays everything from pre-Inca ceramic whistles to Victorian-era dolls. The [Museo de Arqueología](/southamerica/peru/trujillo_1/museo_de_arqueologia) is the place to study Moche and Chimú artefacts before visiting the sites.

Nearby [Huanchaco](/southamerica/peru/huanchaco), about 12 km northwest, is a laid-back beach town where fishermen still use traditional reed boats called caballitos de totora. It makes a pleasant base, and watching the fishermen paddle in through the surf on their ancient craft is one of northern Peru's most memorable sights.

Keep an eye on your belongings in the markets and crowded areas — pickpockets work in groups and are skilled.
