The adobe capital of the Chimú Empire, about 5 km west of Trujillo, is the largest pre-Columbian city in South America and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. At its peak in the 15th century, the city housed around 60,000 people in a complex of royal palaces, temples, storehouses, and residential quarters — all built from adobe brick on a flat coastal desert.

The Tschudi Palace (also called Nik An) is the main restored section open to visitors, with elaborate friezes of fish, seabirds, and geometric patterns decorating the walls of the ceremonial courtyards. The full site covers nearly 20 square kilometres, though much of it remains unexcavated. The Chimú were conquered by the Inca around 1470 AD; the Spanish arrived 60 years later. A site museum near the entrance provides context before you enter. Allow two to three hours.
