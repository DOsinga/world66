Urubamba is a small market town in Peru's Sacred Valley, about 60 kilometres northwest of Cusco along the valley floor. At 2,863 metres, it sits lower than Cusco and has a noticeably warmer, more agricultural feel — the valley here is wide and fertile, planted with maize and vegetables that supply Cusco and the surrounding towns.

The town is a practical base for exploring the Sacred Valley. Ollantaytambo is 25 kilometres up the valley, Pisac 35 kilometres to the east. Urubamba itself has a busy Sunday market and a loose cluster of restaurants and accommodation that cater to travellers moving between Cusco and Machu Picchu. The train to Aguas Calientes and Machu Picchu departs from a station just outside town.

The villages around Urubamba offer some of the Sacred Valley's most interesting community tourism, including agrotourism programs in Chichubamba that let visitors learn traditional techniques for making chicha, chocolate, pottery, and textiles alongside Andean families. The area around Chichubamba also holds two sets of largely unknown Inca ruins — the Tambos del Qéspiwanca and the watchtowers of the Sayhua Ridge — that can be visited with a local guide.
