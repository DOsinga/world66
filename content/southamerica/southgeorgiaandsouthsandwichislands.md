South Georgia and the South Sandwich Islands are a British Overseas Territory in the cold South Atlantic, far east of the Falklands and much closer in spirit to Antarctica than to South America. Almost nobody comes independently; visitors usually arrive by expedition cruise, and the reward is a hard, dramatic island of glaciers, tussock grass, elephant seals, albatrosses, and immense penguin colonies.

The only settlement travellers usually see is [Grytviken](/southamerica/southgeorgiaandsouthsandwichislands/grytviken), the former whaling station on South Georgia. It is a powerful stop: rusting industrial ruins, a small museum, a white Norwegian church, and Ernest Shackleton's grave all sit inside one of the best harbours in the Southern Ocean.

Travel here is tightly managed for good reasons. Weather, wildlife protection, biosecurity, and landing permits shape every visit, and itineraries change quickly when wind or swell turns against a landing. If you do get ashore, South Georgia feels like the edge of the inhabited world.
