Grytviken is a former whaling station on South Georgia, set in King Edward Cove beneath bare hills and often visited by expedition ships on their way to or from Antarctica. It has no ordinary town life now, but it is one of the most memorable landings in the South Atlantic: half industrial ruin, half polar shrine, with seals and penguins taking back the ground between rusted tanks and old boats.

The settlement was founded by Norwegian whalers in 1904 because this was the island's best natural harbour. Whaling ended in 1966, leaving behind a harsh museum of machinery, slipways, boilers, and weathered timber. The [South Georgia Museum](/southamerica/southgeorgiaandsouthsandwichislands/grytviken/south_georgia_museum) gives the place context before you walk the [whaling station ruins](/southamerica/southgeorgiaandsouthsandwichislands/grytviken/grytviken_whaling_station).

Grytviken is also inseparable from Ernest Shackleton. His grave is in the [Grytviken Cemetery](/southamerica/southgeorgiaandsouthsandwichislands/grytviken/grytviken_cemetery), a short walk from the white [Grytviken Church](/southamerica/southgeorgiaandsouthsandwichislands/grytviken/grytviken_church). A visit is usually brief, but it does not feel thin; few places explain the romance and damage of the polar age so directly.
