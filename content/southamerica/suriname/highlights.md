**Walk [Paramaribo](/southamerica/suriname/paramaribo) slowly.** The appeal is not a checklist so much as the combination: white wooden facades, Fort Zeelandia by the brown river, a huge timber cathedral, markets full of ingredients from the interior, and a synagogue beside a mosque. The buildings are fragile and some are in rough shape, but the city has no convincing substitute elsewhere in South America.

**Go deep into the [Central Suriname Nature Reserve](/southamerica/suriname/central_suriname_nature_reserve).** This UNESCO site protects 1.6 million hectares of largely undisturbed Guiana Shield forest. [Raleighvallen](/southamerica/suriname/raleighvallen), with river rapids, birdlife, and the Voltzberg granite dome, is the usual way in. It is expensive and logistically awkward; that is precisely why it still feels like wilderness.

**Start with [Brownsberg Nature Park](/southamerica/suriname/brownsberg_nature_park).** Its plateau trails, waterfalls, noisy monkeys, and views over the Brokopondo reservoir make it the most practical first encounter with Suriname's forest. Conditions are basic and the access road can be rough, so confirm what is open before leaving Paramaribo.

**Travel the [Upper Suriname River](/southamerica/suriname/upper_suriname_river).** Beyond Atjoni, the boat becomes the bus and Saamaka villages line the banks. Come to listen and learn, not to treat a living community as a performance; a locally connected guide makes the difference.

**Cross into [Commewijne](/southamerica/suriname/commewijne).** Fort Nieuw Amsterdam, plantation traces, Peperpot's forest, and river dolphins fit into an easy day from the capital. Do not let the pleasant boat ride soften the history: this landscape was shaped by slavery and later indentured labour.

**Put [Jodensavanne](/southamerica/suriname/jodensavanne) in context.** The synagogue ruins and cemeteries preserve an exceptional Jewish history, but the UNESCO site also records the enslaved Africans and Indigenous people on whom the settlement depended. It is one of Suriname's most important places, not a picturesque ruin with an easy story.

**Watch the water open out at [Bigi Pan](/southamerica/suriname/bigi_pan).** A shallow boat threads from the Nickerie River through a narrow canal and into an immense wetland of lagoons, mudflats, and mangroves. Scarlet ibis are the signature bird; flamingos are a welcome possibility rather than a promise.

**Swim at [Colakreek](/southamerica/suriname/colakreek).** The dark water looks almost black beneath the forest, stained naturally by leaf tannins. It is a much-loved local recreation area, best on a quiet weekday when there is room to linger in the cool creek.
