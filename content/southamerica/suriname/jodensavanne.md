Jodensavanne, beside the Suriname River south of [Paramaribo](/southamerica/suriname/paramaribo), preserves the remains of an unusual Jewish agricultural settlement. It forms a UNESCO World Heritage property with the older Cassipora Creek cemetery. The visible core includes the foundations of the Beraha VeSalom synagogue and gravestones cut with inscriptions in several languages.

The site deserves more than a story about religious freedom. Jewish settlers received land and a measure of autonomy within the colony, while their plantations depended on enslaved Africans and interacted with Indigenous communities. UNESCO's listing explicitly recognises that entangled history.

Access arrangements vary, and the site is too important for a rushed photo stop. Go with a guide who can explain the settlement, both cemeteries, and the lives of the people excluded from older heroic accounts. It is possible as a day trip from Paramaribo by road and boat; confirm the route before leaving.
