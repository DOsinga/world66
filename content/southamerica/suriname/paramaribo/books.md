---
title: "Books"
type: section
---

Suriname is under-represented in English-language literature, but a handful of books offer useful insight for travellers.

**The Cost of Sugar** by Cynthia McLeod is the definitive novel about colonial Suriname — a sweeping, readable saga set on the 18th-century plantations, covering the lives of enslaved people, free Blacks, Jewish colonists, and Dutch planters. McLeod is Surinamese and writes from the inside; the book is widely available in Paramaribo and will deepen everything you see in the city.

**Suriname: Small but Significant** edited by various contributors is an accessible English-language introduction to Surinamese history, culture, and society. Not literary, but practical for getting context before you arrive.

**The Maroon Experience** — various academic collections cover the remarkable history of the Maroon communities who escaped slavery and established independent societies in the interior. Bruce Wishart's work and Richard Price's *Alabi's World* offer the most vivid accounts of Surinamese Maroon culture, though both are more scholarly than casual.

**Green Heritage: Suriname's Nature and Environment** gives a solid grounding in the extraordinary ecology of the country's interior, useful for travellers planning to visit Brownsberg or the deeper rainforest.
