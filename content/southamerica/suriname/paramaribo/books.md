**The Cost of Sugar** by Cynthia McLeod is an absorbing historical novel about Jewish planters and enslaved people in 18th-century Suriname. Read it before Jodensavanne or Commewijne; it makes the comfortable plantation-house version of history impossible to accept.

**On a Woman's Madness** by Astrid Roemer follows Noenka as she leaves an abusive marriage and tries to construct a life in Paramaribo. Lucy Scott's English translation preserves the novel's fractured, sensual intensity and opens a view of race, gender, and freedom far beyond the monumental city centre.

**Off-White**, also by Astrid Roemer, follows a Surinamese family across generations around independence and migration. It is denser and more demanding, but very good on the private costs of national history.

**The Free Negress Elisabeth** by Cynthia McLeod draws on the life of Elisabeth Samson, a wealthy free Black woman in colonial Paramaribo. Pair it with the Elisabeth Samson House and keep in mind that wealth and freedom existed inside a society built on slavery.
