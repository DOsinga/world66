The Commewijne River flows east of Paramaribo through what was, in the 18th century, the most intensively cultivated plantation zone in the colony. A boat trip up the river — easily arranged from the waterfront — passes the ruins of sugar and coffee estates, restored plantation houses, and stretches of forest that have reclaimed the clearings where enslaved people once worked.

The highlight for most visitors is the pink river dolphins (Boto) that frequently appear at the confluence of the Commewijne and Suriname rivers. Sightings are not guaranteed but are common, especially in the morning. The trip takes around three to four hours including stops at the Fort Nieuw Amsterdam ruins and the Peperpot nature reserve. Most tour operators in Paramaribo offer this excursion.
