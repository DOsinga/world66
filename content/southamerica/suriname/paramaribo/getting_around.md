---
title: "Getting Around"
type: section
---

The historic centre of Paramaribo is compact and entirely walkable — all the main sights are within 20 minutes on foot of each other. Walking is the best way to explore, and the heat is manageable in the morning and late afternoon.

For longer journeys within the city, minibuses (locally called "bus") run fixed routes and are cheap and frequent but confusing for newcomers. Taxis are widely available and relatively affordable by South American standards; negotiate the fare before you get in. Ride-hailing apps have limited coverage in Suriname — your hotel or guesthouse will usually be able to call a reliable driver.

Bicycles can be rented from a few places near the centre and are a practical way to explore the wider city. Traffic is not aggressive by regional standards, though the roads are poorly marked.

For excursions outside the city — to Brownsberg, the Suriname River, or the interior — most travellers join organised tours from Paramaribo, as public transport to these areas is scarce.
