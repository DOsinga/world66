About 50 km south of Paramaribo, on the Suriname River, lie the ruins of Jodensavanne — one of the most historically significant Jewish sites in the Americas. The settlement was founded by Sephardic Jews in the 1660s, attracted by Dutch colonial tolerance, and it functioned as an autonomous Jewish agricultural community for over a century.

What remains today is the ruined Beracha ve Shalom synagogue, its stone walls still standing in the forest, and a Jewish cemetery with legible 17th- and 18th-century gravestones. Getting there requires a boat trip on the Suriname River followed by a walk through the forest, making it something of an expedition. Most travellers visit as part of a guided day trip from Paramaribo, which usually combines Jodensavanne with a visit to a Maroon village.
