Suriname has one of the most biodiverse ecosystems on earth — over 90 percent of the country is tropical forest, and the plant and animal life of its interior is extraordinary. The Natural History Museum in Paramaribo provides a useful introduction to this richness: collections of birds, insects, reptiles, and mammals, many of them species found nowhere else. For travellers planning to head into the interior — to the Brownsberg nature reserve, the Suriname River, or the deep rainforest — the museum provides valuable context.

The displays are modest by European standards, but the material is genuinely fascinating. The bird collection alone — Suriname has around 700 species — justifies a visit for anyone with a passing interest in wildlife.
