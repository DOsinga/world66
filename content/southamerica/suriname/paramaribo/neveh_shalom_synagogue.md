The present Neveh Shalom building dates from the 1840s and serves Suriname's remaining Jewish community. Its interior is known for a floor covered in pale sand, a tradition associated with earlier Caribbean synagogues. The congregation's history brings together Sephardic and Ashkenazi communities rather than fitting a single neat label.

The Keizerstraat Mosque stands directly next door. View both as active places of worship and ask the community about access; doors are not kept open simply for sightseeing.
