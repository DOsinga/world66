---
title: "When to Go"
type: section
---

Paramaribo sits close to the equator and has a tropical climate: hot and humid year-round, with temperatures rarely straying far from 30°C. There are two rainy seasons (roughly May to July and November to January) and two drier periods (August to November and February to April). Rain in the tropics often means intense afternoon showers rather than all-day drizzle, so even the wet season is workable. The drier periods from February to April and August to October are generally the most comfortable times to visit.

The most important annual event is Keti Koti (Emancipation Day) on 1 July, when Suriname commemorates the abolition of slavery in 1863. The celebration in Paramaribo is a major cultural event — music, food, and a strong sense of collective memory. Phagwa (Holi) is celebrated enthusiastically by the Hindustani community in March, and Eid festivities are visible across the city. Suriname's religious and cultural calendar reflects its full range of communities, and arriving during a festival of any kind enriches the experience considerably.
