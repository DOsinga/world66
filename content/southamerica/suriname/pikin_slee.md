Pikin Slee is one of the largest Saamaka villages on the [Upper Suriname River](/southamerica/suriname/upper_suriname_river). Houses, farms, landing places, and footpaths stretch along the riverbank; there are no roads linking the village to Suriname's coastal network.

The [Saamaka Marron Museum](/southamerica/suriname/pikin_slee/saamaka_marron_museum) is the reason to make Pikin Slee more than a passing boat stop. Its collection presents Saamaka history and arts from within the community, with woodcarving especially prominent. The village is also home to working carvers, though workshops are workplaces rather than staged attractions.

Reach Pikin Slee by boat from the Atjoni area as part of a locally arranged Upper Suriname journey. Ask your host about museum access, village customs, and photography before arriving. Dress modestly and remember that this is a large living community, not an open-air museum.
