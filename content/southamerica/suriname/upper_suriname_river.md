Above the Brokopondo reservoir, the Suriname River branches through Saamaka country. Roads end around Pokigron and the Atjoni landing; from there, long narrow boats carry residents and visitors between villages, farms, rapids, and small river islands.

The attraction is not a single sight. It is the journey and the chance to learn how Maroon communities built durable societies in the forest after their ancestors escaped slavery. [Pikin Slee](/southamerica/suriname/pikin_slee) has a community museum focused on Saamaka culture, while other villages offer forest walks, rapids, and locally arranged visits.

Arrange the trip with a host or operator who has a real relationship with the community you will visit. Ask before taking photographs, dress modestly in villages, and do not assume ceremonies or sacred places are open to visitors. Atjoni can be reached by road from Paramaribo; journeys farther upriver depend on boat, water level, and daylight.
