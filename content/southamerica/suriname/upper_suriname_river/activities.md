The Upper Suriname River is Saramaccan Maroon country — a string of villages along the water, reached by korjaal from Atjoni, with a handful of lodges built on islands and riverbanks between them. Trips here are three days at the shortest and are as much about the villages as the forest.

You can book the lodge directly or go through an operator in Paramaribo who will bundle the transport, which is the harder part.


**Hiring a korjaal yourself.** Almost all river transport here is informal and none of it is bookable online. The road ends at Atjoni, and dozens of korjalen — long dugouts with an outboard on the back — wait at the landing to run people and cargo upriver. You turn up, ask, and agree a price before you get in; there is no operator, no website and no number to message in advance. Rates depend on how far up you are going and whether you are filling the boat or sharing it, and payment is cash.

If that sounds like more improvisation than you want, every lodge on this stretch will arrange the boat as part of your stay, and the Paramaribo operators above bundle the transfer from Atjoni into their trips. That is what you are paying them for as much as the guiding.
