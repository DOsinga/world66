Wia Wia protects a shifting stretch of mudflats, mangroves, lagoons, and beach on Suriname's Atlantic coast east of [Paramaribo](/southamerica/suriname/paramaribo). It is important habitat for migratory shorebirds and marine turtles, but the coast changes constantly as mud banks move westward.

Migratory birds from North America use the wider coast, alongside scarlet ibis, herons, egrets, and other wetland species. Turtle use also shifts with erosion and the formation of new beaches.

Do not plan Wia Wia as if it were an operating national-park lodge. STINASU currently says protected-area visitor facilities other than Brownsberg and Raleighvallen are closed to tourism. Access is difficult and ecologically sensitive; verify permission and conditions with the managing authority.

For most travellers, Galibi or a responsibly run coastal birding trip is a more realistic choice. Wia Wia remains worth documenting because its conservation value is high, not because it is currently an easy visitor destination.
