Montevideo is the capital of Uruguay and home to about half the country's population — a compact, walkable city on a promontory between the Río de la Plata and an inner bay. It has a strong European character, the result of large-scale immigration from Spain, Italy, and later central Europe, but it also has something distinctly Rioplatense: the same mix of faded grandeur, café culture, and waterfront promenades that defines Buenos Aires across the water, but quieter and less frantic.

The historic core is the [Ciudad Vieja](/southamerica/uruguay/montevideo/ciudad_vieja), where the street grid is still 18th-century and the architecture ranges from colonial to art deco. [Plaza Independencia](/southamerica/uruguay/montevideo/plaza_independencia) marks the transition between old and new city. On it stands the [Palacio Salvo](/southamerica/uruguay/montevideo/palacio_salvo), once the tallest building in South America, and the [Artigas Mausoleum](/southamerica/uruguay/montevideo/artigas_mausoleum), where the national hero lies in state beneath an eternal flame. The [Gateway of the Citadel](/southamerica/uruguay/montevideo/gateway_of_the_citadel), the one remaining fragment of the old city walls, frames the entrance to the old town.

The [Teatro Solís](/southamerica/uruguay/montevideo/teatro_solis) on [Constitution Square](/southamerica/uruguay/montevideo/constitution_square) is Uruguay's finest theatre, opened in 1856 and still in regular use. A block away, the [Museo Torres García](/southamerica/uruguay/montevideo/museo_torres_garcia) holds the best collection of Joaquín Torres García's constructivist work — a major collection that most visitors miss entirely.

[La Rambla](/southamerica/uruguay/montevideo/la_rambla), the 22 km waterfront promenade, is the defining civic space of the city. Locals walk, run, cycle, and sit with their mate flasks along it at all hours. It connects [Pocitos](/southamerica/uruguay/montevideo/pocitos_neighbourhood) — the densest residential neighbourhood, full of apartment towers and good restaurants — with [Palermo](/southamerica/uruguay/montevideo/palermo_neighbourhood), the bar district that has taken on new life over the last decade.

For history beyond the colonial centre, [Fortaleza del Cerro](/southamerica/uruguay/montevideo/fortaleza_del_cerro) sits on the hill west of the bay and offers the best panoramic view of the city. The [Estadio Centenario](/southamerica/uruguay/montevideo/estadio_centenario) hosted the 1930 World Cup final and is still in use for football; the attached museum covers Uruguay's disproportionate football history. On Sundays, the [Tristán Narvaja Market](/southamerica/uruguay/montevideo/tristan_narvaja_market) in Cordón is one of the best flea and book markets in South America.

The [Mercado del Puerto](/southamerica/uruguay/montevideo/mercado_del_puerto) at the old port is the place for a long, smoky lunch of grilled meat and red wine — a Montevideo ritual best done on a weekend afternoon when the market is at full bustle. The [Café Brasilero](/southamerica/uruguay/montevideo/cafe_brasilero) on Calle Ituzaingó, where Eduardo Galeano used to write, is worth a coffee for atmosphere alone.
