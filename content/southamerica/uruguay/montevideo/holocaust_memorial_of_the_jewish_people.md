Montevideo's Holocaust memorial stands on the Rambla facing the Río de la Plata, using stone, voids, and open sky rather than a conventional statue. Its position on the waterfront gives the memorial a solemn spaciousness: the city is behind you, the river and horizon ahead.

It is a reflective stop rather than a long visit, but it adds an important layer to Montevideo's public memory. The memorial pairs naturally with a walk along Punta Carretas and Pocitos, where the capital's everyday seaside life continues around a deliberately quiet place of remembrance.
