Nueva Helvecia, also called Colonia Suiza, is Uruguay's little piece of Switzerland. The comparison is not subtle: family crests, Central European details, dairy farms, and Swiss-inflected food all sit in a quiet town southeast of [Colonia del Sacramento](/southamerica/uruguay/coloniadelsacramento).

The town was founded in 1862 by European settlers, many of them Swiss, and it became an important agricultural centre early in Uruguay's modern history. That heritage is still the point of visiting. [Plaza de los Fundadores](/southamerica/uruguay/nueva_helvecia/plaza_de_los_fundadores) gives the town its symbolic centre, while the old evangelical chapel and the [Molino Quemado](/southamerica/uruguay/nueva_helvecia/molino_quemado) tell the story of settlement, faith, and farming.

Nueva Helvecia is not a place for grand sights. It works because the identity is strong and visible, and because it makes a fine detour between Montevideo, Colonia, and the beaches of southwestern Uruguay. Come on a Wednesday morning if you want the market; otherwise come for a slow walk, local cheese, and a town that still knows where its name came from.
