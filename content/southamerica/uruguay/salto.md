Salto is Uruguay's second largest city, sitting on the Uruguay River near the border with [Argentina](/southamerica/argentina) and about 500 km northwest of Montevideo. It is the centre of a major citrus-growing region, and the orange groves that surround the city give the countryside here a distinctly pleasant character.

The city's main draw beyond the centre is the [**Salto Grande Dam**](/southamerica/uruguay/salto/salto_grande_dam), about 20 km north of town, a joint Uruguay-Argentina hydroelectric project whose construction flooded the original falls that gave Salto its name. Guided tours are available through the tourist office. In the centre itself, [**Plaza Artigas**](/southamerica/uruguay/salto/plaza_artigas) and the [**Cathedral of San Juan Bautista**](/southamerica/uruguay/salto/cathedral_san_juan) anchor the historic core, with the 19th-century Larrañaga Theatre alongside. The [**Museo del Hombre y la Tecnología**](/southamerica/uruguay/salto/museo_hombre_tecnologia) is one of the better provincial museums in the country.

The [**Costanera**](/southamerica/uruguay/salto/costanera) promenade along the river is the city's evening gathering place, with views across to Argentina and the ferry terminal for crossings to Concordia. The area's hot springs are a major draw: [**Termas del Daymán**](/southamerica/uruguay/salto/termas_dayman) is 10 km south — a large thermal complex with public pools popular with Uruguayan families on weekends. Most travellers pass through Salto heading to or from Argentina, but those who stay a day or two find more than they expected.
