  In January 1996 a mysterious "red tide" of toxic algae swept through Morrocoy, killing fish, corals, and seabirds across the park almost overnight. Locals called it the "tragedia de Morrocoy". The reefs have been slowly recovering ever since, helped by stricter limits on anchoring and a ban on fishing inside the park boundary.

Morrocoy spreads across roughly 320 square kilometres of coastline, mangrove channels, and a scattered archipelago of cays between Chichiriviche and the town of Tucacas to the south. Some of the cays are wooded; most are bare sandbars with a fringe of palms and a stretch of beach you could walk in five minutes. Cayo Sombrero, Cayo Sal, Cayo Borracho, Cayo Pelón, Cayo Pescadores, Playuela and Peraza are the most-visited.

The water is shallow and clear over white sand, with patches of coral that bring snorkellers out on calm days. Lanchas (motorboats) leave the Chichiriviche embarcadero on demand — fix a price for the round trip and the pick-up time before you get in. On weekdays out of season you can have a small cay essentially to yourself; on Easter and Christmas holidays the bigger ones get noisy.
