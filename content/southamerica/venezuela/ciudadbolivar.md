Ciudad Bolívar is a colonial city on the southern bank of the Orinoco, and the main jumping-off point for trips to [Angel Falls](/southamerica/venezuela/angelfalls), [Canaima National Park](/southamerica/venezuela/canaima), and [La Gran Sabana](/southamerica/venezuela/lagransabana). Most travelers arrive here by bus from Caracas or by connection from Ciudad Guayana, then book a light-aircraft tour to Canaima.

The historic center along the riverfront — the [Paseo Orinoco](/southamerica/venezuela/ciudadbolivar/paseo_orinoco) — is attractive: colonial-era buildings, a good main plaza, and the 18th-century [cathedral](/southamerica/venezuela/ciudadbolivar/catedral). The [Casa del Congreso de Angostura](/southamerica/venezuela/ciudadbolivar/casa_del_congreso) commemorates the 1819 congress where Simón Bolívar was proclaimed president of Gran Colombia, and the [Casa del Correo del Orinoco](/southamerica/venezuela/ciudadbolivar/casa_correo_orinoco) preserves his revolutionary printing press. The city was originally called Angostura, and the famous bitters of the same name were invented here. The [Museo Jesús Soto](/southamerica/venezuela/ciudadbolivar/museo_soto) on the edge of town holds one of South America's most important kinetic-art collections, named for Ciudad Bolívar's most famous artist son.

Numerous tour operators in the city offer packages to Angel Falls and the Gran Sabana. Prices vary; compare a few before booking. The light-aircraft flights to Canaima are spectacular in themselves, flying low over the tepui landscape.

Ciudad Bolívar is a working-class regional city and shares the security concerns of much of Venezuela. Take standard precautions.
