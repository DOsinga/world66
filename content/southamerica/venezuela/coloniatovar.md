About 60 km west of Caracas, perched in the cool cloud-forest mountains of the Cordillera de la Costa, Colonia Tovar is one of Venezuela's most unusual towns. It was founded in 1843 by immigrants from Endingen, in the Black Forest region of Germany, and for more than a century it remained largely cut off from the outside world — the road linking it to Caracas wasn't paved until the 1960s. That long isolation preserved a distinctive German character that still marks the town today.

Walking through Colonia Tovar feels genuinely dissonant: half-timbered architecture, strawberry farms, Black Forest-style bakeries, and cool mountain air, yet distinctly Venezuelan in its noise and energy. The town is famous for its [weekend market](/southamerica/venezuela/coloniatovar/weekend_market), where locals from Caracas come to buy fresh strawberries, blackberries, cream, and smoked sausage. The original [Iglesia San Martín de Tours](/southamerica/venezuela/coloniatovar/iglesia_san_martin) and the small [Museo de Historia y Artesanía](/southamerica/venezuela/coloniatovar/museo_historia) are worth a look. The mountain scenery — deep ravines, forested ridges, and frequent mist — is reward enough for the winding drive up.
