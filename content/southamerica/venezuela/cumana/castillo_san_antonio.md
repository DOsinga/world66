The yellow stone fort on the hill above Cumaná is the city's most prominent landmark — visible from the seafront and offering, in return, an outstanding view back over the rooftops, the Manzanares River, and the Gulf of Cariaco. It was built in the 17th century, primarily by enslaved labour, to defend the port from Dutch and English privateers.

The interior is largely empty but the ramparts and bastions are worth walking. The climb up from the historic centre is steep but short; allow an hour to take in the views. Sunset is the best time, when the heat lessens and the light turns the painted facade a deeper gold.
