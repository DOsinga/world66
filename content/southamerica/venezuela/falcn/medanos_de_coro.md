story: >
  The sand dunes of the Médanos de Coro are not simply a curiosity — they are geologically active, migrating several metres per year driven by the trade winds that batter the Paraguaná Peninsula. Roads and structures at the dune's margins are periodically buried or uncovered as the dunes shift. The dunes exist because the northwestern corner of Venezuela falls in the rain shadow of the Andes and receives less than 300mm of rain annually — an anomaly in a country most outsiders associate with lush tropical forest.
---

The Médanos de Coro is a national park protecting an extensive field of sand dunes on the outskirts of Coro, the only true desert dune system in Venezuela. The dunes rise up to 30 metres and extend for several kilometres, their shapes constantly reworked by the trade winds that blow reliably off the Caribbean. The contrast with the colonial city just a few kilometres south — whitewashed churches and cobblestone streets against towering red-gold dunes — is genuinely striking.

The park is popular with locals for sunset visits and sandboarding. Quad-bike rentals are available at the park entrance for those who want to cover more ground. Go early morning or late afternoon — midday heat on the open dunes is punishing. The access road runs directly from Coro, and the park entrance is about 5 kilometres from the city centre.
