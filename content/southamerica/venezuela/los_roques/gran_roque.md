The village that grew up around it remained a small fishing settlement for centuries, with no fresh water and minimal resources, before the arrival of tourism in the 1980s transformed it into one of Venezuela's premier destinations."

Gran Roque is the only permanently inhabited island in the Los Roques archipelago and the hub through which all visitors pass. The village is small — a few hundred permanent residents — with colourful posadas (guesthouses) lining narrow sandy streets, a small plaza, a church, and a market. There are no cars, no high-rise hotels, and no chain restaurants. The scale is right.

The airport is a short walk from the village centre. From Gran Roque you can walk to the beach on the leeward side in a few minutes, or arrange boat transfers to the outer cays. The posadas range from basic rooms to more comfortable operations with good food; most include breakfast and often dinner in the rate. The village comes alive in the evenings when the day-trip crowd has left.
