Pampatar is a colonial town on the east coast, founded in the 1530s and named after the Guaiqueri word for "town of salt" -- a salt flat mine still operates nearby. The town is built around a deep, calm bay where fishing boats and yachts sit side by side.

The [Castillo de San Carlos de Borromeo](/southamerica/venezuela/margaritaisland/pampatar/castillo_san_carlos_de_borromeo) dominates the waterfront, a well-preserved 17th-century fortress that saw action during the wars of independence. Behind the castle, the old town has narrow streets lined with colonial buildings, a few good restaurants, and a quieter atmosphere than nearby Porlamar. The [bay itself](/southamerica/venezuela/margaritaisland/pampatar/pampatar_bay) is sheltered and waveless, good for a swim with children, and the small [Iglesia del Santisimo Cristo del Buen Viaje](/southamerica/venezuela/margaritaisland/pampatar/iglesia_buen_viaje) and the [Casa Amarilla](/southamerica/venezuela/margaritaisland/pampatar/casa_amarilla) round out the colonial centre.
