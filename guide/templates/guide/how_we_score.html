How We Score

Travel Profile scores every city, feature, and island on four dimensions, each from 0 to 10. A 5 is present and meaningful but not exceptional; a 10 is one of the world's strong examples of that dimension. The same place can score high on several dimensions at once — each high score has its own reason.

Heritage

High heritage scores mean visible historic depth — old built fabric, ruins, monuments, museums, architecture, long traditions, and layered human memory that a traveler can actually experience. Rome, Kyoto, Angkor, and Luxor sit at the top of this scale.

Vibrancy

High vibrancy scores mean urban electricity: many-layered human activity, dense street life, strong food and night scenes, creative energy, distinctive neighborhoods, and surprise. A 9 or 10 feels like a complete urban world with depth across many scenes — Tokyo, New York, Bangkok, London, Paris.

Nature

High nature scores mean the non-human world is a main reason to go — animal life, green life, ecological richness, beauty, scale, and the feeling of being shaped by forces larger than people. The highest scores go to places that feel alive, wild, rare, vast, or beautiful, from the Serengeti and the Galápagos to Torres del Paine.

Off The Beaten Track

High off-the-beaten-track scores mean the journey takes guts: nerve, effort, uncertainty, hard logistics, perceived danger, unfamiliar systems, physical challenge, and crossing into less managed territory. The highest scores go to places where a normal visit feels bold, exploratory, and story-worthy — Tokyo, Paris, and Singapore sit at the opposite end of this scale.

Similar places

A place's four scores together form a "travel profile" — a point in a four-dimensional space. Similar places are the nearest other points in that space, by plain distance, split into places in the same country and places elsewhere. Two places can be similar in profile without being similar in any single dimension you'd name yourself.
