How We Score

Travel Profile scores every city, feature, and island on five dimensions, each from 0 to 10. A 5 is present and meaningful but not exceptional; a 10 is one of the world's strong examples of that dimension. The same place can score high on several dimensions at once — each high score has its own reason.

City Culture & Historic Culture

High culture scores mean strong human meaning: how deeply a place has been shaped by people, how much human story has accumulated there, and how vividly that story is still felt by visitors. The highest scores go to places with depth, distinctiveness, continuity, creativity, and a strong sense of human presence.

City Culture and Historic Culture are currently the same score. They will diverge once historic culture is scored as its own dimension; until then, treat them as one culture score shown twice.

Nature

High nature scores mean the living and physical natural world at its strongest. Wildlife, plants, ecosystems, landforms, water, ice, and climate shape the score, with animal life especially important when it defines the journey. The highest scores go to places that feel alive, wild, rare, vast, strange, or beautiful.

Leisure

High leisure scores mean cultivated pleasure: taste, beauty, style, indulgence, sensuality, and being well looked after. The highest scores go to places where the ordinary visitor experience feels refined, glamorous, relaxing, delicious, restorative, or fun.

Adventure

High adventure scores mean edge, discovery, and commitment: how much a trip asks of the visitor — nerve, effort, uncertainty, difficult logistics, perceived danger, unfamiliar systems, remoteness, or physical challenge. The highest scores go to places where the ordinary journey feels bold and story-worthy.

Similar places

A place's five scores together form a "travel profile" — a point in a five-dimensional space. Similar places are the nearest other points in that space, by plain distance. Two places can be similar in profile without being similar in any single dimension you'd name yourself.
