Traveling safely is less about following rules and more about developing habits. The most common travel problems — theft, mugging, scams — are not random; they tend to happen to people who are visibly unfamiliar with their surroundings, carrying more than they need, or walking in the wrong place at the wrong time.

The fundamentals have not changed much: don't carry what you cannot afford to lose, split your cash across multiple places, use taxis at night in rough areas, and get travel insurance before you leave. Most of what makes a trip go wrong is predictable in advance. The articles in this section cover the main scenarios — [theft](/travelwise/safetyandsecurity/theft), [mugging](/travelwise/safetyandsecurity/mugging), how to [blend in](/travelwise/safetyandsecurity/blendin), and [packing tips](/travelwise/safetyandsecurity/packing_tips) that reduce your vulnerability. We also cover specific topics like [Naxalism](/travelwise/safetyandsecurity/naxalism) in India and the value of [travel insurance](/travelwise/safetyandsecurity/travelinsurance).

No guide can guarantee your safety, and no destination is entirely without risk. The goal is to travel smart, not to stay home.
