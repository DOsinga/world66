Lima's Plaza Mayor is the historic heart of the city — a grand colonial square flanked by the Cathedral Basílica, the Government Palace, and the Archbishop's Palace. The Spanish founded Lima here in 1535 and the square has been the centre of Peruvian public life ever since. The cathedral holds the tomb of Francisco Pizarro, conquistador and founder of the city, which is as strange a thing to stand in front of as it sounds.

Two blocks from the plaza, the Monasterio de San Francisco contains one of Lima's genuinely unmissable sights: catacombs beneath the church holding the bones of an estimated 70,000 people, arranged in geometric patterns. Tours run throughout the day. The surrounding streets of the Centro Histórico are busy and slightly chaotic — keep your belongings close — but the architecture is spectacular and the street food is cheap and good. The Centro is a 30-minute drive from Miraflores; the easiest way to get there is by taxi or ride-share.
