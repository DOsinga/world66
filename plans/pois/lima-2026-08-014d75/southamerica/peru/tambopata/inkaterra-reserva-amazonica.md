Inkaterra Reserva Amazónica is the upscale option in Tambopata, run by the same Peruvian hospitality group behind the Inkaterra hotels in Cusco and at Machu Picchu. It sits on a private reserve about 45 minutes downriver from Puerto Maldonado — note, downriver, not upriver into the protected zone — and the wildlife is correspondingly tamer than at the lodges deeper in.

What you trade wildlife density for here is genuine comfort. The cabanas are built in traditional style but finished to hotel standard: proper plumbing, electricity, hot water, decent linens, even a small spa. Food is excellent, the bar is well-stocked, and the famous Canopy Walkway — a 344-meter suspended bridge through the upper canopy — is on the property and free for guests.

We would steer serious wildlife travelers further upriver to TRC or Refugio Amazonas, but Inkaterra is the right call if you want a soft introduction to the Amazon, or if you are pairing a couple of jungle nights with a luxury Sacred Valley and Machu Picchu trip and want the standard to stay consistent. It is also the only Tambopata lodge we would recommend for travelers who genuinely cannot tolerate roughing it.
