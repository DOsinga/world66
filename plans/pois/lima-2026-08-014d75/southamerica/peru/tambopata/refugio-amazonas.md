Refugio Amazonas is the middle child in the Rainforest Expeditions family of lodges and probably the best balance of accessibility and real wilderness on the Tambopata. It is a four-hour boat ride from Puerto Maldonado, sitting just inside the buffer zone of the Tambopata National Reserve, with a 25-meter canopy tower on the property and a smaller clay lick within walking distance.

The rooms are large, open on one side to the forest, and comfortable in a rustic way — proper beds, hot showers, decent food, no Wi-Fi by design. Most travelers come on three- or four-day packages, and four days is the sweet spot: enough time for the canopy tower at dawn, an oxbow lake paddle for giant otters and hoatzins, a night walk, and a half-day excursion deeper into the reserve.

This is the lodge to choose if you want a serious jungle experience without the seven-hour upriver commitment of TRC. Wildlife is genuinely good here — you will see plenty — just not at TRC's density. Many travelers combine the two on a single trip, two nights at Refugio plus three at TRC, which is the package Rainforest Expeditions sells as their flagship.
