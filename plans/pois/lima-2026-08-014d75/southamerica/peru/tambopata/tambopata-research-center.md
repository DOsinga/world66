Tambopata Research Center, usually called TRC, is the deepest commercial lodge in the Tambopata National Reserve and the one to pick if wildlife is the whole point of your jungle trip. It sits a seven-hour boat ride upriver from Puerto Maldonado, deep inside the uninhabited core of the reserve, and it shares its clearing with a working scientific station that has been studying macaws here for over thirty years. The famous Chuncho-style clay lick is a fifteen-minute walk away, and you wake up to it.

The lodge is run by Rainforest Expeditions and the standard package is five days, four nights — anything shorter and you spend most of the trip on the boat. Rooms are open-walled with one side facing the forest, mosquito nets over the beds, no Wi-Fi, limited electricity. The food is surprisingly good given how remote you are. Guides here are excellent, often biologists.

If you can only do one Amazon lodge in your life, this is the one we would push you toward. It is not cheap and the journey in is long, but the wildlife density — macaws, monkeys, peccaries, sometimes jaguar tracks on the beach — is on a different level from anywhere closer to town.
