Estepona sits on the western end of the Costa del Sol, and it has managed to hold onto more of its Andalusian identity than most of its neighbours. The [old town](/europe/spain/andalucia/estepona/old_town), with its whitewashed walls and flower-draped balconies, is genuinely charming, and the town has invested heavily in public art and street murals — over 50 large-scale works now cover the facades of the old quarter, the product of an annual street art festival running since 2012.

The [beach](/europe/spain/andalucia/estepona/estepona_beach) runs for about 17 km and is relatively uncrowded by Costa del Sol standards. The [marina](/europe/spain/andalucia/estepona/estepona_marina) area has restaurants and a working fish market where the local fleet brings its catch. The [Orchidarium](/europe/spain/andalucia/estepona/orchidarium), a botanical garden built around a glass-domed rotunda near the port, has one of Spain's largest orchid collections and is one of the coast's more unexpected pleasures. Golf is the other main draw — the surrounding hills hold dozens of courses, and the sport is woven into the local economy.

Estepona makes a useful base for exploring the western Costa del Sol, with Marbella about 20 minutes east and Gibraltar reachable in under an hour.
